The government remains "determined" to deport Rochdale grooming gang leader Shabir Ahmed, a Home Office minister has said, months after it stated it was considering emergency laws to do so.

Ahmed, 73, was released from prison in July after serving 14 years for rape and sexual offences against girls, some as young as 12.

Following his release, the government indicated it would seek to deport Ahmed back to Pakistan, even hinting at emergency legislation to do so.

Having been stripped of his British citizenship, he retains only Pakistani nationality.

However, he is still living in Britain as existing legal rules prevent his deportation, owing to 50-year-old legislation that prohibits the removal of certain Commonwealth citizens.

On Wednesday, a minister confirmed that officials continue to examine "every option" to remove him from the country.

![The government previously indicated it would seek to deport Ahmed (pictured) back to Pakistan, even hinting at emergency legislation to do so](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/07/05/11/00fb4ff0be3cfb812b503e0ed19432a0Y29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzgzMzMxMDIz-2.25546959.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

It came as Lord Collins of Highbury was asked in the House of Lords whether the Home Office had threatened Pakistan with visa sanctions or the withdrawal of foreign aid.

Lord Collins replied: “We are pursuing every option to deport Shabir Ahmed. We talk to a range of countries about returns and deportations, but it would be inappropriate for us to give a running commentary on discussions.”

He added that the Immigration and Asylum Bill gave ministers extra powers to deport “the vilest foreign criminals”.

Lord Collins continued that the Government had deported nearly 12,000 foreign criminals in the two years since it came to office, a 41% increase on the final two years of the previous Conservative administration.

He added: “We have deprived Ahmed of his citizenship, the United Kingdom citizenship, a very rare act to take. We are determined to ensure that he is deported.”

The Government has been examining ways to deport Ahmed since his release.

The 1971 law forbids the removal of a small group of Commonwealth citizens who arrived in the UK more than 50 years ago.

Reports have suggested Pakistan might not accept Ahmed because he had renounced his citizenship, but a Number 10 spokesperson previously confirmed that officials had raised the issue with counterparts in Islamabad.

Shadow Home Office minister Lord Cameron of Lochiel said: “Removing the legal barrier to his deportation is pointless if the Pakistani government refuses to accept him.”
