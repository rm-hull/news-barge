Britons are set for fresh winter misery as household energy costs are set to soar by more than a quarter in January - with the typical bill set to jump by more than £400.

Experts at Cornwall Insight have predicted Ofgem’s energy price cap will rise by 26 per cent to £2,166 a year on January 1, up £443 on the level between October and December to hit the highest since 2023.

It said the government’s £100 household energy payment announced in the Budget for homes in council tax bands A to D in England is not included in the forecast because it is a one-off payment made outside the price cap.

Ofgem will announce the quarterly price cap level for January to March on November 25.

A further jump to energy bills will pile more pressure on Andy Burnham’s government to take additional action on the cost of living crisis as Britons struggle under hefty price hikes for food and energy.

![Household energy costs are set to soar by more than a quarter in January](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/18/08/01M7PVY0VS0M8JNZBDNNW4PRJQ.jpg?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&auto=webp)

Releasing the forecast, Craig Lowrey, retail markets principal at Cornwall Insight, said: “After already climbing in October, energy bills look set to rise again in the new year, and the timing could hardly be worse, with colder weather in January pushing up energy use and households already dealing with the cost-of-living crisis.”

The market intelligence firm says that rising costs continue to be driven by conflict in the Middle East, with oil and gas trade disrupted worldwide since February.

Mr Lowrey said: “Once again, the cause lies thousands of miles away, yet another reminder of how events beyond our control have a very real impact on our energy bills.

“While the £100 Household Energy Payment and the removal of VAT from electricity bills will be welcomed, ultimately this support can only chip away at the edges of a bill that continues to be pushed up by events in the Middle East.”

Official figures released on the same day as the forecast show that inflation in the UK reached 3.6 per cent in August, which analysts say is driven primarily by rising energy costs.

Sophie Hale, research director at the Resolution Foundation, said: “Another global energy shock has delivered a further blow to UK living standards in October, pushing inflation up to its highest level in a year.

“The worst may be yet to come as energy costs spread to the prices of other goods and services. Any further support this winter must be more accurately targeted at the households who need it most.”

## Why are energy bills set to rise again?

Recent increases and the latest projected rise have largely been driven by the Middle East conflict, experts say, which has disrupted global gas supplies and pushed international wholesale prices higher.

Electricity prices in the UK remain closely tied to the cost of gas, and so global market spikes feed directly into household bills.

![The government's £100 one-off household energy payment will start arriving from January](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/11/00/ed4950e4f31f1db01b1969314e008f7fY29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzk0NDA5OTAy-2.86331177.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Wholesale gas prices have actually shown a slight decline since the end of October, with trade intelligence firm Kpler finding that shipping has begun to pick up as exporters adapt to the conflict.

However, energy regulator Ofgem sets its future price caps based on set ‘observation period’, meaning it never reflects wholesale prices in real-time. For January to March’s figure, this will be mid-August to mid-November – with UK gas prices hitting a near-four year high on 30 October.

Meanwhile, the government's £100 one-off household energy payment does not reduce the headline forecast because it is paid directly into bank accounts rather than deducted from energy unit rates. It will start arriving from January.

## What support is available for rising costs?

Alongside the recently-announced household energy payment, the government offers several targeted schemes for certain qualifying households.

Through the Warm Home Discount scheme, around six million households will receive an automatic £150 cut to their electricity bills this winter.

In England and Wales, households must be in receipt of certain means-tested benefits to qualify, including Housing Benefit, Pension Credit and Universal Credit.

For pensioners, the Winter Fuel Payment can also provide between £100 and £300 towards heating costs. The rules have been revised in recent years, and it is now only available to pensioners with a taxable income of under £35,000.

Households in receipt of certain benefits may also be in line for Cold Weather Payments, depending on weather conditions. This scheme pays qualifying households £25 for every consecutive seven-day period of freezing weather (0C or below) in their area between November and March. Nearly 1.5 million households received a payment last winter.
