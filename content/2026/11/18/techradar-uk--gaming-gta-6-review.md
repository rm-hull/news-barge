Grand Theft Auto 6 is an absolute triumph, and one of the most complete, most gripping, and downright coolest video games I have ever played, and is an essential must-play experience for PS5 and Xbox Series X|S owners. I have wondered for days about how to start my *Grand Theft Auto 6*review. Each time I’ve taken a break from my many hours of gameplay, I’ve spent ages just thinking about a great moment, scene, mission, or world event that I’ve convinced myself I need to relate, and perhaps hang an opening paragraph on. Then I boot up my next play session, and I experience new moments, new scenes, new events that make me rethink it all over again.**Platform reviewed:** PS5 (on PS5 Pro)**Available on:** PS5, Xbox Series X|S**Release date:** November 19, 2026

So perhaps that’s exactly where I need to start when trying to paint a picture of the experiences on offer in Rockstar’s latest action-adventure behemoth: it’s filled with brilliance throughout, from big moments of gripping narrative to small unscripted and memorable encounters, from chaotic and bombastic action to quieter moments of character exploration and mature storytelling with deft writing. From the outrageously good graphics and audio to multi-pursuit excellence, be it driving and racing or shooting, and from the joy of exploring one of the densest maps and most alive and breathtaking open-world sandboxes I’ve experienced to the exquisite freedom to do whatever you want, whenever you want.

Simply, *GTA 6* has got it all — it’s the best title in the series, and is the best and most complete single-player open-world experience I've ever played. There’s so much to talk about, and it does everything brilliantly.

## A magnificent memory maker

![Jason and Lucia riding motorcycles in GTA 6](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/bCj2Kp3H4VQyTP9wz7AyEA-1200-80.jpg)

Even now, after spending around 100 hours in *GTA 6*, devouring all that its brilliant open world and enthralling story have to offer, it’s the memorable moments and cinematic missions that, I think, show the game at its very best. To put some flesh on those bones, there are two examples of the experience you’re getting in* GTA 6* that I find myself continually reaching for; a bombastic moment from the story and a rather simple open-world moment from the early hours of the game that show some of the best of the game.

The story moment is a typically Rockstar-style main mission that has high-stakes drama, gunfights, and a bit of absurdity laced throughout it too, all making for a ridiculously fun escape from a hospital that Jason and Lucia have to complete at a chapter-ending crescendo, a turning point in their story. It’s a mission that’s brilliantly designed and crafted, with a setup that gradually crescendos into an escape that’s gripping and heart-racing, and slightly ridiculous too.

The open-world moment occurred early in the game, when I was playing as Jason. I was driving around the first area of the map in his default car — a pretty cool, 80s-style muscle car — after a mission, as the sun set and night came on in the Florida Keys-inspired area, when I was immersed in a brilliant scene of neon lights piercing through a clear black sky, cruising in steady traffic in my open-top ride, with Chris Isaak’s *Lie to Me* playing on one of the many excellent radio stations. The air ‘felt’ warm and summery, the atmosphere felt thick and perfectly 80s-mixed-with-modern, and the vibes were just sublime; it was a perfect in-game moment that seemed to distill everything aesthetically brilliant and scene-setting about the game, and part of me feels like I've been chasing it down in every play session since, such was its lasting impact.

## A deft double act

![Jason and Lucia holding hands in a pool in GTA 6](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/UpMuELppQ2RR43LgxmfETD-1200-80.jpg)

The story of Jason Duval and Lucia Caminos is punctuated with many such moments throughout the game; from action-movie set pieces to tension-filled stakeouts and planning missions. Their Bonnie and Clyde-esque crime partnership takes you on a rollercoaster ride through Vice City and its environs, borrowing the moving settlement or base feature from *Red Dead Redemption 2* expertly, and providing you with blazing highs, calming low points, and absurdly hilarious sideways deviations too.

Jason and Lucia’s relationship is compelling, and both leads will stay with you long after you’ve finished playing. There’s always something driving them forward together, and a mature and nuanced take on a romantic partnership caught up in crime helps foster a compelling narrative; the dual protagonists are characters you come to know more about over time, and become invested in and care about — especially when things are at their most dicey. The relationship also works in terms of the in-game mechanics: swapping between them is simple and quick, each has their own unique personality and ways of reacting to situations, and you can genuinely rely on the other character when taking down other criminal operations across Vice City.

They complement each other perfectly, and even though you can choose to have their relationship cool off romantically, they’ll always be bonded together as a criminal pairing, trying to escape an increasingly complicated and multilayered criminal and nefarious underbelly of Vice City while also taking advantage of the many and lucrative opportunities it offers them.

![Jason and Lucia entering a store in a pool in GTA 6](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/haZdv2WSNhtLvzLNg8KmnK-1200-80.jpg)

The pace of the narrative feels a bit slower than *GTA 5*, particularly at the beginning (and periodically over the course of the game), which largely works to promote a slow-burn effect, though some later lulls and empty spots in the last quarter ort so of the narrative do drag a little. There are also ups and downs galore as the couple bounce between encounters with old contacts, drug dealers, players in the rap music scene, smugglers, and organized crime bosses, and between chaotic high-stakes moments that test their relationship on side quests to main missions, and the quieter, more mundane experiences they share, such as working out in the gym, walking and holding hands, and just hanging out.

Their story is also a more mature one than in previous GTA games; a direction of travel that’s perhaps inspired by the emotional story of Arthur Morgan in *Red Dead 2*, with serious notes about family, relationships, being haunted by past experiences, and the torments of continuously living on the run. Throw in the crescendo of higher-stakes missions rising throughout, and even heist-upon-heist layering in some parts, and it all makes for an excellent tale that you’re both eager to see come to a satisfying conclusion but also never want to end.

> The world offers so much in terms of scripted, location-based things to do, as well as moments of anything-can-happen bedlam

This also feeds into how Jason and Lucia perceive and can react in and to the world: it’s not a GTA game that’s just about chaos and bedlam; it’s also about awareness of the world and the people you share it with, and facing the consequences of your actions. The duo can react in a more restrained manner, channeling empathy and accountability — which can then lead to different outcomes in encounters. You can defuse confrontations on the street, spare each and every criminal by incapacitating them (shooting in the kneecaps or elbows can remove them from the game and act as a stealth takedown, not alerting other enemies), and make conversation choices in key moments that are much less ruthless than GTA of old.

## Under the Vice City lights

![A skyline image of Vice City in GTA 6](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/UBRHLMgtomAdb9M6YhseXU-1200-80.jpg)

Naturally, the story, characters, and systems wouldn’t have as much impact or carry as much weight were it not also for the role of the world itself, which provides some of the best moments in the game that I’ve experienced. As well as the more ambient moments, like the aforementioned experience of driving under lights at night, the world offers *so* much in terms of scripted, location-based things to do, as well as moments of anything-can-happen bedlam.

Of the former moments, some of my favorites have been taking down criminal operations like weapons-smuggling warehouses, drug-manufacturing buildings, or cash storage hideouts. You can attempt to go in quiet and clear out enemies to avoid any confrontation and then steal at will, relying on the Focus system, allowing you to disarm or kneecap (or eliminate) enemies in slow-mo, or you can take the guns-blazing approach, blasting your way to the valuables before the cops arrive and make the situation much more dangerous and difficult. Each is satisfying in its way, though, invariably, you’ll get an action-packed mix of all the above, as you satisfyingly clear out Vice City’s worst while also lining your pockets and topping up your savings account (yep, as well as cash, there’s a bank account system that keeps your money safe). I have loved relying on silenced weapons and the focus system to crawl up ladders and slowly eliminate gangs totally before helping myself to their goodies, and have even built up a list of favorite locations to rob.

> There’s plenty of environmental storytelling in every corner of the map, giving clues to the everyday lives of Vice City’s denizens, from secret cults to signs advertising work that you can respond to

Making such encounters even more impactful, Rockstar has made great improvements to the weapon play generally, with feedback and weight from shots and weapons feeling heavy and forceful. There's less of a spray-and-pray approach now, and you'll need to be much more careful in your targeting, be it lethal or non-lethal.

The unscripted moments, meanwhile, have been some of my best experiences in the world of *GTA 6*. One time, as Lucia, I was checking my phone in my car outside a shop, and a suit-wearing criminal casually got into the rear passenger seat, pointed a gun at me, and calmly instructed me to give him a ride to a location. Along the way, he ensured that my face was captured by security and traffic cameras, and upon reaching the destination, I witnessed him (sort of, behind a closed door) carry out his crime, after warning me that cops would soon be all over the place. I bailed — and I have never seen this man again in more than 50 hours of play since.

![GTA 6](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/PP3nR3hAhDSfesbNZijQc5-1200-80.jpg)

Elsewhere, I’ve interrupted an armored car robbery and helped myself to the swag; made the most of an escaping drug dealer crashing his car and leaving his cash strewn on the street; helped, and benefited from, a man making a fraudulent legal claim after having a car crash with a corporation's truck; interrupted a botched jewelry store robbery by female thieves dressed in what appeared to be onesies crossed with gimp suits; stopped kids throwing stones over a bridge, only to have my car stolen; encountered a lawyer working out of a dumpster; and even crashed my car accidentally into Jason’s during a ‘race’ over a bridge in a mission that caused him to tumble devastatingly over the edge of a highway.

It really is a fantastic memory-maker of a world; it begs to be discovered and explored, and, even when you’ve set out with the keenest of intentions, you’ll regularly find yourself being diverted from your mission and spending hours engaging in side-hustles and other diversions. The distractions are plentiful and alluring, and there’s always the possibility of unpredictable and spontaneous experiences; even accidental fender-benders can result in a mob of angry citizens chasing after you.

> You could spend a whole game’s worth of time in each micro-area just wandering about and soaking up the people and atmosphere, and you’ll undoubtedly learn about the culture of a place

This being Rockstar, the world is alive beyond the activities you’re engaged in at any given moment. There’s plenty of environmental storytelling in every corner of the map, giving clues to the everyday lives of Vice City’s denizens, from secret cults to signs advertising work that you can respond to. The people and NPCs have all been unique in my experience; I honestly haven’t heard any NPC chatter or background chit-chat twice.

![GTA 6](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/hK2meKtt7KV5yAnrdHNTVX-1200-80.jpg)

The cast of supporting actors who come into and out of Jason and Lucia’s lives are also brilliant and worthy of mention. Friendlies like Cal and Brian are some of my favorites, with every scene in which they appear being memorable and expertly delivered. Elsewhere, both enemies and companions who appear later in the game are captured effortlessly by both performers and the tech, while every person in the world can be interacted with, and you can get a unique reaction out of them.

Each region and urban area is captured brilliantly, and with its own distinct vibes, from the bustle and nightlife of downtown VC to the surfer-dude vibes of the Keys area and the small-town feeling of smaller settlements; even the natural and wild landscapes are, unsurprisingly, highlights. You could spend a whole game’s worth of time in each micro-area just wandering about and soaking up the people and atmosphere, and you’ll undoubtedly learn about the culture of a place, and maybe even divisions between the different areas of Vice City.

![GTA 6](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/WS6AvT295UAPDhhbNGUyYJ-1200-80.jpg)

The way that Rockstar then layers audio excellence on top of this world-design is also utterly brilliant. The ambient music is full of rumbly synthy bass to accentuate tension and atmosphere at key moments; the radio stations are superbly realized, featuring everything from iconic ‘80s songs to absurd podcasts. There are moments in the game that use famous songs *brilliantly* in goosebump-inducing, spine-tingling ways that heighten your own adrenaline and enjoyment. These, plus homages to famous ‘80s films that are enjoyable and downright funny, as well as plenty of nods to contemporary popular culture, especially through the game’s Instagram-like social media app.

This pitch-perfect creation of place, sense of place, atmosphere, and deployment of media mean that *GTA 6*offers a brilliant combination of a modern, of-the-times aesthetic and setting that’s often simultaneously imbued with the ‘80s vibes that Vice City became famous for in its original guise (the city first appeared in* Grand Theft Auto: Vice City*, a game set in 1986).

## Systems on systems on systems

![GTA 6](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/MbhPDbAaUMVSfbQmTGhgCi-1200-80.jpg)

The glue that holds the characters, story, and world together is the game’s many and varied systems. *GTA 6* is as deep as it is wide, and there are an incredible number of mechanics and feature sets here that factor into each and every scenario and gameplay session.

There are RPG-like elements in terms of decision trees and dialogue, plus an emphasis on spending time on your character with their systems too: working out makes them buff, and boosts their health, stamina, and Focus ability; eating and drinking — making sure you don’t go hungry is important — and sleeping regularly can boost these further.

You can track your character’s health status on their cellphone, which in itself is an integral system of the character, as well as receive locations and targets, info and pictures from your contacts, do your online banking, call RydeMe (Uber-esque taxi rides) drivers, and engage a scooter service that can deliver your car to you, no matter where you are.

![An image of a sports car outside a club in GTA 6](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/7L7X4de947exevQxgbzFGV-1200-80.jpg)

Cars themselves? Another system, but in a good way — for one thing, they're plentiful, varied, responsive, and a pleasure to drive, be it in the streets of downtown Vice City or on the long and winding roads through the rolling landscapes of Lummox County. With plenty of unique models to find and keep or sell, Jason and Lucia’s cars really are the ‘Red Dead 2 horse’ of *GTA 6*. You’ll use a car to store your long guns and outfits, and also any stash bags that you’re carrying, or keep hunting kills in the trunk for later sale.

I’ve found it absolutely essential to have a dedicated car within reach or on call at all times, as it’ll undoubtedly get you out of a hole by providing you with the right weapons, storage space, or enabling you to change outfits to evade police attention. That said, relying on your own car might also be the very thing that gets you recognized and back into trouble — unless you take the time, and have the money, to have it resprayed.

Attention from the law is another serious system which is multi-faceted, and all the better for it. The experience of being pursued by the police feels like it strikes the right balance between a sense of pressure, of you being a nuisance, and the odds being against you, with being able to actually get away. Importantly, though, there’s no longer just a six-star system to evade and wait out; there’s an added element of how you escape too.

Being caught on security cameras, having witnesses or cops recognizing your clothes, face, car, or gun can all play into how much heat will be on you, and will all need managing and addressing individually — camera footage will need to be destroyed if stored locally; you’ll need a different outfit and car if possible, for example — when you're on the run; it's not just a matter of running and hiding and waiting.

> It’s in the game’s systems that you really feel like you’re not just playing the game of GTA 6, but living the life of Jason or Lucia.

And you definitely won’t always want to attract attention or be overly violent, as this will affect your criminal profile — it’s all part of the game’s system of judging *how* you go about your actions, not whether they are ‘good’ or ‘bad’. Using restrained, non-lethal incapacitations will keep your profile toward the ‘professional’ end of the spectrum, while excessive violence and aggression toward innocents will move you more toward the ‘psycho’ end of the spectrum.

The game’s systems and mechanics are always and everywhere brilliantly deployed, too, perfectly balancing the need and benefits of immersion with that of practicality given the context. During missions, for example, you’ll get moments when you’re switching between characters that not only embrace the dual-protagonist setup, but make for incredibly cinematic experiences that add something extra to set pieces and the narratives of each operation. The experience of switching between Lucia and Jason as one disables security in one location while the other holds off enemies and buys time adds an exhilarating edge to grand cinematic finales.

It’s in the game’s systems that you really feel like you’re not just playing the game of *GTA 6,* but living the life of Jason or Lucia. There’s so much to do with your characters, and so much to go and see. Take it all in.

## The technicalities of the law

![An overhead view of a marina in GTA 6](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/m7Ydq4sLiC7hTsi3tzCaca-1200-80.jpg)

These systems work so well because the game is a marvel from a technical perspective. In every way that the game can or needs to work technically, it excels. It looks spectacular all the time, with a world that’s vibrant and vivid, but also changeable and subject to weather systems that then have knock-on effects. It sounds brilliant too, with every noise, from distant sirens to cars screeching around corners, and from hailstorms of gunfire in enclosed areas to the rustling of wind through foliage, executed wonderfully.

I know some folks won’t be able to see past the 30 frames per second performance of the game, but that’s not what I’m talking about here — and the game is brilliant even at that frame rate, even in 2026. I’ve been able to enjoy the immense graphical fidelity, detail, reflections, lighting, particles, and water, even at 30fps on PS5 Pro — channeling my experiences with loads of great games that were locked to 30fps at release, like *The Witcher 3*,* The Last of Us*, and, of course,* Red Dead Redemption 2*.

It’s actually, perhaps, in this technical brilliance that some tiny negatives about *GTA 6*appear — and they really are tiny. The weapon wheel UI is very simple, and almost unbecoming of the overall experience that* GTA 6* offers. Elsewhere, the systems are so efficient they’re predictable; for example, everyone picks up their phone after three rings when you call them, RydeMe drivers appear nearby instantly when you request a ride, and Jasona or Lucia’s replies to your text messages appear even before you’ve put your phone away.

## Superb satire and acute absurdity

It wouldn’t be a great Grand Theft Auto game without a big serving of satire, absurdity, and humor too. I’ve always found this aspect of the titles enjoyable, with the humor often channelling the zeitgeist to poke fun at the mores and fashions of our own society.

This has manifested in a whole host of ways during my gameplay, and I’m sure there will be many more examples I haven’t experienced yet. Some standouts have included poking fun at gym-bro culture, hucksters hawking get-rich-quick schemes on the radio, having run-ins with crypto scam artists, and even a whole storyline about a Juice Plus-style pyramid-selling scheme.

![An image of a gym bro character in GTA 6](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/Wt5EN4au9DpEHcvsKVEiRk-1200-80.jpg)

Overall, I have never, ever been in a world quite like *GTA 6*’s Vice City; it is unbelievably brilliant, and utterly full of fabulously memorable moments — moments that are over too quickly sometimes.

But that’s part of the brilliance and charm too — that these moments are fleeting one-offs, transient moments of magic, encounters destined to happen only once. And *Grand Theft Auto 6* is filled with them, and they’re destined to keep us immersed and engaged for years to come.

This is a generational release in video games, a must-play experience, and the first and only game I’ve awarded TechRadar Gaming’s maximum five stars — and I still feel like I’ve barely scratched the surface of what it has to offer. It’s an exceptional video game that shows what a true sandbox environment can be and what such a breathtaking world can offer players. I can’t wait to keep diving back in, hour after hour, chasing those magic moments down to experience more of the absorbing world of *GTA 6*.

## Don't play it if...

**A targeted 30 frames per second is a deal-breaker to you**  
Perhaps the only thing holding *GTA 6* back in the year 2026, and on expensive home consoles, is the game’s limited target of 30fps. If you really can’t play games at this framerate, then you might be better off holding out hope for the PC version.

## Accessibility features

There are several accessibility-adjacent settings and a couple of dedicated accessibility options to work with in *GTA 6*. In terms of the latter, there’s a high-contrast shader setting that applies a color to entities and interactions in the world, a setting to reduce the brightness of lights, and an option to add color streaks to the focus-trail ability, too. There are also a bunch of accessibility gameplay settings that can make the controls of most of the game’s activities easier, or simplify inputs, and you can reduce damage taken, enable auto driving, and simplify mini games. There are also dedicated audio accessibility settings, such as a tinnitus-reduction toggle.

On top of those accessibility-adjacent settings are a host of options on control mapping, camera angles, shake, and bob, and you can also tweak the scale and readability of UI and subtitles, making for a decent selection of onboard accessibility settings from Rockstar.
