The UK-EU “relationship reset” agreement, which began in earnest under Keir Starmer, is expected to be signed off by Andy Burnham in Brussels on Friday.

A leaked text of the second draft shows an agreement has largely been reached with some outstanding points.

Here’s what is and isn’t addressed in the 72-paragraph joint communique, from pet passports and cheese imports to student fees and artist touring visas.

## I’m 29 – will the under-30s youth mobility scheme help me live in an EU country?

You may not make it. The deal states it will “allow people from the EU and the UK to reside for up to three years to engage in activities such as work, studies, au pairing, volunteering, or simply travelling”.

But the deal may not be active until 2030 because work permits are the preserve of national governments, not something Brussels can solve.

“We aim to apply this agreement no later than 2028 and endeavour to ensure that opportunities under the scheme will be available to our young people no later than January 2030,” the draft says.

## I am an EU student. I want to apply for a year’s study in the UK. Will I pay home fees?

This has yet to be agreed. The two options in the draft text are a halfway house between international and home fees, or a concession from the UK to allow EU citizens pay the same as British students (or Irish ones, who are also eligible for home fees under a separate longstanding agreement).

The choices, according to the draft, are “significantly reduced undergraduate” or “equal treatment” with UK nationals on tuition fees.

## Will there be a limit on the youth mobility scheme?

The British government wants to cap youth mobility at between 50,000 and 80,000 places to reduce impact on overall migration numbers. The EU is pushing for at least 100,000.

Some EU states, including Germany, are open to the idea of allowing unlimited numbers of British young people to work, live or study in their countries regardless of UK reciprocation.

## Will the government finally get a deal to help UK artists touring in the EU?

Elton John has long been among those demanding the government fix a flaw in the Brexit deal which saw gigging bands or theatre groups unable to tour Europe without major bureaucracy (and its associated cost) since 2021.

The EU has resisted negotiating on the issue because, like youth mobility, it touches on work permits which are a national remit. But in what appears to be a win for the UK, touring artists do feature in the draft text.

No deal been reached, but it has been agreed that “the EU and the UK should explore further mutually beneficial solutions” for “touring artists”.

## I am a small cheesemaker – can I export to EU customers without health certificates and physical checks again?

Not yet. The draft agreement says the sanitary and phyto-sanitary (SPS) agreement will not be implemented until 1 March 2028.

This takes into account primary legislation that is needed on the UK side to dynamically align with EU standards.

## Any mention of pet passports?

No. These are tied to the SPS deal, meaning British travellers will still have to fork out up to £200 every time they take their dog or cat to an EU country.

Travel and transport officials are hoping relevant changes to the SPS agreement can be brought forward.

“Around 15% of our customers have pets with them. For them it will make a huge difference,” Yann Leriche, the chief executive of Getlink, the owner of Eurotunnel, told the Guardian.

## Has the UK agreed to tariffs on Chinese EVs?

No. The EU has pressed the UK to follow its trade defence measures which include quotas on Chinese hybrid-electric cars and tariffs on fully electric cars.

The UK has said it will not be coerced into a decision but is demanding that British manufacturers are not locked out of the upcoming Made in Europe legislation in exchange for forming a kind of trade club with the EU on autos.

Both sides agree to protectionist measures in “a particular supply chain” but there is no agreement yet on how that will apply. According to the text, the EU proposes that “the UK would be bound to ensure in its policies and national legal order the same outcomes” as measures imposed by the EU. The UK’s proposed text says: “We will aim to protect supply chains consistently across our markets and guard against potential risks of circumvention.”

## What about the new ‘trusted partnership’?

It’s gone. Instead of a vague “trusted partnership”, both sides commit to a “partnership for economic resilience”.

The language in the latest draft agreement has also been tightened up in a concession to the UK, say diplomats.

## What about migration and security?

The UK wants to restore pre-Brexit access to EU databases but this will not be agreed on Friday. The EU will instead explore giving the UK access to its proposed Prüm International scheme “on data exchange for police cooperation to chosen third-country partners”.

## And what about the EU’s biometric database?

There is no mention of Eurodac, the database that stores biometrics of asylum seekers in the EU, and diplomats say sharing this data, which was not collected for law enforcement purposes, could be tricky.

But according to the draft, the EU will “continue exploring ways to reinforce mutual and reciprocal exchanges of data on criminal records of third-country nationals”.
