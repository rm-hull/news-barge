## Polls by Ipsos, Data10, GAD3 and Hamalgama Métrica all place the PP as the leading force, though they differ on the gap to the socialists and far-right Vox’s result. All four surveys suggest a right-wing coalition would secure a majority.

The four opinion polls published this Sunday, one week before Spain's general election on 29 November, all place the Popular Party (PP) of Alberto Núñez Feijóo as the leading political force, ahead of the PSOE of Pedro Sánchez.

However, the surveys show differences in the distance between the two parties and in the representation that would be won by the far-right party Vox, led by Santiago Abascal.

In general, the polls in the middle of the election campaign suggest that together the PP and Vox would win between 190 and 210 seats, exceeding the 176 needed for an absolute majority in the Congress of Deputies. The PSOE would secure between 98 and 119 seats, while Frente Amplio would become the fourth-largest force in those estimates that provide specific figures for its representation.

Ipsos, in its poll published *(source in Spanish)* by 'La Vanguardia', puts the PP at 30.3% of the vote and 127 seats, compared with 29.8% and 119 seats for the PSOE. Vox would obtain 17.9% and 63 MPs. The two right-wing parties together would reach 190 seats.

The poll also assigns 11 seats to ERC, seven to EH Bildu and six to Frente Amplio. Junts would win five and Adelante Andalucía four.

Data10, in its tracking survey published *(source in Spanish)* by 'Okdiario', gives the PP 32.1% of the vote and 133 seats, and the PSOE 27% and 111 seats. Vox would reach 19% and 63 MPs. The combined total for PP and Vox would be 196 seats. Frente Amplio, together with territorial coalitions in its space, would win 6.6% of the vote and ten seats.

GAD3, in its poll published *(source in Spanish)* by 'ABC', estimates that the Popular Party would obtain 31.1% of the vote and 131 seats. The PSOE would be on 28.4% and 110 seats, while Vox would reach 18.1% and 63 MPs. The PP-Vox total would be 194 seats. Frente Amplio would win seven seats, with 5.8% of the vote.

Hamalgama Métrica, in its poll published *(source in Spanish)* by 'Vozpópuli', calculates that PP would receive 31.8% of the vote and 130 seats, while the PSOE would fall to 25.4% and 98 seats. Vox would reach 20.6% and 80 MPs, which would bring the combined total for the two parties to 210 seats. ERC and Frente Amplio would each win nine seats.

The differences between the four projections reflect different scenarios in this final stretch of the campaign. The gap between the PP and PSOE ranges from the 0.5 points calculated by Ipsos to the 6.4 points estimated by Hamalgama Métrica. The projected representation for Vox also varies, with a band of between 63 and 80 seats.

In any case, all four studies place PP and Vox above the threshold for an absolute majority and do not assign any representation to Sumar in their estimates.
