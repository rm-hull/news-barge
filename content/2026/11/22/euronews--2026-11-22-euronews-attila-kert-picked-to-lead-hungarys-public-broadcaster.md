## Euronews Hungary head Attila Kert has been tapped to run Hungary's public broadcaster, which faces an overhaul after years of bias criticism under Viktor Orbán. He pledges to restore credibility, launch streaming and probe Russian influence.

Euronews journalist Attila Kert has been selected as chief executive of Hungary's public broadcaster, Hungarian Radio and Television (MRTV).

Kert has led Euronews' Hungarian team in Budapest since 2013.

Under the previous government, led by Viktor Orbán, the national broadcaster was criticised by NGOs and the European Union for political bias. The institution now faces a reorganisation aimed at delivering objective, independent coverage and completing its digitalisation.

Twenty-seven candidates applied for the post, of whom 14 were found eligible. They set out their plans at a hearing earlier this week, ahead of the decision by the Independent Public Media Board, the body overseeing the transition.

"I am proud to have worked with Euronews for over 13 years. The experience I gained here has helped me a lot in my application for the new position. Without Euronews, I would be less. I am grateful to all my colleagues and thank them," Kert said.

In his presentation, Kert vowed to restore the broadcaster's credibility while reorganising it to become financially sustainable. His plans include setting up a streaming platform and investigating possible Russian influence.

Editorial Director of Euronews, Claus Strunz, said the decision is proof of the journalistic quality and editorial independence of Euronews in Hungary.

“Attila Kert believes in freedom of expression and the power of images and knows how to exercise both responsibly in every situation.

Over the past 13 years as head of our Hungarian service, he has played a pivotal role in safeguarding and strengthening Euronews’ independence and editorial neutrality, both internally and externally, in a highly polarised political and media environment.

His move from a renowned private media organisation to public-service broadcasting therefore marks a significant moment in the history of Hungarian media. Euronews is losing one of its finest journalists, while Hungarian public-service television is gaining in journalistic quality, stature and profile.

We also view his appointment to this important position as a testament to the journalistic quality and independence of Euronews in Hungary,” Strunz said.

## Public broadcaster faces reorganisation

The Hungarian public broadcaster faced years of criticism under the Orbán government over bias and disinformation.

The Organisation for Security and Co-operation in Europe (OSCE) found that MRTV's coverage was systematically skewed, with news programmes "openly and disproportionately" supporting the ruling parties' narrative while marginalising or negatively portraying opposition voices.

After his landslide election victory in April, Péter Magyar vowed to shut down the organisation and create an independent, objective outlet.

In July, the broadcaster went off air for one day, replaced by an on-screen apology: "Public media cannot lie. We apologise because we did this anyway."

Before joining Euronews, Kert held several senior roles in Hungarian media. He was editor-in-chief of the commercial networks TV2 and ATV, and later head of news at the national broadcaster.

He was appointed to lead the Hungarian-language edition of Euronews in February 2013.

In 2026, the independent Italian media research institute Osservatorio di Pavia published an audit of Euronews Hungary's coverage of the election campaign. It scored the channel 99% for editorial independence and 98% for balanced coverage.
