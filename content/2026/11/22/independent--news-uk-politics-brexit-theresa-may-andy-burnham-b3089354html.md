Former prime minister Theresa May has urged Andy Burnham to “move on” from Brexit and said she was “aghast” at the idea of reopening the debate on Britain’s membership of the EU.

It comes as another former prime minister Sir Tony Blair repeated his desire for the UK to rejoin the EU but on condition of the bloc reforming itself.

As prime minister between 2016 and 2019, Baroness May was landed with attempting to negotiate the UK’s path out of the EU following the referendum and David Cameron’s subsequent resignation.

Her entire premiership became bogged down in the issue, where she also lost the Tory majority when she called an early election in 2017 against a Jeremy Corbyn-led Labour.

As a result, a pro-Remain majority in parliament dictated events with a series of “meaningful votes”, before Boris Johnson replaced Baroness May and subsequently won the 2019 general election.

Baroness May relived her nightmare in Downing Street in an exclusive interview with the BBC’s Laura Kuenssberg on her Sunday morning political show.

![Theresa May is aghast at Brexit being reopened](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/22/8/47/Screenshot-2026-11-22-083950.png?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

With Mr Burnham now reopening the EU debate and charting a course to get the UK to rejoin the EU via the next election, she had some advice for him to “move on” instead.

She said: "What I saw was that the extent of those negotiations, the hours that have to be put in – it's across the whole of government.

"It's not just prime ministerial time, cabinet ministers' time, officials' time, to-ing and fro-ing on those negotiations. Because you have to negotiate hard in order to make sure that you're getting out what you want.

"And that's one of the reasons why I look slightly aghast at what Andy Burnham is talking about, further negotiations of going into the EU. And also we, as a country, we voted for Brexit.

"When I was prime minister I was very clear, we should deliver on that vote. So I'm afraid my message to him really is, you know, people voted for Brexit. Brexit is done. Just accept Brexit and move on."

Warning against another vote, Baroness May added: "I almost think this is what Andy Burnham is saying to the British people, sort of patting them on the head.

"He wants to pat them on their head and say, you know what, remember that vote? Well, you got it wrong, didn't you? So have another go and come out with the right answer.

"Or what he thinks is the right answer. But as I say, let's just get on with making sure the UK is as strong economically, in security terms, and in terms of opportunities for people as it can be."

![Blair wants the UK to rejoin but only if the EU reforms](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/22/10/2305674154..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Her comments came after *The Independent* revealed that the new official rejoin campaign Get In! has launched following the formation of the pro-Brexit Stay Out group.

Meanwhile, Sir Tony Blair insisted that his hopes of the UK rejoining the EU are “heavily conditioned”.

Discussing a report his thinktank the Tony Blair Institute brought out in September, he told *The Sunday Times*: "I was painted as saying Britain should just go back and rejoin - I'm not saying that - it's heavily conditioned."

He went on: “"I do think Brexit was a real mistake."

But he said: "The problem if we go back and join Europe is that; I can't say that the technology revolution is the single biggest thing, and then if Europe moves in the wrong direction on technology innovation, which they might do and presently probably are doing, that it's a good idea for Britain to rejoin. That's what's changed in this last ten years."
