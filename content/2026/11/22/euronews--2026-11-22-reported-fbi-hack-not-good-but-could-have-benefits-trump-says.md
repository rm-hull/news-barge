## The reported breach comes amid a wave of tech outages in the US, which have impacted travel, communications and banking, as well as an internal system used by the National Security Agency.

US President Donald Trump has said that a reported hack of the Federal Bureau of Investigation's (FBI) criminal justice database was "not good" but that it could have some benefits.

US media outlets reported earlier this week that the FBI's National Crime Information Center (NCIC) appeared to have been breached by hackers and that the agency had launched an investigation.

Asked about the subject on Saturday, Trump told Fox News: "We just heard about it, and I will say that it's not good. I don't like that."

"But maybe we'll learn things that we would have never learned otherwise," he continued, before referring to the "Russia hoax," the "Ukraine hoax," and alleged voter fraud.

"Maybe we'll learn about them, maybe we'll learn about the voter fraud, all the voter fraud that took place," Trump said. "I always like to put things in a positive spin."

The NCIC is a computerised index of criminal justice information and includes records on criminal history, fugitives, missing persons and stolen properties, among other topics.

The reported hack comes amid a wave of tech outages in the US, which have impacted travel, communications and banking, as well as an internal system used by the National Security Agency.

National cyber director Sean Cairncross was ousted from his role as the issues mounted, with Trump telling reporters on Friday that he "didn't think that person was good enough." The national cyber director is tasked with advising the president on cybersecurity policy and strategy, including information security and data protection as well as deterrence of "malicious" activity.
