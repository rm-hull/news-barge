## Amid conflicting reports that an Iranian tanker departed Iran’s Kharg Island on Sunday to force the US military blockade in the Strait of Hormuz, an incident was reported near the US navy blockade line. Iran threatened that any US attempt to stop its tankers would result in immediate attacks.

The UK Maritime Trade Operations (UKMTO) announced on Sunday evening that “a tanker was involved in an incident with military forces in the Gulf of Oman,” east of the Strait of Hormuz, near the southern Iranian city of Charbahar.

The UKMTO wording identifying “military forces” may indicate a US military action against a tanker, while the reported location is close to the US navy blockade line.

Also, the location and the timing of the incident are consistent of where an Iranian tanker would be in its attempt to break the US military naval blockade had it sailed from Kharg Island on Sunday morning.

“A company security officer alerted UKMTO to the incident. Authorities are aware and investigations are ongoing,” the UKMTO bulletin also said.

Iran had threatened for several days that any attempt by the US military to stop the Iranian tankers would result in a “fierce military response” and immediate Iranian strikes on energy infrastructures across the Gulf.

Earlier on Sunday, Iranian media quoting Iran’s navy said two supertankers caught fire after hitting mines in a “restricted area” east of the Strait of Hormuz and north of Ghanam Island. It was unclear if the two incidents were connected.

On Saturday, Iran’s deputy minister of foreign affairs Kazem Gharibabadi told Iranian media that the US blockade of Iranian ports “is an act of war and we will take any necessary measures to break this economic siege,” amid reports that as more than 14 Iranian tankers were being prepared off Kharg Island to break out of the US military blockade.

Iran’s president Masoud Pezeshkian also said that Iran is subject to a “silent economic war” which is “tougher than a war with missiles,” adding that Iran has the capability to withstand it.
