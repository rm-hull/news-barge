Mall of America has an AI assistant to help shoppers navigate the gigantic space — but it seems people might have better luck just aimlessly wandering around because the system isn’t much help.

The Minnesota landmark — the 13th largest shopping mall in the world and the biggest in the US — introduced an AI-powered digital concierge, dubbed Mallie, just in time for Black Friday to help shoppers make the most of their trip, but it might create more headaches than it helps prevent, at least according to one report from local outlet *Racket*.

How it works — or at least how it is supposed to work — is that visitors share what they are looking for, and the digital concierge helps with suggestions and how to get to stores that have the desired products. But it’s reportedly not going so well, at least for one journalist who tried it out.

“Mallie seems to know about as much about the mall as I do, responding with basic or outright bad information and encouraging me to sign up for some sort of marketing program,” Racket’s Jessica Armbruster wrote.

She continued, “For example, when I ask it where to take a vegan friend for lunch, it tells me to head to the food court for a salad. Which food court? I'm not sure. Which salad? Who knows! For goth-inspired clothing at Mall of America, ‘You might want to check out Nordstrom on Level 1, Macy's on Level 3, and Ann Taylor on Level 2,’ it responds when I tell it I'm ready to go full goth. Hot Topic, Ragstock, and the piercing studio are all right there, Mallie!”

![Mallie is available through the mall’s app and website, and as Armbruster did, shoppers can type or speak open-ended questions to learn about the complex](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/22/14/84579971..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Mallie is available through the mall’s app and website, and as Armbruster did, shoppers can type or speak open-ended questions to learn about the complex’s more than 500 stores, restaurants and attractions. And the AI assistant is also supposed to highlight deals, per Retail Dive.

Armbruster asked the AI assistant several other shopping-related questions, and all were off-kilter, the reporter claims, summing the experience up with: “AI is still bad. Just wander the mall or check in with a map kiosk or use your human brain like a normal person.”

*The Independent* has reached out to the Mall of America for comment.
