Unusually high water levels, an incoming swell, and elevated sea levels are combining to create an increasing risk of coastal flooding across Los Angeles County from potentially record-breaking king tides this week.

The National Weather Service described the Thanksgiving week period as "particularly concerning" due to a Kelvin wave, thermal expansion of ocean water, perigean spring tides, and a large, long-period west swell.

"Still some uncertainty in regards to how timely these will combine, but regardless significant impacts seem probable," according to the NWS.

A coastal flood advisory takes effect at 3 a.m. Monday for the Malibu coast and Los Angeles County beaches, with a coastal flood watch taking over late Tuesday night through Thursday afternoon.

An advisory will also cover Catalina Island from 3 a.m. Monday until 3 p.m. Thursday.

Across Orange County, a coastal flood advisory is set from 4 a.m. Sunday to 4 a.m. Tuesday, alongside a coastal flood watch in place from late Monday through Thursday afternoon.

Elevated to high surf will persist through much of the week. The highest flood threat is expected Wednesday and Thursday during morning high tides between 8:45 and 10 a.m.

Predicted tide levels along the Los Angeles County coast could reach 8.2 to 8.6 feet, with a reasonable worst-case scenario adding another 0.3 to 0.5 feet, forecasters said.

![](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/22/14/00/AP26319034240921.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The regions of greatest concern include west-facing beaches from Santa Monica down to Palos Verdes, in addition to the Long Beach and Naples areas.

Weather officials warned that a worst-case scenario could cause broader inland flooding, bringing a moderate to high risk of damage to coastal roads and structures, along with major beach erosion.

Shallow flooding in beach parking lots and unprotected roads, as well as moderate beach erosion, remain possible during advisory periods.

![](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/22/14/19/AP26319042103013.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

"The coastline will be dangerous with tide levels that could set new records," said Ben Ruttenberg, director of Cal Poly's Center for Coastal Marine Sciences.

"There will likely be waves and water coming to places they normally don't, and there will likely be significant coastal erosion."

The NWS advised local residents to protect vulnerable properties, follow closures, and refrain from entering flooded roads, walkways, rock jetties, and coastal areas. Officials in Los Angeles County are distributing sandbags at multiple fire stations.

Light rain could arrive Sunday night into Monday, though rainfall totals are expected to stay at a quarter-inch or less in Los Angeles County. Another weak system may bring light rain Wednesday.
