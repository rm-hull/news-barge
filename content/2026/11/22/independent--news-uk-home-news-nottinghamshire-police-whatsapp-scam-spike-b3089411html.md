Members of the public are being urged to remain vigilant after scammers used WhatsApp to impersonate banks and the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA).

Police in Nottinghamshire have recorded a spike in reports of fraudsters posing as FCA or banking staff through WhatsApp messages, voice calls and video calls.

Protect officers from Nottinghamshire Police's Economic and Cyber Crime Unit, said red flags include a caller claiming that your bank account has been compromised.

Fraud protect officer Dale Richardson, of Nottinghamshire Police, said: "We've had a number of reports of people receiving video calls via WhatsApp from a person purporting to be from the FCA or the person's bank.

"To trick victims, fraudsters have displayed the FCA logo or bank logo to make themselves appear genuine.

"This makes the scam feel more real and convincing so it's really important people are aware of this tactic and to encourage friends and family to be vigilant.

"There have also been incidents of screen sharing during these video calls and this risks fraudsters being able to obtain sensitive information or accessing a person's online banking."

![Online scams are spiking](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/10/16/01M3GTZAAN6W22B68KVBFPRRMJ.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

He said these impersonation scams form the initial stage in courier fraud. Having posed as a trusted authority, the criminal tricks their victim into surrendering bank cards, PINs, cash, or valuables to a "courier" dispatched to their home.

Adding: "In some of the reports we've seen in Nottinghamshire, Uber taxis have been used as the courier and bank cards and pins collected. The courier has then used these in stores, including Apple, to purchase goods."

Some victims have lost hundreds of pounds having fallen victim to a WhatsApp scam.

Police issued the following guidance to do with scams:

* The FCA, banks and police will NEVER ask you for personal information or ask you to transfer money.

* Never hand over sensitive personal information, like bank account PINs and passwords.

* Do not trust logos, video calls, or official-looking profiles.

* Hang up and call your bank to check for any suspicious transactions.

* Update your password and set up 2 Step Verification.
