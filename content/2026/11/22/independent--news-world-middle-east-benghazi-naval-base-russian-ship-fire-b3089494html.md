A Russian warship has been hit off the coast of Libya in a suspected Ukrainian drone attack, officials said on Sunday.

The explosion took place on board Merkury, a Russian navy warship docked at the Benghazi naval base and is suspected to have been caused by drones launched by the Ukrainian military, Libyan officials told the *Associated Press.*

Satellite images by Vantor showed the ship was a Russian Navy corvette, or small warship. The pictures show that most of the vessel had capsized and sunk on Sunday, with only its bow still visible above water while smoke rises.

No one has officially claimed responsibility for the attack.

A fire is reported to have broken out at the base and the ship is said to be severely damaged. The drones are said to have been launched from a base run by the Ukrainian military in western Libya according to an initial investigation.

Libyan military officials told *AP* that four drones had been intercepted and destroyed while two hit the warship.

The sources are affiliated with the Libyan National Army, which controls eastern and southern Libya.

![A satellite image shows fires near a cargo ship with vehicles onboard after an explosion aboard a Russian ship docked at the Benghazi naval base, which Libya's eastern-based government said was caused by a suspected drone attack, in Benghazi, Libya, November 22, 2026.](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/22/19/2026-11-22T195244Z_1029837503_RC2YSOARSIE6_RTRMADP_3_LIBYA-PORT-EXPLOSION.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Ten people, both Russians and Libyans, were injured in the attack and are receiving treatment in Benghazi, medical sources told Reuters.

The eastern-based government said in a statement that the Russian ship had stopped at the Libyan port to refuel and resupply on its way back to Russia, under maritime cooperation agreements between the two countries.

In a separate incident, a commercial vessel flying the Maltese flag was also targeted by a drone off the coast of Benghazi on Sunday, two Libyan military sources told the outlet.

![Pictures show that most of the vessel had capsized and sunk on Sunday, with only the vessel’s bow still visible above water while smoke rises](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/22/22/2026-11-22T201301Z_675733923_RC28TOA4ACEO_RTRMADP_3_LIBYA-PORT-EXPLOSION.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The eastern-based government said that port and naval base facilities, including warehouses, were safe and that normal operations had resumed after the incident.

It condemned the suspected attack as a violation of Libya's sovereignty and a threat to maritime safety and warned that it would take legal and diplomatic measures against anyone found responsible.

The government emphasised that Libya was not involved in the conflict or to any war beyond its borders.

![Fire burns after an explosion aboard a Russian ship docked at Benghazi port](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/22/19/2026-11-22T195321Z_1932497308_RC27TOAYALIU_RTRMADP_3_LIBYA-PORT-EXPLOSION.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“The Libyan government will not allow Libyan ports, territory or territorial waters to be turned into an arena for settling scores or exporting international conflicts,” it added.

It comes a day after Finland’s prime minister Petteri Orpo warned that the Russia-Ukaine conflict was “spilling over” after a Russian ship was reportedly on fire in Finnish water on Saturday.

“The war is spilling over into our neighbouring areas. Finnish authorities are handling the situation in the Gulf of Finland, where a drone strike has hit a Russian merchant vessel. Ukraine has the right to fight ‌against the aggressor,” Orpo said on X.

![In this photo released by the Finnish Border Guard, rescuers' ships try to extinguish a fire in the Russian cargo ship Sparta II in the Gulf of Finland, Sunday, Nov. 22, 2026.](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/22/15/download..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Finnish authorities said Russian vessels might eventually tow the wreckage back to Russia after the attack.

The 16-member crew of the Sparta II abandoned the vessel for lifeboats before transferring to a nearby Russian ship to make their way back to the country, Finnish Border Guard Commander Ilja Iljin told public broadcaster Yle late on Saturday.
