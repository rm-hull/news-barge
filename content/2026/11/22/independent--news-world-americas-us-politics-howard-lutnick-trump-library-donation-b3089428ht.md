Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick was on the chopping block — until he made an eight-figure donation to President Donald Trump’s planned library, according to a new report.

Trump had been on the cusp of firing Lutnick after he acknowledged during congressional testimony in February that he had visited Jeffrey Epstein’s private island more than a decade earlier.

But after Lutnick contributed $25 million to Trump’s planned presidential library in Florida, “Trump stopped talking about replacing” him, *The Wall Street Journal* reported, citing people familiar with the matter.

Lutnick, a billionaire who has known Trump for more than two decades, has held on to his post despite clashes with senior administration officials and scrutiny over his former Wall Street firm and his role in the Nvidia controversy. The *Journal* dubbed him the “ultimate survivor.”

“One thing that I find both admirable and vexing about the president is his loyalty to past friends,” outgoing North Carolina Senator Thom Tillis, a frequent Trump critic, told the outlet.

![Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick was on the chopping block until he donated $25 million to Donald Trump’s planned presidential library, according to a new report](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/22/15/06/GettyImages-2297605826.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Former White House chief of staff Susie Wiles often clashed with Lutnick and held him on a “tight leash.”

So, when Trump announced that Wiles would step down and be replaced by her deputy Stephen Miller earlier this month, Lutnick felt unshackled, telling people: “I’m not going anywhere.”

The commerce secretary came under renewed pressure last week after Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent blamed the department for delaying export restrictions on Megaspeed, a Singapore-based firm under investigation for allegedly diverting advanced Nvidia AI chips to China. Bessent and Lutnick had previously clashed over a Ukraine minerals deal, according to the book *Regime Change*.

Amid the fallout from the Nvidia scandal, White House officials privately signaled that Secretary of State Marco Rubio would be put in charge of the export-control process.

![Former White House chief of staff Susie Wiles often clashed with Lutnick and held him on a ‘tight leash’](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/22/15/50/GettyImages-2301237557.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Senior White House officials also debated removing Lutnick from office in the wake of the controversy, which centered on allegations that co-founder Jensen Huang had a romantic relationship with a suspected Chinese spy, *Reuters* reported this month.

A White House official, however, said the president has total confidence in Lutnick. And the president publicly praised him Friday, telling reporters he’s doing a “very good job.”

Lutnick also faced scrutiny over his ties to Cantor Fitzgerald, the Wall Street firm he led before handing control to his sons when he joined the Trump administration.

![The commerce secretary came under renewed pressure last week after Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent blamed the department for delaying export restrictions on a firm tied to the Nvidia scandal](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/22/15/14/GettyImages-2282068646.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“Some of the president’s senior advisers have looked at whether Lutnick’s moves as Commerce secretary benefit the firm’s investments, an issue that the president has himself raised at times,” the newspaper reported. The firm has denied there is any conflict of interest.

Emboldened by their midterm victories, Democrats have signaled plans for investigations targeting Trump administration officials and members of the president’s family, including his eldest sons, Stephen Miller and Attorney General Todd Blanche.

Lutnick and Cantor Fitzgerald, which has done business with artificial intelligence companies in Asia, are among their potential targets.

*The Independent* has contacted the Commerce Department for comment.
