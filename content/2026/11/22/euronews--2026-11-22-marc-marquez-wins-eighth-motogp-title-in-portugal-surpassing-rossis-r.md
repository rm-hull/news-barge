## The Spanish rider is crowned top-category champion in Portugal after a historic comeback in an injury-hit season. He now has ten world titles, eight in the top class.

Marc Márquez clinched his eighth MotoGP world title this Sunday by being crowned world champion at the Portuguese Grand Prix, held at the Portimão circuit.

The Spanish Ducati rider finished in eighth place, a result that was enough to secure the championship mathematically and thus move past the seven premier-class titles of Italian rider Valentino Rossi

Márquez needed to finish at least ninth to guarantee the title without depending on other results. In the end, he took the flag in eighth after Brazilian Diogo Moreira crashed on the final lap.

The race win went to Italian Marco Bezzecchi, on an Aprilia, ahead of his teammate Jorge Martín and fellow Spaniard Raúl Fernández, who completed the podium.

The championship is the tenth motorcycle world title for the rider from Cervera, who also won one in 125cc and another in Moto2. With this total, he overtakes Rossi's nine championships, seven of them in the premier class, and draws level with Italian Giacomo Agostini in the all-time ranking of premier-class titles.

The triumph is particularly significant in view of the difficulties he has faced during the season. At the end of May, after the Italian Grand Prix at Mugello, Márquez found himself 102 points behind the then leader, Bezzecchi.

A crash in the sprint race at the French Grand Prix, in Le Mans, forced him to undergo surgery, which also meant he missed the following round in Montmeló. His comeback was hampered by injuries, but from the Hungarian Grand Prix onwards he began a fightback that allowed him to claw back ground and take the lead at Misano.

After wrapping up the championship, Márquez dedicated the victory to his fans and described the title as "very special", referring to the years of work, injuries and recoveries he has had to go through to get back to the top.
