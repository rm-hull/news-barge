## Gadi Eisenkot has until early December to form a new government after both his and Netanyahu's blocs fell short of a majority in Israel's October legislative elections.

Gadi Eisenkot has invited the right-wing Likud party of caretaker Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu for "marathon" coalition talks as he continues to work to form Israel's next government, his party said on Saturday.

The talks are due to begin on Sunday and represent a "sincere and genuine effort to form a government that is as broad as possible," the centrist Yashar party said.

"The Yashar negotiating team will leave no stone unturned, enable bold moves and bring about a breakthrough that will lead to the formation of a government," it added.

Eisenkot has until early December to form a new government after both his and Netanyahu's blocs fell short of a majority in the 120-seat Knesset in Israel's October legislative elections. Netanyahu's camp finished on 53 seats, while the opposition, led by the Yashar party, ended on 48.

In Israel, a candidate tasked with forming a government has 28 days to carry out the job. The president is able to extend this by a maximum of 14 days if necessary.

Likud on Saturday outlined its position on forming a national unity government, which Netanyahu has been pushing for, including demands to halt "all personal attacks and incitement" as well as political "boycotts" against Netanyahu and the government.

It also called for a joint declaration committing to a unity government comprised of "all parties that support the existence of the State of Israel as the nation-state of the Jewish people" and without the involvement of Arab parties - which secured a record 19 seats between them in last month's elections.

"As for the talks with the Likud, we are making sincere and genuine efforts, but unfortunately there is a wide gap between rhetoric and actions, and between the Likud’s rhetoric and its actual willingness to cooperate," Eisenkot wrote in a post on social media on Friday.

He added that he intended to continue trying to "achieve a breakthrough in each of the options before us for forming a coalition that is as broad as possible" but that he did not plan to "drag this out."

"They must understand that, at the right time, I will bring a new and strong government headed by me to the Knesset for approval," he said.
