One of its most striking attractions, ***The House Fallen from the Sky***, was created in 2010 as a tribute to the artist's grandfather. Resting on its roof at a **17-degree angle**, the house contains inverted furniture, windows and bathroom fittings,** creating a disorientating experience for visitors**. Elsewhere, enormous chairs, tables, notebooks and wardrobes invite guests to** rediscover the world from a child's perspective**.

The park also features **farmers and gardeners with oversized pumpkin and plant-shaped heads**, giant ants surrounding a mud-built structure, and colourful architectural installations. Fourtou says these unusual combinations of human and botanical forms are designed to** provoke curiosity and unease**, while his oversized household objects recreate the sensations he experienced as a young child exploring his great-grandmother's home. The artist began transforming the site in 1999, before opening it to the public in 2024. Today,** Ramakech attracts around 5,000 visitors annually.**For Fourtou, the sculptures are less about nostalgia than** exploring emotions, family relationships and the memories that shape our understanding of the world**.
