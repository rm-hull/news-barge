## Xiaomi Clip: Two-minute review

During the years I've spent testing the best open earbuds, I've patiently been waiting for that one 'wow' model — the set I use as a default when I've got nothing to test; the pair I can leap to when someone asks which model they should buy. Well, finally, I can say I've found that product.

The Xiaomi Clip come from a pretty unsuspecting company, given that the Xiaomi OpenWear Stereo Pro were the second-worst open earbuds I've tested. Clearly the company's learnt from its mistakes, though, because its debut cuff-style buds are a winner.

Clipping (or ear cuff-style, if you prefer) earbuds like these don't have much scope for variety. And Xiaomi wins here not by launching some surprising spec or trait unavailable in rivals, but simply by ticking all the boxes and not presenting any major issues.

The Xiaomi Clip fit well, locking into your ear without wobbling or falling out, and I spent hours running and cycling with them during the testing process with no design issue uncovered. I love the carry case too, because it's tiny and feels premium.

While open earbuds will never sound truly fantastic to an audiophile (given that wired IEMs exist), the Xiaomi Clip are amongst the best I've heard; they offer the simple but solid V-shaped sound that fitness buyers generally prefer. They're detailed and hearty, even if you can't tweak the sound yourself.

In terms of features, they tick most (though not all) of the usual boxes, but don't excel as much as in other areas; the battery life and app offerings are all what you'd expect. The exception is a recording feature, which a few audio brands are starting to offer, letting you record voice memos or conversations using the case or buds.

When I first started testing the Xiaomi Clip, I wasn't told the price, and made a few mental guesses about the eventual cost. I was wrong — they're cheaper than I expected. So, these are in fact relatively affordable mid-range open earbuds that match Bose and Shokz' budget offerings.

I spent most of the review process sure that these would be five-star buds, but I decided to dock a half-star at the eleventh hour due to some missing features. The buds don't support LDAC and nor do they have an equalizer. The first is a shame but I can overlook it, however the latter will be a problem for a lot of listeners. Hey, Xiaomi: if you make a follow-up bud, you know what you need to do to get that perfect score.

I've used the Xiaomi Clip for several weeks, and I know they'll continue to be my go-to open earbuds for the foreseeable future. They're the best clipping buds so far, and one to beat for all other brands in this highly competitive area. If you're on the market for an option, it's easy for me to recommend them.

* **Released in November in Europe and Australia**
* **Sells for £129.99 / AU$199 (about $160)**
* **Not on sale in the US**

The Xiaomi Clip were unveiled on November 17, 2026, alongside some new wearables from the company as well as the Xiaomi 18 Pro smartphone series. That’s a little over a year on from the release of its first open earbuds.

You can buy the buds for £129.99 / AU$199 so they’re mid-priced options which undercut the premium clip buds on the market, but will set you back more than the true bargain buds. It’s a competitive price point and the competition section later will run you through some alternatives.

You’ll see that there’s no US price there. Xiaomi doesn’t sell its tech in the US. The regional pricing converts to about $160 though, if you choose to import from elsewhere. How does that look in the market? The four-star Shokz OpenDots Air also sell for $129.95 / £129.99 / AU$219, while the Anker Soundcore Aeroclip 2 (also in receipt of a four-star review from the TechRadar team) are a little pricier, at $169.99 / £159.99 (about AU$240).

What I'm saying is, there's an open goal here for a decent product at the level — and Xiaomi's just scored the winner..

## Xiaomi Clip review: Features

![The Xiaomi Clip earbuds looped around each other.](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/niWPKySkfBigDcM8zXiBA5-1200-80.jpg)

* **6-hour battery life isn't amazing**
* **ANC is surprisingly solid**
* **App has EQ including useful presets**

Xiaomi puts the Clip's battery life at 9 hours per bud, and I'd largely concur based on my own testing. The case itself brings that figure up to 38 hours, both of which are roughly average for this kind of earbud.

Usually in an earbuds review I'd talk about ANC next, but as is the case with most open-ears, there's no noise cancellation here. There's something similar, in a sound leakage tool, but we'll talk more about that later.

The Xiaomi Earbuds app brings you plenty of extra tools for the earbuds; sometimes these apps feel optional, but in the Clip's case, the app is really worth a download.

![The Xiaomi Clip and their case on the arm of a sofa.](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/p2NVNxuQHBzBfgAb8U7Uq5-1200-80.jpg)

Many of the 'standard' app features are here: there's the ability to change what the touch controls do, change between audio presets, toggle settings like in-ear detection, and play an alarm if you've lost your earbuds.

Notably, the missing feature is an equalizer; if you want anything beyond 'Enhance Treble' or 'Enhance Voice', you're out of luck. People who like to tweak their sound won't be happy with this, and it's an odd omission for any kind of earbuds.

So what makes the app worth downloading, then? Well, it's to use the audio recording tool. This lets you use the earbuds or case to record what's going on around you, as well as from calls or videos you're watching. Obviously, you'll need to abide by local laws when you're doing that kind of thing in calls, but I found it very useful for leaving myself voice memos on the fly.

* **Features score: 4 / 5**

## Xiaomi Clip review: Design

![The Xiaomi Clip case.](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/dbLh4YT58JCoDX9pYCACq5-1200-80.jpg)

* **Tiny, light carry case**
* **Reliable fit, though sometimes slight pinch**
* **Each bud is one-directional**

The Xiaomi Clip comes in the smallest carry case I’ve seen for clipping earbuds. It’s a small square box of textured plastic, which opens in a clamshell style and weighs 52g. It bears a USB-C port for charging, as well as a grille for the microphone (more on that later).

Given its size and shape, it bears more than a passing resemblance to an engagement ring box — don’t hand it to a loved one for fear of sending the wrong message — but I appreciate how small and portable it is.

You can pick the earbuds up in four colors: Sand Gold, which you see in pictures, as well as Pearl White, Graphite Black and Frost Purple. The gold model is shiny and reflective: impossible to photograph for review images without being in the snaps yourself. It's a little bit like jewelry (think stylish ear cuff, perhaps?) but I must concede that there's a small chance you *could* consider it overly blingy, depending on your tastes.

![The underside of the Xiaomi Clip.](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/hQbQji3ZDLy93eyXMAMzQ5-1200-80.jpg)

Now, onto the earbuds: given that these are clip-style earbuds, you can probably (and correctly) predict that they’re C-shaped units which clip to your ear. There’s a counterweight, and the driver unit which goes into your ear-well (or the lower part of your concha, the cavum).

Some open earbuds have softer, flexible arches, but that’s not the case here, and thus the buds aren’t as malleable as many alternatives. I thought that’d make them a little uncomfortable to wear, and there was initially a little pinch when I put them on, but my fears were thankfully unfounded. They sit firmly in the ear too, and I never had any stability issues.

The buds have some on-ear controls too: you can double or triple tap each earbud for different functions. My taps were generally picked up, even when I had gloves on, but sometimes they didn't work perfectly.

If there’s one small frustration, it’s that there’s a right bud and a left bud; most clip-style open ears can be used in either ear. It means you constantly have to check which bud is which, and make sure you’re putting the correct one in the right ear. It’s not a huge chore, but it’s something you don’t need to do in most rivals.

* **Design score: 5 / 5**

## Xiaomi Clip review: Sound quality

![The Xiaomi Clip in a man&#039;s ear.](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/q3Do9BXEYdRYfh6TRmoY25-1200-80.jpg)

* **11mm driver per bud**
* **V-shaped sound profile**
* **No support for LDAC playback**

Xiaomi has clearly changed tack from the OpenWear Stereo Pro’s ‘the more drivers, the merrier’ approach. Each Clip bud has a single 11mm driver, instead of multiple drivers to hit each point of the sonic spectrum.

It doesn’t matter, though, because the Xiaomi Clip are some of the best sounding open earbuds I’ve tested to date. They give you exactly what you expect from earbuds designed for active wear: a rich V-shaped sound.

Bass is clearly emphasised in a way that’d be a little too much in normal earbuds, but works well to compensate for how much low-end gets lost due to the form factor. In a song like ALO’s *Girl, I Wanna Lay You Down*, the rhythmic bass stands out even if you’re listening while cycling down a busy high street. One of my favorite running songs,* Lack of Water* by The Why Store, had more of a punch than many other open-ears offer.

![The Xiaomi Clip in a man&#039;s hand.](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/KYt4pKssXDRvGTTB3pAAx4-1200-80.jpg)

Then there’s the other side of the valley, treble: it’s crisp and clear enough to hold its own against the low-end, with plenty of detail to catch vocal flair and string timbre. I’ve been enjoying listening to the soundtrack to *Tenzing* and the Xiaomi Clip are some of the only open earbuds which could do justice to the music.

Of course, there’s also the bottom of the V-shaped valley, and it’s the mids. These aren’t as prominent in the mix, with songs often losing their accompanying rhythmic instruments. Hey, I’d be more critical of this in standard earbuds, where you want to hear the totality of a song — in open-ears, which are more often for people working out or in busy areas, I can be more forgiving.

The Xiaomi Clip don’t let you play via LDAC; instead, they’re stuck to AAC, SBC or LHDC. The latter's quite rare in earbuds; it's similar to LDAC but works offers lower latencies and doesn't have as high a bitrate. It's close, but not quite LDAC.

Like the OpenWear Stereo Pro, they have a neat noise leak reduction feature, which stops people around you from hearing your music as loud, solving a common open earbuds problem.

* **Sound quality: 4.5 / 5**

## Xiaomi Clip review: Value

* **Priced as mid-range clip earbuds**
* **Some cheaper options offer better value...**
* **... but it's still good here**

I'm not going to pretend that the Xiaomi Clip are the best-value open earbuds on the market. I've reviewed models that cost a fair bit less, which were also worth buying.

However they're still great value when you bear in mind that they're basically premium open-ears, at a middle-of-the-road cost. They're a worthy alternative to the Ultras of the market, but without the high price.

* **Value: 4 / 5**

## How I tested the Xiaomi Clip

![The Xiaomi Clip on the arm of a sofa.](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/nfjRDWqTMF7UujNgnyuyo5-1200-80.jpg)

* **Tested for three weeks**
* **Paired with Samsung phone**
* **Used on runs, cycles and at home**

I used the Xiaomi Clip for three weeks before writing this review, and they were connected to my Samsung Galaxy Z Fold 8 Ultra throughout the testing process.

To test the Clip I did some listening at home, but most of the use was on runs or while cycling. I'd estimate I used them for about an hour a day during the three weeks. I mostly listened to music via Spotify or my library of lossless music, but also played some games and watched videos too.

I've been reviewing audio gadgets for years, including open earbuds from the first few models, and I've also been testing Xiaomi phones and earbuds for years too. Several of the Xiaomi Clip's contemporaries were also reviewed by me.
