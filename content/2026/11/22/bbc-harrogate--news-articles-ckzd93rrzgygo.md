The first Great Ouseburn pantomime in 30 years played to three sell-out audiences

**Amateur dramatics performers said they were "overwhelmed" to play to sell-out crowds as they brought panto back to a village for the first time in 30 years.**

Newly formed group The Ouseburn Players (TOPS) performed The Very Hairy Tale of Ouseburn's Rapunzel on Friday and Saturday at Great Ouseburn Village Hall in North Yorkshire.

An annual pantomime was staged in the village by The Ouseburn Follies, until declining interest put paid to the play in 1996.

Lynsey Farnworth and Louise Farmer, who had previously been involved in amateur dramatics in the nearby village of Whixley, began working on the Great Ouseburn pantomime in November last year.

"It's about more than panto - it's about community and people having fun," said Lynsey, who wrote and directed the production.

"I don't think the audience knew what they were expecting and they've come and they just love it. We're more than pleased."

Assistant director and cast member Louise said the pair were "a little bit overwhelmed", having "done so much work for it".

The story - loosely based on the fairytale of Rapunzel - featured a princess being kidnapped and locked in a tower.

The evil mastermind responsible hoped Rapunzel's parents, the King and Queen, would sign a ransom agreement giving him the power to cut down local trees and build new housing developments.

As well as a prince looking for her in the "spooky" Bluebell Wood (a woodland local to Great Ouseburn), a window cleaner tracked her down, partly with assistance from a witch and wild animals.

![Two women stand side by side embracing each other while smiling at the camera. One is wearing dungarees with large brightly coloured flowers. She also has a microphone around her neck.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/8340/live/42cf7570-e615-11f1-b5b6-358e38ce4b5a.jpg)

Louise and Lynsey co-directed the show after founding The Ouseburn Players

Despite writing a "skeleton script" before the 16-strong cast was selected, Lynsey, 44, said some characters were determined by the actors playing them.

An actor with limited speech was given a non-speaking part, while an actor living with dementia was able to read their lines from a script.

"We don't get anyone to do anything they don't want to do - we play it to their strengths," Louise, 52, explained.

She added: "We're not professionals, we go up there to have a laugh and a giggle and watch the audience laughing along with us.

"That's the most important thing about it, that we've brought the whole village together for three shows."

## 'Support community theatre'

The Pannal Players - based in the village south of Harrogate - recently announced their own panto in February would be their last.

Louise said: "[The audience] will have more fun here than what they'll have at a professional theatre and I wish more people would come out and support community theatre because then they wouldn't be closing down."

Two members of the cast in Great Ouseburn had previously been in panto in Pannal.

Kirsty Henry said the new production "just proves you can start again and the community wants it".

"I can't believe how many people in the village have got involved. It's brought so many people together," the 55-year-old added.

Zak Wild, 20, said he was "very sad" about the Pannal productions coming to an end.

He said he and Kirsty had been warmly welcomed into the Great Ouseburn group and that his favourite part of the production was the "very good laughter" from young audience members during the Saturday afternoon matinee.

Zak and Kirsty's former amateur dramatics group is staging its last panto this winter

Karen Coles, who played the Queen and witch, said she had not done "very much" performing before this show.

"You might do it at school, might test again at some point along the line, and then life happens."

She said the village panto cast was a "really lovely group of people and we're all learning as we go".

Former teacher Caroline Smale, who played parts including a singing hairdresser and forest gnome, said the cast and villagers were "all in this together".

"Loads of people have come. When you're walking your dog, you meet people and say 'are you coming to the panto?' and they say yes."

![A woman and her adult son are standing in a foyer and smiling at the camera. A poster with the words "Hairy Ouseburn Rapunzel" is behind them on the wall.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/a034/live/2d0c3c60-e61e-11f1-94c8-1db910b5b42e.jpg)

Marilyn and Joshua are involved in the Whixley panto and came to see the Great Ouseburn production

Members of the audience on Saturday evening praised the performers.

Marylin Hill, 62, watched the show with her son Joshua and said it was "actually great".

"We are doing the pantomime in Whixley and this is totally different," she said.

Joshua, 23, added: "I loved how personal it was. The cast had a lot of humour and were allowed to react with the audience.

"Everyone knowing each other adds that interpersonal aspect where you interact, you have fun, you laugh and that builds your relationships more for the next year."

Another pantomime for Great Ouseburn is already in the works for 2027, based on Treasure Island, although Lynsey said it had not yet been written.

![A line of people of three generations, smiling at the camera.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/5c2f/live/9c193b70-e61f-11f1-94c8-1db910b5b42e.jpg)

Terry, Janet, Megan, Michelle and Sam were supporting a relative who was helping backstage

Three generations of one family were in the audience to support a relative volunteering with the backstage crew.

"From start to finish, it was just fantastic," said Terry, 42.

"It was nice to be part of a brand new thing that's never been here before."

Michelle, 37, said she was "always laughing", while 13-year-old Sam thought it was "really good".

His sister Megan, 10, said she loved to see "the kind spirits everybody has and how passionate everybody is about doing it".

Janet, 67, added: "It's bringing the village together again."
