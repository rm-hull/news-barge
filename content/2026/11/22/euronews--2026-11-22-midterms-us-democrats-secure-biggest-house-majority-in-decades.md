## Democrats have secured their largest US House of Representatives majority in more than three decades, dealing a crushing blow to President Donald Trump and setting the stage for two years of divided government, political confrontation and ambitious legislative battles.

The Democrats have won at least 258 seats in the 435-member House, gaining 44 from Republicans in its strongest general election performance since 1992.

The latest victory came in North Carolina, where Democrat Chuck Hubbard defeated veteran Republican congresswoman Virginia Foxx. Four House races have yet to be decided, with contests in Minnesota and Tennessee still awaiting a result and two Louisiana races heading to runoffs in December.

The result surpasses the Democrats' gains in the 2018 midterm elections, when they picked up 40 seats during Trump's first presidency. Republicans are now set to hold no more than 177 seats, leaving them with one of their smallest House delegations in decades.

The Democratic wave extended across traditional Republican strongholds, the Midwest and the East Coast, reflecting widespread voter frustration over the cost of living, the war in Iran and allegations of corruption surrounding the Trump administration.

Democrats will also control the Senate, where they have secured 56 seats, giving the party its strongest position in Congress in more than a decade.

## An ambitious agenda — and high expectations

With control of both chambers from January, Democrats are preparing an ambitious first 100 days focused on household finances, healthcare and government accountability.

Their priorities include reversing Republican cuts to Medicaid and restoring Affordable Care Act tax credits that expired under Republican control. The party also intends to use congressional investigative powers to scrutinise Trump's administration, including the financial interests of the president and his family.

Hakeem Jeffries, the Democratic leader, is expected to become the first Black Speaker of the House when the new Congress convenes on 3 January 2027.

Unlike outgoing Republican Speaker Mike Johnson, who struggled to manage a razor-thin majority, Jeffries would have considerable room to accommodate disagreements between progressive and moderate Democrats.

Nevertheless, the scale of the victory has raised expectations that the party will produce tangible results.

“Like, hell yeah, we should feel pressure,” Democratic congressman Jason Crow of Colorado told the Associated Press, pointing to Americans struggling with healthcare costs, economic insecurity and immigration enforcement.

Progressive congresswoman Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez described the election result as “a mandate for change”, while centrist Democrat Tom Suozzi argued that the party should devote most of its attention to affordability rather than investigations.

## Confrontation with the White House

The Democratic takeover threatens to significantly constrain Trump during the final two years of his presidency.

Congressional committees are expected to launch investigations into the administration, potentially issuing subpoenas and demanding testimony from senior officials. Jeffries has also declined to rule out a third impeachment attempt against Trump.

The White House has responded aggressively, suggesting that some proposed investigations could amount to criminal misconduct — a claim that has intensified tensions over Congress's constitutional oversight responsibilities.

Republicans, meanwhile, face a difficult period of rebuilding. Jim Jordan of Ohio, chosen to lead the party's diminished House minority, has pledged to fight for American families and regain political ground ahead of the 2028 elections.

For Democrats, however, the immediate challenge is turning an overwhelming electoral victory into effective government. Their substantial congressional majorities offer an unusual opportunity to advance legislation, but Trump's continued control of the White House means major bills could still face a presidential veto.

Fact is: The midterm elections have fundamentally shifted the balance of power in Washington.
