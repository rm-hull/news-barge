## York RLFC asked to 'put record straight' over famous New Zealand win

Images from the Yorkshire Evening Press match report show Colin Forsyth going over for the game's first try *(Image: Newsquest)*

York RLFC are being asked to “put the record straight” by a member of the men's team which triumphed over the might of a formidable international touring side.

Ken Hunter was a member of the winning side which defeated New Zealand by a score of 11 - 5 at the Clarence Street ground on October 31, 1971.

The team’s achievement was made all the more remarkable as the home side was half-filled with 11th-hour call-ups from the local semi-professional ranks, all too late for the matchday programme to be changed.

![The 1971 touring New Zealand men's rugby league team](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21741399.jpg?type=mds-article-620)"This touring side did not come around the world in order to lose games" - New Zealand had already wrapped up the series win against Great Britain before heading to Clarence Street *(Image: Contributed)*

A "heritage wall" in the Knights’ club shop at the LNER Community Stadium in Huntington displays the printed programme and Ken has called on the club to correct its account of the night in recognition of all those who played.

The club said it has spoken to Mr Hunter, and that the heritage wall is being updated.

Ken said: “This touring side did not come around the world in order to lose matches.

“The travelling Kiwis had already secured the Test series against Great Britain in close games at Salford and Castleford and had a world class squad full of stars.

“York had won only two of their first 14 league matches and stood 28th in Northern Rugby League the league with preceding defeats against Featherstone, Widnes and Wakefield in the build-up.”

![Ken Hunter](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21741400.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Ken Hunter at home today *(Image: Kevin Glenton)*

Just two days before facing New Zealand, York RLFC played away at Leigh and lost 10-4.

Ken said coach Tommy “Bomber” Harris and his assistant coach (and Ken's brother) Geoff Hunter had their work cut out to select a team after what he described as a mixture of injuries picked up in the game on the Friday.

He said: “Six changes were made to a side that included 10 very proud players born and bred in York who regarded the opportunity as the biggest experience of their lives.

“All 15 played their hearts out in the face of brutal combat with a truly exceptional performance.

“With tries from Colin Forsyth, Jim Neale and Clive Hill, and a Ron Hill conversion, allied to some incredible defensive sets the match was won against all odds.

“There are many unsung heroes, past and present, who will be remembered but too many forgotten.”

In his match report of November 1, 1971 Yorkshire Evening Press reporter Mike Hope said York “never looked like being on the wrong end of the result” and “the Kiwis took a battering” in a game played out in front of 2,868 fans.

![The Yorkshire Evening Press November 1, 1971](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21741389.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The Yorkshire Evening Press reported the extent of a one-sided win the following day (Monday, November 1, 1971) *(Image: Newsquest)*

Ken said the names of Frank Dunham, Colin Forsyth, Richard Horner, Keith Jackson and Gary Johnson, along with his own, are not on display at the shop.

A spokesperson for York RLFC said: "The heritage wall in our club shop is a timeline of major events and matches in our history.

"It displays photographs, documents, and copies of match programmes going back more than 100 years.

"The match against New Zealand referred to by Mr Hunter is represented on the wall by a copy of the match programme from that night.

"As is pointed out, it does not name the actual team that played that night, but it does give some context to the match.

"Printed match programmes rarely have the correct team line-ups because of the timescales involved in printing and publishing ahead of game day.

"There are many big occasions in our history represented by memorabilia on the wall in a similar way.

"Our heritage wall is currently being updated and we have spoken to Mr Hunter about this.

"He has provided more memorabilia from this game, and we are happy to include it in our display."

Ken Hunter was a director of the club in the 1990s, and with his brother Geoff, were the first to have a box in the Clarence Street ground, and via their garage and car sales and repair business were sponsors of York RLFC.
