Gerwyn Price has won the Grand Slam of Darts four times

**Gerwyn Price ended his four-year wait for a major PDC title as he powered to a brilliant 16-11 victory over Gian van Veen in the Grand Slam of Darts final.**

The Welshman last won a major title when he claimed the World Series in 2022, but finally got over the line after defeats in three finals in 2026.

The 41-year-old was the runner-up at the World Matchplay, World Grand Prix and World Series, as well as suffering semi-final exits in the Premier League, World Masters, UK Open and European Championships.

This title has been long overdue and Price won it in spectacular fashion with a mid-match burst.

Despite six breaks of throw in the opening 14 legs - including brilliant 150 and 145 checkouts from Price - the game was level at 7-7.

But Price claimed a crucial lead in a scrappy 15th leg when he finally took out double two at the sixth attempt.

That flicked a switch after a mini-interval and Price won the next five legs to open up a six-leg advantage.

Van Veen broke back after another interval, but Price was able to hold off any hopes of a comeback from the Dutchman and seal his fourth Grand Slam title.

"It's amazing - I've had some close calls this year when I probably should have won games and tournaments, but when it finally comes around it's nice," Price, who moves to world number three, told Sky Sports.

"I was probably more nervous in this game than ever. Just to keep the media off my back, I mean I do mind and I don't mind, but just to keep them off my back.

"Now they can leave me alone!"

The triumph, which sees Price earn £200,000 and the Eric Bristow Trophy, is his first in a PDC ranking and TV major since he won this event in 2021.

The pair will now switch their focus to the Players Championship Finals from Friday, where they could meet in the second round.

That event will determine Price's place in the PDC order of merit and his seeding for the World Championships which begin on 11 December. The draw takes place on 30 November.

## What does this mean for the World Championship?

Across the 19 editions of the Grand Slam, seven of the winners have gone on to triumph at the World Championship in the weeks that follow.

It has happened in each of the past four years, with Luke Littler (twice), Luke Humphries and Michael Smith doubling up.

The past 10 years have delivered five winners of both in the same season, while another two Grand Slam winners have reached the Worlds semi-finals and one the quarter-finals.

Therefore a Grand Slam winner usually has a pretty decent run at the Worlds, although with Van Veen as second seed and Price either third or fourth, that would hardly be a huge surprise.

Of the 19 World Championship winners since the Grand Slam's inception, 11 have reached at least the semi-finals in Wolverhampton - or Coventry, which staged the 2020 event (seven winners, two runners-ups and two semi-finalists).

The good news for Littler and Humphries, who were both beaten in the last 16 this week, is that five winners of the Worlds have also been knocked out at the same stage of the Slam.
