A YORK primary school has been praised for its "inclusive community" and "dedicated pastoral support" in a recent Ofsted report.

Lakeside Primary Academy, part of the Ebor Academy Trust, has said that it is 'completely focused on where we want to go next' after a recent Ofsted inspection.

The school - which was inspected in October 2026 - was found to have met safeguarding standards and achieved the standard across key areas including early years provision, inclusion, leadership and governance, and personal development and wellbeing.

Inspectors found that "pupils at Lakeside Primary Academy typically enjoy school" and "most pupils behave well."

The report - published on Wednesday (November 18) - stated: "They treat one another kindly and play sensibly together on the playground. Pupils are safe in school and know that staff are there to help them if they have any concerns.

"Pupils respond positively to staff and follow instructions as they move sensibly around the school."

They added that most pupils demonstrate the school’s expectations for behaviour - listening to their teachers, responding to questions and completing work, and that bullying is rare.

Inspectors said that staff respond promptly when pupils raise any concerns and praised the school’s pastoral care, noting that leaders and staff work tirelessly to understand pupils’ challenges and provide them with timely support.

It also said: "Pupils generally speak positively about school and value the opportunities available to them to participate in wider experiences.

"For example, older pupils enjoy working together and developing their independence during overnight residential visits.

"Pupils also take part in a range of extra-curricular activities, including girls’ football and other sporting opportunities throughout the year."

Inspectors found that the early years staff have a sound understanding of how young children learn, providing well-matched, purposeful activities that prepare children effectively for their next steps.

The report also commended the school’s inclusive approach, noting improvements in how staff identify and support pupils with special education needs and/or disabilities (SEND) to manage their emotions and engage successfully in school life.

Under new headteacher Jo Evans, appointed in September 2026, leaders and trustees were praised for taking a methodical approach to improvement while actively supporting staff workload and professional development.

To help the school build on these solid foundations, inspectors outlined a few clear next steps: working closely with families to boost attendance, fine-tuning classroom feedback to catch learning gaps early, and making sure disadvantaged pupils get the targeted support they need to achieve their best by Year 6.

Jo Evans, headteacher at Lakeside Primary Academy, said: "We are pleased that the inspectors saw what makes Lakeside such a special place to learn.

"Our team works hard every day to create a safe, warm, and happy school where children feel listened to and supported. We are completely focused on where we want to go next.

"With our new leadership team in place, we’re already working on refining our curriculum checks and attendance strategies so that every pupil is supported to thrive and given the knowledge they need to excel."
