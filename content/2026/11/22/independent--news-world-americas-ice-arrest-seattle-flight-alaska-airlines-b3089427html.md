The Washington state governor criticized federal immigration officials on Saturday following the arrest of two individuals on an airport jetway near Seattle.

Immigration and Customs Enforcement carried out the operation around 8 a.m. local time at Seattle-Tacoma International Airport as passengers boarded an Alaska Airlines flight to Los Angeles.

In a statement, ICE said officers acted to detain Ricardo Larrondo Mendoza because he had overstayed his visa.

The officers also arrested a second individual, Sofia Hernandez Mejia, who threw hot coffee at them, ICE said in a post on social media.

Mejia had also overstayed her visa, ICE said, adding that she would be charged with assaulting a federal officer.

Reuters was not able to identify lawyers for Mendoza or Mejia.

![Protesters quickly gather at Sea-Tac airport](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/22/15/2026-11-22T055627Z_30247294_RC2SSOAZBBNS_RTRMADP_3_USA-IMMIGRATION-SEATTLE.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

"ICE is completely and totally out of control and today we are confronted with yet more evidence of this," said Democrat Gov. Bob Ferguson during a news conference.

Video of the arrests quickly circulated on social media and news outlets, which Ferguson called "horrific" and "chilling," adding that he told Alaska Airlines representatives that “they need to call on ICE to get out of our airports."

The governor said he would make the same request of every other airline that flies out of Sea-Tac airport.

Alaska Airlines said it is "concerned" by the federal action.

"Alaska Airlines was not provided advance notice of the enforcement action and we are working to understand the circumstances surrounding what occurred," the company said in a statement.

![](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/22/15/2026-11-22T053401Z_447467555_RC2SSOA630VX_RTRMADP_3_USA-IMMIGRATION-SEATTLE.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

April Sims, president of the Washington State Labor Council, AFL-CIO, called the ICE arrest a "smash-and-grab" action that injected fear and chaos during a delicate time for air travel.

"Just days after our nation experienced widespread disruptions to air travel and flights being grounded, federal agents chose to carry out this operation at one of our busiest airports, during one the busiest travel weeks of the year," Sims said in a statement sent by the union, which includes local chapters representing flight attendants and ground crew at Sea-Tac airport.

Alaska Airlines said many passengers were put on other flights shortly after the incident and the originally scheduled flight departed at 11 a.m. PST.
