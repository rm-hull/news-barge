A holiday park on the Yorkshire coast could be expanded for a major investment, worth more than £1 million, designed to boost tourism.

North Yorkshire Council has unveiled proposals to upgrade Filey Brigg Caravan and Camping Park, which attracts thousands of visitors to the seaside town each year.

Located within Filey Brigg Country Park, north of Filey town centre, the nine-acre site currently offers 145 pitches for caravans, motorhomes and campervans, along with 14 grass tent pitches and a range of facilities for visitors.




Under the plans, up to £1.6 million could be invested in the park, including the creation of six new glamping lodges, additional hardstanding pitches, improved changing facilities, upgraded Wi-Fi and enhancements to the site's electrical infrastructure.

Council bosses say the improvements would help secure the long-term future of the holiday park while increasing visitor numbers and supporting local businesses through greater footfall.

The proposals remain at an early stage and would be subject to planning permission.

North Yorkshire Council's executive member for finance and property, Cllr Peter Wilkinson, said: "Filey Brigg is a popular holiday destination for families who come to enjoy the spectacular scenery and everything the Yorkshire coast has to offer.

"The proposals being brought forward would explore opportunities to invest in and modernise the holiday park, helping to safeguard its long-term future as a valuable council-owned asset.

"If approved, the investment could enhance the facilities, broaden the accommodation on offer and improve the visitor experience, which in turn would help the local economy and support local businesses.

"The proposals form part of our wider commitment to support North Yorkshire's coastline, with significant investment in major regeneration and development projects underway in coastal towns including Scarborough and Whitby.

"We want Filey Brigg to remain a high-quality visitor destination for years to come, and I look forward to receiving more details on the proposals early next year."

The proposed programme of works would also include improvements to the site's main changing facilities and essential infrastructure upgrades.

Council officials say the changes would improve the visitor experience, reduce disruption during poor weather and help extend the site's operating season.

A business case is expected to be presented to the council's executive in 2027.
