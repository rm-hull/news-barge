**Mauricio Pochettino believes even the best team in the world could have conceded four goals atLiverpoolinManchester United's circumstances - but should the struggling Red Devils have done more in another disappointing defeat?**

The former Tottenham and Chelsea manager's first match in charge of United got off to a dreadful start as they had defender Leny Yoro sent off in the eighth minute and were 2-0 down by the 13th minute.

That left them with a mountain to climb, with Liverpool scoring twice more in the second half to secure a 4-0 win that kept United second from bottom in the table.

"Do you think that is easy?" said Pochettino of the situation that faced his new side after those woeful opening 13 minutes.

"Even the best team in the world playing like this in this situation, in our circumstance, maybe concede the four goals or maybe more. And now what we are going to say?

"We are in a situation that this is very complicated. It's difficult, yes, but I think we fight, we made the effort, and I know how they feel now in the dressing room."

## 'Do you down tools and throw the towel in?'

Mauricio Pochettino post-match interview - Liverpool v Man Utd

Pochettino certainly does have a point.

Trying to win any game when reduced to 10 men in the first half is difficult. In the entire history of the Premier League Manchester United have managed it on four occasions - each of those at Old Trafford.

Indeed, in the 509 instances of a team receiving one or more red cards in the first half of a Premier League match, they have gone on to win just 88 times.

At Anfield it gets even more difficult, with Arsenal the only team to have beaten Liverpool on their own ground after a first-half dismissal.

For ex-Manchester United captain Roy Keane, though, there are ways to lose if you have a man sent off - and a 4-0 defeat was not one of them.

Speaking before Pochettino's comments, he told Sky Sports: "Are you telling me they couldn't have done better? They lose 4-0 and everyone is saying it's not too bad.

"Has a team ever got a result before with 10 men? It can be done. At least show a bit more fight, a bit more quality. I can't believe what I'm hearing from people who have played for the club saying 4-0 is not too bad."

Keane was responding to his former team-mate Gary Neville, who was part of the commentary team for Sky Sports.

"That's where we've got to, where we're starting to write off 4-0 defeats at Anfield as though it's just the norm and what should be accepted," said Neville in the closing stages of the game. "The club's standards have dropped so far it's incredible."

The former England full-back added: "There'll be an element of, 'let's just forget about this game because Yoro got sent off, we've got 10 men and all that sort of stuff', but it rides into the game in midweek and from a defensive perspective I've never seen anything like this."

Keane did not agree with that view as he said: "Liverpool should have scored eight or nine. Unbelievable chances they had and everyone's coming off the pitch saying four is not too bad.

"I feel like swearing. I'll try not to. How can anyone say 4-0 is not too bad? Just because they went down to 10 men. That obviously made it a lot more difficult, but if you go down to 10 men is that it? Do you down tools and throw the towel in?"

## 'I've never seen anything like this' - defeat provides more defensive concern

Despite Keane's comments, a 4-0 loss at Anfield isn't unthinkable - certainly better than losing 7-0 as United did three years ago.

But in the wider context it provides more concern about a defence that is now officially the worst in Europe's top five leagues.

No other team in the Premier League, La Liga, Bundesliga, Ligue 1 or Serie A has conceded more than United's 34 goals in all competitions so far this season.

It is also the second successive game they have conceded four goals, while in their last six games in all competitions they have conceded 19.

"I don't think Mauricio Pochettino can write this off," said ex-Leeds and Spurs goalkeeper Paul Robinson on BBC Radio 5 Live.

"He has to learn from it. He will have learned about his players and their attitude.

"Liverpool had 27 shots in that game. There were mitigating circumstances early in the game... but why did we get to those circumstances?

"It was because defenders were out of position again. The long ball over the top that catches Matthijs de Ligt out, the lack of communication, the tackle Leny Yoro makes and the defenders being caught out."
