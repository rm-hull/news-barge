Michigan US senator-elect Abdul El-Sayed on Sunday doubled down on naming healthcare reform legislation after Donald Trump as long as it meant the president would not impede its passage.

“If he’s open to the conversation, I am open to it, too,” El-Sayed said of Trump on Sunday. “If the president is opening to signing that bill, let’s get it done.”

El-Sayed made those remarks on Sunday on NBC’s Meet the Press, where he reiterated his pledge to pursue a single-payer, national healthcare program after the Democrat became the first Muslim person elected to the US Senate during the midterms earlier in November.

Trump’s Republican party is going to lose control of Congress for the second half of his second presidency. But the president has made clear his intention to wield his legislative veto powers whenever he sees fit.

Appearing on Sunday’s Meet the Press, El-Sayed said he would not mind Medicare for All – a big part of his campaign platform – being named “TrumpCare” as long as such a program came into existence.

“I care that the people of this country finally have the unmet promise of guaranteed healthcare in this country. And if the president is serious about leaving a lasting legacy, there is nothing better than the ultimate Trump card,” El-Sayed said to Meet the Press host Kristen Welker, making a double entendre for a term that – when the T is lowercase – also refers to a card that beats out others in card games.

El-Sayed told Welker that such a Trump card would be in effect “from the moment [people] are born to the moment that they pass away – that guarantees them the healthcare that has been so elusive in this country without premiums, co-pays and deductibles”.

The senator-elect’s comments come after he previously suggested to CNN that he is not above stroking Trump’s prodigious ego to get legislation passed – including by naming Medicare for All “TrumpCare”.

He also previously wrote on social media, alongside images of a gold health insurance card with “TrumpCare” inscribed on it: “Donald, this is on the table. Guarantee healthcare for every American, from cradle to grave.”

Trump seemingly responded to El-Sayed’s post by reposting a screenshot of the senator-elect’s tweet. Trump has not formally responded to the push to create universal healthcare.

Trump has spent much of his second presidency trying to get as much as possible named after him – from institutions, physical buildings and currency.

El-Sayed also said that he supports healthcare reform proposed by Bernie Sanders, the progressive US senator.

Sanders’ proposal would create a single-payer system in which the federal government insures everybody, financed by an increase in taxes, while eliminating private insurance.

But El-Sayed said he is not focused on eliminating private insurance as an option, which Sanders aims to eliminate by having everything primary and preventive services to hospital stays covered under his proposed plan. Out-of-pocket costs on deductibles and co-pays – staples of the rapacious private insurance industry to which most Americans must resort – would also be eliminated under Sanders’ plan.

El-Sayed said he wants to “provide you basic public health insurance no matter who you are in this country” while being open to the idea of not taking alternative options away.

The senator-elect said he has a meeting on the books with JD Vance. El-Sayed would not share the agenda of the meeting, but he confirmed that the vice-president’s chief of staff contacted him after his election victory.
