In the first regular-season week of college football in three years with no AP Top 25-ranked teams pitted against each other, there has been no shortage of excitement.

And much of that came from the Big Ten, where the top-two ranked teams — No. 4 UCLA and No. 6 Indiana — both lost in overtime.

Michigan handed UCLA its first loss of the season — an 18-16 triple-overtime defeat — after quarterback Ty Dieffenbach was carted off the field with an injury in overtime. Madden Iamaleava struggled under difficult circumstances, failing to complete a pass and losing a yard on two carries.

In Seattle, Indiana came up short when a game-tying 37-yard field goal attempt was blocked by Washington's Drew Azzopardi, carrying the Huskies to a 27-24 overtime win. Grant Wilson started the game at quarterback for the Hoosiers and threw a 74-yard touchdown pass on his first snap, before Curt Cignetti turned to Josh Hoover in the third quarter.

There was more. Former front-runner Ohio State fell 31-28 to Nebraska on the road and dropped to 7-4 just days after surging six spots to No. 14 in the latest CFP rankings. The Buckeyes had been clinging to a glimmer of hope with three losses, but a fourth slams that door shut.

It was Nebraska’s first Top 25 win in more than 10 years, ending a 31-game losing streak against ranked opponents.

And Penn State, playing with only pride on the line, fell to Rutgers 25-24 after giving up a touchdown and subsequent 2-point conversion in the final seconds.

Overtime wasn’t all bad news

BYU may not feel like a winner after narrowly dodging a playoff-shattering loss to Kansas, but it was the only ranked team to escape overtime with a success story on Saturday afternoon.

The Cougars had nearly fumbled away their playoff chances, but they won the turnover battle when it mattered most. BYU turned a forced fumble into a two-point conversion to improve to 9-2 with a 31-29 triple-overtime win and stay in the thick of the playoff race.

Elsewhere in the Big 12, Houston narrowly beat West Virginia, and Texas Tech, the first team out in the most recent playoff picture, outscored Baylor 20-10.

Over in the SEC

No. 9 Tennessee stayed alive in the playoff race with a 27-13 win against LSU — an effort that required both quarterbacks.

Tennessee quarterbacks Faizon Brandon and George MacIntyre split time in the win. MacIntyre completed 12 of 21 passes for 79 yards and a touchdown. Brandon had the more unusual stat line, completing 1 of 6 passes for six yards, an interception and a lost fumble, while carrying the ball 9 times for 90 yards and two scores.

Texas and Alabama both put up more than 40 points against Week 12 opponents. The Longhorns routed Arkansas 45-3, and Alabama beat Chattanooga 47-0 under a strong performance from backup quarterback Austin Mack.
