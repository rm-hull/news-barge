More than 100 million parcels are expected to be sent and collected through Post Office branches over the festive period, the company has said.

It expects the two-month Christmas rush, which runs from early November into January, to produce that total.

Monday December 7 is predicted to be the peak posting day of the season.

The Post Office is encouraging customers whose parcels are ready to post them early wherever possible, particularly when sending Christmas gifts overseas.

It said it is reminding customers they do not need to wait until the busiest period.

Demand at this time of year is being driven by online Christmas shopping, second-hand marketplaces and post-Christmas returns.

The United States is the most popular overseas destination for Christmas parcels sent through the Post Office.

Australia, Ireland, Germany and Poland follow the US in the rankings.

Shanaz Nandha, postmaster at Biggleswade Post Office, said: “Christmas is always a busy time for us in branch, with lots of customers sending presents to friends and family across the UK and overseas.”

The Post Office suggested that people check last posting dates, particularly if they are sending items overseas.

Information about last posting dates and customs forms is available on the Post Office’s website.

Meanwhile, Royal Mail has revealed a worsening service performance against letter delivery targets despite turnaround efforts that will see it scrap second-class post on Saturdays.

The group – whose owner International Distribution Services (IDS) was bought last year by Czech billionaire Daniel Kretinsky – said it delivered 82.5% of first-class deliveries the next day and 87.2% of second-class mail within three working days during the second quarter of the year.

This is down from 85% for first-class and 91.4% of second-class delivered on time in the first quarter of the year.

Under targets set by regulator Ofcom, 90% of first-class mail should be delivered the next day and 95% of second-class mail should be delivered within three days, having been lowered from April as part of universal service reforms.

Royal Mail insisted it was making progress as it overhauls its operations and was “broadly on track” with reforms, including the move to deliver second-class every other day and ditch *post* on Saturdays, but said the turnaround would take time.

Last month it also announced up to 2,500 head *office* and support function jobs would go by the end of 2027 to help cut costs.
