Tommy Robinson has been reported to the Gambling Commission over concerns he may have broken the law by advertising an unlicensed crypto betting platform, *The Independent* can reveal.

The Good Law Project has urged the Gambling Commission to investigate potential breaches of gambling regulation by the far-right activist, real name Stephen Yaxley-Lennon, and offshore crypto gambling platform BetBolt.

*The Independent* found Mr Yaxley-Lennon shared a number of posts in connection with BetBolt between June and September, including while trying to raise money for far-right activist Danny Tommo.

The Good Law Project raised concerns that BetBolt may have breached UK laws by offering facilities to gamble in Britain without a licence and advertising to a British audience.

They also highlighted that Mr Yaxley-Lennon advertised an unlicensed gambling platform on his social media and appeared in BetBolt’s promotional videos, which they say could have encouraged use of the unlicensed gambling facilities by a UK audience.

![Stephen Yaxley-Lennon first shared posts about BetBolt in June around the World Cup](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/04/11/25/Screenshot-2026-11-04-at-11-48-43.png?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

BetBolt is not on the Gambling Commission’s register, which makes it an unlicensed platform in Britain. It’s licensed and regulated by the government of the Autonomous Island of Anjouan, Union of Comoros, which sits between Madagascar and Mozambique in the Indian Ocean.

A Gambling Commission spokesperson told *The Independent* it does not comment on individual cases.

But they added: “It is an offence under section 33 of the Gambling Act 2005 to provide facilities for gambling in Great Britain without a Gambling Commission licence.

“Anyone who engages in advertising arrangements with unlicensed gambling operators is at risk of committing the offence of advertising unlawful gambling under section 330 of the Gambling Act 2005.”

Charities have warned *The Independent* that Britons are still able to access unlicensed gambling websites, such as BetBolt, which are often unaffected by self-exclusion tools that help those affected by gambling-related harms, using VPNs.

Mr Yaxley-Lennon first posted about BetBolt on 23 June regarding a bet he made using the platform on the World Cup game between England and Ghana, and he encouraged followers to share their predicted score.

He said that, if his bet was a success, BetBolt would pick five people who got the exact score right and give each of them $1,000. He added that the platform had the “highest odds around” for England and linked a code below for followers to use.

![Stephen Yaxley-Lennon reshared a video of himself appearing as a Blackjack dealer on the platform](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/04/11/40/Screenshot-2026-11-04-at-11-49-54.png?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

He would go on to share a number of posts with BetBolt on 28 September when he went on the platform’s Blackjack Live game as a dealer, pledging to donate all the money to the family of Patriot Platform leader Daniel Thomas, better known as Danny Tommo, and “his fight for freedom”.

The week beforehand, Thomas was charged with criminal damage after allegedly slashing a dinghy in the English Channel. He has now pleaded guilty to that offence.

BetBolt shared promotional posts ahead of Mr Yaxley-Lennon’s appearance, including an AI-generated video showing the activist at a rally with Union Jacks and St George flags. They later shared a video of him arriving at the Blackjack Live table as “Rule Britannia” played in the background.

In a letter seen by *The Independent,* the Good Law Project argued the posts and Mr Yaxley-Lennon’s appearance as a guest blackjack dealer “were intended to encourage a GB audience to take advantage of, and to increase use of, BetBolt’s website for gambling”.

Analysis by the Centre for Countering Digital Harm, shared with *The Independent,* shows the posts reached a largely British audience. Researchers captured 643 replies to Robinson’s posts and found that, where users disclosed their location, 54 per cent came from UK-based users.

![BetBolt shared an AI-generated video of Yaxley-Lennon ahead of his appearance on its Blackjack Live game](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/04/11/40/Screenshot-2026-11-04-at-11-51-01.png?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

This is not the first time BetBolt has made headlines in the UK for connections with British influencers.

The platform paid $1.2m to name British OnlyFans star Bonnie Blue’s child after itself. When asked by *Metro*why it had decided to align with the OnlyFans star, a spokesperson said: “She quite literally f\*\*\* s thousands of men physically and we f\*\*\* thousands of men financially.”

The platform’s terms of service state, “individuals residing in countries where gambling is illegal or restricted or where special licensing is required are strictly not permitted to create an account on BetBolt.com or use any services offered by BetBolt.com and take upon themselves full responsibility”.

Despite being licensed out of Anjouan, BetBolt does not accept players from the country that regulates it, nor does it accept players from Malta, Curacao, or Costa Rica.

![Videos to advertise Yaxley-Lennon’s appearance on the platform showed Union Jacks](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/04/11/40/Screenshot-2026-11-04-at-11-52-01.png?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The terms of service also show that “while VPN use is not prohibited, be mindful that it may cause issues with game performance or connectivity”.

The Good Law Project argued BetBolt was not taking “proper steps” to prevent people based in Britain from gambling on its site. It claimed that not only could it create an account with the gambling site, but it could also deposit cryptocurrency into that account from the UK.

As part of a test by the non-profit campaigning group, it found BetBolt did not ask for, or verify, its address or jurisdiction in the process of setting up an account, despite signing up with an email address that indicated a UK domain (.co.uk). BetBolt did not ask for or verify its age either.

Charities have expressed concerns that Britons struggling with gambling-related harm could access these unlicensed websites through VPNs.

![Yaxley-Lennon repeatedly shared videos promoting his appearance on BetBolt’s Blackjack Live game](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/04/11/51/Screenshot-2026-11-04-at-11-53-15.png?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

A spokesperson from GamCare, Britain’s national gambling helpline, said: “While blocking software can help reduce access, some unlicensed sites may still be accessible via methods such as Virtual Private Networks.

“This can make it harder for people in recovery from gambling-related harms, who have already taken steps to address their gambling but are often not protected from these sites.”

Nick Harvey from the Coalition to End Gambling Ads said: “It’s deeply irresponsible for high-profile public figures to promote any online gambling, given the serious risks of harm and addiction.”

*The Independent* found BetBolt’s terms of service had signposted GamCare in a section dedicated to responsible gambling.

![BetBolt promised that all dealer tips raised by Yaxley Lennon would go to Danny Tommo](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/04/14/12/Screenshot-2026-11-04-at-14-11-03.png?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

GamCare is only able to support people in Britain and is not set up as an official charity anywhere else in the world.

A spokesperson said: “GamCare is independent and has no affiliation with websites or operators.

“We urge anyone struggling with gambling to contact GamCare’s National Gambling Helpline on 0808 8020 133 for free, confidential support, 24/7, 365 days a year.”

Neither BetBolt or representatives for Tommy Robinson responded to *The Independent’s* request for comment. Robinson’s representatives told Good Law Project that he “is currently uncontactable”.
