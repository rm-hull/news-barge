Kemi Badenoch will accuse Andy Burnham of securing a “terrible” deal with the European Union and say he should be “aiming a lot higher” in terms of driving growth, as Nigel Farage warns that closer ties with Brussels would be a “disaster” for British businesses.

The Conservative and Reform UK leaders will both use speeches at the Confederation of British Industry (CBI) annual conference on Monday to attack the Prime Minister’s efforts to reset relations with Brussels.

Mrs Badenoch will argue that Mr Burnham has set his sights too low with his deal aimed at boosting Britain’s economy by 0.4%, while Mr Farage will warn that closer alignment with the EU would hit small businesses with more red tape.

Their interventions follow Friday’s UK-EU summit, at which Mr Burnham struck deals covering food and agriculture exports, emissions trading, and a youth experience scheme.

The Labour leader now intends to discuss options for the future relationship with the bloc over the next 12 months.

In her speech, Mrs Badenoch will acknowledge the benefits of closer trading ties with Europe, but accuse the Prime Minister of weakening Britain’s negotiating position.

She is expected to say: “Believe me, as a former trade secretary, I understand why some businesses want easier trading relationships with the EU.

“Only around 8% of businesses in the UK trade with the EU, but for those that do closer trading relations would be beneficial.

“Europe is our closest neighbour.

“Despite the challenges countries like Germany and France are facing, they remain huge economies and lucrative markets for our goods and services.”

She will point to the EU’s veterinary agreement with New Zealand, under which the two sides recognise each other’s standards without relinquishing control over their laws.

“Why will the EU give New Zealand something it won’t give us?

“It’s because they knew Andy Burnham would sign Britain up to be a rule taker from the EU instead, because he has no other ideas to grow our economy.”

She will cite Mr Burnham’s remarks that Britain was “not in a position to turn its back on a deal that could add up to 0.4% to GDP.”

“Of course he got a terrible deal, because he made it clear he wouldn’t turn it down.

“It is also thinking way too small.”

The Tory leader will argue that Europe “is not the market of the future” and that Britain should focus on faster-growing economies such as India, Indonesia and the US.

“The question has always been: is it worth locking yourself into one market if it locks you out of another?

“The fact is, Britain needs to be aiming a lot higher than 0.4% growth if we’re going to get out of the economic mess we are in.”

Meanwhile, Mr Farage will accuse large businesses of supporting closer ties with Brussels because of access to cheap labour and regulations that disadvantage smaller competitors.

The Reform UK leader is expected to say: “Rejoining, or even the half-in, half-out alignment some of them want, would plunge millions of businesses into chaos when they now have certainty.

“It will hit the small firms that do not even trade with Europe with more red tape, more compliance costs, more Brussels rules they never asked for.

He will argue that British businesses have benefited from diverging from EU rules in areas including agricultural technology, financial services and artificial intelligence.

“Divergence is our strength. Going backwards into the EU’s orbit would be a disaster for the very British businesses that are trying to compete and innovate. “
