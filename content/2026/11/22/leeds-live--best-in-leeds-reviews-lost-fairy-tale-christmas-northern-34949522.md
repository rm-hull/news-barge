# Get lost in a fairy tale this Christmas at Northern Lights Leeds Temple Newsam

## The Lost Light of Christmas runs until New Year's Eve

Step into a magical, mystical fairy world hidden in the grounds of Temple Newsam this Christmas with its Northern Lights trail.

Set amongst the imposing, gothic-style buildings lives a land of fairies and magic come to life. The Northern Lights Leeds has taken on folklore and magic as part of its trail this year, with The Lost Light of Christmas running from now until New Year's Eve.

On Saturday, November 21, my family pulled into the car park as one of Leeds most well-known attractions loomed into view behind us. Darkness had fallen but there was still plenty to see as lights and lasers lit up the sky around us.

It was our first time visiting the Northern Lights Leeds and already surpassed all expectations as soon as we arrived.

Making our way into the Christmas village we grabbed a bag of sugared doughnuts and some hot chocolates to keep us warm as we ventured into the world of the faeries and spraggans.

The Lost Light of Christmas is inspired by folklore, and as you make your way through the mile-long trail full of lights and sounds and stories, the secrets of the fairies begin to unfold.

After seeing the light of the spraggans floating through the wind, we came across The Keeper of Christmas.

A face imposed onto a huge tree in the grounds of Temple Newsam, not so unlike the typical image of Father Christmas, he asks you to help the faeries, as they begin their quest to restore the Lost Light of Christmas before it is gone forever. To help, you must follow the illuminations and music through magical forests, meeting friends and foe as you go.

The first zone takes you to the Geyser of Eld, a stunning light show in the middle of the lake of the grounds. Purples, pinks and yellows splash into the air above the water in time to music before you make your way down a hill to a clearing of trees.

Standing high above the ground, the beautiful Whisper a Wish installation takes you to an otherworldly place. It was one of our favourite parts of the trails.

We tapped the stars dotted around the clearing and made wishes to help the faeries save the Lost Light of Christmas before making our way to the Faerie Tree.

This is a perfect place to sit and take it all in. Not only are you surrounded by stunning lighting, you can also see others making their wishes across the lake and part of the water show.

It was time for a break and what better way to take one than by toasting some marshmallows? There's plenty of other options at the Fireside Fiesta, but we thought it would be a shame to waste the open fires dotted around.

Grabbing our marshmallows on a stick, we sat for a few minutes toasting and eating and chatting as a radio presenter spoke over us, playing songs from the world we had entered telling us to clap, dance and spin.

The "Breaking news" section was a highlight as it told us exactly where our faerie friends had made it to on their mission, and with that it was time to go again.

As we made out way through the forest, through the Wish Reveal, we came across a fallen star, its light shining through the trees before we spotted faeries waving at us as we continued.

Disco balls hung from the trees as light reflected from them and music played for the Beacon of Light installation.

This was my favourite part of the trail as light bounced around, making you feel as though magic had really been cast. The dizzying dots of shining light took you to a far away world, away from Leeds and Temple Newsam, making you forget you hadn't just really helped save Christmas.

A starlit passage took us to Christmas itself, as gifts adorned the grounds of Temple Newsam in a stunning light installation that led you to what we called "The Rainbow Road." Striped lights hit the ground, changing colour now and again, as we ran through back to the Christmas village.

No Christmas attraction would be the same without a ferris wheel ride for us and as we reached the dizzying heights, we could see other families making their way through the trail. We'd done our bit and saved The Lost Light of Christmas and now it was time for home.

*The Lost Light of Christmas runs until New Year's Eve. Off-peak tickets cost £19.50 for adults and £13.50 for children. Parking is pre-booked and costs £7.95.*
