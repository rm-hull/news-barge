The incoming US House speaker, Hakeem Jeffries, has said he and his fellow Democrats are focused on affordability, expanding healthcare and fixing the “broken” immigration system in the country – and willing to cross the political aisle to do so.

Jeffries laid out that agenda in an interview on Sunday with CBS News’s Face the Nation, less than three weeks after Democrats seized control of Congress during the 3 November midterms for the latter half of Donald Trump’s second presidency.

“We need to drive down the high cost of living and deal with the affordability crisis,” the New York representative told Face the Nation host Margaret Brennan.

“We have a broken healthcare system. We need to fix it, and then make sure that we actually don’t simply return to the status quo, but we do much better than what currently exists. And we need to fix our broken immigration system as well.”

Trump’s Republican party resoundingly lost the midterms amid dissatisfaction with both high living costs worsened by the Iran war as well as his aggressive but unpopular mass deportation campaign.

Jeffries said those areas were among the ones to possibly “explore whether we can work together with the president and our colleagues on the other side of the aisle”.

He told Brennan that he envisions Democrats and Republicans working together particularly on a bipartisan healthcare plan.

Additionally, Jeffries reiterated his support for legislation banning members of Congress from trading stock, referring to it as an anti-corruption measure.

“No one is going to get a pass because there’s a cost to corruption that adversely [affects] everyday Americans,” Jeffries said. “And we should begin by dealing with our own House.”

Those remarks from Jeffries came four days after Democrats nominated him as House speaker when the new Congress is sworn in. That nomination put him in line to become the first Black lawmaker to lead the US House beginning in early January.

The midterms’ outcome have given Democrats power to conduct tough oversight of Trump for the final two years of his presidency – after his Republican party had controlled Congress since the second half of Joe Biden’s Oval Office term. But Jeffries said that Democratic lawmakers were also compelled to meet more practical everyday needs for Americans.

“Can you imagine how angry the American people will be with Washington if over the next two years, we fail to do anything about affordability, fail to do anything about healthcare, and fail to do anything about immigration?” Jeffries asked.

“We’re committed to bringing about the type of dramatic change the American people are demanding.”
