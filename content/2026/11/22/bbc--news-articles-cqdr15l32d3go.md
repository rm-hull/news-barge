'Danger seems greater all the time': Ukrainians search for missing after Russian strikes hit Kyiv

**A seven-year-old boy has been rescued from the rubble of a residential building near Kyiv 14 hours after a Russian strike, Ukraine's state emergency service in the capital region has said.**

The attack early on Sunday morning in the village of Dymer killed six people, Kyiv's regional head Tymur Tkachenko said. Six people were injured, while others are still missing.

Ukrainian authorities said the boy is in stable condition and was taken to hospital, and have thanked everyone involved in saving his life.

Zelensky also said three people were killed and 13 wounded in a separate strike on Vyshneve, also in the Kyiv region, that hit a grocery store on Sunday.

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky and Ukraine's national police also confirmed the rescue of the boy.

Response teams including police, explosive technicians, dog handlers and medics are continuing with rescue efforts.

![Emergency teams passing a stretcher through the rubble](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/624/cpsprodpb/cce0/live/b754c730-e6bd-11f1-a023-d502158f79f8.jpg)

The attack on the residential building occurred in the early hours of the morning while people were asleep

Zelensky said more than 130 people are involved in emergency operations at the scene, and that they will continue until they have "complete information about every single person".

Sending his condolences to the families and loved ones of those killed, he said the strike struck the residential building, "practically blowing a section of it to smithereens".

Zelensky added that "rescue efforts are complicated by constant new air raid alerts and renewed threats".

The strike hit the building at 05:00 local time, when families were still asleep, Ukraine's minister of internal affairs Ivan Vyhivskyi said.

Emergency workers continue to search for those missing after Russian drone and missile attacks in Dymer.

More than 70 strikes have been launched across 10 Ukrainian regions as of Sunday evening, Vyhivskyi added.

Russia launched its full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022.
