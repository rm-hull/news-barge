Thousands gathered in Croatia's capital on ​Saturday evening to protest over the death of 32-year-old Kristian Josip Peris in a psychiatric hospital, demanding accountability for those responsible and better regulation of treatments ⁠in hospitals.

Protesters filled Ban Jelacic Square on Saturday evening, holding candles and white pillows in memory of Kristian Josip Peris. Peris, who was 32, was a former athlete and coach.

He died on October 21 after being admitted to the clinic.

Images and video from the hospital's surveillance cameras were published by local media, and the footage showed staff holding a pillow over the restrained patient.

The publication of the footage sparked public outrage across Croatia.

The demonstrators demanded accountability for those responsible for his death and better regulation of treatments in hospitals.

"Kristian Josip Peris asked for ⁠help at the St. Ivan Jankomir clinic and during ​that ⁠hospitalization he passed away," the family lawyer Leon Zrnic said. "In the meantime, a surveillance video was released to the public and it was established ‌that they had... hit him, ‌strangled him, that he had broken ribs, and as a result of that illegal procedure, he lost his life."

A similar gathering took place in the coastal town of Rijeka, where media reported that about 1,000 people came together for the same reason and held a protest march.

"I watched the video (the surveillance tape) and I was in shock to see that evil," Mirna Strugar, who attended the protest in Zagreb, said. "I ​hope this will never be repeated."

Several hospital employees have been arrested by Croatian authorities as part of an investigation.

Four medical technicians are under investigation on suspicion of the aggravated murder of a vulnerable person.

Prosecutors have expanded their inquiries to other staff who were present during the incident.
