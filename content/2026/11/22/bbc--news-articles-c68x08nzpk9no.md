**Britain's next-generation fighter jet could be the last crewed combat aircraft the UK builds, the head of the RAF has said.**

Air Chief Marshal Sir Harv Smyth, said Artificial Intelligence (AI) enabled, uncrewed aircraft would "transform military aviation".

His comments refer to the Global Combat Air Programme (GCAP), a partnership between the UK, Italy and Japan to develop a sixth-generation fighter jet, expected to enter service by 2035.

The aircraft, commonly known as Tempest, is expected to provide speed stealth, use advanced sensors and even AI to assist the human pilot when they are overwhelmed, or under extreme stress.

Writing in the Telegraph, external, Sir Harv said the GCAP aircraft was "perhaps the last crewed fighter we will ever build", not because people would become less important, but because advances in technology were changing the nature of air combat.

He said AI and machines would take on "the most dangerous tasks instead of humans", while stressing that technology should enhance human judgement and "not replace accountability". He added: "Uncrewed fighter jets will start flying in UK airspace next year. This is exciting and necessary in equal measure."

Sir Harv also said the RAF of the future would combine Typhoons and F-35s with autonomous systems.

The programme was announced in 2022 by the UK, Italy and Japan, which are jointly developing the aircraft. In July, the three countries signed a £4.6bn contract covering the next phase of its design through industry joint venture Edgewing.

According to GCAP, the aircraft will be powered by advanced data processing and artificial intelligence, while also featuring enhanced stealth technology. The UK government says the project already supports 3,500 jobs and 1,000 apprenticeships.

Canada was granted "observer nation" status in July, giving it privileged access to the programme without a decision-making role.

In October, Prime Minister, Andy Burnham, said the UK government would "seek to progress" Germany joining GCAP. He said the partnership could also support cooperation on issues including "countering hybrid attacks" and "countering interference in our democratic processes".
