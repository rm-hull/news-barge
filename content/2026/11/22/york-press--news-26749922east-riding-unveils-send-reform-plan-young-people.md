A plan to improve support for children and young people with special educational needs and disabilities has been approved.

The East Riding SEND Reform Plan, backed by the Department for Education, aims to strengthen inclusive education, improve early intervention, and expand local provision.

The plan sets out five goals to be achieved by 2029, including earlier identification of needs, better-trained staff, increased specialist provision, and greater confidence in the system from families.

Merlin Joseph, chair of the SEND and Inclusion Board and executive director for children, families, and schools, said: "We welcome the Department for Education’s approval of our SEND Reform Plan, which reflects the strength of our partnership and our collective determination to improve the experiences of children, young people, and families.

"The plan has been shaped through collaboration across education, health, the local authority, parent carers, and wider partners."

Councillor Victoria Aitken, cabinet member for children, families, and education, added: "Our ambition is clear: every child and young person with SEND should have the opportunity to thrive, achieve their potential, and feel included in their local community.

"I look forward to seeing our partnership turn these ambitions into lasting improvements for families across East Riding."
