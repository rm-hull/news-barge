I Am Helen charts a relationship spiralling after a non-consensual sexual experience

*This article contains spoilers for I Am Helen*

**If you want to unwind on the sofa after a long day, I Am Helen probably isn't the show for you.**

The Channel 4 drama starring Bridgerton star Nicola Coughlan and Joe Cole from Peaky Blinders has been called a must-watch by critics - but the cast don't want viewers sitting comfortably.

"There's going to be a few awkward sofas," Cole, who plays Helen's partner Steve, says.

"There's going to be people up and down the country that will be able to relate to this in varying degrees.

"And if we can put that in their living rooms and open up a debate, open up a conversation - and maybe help people be better - then we've done our job."

## Coughlan's 'most challenging' role

I Am Helen is the latest project in director Dominic Savage's I Am... series, female-led stories focusing on themes like mental health and relationships.

The two fully-improvised episodes, devised in collaboration with Coughlan, follow a relationship spiralling after Steve tries to choke Helen during sex without consent.

"We never realised how incredibly timely it would be," Coughlan tells BBC Newsbeat.

"We filmed this over a year ago.

"It feels incredibly relevant right now, and specifically right now, because pornography that includes strangulation has just been outlawed in the UK."

Earlier this month, the government made it illegal for anyone to possess or publish porn that shows strangulation or suffocation.

That decision followed a review last year that found choking was "rife" on porn sites and had become normalised, particularly among younger people.

Coughlan and Cole say they were keen to make everything about their characters and the setting as relatable as possible to emphasise the shocking fallout.

"We needed one inciting incident that felt like something that could really plausibly happen between a couple, that it just goes slightly too far without consent, and it just blows everything up," Coughlan says.

"It's really important we see Steve's perspective in it because it makes him a normal guy.

"He's not this evil guy who's attacking Helen. He does bad things, but he's complicated. And hopefully you see them both as real people."

Bridgerton star Nicola Coughlan collaborated with director Dominic Savage on the drama

Filming the series without a script meant the actors were fully immersed in their characters, making them real not just for viewers but for themselves too.

"I'm not playing myself, but there's far less, let's say 'character' here than anything else I've ever done because a lot of this is about just reacting," Coughlan says.

She and Cole would film long takes, some lasting about an hour and often with heated exchanges, which might need to be shot multiple times.

Coughlan describes I Am Helen as "the most challenging thing in terms of material that I've ever worked on", adding for the first time in a role she needed counselling.

"You literally would be physically and mentally quite exhausted," she says.

"There were times that I said to Dominic [Savage] if he wanted to do a third take for example - and I've never said this before on set - but I actually don't think I have that in me because you hit a wall.

"It's a lot to keep going."

Cole agrees that "it was tough".

"When you're given that freedom, things happen and I think you can get some really interesting and also quite shocking stuff," he says.

For him, that came when he damaged a fridge on set with a punch.

But it's that authenticity of emotion which, while difficult for the actors, is what Coughlan thinks makes the series so engaging.

"When people watch the 'I Ams', they feel quite truthful," she says.

"I think that's a big reason as to why, to be honest."

Joe Cole and Nicola Coughlan wanted the fallout to feel plausible in a normal relationship

Cole researched the manosphere online while developing his role and says he could see how people like Steve "quickly become lost in this quite simple ideology".

"The more interesting thing was the lower level, the kind of 'bro influencer' type people, who a lot of what they say is actually quite good," he says.

"You know, 'go to the gym, be healthy, look your best'.

"But occasionally they veer into comments on male and female societal roles and how a woman should stay at home."

For someone like Steve, who Cole says wants a traditional relationship and a family, those ideas left him "completely lost".

The characters quickly "get caught in a cycle", he says, as they find it harder and harder to understand each other.

"It starts off as a question, which becomes a debate, which becomes an argument, which becomes a huge bust-up, which becomes forgiveness, an apology, tears."

Coughlan says viewers have told her they expected the series to end with serious violence, perhaps even with Steve killing Helen.

But she was determined to avoid a shock factor so the characters didn't tip into being unrelatable.

"There's some very extreme moments in this, but some of them are not extreme," she says.

"It's better that he didn't [kill Helen] because as a viewer, you don't want people to go, 'well, if he does X, then that's the moment to leave'.

"From my perspective, you watch it and you go, 'you don't need to hit a threshold'.

"If you feel scared and uncomfortable, that's enough reason to get out of a situation."
