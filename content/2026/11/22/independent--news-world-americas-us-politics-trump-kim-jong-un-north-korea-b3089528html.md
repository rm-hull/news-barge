North Korean leader Kim Jong Un appears to be ghosting President Donald Trump, according to a new report.

Last year, Trump tried to contact Kim via phone and “communications channels,” but his calls went unanswered, *The Wall Street Journal* reported, citing sources familiar with the matter.

The president then tried a letter. U.S. officials handed one to a North Korean diplomat in New York City. However, the diplomat later returned it, unopened, to a U.S. representative.

The apparent rebuffs suggest Kim, a hereditary dictator Trump has called a “friend,” has little interest in speaking with the president — a contrast to Trump’s first term, when the two exchanged messages and met in person.

For Trump, the silence comes as he searches for a legacy-defining foreign policy victory following his party’s crippling midterm losses. A breakthrough with North Korea — where the long-standing sticking point has been denuclearization — could deliver one.

![North Korean leader Kim Jong Un appears to be ghosting President Donald Trump, according to a new report](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/22/22/14/GettyImages-1152948604.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

For decades, U.S. officials have tried unsuccessfully to halt or curb North Korea’s nuclear weapons program. Founded in 1948, the country conducted its first nuclear test in 2006.

North Korea now possesses an estimated 60 nuclear warheads — an increase of as much as 200 percent since 2019 — and intercontinental ballistic missiles capable of reaching the U.S. mainland.

Kim has strengthened North Korea’s ability to withstand American pressure through closer ties with Russia and China and revenue from cryptocurrency thefts. He is seeking U.S. recognition of North Korea as a nuclear power, arguing that it could pave the way for peaceful coexistence. The White House, however, said told the *Journal* it remains committed to North Korea’s denuclearization.

Trump’s latest effort to reach Kim came during his trip to China this past week, when he publicly floated a possible meeting and reportedly pressed aides to arrange one.

Ahead of the trip, U.S. officials discussed ways to lay the groundwork for a sit-down, including a statement that would stop short of formally recognizing North Korea as a nuclear power but omit the long-standing U.S. demand that it dismantle its nuclear weapons program.

In exchange, U.S. officials would seek limits on nuclear and missile testing while pursuing closer diplomatic ties.

![North Korea now possesses an estimated 60 nuclear warheads — an increase of as much as 200 percent since 2019 — and intercontinental ballistic missiles capable of reaching the U.S. mainland](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/22/22/16/GettyImages-2264251655.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The Trump-Kim meeting, however, never materialized.

A Trump administration official told the *Journal* that there had been no attempts to arrange a meeting with Kim.

Last October, Trump spoke to the difficulties of getting in contact with the North Koreans, telling reporters aboard Air Force One: “They have a lot of nuclear weapons but not a lot of telephone service.”

During Trump’s first term, the pair went from trading insults — Trump dubbed Kim “Little Rocket Man,” while Kim called Trump a “dotard”— to exchanging flattering letters that prompted Trump to declare they “fell in love.”

They met three times, with Trump becoming the first sitting U.S. president to enter North Korea in 2019, but their diplomacy yielded no major breakthroughs on denuclearization.

Earlier this month, the International Atomic Energy Agency reported that North Korea was expanding its original uranium enrichment facility, a further sign that the country is following through on its pledge to bolster its nuclear weapons program.
