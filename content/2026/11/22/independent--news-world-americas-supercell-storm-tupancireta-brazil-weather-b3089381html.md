A rare supercell storm has killed at least three people in Tupancireta, a town in Brazil's southernmost state of Rio Grande do Sul.

The state Civil Defense force said the extreme weather was caused by a supercell, a rare type of thunderstorm sustained by a long-lived rotating updraft.

A supercell, sometimes referred to as a “rotating thunderstorm”, is characterised by a heavy, constantly-rotating current of rising air.

Although the least common type of thunderstorm, they can often be the most severe.

Rain and wind gusts of 114 kilometers per hour (71 miles per hour) struck the town early on Friday, according to Brazil's National Meteorological Institute (Inmet).

The Civil Defense said at least 5,000 homes were damaged.

Gabriel Souza, the state's vice governor, said a fourth death was being investigated.

Drone footage showed widespread devastation throughout the town, with bent and buckled beams and tiles torn from a church.

"Something was spinning around, from what I could see," said shop owner Marilia Amaral Ferreira. "After I went outside, I could ​see ⁠everything. Everything was broken, ‌everything devastated. It was horrible, people were just begging for help."

Felipe Rodrigues Lima, 35, his partner and two stepchildren lost their house during the storm.

"It’s so sad," he said. "You have things, and then you end up having to start everything from scratch. It’s really ‌tough, you know?"

Animals had been left stuck at the municipal shelter since the storm hit.

In the early hours of Saturday morning, firefighters worked to bring them food and water.

"This is an ⁠atypical mission compared to what we usually do," said fireman Luciano Corje. "We ‌came here ​to rescue lives but... they aren’t ‌human lives — they’re four-legged ones."

The town was reeling on Saturday after the storm damaged thousands of buildings.
