Iran unveiled a new missile launcher at an underground facility, touting what it says is its continued capability to strike if the war with the United States accelerates once more.

Iranian state television aired footage from an underground missile facility where the paramilitary Revolutionary Guard unveiled a new launcher it said was capable of firing two liquid-fuelled missiles.

The launchers appear to be an advance over a dual launcher that Iran previously used that fired two solid-fuel missiles at a time. Liquid fuel is more efficient and gives operators some capability to adjust the missiles’ speed and trajectory.

The Fars news agency posted a video on X of IRGC commanders visiting what it described as an “underground missile city.”

![Commuters travel past a billboard depicting a launched missile and a logo that reads in Farsi: "The sky is a place for our pride and loyalty," in Valiy-Asr Square in Tehran on 17 November 2026](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/22/04/2305023871..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The location of the missile site was not revealed, but the broadcast aimed to tout Iran’s missile capabilities and counter past US claims to have destroyed the missile programme, amid fears that the war with the US could heat up once again.

During the unveiling of the launchers, Brig Gen Seyyed Majid Mousavi, commander of the Guard’s Aerospace Force, boasted that Iran’s “capabilities have expanded significantly,” adding, “We have integrated ‘surprises’ and highly effective assets into our combat structure.”

At least three oil tankers were attacked in or near the Strait of Hormuz on Saturday in what appeared to be the latest Iranian strikes on shipping through the strategic chokepoint.

The British military’s maritime security centre said in online postings that two tankers were struck separately by projectiles early Saturday while exiting the Persian Gulf through the strait.

One was hit in the stern, the other in the engine room, and there were no reports of casualties, the United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations Center reported.

There was no immediate comment from Iran on the centre’s report.

The Iranian Navy said in a statement carried in state media that a tanker hit a naval mine east of the strait before noon and caught fire. State media also said the military carried out its most “intense and varied” operations yet in the strait overnight to stop vessels, aimed at foiling the US military’s efforts to escort shipping through the narrows.

Iranian forces have been attacking shipping through the strait in a campaign aimed at hiking global energy prices to pressure the United States, which has imposed a crippling blockade on Iranian ports.
