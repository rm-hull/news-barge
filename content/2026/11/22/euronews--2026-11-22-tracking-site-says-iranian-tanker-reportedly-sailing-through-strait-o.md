## The US military said Saturday marked 130 consecutive days of its naval blockade against Iran, which it says is preventing the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps from exporting "billions of dollars in crude oil."

Amid growing speculation that Iran could attempt to break the US' naval blockade of its ports, MarineTraffic data appeared to show an Iranian tanker called "Khat-Shekan" sailing through the Strait of Hormuz.

However, the tanker-tracking site TankerTrackers has said it was a "spoof" and that the vessel does not exist.

"All of the data supplementing that AIS transmission is wrong and there's nothing on satellite imagery to corroborate those movements," TankerTrackers said in a post on social media, adding that the "Iranians know better than to attempt to ship out a VLCC right now given how many were fired upon in recent months."

The reported movement came as satellite images and ship-tracking data published in recent days showed more than 14 oil tankers had gathered around Kharg Island, a key Iranian export terminal.

Iran's foreign minister, Abbas Araghchi, said on Friday that the US' naval blockade "must be broken."

"We are making every effort to export oil and to conduct negotiations, and the blockade must be broken," he said.

The US military said Saturday marked 130 consecutive days of its blockade against Iran, which it says is preventing the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps from exporting "billions of dollars in crude oil."

US President Donald Trump has called the blockade, which focuses on restricting shipping to and from Iranian ports in the waters surrounding the Strait of Hormuz, an "impenetrable steel wall."

The Strait of Hormuz is a crucial oil and gas transit chokepoint that has been at the heart of tensions between Washington and Tehran since the Iran war broke out in late February.

The strategic waterway usually carries around 25% of the world’s seaborne oil trade.
