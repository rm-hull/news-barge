The family of a British man who died in Portugal has been told they could wait for up to nine months for answers on his death.

Julian Amaral had travelled from High Wycombe in Buckinghamshire to Portugal to attend two weddings with family. The 23-year-old is believed to have fallen overboard during a boat party on 1 September in Lisbon. His body was found five days later in the water at the Port of Lisbon.

Mr Amaral’s sister, Cassie Amaral, 29, criticised the communication of Portuguese authorities as she told *The Independent* that the family has been told they can expect to wait between six to nine months to be told the results of Mr Amaral’s autopsy.

In Portugal, post mortem details are usually not disclosed to family members of a deceased person until any inquiry into the death is closed.

“We’ve done two autopsy reports, one in Portugal, one in England. The one in Portugal, they said it would take six to nine months. I personally feel like that’s quite a long time,” she said. “We’ve literally had no communication… the communication has been really really poor from the Portuguese authorities. We’re really not happy with it.”

Ms Amaral said she has been reliving the moment she found out her brother had disappeared for the past two months.

![Ms Amaral described her brother as ‘very loving, very kind, very cheeky’](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/20/11/49/50085d2e-a04b-4aa1-8048-900a6875ecb3.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“I’m always sort of reliving that moment where my mum woke me up in the middle of the night to say that Julian was missing,” she said. “And I just remember my heart sinking and thinking like, how is he missing?

“It’s terrifying how somebody could be there and then not just at the blink of the eye.”

Mr Amaral’s funeral was well-attended, his family has said, with Ms Amaral describing her brother as “very loving, very kind, very cheeky”. She added that her brother wasn’t even sure he wanted to go to the boat party.

“It was his first ever party on a boat and he was a bit like ‘should I go? Should I not?’”

![Mr Amaral’s family have demanded to see the CCTV footage.](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/20/11/54/61cb5ca9-50fc-4764-a06f-9a9ab8abf84f.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The family have said can’t accept any assumption that he just slipped and fell, and will not rest until foul play and safety failures have been ruled out.

The family’s lawyer told *The Independent* a request had been made for the family to become assistants in the investigation process, which would make them a private party who can collaborate with the prosecutor.

Weeks ago, the lawyer submitted a set of doubts and suggestions for lines of investigation and evidence in the case. The prosecutor and police must now decide whether to act on them.

After a whistleblower from the boat company that facilitated the party told the family that Mr Amaral’s last hours were recorded on CCTV, the family have been desperately trying to access the footage from the night he disappeared to better understand what happened.

“We’re just desperately trying to get access to the CCTV footage,” Ms Amaral said. “And we’ve made it very clear if we as a family can’t view it, at least allow our lawyer to. And he hasn’t been given that opportunity either.”

She added: “Until we see that CCTV footage of Julian physically slipping and falling into the water, we're not just going to accept that.

“We just want to know what happened to him. We want to know how he was able to go to a party on a boat and end up dead.”

*The Independent* has contacted Portuguese police for comment.
