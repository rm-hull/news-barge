## Residents of Barnham back symbolic independence vote after government proposes accommodating around 930 asylum seekers at a nearby former RAF base.

A village in eastern England has voted overwhelmingly in favour of symbolically leaving the United Kingdom in protest against government plans to accommodate hundreds of asylum seekers at a nearby former military base.

Residents of Barnham, in Suffolk, voted 257 to 16 in favour of seeking a special status similar to that enjoyed by the Crown Dependencies of Jersey, Guernsey and the Isle of Man. One ballot was spoilt in Saturday’s vote, in which around 500 residents were eligible to take part.

The vote is purely symbolic and has no legal force. It does not mean Barnham can leave the United Kingdom or acquire Crown Dependency status. Any such change would require formal agreement and constitutional action by the UK authorities.

The former Royal Air Force site has been identified as a potential location for accommodating around 930 asylum seekers while their applications are processed. The government is seeking to move away from using hotels and towards accommodation at former military sites.

## **No final decision on RAF Barnham asylum plans**

The government has stressed that no final decision has been made on whether RAF Barnham will be used to accommodate asylum seekers.

It said that, should the plans go ahead, it would work closely with local authorities and Suffolk Police to ensure the safety and security of the community.

The Home Office also cited the example of Wethersfield in Essex, where male asylum seekers have been accommodated since July 2023, saying there was “no evidence of rising crime in the area”.

A Home Office spokesperson said the government was pursuing a fairer distribution of asylum accommodation across the country and had saved more than £1bn (approximately €1.19bn) in costs since the general election.

The plans include closing all asylum hotels and relocating people to alternative accommodation, including former military sites.

The spokesperson added that the proposed facilities would be self-contained and equipped with round-the-clock security and CCTV to minimise the impact on surrounding communities.

## **Residents protest over lack of consultation**

The proposal has prompted protests among residents, who say they were not adequately consulted about a development that could have a significant impact on their small community.

A similar vote took place in Piddington, Oxfordshire, in September, where residents also backed a symbolic move to leave the UK in protest against plans to accommodate more than 1,000 asylum seekers at a nearby former military base.

Piddington’s symbolic vote failed to halt the plans, and the village is now preparing a legal fight after the government granted planning permission.
