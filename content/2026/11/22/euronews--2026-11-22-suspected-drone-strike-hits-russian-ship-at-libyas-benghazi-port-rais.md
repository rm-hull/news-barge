## A suspected drone strike on a Russian ship at Libya’s Benghazi port has raised concerns over the spread of the Russia-Ukraine conflict. Authorities are investigating the blast amid growing fears for regional security.

A powerful explosion aboard a Russian vessel docked at Libya’s eastern port of Benghazi has raised concerns that the Russia-Ukraine war could be extending into North Africa, after local authorities suggested the ship may have been targeted in a drone attack.

Officials from Libya’s eastern administration said the explosion occurred on Sunday while the Russian ship was stationed at the port for refuelling and resupply before returning to Russia.

No casualties were reported, although the incident prompted an emergency response and heightened security concerns around the port.

In an official statement, the eastern authorities indicated that drones were suspected of striking the vessel. No group or government immediately claimed responsibility.

The administration also emphasised that Libya was not involved in the conflict between Russia and Ukraine, seeking to distance the country from any suggestion that its territory was being used as a battleground in the ongoing war.

## **Conflicting initial reports and investigation**

Early accounts of the explosion suggested that a fire had erupted at an ammunition storage facility within a naval installation in Benghazi. However, Libya’s eastern authorities subsequently clarified that the blast originated aboard a Russian ship docked at the port.

Local television reports described emergency crews being dispatched to the affected area to contain the fire and prevent it from spreading to nearby facilities.

Al-Masar television, citing civil defence personnel, reported that firefighters had been deployed to a military facility following the explosion. Libya Alhadath television also reported that residents were advised to avoid the surrounding area while emergency operations were underway. The fire was later brought under control.

Although Libyan authorities did not identify the vessel or disclose details of its cargo, naval analysts have provisionally identified it as the Russian missile corvette Merkury, which is believed to have sustained extensive damage in the explosion.

Libya’s eastern-based government announced plans to conduct a joint investigation with Russian officials to establish the circumstances surrounding the explosion.

## **Suspected attack comes amid expanding conflict**

The Benghazi incident comes against the backdrop of increasingly extensive Ukrainian operations targeting Russian military infrastructure, energy facilities and maritime assets beyond the conventional battlefield.

According to the supplied reports, a Russian cargo vessel was struck by a drone in the eastern Baltic Sea on Saturday, near Finnish waters. Finland’s military said Ukraine was likely responsible for the attack, although Kyiv has not claimed responsibility.

Although no evidence has been publicly presented linking Ukraine to the Benghazi explosion, the timing has drawn attention to the growing geographical reach of attacks associated with the war.

Russia has previously accused Ukraine of targeting vessels involved in transporting Russian energy exports, including ships operating far from the Black Sea.

The latest developments have raised questions about the vulnerability of commercial shipping routes, port facilities and maritime logistics networks in regions traditionally considered outside the immediate conflict zone.

## **Libya warns against becoming a battleground for foreign powers**

The suspected strike has also exposed the security challenges facing Libya, where competing political authorities continue to exercise control over different parts of the country.

The internationally recognised Government of National Unity, based in Tripoli, expressed concern about the possibility of civilian maritime infrastructure being drawn into foreign military operations.

In a statement, the Tripoli administration warned against using Libyan ports and other civilian facilities for activities connected to international conflicts.

It stressed that Libya should not become a staging ground for foreign military confrontations or logistical operations that could threaten national security and sovereignty.

The statement reflects broader concerns that Libya’s strategic Mediterranean location, combined with its prolonged political fragmentation, could expose the country to escalating geopolitical tensions.

## **Previous maritime incidents heighten concerns**

The Benghazi explosion follows other reported incidents involving Russian vessels in and around the Mediterranean.

In March, a Russian liquefied natural gas carrier was severely damaged off the Libyan coast following an attack. Russian President Vladimir Putin blamed Ukraine for the incident, although responsibility remained a matter of dispute.

The vessel was reportedly associated with Russia’s so-called shadow fleet, a network of ships used to transport Russian energy exports while navigating Western sanctions and restrictions.

Ukraine has previously acknowledged operations against vessels linked to Russian maritime trade, including an attack in the Mediterranean in December of the previous year.

Such incidents have intensified concerns over the potential consequences of maritime attacks, particularly near civilian ports and international shipping corridors.

Apart from the immediate threat to vessels and crews, explosions involving ships can pose risks to port workers, coastal communities, commercial operations and the marine environment.

## **Russia’s presence in Libya adds strategic significance**

Russia has maintained longstanding military and political relationships with influential actors in eastern Libya, including forces associated with military commander Khalifa Haftar.

Libya’s geographical position makes it strategically important for access to the Mediterranean, North Africa and the wider Sahel region.

The country has remained politically divided since the 2011 uprising that overthrew longtime leader Muammar Gaddafi. The internationally recognised administration in Tripoli, headed by Prime Minister Abdulhamid Dbeibah, continues to face a rival eastern political and military establishment supported by Haftar.

This fragmentation has complicated efforts to establish unified national security institutions and limit foreign military involvement.

## **Investigation could determine wider implications**

For now, significant questions remain unanswered, including whether the explosion was caused by a deliberate drone strike, who may have carried it out and whether the vessel was transporting military equipment.

The planned Libyan-Russian investigation may provide further information about the incident, although any conclusions will require examination of the available physical and technical evidence.

If a deliberate attack is confirmed, it would raise fresh concerns about the possibility of Russia’s war with Ukraine affecting ports and maritime facilities in North Africa.
