Award-winning journalist David McKittrick, *The Independent’*s renowned former Irish correspondent, has died aged 77.

Mr McKittrick was acclaimed for his all-encompassing coverage of Ireland’s Troubles, a period of upheaval and sectarian violence following the partition of the country in the 1920s.

As part of this commitment, he worked on a project documenting the lives of the more than 3,700 people who were killed in the decades-long conflict.

The work, which he completed along with the help of five other reporters, was recorded in his comprehensive 1,600-page book *Lost Lives* published in 1999. Chris Thornton, Brian Feeney, David McVea and Seamus Kelters co-authored the text.

In 2012, he told *Agenda Ni*of the project:*“* It was impossible to ignore the fact that so many families had been bereaved or otherwise deeply affected by the Troubles.”

![Belfast journalist and author David McKittrick with his book Lost Lives in 1999 (Paul Faith/PA)](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/21/17/8dadd3a459bb89790c041597ed3e88bdY29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzk1MzUzNDcy-2.1233340.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Born in Belfast in 1949, Mr McKittrick began his career at the *East Antrim Times*, before joining the* Irish Times* as a reporter in 1973.

Following a brief stint at the BBC, he joined *The Independent* as the paper’s founding Irish correspondent in 1986, describing it as “an offer I couldn’t refuse”.

He held the role for decades, leaving the role around the time the paper became digital-only. He continued to write occasional pieces for this publication until 2021.

During his expansive career, he twice won the Christopher Ewart-Biggs Memorial Prize for the promotion of peace and understanding in Northern Ireland and the Orwell Prize for journalism.

![File photo dated 12/12/16 of David McKittrick (centre) attending a Service of Thanksgiving for Northern Ireland journalist Austin Hunter at Second Presbyterian Church in Comber, Newtownards.](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/22/16/01M7ZP2KJMTN5WS1N611BSR3MC.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Fellow founder member David Lister, who still works at *The Independent,* said: “David McKittrick looked a bear of a man, bulky shape and bushy beard.

“But his imposing physical presence belied a gentle, humorous personality. I spent time with him when he covered one of the Irish elections in the late 1980s.

“It was striking how it seemed that everyone in Dublin knew him. His contacts extended from government to the most shadowy (and frankly dangerous) inner circles of the IRA. He not only revealed a lot about the Troubles, he explained so much. He was a formidable asset for *The Independent.*”

In 2019, his book *Lost Lives* was made into a documentary by Michael Hewitt and Diarmuid Lavery.

He said of his entry into journalism: “Twice in my life things have clicked into place naturally and perfectly for me: one was marrying my wife Pat. The other happened in the early 1970s when my main interests in life – politics, history and writing – came together.

“While the Pat chronicles are too intricate to relate here, the other themes have entwined in a career which has taken in almost the whole Troubles. It was an era simultaneously terrible and fascinating.”

SDLP leader and MP Claire Hanna described Mr McKittrick as a “calm, empathetic and rigorous” journalist who “combined exceptional integrity with an unrivalled network of contacts”.

She added: “Throughout decades of conflict, he preserved his humanity and remained true to his moral compass.

“He leaves behind a remarkable body of work through the *Irish Times**, The Independent,* * The Economist* and electronic media, which helped people understand the reality of Northern Ireland’s troubled past.”
