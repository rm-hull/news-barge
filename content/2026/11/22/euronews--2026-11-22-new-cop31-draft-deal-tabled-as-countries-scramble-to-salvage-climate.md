## The latest draft again failed to mention fossil fuels, which have become a flashpoint at the talks.

A new COP31 draft agreement was published early on Sunday as nations continued to struggle to reach a climate deal.

The new draft, published deep into overtime, kept a much-sought goal to electrify the global economy but again failed to mention fossil fuels, deepening divisions at the UN conference - which is being held in Antalya, Turkey.

The latest text needs consensus approval from almost 200 nations at a closing session on Sunday.

An EU official told the AFP news agency that the draft was a "backwards" step and warned that there was a "very real possibility" of the talks ending without a deal.

"I am more pessimistic today than I was yesterday," they said.

"The situation is difficult, and the outcome is uncertain. The oil-producing countries are putting up a fierce fight," a German official added.

Planet-heating fossil fuels have become a flashpoint at the talks, which come amid soaring energy prices and simmering global tensions stemming from the Iran war.

A draft deal unveiled earlier this week had called for a sped-up phasing out of fossil fuels. But a second version released on Thursday removed all mentions of the energy sources, forcing negotiators back to the bargaining table.

A group of countries including France and Panama had released a letter on Friday insisting that a deal include "a clear transition away from fossil fuels."

The latest offering acknowledged a previous global deal to transition away from fossil fuels but stopped short of naming them explicitly.

It also kept a contentious push to expand carbon capture and storage, a technology backed by the fossil fuel industry but criticised by opponents as costly and ineffective at reducing emissions.

"We are very disappointed with how the text looks," Michael Joseph, the environment minister of Antigua and Barbuda, told AFP.

"I can't honestly say that I'm okay with it passing" as it is, Joseph said, adding that the draft was "quite silent" on fossil fuels.
