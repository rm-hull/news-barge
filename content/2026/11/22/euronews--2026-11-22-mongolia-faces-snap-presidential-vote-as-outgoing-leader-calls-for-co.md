**Dogyu Lee and Euronews Doha bureau**

## Mongolia is preparing for its first early presidential election after President Khurelsukh Ukhnaa resigned amid corruption allegations, having called for constitutional reform before leaving office.

Mongolia will hold a snap presidential election after President Khurelsukh Ukhnaa resigned amid corruption allegations.

Mongolia’s parliament, the State Great Khural, approved the president’s resignation on Thursday, with 96 of the 104 lawmakers present voting in favour of the motion, according to state news agency MONTSAME.

This marks the first time a sitting Mongolian president has resigned before completing their term.

On Tuesday, the 58-year-old president announced his resignation to allow him to prove his innocence in relation to corruption allegations. Khurelsukh denies any wrongdoing.

“I have never lied, stolen or deceived others,” Khurelsukh said at a press briefing on Tuesday, according to MONTSAME.

Khurelsukh has been accused by Nyambaatar Khishgee, former mayor of Ulaanbaatar and a member of a rival faction within the ruling Mongolian People’s Party (MPP), of taking $25 million (€23 million) in bribes.

The allegations concern a railway contract awarded to Bodi International, a Mongolian conglomerate, in 2019, when Khurelsukh was serving as prime minister.

The project involved the Tavantolgoi-Gashuunsukhait railway, which connects Mongolia’s coal-producing region to the border with China.

Bodi International has denied paying bribes to government officials.

## Calls for constitutional reform

Before leaving office, Khurelsukh called for a constitutional referendum alongside the upcoming presidential election.

He suggested either introducing a bicameral parliament that would elect the president or shifting to a presidential system with greater executive powers.

Parliament has yet to announce a referendum on the proposed constitutional changes.

A new presidential election must be held within four months of the president’s resignation. Until a new president is elected and sworn into office, Parliamentary Speaker Byambatsogt Sandag is exercising presidential powers.

Parliament must announce the election date within 20 working days of the vacancy, and at least 70 days before the election.

Originally, the next presidential election was to be held in the summer of 2027. The winner of the early election will serve a single six-year term.

This means they will serve beyond Mongolia’s next two parliamentary elections, expected in 2028 and 2032 under the regular electoral cycle.

## Political instability

Mongolia has experienced several high-profile political resignations in recent years.

In June 2025, former Prime Minister Luvsannamsrai Oyun-Erdene resigned after losing parliamentary support amid corruption allegations, which he denied.

In March 2026, Oyun-Erdene’s successor, Zandanshatar Gombojav, also resigned amid rising tensions within the ruling MPP and a parliamentary boycott by the opposition.

## Relations with Europe

Mongolia has been seeking to improve relations with international partners other than its two powerful neighbours, China and Russia.

The country has long followed a foreign policy known as the ‘Third Neighbour Policy’, with the aim of improving relations with international partners, including the European Union.

Just one month before his resignation, Khurelsukh went on a state visit to Germany. During this visit, he met German President Frank-Walter Steinmeier.

As part of the visit, Mongolia and Germany signed a joint declaration to strengthen their existing strategic partnership. This includes cooperation on renewable energy, education and security.

In a statement following a meeting in Brussels on 29 June, the EU reaffirmed its support for Mongolia’s Third Neighbour Policy.

The EU described its partnership with Mongolia as based on democracy and the rule of law, while reaffirming its support for the country's economic diversification.
