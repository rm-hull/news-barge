A former North Yorkshire Police officer would have been dismissed without notice after sending more than 1,100 messages to a vulnerable 13-year-old girl.

The former police constable, who has not been named due to an anonymity order, was found to have committed gross misconduct at a hearing.

He resigned from the force on 29 June 2026 while under investigation. The details of the hearing were published on Friday.

The former officer, who transferred to North Yorkshire Police on 2 November 2020, did not attend the two-day hearing in October and voluntarily absented himself.

The panel heard that the 41-year-old owned a kickboxing club, which was an approved business interest, where he coached a vulnerable 13-year-old girl referred to as Miss A.

While the girl’s mother, referred to as Ms X, had given permission for him to contact her daughter on social media to assist with coaching, the panel found the contact went far beyond what was required.

Between March 2023 and March 2024, at least 1,126 messages were exchanged between the officer and Miss A on Facebook Messenger, including 530 in December alone.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/north-yorkshire-police-hq-alverton-court-northallerton-gsv.webp)

The panel found all 11 sub-allegations against the former officer proven on the balance of probabilities, establishing that he:

* Messaged Miss A when he knew she was at school, replying on one occasion: “cool…. Now get some learning done”.
* Asked about her romantic relationship with a 16-year-old boy and told her: “Don’t worry, I won’t tell your mum”.
* Discussed adult topics, including his partner wanting another baby, his relationship, serial killer Jeffrey Dahmer, and the film *Predator*.* Called Miss A a “tithead” in one message thread and sent a photograph of himself making a middle-finger gesture.
* Discussed getting “very very very very very drunk” and needing whisky.
* Sent photos of a tattoo session and made a disparaging remark about the sporting kit worn by another girl at the club.
* Commented on Miss A’s ADHD medication, telling her “you’re a legend when you’re off them” after she indicated she was off her medication.

The panel noted that a martial arts teacher with previous experience of Miss A and her mother had specifically warned the officer to exercise caution.

## ‘Unfair’ portrayal

In his written responses, the former officer denied the factual allegations and claimed he had no intention of forming an improper relationship.

He stated he did not remember the volume of messages and claimed his mental health and ADHD meant he was not acting in his normal manner, though he later accepted his ADHD was not linked to causation.

In a statement to the panel, he said: “The way I get portrayed is unfair. I am not totally unblameworthy in the situation and there are things I could have done differently or approached better”.

His Police Federation representative, Rob Bowles, argued that the officer’s personal letter showed contrition and remorse, and the panel noted he had three commendations on his service record.

However, the panel rejected his explanations, finding he showed a “concerning lack of insight” and minimal contrition. They concluded there was an “unambiguous power imbalance” between a 41-year-old coach and serving police officer and a vulnerable 13-year-old child.

## Overwhelming evidence

After the two-day gross misconduct hearing, held at North Yorkshire Police headquarters in Northallerton on 27 and 28 October, the panel ruled his actions breached the Standards of Professional Behaviour relating to discreditable conduct.

It amounted to gross misconduct.

In their written decision, the panel stated: “The evidence against the officer was overwhelming. The matter concerns a serious breach of trust involving a child and clearly could have an impact on public confidence in policing and the reputation of the police service.

“His behaviour is such that he has demonstrated that he plainly has no future in the police service.”

The panel concluded that the former officer would have been dismissed had he remained in service and ordered that he be placed on the police barred list.

The panel was chaired by Assistant Chief Officer Sarah Jackson alongside independent panel members Paul Harvey and Cai Mallett.
