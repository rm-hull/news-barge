Tasha Suri, author of the Burning Kingdoms trilogy and several other books, will be writing Baldur's Gate 3: Shadowheart, the official BG3books website has announced. Another prequel to Larian's videogame, this one focuses on Shadowheart's time as a priestess in the Church of Shar, and is about a murder mystery.

"When a member of their order is killed in the Upper City," the blurb goes, "Shadowheart and her so-called allies are called to investigate. She trusts none of them: not Mirie, the ambitious Griefguard; not Caldan, the cynical propagandist; and certainly not Nocturne, the charming quartermistress, who claims to have only Shadowheart’s best interests in mind."

Mirie and Nocturne are both characters you can meet in Baldur's Gate 3, while Caldan Darkwood is mentioned in a note you can find. Nocturne in particular has a close relationship with Shadowheart, which could be interesting to see developed. "Torn between the familiar comforts of the dark and the thrill of new desire," the blurb continues, "she must solve the mystery to prove her devotion… or be cast into the light."

Which sounds fine and dandy, but it'll have an uphill battle with the Baldur's Gate 3 fandom, who've been vocally critical of the Astarion novel for its lightness of tone—especially given his traumatic backstory—and apparently mischaracterizing Astarion and his sire, Cazador, as well as being inaccurate to the lore.

I haven't read the Astarion book, but I did read the original Baldur's Gate novels, which were terrible. So were almost all the D&D books I read in a youthful post-Tolkien haze where I'd put up with anything that had wizards in it. My expectations are pretty low, but I do like to slip a trashy fantasy novel between heavier reads as a palate-cleanser so maybe I'll try this one some day.

Baldur's Gate 3: Shadowheart will be out in a 304-page hardcover edition and in ebook form on June 8, as well as a 10-hour audiobook. The Astarion audiobook was read by Astarion performer Neil Newbon, we'll have to wait and see if Jennifer English reads the Shadowheart book.

If the series continues I could see a Wyll prequel being interesting, since he gets a bit short-changed in the videogame, though I wouldn't expect it to sell as well as novels about the more popular companions.
