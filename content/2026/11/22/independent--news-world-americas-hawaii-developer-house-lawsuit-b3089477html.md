A California woman who purchased a vacant acre of land in Hawaii for roughly $22,500 was reportedly sued by a real estate developer after contractors mistakenly built a $500,000 house on her property without her permission.

The dispute — which ended in a confidential settlement in May 2025 — centered on a parcel in Hawaiian Paradise Park, a residential subdivision in the Puna district of Hawaii’s Big Island. Annaleine “Anne” Reynolds bought the empty lot at a county tax auction in 2018, as reported by the *Associated Press*, and remained in California during the Covid-19 pandemic without visiting the site.

Reynolds did not learn that a home had been constructed on her land until 2023, when a real estate broker contacted her to say he had sold a house on the property valued at around $500,000, according to *Hawaii News Now*.

The home was part of a larger project by Keaau Development Partnership, which had acquired several plots in the subdivision and hired PJ’s Construction to build roughly a dozen residences. After builders constructed one of the houses on Reynolds’ parcel by mistake, the developer filed a lawsuit naming Reynolds, the construction firm, the architect and other parties involved in the project.

To resolve the error, Keaau Development Partnership offered to swap Reynolds’ lot for a neighboring parcel of comparable size and value, according to court documents. Reynolds rejected the offer and filed a counterclaim, maintaining she had no knowledge of the unauthorized work, *The Times of India* reported.

Her attorney, James DiPasquale, argued that accepting a land swap would undermine property rights.

“It would set a dangerous precedent, if you could go on to someone else's land, build anything you want, and then sue that individual for the value of it,” he said.

Peter Olson, an attorney for the developer, told the *Associated Press* that his client believed Reynolds was attempting to exploit an error made by PJ’s Construction for financial gain, noting that many lots in Hawaiian Paradise Park share similar dimensions and layouts.

Disagreements also emerged between the companies involved in the build over how the mistake occurred.

“There’s a lot of fingers being pointed between the developer and the contractor and some subs,” DiPasquale said.

An attorney for PJ’s Construction told *Hawaii News Now* that Keaau Development Partnership had declined to pay for land surveyors before construction began. Instead, contractors reportedly used telephone poles to estimate property lines.

In June 2024, a Hawaii County judge ruled in Reynolds’ favor and ordered the developer to remove the house, as reported by ABC affiliate KITV. The court found that the developer and contractor had assumed the risk of building without a property survey, dismissed the developer's lawsuit against Reynolds and rejected claims that her property title was invalid. However, the judge denied Reynolds’ request to have the land restored to its original condition with native plants and wildlife.

The litigation continued until May 2025, when newly filed court records showed that Reynolds and the developer had reached a settlement, as reported by *Hawaii News Now*. The terms of the agreement remain confidential.
