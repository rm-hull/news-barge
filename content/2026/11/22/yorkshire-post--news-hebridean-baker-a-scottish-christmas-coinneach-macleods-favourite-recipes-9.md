# Hebridean Baker, A Scottish Christmas: Coinneach MacLeod's favourite recipes

## Chicken Braemar with whisky sauce

“This is my Braemar take on the much-loved Balmoral chicken,” says Coinneach MacLeod. “Instead of haggis, Stornoway black pudding pairs beautifully with the chicken and bacon. Wrapped, baked and finished with a simple whisky cream sauce, this dish is perfect when you want something that feels a bit special.”

Serves 6. Ingredients: 4 skinless chicken breasts; 20 rashers of streaky bacon; 300g Stornoway black pudding

For the whisky sauce: Knob of butter; 3-4tbsp whisky; 1tsp Dijon mustard; 50ml vegetable stock; 100ml double cream; Sea salt and black pepper

Lay a chicken breast between two pieces of baking parchment. Using a rolling pin, gently flatten the chicken until it’s an even thickness of about 3cm. Repeat with the remaining breasts.

Lay five rashers of streaky bacon side by side on a piece of baking parchment. Place a flattened chicken breast on top, positioned across the bacon. Shape about 75g of the black pudding into a neat log and sit it along the centre of the chicken.

Using the parchment, roll the chicken up around the filling, keeping it tight, while letting the bacon wrap neatly around the outside. Repeat with the remaining chicken breasts, then cover them all tightly in clingfilm before transferring them to the fridge to chill for at least an hour.

Heat the oven to 160°C fan/180°C/gas mark 4. Unwrap the chicken parcels carefully, keeping the bacon in place, and sit them seam-side down. Wrap each one tightly in foil, place on a baking tray and cook in the oven for 20 minutes. Remove the tray from the oven and let the chicken rest briefly in the foil. Turn the oven up to 180°C fan/200°C/gas mark Carefully remove the foil, then return the chicken to the oven, uncovered, for 15 minutes, until the surrounding bacon is crisp and golden. Take the chicken out of the oven and leave it to rest for five minutes.

To make the whisky sauce, melt the butter in a small saucepan over a medium heat. Add the whisky and let it bubble gently for a minute or two, just to cook off the alcohol.

Stir in the Dijon mustard and vegetable stock and bring back to a gentle simmer.

Season with salt and black pepper, then pour in the cream and let it simmer until thickened to a spoonable sauce. Slice the chicken and spoon over the whisky sauce.

Stornoway black pudding and apple croquettes

“A visit to Stornoway always means a trip to Charles MacLeod butchers to stock up on their black pudding,” says Coinneach MacLeod. “Theirs is rich, perfectly spiced and features here as the star of these, crisp on the outside and soft within, croquettes.”

Makes 18-20. Ingredients: 50ml milk; 150ml vegetable stock; 85g butter, plus a little extra for the pan; 120g plain flour; 75g mature Cheddar, finely grated; A splash of olive oil, for frying; 150g Stornoway black pudding, chopped; Knob of butter; 1 apple, peeled, cored and finely chopped; Pinch of sugar; 2 large eggs, beaten; 150g breadcrumbs; Vegetable oil, for shallow frying; Sea salt and freshly ground

Pour the milk and vegetable stock into a small saucepan and warm gently over a low heat. Once hot, remove from the heat and set aside. Melt the butter in a large saucepan over a medium heat. Add the flour and cook for two to three minutes, stirring constantly, until it smells lightly toasted. Slowly add the warm milk and stock, stirring well after each addition, until you have a thick, smooth bechamel. Season generously, then stir through the grated Cheddar until fully melted and smooth. Remove from the heat.

Heat a splash of olive oil in a frying pan and cook the black pudding for a couple of minutes, until it starts to crisp at the edges. Lift out and set aside. Add a knob of butter to the same pan, then add the chopped apple with a pinch of sugar. Cook for two to three minutes, until lightly caramelised. Fold the black pudding and apple through the cheese sauce until evenly combined. Spoon the mixture into a bowl. Cover and chill in the fridge for at least two hours, until completely firm.

Put the beaten eggs into one bowl and the breadcrumbs into another. Once the mixture is chilled, lightly oil your hands, scoop out portions, rolling each one into a ball of around 30g to make a croquette. Dip each croquette into the egg, then roll in the breadcrumbs until well-coated. Set aside and repeat.

Pour enough vegetable oil into a wide, deep, frying pan so it comes about 3cm up the sides. Heat over a medium-high heat until a cube of bread turns golden in about 20 seconds. Fry the croquettes in batches for two to three minutes, turning gently, until golden and crisp. Lift out with a slotted spoon and drain briefly on kitchen paper. Serve slightly cooled if you like the centre gooey or later if you prefer it set.

beetroot and caramelised onion galette

I love making a galette – no tin, no perfect edges, just excellent ingredients brought together on a sheet of pastry,” says Coinneach MacLeod. “This one makes an ideal lunch, with slow-cooked onions for sweetness and depth, beetroot for earthiness and colour, and a little goat’s cheese to sharpen everything up. I know this recipe looks like it calls for a lot of onions, but this should be the perfect amount for your galette. Though sometimes I make double as I know I’m going to want some to top my burgers the next day!”

Serves 4. Ingredients. For the caramelised onions: 2tbsp vegetable oil; 15g butter; 750g onions, peeled, halved and finely sliced; Good pinch of salt; 1tbsp water; 1½tbsp soft brown sugar; 1½tbsp balsamic vinegar

For the galette: 1 x 320g ready-rolled shortcrust pastry; 250g cooked beetroot, thinly sliced; 125g soft goat’s cheese; 1tsp fresh thyme leaves (optional); 1 egg, beaten, for glazing; Olive oil, for drizzling; Salt and freshly ground black pepper

Begin with the onions. Set a large, wide frying pan over a low heat and add the oil and butter. Tip in the sliced onions with a good pinch of salt and stir well to coat. Add a tablespoon of water, cover with a lid and cook gently for eight to 10 minutes, stirring once or twice, until the onions have softened. Remove the lid and continue cooking slowly, stirring regularly, until the onions collapse and begin to take on colour.

Stir in the soft brown sugar and keep cooking gently for a total of 60 minutes, scraping up any sticky bits from the base of the pan as you go. Once the onions are deeply golden and jammy, stir in the balsamic vinegar and cook for a further two to three minutes until glossy. Remove from the heat and allow to cool slightly.

Heat the oven to 180°C fan/200°C/gas mark 6 and line a large baking tray with baking paper. Unroll the pastry onto the tray and, if needed, gently roll it into a rough circle about 30cm across. Spread a generous layer of the caramelised onions over the pastry, leaving a 3 to 4cm border clear around the edge. Arrange the beetroot slices on top, overlapping slightly, then season lightly with salt and black pepper.

Crumble or slice over the goat’s cheese and scatter with the thyme leaves, if using. Fold the edges of the pastry up and over the filling, pleating as you go to create a rustic border. Brush the exposed pastry with the beaten egg. Bake for 35 to 40 minutes, until the pastry is crisp and golden. Remove from the oven, drizzle lightly with olive oil and leave to cool for five to 10 minutes before slicing and serving.
