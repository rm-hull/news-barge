## Rare 18th century fishing reel to go under the hammer at Tennants

A Very Rare John Herro, Temple Bar, London 2” Brass Multiplying Winch – Estimate: £1,500-1,800 *(Image: Tennants Auctioneers)*

A rare mid-18th century fishing reel is expected to catch the eye of people at an upcoming auction.

The reel, a two-inch brass multiplying winch, was made by John Herro of London during a period when fly fishing was becoming popular among the aristocracy.

It is set to be auctioned at Tennants Auctioneers’ Toys and Collectables, Sporting and Fishing Sale on December 4, with an estimated price of between £1,500 and £1,800, excluding buyer’s premium.

The earliest known reference to a fishing reel dates back to 1650 in The Art of Angling by Thomas Barker.

John Herro operated from London's Temple Bar, an area known for its fishing tackle suppliers, from around 1730 to 1760, before Onesimus Ustonson took over his premises.

The auction will also feature a range of vintage advertising materials, including a 1930s LNER poster for Cambridge, valued at £500 to £700, and various enamel advertising signs.

A Roundhouse UFO Concert Poster from 1967 is estimated to fetch £400 to £600.

The auction will also include the first part of a large collection of film campaign books and leaflets, primarily from the 1960s to 1980s, valued at various estimates.

The remaining collection will be auctioned in the spring.
