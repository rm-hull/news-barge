A major machinery dealer with a site in Darlington has entered administration with 200 jobs lost.

Molson Group, a distributor of heavy machinery, called in administrators on Friday (November 6) after facing "challenging trading conditions" and "weaker demand" in the UK construction market.

The Bristol-headquartered business, founded in 1996, operated from 16 UK sites, including a depot on Red Barnes Way in Darlington.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21683361.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Molson Group's Darlington site. *(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)*

The business employed 345 people in the UK when it collapsed, administrators said, with 203 immediately made redundant.

The number of employees affected in Darlington has not yet been confirmed.

Ross Connock, Tom Crookham and Toby Banfield of PWC UK were appointed joint administrators of Molson Group Limited, and its trading subsidiaries, Molson Equipment Services, Capital Equipment Finance, Aggregate Processing Solutions and Molson Scotland.

Ross Connock, joint administrator and director at PWC, said: “It is regrettable that Molson Group’s UK businesses have entered administration and that this has resulted in a significant number of redundancies

"We recognise how difficult this will be for those affected and their families.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21683360.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Molson Group's Darlington site. *(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)*

“Our immediate priority is to support affected employees through the redundancy process, while working with other stakeholders to undertake an orderly wind-down of the UK businesses and maximise value from the companies’ assets.

Administrators said they explored several options, including selling the UK operations and assets, but no buyer completed a deal in time and so the firm will now be wound down.

Molson Group's shares in its American operations were transferred to newly incorporated Fieldsec Hastings Limited along with a small number of UK staff and all US employees.

Mr Connock added: "The sale of the US businesses means those operations can continue outside the UK Group, preserving the business and employment there."

The company's website now carries a notice confirming the appointment of joint administrators.
