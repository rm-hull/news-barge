Councillors are concerned children are "falling through the gaps" and "risks are being missed" in the wake of a Bradford service being rated inadequate.

Bradford Youth Justice Service, which supervises 10- to 17-year-olds with complex needs including children in care, was rated inadequate after a reactive inspection by His Majesty’s Inspectorate of Probation.

The report found "significant weaknesses in the ability of the YJS to keep children and communities safe" and said its governance and partnership arrangements urgently needed stronger oversight and challenge to improve frontline practice.

Partnership working was also branded ineffective, with agencies working in isolation and putting their own priorities ahead of a co-ordinated response for children in the youth justice system.

Cllr Rebecca Poulsen, Conservative group leader, said: "This report is deeply concerning as it highlights weakness in co-ordination between partners of the Youth Justice Service and that the risks to the safety of the individual child and the wider public are not being properly managed.

"When organisations don’t coordinate properly vulnerable children can fall through the gaps and risks can be missed and not properly managed.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21679282.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Cllr Rebecca Poulsen, Leader of the Bradford Conservative Group *(Image: UGC)*

"The public will rightly be concerned that inspectors found the service inadequate in its inspection."

The report criticised the service’s governance and risk management but praised staff for supporting children and building positive relationships with families.

Cllr Poulsen said: "We also welcome that the Youth Justice Partnership Board have accepted the recommendations for improvement and are already working on implementing them.

"The Conservative councillors will continue to scrutinise the findings further and the response of all the agencies involved in the Youth Justice Service."

Cllr Richard Downey, chair of the Children’s Services Overview and Scrutiny Committee, said: "(Inspector) Martin Jones' report into Bradford Youth Justice Service has found significant weaknesses in the service's ability to keep our children and communities safe.

"It is only right and proper that this is urgently reviewed and therefore I have asked for this to be added to our works programme as a matter of urgency."

The Conservative group said it will continue to scrutinise the service’s response to the inspection.

The Youth Justice Partnership Board has accepted the recommendations for improvement and is already working on implementing them.

Ruth Terry, chair of the Bradford Youth Justice Partnership Board, said: "The Board and all the statutory partners collectively own the changes that are needed.

"Through stronger oversight, consistent partner contributions, and a continued focus on performance, we will deliver the rapid progress required to strengthen practice and keep children and communities safe.

The inspectorate made nine recommendations, including a robust assurance framework for the management board and improved assessments, planning and delivery by the Youth Justice Service.

Inspectors also linked inconsistent practice to staff vacancies, high sickness absence and gaps in partner provision, which had contributed to excessive workloads.

The inspection involved a two-week review in June 2026 of cases including bail or remand, court disposals and out-of-court resolutions, alongside interviews with case managers.

Bradford Children and Families Trust said a partnership improvement plan was in place, with work under way on multi-agency working, quality assurance, analysis, management oversight, staffing and workforce development.
