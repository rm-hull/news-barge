A Second World War veteran believed to be the North East's oldest man has celebrated his 107th birthday - and revealed the sweet secret to his long life.

Harold Hodgson marked the extraordinary milestone at The Grange Care Home in Darlington on Saturday (November 7) where he was showered with an equally impressive 303 birthday cards.

The celebrations brought together Harold's family, fellow residents and representatives of the Royal British Legion, alongside the Mayor of Darlington, Helen Crumbie.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21683625.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The 107th Birthday of Harold Hodgson at The Grange, Care Home, Darlington *(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)*

Born in Darlington on November 7, 1919, Harold grew up in Rise Carr and has spent almost his entire life in the town.

He joined the RAF aged 22 and served for five years as ground staff, working as a bricklayer during the war.

His service took him to Belgium, France, Germany, the Netherlands and Norfolk.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21683637.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The 107th Birthday of Harold Hodgson at The Grange, Care Home, Darlington *(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)*

Speaking about his wartime experiences, Harold said: "I'll tell you what, it was like Dad's Army. It was easy.

"We were among the bombs, but we managed to laugh and enjoy ourselves."

He said he was "really proud" of his time in the RAF and would remember the friends he made during his service.

Despite reaching 107, Harold remains a sociable character with a wonderful sense of humour.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21683627.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The 107th Birthday of Harold Hodgson at The Grange, Care Home, Darlington, pictured with manager Carmen Miclea *(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)*

Asked the secret to his remarkable longevity, he said: "It is being active, I have been active since I was eight years old when my dad bought me a trike and I never looked back. I was cycling, swimming, playing football. You name it, I was doing it.

"I also had some lovely ladies in my life, number one was my dear mother, five kids, no money but she looked after me.

"And then my wife Sylvia who I got married to in London. She was a Londoner.

"She passed away a few years back and then my daughters, they're angels looking after me.

"These women brought me to the standard I am today."

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21683638.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The 107th Birthday of Harold Hodgson at The Grange, Care Home, Darlington pictured with daughters Christine Gibbon and Margaret Hart *(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)*

But alongside his active lifestyle, he also made sure to enjoy his daily morning coffee and a McVitie's chocolate biscuit.

He admitted he also loves the occasional sherry and "absolutely loves chocolate".

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21683634.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The 107th Birthday of Harold Hodgson at The Grange, Care Home, Darlington *(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)*

Staff at the care home had appealed for people to send Harold 107 birthday cards, allowing him to open one for every year of his remarkable life.

The response saw him receive almost three times that number, with the cards collected and organised by registered care home manager Carmen Miclea.

Harold was also presented with a certificate of appreciation by John Smith, chairman of the Darlington branch of the Royal British Legion.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21683633.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The 107th Birthday of Harold Hodgson at The Grange, Care Home, Darlington *(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)*

Music from Kay Sweet Heart added to the celebrations, with guests waving flags and dancing and swaying along to tunes from the Second World War.

Harold, who lost his beloved wife Sylvia a decade ago at the age of 95, also has three grandchildren and four great-grandchildren.

Reflecting on his birthday celebrations, he said: "It was marvellous, it couldn't have been better. Everybody has been here."

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21683635.jpg?type=mds-article-620)303 cards for The 107th Birthday of Harold Hodgson at The Grange, Care Home, Darlington *(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)*

His daughters Christine Gibbon and Margaret Hart said it was "overwhelming" to see so many people celebrate their dad, bringing a tear to Christine's eye.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21683631.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The 107th Birthday of Harold Hodgson at The Grange, Care Home, Darlington *(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)*

Carmen said her research suggested Harold was the North East's oldest living man and the country's second-oldest, behind a 111-year-old man in Essex.

She said: "It's been amazing, it feels so honourable and as if everything you are doing is making a difference, so the care, the love, the kindness that we stand for, not just as a company but as each individuals this is what we do and it is amazing to see that we were able to contribute and make someone happy and make someone's special day extra special."

Before his birthday, Harold was welcomed at a Royal British Legion charity event at Darlington's Springfield Hotel on October 23.
