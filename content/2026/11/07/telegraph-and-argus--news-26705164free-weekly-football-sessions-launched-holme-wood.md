FREE weekly football sessions are giving young people a safe place to play and build confidence.

The Premier League Kicks: Police Partnerships project is running in Holme Wood, backed by the Bradford City FC Community Foundation, West Yorkshire Police, and First Bus.

Funded by the Premier League Foundation, the scheme aims to reduce anti-social behaviour by offering football coaching, mentoring and positive engagement.

Oliver Read, head of Premier League and business development at the Bradford City FC Community Foundation, said: "We are proud to deliver this project for young people in our city, and I’d like to thank West Yorkshire Police and First Bus for their support in bringing it to life.

"Together, we are demonstrating the impact of partnership working, and the massive impact we can have on young people in our city."

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21678762.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The Police Partnerships session is delivered as part of the Foundation’s pre-existing Premier League Kicks project, a scheme funded by the Premier League Foundation *(Image: Supplied)*

Adam Beecroft, neighbourhood policing inspector for Bradford South, said: "By taking services directly into hotspot areas and building relationships with young people, we can help steer them away from anti-social behaviour and towards positive activities."

Since starting, 72 young people have engaged in the Police Partnerships project, and 100% of attendees have fed back that the sessions have helped them feel included, safe, form better relationships and improved their confidence and self-esteem.

The sessions take place every Wednesday from 4.30pm to 5.30pm at Tong Leadership Academy.
