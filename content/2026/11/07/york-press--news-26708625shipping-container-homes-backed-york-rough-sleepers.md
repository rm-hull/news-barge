## 'Anywhere is better than the street': Homeless in York back shipping container homes

Ryan Hutchinson, 43, has backed suggestions to convert shipping containers into temporary accommodation for the homeless *(Image: Newsquest)*

PEOPLE experiencing homelessness in York have welcomed proposals to explore shipping container homes, arguing any safe shelter beats sleeping rough.

The comments come after a City of York Council report suggested alternative options to traditional housing could be used to tackle rough sleeping in the city.

The plans form part of wider efforts to address housing pressures, with 1,387 housing applications live in York in October 2026.




Ryan Hutchinson, 43, has been homeless for a year and a half and is currently sleeping rough while waiting for a home.

Originally from Doncaster, Ryan has been on the housing waiting list in York for four months.

He said: “I think turning shipping containers to homes is a good idea.

“I’ve seen what people can do with them and you can turn them into decent accommodation.

“It’s getting colder, so anything that keeps us warm and dry is welcome.”

![HOPING Street Kitchen host sessions, described as 'lifelines' by users, outside of King's Manor each week](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21683317.jpg?type=mds-article-620)HOPING Street Kitchen host sessions, described as 'lifelines' by users, outside of King's Manor each week *(Image: Newsquest)*

Ryan was joined by around 60 rough sleepers at a weekly HOPING Street Kitchen session outside King’s Manor.

York has been allocated £483,000 – the first tranche of funding as part of a government scheme aimed at ending rough sleeping by Christmas 2026.

The money must be spent by March 2027, with more funding expected over the next three years.

Alex Younis, 44, who was made homeless in March, said the city should look at bringing empty buildings back into use.

He said: “If you look in the city centre, there are first-floor spaces above businesses that I can see are empty and derelict.

“They could serve as accommodation, and I’d like to see them utilised.”

Alex said his situation has become more difficult after his belongings were stolen.

He said: “I went from sleeping in a tent in a field to sleeping in doorways – and I had all my things taken.

“It’s getting colder and colder. I just want to be somewhere out of the elements.”

Alex said services like HOPING Street Kitchen and Carecent York, which provides a breakfast service for the homeless from Monday to Saturday, were a lifeline.

He said: "If places like this weren't here, we'd starve.”

An update on the council’s Homelessness and Rough Sleeping Strategy was discussed on Monday (October 26).

A council report before the meeting found 800 people needed one-bedroom homes, including 116 in the high-priority Gold band.

An additional eight people needed emergency accommodation.

## 'I'm like a lot of people, even those with families. It shouldn't be like this'

A 64-year-old woman, living with Chronic Lymphocytic Leukaemia (CLL), who was asked not to be named, said she was currently living in temporary accommodation after rising bills left her homeless for the first time.

She described her experience as a “total shock” and said: "I just don't want to be like this anymore.

"I've got my temporary place and I've been lucky.

"I’m now waiting for my new place.”

Having returned to York in recent weeks, she said services such as Hoping Street Kitchen had been invaluable after she fell on hard times.

She said: "I'm like a lot of people, even those with families. It shouldn't be like this.”

## 'I know what it’s like to be homeless. It’s terrible'

For Stan Jacques, 77, the wait for permanent accommodation is almost over.

The York resident has spent the last five months at James House after becoming homeless and is due to move into a one-bedroom flat next week through a shared-ownership scheme linked to Joseph Rowntree Housing Trust.

He said: "I know what it's like to be homeless. It's terrible.”

Speaking about the temporary accommodation proposals, he said: “Anywhere is better than the street.

“I know what it’s like to be homeless. It’s terrible.

“I don’t know how the people on the streets survive when it comes to winter.

“I just hope they can find a suitable place.”

City of York Council officials said some temporary housing solutions, including garage conversions and modular homes, could be delivered quickly.

The report added: “There are still planning implications, however it seems that to achieve our strategic aims and offer housing to everyone in need it will be imperative to think outside of traditional routes.”
