A new television series could soon bring the dramatic landscapes of North Yorkshire to screens worldwide.

The Moonstone Legacy Quartet, a series of novels by Thirsk-based Tony Wild and Diana de Gunzburg, is in advanced development to become a major TV production.

A sequel to Wilkie Collins’ classic The Moonstone, the books follow the adventures of young heroine Lizzie Abercrombie and feature a blend of realism and mysticism set against a backdrop of striking locations across Yorkshire and beyond.

Diana de Gunzburg said: "Determined to uncover the truth, Lizzie's investigation takes her from Lizzieland - our fictionalized area nestled in the North York Moors, inspired by real locations like Kepwick, Hawnby, and Brimham Rocks - to the Temple of the Moon in India."

"There she uncovers the dark history of her ancestor's actions, which plunges her into mortal danger from a Louis d’Harcourt, a charismatic Swiss industrialist obsessed with religious artefacts and idols who is determined to have the Moonstone for himself.

"Ultimately, the quartet concludes with Lizzie and her friends uncovering everything that had been hidden, confronting their enemies, and revealing the concealed cosmic marvels that lie at the centre of their interconnected lives.

"Our plot weaves through high-profile celestial events — including a pivotal total solar eclipse on a beach in Spain — forcing our characters to navigate the thin, blurring boundary between science fiction, historical fact, and reality."

Describing the cinematic potential of the story, Ms de Gunzburg explained why she and co-author Mr Wild believe the books are suited for television.

She said: "Our books are very visual, with stunning locations not just in Yorkshire, but across the world, which is why we believe they would make a wonderful television series.

"Plans for this are well advanced, with a script written, a detailed pitch deck prepared for potential investors and a producer lined up.

"So, watch this space."

The series features several North Yorkshire locations under fictional names, including the Cleveland Way (The Drove Road), Brimham Rocks (Grimstone Scar), Kepwick Hall Estate (Shalimar), and Thirsk (Knowlesby).

Other settings include The Strid, Thornborough Henge, and the Ritz Cinema in Thirsk.

Ms de Gunzburg added: "There is no doubt that North Yorkshire boasts some of the most stunning and magical landscapes in the whole of the UK, and they make the perfect backdrop to Lizzie’s adventures.

"So, we are excited that they will feature again in a major television series, following in the illustrious footsteps of All Creatures Great and Small, Wuthering Heights, and Brideshead Revisited.

"They will be alongside other incredible locations in Spain, India, and Sri Lanka, but – speaking as proud Yorkshire people – we know they will hold their own."
