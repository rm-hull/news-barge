Almost half of children in one part of Bradford suffer from dental decay - a figure that led one councillor to describe the issue as a “dental pandemic.”

And one public health officer has said that poor dentist provision in the city is the “elephant in the room.”

In the Bradford West constituency 44.8 per cent of five year olds have experienced dental decay.

This is double the England average of 22.4 per cent, and much higher than the Bradford average of 37.1 per cent.

The average number of decayed teeth in all children who had been examined in Bradford West in 2024 was 2.4.

The stark figures were discussed by members of Bradford Council’s Bradford West Area Committee last month, when members were given an annual update on public health schemes in the area.

At the meeting public health officer Mikhail Foster told members that high levels of deprivation in the Constituency were a major factor in the way above average dental decay figures, but said the issue was made worse by an inadequate provision of dentists.

A report to members said: “Dental decay remains a significant issue for children in Bradford West.

“Despite ongoing preventative programmes, oral health inequalities persist, highlighting the need for continued investment in prevention, early intervention and support for families and schools.”

Work being done to tackle the issue includes supervised toothbrushing sessions in schools and nurseries, with 31 providers in the Constituency taking part.

On these efforts, the report says: “By targeting children from an early age, the programme aims to promote good oral hygiene practices, prevent tooth decay, and contribute to improved health outcomes for Bradford West's youngest residents.”

During the discussion on public health, members raised concerns about the dental health of the Constituency’s children.

Councillor Melanie Milnes (Reform, Thornton and Allerton) said: “The dental situation – the figures seem to be getting worse and worse. Poor dental health contributes to not only to future ill health, but children being in pain in school.

“Children are getting very depressed because of the state of their teeth.”

She asked if there was any way of increasing the dentist capacity in Bradford to tackle the issue.

Mr Foster said that provision of dentists was up to the NHS rather than Bradford Council.

Councillor Mohammed Ali Islam (Manningham, Bradford Independents) said: “The dental issue is a massive issue.

![Councillor Mohammed Ali Islam](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/20562504.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Councillor Mohammed Ali Islam *(Image: Cllr Ali Islam)*

“There is a dental pandemic out there – it is a real issue that is being overlooked.

“44.8 per cent of five years olds in Bradford West have dental decay. The England average is 22.4 per cent. How can we explain that two-fold disparity?

Mr Foster said: “There are many underlying factors, the type of food a child is eating, the quality of that food. That can be based on what food parents can afford, and that goes back to issues like employment and deprivation.

“There is a clear correlation between a child growing up in a deprived family and the increased likelihood of having poor dental health.”

He highlighted the community health schemes were carried out in Bradford to teach children, and their parents, of the importance of good dental health.

But he added: “We can’t shy away from the elephant in the room, which is the poor level of dental service to fully meet the needs of the Bradford population.”

He pointed out that the Council has no power to force dentists to open in an area, but he would discuss the issue with NHS partners.

Members heard there was an ongoing floride varnish scheme involving local schools. The report said: “Delivered through 44 schools and early years settings, the programme helps reduce oral health inequalities by providing evidence-based preventative support to children, particularly those at greatest risk of poor oral health outcomes. Continued participation demonstrates a strong commitment to prevention and improving children's oral health.”

The committee also head how the Council’s public health team were carrying out a number of schemes to prevent young people from taking up vaping.

This included the Catch Your Breath campaign to “combat the rise of youth vaping within the District.”

The Ditch the Vape campaign highlights “the mental bind caused by vaping among young people, and the hindrance it can be in their school life.”
