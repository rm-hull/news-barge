Uncertainty still hangs over the future of a Sunderland shop after a council licensing meeting had to be adjourned.

The meeting was to decide upon a premises licence, including the sale of alcohol, to an empty shop on Ryhope Road in Grangetown, but the agent for the applicant was unable to attend due to illness. The meeting will be rescheduled for a later date.

The applicant is Mr Samarth Gakhar, director of North Shields based SRR Stores Limited which, according to Companies House, is a business dealing in “Retail sale in non-specialised stores with food, beverages or tobacco predominating.”

Initially, the application was to sell alcohol from the premises between 6am and 2am every day.

That has now been amended to Monday to Thursday 8am-11.30pm, Friday to Sunday 8am-12.30am. Council officers said this represents “a significant reduction from those previously applied for.”

The application says that goods would be served through a serving hatch from 11pm to 11.30pm Monday to Thursday and from 11pm to 12.30am Friday to Sunday.

# Strong opposition to the application

Following the shortening of opening hours applied for, Northumbria Police have told Sunderland City Council they they have no objections to an alcohol licence being granted.

However, the application is proving controversial and has met with strong opposition from local residents and businesses.

The licensing department has received letters, including one from a pharmacy two doors down. The pharmacy is concerned about the sale of alcohol and “alcohol-related gatherings” at a nearby bus stop, but has no issue with the premises operating as a convenience store.

Other objectors have voiced similar concerns about anti-social behaviour, noise, drunkenness, litter and people parking on the pavement while they use the shop.

Opponents also point out that there are already off-licences nearby, including a Premier shop just over the road and the large Asda supermarket in Leechmere.

The Ryhope Road shop was recently a Turkish barber’s and has previously been a tanning salon, a foot spa business and a health & beauty shop.

In the application it is stated that: “The premises will work closely with the police with regard to the prevention of antisocial behaviour.”

Drunk people would be refused service, as would “those that are recognised as street drinkers”.
