A towering 26-foot Christmas tree now stands in the Great Hall.

It marks the start of festive preparations for this year’s Victorian-themed event, A Dickens of a Christmas at Castle Howard.

The tree, sourced from York Christmas Trees in Wiggington, was installed by the estate’s forestry, gardens and building services teams using a specialist forklift and winch system.

It will be decorated over four days with over 30,000 baubles.

Castle Howard Curator Matthew Wood said: "We’re very excited to display this beautiful piece of history as part of the Dickens-themed installations – perhaps Dickens himself may have travelled in something similar."

Alongside the tree, a Victorian carriage will be displayed inside the house for the first time.

The Brougham carriage was commissioned in 1879 by Rosalind, the 9th Countess of Carlisle, and built by Barker & Co of Chandos Street.

It cost £190 at the time and has since been restored by Castle Howard’s team.

Matthew Wood, curator at Castle Howard, said: "The carriage is a Brougham, a type of enclosed carriage developed in the 1830s by Lord Brougham which was designed to be light, fast and economical, requiring only one horse.

"The carriage has yellow and black livery which was used by the Howard family on their carriages in the Victorian era, and the doors are further decorated with the Howard lion."

This year’s Christmas event draws inspiration from the works of Charles Dickens, featuring installations, soundscapes and projections.

The experience opens to the public on 12th November, and runs until 3rd January.

Displays will incorporate items from Castle Howard’s collection and draw on other connections from Castle Howard’s history.

The installations have been designed by CLW Event Design, with soundscapes and projections by imitating the dog.

Castle Howard’s Christmas events have previously won multiple awards and attracted record visitor numbers.
