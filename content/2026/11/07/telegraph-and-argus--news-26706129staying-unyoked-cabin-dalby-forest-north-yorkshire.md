A bed and a book, does life get any better? Maybe. A book and a bed in a cabin with huge windows that frame a forest?

This pocket of reading peace in Dalby Forest pretty much summed up perfection in terms of getting away from it all.

The book nothing too challenging (The Correspondent, would recommend), so as not to disturb the calming vibe.

Home for a couple of nights, an Unyoked cabin called Briar, one of just five hiding away in Dalby Forest.

![Unyoked cabin at Dalby Forest](https://www.greatbritishlife.co.uk/resources/images/21361481.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Books and birdsong. *(Image: Henry Terry)*

A cool spot in every sense. We arrived on a hot summer’s day but the small clearing that was home to the cabin was shady and peaceful – its deck and deep chairs inviting places to sit and take stock.

The cabins are new to Dalby Forest – the first of their kind within the forest ‒sensitively designed to invite visitors to share and be at one with the surroundings, but with some luxe comfort in the glamping mix.

Leave your car at the designated point and pop your belongings in the wheelbarrow to steer your way to your cabin. Not too much of a journey.

![Unyoked cabin at Dalby Forest](https://www.greatbritishlife.co.uk/resources/images/21361476.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The hideaway Unyoked cabins are places of peace. *(Image: Henry Terry)*

The black-clad cabins look chic but not shouty in their minimalist surroundings. They have all you need for a comfy stay. The bedding is super softy, there are nice throws and blankets. Cook on a small stove and put your wine and food in the fridge. It is wise to bring food as there’s no handy corner shop or pub in walking distance – but there are plenty of lovely farm shops and delis en route for excellent supplies. Thornton le Dale is probably the nearest village if you’re seeking civilisation.

But the point of the Unyoked cabin is to get away from it all. The company is a pioneering nature start-up behind the off-grid cabin trend, which has a 10-year partnership with Forestry England, the first of its kind.

The aim is to allow people to stay deep inside ancient and impressive woodlands and help them disconnect from daily pressures and reconnect with nature.

![Unyoked cabin at Dalby Forest](https://www.greatbritishlife.co.uk/resources/images/21361475.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Park your bike with ease. *(Image: Henry Terry)*

Set deep within the forest where the public typically hasn’t ever been able to stay before, these cabins are designed to offer a private, restorative escape, encouraging guests to slow down, switch off and experience the health benefits of nature firsthand.

It’s very easy to do so. Walk ‒ or jog ‒straight from you cabin door into the forest. At Dalby, walking and cycle trails are very well signposted. The visitor centre is good place to stop and find out more, but the vast forest is wonderful to explore, whether you want extreme treks, nature trails or sculpture walks.

Resist the urge to look at your watch or log your steps. Wallow in the book, explore the forest and find the views. Play cards and listen to old school CDs (provided). There is WiFi but it’s patchy ‒ that suited me fine..

A two-night stay starts from £438.

**DO IT AT DALBYLakeside trail**

This easy trail circles Staindale Lake along boardwalks and well-surfaced paths. It’s particularly worth stopping at Staindale Lake dam on the western edge of the lake to enjoy its tranquil blue waters and spot the wildlife that call this valley their home.

See if you can find the artwork Dragonfly by Peter Maris and Ann Sotheran on your way round.

**Big views** This circular walk starting from Low Staindale car park features a steep incline before reaching a plateau of open moorland and impressive views across the North York Moors. This trail features the iconic Bridestones, a series of sandstone rock formations carved out of the landscape during the last ice age, which have been weathered by the action of frost, wind and rain over thousands of years to create a bizarre series of shapes. The Bridestones themselves are a perfect place to stop for a mid-walk picnic.**Natural highs** Fly down zip wires, leap off Tarzan swings and test your nerve on daring crossings while enjoying views (if your eyes are open!) of the Dalby woodlands at Go Ape. This high ropes experience is ideal for adults and more intrepid adventurers.**Pedal power**

Bring a bike or start your biking adventure at Dalby Forest Cycle Hub. An array of trails catering to all ages and skill levels as well as bike hire is available for you ‒ pedal power or a helping hand from an electric e-bike.
