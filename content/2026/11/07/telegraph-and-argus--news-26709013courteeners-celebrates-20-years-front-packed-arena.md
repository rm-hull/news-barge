2006 – a year where social media giant Twitter was launched, Italy became football world champions and four childhood friends from Middleton, Manchester, created a band that would go on to define the Indie music scene for the next two decades.

Courteeners began their God Bless the Band November tour at Leeds' First Direct Arena on Friday performing to a packed out crowd featuring spectators of all ages.

And it is that feeling of timelessness which absorbed me.

From people who had witnessed the entirety of that 20-year journey to teenagers getting their first taste of the Liam Fray-written bangers.

Whisper it quietly but it was almost Oasis-like, albeit on a smaller scale.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21683846.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Courteeners are still going strong after two decades *(Image: Newsquest)*

## What songs do Courteeners play in their latest tour?

Anyway, to the music itself and the setlist was quite a different greatest hits selection.

As someone who has experienced the band live a dozen or so times, I was pleasantly surprised to see the likes of 'Winter Wonderland', 'Sycophant' and 'Beautiful Head' return to the line-up.

Although I did miss 'Modern Love', 'Summer' and 'Smith's Disco'.

The gig did not come without its issues, Fray himself admitted to a few technical problems, which can always happen at the start of a tour.

When addressing the matter, I feel he summed it up perfectly – "I hope we sound alright and the speakers are working. It’s only a gig, what are we obsessing about?"

Most of the crowd could not care less as they were fed a litany of sing along tunes – 'Are You in Love With a Notion?', 'Cavorting', 'Please Don’t', and 'Bide Your Time' the list goes on.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21683845.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The God Bless the Band tour is a accumulation of 20 years of hits *(Image: Newsquest)*

It is a down-to-earth mantra that is one of the frontman's most adhering qualities.

Entitled rockstar, Fray is not.

Again, this was shown when he stopped 'The 17th' after what looked like a medical incident in the standing area which forced the stewards to intervene.

The band briefly exited the stage before returning minutes later and performing the full song.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21683844.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Fray enjoys getting the crowd involved *(Image: Newsquest)*

## Another 20 years?

As the concert drew to a close, it was pleasing to see the Courteeners range.

"Hanging Off Your Cloud" is a criminally underrated song.

This was followed up by a cover of James' Sit Down – the fellow Mancunians have forever been a huge inspiration for Fray.

Of course, "Not Nineteen Forever" was played.

Fray remarked: "This is for anyone who has brought a mate who has never heard of us".

It’s a crying shame that this is the only song that has really reached that casual audience.

I’ve always felt the Courteeners’ back catalogue deserves more love.

Anyway, here’s to another 20 years!
