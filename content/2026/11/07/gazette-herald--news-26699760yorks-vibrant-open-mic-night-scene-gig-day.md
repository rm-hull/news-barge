*York's vibrant open mic night scene means you're never from live entertainment. Eddie Monkman reports.*

There’s an old saying that York has a pub for every day of the year. Whether it’s true or not is a different matter (it isn’t), but one thing is for certain in this city: you’re never far from an open mic night.

Each weeknight, venues throw open their doors to allow artists the chance to perform for free.

“Almost every week there is a standout act that leaves me thinking, wow, where have they come from?” said Alistair Lawrence, who runs the Snickleway Inn’s open mic in Goodramgate every Tuesday.

The Snickleway Inn attracts a “really diverse bunch of folk turning up”, he said.

Tried and tested crowd-pleasers are welcome – Oasis’ Wonderwall, Amy Winehouse’s Valerie, John Denver’s Take Me Home, Country Roads – but there is one exception: “I have banned House of the Rising Sun,” Alistair said.

One of the joys of York’s open mics is the diversity of styles and personalities that get up to perform to a room of strangers.

“We’ve got students [and] older folk, both in the audience and as performers,” Alistair said. “There is a real mix of all ages, and I’d say about 90 per cent are singers and musicians and then about 10 per cent [are] comedians, conjurors and poets.”

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21671406.jpg?type=mds-article-620)A busy Snickleway Inn during the open mic night *(Image: Eddie Monkman)*

Meanwhile in Bishophill, Tom Beer runs the open mic night on Mondays at the Golden Ball in Cromwell Road.

“I never know who is going to be around,” Tom said, adding: “I always look forward to the next time I’ll get to see something or someone who I have really enjoyed watching previously.”

The Golden Ball has hosted an array of talent – from as far as Iceland, Australia and the United States – since the open mic was first held in 2015.

It has also been a place for emerging bands to play when passing through the city, such as the Mercury Prize-nominated Sports Team.

But while open mic nights continue to draw in crowds and provide exposure for emerging talent, their existence shouldn’t be taken for granted, according to Alistair from the Snickleway Inn.

“Open mics are quite a thing in York really,” he said. “I’m worried it died off a bit through this year, but still, it’s quite buoyant.

“We’ve had an absolutely storming year – this week we had to start half an hour earlier just to fit all the acts in.”

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21671408.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Performer at the open mic night at the Snickleway Inn in Goodgramgate, York *(Image: Eddie Monkman)*

It’s also been busy at the Golden Ball, Tom said. "I can’t remember not having a full list, maybe ever.”

Tom and Alistair put the success of the open mic nights down to the diverse range of performances – but admit the offer of a free drink also helps.

“The bottom line is that every week there is something that I feel lucky to have seen,” Tom said. “And, perhaps more importantly, of all the musical moments of my life so far – including many gigs in venues and at festivals – many if not most of my favourites have been in the bar room at the open mic on a Monday night.”
