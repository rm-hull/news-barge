Scottish Enterprise has confirmed that a £7m loan to Sanjeev Gupta's Dalzell steelworks has been repaid in full.

News that the troubled tycoon had cleared his decade-long debt to the Scottish taxpayer comes as new documents released under freedom of information reveal that just weeks ago the Scottish Government believed there was a "high risk" the Motherwell plant could be called in by the official receiver.

A ministerial briefing for Economy Secretary Stephen Flynn shows he asked for talks with his UK counterpart about the impact of the insolvency of another of Mr Gupta's businesses.

The briefing, prepared for a Teams call with UK Reindustrialisation Minister Blair McDougall on September 4, said Mr Flynn had asked to meet him "given the high risk that the business could be called in by the official receiver as part of the insolvency administration process for Speciality Steel in Scarborough [sic], another GFG Group entity."

![Sanjeev Gupta](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/13214730.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Sanjeev Gupta has paid off the £7m loan to Scottish Enterprise *(Image: PA)*

It said Dalzell was operating intermittently because of market conditions, with staff on furlough, and that the public sector's exposure was the £7m loan from Scottish Enterprise to Liberty Steel Dalzell.

Officials suggested Mr Flynn ask about the UK Government's longer-term or strategic interests in the Dalzell site, and when British Steel at Scunthorpe, a supplier to Liberty, would be fully operational.

Speciality Steel UK, the Gupta steelmaker with sites in Rotherham and Stocksbridge, was put into liquidation in August last year. The UK Government has since paid its workers' wages at a cost of £3.5m a month.

Ten days after the call, on September 14, Business Secretary Jonathan Reynolds announced that Whitehall would work towards the public acquisition of the business, after concluding it could not support a sale to preferred bidder Blastr.

The briefing said Mr Flynn raised the risk with officials on August 26 and wrote to Mr McDougall that day to suggest a meeting.

Two days earlier, Lib Dem MSP Willie Rennie had written to him with seven questions about Dalzell, as The Herald revealed at the time. He told the minister his contacts had "warned that the £7 million owed to the Scottish Government was not spent on the Dalzell plant but was in fact transferred elsewhere".

Mr Flynn replied on October 13, seven weeks later and after his call with Mr McDougall. He said the condition of the plant, the sourcing of raw materials and the availability of fitters and electricians were matters for the company.

The letter came on the same day, creditors of Liberty Steel Newport, the first of Mr Gupta’s UK steel plants, appointed liquidators to oversee a wind-down of the business.

In his correspondence with the Lib Dem, Mr Flynn wrote: "The Scottish Government has not loaned money to Liberty Steel. Scottish Enterprise made a working capital loan to Liberty Steel in 2017 and Scottish Enterprise is responsible for monitoring that transaction."

Scottish Enterprise lent the money to Liberty Steel Dalzell in 2017, after ministers bought the Dalzell and Clydebridge mills from Tata Steel for £1 and immediately sold them on to Mr Gupta's Liberty House. It took security over the company, a legal claim over everything it owns that ranked it ahead of ordinary creditors if the loan was not repaid.

Ten days after Mr Flynn's letter, on October 23, a filing at Companies House said that security had been satisfied in full. A charge is marked "satisfied" when the debt behind it has been paid or otherwise discharged.

A Scottish Enterprise spokesman confirmed the loan had been repaid. He said: "I can confirm that Liberty Steel's outstanding debt to Scottish Enterprise has been settled in full. As a result, our charge was satisfied and security released."

As the charge was settled, the company gave security over all of its property to two other firms: Alvance British Aluminium Limited, which runs the Lochaber smelter, and Jahama P16 Limited. They were registered on October 27 and 28.

Dalzell has not filed accounts for five years. Mr Gupta is facing prosecution by Companies House over failures to file accounts across more than 70 entities, and the Serious Fraud Office's investigation into alleged fraud and money laundering within GFG and Greensill remains live.

The charges were created as the Scottish Government pursues GFG in court over alleged breaches of the terms of the Lochaber guarantee. Ministers have asked a judge for an interim interdict. GFG says the dispute relates to support the Lochaber smelter had been providing towards the restart of the Dalzell mill.

Mr Rennie said: "The web of companies run by Sanjeev Gupta is complex and tightly wound but in financial turmoil.

"The SNP is obviously embarrassed by how enthusiastically they partnered up with someone who has never delivered on his end of the bargain but that is no excuse for their lack of transparency.

"The Scottish and UK governments should set out the risks facing the Dalzell plant and commit to being more open with taxpayers about the risks to public funds, not least because if the worst were to happen and the plant were to close, the question of who pays the vast clean-up costs will rear its head again."

A UK Government spokesperson said: "We know this is a difficult time for the staff at Liberty Dalzell and the local community, and we want to see the site operational once more.

"This is separate to our work towards the public acquisition of Speciality Steel UK and we will continue to work with local leaders, workers, industry and investors to determine the best long-term future for these sites."

A Scottish Government Spokesperson said: “The Scottish Government recognises the importance of the Dalzell site to Scotland’s industrial landscape and therefore, rightly, sought engagement with the UK Government given the powers that they hold and their recent interventions in England.

“The Scottish Government remains in regular contact with GFG Alliance and has robust arrangements in place to monitor and manage its interests.”
