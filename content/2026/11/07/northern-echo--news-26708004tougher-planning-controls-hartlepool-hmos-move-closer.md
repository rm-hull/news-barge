Plans for tighter controls on new houses in multiple occupation in Hartlepool have moved a step closer.

Hartlepool Borough Council’s neighbourhood and regulatory services committee has recommended that full council adopt an Article 4 Direction across the borough.

It means that anyone intending to turn a family home into a house in multiple occupation (HMO) needs planning permission regardless of size.

Under permitted development rules smaller HMOs can be brought forward without planning permission and incoming Article 4 powers would essentially withdraw this automatic right.

A similar Article 4 Direction was recently implemented by the Hartlepool Development Corporation on areas of the town covered by the HDC.

The wider Article 4 direction, which was agreed on a ‘non-immediate’ basis by full council back in September, 2025, applies to the entire borough.

A statutory consultation has already taken place as well as an information gathering exercise which showed public support.

The council’s neighbourhood and regulatory services committee on November 4, recommended that full council confirm the direction.

Subject to approval by Hartlepool councillors, the planning powers are expected to come into force on December 1, 2026.

The report to the neighbourhood and regulatory services committee also referenced a recent motion referred from full council calling for progress on work relating to additional HMO licensing and selective landlord licensing.

Councillor Karen Oliver, while welcoming progress on the Article 4 Direction, stressed it was “not the end of the story” and said other licensing measures were “really critical” to tackle HMO issues in the town.

Council officers clarified that the committee report’s recommendations only addressed the Article 4 Direction but added councillors were due to receive a separate update on licensing in future.

A report to the committee said the HMO Article 4 Direction is considered necessary to “protect local amenity and the wellbeing of residents across the borough”.

It added: “Whilst the council is likely to be aware of the vast majority of the large HMOs above the planning threshold, there are concerns that all other smaller HMOs are not coming under the control and monitoring of planning.

“This could lead to a position whereby the true impact of HMOs is not being effectively considered and/or controlled and the harm to resident amenity and wellbeing is not properly understood and managed.

“Bringing in an Article 4 Direction would give the council more decision-making power and therefore greater potential to manage the overall quality, location and number of HMOs and better ensure residential amenity and wellbeing is maintained and/or enhanced.”
