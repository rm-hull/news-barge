## I was 'frozen' to -126°C: Inside York's first cryotherapy chamber

Reporter Maxine inside the cryotherapy chamber at Flourish in York

*I was frozen to -126°C in York's first cryotherapy chamber, by MAXINE GORDON*

IT'S not every day you get 'frozen' to -126°C; but Flourish in York is not your everyday kind of place.

This new health and wellbeing centre based at The White House, a stunning Grade II-listed Georgian building in Clifton, is anything but your run-of-the-mill fitness hub.

From the tree growing in the middle of its stylish cafe and its beetroot and ginger latte, to the reflexology facial menu, the pelvic floor chair and the sauna, plunge pool and floatation tank earmarked for its upper floors, it is safe to say that Flourish is giving the fitness sector a fresh look.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21675909.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Flourish at Clifton in York

And nothing is fresher than its cryotherapy chamber – a first for York – which invigorates the body with a two-minute blast of teeth-chattering temperatures that plummet as low as minus 140 degrees Celsius.

My fitness endeavours have included ice plunges and cold-water swimming – and hailing from Edinburgh, I've survived many a Scottish winter – but the prospect of locking myself in a cubicle as the temperature drops to a level below anywhere that naturally exists on earth, filled me, well, with the chills.

For context, a home freezer dips down to -18°C while the coldest temperature ever naturally recorded on Earth was about -89°C in Antarctica.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21675908.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Reporter Maxine inside the cryotherapy chamber at Flourish in York

A home freezer is chilly, but a cryotherapy chamber is nearly 40 degrees colder than the most brutally frozen place on the planet.

Sounds risky? That's what I thought until Vanessa Warn, one of the three co-founders of Flourish, explained how the dry air of the cryo chamber acted like a protective buffer, to give a massive, shocking rush of endorphins without actually freezing your tissue.

And I was wearing a pair of gloves, socks and fur-lined Crocs, so what was there to fear?

Summoning all my Girl Guide courage, I stepped into the tall grey cylinder, the doors of which opened in a blaze of white smoke in Stars-In-Their-Eyes style.

A platform raised me up until my head peeked out the top; a cape was placed around my shoulders to prevent fumes escaping and Vanessa pressed 'go'.

Videoing the experience (so you can see for yourself how I coped!), Vanessa gave a running commentary as the temperature quickly fell. I started deep yoga breathing to keep calm and stop panic flooding through me.

As the temperature reached -126°C, I was amazed I could tolerate it.

Yes, the skin on the outside of my arms and legs felt chilled, but my core still felt warm.

I've definitely felt more chilled on a winter's night in my Victorian house in York when the heating's gone off!

It was only in the last few seconds that I really started to feel icy.

As the session finished and I stepped out of the machine, I ran my hands over my calves to check they hadn't frozen solid.

They hadn't. Phew!

In fact, I didn't feel too cold at all. Instead, I felt awake – the sluggishness and sleepiness from lunch and my earlier treatments at Flourish (more of which later), had evaporated as quickly as the dense white fog from the cryotherapy chamber.

I felt re-energised with enough spring in my step for the 30-minute walk home and a game of tennis later that night.

I also slept like a log right through to the next morning – and felt alert and enthusiastic for the rest of the day.

Vanessa said this was to be expected.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21675915.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Co-founder Vanessa Warn at the cryotherapy chamber at Flourish in York

"It is fantastic for your body. Your body will be in shock and will release a load of red blood platelets to reduce inflammation and boost your immunity.

"It gives you such a buzz and continues working for 12 to 24 hours after you finish."

A two or three minute blast in the cryo chamber doesn't come cheap: prices start at £45. Vanessa said it was particularly useful for recovery after extreme exertion such as a marathon.

I found it revived me after my 'marathon' visit to Flourish, which gave me a taster session of what the centre has to offer.

I began with a beginner's session of Reformer Pilates with Flourish co-founder Stacey Dix. I've done mat-based Pilates for 30 years, but this was another level of challenge.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21675910.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Reporter Maxine Gordon tries Reformer Pilates and has a work out at the Flourish gym

Using a Reformer machine mounted on a moving carriage with ropes and pulleys, each exercise is performed against resistance while encouraging correct posture and precise movement.

My thigh muscles were still feeling the effects of the workout days later.

Next, I had a short personal training session with Claire Riddell, the third co-founder at Flourish who began her career as a midwife. Again, I tried several new things, including working out on a rebounder (a small trampoline) in front of a red heat lamp in the Flourish gym.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21675913.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Personal trainer and Flourish co-founder Claire Riddell

A range of classes including yoga, body combat, stretch, circuits, are all timetabled at Flourish on a pay-as-you-go basis. Membership packages will be available soon.

Therapist Zoe Patterson practices reflexology and Reiki and Stephanie Daniel will be offering reflexology facials too.

I had half-an-hour treatments with each of them and felt thoroughly pampered and relaxed from my head to my toes.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21675911.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Therapists at Flourish - Stephanie Daniel will be offering reflexology facials and Zoe Patterson practices reflexology and Reiki

The Flourish cafe – with a take-out window at the side of the building – is open to the general public and is worth a visit if you want some nourishing and home-made food.

During my visit, I enjoyed a lunch of lamb kofta with a selection of healthy salads as well as a surprisingly moreish beetroot and ginger latte (a novel experience for me and one thoroughly recommended).

The coffee was delicious too and comes from Exhale which uses a special process to boost its nutrient qualities.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21675914.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Healthy lunch and a beetroot at ginger latte at Flourish

Stacey said: "One cup of coffee gives you the same amount of anti-oxidants as 1.8kg of blueberries."

If that's not a reason to order a second flat white, I don't know what is. After all, this was the place that persuaded me stepping into a chamber colder than Antarctica was a good idea. And they weren't wrong.
