**Edinburgh University paid an external consultancy firm more than £400,000 to help develop controversial restructuring plans.**

According to a Freedom of Information request, the university entered into a ‘Workshop Facilitation’ contract with PA Consulting on January 21, 2026, paying the firm a total of £435,660.75 as of September 23.

The Herald previously reported that the university had paid PA an estimated £200,000 this spring, and that the firm’s work in the College of Arts, Humanities and Social Sciences (CAHSS) had been subsequently extended to Edinburgh’s two other colleges.

In mid-September, The Herald revealed that 90 programmes in CAHSS would close to new enrolments from 2027, sparking anger and concern from staff and students at Edinburgh.

The FOI request provided the titles of reports sent to the university by PA over the last ten months.

![Edinburgh University students. Picture: Gordon Terris](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/20062369.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Students at Edinburgh University will have 90 fewer programmes to choose from after 2027. *(Image: Gordon Terris)*

These provide a rough sketch of the restructuring programme, showing how the firm provided weekly updates on their work in CAHSS throughout the winter and early spring.

Reports sent to the university include “CAHSS AS&S Roadmap v0.3” and “CAHSS Programme Delivery Plan Draft v.0.5”.

Similarly, a number of "financial impact” reports sent by PA refer to schools later affected by programme closures, including the Edinburgh College of Art, the School of Social and Political Science, and the School of Literatures, Languages and Cultures.

The FOI request confirms that PA has been involved in developing restructuring plans for the university’s College of Science and Engineering (CSE) and College of Medicine and Veterinary Medicine (CMVM).

For example, the firm sent one report to managers entitled “CSE & CMVM workshop schedule” and another entitled “CSE & CMVM project plan”.

Subject-specific reports include “programme course analysis” for Biology, Physics, Informatics, Engineering, Geosciences, Mathematics, and Chemistry, among others.

In June, The Herald reported that PA’s role had been expanded into all three colleges.

“External support to help develop a cost model for CAHSS, as part of their Academic Size and Shape activity, will now be extended to CSE and CMVM,” university principal Sir Peter Mathieson and provost Kim Graham told staff.

“This aims to ensure consistency of approach across our colleges.

“The college leadership teams will work with external contractors to ensure this work builds on academic reshaping work already underway in CSE and CMVM.”

In April, sources told The Herald that the university had paid PA around £200,000 for their work in CAHSS.

The Herald understands that PA’s scope of work includes facilitating business transformation workshops with members of the university’s academic and professional services teams.

After each workshop, the firm is required to make recommendations to inform the university’s leadership team as restructuring work continues.

![University College Union (UCU) members and student supporters from universities across Scotland take part in a rally against cuts and job losses in universities outside the Scottish Parliament.](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21529900.jpg?type=mds-article-620)University College Union (UCU) members went on strike for five weeks last month. *(Image: Jane Barlow/PA Wire)*

A University of Edinburgh spokesperson said: “The university is a large and complex organisation, currently undertaking a number of projects designed to support a strong, sustainable and academically ambitious institution for our students and staff, both now and into the future.

“We have been open about using external support for the work underway to reshape the university. Building on existing work, consultants have worked closely with leaders within each college, who have first-hand knowledge and understanding of their respective priorities and requirements."

PA Consulting declined to comment.

The UCU has criticised the findings.

"It is worth remembering that the initial figure for PA Consulting involvement was said to be approximately £200,000," said branch co-vice presidents Dr Marion Lieutaud and Dr Courtney Stafford-Walter.

"As is typical of senior managements’ pet projects though, the budget inflated massively and is now, only 9 months in, more than double the figure initially mentioned to staff.

"As much as the university senior management tries to redirect attention and to present their programme of job cuts as an inevitable and mechanical result of external financial pressures, the reality is that are clearly choices being made about what expenses are worthwhile, and where the very large income of the university should go: we have yet to see any shortage of money when it comes to consultancies, high-end salaries and capital expenditure projects."

The pair said that the total sum is "equivalent" to the £440,000 cost savings recently announced by the School of Social and Political Science (SPS), which will cut nearly all of its tutor positions.

Dr Stafford-Walter and Dr Lieutaud added:

"Independent reviews of restructuring of higher education elsewhere - e.g. in New South Wales - have established that the use of consultants were part of systematic governance abuse and played a key role in the production of the false narrative that 'there is no other option' to job cuts.

"In fact, as a parliamentary review showed, financially viable alternatives were available, which would have avoided job losses; but these were dismissed in favour of mass redundancy plans.

"Ultimately, it always boils down to what the university senior management prioritises, and at Edinburgh University, it is never the staff or the students."
