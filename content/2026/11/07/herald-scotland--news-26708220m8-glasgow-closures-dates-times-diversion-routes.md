Drivers are being warned to expect significant delays on Scotland’s busiest motorway as the first of two weekends of closures gets under way in Glasgow.

Motorists are also facing disruption near Glasgow Airport, with a separate closure of the A737 St James’ Viaduct eastbound link to the M8 at Junction 28A now in place.

The rescheduled closure began at 8pm on Friday, November 6, and is due to remain in place until 6am on Tuesday, November 10.

Sections of the M8 will shut in both directions to allow two refurbished pedestrian bridges to be lifted back into position, with disruption also expected on surrounding roads.

The M8 will be closed in both directions in Glasgow between Junction 19 (Anderston) and Junction 24 (Helen Street).

The first closure will begin at 6pm on Saturday, November 7, and remain in place until 1pm on Sunday, November 8, affecting the motorway between Junction 22 and Junction 24 at Helen Street.

The closures are required to allow the Kirkwood Street and Cornwall Street footbridges, south-west of Glasgow city centre, to be reinstated following refurbishment.

Motorists will be diverted through the south side of Glasgow, with routes taking in Paisley Road West, Paisley Road, Helen Street and Dumbreck Road before drivers can rejoin the motorway network.

A separate closure is also in place on the A737 St James’ Viaduct eastbound link to the M8 at Junction 28A.

The closure, which has been rescheduled, began at 8pm on Friday, November 6, and will continue until 6am on Tuesday, November 10.

Drivers using the affected link will need to follow the signed diversion route.

The closure is separate from the M8 footbridge works but overlaps with the first weekend of restrictions, potentially adding to disruption for motorists travelling through the west of Glasgow and towards the airport.

Amey, which is carrying out the works on behalf of Transport Scotland, said motorists are "strongly advised to avoid the area" while the closures are in place.

There are concerns that the diversions could lead to substantial congestion, with similar engineering works in 2024 resulting in delays of up to an hour.

Officials have warned that comparable disruption could occur if large numbers of drivers attempt to use the diversion routes.

Emergency vehicles will also be unable to travel through the closed sections of motorway during the operation.

The disruption is expected to affect thousands of people travelling to major sporting and entertainment events across Glasgow and the west of Scotland.

Rangers supporters heading to Paisley for their team's Premiership clash with St Mirren at 8pm on Saturday could face delays, while Celtic are due to play at home at 4pm on Sunday.

The OVO Hydro will also host the Bare Knuckle Fighting Championship on Saturday evening, with concertgoers travelling to see singer Beabadoobee at the venue on November 14 also likely to be affected by the second weekend of closures.

Glasgow's SEC campus, which includes the Hydro, has already issued a warning to visitors travelling to events on both weekends to anticipate delays and disruption.

The motorway works will coincide with further transport disruption in Glasgow, with weekend closures affecting the city's Subway network from the following weekend.

The bridge installation will involve large cranes lifting the refurbished structures into position above the motorway.

However, the operation is dependent on favourable weather, with strong winds or heavy rain potentially forcing engineers to postpone the works.

A contingency weekend has been set aside for November 21 and 22 should conditions prevent the lifts from going ahead as planned.

Although the bridges will be returned to their positions above the M8 this month, they will not immediately reopen to pedestrians and cyclists.

Further work will be required before they can be brought back into use by the end of February.

The refurbishment programme includes new bridge surfaces, improved drainage and higher safety barriers designed to provide greater protection for cyclists.

Motorists are being urged to plan journeys carefully, allow additional travelling time and consider alternative routes to avoid the affected motorway sections.

Drivers are also advised to check Traffic Scotland for the latest information on the additional A737 closure, which is scheduled to remain in place until Tuesday morning.
