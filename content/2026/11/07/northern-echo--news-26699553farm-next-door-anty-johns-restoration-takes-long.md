Amanda Owen's ambitious restoration of a historic Yorkshire Dales farmhouse has become one of the most popular features of Our Farm Next Door.

But after years of building work, viewers may be wondering why Anty John's has taken so long to transform into a family home.

Amanda and former husband Clive purchased the building in 2020, with the intention of bringing it back into use for their family's future.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21671057.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Anty John’s Aerial View *(Image: CHANNEL 4)*

However, Anty John's was far from ready to be lived in.

By the time the Owens acquired it, the property was in a semi-derelict condition.

Parts of the roof had collapsed, while the interior required extensive work before the building could function as a home.

The farmhouse is also Grade II listed, having received the designation in 1986.

That status recognises its historic significance and places additional considerations on alterations to the property.

Planning permission for its conversion into a two-bedroom home was granted in 2022.

But obtaining permission was only one stage of the process.

The family has since worked alongside local builders and tradespeople to repair the structure and gradually create usable living spaces.

Their efforts have been documented across four series of Our Farm Next Door: Amanda, Clive and Kids.

The programme has shown the family dealing with the practical difficulties of renovating a centuries-old building in a remote part of North Yorkshire.

Unlike a modern property, Anty John's required considerable attention to its existing structure and traditional features.

The restoration has also had to fit around everyday life at Ravenseat.

Amanda, Clive and their children continue to manage livestock and carry out the demanding seasonal work associated with hill farming.

During the fourth series, the family made progress with interior work, including flooring, plastering and decorating.

Amanda and the girls also spent their first night at the property, marking a significant milestone after years of restoration.

However, the project has not yet been presented as fully complete.

On November 2, Channel 4 confirmed that the programme would return for a fifth and sixth series.
