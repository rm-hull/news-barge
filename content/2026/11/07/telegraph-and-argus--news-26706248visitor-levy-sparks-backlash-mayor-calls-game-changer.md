A proposed overnight visitor levy has sparked a backlash, with fears it could deter tourists and harm the hospitality sector.

The criticism follows comments by West Yorkshire Mayor Tracy Brabin, who described the levy as a "game changer" for regional investment and growth.

The proposed levy, which would give regional leaders the power to introduce a levy on overnight visitors, would allow elected mayors and combined authorities in England to charge a percentage-based fee on overnight stays, with funds reinvested locally.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/13257193.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Tracy Brabin, Mayor of West Yorkshire

Ms Brabin said: "Bradford’s year as UK City of Culture proved what we can achieve when we invest in our visitor economy, which is why I’m supportive of these powers to ask those who stay overnight to make a small contribution to support our great region.

"Combined with keeping a share of income tax and business rates, an Overnight Visitor Levy could be a game changer for West Yorkshire.

"Money raised and spent here, without interference from Whitehall, means we can deliver further and faster on safer streets, better transport, more vibrant communities and more to do across our region.

"A small contribution from visitors can help build a brighter West Yorkshire that works for all."

But social media users fear the levy could leave visitors with less to spend in cafes, museums and shops.

One commentator said: "How much is a small contribution?

"When travelling, I have a fixed travel budget.

"Any tourist levy just means I have less to spend directly in the local economy—affecting your local cafes, pubs, museums etc.

"Taxing people slows growth; it doesn't build it."

Another said: "Economic stupidity. You want hotels and cafes and pubs and leisure facilities to get more visitors. Let’s make it more expensive. What can possibly go wrong?"

A third wrote: "Ok, crossing you off our destination list. We won’t pay a visitor tax from taxed money to stay in a taxed hotel with taxed money."

Other regional leaders have backed the plan despite the backlash.

David Skaith, Mayor of York and North Yorkshire, said: "We’re home to historic towns and cities, two of the UK’s best National Parks and a coastline brimming with character.

"There’s something for everyone, and we welcome more than 40 million visitors every year.

"That’s why an Overnight Visitor Levy will be a total gamechanger for our county.

"Investing in York and North Yorkshire’s cities, towns and villages to make them a better place to visit, and to live in, is at the heart of why I will be introducing a levy in our county."

The levy would apply to the first 28 consecutive days of a stay, with longer stays exempt.

Authorities would have to consult businesses and residents, publish a draft plan and impact assessment, and report each year on revenue and spending.

The scheme would exempt student accommodation, care homes and shelters, while allowing further local exemptions such as camping pitches.
