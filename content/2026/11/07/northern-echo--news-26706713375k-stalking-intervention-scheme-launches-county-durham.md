A NEW programme will target early-stage stalking behaviour to prevent further harm to victims.

In County Durham and Darlington, the EASI (Early Awareness Stalking Intervention) scheme will be funded by £374,936 of Government funding secured by Police and Crime Commissioner Joy Allen.

It forms part of Ms Allen’s prevention-first approach to tackling crime and supporting victims.

She said: "Stalking has a debilitating and profound emotional impact on victims and is treated very seriously in Durham.

"We must do everything in our power to protect future victims from these appalling crimes but this can’t be achieved with punitive measures alone; we must tackle the source of the offending and work directly with offenders to achieve lasting behavioural change to reduce the risk they pose to future victims and our communities."

The EASI programme takes a public health approach to stalking, aiming to identify and intervene in early-stage behaviours before they escalate.

It will focus on adults who commit a first, lower-risk stalking offence and who acknowledge their actions.

Over the course of six sessions, individuals will work with a specialist clinical or forensic psychologist to address issues of fixation, obsession and emotional regulation.

The goal is to reduce unwanted contact, harassment, monitoring and repeat offending, while improving accountability and victim safety.

The programme will be delivered by the County Durham and Darlington Stalking Intervention Partnership (D-SIP), a multi-agency collaboration including the Commissioner’s office, Durham Constabulary, North-East Probation Service, Tees, Esk and Wear Valleys NHS Foundation Trust, and victim care services.

It also involves members of the National Stalking Consortium, including the Alice Ruggles Trust and Paladin.

Eligible participants will be identified through daily police screening and may enter the programme through a Stalking Prevention Order, conditional caution or post-conviction licence condition.

Higher-risk individuals will continue to be managed through existing safeguarding and criminal justice processes.

A multi-agency D-SIP panel will coordinate safeguarding, risk management and additional support for those involved.

Ms Allen said: "This is not about replacing enforcement.

"Perpetrators must be relentlessly pursued and held accountable for their crimes.

"It is about making our criminal justice sanctions more meaningful and working more holistically to achieve better outcomes in the long-term and ultimately saving and protecting lives."
