Wintry showers could arrive in northern Scotland next week as meteorologists monitor an unusually early disruption to the polar vortex which could increase the risk of colder weather later this month.

Scotland could face an increased risk of freezing temperatures and wintry weather later this month as an unusually early weakening of the polar vortex threatens to allow Arctic air to move south.

The Met Office has warned that changes taking place high above the North Pole could increase the likelihood of a cold snap across Scotland and the wider UK in the coming weeks.

Forecasters have also identified the possibility of wintry showers in the far north of Scotland next week, although there is considerable uncertainty about whether the atmospheric disruption will bring a more prolonged spell of cold weather.

The polar vortex is a ring of powerful winds circulating high above the Arctic which helps contain extremely cold air around the North Pole.

When those winds weaken, they can disrupt the jet stream and alter weather patterns across northern Europe, increasing the possibility of Arctic air reaching Britain.

Met Office forecaster Marco Petagna said: “At the moment, we’ve got a warming taking place in the stratosphere.

“The polar vortex in the stratosphere is starting to weaken.”

If the winds surrounding the polar vortex reverse direction, the disruption would be classified as a major sudden stratospheric warming event.

Such an event would be exceptionally early in the winter season and could be among the earliest recorded.

The Met Office said forecasts from the European Centre for Medium-Range Weather Forecasts suggest the winds could briefly reverse during the coming week, although it remains uncertain whether the conditions for a major warming event will be met.

Mr Petagna said: “For the UK, it does increase the chance of cold weather, normally a few weeks down the line, so sort of deeper into November is when we’re more likely to see an influence if it does happen.

“It’s important to emphasise that there was a major warming event last November, and on that occasion it didn’t directly lead to much cold weather in the UK.

“It doesn’t always happen, it just increases the chance of it occurring.”

Scotland's northern location means Arctic outbreaks can bring wintry conditions to parts of the country, particularly higher ground, even when temperatures remain relatively mild elsewhere in Britain.

The Met Office's latest outlook, covering November 11 to 20, suggests a few wintry showers are likely in the far north of the UK as clearer and colder conditions spread southwards.

However, much of Scotland is expected to experience a relatively settled start to next week.

In the Highlands and Western Isles, conditions are forecast to remain largely dry through Monday and Tuesday, with bright or sunny spells and the possibility of overnight frost.

Aberdeen, Dundee, Perth and surrounding areas are also expected to enjoy some drier and brighter weather, although frost and fog could develop where skies clear.

Daytime temperatures across large parts of Scotland are expected to reach around 10C this weekend.

The outlook becomes more changeable later next week, with Atlantic weather systems expected to bring further spells of rain and potentially strong winds across the UK.

Despite the possibility of an Arctic incursion, the Met Office's longer-range forecast currently suggests temperatures are likely to remain above average overall during the second half of November and into early December.

Meteorologists have stressed that a sudden stratospheric warming does not automatically result in widespread snow or freezing conditions.

In an analysis published this week, the Met Office said the effects of such an event can sometimes take between two and six weeks to influence weather nearer the Earth's surface.

The impact depends on how atmospheric pressure systems develop across the North Atlantic and Europe.

High pressure to the north of Britain could encourage colder easterly winds, while blocking conditions to the west could increase the likelihood of northerly winds bringing Arctic air towards Scotland.

However, the same disruption could also result in relatively dry and settled weather, with frost and fog rather than widespread snowfall.

The Met Office has also identified a possible connection between the current disruption and the unusually strong El Niño event in the tropical Pacific.

El Niño can influence large-scale atmospheric waves which travel towards the Arctic and disturb the circulation of winds around the polar vortex.

Forecasters say they will continue monitoring developments over the coming days to establish whether the weakening vortex will have any significant impact on Scotland's weather later this month.

Elsewhere in the UK, a yellow warning for fog has been issued across parts of southern and central England on Sunday morning, potentially affecting motorists travelling to Remembrance services.

Visibility could fall below 50 metres in places before conditions gradually improve through the day.
