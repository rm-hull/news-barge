A powerful community artwork to commemorate sexual violence and domestic abuse survivors will form the centrepiece of an awareness event in Middlesbrough this month.

Middlesbrough Council is partnering with White Ribbon UK to mark National White Ribbon Day on November 25, hosting an event to raise awareness about sexual violence and domestic abuse against women and girls.

The event will be hosted at The Fire Station, Middlesbrough Town Hall, featuring survivors, local support organisations and a collaborative art project designed to leave a lasting symbol of healing, solidarity and community action.

The event will coincide with the UN International Day for the Elimination of Violence against Women, running from 1.30pm to 4.30pm and offering attendees a chance to discuss how to best prevent sexual violence and domestic abuse.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21681665.jpg?type=mds-article-620)White Ribbon Day's theme for this year is 'It Takes All of Us.' *(Image: White Ribbon UK)*

White Ribbon UK is a charity which works with individuals, workplaces and communities to challenge attitudes and behaviours which make violence against women and girls possible.

White Ribbon Day's theme for this year is 'It Takes All of Us.'

Members of the public and professionals will be able to learn from survivors and support organisations about specialist violence against women and girls services, and the part individuals can play in preventing abuse.

Teesside-based artists Claire Harrison and Beth Johnson will lead a collaborative art activity.

Attendees will be asked to each decorate a tile for a large mosaic mural, which will serve as a collective symbol of healing, solidarity and collaboration, while reflecting the experiences of survivors of violence against women and girls who are rebuilding their lives.

Three sessions will be available over the afternoon, with those interested asked to book places online through Middlesbrough Council's website.

Middlesbrough Mayor Chris Cooke said: “On this White Ribbon Day we are encouraging everyone to make the White Ribbon Promise never to commit, excuse or remain silent about violence against women.

“As a Council, we are committed to doing everything within our power to help prevent violence against women and girls by challenging harmful attitudes and behaviours, tackling misogyny and promoting equality and respect.”

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/18519972.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Chris Cooke said Middlesbrough Council was "working across all departments and with our partners to raise awareness, drive cultural change and create safer communities for women and girls." *(Image: MIDDLESBROUGH COUNCIL)*

Cooke added: “We are working across all departments and with our partners to raise awareness, drive cultural change and create safer communities for women and girls.

“We all have a role to play in challenging unacceptable behaviour, supporting those affected and helping to end violence against women and girls.”

The Cleveland Police area, which includes Middlesbrough, has one of the highest rates of violence against women and girls in the UK.

Middlesbrough Council was granted White Ribbon status in 2016 after pledging to work with Middlesbrough FC, Cleveland Police and Teesside schools to raise awareness.

Claire Moore, Domestic Abuse Strategic Lead at Middlesbrough Council, said the event was a "powerful reminder that tackling violence against women and girls was not something we can do on our own."

She added that council wanted to create a "lasting legacy" to survivors of sexual violence and domestic abuse.

Alongside the event, White Ribbon and Middlesbrough Council have devised a three-year action plan against sexual violence and domestic abuse, which includes campaigns, training and events.

It aims to create cultural change across council services, its workforce and local communities.

Anyone who would like to offer assistance with the November 25 event is asked to email [whiteribbon@middlesbrough.gov.uk](mailto:whiteribbon@middlesbrough.gov.uk)
