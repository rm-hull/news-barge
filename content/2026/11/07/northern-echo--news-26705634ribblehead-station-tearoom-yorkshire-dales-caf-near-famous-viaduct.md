A tiny café tucked away inside a remote Yorkshire Dales railway station is welcoming walkers and visitors after receiving rave reviews.

Ribblehead Station Tearoom sits on the famous Settle-Carlisle Railway, surrounded by some of North Yorkshire's most dramatic countryside.

Just a ten-minute walk from the world-famous Ribblehead Viaduct, the café offers an unusual place to enjoy homemade food, cakes and hot drinks while exploring the Yorkshire Dales National Park.

And despite its isolated location, the historic station has become a destination in its own right for railway enthusiasts, walkers and visitors exploring the Yorkshire Three Peaks.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21679193.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Ribblehead Station Tearoom *(Image: TRIPADVISOR)*

The tearoom reopened earlier this year following a £60,000 refurbishment, with improvements including underfloor heating, a new kitchen and traditional Yorkshire stone flooring.

The official Settle-Carlisle Railway website said the improvements included "better insulation, enhanced internet connectivity and a new menu featuring food prepared on site or supplied by local producers."

The tearoom began operating under its new arrangements in April, before being formally launched in July as the railway celebrated 150 years of passenger services.

Ribblehead Station stands in open countryside between Ingleborough, Whernside and Pen-y-ghent, three of Yorkshire's most famous peaks.

The surrounding landscape attracts walkers throughout the year, many of whom visit the nearby viaduct.

The Grade II\*-listed structure, with its 24 enormous arches, carries trains across Batty Moss and is among the most recognisable railway landmarks in Britain.

The café serves hot and cold sandwiches, light snacks, homemade sweet treats and drinks, with an emphasis on local ingredients.

There is also a small shop selling railway-themed gifts, books, maps and souvenirs.

But the station offers more than somewhere to stop for lunch.

Inside the building is a free visitor centre telling the story of the Settle-Carlisle Railway and the people who built it.

Displays include railway memorabilia and original features from the station, giving visitors an insight into the line's history.

The station itself has a remarkable past, having closed in 1970 before passenger services returned, while restoration work eventually transformed the deteriorating building into a heritage attraction.

Visitors are advised to check ahead because winter weather can affect opening arrangements and times.
