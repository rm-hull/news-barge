A 'reckless' teenager ran red lights and narrowly avoided a crash as he led police on a 100mph chase in a 30mph zone.

Lewis Naisbitt, of Middlesbrough, weaved through the streets of Carlisle at high speed then sped up the hard shoulder of the M6 being being stung by officers.

The court heard how he drove a suspected cloned Nissan X-Trail from Scotland to Cumbria on September 9.

When officers attempted to stop the vehicle, Naisbitt failed to pull over and instead came off the motorway before leading police on a high-speed chase through Carlisle.

He hit more than 60mph in a 30mph zone before weaving through traffic and running red lights.

Naisbitt, 19, then reached speeds of 100mph in another 30mph zone, narrowly avoiding a crash with an oncoming vehicle.

The pursuit continued onto the M6, where he drove between 100mph and 115mph and undertook vehicles using the hard shoulder.

Police eventually deployed a stinger after Naisbitt, of Sunningdale Road, left the motorway.

He then crashed into a parked HGV.

Despite the collision, Naisbitt attempted to flee on foot but was tackled and arrested by officers.

He admitted dangerous driving, driving while disqualified, driving without insurance, fraudulent use of a registration mark or document, and failing to comply with a roadside drugs wipe.

Appearing at Carlisle Crown Court on Friday, November 6, he was sentenced to 14 months in a young offenders' institution and banned from driving for two years following his release.

Chief Inspector Sam Turner of Cumbria Police said: "The driver's behaviour was incredibly reckless and could easily have resulted in him causing serious injury to himself and others.

"However, thanks to the professionalism and teamwork of officers, led by the force's operations team, the pursuit was brought to a safe conclusion and the driver arrested."
