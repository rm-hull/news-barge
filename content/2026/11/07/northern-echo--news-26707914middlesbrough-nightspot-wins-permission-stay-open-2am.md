A North East venue will be allowed to stay open until the early hours of Sunday morning.

The Den, located on Baker Street in Middlesbrough town centre, has received the green light from the council to remain open until 2am on Sunday November 8, as councillors decided to side with the local business, over the council’s own objections.

The decision notice details how on Sunday, October 11, an application was made by Adam Duce, on behalf of The Den, for a Temporary Event Notice (TEN), to extend the time for “licensable activities” – the sale of alcohol and provision of regulated entertainment.

The application sought an extra two hours, from midnight to 2am, to lengthen Saturday night’s event on November 7 into the morning of Sunday November 8.

On Monday October 12, Middlesbrough Council’s Emma Tindall submitted an objection, on behalf of the authority’s environmental protection service, on the grounds that the TEN would undermine the licensing objectives, specifically that of public nuisance. This resulted in a meeting of the licensing sub-committee on Monday, October 26.

It was up to the committee to decide whether they would impose conditions which already apply to an existing premises licence or issue a counter notice to prevent the event going ahead.

The Den’s website describes the venue as “an underground music venue and creative space built for DJs, producers, and live performers”.

Dan O’Leary, co-owner of The Den, spoke at the meeting on Monday, October 26. He explained that since taking over the venue, they have implemented various measures to try and improve the sound system to reduce the amount of noise emitted from the building.

He told councillors that they have consulted a sound engineer to review the sound system to ensure that frequencies do not bounce to the front of the building and have also implemented a sound decibel meter in the corner of the room to ensure that the sound never goes above the levels set in collaboration with the council.

The changes made at The Den have limited the rattling of the windows and doors, the committee heard. Further measures such as secondary glazing are being considered with a view to implementation in the future, if operations are to continue at the premises.

Councillors were also shown a video and played sound recordings from early May 2026, which highlight high levels of noise at 1.30am, 2.45am and 3.02am.

The concerns were not just limited to noise escaping the premises from the music, but also around patrons on the street which caused disturbances to local residents.

Mr O’Leary responded to the concerns and videos presented by the objectors, questioning the accuracy and reliability of the recordings, arguing that there is no certainty that they came from The Den.

The committee also heard from Tim Hodgkinson, licensing manager at Middlesbrough Council. Mr Hodgkinson made two visits, in July and August, to the Baker Street area, during which he had taken recordings to demonstrate the impact the noise from the premises on the street is having on local residents.

The findings section of the report details how councillors found that there had been noise issues in the past on the street, relating not just to The Den.

The committee further determined, following consideration of Mr Hodgkinson’s representations, that on at least one of the occasions it was neighbouring premises that were the cause of the noise issues.

The committee did find that breaches of The Den’s licence had occurred, as evidenced by Mr Hodgkinson and the concession of Mr O’Leary.

The committee considered that this was accepted by Mr O’Leary, who explained that they were unaware of the licensable hours. Whilst the committee noted it is the responsibility of premises owners to be aware of such hours, The Den did seek to rectify this issue with a minor variation to its licence.

The licensing sub-committee decided to grant the TEN, allowing The Den to operate until 2am on Sunday, November 8, adding that the premises must provide a door supervisor as per the licence, as this is expected to be a busy event.

Commenting in response to the decision, co-owners Adam Duce and Dan O’Leary, said: “We’re really pleased with the decision and appreciate the licensing committee taking the time to consider all the evidence we put forward.

We’ve put a lot of time, money and effort into creating The Den, and our aim has always been to bring something different to Middlesbrough’s nightlife and electronic music scene.

This is a positive step forward for us, and we’re looking forward to showing that we can operate later events safely and responsibly. Hopefully, it’s the start of more opportunities for The Den and the local music scene.”
