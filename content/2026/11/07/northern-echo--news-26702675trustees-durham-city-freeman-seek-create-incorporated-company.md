Plans to amend the future operation of the Durham City Freemen has caused a division of opinions between its leadership and some members of the long-standing trade guilds.

Amid a rash of resignations of post-holders, the Trustees and Wardens of Durham City Freemen have written to members of the guilds to outline their position.

The Trustees said the “important announcement” was intended to inform members about “ongoing discussions” as to how the Freemen may operate, “in the most appropriate and robust way in future”.

But elements of the rank and file believe the suggested changes threaten their democratic base, which they say has been largely unchanged since the Middle Ages.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21675458.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Dave Howard, left, and Alan Metcalf, two of the wardens who have resigned from their posts in the light of proposed changes in the running of Durham City Freemen *(Image: Geoff Kitson)*

The Durham City Freemen is now an unincorporated association of individuals whose assets are managed by a board of Trustees for the benefit of the Freemen.

But the Trustees say changes in legislation mean there are no longer any documented provisions as to how the trust arrangement was to work.

The Trustees newsletter stated: “The current situation is clearly not the optimum way in which a trust should be governed in today’s world”.

Due to what they say are the “operational impact”, on issues like banking arrangements, the Trustees said they have taken legal advice on how best to formalise the arrangements, “to make them fit for current and future purposes.”

The Trustees said they have approached the wardens for their views on the proposal to replace the current trust arrangement with a newly incorporated trust company, to act as the Trustee of the Durham City Freemen.

Should it be approved, there would initially be eight directors of the new company, half to be current Trustees and the other four to be Freemen.

The four Freemen directors would include the Wardens’ chair and three others elected by the Wardens and Deputy Wardens from within their ranks.

The Trustees say the change will bring a number of benefits as a legal company would hold the assets of the Durham City Freemen and enter into contracts on its own behalf, making it easier to deal with third parties, including, “importantly”, the banks.

They say it will add “clarity and modernise” what it terms “the out-dated, undocumented arrangements”, replacing them with, “improved governance and stronger risk management.”

The Trustees’ statement pledged the proposed changes would not mean, “the historic customs, rights, ceremonies or traditions of the Durham City Freemen will be abandoned”, adding it would “preserve the title and status of the Durham City Freemen, retain the traditional membership criteria, continue with the traditional ceremonial practices and retain the historic objectives of the Durham City Freemen.”

The Freemen’s eight wardens, who lead the 270 sworn men and women making up the surviving craft guilds, have twice unanimously rejected the trustees’ idea, insisting it would create not only, “a conflict of interest but pose a major threat to centuries-old custom and practice which have proved the cornerstone of their survival and continuing success.”

In the aftermath of a previous contentious meeting on the issue, in late September, Alan Metcalf, leader of the Cordwainers’ Guild and a former chairman of wardens, and Dave Howard, warden of the Drapers’ Guild resigned, joining eight others who stepped down, among them another two wardens, three deputies and three specialist appointees.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21675459.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Dave Howard has voiced concerns about proposed changes to the running of Durham City Freemen *(Image: Geoff Kitson)*

In a joint statement the duo, who represent 90 members, said the membership “must not be kept in the dark” about such major changes.

“A member’s vote must be taken before wardens and trustees appear from behind closed doors with a fait accompli threatening the future of our in-house democracy

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21675460.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Alan Metcalf has added his voice to opposition to proposed changes in the governance of Durham City Freemen *(Image: Geoff Kitson)*

“If the suggested plan goes ahead the wardens left outside the boardroom risk being disenfranchised and effectively side-lined.

“The number of resignations testifies to the fact something is seriously amiss.

“We are being asked to surrender a successful democratic system, tried and tested over many centuries which helped make the city what it is today.

“It has survived because of its strength, a unified system, applying tight membership controls working in the best interests of the city’s craftsmen and apprentices.”

The pair added the proposed limited company “divides us”, and, “with the best will in the world, there will be times when Freemen on the board will face conflicts of interest which will risk destroying what has been so faithfully built.

“Where will their oath leave them then?”

The members have recently opened a new WhatsApp group, “run by members for members”.

In light of complaints of the membership being “kept in the dark” the trustees issued their statement, dated October 31, and have pledged to write again with an update, once the proposal, “has developed further.”
