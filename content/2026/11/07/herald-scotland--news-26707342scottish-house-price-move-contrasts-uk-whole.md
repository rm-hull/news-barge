*The year-on-year move in**Scottish** house prices last month contrasted with the trend in the UK as a whole.*

*The latest moves are revealed in a closely watched**survey**.*

House prices in Scotland are bucking an overall UK decline, a key survey reveals.

The average UK house price last month showed its sharpest year-on-year fall since October 2023, according to banking group Lloyds’ latest figures.

It was last month down 1.2% on October last year, at £296,285.

In contrast to this fall in the UK as a whole, the average house price in Scotland was up by 2.8% year-on-year last month, at £222,667.

Lloyds said: “Scotland continues to post solid growth.”

The average UK house price in October was down 0.6% from the previous month.

Andrew Asaam, mortgages director at Lloyds, said: “Housing demand is currently being tested by a challenging economic backdrop. Renewed inflationary pressures, driven in part by the conflict in the Middle East, are keeping borrowing costs high and adding to the squeeze on household finances, making some buyers understandably more cautious.

“However, it’s important to put October’s fall in context. House prices have changed relatively little over the past couple of years, as the market has gradually adjusted to higher interest rates. The recent rise in energy and other household costs has added to the pressure on buyers, so some adjustment in prices is not surprising.”

He added: “Looking ahead, conditions are likely to remain subdued in the near term, but we don’t expect this to lead to a sustained period of falling house prices. With wages continuing to rise faster than property prices, the gap between earnings and house prices should gradually narrow, helping affordability to improve over time.”

Lloyds said Northern Ireland “continues to lead the UK, with annual house price growth at 4.4%”, while noting this year-on-year rate of increase had slowed considerably from 6.9% the previous month. The average property value in Northern Ireland is now £228,708, Lloyds observed.

The bank added that year-on-year growth in the average house price has “strengthened slightly” in Wales, accelerating to 1.6%, with the typical property now valued at £233,116.

In England, three regions recorded annual growth in the average house price, according to Lloyds’ house price index, with the North West recording an increase of 2.2%, taking the average property price to £249,916, while the North East saw a 2% rise to £184,318.

The West Midlands was the only other English region to see positive annual growth, at 0.3%, Lloyds noted, with an average property value of £261,656.

The bank added that house prices “remain under pressure across much of southern England”, with Greater London recording the largest annual decline, down 3.1% year-on-year to £524,892, closely followed by the South East and South West, both 2.4% lower at £379,258 and £298,057 respectively.
