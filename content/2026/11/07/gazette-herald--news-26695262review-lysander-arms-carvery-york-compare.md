As I recently said, everybody likes a good carvery, which certainly seems the case as our reviews of carveries are amongst our most read.

Following a review of the carveries of The Anchor Inn at Whixley, which in turn followed on from a review of the Fox and Hounds at Langthorpe, our readers came up with a few suggestions.

Among them was the Lysander Arms at Rawcliffe, near Clifton Moor, which I was already aware of, having seen its good reviews online.

Earlier this year, I called in at the Lysander Arms one Sunday for the carvery with mum and dad but was told they were full up.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21668144.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The exterior of the Lysander Arms *(Image: Darren Greenwood)*

With this in mind and our reader recommendations, I booked in advance the day before.

They only had 12 noon available for the three of us.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21668147.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The bar of the Lysander Arms *(Image: Darren Greenwood)*

We turned up a good quarter of an hour or so before then and even though the pub had just opened by 11.30am, it was already getting busy.

A back room was almost full.

We gave our names at the bar and ordered two lattes (£3.30 each) and a pint of Aspalls Cider (£5.60).

The adult carvery was £14.95 each.

We paid upfront (£57.05) and were given tickets to take to the carvery and were told it would not be open until 12 noon.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21668177.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Darren's Sunday lunch, which featured beef and pork. *(Image: Darren Greenwood)*

But five minutes or so before noon people started queuing up and by noon, the queue was quite long.

It reminded me of the queues in large Spanish hotels for the dinnertime buffet.

The queues kept getting longer, despite the servers being quite fast.

Thus, we decided to join the queue taking around ten minutes to get served.

At least you could have a friendly chat with some of the other diners, including a young couple who had driven in from Sherburn-in-Elmet, having heard how good the carveries are.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21668187.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Dad's Sunday lunch, which included the beef pie. *(Image: Darren Greenwood)*

Now, there was a decent choice of pork loin, buttered turkey breast, topside of beef, honey roast ham and beef pie, plus a vegetarian option.

The portions of meat were generous and you could have two.

So I went for beef and pork, mum went for ham and dad went for the pie.

They were served with roast potatoes, cream and leek new potatoes, fresh seasonal vegetables and handmade Yorkshire Puddings, pork sage and onion stuffing and stock pan gravy.

By the time we had got back to our tables, mum and dad’s coffees had been delivered and were starting to get a little cold.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21668200.jpg?type=mds-article-620)A back room, which was getting full. *(Image: Darren Greenwood)*

Mum had wanted some gravy and had to go and get it herself.

By contrast, at the Fox and Hounds, a member of staff delivered a small gravy boat to us unprompted, which seemed far more civilised.

Anyway, the food was certainly good - perhaps the best of all even.

The meat was deliciously soft, tender and flavoursome.

I am not a lover of ham but a piece of mum’s thick cut ham was very nice.

Dad said he liked his pie though he said he had had better.

The vegetables seemed very fresh as they would be, considering the staff were regularly coming out with fresh trays of vegetables, Yorkshire puddings and so on.

I particularly enjoyed the cream and leek new potatoes, which were exceptionally soft and the creamy flavour gave them a boost.

The seasonal veg of savoy, beans and peas were tender, soft and tasty.

We all remarked how delicious the turnip and carrot mash was.

Unlike the other carveries, you could even have mushy peas, which dad enjoyed.

If there was anything wrong with such delicious food, some of it did seem a little cool, or at least not as hot as we would prefer.

We all cleared our plates and a young server asked if we would like one of the homemade desserts (£8) but we were all quite stuffed.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21668200.jpg?type=mds-article-620)A back room, which was getting full. *(Image: Darren Greenwood)*

Now, I’d already paid the bill (£57.05) so we left, highly satisfied with a good carvery meal.

We had all enjoyed the food but the queuing had certainly let things down somewhat.

It all seemed a bit chaotic with something of a rush in the minutes before noon.

We all agreed it was far more civilised at the Fox and Hounds, where you are told when to go and get your meal.

So whilst the Lysander might have just had the edge on its food, its temperature and the queuing made the Fox and Hounds the overall winner, if I had to choose.

Nonetheless, York can be proud of such a lovely pub on its doorstep, which also serves the usual pub classics at other times.

Such is reflected in the review websites with Google awarding 4.5 stars out of five based on 901 reviews.

TripAdvisor awards 4.1 stars out of five, based on 278 reviews, ranking it 58th out of 135 York pubs and bars.

I’d probably say TripAdvisor was somewhat harsh on this.

Now, TripAdvisor features some mixed reviews, whilst the latest review on Google gave five stars out of five.

The review also said: “Absolutely brilliant carvery at the Lysander Arms.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21668177.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Darren's Sunday lunch, which featured beef and pork. *(Image: Darren Greenwood)*

“I had the beef and pork, both beautifully tender, and the gammon looked amazing and really juicy too. The vegetables were cooked perfectly, served lovely and hot, and the whole meal was excellent value for money.

“Forget Toby Carvery. If you want a proper good carvery, this is the place to go!”

Another recent carvery customer said on Google: “First time visiting here, for Sunday carvery, but I'd certainly go back. The food was really good and reasonably priced, and the staff were very friendly, all in pleasant surroundings. It was very busy, but I take that as a good sign!”

I could not agree more, adding it is all good value.

So to sum up, the Lysander Arms is great for a good carvery, though it might be a victim of its own success where queueing is concerned.

It comes highly recommended and I trust we will return one day as we have done with the Fox and Hounds and the Anchor Inn.
