The SNP has accused Andy Burnham of leaving families "to suffocate under the weight of soaring bills" and demanded an emergency plan from the Prime Minister to help households through the winter.

Dave Doogan, the party's Westminster leader, said the Labour leader needed to "wake up" after a week in which the Bank of England raised interest rates to 4% and forecast inflation of almost 5% next year, days after John Healey's first Budget.

The SNP says families will be thousands of pounds worse off.

Labour accused Mr Doogan of "getting what he asked for and being furious about it", saying the SNP has a record funding settlement and an extra £600 million from the Budget and should use the powers and money it already has.

Earlier this week, Mr Burnham signalled that his long-awaited 10-year plan may now be delayed until January.

In his speech outside No 10 on the day he became Prime Minister , he promised that "later this year" he would "bring forward a new plan for Britain, a 10-year plan, laying out a path from where we are now to where I believe we all want Britain to be".

But in an interview with the Economist, when asked when it would appear, Mr Burnham said: "We may go into the new year, to be honest ... December or January, I think we will need to leave that open."

It looks set to be an expensive winter. Money Saving Expert's average of three forecasts puts the January price cap at £2,219, up 28.8% on the current £1,723.

The Bank of England expects the cap to reach £2,150.

Announcing the rise in interest rates to 4%, Bank of England Governor Andrew Bailey said it should not be seen as "the first in an inevitable long sequence of hikes", but that it is unlikely to be the last unless the conflict in the Middle East is resolved.

The average two-year fix is around 6%, up a point since the war began, and around three million households are due to come off fixed deals before the end of 2027.

The Resolution Foundation says with inflation forecast to peak at 4.9% in the second quarter of 2027, the average private-sector worker will be £670 worse off in real terms next year than previously forecast.

Food is a further pressure. The Bank expects food inflation of 4.8% next year, with an El Niño weather event expected to push up the price of fruit and vegetables from early 2027.

![SNP Westminster Group Leader Dave Doogan delivers his address at the Scottish National Party (SNP) annual conference](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21634108.jpg?type=mds-article-620)SNP Westminster Group Leader Dave Doogan delivers his address at the Scottish National Party (SNP) annual conference *(Image: Jane Barlow/the Press Association)*

Mr Doogan said: "With the UK rapidly heading to the cliff edge, it's time for Andy Burnham to finally wake up and bring forward an emergency plan to help families with the cost of living crisis this winter.

"The UK budget was embarrassingly weak and the minuscule announcements made have been completely obliterated by the news that household bills will soar in the months ahead, leaving many families thousands of pounds worse off under Burnham's government.

"Energy bills could be £1,200 higher than the Labour Party promised, families could face a £5,000 annual hike in their mortgage bills, and the soaring cost of food and fuel could reduce real incomes by £670 more than expected."

He added: "What use is a delayed 10-year plan, if the Prime Minister does not even have a plan for the next 10 weeks?

"Britain is broken. The UK has had 20 years of declining living standards and 20 years of excuses under successive Labour and Tory governments. Andy Burnham promised to be different but instead of delivering breathing space he is leaving people to suffocate under the weight of soaring bills."

A Labour source said: "Families are worried about their bills and they deserve serious answers, instead they've got Dave Doogan, who's pulled off the rare political feat of getting what he asked for and being furious about it.

!Just a few years ago, he stood up in Parliament making the argument against standing charges — now Labour is scrapping them on gas and suddenly he thinks it's a bad idea. He wants a plan for the next ten weeks, but I'd settle for him holding the same position for the next ten minutes.

"The SNP have a record funding settlement and an extra £600 million from this Budget. They should get on with using the powers and the money they have to do more. Scots want everyday help, not Groundhog Day on indyref."

A UK Government spokesperson said: "The Prime Minister recognises the pressure people right across the country, including Scotland, are facing due to the cost of living. That's why this Budget gave families and businesses breathing space.

"From cutting standard charges from gas bills and extending the VAT cut on electricity bills to July, to keeping fuel duty frozen and removing the tariff on heating oil, we are keeping costs down for people. Because of the action we've taken, 345,000 Scottish households will automatically receive the £150 Warm Home Discount this winter.

"The Prime Minister is also exploring how we can work with the Scottish Government to contribute to reindustrialisation and good growth in every postcode."
