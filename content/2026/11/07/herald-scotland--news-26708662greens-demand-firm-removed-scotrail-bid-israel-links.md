The Scottish Greens have urged John Swinney to block a Spanish train manufacturer from bidding for a major ScotRail contract over its alleged links to illegal Israeli settlements.

The party wants Construcciones y Auxiliar de Ferrocarriles (CAF) removed from the shortlist of companies competing to supply a new fleet of suburban trains.

Trade unions, including the TSSA, have also called on the Scottish Government to exclude the company from the procurement process.

The demands follow a vote at Holyrood in September 2025, when a majority of MSPs backed a Green amendment calling on the Scottish and UK governments to “immediately impose a package of boycotts, divestment and sanctions targeted at the state of Israel”.

The Greens argue that allowing CAF to compete for the ScotRail contract is inconsistent with the position adopted by Parliament.

Scottish Green MSP Patrick Harvie called on the First Minister to intervene, accusing the Scottish Government of failing to act on the vote.

He said: “Allowing CAF to be short-listed for a ScotRail contract is a clear breach of the boycott policy that the Scottish Parliament voted for.

“Campaigners are right to call on John Swinney to take action and stop Scotland from backing companies profiting from apartheid, genocide or human rights abuses.

“If a company has been named by the UN for profiteering from the repression and abuse of Palestinians, it should not be competing for public contracts in Scotland.

“If this contract is awarded to CAF then it is clear that the Scottish Government are intent on taking no action to implement the boycott and refusing to listen to trade unions and rightly outraged campaigners.”

CAF is one of five manufacturers shortlisted to supply up to 106 new trains for ScotRail, alongside Alstom, Hitachi Rail, Siemens Mobility and Stadler.

The proposed fleet, comprising electric and battery-electric trains, is intended to serve routes including East Kilbride, Fife, the Borders and suburban services around Glasgow.

The Spanish manufacturer has faced criticism over its involvement in the Jerusalem light rail project, which has been the subject of controversy because of its links to Israeli settlements in occupied Palestinian territory.

A Transport Scotland spokesperson said: “We understand Scottish Rail Holdings, which oversees the procurement being conducted by ScotRail Trains, is continuing to consider this issue in the context of the procurement, and will take account of that assessment and the evidence available.

“Ministers have been clear that Scottish Rail Holdings must satisfy themselves that all participants in their tender exercises are fit and proper partners for public contracts, and the Scottish Government is monitoring this situation closely.”

The CAF has been approached for comment.
