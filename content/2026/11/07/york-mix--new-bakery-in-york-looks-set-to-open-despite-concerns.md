Plans for a new bakery in York have taken a step forward.

City of York Council has granted permission for Greggs to put new signage up at an empty store on Fulford Road.

The bakery chain wants to open a branch at the former Iceland store. It’s found next to Aldi.

The unit is part of the same retail block next to Aldi that drew controversy in 2024, when McDonald’s applied to turn it into a 110-cover restaurant and takeaway.

More than 3,100 people signed a petition against it. The bid was refused by the council, largely on health grounds.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/former-iceland-building-greggs-york-fulford-road.webp)

Greggs put in two planning applications with York council in June.

Officials have now granted advertisement consent for new Greggs signage – including a double-sided totem sign in the chain’s trademark branding.

Conditions include only illuminating the signs during business hours and “all lighting shall be positioned and angled to prevent glare, reflection or distraction to highway users”.

Another application, to alter the shopfront, is still to be decided.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/greggs-iceland-store-signs-fulford-road-york-pd.webp)

A number of people have objected to the plan to open a Greggs. One York resident wrote: “How can the council allow this application when one of their reasons for turning down McDonalds was the proximity to schools?

“How can Greggs, with the overwhelming popularity of sausage rolls, steak bakes and other unhealthy food choices, not be seen as a pull for the school children who pass by daily?”

Labour Fishergate ward councillor Conrad Whitcroft said he had been contacted by a number of residents about the plans.

He said: “Some residents have highlighted previous experiences of early morning noise associated with the site and are concerned about the potential for further disturbance to nearby households.”
