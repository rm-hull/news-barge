A city centre restaurant which celebrates South Asian heritage with a “twist on modern food” is celebrating a good start to business.

Manor Row, named after the road it is located on in Bradford, opened on September 29.

The building was bought at auction and now it is run by four local restaurateurs.

## Building had been empty for 15 years

The historic Grade II-listed building housed a Nawaab curry house for years before standing derelict for about 15 years.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21681057.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The outside of Manor Row, which opened in September *(Image: Newsquest)*

## Who runs it?

Eden’s Tearoom & Patisserie owners Saeqa Gazi-Hussain and Nurul Hussain are behind the project with Marha Lounge duo Anisa Ali and Hasnain Akhmat.

Ms Gazi-Hussain said: “We’re all good friends, and it’s nice to have multiple voices.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21681075.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Saeqa Gazi-Hussain and Nurul Hussain is the India inspired front room of the Manor Row restaurant *(Image: Newsquest)*

“We all have our strengths, but we have one vision.”

## Major refurbishment

The restaurant took 18 months to open after a major refurbishment.

Ms Gazi-Hussain explained: “We spent a year-and-a-half refurbishing the building.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21681091.jpg?type=mds-article-620)With the wood timbers on the ceiling, this room at Manor Row has a more Bristish inspired design *(Image: Newsquest)*

“We wanted to open a Betty’s Tea Room kind of place.

“We wanted to keep its Victorian look but modernise it."

## Curries, pasta and fish and chips

Ms Gazi-Hussain said the restaurant’s tagline is “modern South Asian dining”.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21681103.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Wagyu biryani, one of the mains at Manor Row *(Image: Newsquest)*

“We celebrate South Asian heritage through food,” she said.

Alongside curries and other traditional dishes, the restaurant serves pasta and fish and chips.

“They all have South Asian flavours in them,” she said.

“It’s a twist on modern food.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21681115.jpg?type=mds-article-620)A kale and spinach chaat, which is the most popular starter at Manor Row *(Image: Newsquest)*

## First 6 weeks 'been great'

“We’ve been open for six weeks, and to be honest, it’s been great.

“We believe our food has its own identity.

“We’re not competing with the curry restaurants.

“We have a menu that has something for everybody.

“We brought in a consultant chef from Dubai who helped create the menu.”

## Historic building

The building is steeped in history.

Ms Gazi-Hussain said: “This building was originally a Victorian-style house.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21681122.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Manor Row still retains an old vault as a special feature in its dining area *(Image: Newsquest)*

“Then a mortgage company took it over, and they installed a big vault in the building, and we’ve retained that as a feature inside the restaurant.

“We want to make this restaurant an experience that is beyond the plate.

“There is a lovely ambience to the décor and lighting in the restaurant.”

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21681084.jpg?type=mds-article-620)This room at Manor Row has a Mediterranean design *(Image: Newsquest)*

The décor moves from an Indian-inspired front room to a Mediterranean-style room and a function room with a traditional European and British design.

Ms Gazi-Hussain said: “The décor is inspired by the journey of Lady Meherbai Tata, a pioneer in women’s rights who also celebrated her South Asian heritage.”

Ms Ali and Mr Akhmat added: “We wanted to create somewhere that feels considered from the moment you walk through the door – from the food and interiors to the atmosphere and service.

“Bradford has an incredible South Asian food scene and we’re proud for Manor Row to be part of its next chapter.”
