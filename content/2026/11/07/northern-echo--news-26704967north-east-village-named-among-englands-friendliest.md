A village that draws in tourists from across the country has been named as one of the friendliest in England.

World Atlas has released a list of the friendliest small towns and villages across the country, featuring coastal spots and historic areas with pretty cottages.

Heddon-on-the-Wall made it onto the list thanks to its experiences led by locals who are willing to tell you more about the area.

## Why is Heddon-on-the-Wall one of the friendliest places in England?

On the village, World Atlas said: “Heddon-on-the-Wall in Northumberland is closely associated with the Roman frontier that gives the village its name.

“A surviving section of Hadrian's Wall extends for about 220 metres and represents the broad-gauge design used when construction of the Wall began around 122 CE.

“Another local experience is the alpaca walk 'n' talk at Barnacre Alpacas, about ten miles west of Newcastle upon Tyne.

“The experience follows a gentle one-mile route around the farm and lasts at least an hour.

“Each participant walks an alpaca on a rope and halter, with visits arranged in advance.

“Back in Heddon-on-the-Wall, The Swan offers another stopping point for food and drinks in a traditional pub setting.”

If you’d like to live in this friendly village, the average cost of houses over the last year was £310,300, according to Rightmove.

The majority of properties sold in the area were semi-detached houses that cost around £291,000.

Terraced properties went for an average price of £267,500 while detached properties were more expensive at £492,500.

After exploring the village and Hadrian’s Wall, you can stop off at The Swan, a pub that was originally a cottage with a blacksmith's shop next door.

The Swan was turned into a pub in 1842 and is now a cosy spot for locals and tourists.

The pub has a rating of 4.1 out of 5 on Tripadvisor, with many reviewers saying the staff are “incredibly pleasant and friendly”.

The pub has a range of menus on offer, from a Sunday roast menu to a weekend treat menu.
