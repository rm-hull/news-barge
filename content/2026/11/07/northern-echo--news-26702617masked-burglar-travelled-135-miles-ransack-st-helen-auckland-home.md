A burglar who travelled 135 miles with a crowbar-wielding gang to ransack a County Durham home has avoided jail.

Rahand Abdullah and two unknown others broke into a family home at Northbridge Park, in St Helen Auckland, on May 10, 2024.

The trio had travelled from Nottingham to carry out the raid, believed to have been over a debt, buying items from B&Q to help snatch two watches, jewellery, AirPods, a phone, and £60 cash.

But the burglars were caught in the act by a neighbour - and darted out the back garden gate.

Abdullah, a refugee originally from Iraq, was arrested by police after blood left on a drawer and door frame was traced back to the then-19-year-old.

The other two are yet to be caught, Durham Crown Court heard on Thursday (November 5).

Rachael Landin, prosecuting, described how the family were away at the time of the 10.45pm burglary.

She told the court how a neighbour, after receiving a call from his wife, arrived at the home and saw one of the burglars, who shouted “get out”.

He then saw two men running from the garden towards him, one of whom “ran straight into him carrying an object across his body”, bruising his arm.

Ms Landin said police were called and located a crow bar before discovering how clothing, bedding, and drawers had been left “strewn across the floor” upstairs.

“The house had been ransacked,” she said. “Items had been thrown across the landing.”

The court heard how the family were able to recover some items after being called by a “company acting as an intermediary”, who handed them a bag full of their belongings.

Ms Landin read out a statement from the homeowner, who said the incident had caused “significant emotional damage”.

“We make sure we lock the back gate with a bolt every night,” she read out.

“She does not unlock doors when she is home by herself. We are worried and concerned that these males may return. My wife is always looking out of the window.

The family said they do not feel safe in their home.

Dan Wilberforce, mitigating, said aspiring paramedic Abdullah, now 22, previously pleaded guilty to the burglary in September and was previously of good character.

He described how Abdullah had arrived in the UK from Iraq when he was 13 and struggled at school as he didn’t speak English.

He said the defendant, who now works in a supermarket and as a delivery driver, had started drinking alcohol and smoking marijuana at the time.

“He accepts that he had the option to step away from it but he also says he was not the ring leader,” he said, branding the behaviour as “impulsive”.

The court heard Abdullah “expresses significant remorse” and has moved to Cambridgeshire, completely removing himself from the group responsible.

He has also stopped drinking and only smokes marijuana occasionally.

“It was very much out of character,” Mr Wilberforce said.

Sentencing, Judge Richard Bennett said: “Your blood was found in the property which is why you were traced and the others have not been.

“They appear to have gotten away with it. You haven’t.”

Abdullah, of St Mary Square in Newmarket, was handed a 16 month prison sentence suspended for two years.

He must carry out 250 hours of unpaid work and pay £150 in court costs.
