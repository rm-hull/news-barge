A YORKSHIRE care home helped turn back the clock for one of its residents by reuniting him with a classic car similar to one he owned nearly 70 years ago.

Staff at Care UK's Driffield Manor, in Middle Street North, arranged the special surprise after discovering that resident Chris Hughes, 85, wanted to see a Morris Minor like the one he drove in his younger days.

The team appealed to the local community in the hope of finding an owner willing to help. Their search led them to classic car enthusiast Steve Smith, who owns a 1932 Morris Minor.




Working alongside Mr Smith, staff organised a reunion at Langlands Garden Centre, where Mr Hughes was able to see the vehicle up close and even take a seat inside.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21682997.jpg?type=mds-article-620)"Seeing the smile on his face as he sat behind the wheel again was so heart-warming" *(Image: Driffield Manor Care Home)*

Mr Hughes said: "I didn't think I would see one again. Even the smell takes me back to the young days before being married and having a family."

His passion for the iconic model began in 1958 when he bought his own 1933 Morris Minor for £50. He recalled using the car to drive his girlfriend to work while they were both employed in Leeds.

The visit formed part of Driffield Manor's Wishing Tree Initiative, which enables residents to fulfil long-held ambitions, revisit favourite hobbies and create new experiences.

![Chris Hughes was delighted to get back in a Morris Minor - akin to the one he drove in his youth](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21683001.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Chris Hughes was delighted to get back in a Morris Minor - akin to the one he drove in his youth *(Image: Driffield Manor Care Home)*

Donna Henderson, home manager at Driffield Manor, said: "We had a wonderful afternoon sharing Chris' love for classic cars and hearing stories about his Morris Minor.

"When Chris told us about his beloved Morris Minor and the adventures he had in it over the years, we knew we had to reunite him with the vehicle. Seeing the smile on his face as he sat behind the wheel again was so heart-warming.

"Creating experiences like these are so important to us. Our Wishing Tree initiative is a wonderful way for residents to share their ambitions and revisit things that are important to them. It was an incredibly special day for everyone involved and one we'll never forget."

Driffield Manor provides residential, dementia and respite care and offers a range of facilities including a cinema, hair and beauty salon, bar and bowling green.
