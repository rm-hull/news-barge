A shawarma spot serving kebabs, burgers and doner has been given a one-star food hygiene rating.

Ahmed Shawarma, at 65 Kirkgate in Bradford city centre, was inspected on June 24, 2026.

The business told the Telegraph & Argus that the low rating was "due to administrative issues".

It adds that food safety and cleanliness have "always been our top priority".

## What did inspectors find?

Inspectors found raw meat stored above ready-to-eat food in a refrigerator, which they said could contribute to cross-contamination.

There was no sanitiser or disinfectant to clean work surfaces properly.

The report also found there was no thermometer to check cooked food or hot-holding temperatures.

There was no designated hand-washing basin in the kitchen and staff were reportedly using the equipment sink for hand-washing instead.

Ahmed Shawarma was told to properly connect the hand-washing basin to the drainage system and use it only for hand washing.

Inspectors also found missing tiles and exposed wood in the kitchen.

The toilet door in the kitchen area did not have a self-closing device.

The business had no documented food-safety management system when it was inspected.

## Concerns regarding staff knowledge

The report said: "At the time of my inspection, you were unable to provide documentation to show where you had purchased your food."

Staff did not know which allergens were in the food or how to prevent cross-contamination when preparing allergy-free meals.

The report also stated that staff were not aware of proper cleaning procedures and added that any antibacterial wipes used must be safe for use with food.

There was no system for checking the temperatures of chilled and frozen food deliveries.

Refrigerated food should be kept at 8°C or colder, while frozen food should be kept at -18°C or colder.

## Waste oil must be regularly taken away

The inspector said: "For your information waste oil must be properly contained in leak-proof containers (e.g. bottles, drums etc.) before it is put into the main waste bin.

"This is to prevent it from causing contamination to land and watercourses when it is taken to the tip.

"I recommend that you make arrangements with a licensed waste oil contractor to collect your waste oil on a regular basis for recycling."

## What the business had to say

A spokesperson for Ahmed Shawarma said: "​We would like to clarify to our customers and the community that the 1-star rating was purely due to administrative issues - specifically, missing compliance paperwork and incomplete daily temperature logs for our refrigeration units.

​"Since the inspection, we have fully addressed all of the council’s requirements and updated our record-keeping procedures.

"Food safety and cleanliness have always been our top priority, and we are confident that upon re-inspection, we will achieve a 5-star rating."
