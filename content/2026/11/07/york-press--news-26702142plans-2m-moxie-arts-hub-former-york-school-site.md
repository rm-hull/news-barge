## York couple reveal £2m vision for arts hub, gallery and cafe at former school site

Chris and Lucy Haltiner outside new arts hub for York - The Moxie Centre for Art

*Plans for £2m Moxie arts hub at former York school site have been revealed, reports MAXINE GORDON*

THE York couple behind a £2 million arts hub and cafe have revealed plans for their transformation of a former York school site.

Chris and Lucy Haltiner are the York couple behind the Moxie Centre for Art, which The Press exclusively revealed is earmarked for a former part of The Mount School.

Now the couple are giving Press readers a first look at plans about to be submitted to the City of York Council for the site.

The Moxie centre will be housed in a Victorian building in Dalton Terrace, near Holgate Bridge, formerly part of The Mount School.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21674580.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The Moxie Centre for Art in Dalton Terrace. Photo by Lucinda Grange

Lucy and Chris, who plan to run the centre as a not-for-profit project, bought the two-storey former school house and dance studio from The Mount School several months ago and have put together a planning application to turn the building into a major arts hub for the city.

The rest of The Mount School is unaffected by the plans.

Under the scheme, the 8,000 sq ft site will be converted into a multi-purpose arts venue combining a free public gallery, affordable artist studios, a professional photography studio, a traditional black-and-white darkroom, workshop and event space, cafe, and small shop.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21674586.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Courtyard of The Moxie Centre for Art - in a former Mount School building. Photo by Lucinda Grange

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21674568.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Courtyard at Moxie arts centre - Credit Grid + Grain

The couple said the centre had been designed to fill a gap in York's arts provision by bringing exhibition, education, creative production and community facilities together under one roof.

Chris said York had a wealth of talented artists but a shortage of studio space, with many existing facilities fully occupied and long waiting lists in place.

Speaking exclusively to The Press, the couple said: "We're very excited to be submitting our planning application for the Moxie Centre for Art.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21290044.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Lucy and Chris Haltiner - founders of the Moxie Centre for Art in York

"After the last article, we received so much positive feedback, from artists, enthusiasts and local businesses alike. We're looking forward to becoming part of the city's creative ecosystem, and supporting all the already thriving creative spaces the city has to offer."

The plans go in just as another project to bring a new gallery to York have been unveiled.

As reported in The Press, Cherie Federico, director of Aesthetica Magazine, wants to turn the De Grey Rooms in York into a Centre for Contemporary Art (CCA).

A campaign has been launched appealing for backing to support its founding ahead of a planning application being submitted to City of York Council.

Following the publication of our first article on the Moxie, The Mount School spoke of its enthusiasm for the ambitious project.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21674567.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Front Elevation of Moxie art centre in York - Credit Grid + Grain

Head of The Mount, Anna Wilby, said: "We are delighted that The Moxie Centre for Art opens soon, hot on the heels of our new dance studio, DT suite and preschool," she said.

"Our values align perfectly with the visionary team at The Moxie and we look forward to collaborating on both community and school art events.

"We already run jewellery, dance, photography, sewing, acting and ceramics clubs in school, which will provide perfect opportunities for partnership working."

Chris and Lucy said The Moxie would be more than just a space to work and create art.

Chris said: "It's also about a space where people meet: artists, visitors, collectors, someone who came in just for a coffee.

"That's where conversations start, and conversations are where ideas, inspiration and new art come from."
