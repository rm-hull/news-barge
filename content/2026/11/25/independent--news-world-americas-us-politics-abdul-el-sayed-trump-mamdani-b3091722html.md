Senator-elect Abdul El-Sayed is getting advice on dealing with Donald Trump from a fellow Democrat who has managed to stay on good terms with the president: New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani.

El-Sayed, a Michigan progressive, was a fierce critic of Trump on the midterm campaign trail, slamming the Iran war, tariffs and the immigration crackdown. Trump, in turn, labeled him a “jihadist lunatic.”

But El-Sayed has since signaled he wants to work with the president on health care reform, courting him by publicly pitching the idea of “Trumpcare.” Trump has now softened his tone, calling El-Sayed a “smart guy” and indicating a sit-down is coming.

Ahead of a potential meeting, the senator-elect told *Politico Playbook* that he has been trading notes with Mamdani, another Democrat far to Trump's ideological left who has nonetheless kept up good relations with the White House. Fox News even dubbed their first summit last year a “love fest.”

“He understands, just like I understand, that even if you might disagree with the president on a whole host of issues, he’s the president of the United States,” said El-Sayed, an epidemiologist and the first Muslim elected to the Senate.

![Michigan Senator-elect Abdul El-Sayed said he's been getting pointers from New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani on how to deal with President Donald Trump](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/25/18/06/GettyImages-2304323743.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“I’ll work with anybody,” he said. “When it comes to try and get money out of politics, put money in pockets, and pass Medicare for All.”

Asked whether he believes the president is determined to make a deal, he replied: “Let’s see what comes up in the conversation.”

Since Election Day, El-Sayed, 42, has repeatedly floated “TrumpCare,” an apparent rebrand of the Medicare for All proposal he ran on. He has posted images of gold cards bearing the president's signature, along with a video saying he's ready to work with Trump on guaranteeing health care for all Americans.

The pitch appears to have landed. In a Tuesday interview with *The New York Post*, Trump said he plans to meet with El-Sayed to discuss passing "Medicare for All" under the "Trumpcare" banner.

The openness marks a sharp turn for a Republican president who has long attacked the idea, previously deriding it as “Medicare for none.”

According to an October *Axios* poll, supporters of Medicare for All — an idea popularized by Senator Bernie Sanders — now outnumber opponents by a nearly 2-to-1 margin.

![Zohran Mamdani, another Democrat far to Trump's ideological left, has kept up good relations with the White House. Fox News even dubbed their first summit last year a ‘love fest’](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/25/18/09/GettyImages-2295983484.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Elsewhere in his interview with the *Post*, Trump said that El-Sayed “actually did a very good job,” adding, “I know I’ll be meeting with him…Maybe there’s some place in the middle that we could go.”

Whether Trump and Democrats can actually work together — let alone on something as contentious as overhauling the American health care system — is the million-dollar question. Both sides have offered mixed views.

Democrats, set to control the House and Senate come January, have signaled they want to collaborate with the president on key issues like affordability. At the same time, they plan to take an aggressive approach to ending the Iran war and dismantling tariffs. They’ve also hinted at investigations into Trump officials and allies as well as impeachment efforts.

Trump, for his part, initially congratulated Democrats on their midterm wins and said he could work with them, including on immigration. He later questioned the legitimacy of their electoral victories, called them “CORRUPT AND EVIL” and vowed to use his veto power.
