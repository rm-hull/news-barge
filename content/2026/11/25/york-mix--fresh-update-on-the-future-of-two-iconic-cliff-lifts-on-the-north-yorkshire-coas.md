The future of two of the Yorkshire coast’s best-known cliff lifts remains unclear, with no funding in place and no reopening dates yet identified for either attraction.

Both the Whitby Cliff Lift and Scarborough Spa Cliff Lift remain closed as questions about the potential cost and timetable for reopening the sites remain unanswered, and councillors await the results of investigations and surveys into their condition.

A new interim report states that there is “no capital funding allocated for these repairs”, and if the works were taken forward, a funding source “would need to be identified”.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/whitby-cliff-lift-gsv.webp)

It comes after months of campaigning by residents, businesses, councillors, and community groups seeking answers over the future of the two historic transport links.

In Whitby, councillors are still waiting for the findings of a major survey commissioned earlier this year after plans to permanently decommission the cliff lift were shelved following a public backlash.

The lift has been closed since April 2022 due to severe corrosion, water ingress and structural defects. At the time, reopening costs were estimated at £4.58 million, although officers warned the final figure could be significantly higher.

According to the latest report, consultants from Align Property Services have carried out surveys over the summer, but the final findings have not yet been published.

Officers expect any reopening programme to involve renovating the top station, installing new mechanical and electrical systems, adding a new ventilation system, and replacing the lift.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/scarborough-cliff-lift-nyc.webp)

Meanwhile, in Scarborough, uncertainty about the scale and cost of repairs needed to restore Britain’s oldest cliff railway complicates the Spa Cliff Lift’s situation following its closure in August.

Despite the concerns, council officers revealed they have received a proposal outlining how the lift could be reopened through a phased programme of repairs.

Engineers examining Scarborough Spa Cliff Lift identified problems impacting tracks, sleepers, braking rails, carriages and other safety-critical infrastructure, the report states.

They also found cracked sleepers, loose fixings, worn components and corrosion, while specialists are investigating whether movement within the cliff itself could be contributing to track misalignment.

The report states that costs “will vary depending on the specific part that may have failed and will only be known after failure”.

![Fresh update on the future of two iconic cliff lifts](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/fresh-update-on-the-future-of-two-iconic-2.webp)

The proposed works are intended only to make the lift operational again and do not include any measures to address cliff stability issues, which may need to be tackled separately at a later date.

While there is some optimism that Scarborough’s lift may eventually be brought back into use, the council has acknowledged that neither project currently has funding allocated.

Any decisions on whether the lifts will be repaired and reopened would ultimately be taken by the council’s executive committee.

The authority acknowledged that both lifts have strong public support because of their importance to accessibility, tourism and local identity.

The report added that councillors have received a “significant volume of correspondence” from residents and visitors concerned about the impact of the closures.

It concluded: “There is a very strong feeling locally that every effort should be made to restore and reopen both lifts.”
