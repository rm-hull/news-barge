Off-road drivers have been ripping up peatland and destroying habitats in the Yorkshire Dales

**Thermal imaging drones could catch illegal off-road drivers who damage sensitive moorland habitats in the Yorkshire Dales.**

The Yorkshire Dales Moorland Group said it was stepping up efforts to track down offenders and prosecute them as damage to moorland habitats continues to grow.

Over the past two years, the group has reported vehicles tearing up peatland, destroying sensitive vegetation and disturbing wildlife.

"This senseless abuse of our moors has to stop," the organisation said.

It is now working with the Yorkshire Dales National Park Authority, North Yorkshire Police, Natural England and the National Gamekeepers Organisation to tackle the problem.

According to the Local Democracy Reporting Service, these would include increased vigilance, with thermal imaging drones used in some circumstances to help identify those responsible.

The group warned that offenders could face prosecution and potentially have their vehicles confiscated.

![A dark green off-road SUV is deeply bogged in thick mud on a grassy moorland track, with the wheels and lower body partially submerged.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/e7aa/live/da7cf9d0-e82a-11f1-8d9d-c156c75bcdb5.jpg)

The group is calling for illegal off-roaders to face legal action

Darren Chadwick, from the organisation, said: "This is a big, big problem for upland managers here in the Yorkshire Dales.

"Over the last couple of years it's got worse and the damage to upland habitats is completely unacceptable.

"These images show the long-term, sometimes irreversible damage inflicted on our landscape caused by selfish and irresponsible off-road drivers and riders."

The group said much of the damage was occurring in protected areas, including Sites of Special Scientific Interest, where landowners and gamekeepers must comply with strict environmental regulations.

It said those responsible for managing the moors could face legal action for breaching conditions designed to protect the habitats, while illegal off-roaders were causing damage without facing similar consequences.

Incidents would be logged and reported to the relevant agencies as part of a coordinated effort to bring offenders to justice, the group added.

## 'Significant damage'

The Yorkshire Dales National Park Authority confirmed it was working with North Yorkshire Police to tackle illegal vehicle use.

A spokesperson said: "Illegal off-roading can cause significant damage to sensitive habitats, historic features and access land within the national park."

According to the authority, its ranger teams supported police operations to check that vehicles were using legal routes and that drivers and riders were behaving responsibly towards other countryside users.

"We have a strong and constructive working relationship with a number of responsible off-road user groups," the spokesperson added.

"Their support plays an important role in promoting good practice and helping to protect these special places."
