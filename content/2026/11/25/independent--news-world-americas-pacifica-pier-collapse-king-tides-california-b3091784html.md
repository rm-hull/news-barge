Part of a pier in the Northern California city of Pacifica has collapsed as powerful waves batter the West Coast.

Video captured the moment a massive wave tore down a portion of the pier and slammed it into the ocean.

The pier, which is L-shaped and 1,140 feet (350 meters) long, had been closed by officials in the summer after cracks were found in the structure.

Officials warned that more beachfront homes in the area are at risk of flooding.

![The Pacifica Pier on Wednesday before part of it collapsed into the sea](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/25/22/29/AP26329726547346.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Beachfront homes in Pacifica, which sits south of San Francisco, began flooding this week as king tides arrived in the area. King tides are the highest tides of the year.

North of San Francisco, officials in Marin County issued an evacuation warning for residents of beachfront properties in Stinson Beach.

They said large waves expected through the afternoon could send water and debris into roads and homes.

![A wave crashes over a breakwater as high surf and tides impact Pacifica, Calif., on Wednesday, Nov. 25, 2026](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/25/21/29/AP26329697638017.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The National Weather Service issued a coastal flood warning for the Pacific Coast and bay shorelines along San Francisco Bay and San Pablo Bay through 4 p.m. Wednesday.

It also issued a high surf warning in the Bay Area for Wednesday, when breaking waves of 20 to 30 feet (6 to 9 meters), about two to three stories high, could overpower swimmers and sweep people into the ocean.

![A car in floodwaters on Nov. 25, 2026 in Pacifica, California](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/25/21/27/GettyImages-2306291561.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“Rising tides will bring water and waves farther up the beach than normal, restricting escape from beaches and coastal area,” the weather service said.

King tides occur when the gravitational pull of the moon and sun aligns to raise water levels.

Their effects this year are being amplified by the ongoing super El Niño, which has warmed the ocean and led to an increased volume of seawater.

![November’s King Tides in the Bay Area are being amplified by El Niño conditions](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/25/21/2306291598..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

High tides have already flooded several communities this week along California's coast.

In the Bay Area, some homes and streets flooded in the coastal Marin County cities of Mill Valley, Larkspur, Sausalito and Corte Madera.

Farther north, in Humboldt County near the Oregon border, the sheriff's office ordered evacuations in a small coastal community where most of the buildings flooded.
