A self-taught birth keeper who failed to recognise a woman was carrying twins says she doubted her psychic abilities after two babies died in free-birth tragedies.

Sita Kali was present in 2022 when a New South Wales northern rivers baby was born at home three weeks after his hospital-appointed due date – and a week past the point of typical medical intervention.

Baby R was white in the face, struggled to breathe, and did not latch before dying in the back of an ambulance 14 hours after his birth.

Two years later, Kali charged a couple $4,100 for her services, which included providing five hours of antenatal care involving massaging and mapping the mother’s body.

But she failed to detect the mother was carrying twins – Baby M, who was stillborn, and Baby D, who died shortly after birth.

Kali told an inquest into the deaths she has attended up to 200 births – most of which were free births – over two decades without any medical training.

Free birth is a choice not to involve a medical professional in birth and can instead involve a doula, whose role is to provide emotional support and information but not to perform medical tasks.

The inquest is examining what benefits, limitations or dangers exist if parents place their trust in guidance from doulas and birth keepers who are not clinically trained.

Kali told the coroner she relied on her experience, knowledge from books and conversations with respected wisdom keepers, and her psychic abilities to support women during births.

When asked why she had not been able to intuit that the second mother was having twins, the self-described birth keeper said she had asked herself the same question.

“There’s a lot of mystery in this area of birth,” she told the inquest.

Kali said she only felt one heartbeat and believed baby M was already dead at the time.

She said the deaths of the three babies in separate tragedies left her questioning the psychic and empathetic abilities she relied on.

“In both of these circumstances, I was very upset and doubted these abilities in hindsight,” she told the inquest.

But she rejected the suggestion that the sixth sense is unreliable, arguing a lot more education is required to understand the connection between the emotional, physical and spiritual.

“The woman’s own intuition, her own ability to sense, to feel, definitely has more of an impact than my own,” Kali said.

She accepted clinical treatment also has an important role to play in ensuring the safety of women.

Kali said she did not carry oxygen and had no training in newborn resuscitation or how to identify whether a baby’s breathing, colour or heart rate was normal.

She accepted she did not have the clinical training to assess whether medical intervention was required during birth.

Kali said she has since retired as a result of post-traumatic stress and anxiety.

The inquest continues on Thursday.
