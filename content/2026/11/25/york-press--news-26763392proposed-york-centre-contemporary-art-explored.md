## Event to look at York's proposed Centre for Contemporary Art

How the gallery could look *(Image: Pic supplied)*

A new vision for contemporary culture could soon reshape the heart of York.

A proposed Centre for Contemporary Art is part of an emerging conversation about how York can build on its creative identity and heritage to become one of the UK’s most ambitious cultural cities.

If approved, the CCA would be housed in the Grade II\*-listed De Grey Rooms, in St Leonard's Place.

The idea will be explored at Reignite XVIII: Reimagining the City – Exploring York’s Cultural Future, a free public event taking place on December 9 from 6.30pm to 9pm.

Cherie Federico, director of Aesthetica and organiser of Reignite, said: "York has an extraordinary cultural identity and a rich creative history, but I believe there is so much more the city can achieve.

"We have incredible artists, organisations, audiences and spaces, and the opportunity to bring these together in a way that gives York a much stronger contemporary cultural presence.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21764866.jpg?type=mds-article-620)How the gallery could look *(Image: Pic supplied)*

"There is still a long way to go, and that starts with having the ambition to think differently about what the city could become.

"For me, this is about creating opportunities: for artists to make ambitious work, for audiences to experience something unexpected, and for York to be a place where contemporary culture can grow and flourish."

The event will bring together artists and cultural leaders to discuss York’s creative ecosystem, including ways to reach new audiences, support artists, and make better use of the city’s cultural and historic assets.

As a UNESCO City of Media Arts, York already has a strong creative foundation.

The challenge now is how to build on that with new opportunities for artistic experimentation, collaboration, and public engagement.

The conversation will feature five perspectives from across the cultural sector.

Photographer Hannah Starkey will discuss her career and the role of place in developing an artistic voice.

Hadeel Eltayeb, displays curator at the Design Museum, will talk about how cultural organisations can connect with audiences through relevant and accessible programming.

Mat Lazenby will explore the relationships that shape creative ecosystems, from independent businesses to cultural partnerships.

Guy Bowyer, chief executive of York Conservation Trust, will discuss how historic buildings can be reimagined to support contemporary creativity.

Ms Federico said the proposed Centre for Contemporary Art is just one part of a bigger vision.

She said: "The idea of a Centre for Contemporary Art is part of that bigger picture.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21764865.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Cherie Federico of Aesthetica *(Image: Pic supplied)*

"The project is still developing, and we are at the stage of exploring its possibilities, shaping the vision and building the relationships that will help inform its future.

"It is an opportunity to consider what a new cultural space could bring to the city and how it might connect with the extraordinary places and heritage that already make York distinctive."

Reignite XVIII will begin at City Screen before continuing at De Grey Rooms.

Artwork will be available for purchase, with proceeds supporting the development of the proposed centre.

Attendance is free, but advance booking is required via Eventbrite.
