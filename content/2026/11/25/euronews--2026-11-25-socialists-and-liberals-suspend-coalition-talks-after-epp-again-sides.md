## The three centrist groups had been negotiating a coalition for the second half of the European Parliament's term, including a joint push to secure Roberta Metsola's re-election as president.

The Socialists and Democrats (S&D) and the liberal Renew Europe groups in the European Parliament suspended coalition talks with the conservative European People's Party (EPP) on Wednesday, parliamentary sources familiar with the matter told Euronews.

The move followed the centre-right EPP’s decision to join forces with far-right groups to pass a revision of environmental assessment rules, a strategy it has repeatedly employed during the current parliamentary term.

The EPP, S&D and Renew make up the centrist majority supporting European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen. Their leaders, Manfred Weber, Iratxe García Pérez and Valérie Hayer, were due to negotiate a cooperation agreement for the second half of Parliament’s term, including a deal on Roberta Metsola’s re-election as parliament president next January.

The EPP clashed with its traditional partners on legislation called "Omnibus VIII", proposed by the European Commission to speed up environmental assessments. The centre-right group privileged simplification over environmental safeguards and turned to the far right after it could not clinch a deal with S&D and Renew, which supported the inclusion of more safeguards in the legislation.

The coalition agreement would also outline shared legislative priorities and working arrangements, although negotiations remain difficult.

S&D and Renew are demanding a written EPP commitment to stop voting with the right-wing European Conservatives and Reformists (ECR), the far right Patriots for Europe (PfE) and Europe of Sovereign Nations (ESN).

The main sticking point of the negotiations is how to prevent the EPP from joining forces with the far right. EPP leader Manfred Weber ushered in a right-wing majority during the current parliamentary term by backing legislation on environmental and migration issues alongside the ECR, PfE and ESN.

Speaking at a press conference on Tuesday, Weber stopped short of committing to an agreement that would oblige the EPP to consistently vote alongside its centrist partners.

All negotiations on the coalition agreement for the second part of the legislaturehave been suspended until further notice. Parliamentary sources say the Socialists and Liberals are demanding “strong safeguards” in the agreement to prevent a repeat of the episode.

Sources within Renew Europe say the group regards a written agreement as a prerequisite for supporting Metsola’s re-election.
