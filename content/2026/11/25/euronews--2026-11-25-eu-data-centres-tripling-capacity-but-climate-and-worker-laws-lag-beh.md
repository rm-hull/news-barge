## The European Commission wants to triple EU data centre capacity for AI and cloud computing within five to seven years, but the rules to measure their environmental impact and protect construction workers are still being written. Watch the video!

The EU proposed the Cloud and AI Development Act, creating "acceleration zones" in each member state with a 12-month permitting ceiling. Projects meeting two of five criteria could gain "strategic" status. The act is still a proposal, and the 12 months cover the permit only, not construction or grid connection.

On the environment, measurement comes first. Data centres with at least 500 kilowatts of IT power must report annual energy and water use. In September, the Commission adopted an A-to-G efficiency label, with the first ratings due in August 2027. But a label is not a limit: it grades performance without forcing any data centre to reach a certain grade. A consultation on binding minimum standards runs until 14 December, with a proposal planned for 2027.

The stakes are high. EU data centres used 68 terawatt-hours of electricity in 2024, projected to reach 114 by 2030, or 3.2% of EU demand. In Ireland, they took 23% of metered electricity in 2025.

Workers face gaps too. The fast-track framework has no specific labour-rights conditions, and a 30 October investigation reported injuries, unpaid wages, and barriers to union access at European sites. The Quality Jobs Act could tighten subcontracting rules but is not yet law.

Meanwhile, national approaches diverge across Germany, Ireland and the Netherlands.
