Sony has been crowing about Grand Theft Auto 6 setting a new record for concurrent PlayStation players, which isn't bad for a game that's currently only singleplayer. It isn't just beating the records set by the giants of multiplayer gaming back when they released, though. It's also eating their lunch right now.

Alinea Analytics reports that Call of Duty, Fortnite, and EA Sports FC 27 all saw their number of daily active users drop when GTA 6 released. "Call of Duty got it the worst of the three," Alinea's head of market analysis Rhys Elliott wrote, "dropping 42% from an average of 7.5M DAUs to 4.4M" according to the company's estimates.

Fortnite only lost 28% of its players, the firm claims, with an estimated five million of them choosing to buy groceries and fix the stairs instead of parachuting out of a bus over the weekend, and EA Sports FC 27 suffered a similar loss. "FC 27's DAUs dropped by about 27% when GTA 6 launched," according to Elliott, "from 5.9M to 4.3M."

But where player numbers for both of those games were trending back up four days after GTA 6's release, Call of Duty has struggled to win its players back, Elliott says: "Sadly, CoD has barely bounced back after four days. It's still down 41% from its baseline."

During that time Call of Duty had a Thanksgiving-themed event, while FC 27 had a Black Friday discount and in-game "Thunderstruck" promotion, and Fortnite's ongoing Treasure Hunters event is adding a bunch of One Piece stuff. If you're going up against a cultural juggernaut like GTA, I guess crossing over with something as massive as One Piece is the way to do it.

As Elliott concludes, this is only part one of the story. When Rockstar comes back for the second bite of this apple its rivals might have an even harder fight on their hands: "When the online component and PC port of GTA 6 hits (some time next year simultaneously, I'd imagine), then the bounce back will not be so obtainable."
