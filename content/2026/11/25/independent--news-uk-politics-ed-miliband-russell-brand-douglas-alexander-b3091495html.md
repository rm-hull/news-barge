The revelation by the Scottish secretary Douglas Alexander that he advised Ed Miliband not to have anything to do with Russell Brand back in 2015 but was ignored has a worrying echo of recent scandals to it.

A bit like the scandals which engulfed Sir Keir Starmer with Peter Mandelson and Boris Johnson with Christopher Pincher, it exposes a fatal flaw in Ed Miliband’s political judgement.

Back in 2015, Mr Alexander was in charge of Labour’s election campaign and Mr Miliband was the leader taking his party to defeat.

In a bid to turn things around late on, Mr Miliband sat down and did a cosy video with the comedian in his East London flat. Even then it was cringe, but now it looks far worse, with Brand serving time in prison for rape and other sexual offences.

While Mr Miliband could not have known about the serious sexual offences 11 years ago there were qualms at the time over his personal judgement in doing a stunt with a celebrity who even then courted controversy.

![Miliband went to Brand’s flat for a campaign video for the 2015 election](https://static.independent.co.uk/s3fs-public/thumbnails/image/2015/04/28/18/v3-Russel%20brand%20and%20ed%20miliband.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Mr Alexander is quite clear in his comments that this is about personal judgement.

“I disagreed with the judgment of Ed going,” he said, in an exclusive interview with The Independent editor-in-chief Geordie Greig for The Talk Show. “You can ask anyone who was there. I genuinely thought it was the wrong thing to do. I thought if you aspire to be the prime minister of the country, you should not be going to Russell Brand's flat in the East End of London.”

He said he was not the only one to warn Mr Miliband at the time, noting that the then shadow chancellor Ed Balls also opposed the stunt.

Yet Mr Miliband went ahead with it anyway. And his rather mealy mouthed words on it since Brand’s conviction with a lack of a proper apology to the victims of the former TV personality have not helped his cause.

When leading politicians indulge the egos of these big celebrity personalities, they imbue them with power and influence.

The then Green leader Caroline Lucas did the same but has been far more fulsome in her admission that she got it badly wrong.

Why is this important? Well it’s because we now we know that the man who is currently in charge of British foreign policy - one of the great offices of state - was warned about hanging out with an “unsavoury” character like Brand but decided to ignore those warnings. It brings his personal judgement very much into question.

And if we need examples of why this important let us look at very modern history.

One of the reasons Sir Keir is no longer prime minister is because he chose to ignore warnings about Mandelson’s friendship with the convicted paedophile financier Jeffrey Epstein and the former peer’s failed security vetting.

Despite the fact that he only needed to do a quick Google search to work out what was wrong with Mandelson, Sir Keir appointed him as ambassador to the US, the most important and sensitive diplomatic posting.

![Douglas Alexander said he warned Ed Miliband not to get involved with 'unsavoury' Russell Brand](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/25/13/04/s6.png?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Before that we had Boris Johnson who chose to appoint Christopher Pincher as his deputy chief whip, responsible for the welfare of MPs. This was despite the fact he had been warned about Pincher’s sexual behaviour being problematic to say the least.

So when in 2022 Pincher was caught in another scandal in the Carlton Club - the private club linked to the Conservative Party - it was this scandal, rather than partygate rule breaking during lockdown, which saw Rishi Sunak and Sajid Javid resign and force the end of the troubled Johnson government.

Time and again we see that it is not so much what leaders do but the bad people they put their trust in which leaves them exposed and leads to their downfall.

The joke about nepotism is “it is not what you know but who you know” but ironically that can also work in reverse when “who you know” turns out to be a wrong’un.

This row with Brand does not only drag in Mr Miliband, it has involved another senior Labour figure Lucy Powell, the party’s deputy leader and education secretary.

She was Mr Miliband’s chief of staff and was apparently fine to go along with the Brand stunt at the time.

Question marks had been hanging over the so-called comedian right back from 2008 when he and Jonathan Ross did that ill-advised spoof call to Andrew Sachs, the actor who played Manuel in Fawlty Towers.

He had spoken openly about his promiscuity, but nobody seemed to consider whether there might be something more troubling behind the behaviour of a man wielding such power over young women.

Hindsight has 20/20 vision, but it seems obvious now that those questions should have been asked - not least in a risk laden profession like politics.

As ever the large popular following was more important than any questions about the personality involved.

More level-headed politicians such as Mr Alexander - who like Gordon Brown is the son of the Church of Scotland manse - appear to have taken a different view. He described Brand as “unsavoury” - an understatement it turns out.

The lesson is also perhaps one which the current prime minister Andy Burnham should take head of.

The furore over the odd behaviour he allowed from so-called “influencers” at a recent Downing Street reception for them shows a man who puts social media following ahead of common sense.

Already one of them has had to remove a video taken in Downing Street which has caused Burnham some embarrassment.

It may be small fry when compared to Brand, Mandelson or Pincher but it was yet another example of politicians forgetting that they are judged by who they associate with as they pursue the fool’s gold of popular influencers.

Sage advice is often unwelcome, but perhaps the warnings of people like Mr Alexander should be heeded more often.
