The Australian government issued a formal apology on Wednesday to current and former LGBTQ service members for harm caused by decades of discriminatory policies.

"You and your families offered ⁠this nation everything that service asks. In return our nation offered condemnation, derision, and suspicion," defence minister Richard Marles told service members in a speech to parliament.

"This country ⁠did not keep faith ​with ⁠you. But today, it says sorry."

Australia lifted a ban on lesbian, gay and ⁠bisexual people serving in the Australian Defence Force ​in ⁠1992.

Until then, consensual ‌homosexual activity could be prosecuted under military law. It maintained its ban on transgender people until ‌2010.

![](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/25/04/2306753053..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Marles said the discriminatory policies ‌resulted in "witch hunts" where service members were tracked, interrogated and forced to either resign or be discharged dishonourably.

"Members of ⁠the Australian Defence Force were subjected to appalling treatment based on their sexual orientation," he said.

"It is a shameful chapter in the history of the Australian Defence Force."

He continued: "We asked these Australians to defend a fair go. Then we refused them ‌one. None of this made our nation safer."

​Marles added that while the ban ‌on lesbian, gay and bisexual ⁠people serving was lifted in 1992, "it ⁠was a long time before Defence's culture and attitudes caught up".

In September, ​parliament passed a law ‌to have historical homosexual convictions ​extinguished from service records.
