Ofgem's cap rises 26% to £2,166 from 1 January, adding £443 a year to a typical bill. Harrogate MP Tom Gordon wants the Prime Minister to halt the rise.

The energy price cap will rise by 26% to £2,166 for 1 January to 31 March 2027, adding £443 a year to the bill of a typical household, after an Ofgem announcement on Wednesday 25 November. Tom Gordon, the Liberal Democrat MP for Harrogate and Knaresborough, has backed his party leader Ed Davey’s call for the Prime Minister to step in and halt the January rise.

## Overview

* **The rise:** the price cap goes up 26% to £2,166 from 1 January 2027, which Mr Gordon’s office says adds £443 a year to a typical household’s bill.
* **Fuel poverty:** 14.7% of households in Yorkshire and the Humber are in fuel poverty, according to House of Commons Library data cited by his office.
* **Government help so far:** a £100 payment for households in Council Tax bands A to D.
* **What the MP wants:** the rise stopped, paid for from oil and gas tax receipts and network company windfalls.

## “Left to fend for themselves”

“Families across North Yorkshire are being left to fend for themselves while bills spiral out of control,” Mr Gordon said.

“The Prime Minister must listen to Liberal Democrat calls and stop this hike going ahead in January. The money is there, and it should go back to the families who are paying for this crisis, not sit in Treasury coffers or shareholders’ pockets.”

## The £100 payment

The Government’s response so far is a £100 payment for households in Council Tax bands A to D, which Mr Gordon’s office points out is less than a quarter of the expected rise. It cites the Resolution Foundation’s finding that the payment is so badly targeted that one in eight of the poorest households will not be eligible at all, and says the Government has not set out what support, if any, will be available for groups such as students, who are on low incomes but do not pay council tax.

Earlier this month we looked at which Harrogate and Knaresborough homes fall in the qualifying bands, and Mr Gordon has already warned that terminally ill people face a postcode lottery for the same payment.

## Where the Liberal Democrats say the money is

The party points to two sums. The Office for Budget Responsibility expects the Treasury to take in £2.6 billion more in oil and gas tax revenue this year than it forecast in March, rising to £7.6 billion more over the next five years. Citizens Advice estimates that energy network companies, which operate as monopolies, are on course for windfall profits of £6.3 billion to £7 billion under their current price controls, because inflation has run higher than expected.

The Liberal Democrats want that money used to cut bills now, for example through the party’s Essential Energy Guarantee. It would give every household an essential amount of energy, equal to 50% of typical consumption, at a discounted rate. The party says it would save the average family £100 a year, rising to £140 on average for the least well-off households, with extra allowances for each child and all energy at the discounted rate for people with disabilities.
