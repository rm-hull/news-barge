## Two York schools could be given share of £4.6m for additional SEND places

Joseph Rowntree School. *(Image: Haydn Lewis)*

A £4.6 million investment could create 70 new specialist school places for children and young people with SEND in York.

City of York Council is being asked to approve a package of projects that would expand Applefields School, in Tang Hall, by up to 50 places and increase capacity at the autism specialist unit at the Joseph Rowntree School, in New Earswick, by 20 places.

If approved, the investment would form a key part of York’s SEND (Special Educational Needs and Disabilities) programme, helping ensure more children can access schooling closer to home and meet growing demand for specialist places.




A high-level feasibility study has been carried out for the extra places at Applefields School.

This comes as demand for SEND provision at the school has risen by around 45 per cent in recent years, with 246 pupils currently enrolled.

In the study, an estimated £2.6 million from the Department for Education SEND Capital Grant is earmarked for establishing five new classrooms at the specialist school.

Officers said: “Without additional capacity, Applefields School is likely to face continuing pressure on teaching spaces, specialist facilities and pastoral areas.

“The number of pupils in specialist primary provision has grown and there are not enough secondary places in the city to meet this demand when the pupils transition from primary to secondary school.”

More than £2 million would be proposed for the 20 extra spaces at the Joseph Rowntree comprehensive school – which currently has 1,248 pupils on roll, would be delivered in two phases, the report suggested.

Initially, the existing school’s Enhanced Resource Provision for autism, located in the Wiltshire Centre, will be expanded, with the inclusion of classrooms, break out space and new toilets.

The report added: “Work will also include the permanent relocation of the school’s SEMH provision from the 2nd floor.

“Last summer term, the school relocated their pastoral provision to the ground floor and created a student reception at the front of school.

“This has worked well and enabled staff to more easily deal with behavioural issues at the beginning and end of the school day when pupils are arriving and departing.”

The building work has been proposed to start in July 2027 and will be carried out over three years.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/20623544.jpg?type=mds-article-620)City of York Council is being asked to approve a package of projects that would expand SEND provision at Applefields School and the Joseph Rowntree School *(Image: Ben Birchall/PA Wire)*

Speaking about this, Cllr Bob Webb, the council’s executive member for children, young people and education, said: “This investment is rooted in our vision to support children, young people with SEND and their families in the city.

“We need to make sure all our young people and their families feel they can access the right support at the right time, that they belong in their school or setting, and are able to shape the support available to them locally.

"Proposals to increase specialist support within the city are a key element of our Reform Plan.”

Council officers say the additional places would help keep more pupils within York, reducing travel times for families and cutting transport costs.

They added: “If additional places are not provided at Applefields and Joseph Rowntree there is a risk of higher revenue costs accessing independent places outside York. Independent specialist places cost an average £63k per pupil per year.

“Transport costs are also higher for out of York placements.”

The report has been put to the council’s executive for discussion on Tuesday (December 1).
