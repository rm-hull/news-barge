# More scrutiny of mayors required as their spending and powers grow: The Yorkshire Post says

## The introduction of regional mayors has already resulted in reshaping political power structures across the country where the roles have superseded those of local MPs and arguably even junior Government ministers in terms of power, prominence and prestige.

Mayors have become the political faces of their regions, responsible for huge sums of money, and as a consequence are now often the first port of call for everyone from international investors to local campaigners who want action on different issues.

Andy Burnham, whose immediate ascent from Greater Manchester mayor to Prime Minister highlights their influence, is now seeking to give mayors even more power under his devolution agenda.

But as the scope and influence of their organisations grow – Tracy Brabin’s West Yorkshire Combined Authority alone has close to 1,000 employees already – so does the need for genuine accountability and scrutiny of their decisions.

This is a key point made in a new report by the Public Accounts Committee, which highlights there is currently no clear role for Parliament and locally-elected MPs to hold them to account for their use of public money.

Cross-party Parliamentary Select Committees of MPs consistently prove themselves to be effective in holding Government departments and ministers to account, highlighting where things are going wrong and suggesting improvements.

Consideration should be given to whether a Select Committee of MPs should be established to specifically examine the work and spending decisions of mayors and strategic authorities given the growing importance of the roles.

Deepening devolution is the right thing to do – having adequate checks and balances in place while doing so is equally vital.
