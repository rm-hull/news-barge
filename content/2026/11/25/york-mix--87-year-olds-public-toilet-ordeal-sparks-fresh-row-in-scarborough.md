An 87-year-old visitor’s distressing experience searching for a toilet in Scarborough has become the latest rallying cry in a long-running campaign to restore public conveniences to the town centre.

Scarborough councillors have unanimously backed a motion calling for publicly accessible toilets and a Changing Places facility to be included in the £18.5 million Square One redevelopment of the former Brunswick Centre site.

At a meeting on Tuesday, November 24, Cllr Robert Everall shared a personal story about how his elderly mother was left with an unhappy lasting memory of her favourite seaside town after being caught short during a visit.

![87-year-old’s public toilet ordeal sparks fresh town centre row](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/87-year-olds-public-toilet-ordeal-sparks-fresh-town-centre.webp)

He told councillors: “My own 87-year-old mother travelled hundreds of miles to visit her favourite town one last time.

“She got caught short. Now her lasting memory of Scarborough is of having to go into Boyes to buy dry pads and underwear.”

The comments came as councillors argued that a lack of public toilets is affecting residents, visitors, people with disabilities and businesses in the town centre.

Proposing the motion, Reform’s town mayor, Thomas Murray, said some people had told him they were reconsidering whether to visit Scarborough because of uncertainty over finding toilet facilities.

He said: “People have told me they’re reconsidering whether to come to the town because they can’t be sure they’ll find a toilet.

“For a family who needs a Changing Places facility, that can decide whether they come at all.”

## Contentious concerns

![87-year-old’s public toilet ordeal sparks fresh town centre row](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/87-year-olds-public-toilet-ordeal-sparks-fresh-town-centre-2.webp)

The issue has remained one of the most contentious local concerns since the closure of the Brunswick Centre in 2025, which removed a key part of the public toilet provision available in the town centre.

Earlier this month, Alison Hume, Labour MP for Scarborough and Whitby, said residents were “absolutely sick of not having any public toilets in the town” and expressed hope that facilities would form part of the Square One scheme.

The redevelopment is being supported by an £18.5 million package of public investment and is expected to include a six-screen cinema, family leisure attraction, retail units and food-and-drink outlets.

However, a recent council report noted that toilets within individual businesses would primarily be intended for customers and would not replace the level of public provision previously available through the Brunswick Centre.

![87-year-old’s public toilet ordeal sparks fresh town centre row](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/87-year-olds-public-toilet-ordeal-sparks-fresh-town-centre-4.webp)

Cllr Sarah Mason told the meeting: “Square One is now being propped up with £18.5 million in public money.

“I think the least that public money can do is provide some toilets.”

Other councillors highlighted both accessibility concerns and the impact on local trade.

Cllr Janet Jefferson said existing facilities in the town were often located in unsuitable places and were not always easily accessible for people with mobility issues.

Councillors also said they had heard concerns from businesses that customers were avoiding the town centre because of the shortage of facilities.

The council’s motion calls for dedicated publicly accessible toilets, accessible toilets and a Changing Places facility to be secured as an integral part of the Square One development.

It also asks North Yorkshire Council, the York and North Yorkshire Combined Authority and developer Scarborough Group International to explain why public toilet provision was removed from earlier iterations of the scheme and what steps can now be taken to reinstate it.

Councillors further argued that facilities should be available to the general public without requiring visitors to purchase goods or services from businesses within the development.

The motion was approved unanimously.
