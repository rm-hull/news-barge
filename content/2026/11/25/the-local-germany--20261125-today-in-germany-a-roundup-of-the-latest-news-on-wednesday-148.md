**Wednesday's top story: Die Linke expels local lawmaker in Berlin over her criticism of Israel**

The Left Party (*Die Linke*) in Berlin voted to expel Vanessa Emde, who had won a direct mandate to the city's House of Representatives in Neukölln.

In a three-quarters vote (33/44), *Die Linke* MPs in Berlin cast out Emde who had been seen as a risk to forming a Left-led government – with the Social Democrats and the Greens – for her criticism of Israel and support for Palestine.

Emde had been at the center of controversy for weeks for her comments on Israel – controversy that she has referred to as a "media smear campaign".

She was accused of antisemitism on the grounds that she questions Israel's right to exist. Asked about the issue, Emde had said there is no right to exist for states, only for people.

"The decision is a turning point for the Left...," Emde said in comments reported by Tagesschau and other German media. "My former parliamentary group has obviously collapsed under pressure from the SPD and the Greens."

Emde had won 46.2 percent of the direct vote in Neukölln, and said following the party decision that she intends to serve as a non-affiliated Member of Parliament in Berlin.

As a member of parliament, she would receive 8,161 euros per month, and maintain the ability to submit parliamentary questions to the Senate and to speak to some extent in plenary sessions.

*Die Linke* hopes to make Elif Eralp, a lawyer and long-time local housing activist, the first mayor of Berlin from the far-left party.

![Members of the Left Party in berlin leave the group meeting after expelling Vanessa Emde.](https://apiwp.thelocal.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/634930006.jpg)

Berlin Left parliamentary group leader Tobias Schulze, emphasized that the decision had not been made lightly: "Many members of parliament have expressed that they find this step difficult but understandable."

Berlin SPD chairpersons Steffen Krach and Bettina König welcomed the decision taken by the Left faction.

**Evacuations planned after unexploded WWII bomb discovered in Nuremberg**

A World War II-era aerial bomb was discovered in Nuremberg’s Lichtenreuth district on Tuesday.

The 225-kilogram American device, which was found in Brunecker Straße during construction work, is set to be defused by experts on Wednesday.

A cordoned off area of around 500 meters has been established, with evacuations scheduled to begin around 11am.

People living or working in parts of Frankenstraße, Münchner Straße, Oskar-von-Miller-Straße and Sofie-Keeser-Weg are affected.

The Federal Office for Migration and Refugees, a gas station, and the furniture retailer XXL Lutz will also need to be evacuated temporarily, the city said.

The disposal of the bomb is scheduled for around 2pm.

Second World War bombs are regularly discovered in Germany. Highly skilled experts are needed to disarm them safely.

**German business morale rises again in November**

German business sentiment rose again in November, staying at its highest level in more than three years, a survey showed Tuesday, as a recovery gathers pace in Europe's biggest economy.

The Ifo Institute's closely watched business climate index increased for a seventh straight month, hitting 90.6 points, slightly above analyst expectations.

As in October, the gauge was at its highest level since May 2023, underscoring growing optimism among companies that the economy is turning a corner after several years of stagnation.

"Companies were more satisfied with their current business conditions. They also revised their expectations upward," said Ifo president Clemens Fuest.

On the International Day for the Elimination of Violence against Women, the Police Union (GdP) is calling for more staff and better protection services for those affected.

"Politics must now ensure that protection concepts, counselling centres, women's shelters, and the police do not just work on paper but reach where those affected need help," said GdP chief Jochen Kopelke to Redaktionsnetzwerk Deutschland.

Police officers face a highly sensitive and often dangerous situation in every operation, he added.

On Tuesday it was revealed that recorded cases of domestic violence had reached a new high in 2025. This is according to figures from the Federal Criminal Police Office published by the federal government.

Last year, 342 women and girls in Germany were fatally injured, 209 of whom were caused by violence in the partnership or family.

Since 1999, November 25th has been officially designated by the United Nations as the day for the elimination of violence against women, known as 'Orange Day.'

**Green party proposes fund to save village pubs**

In rural regions the village pub can be at the centre of small communities, yet many small bars and restaurants are closing across Germany, and as they do loneliness increases and cohesion crumbles as trust in politics and institutions also declines.

In a motion that the Green parliamentary group leadership intends to submit to the Bundestag in December, the party therefore proposes a fund worth ten million euros to save Germany's village pubs.![A Kneipe in Berlin in 1998](https://apiwp.thelocal.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/5817817-1-scaled.jpg)

The money would be designated to support village pubs as well as clubhouses, shops, and co-working spaces that are open to everyone. It would also go toward other start-ups and advisory services in some cases.

"We don't need expensive prestige projects, but low-threshold meeting places for everyone – young and old," said Green parliamentary group leader Katharina Dröge to Bild newspaper.

The Green's proposal notes similar programs are taking off elsewhere in Europe, including in Spain which has funded village pubs since 2024, or Ireland which will support pubs with 15 million euros by 2027.
