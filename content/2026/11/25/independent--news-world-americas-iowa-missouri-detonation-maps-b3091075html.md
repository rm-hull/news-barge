The Army has issued new maps hours before it detonates nearly 70 barrels of lead azide in Iowa, showing a toxic cloud spreading toward northern Missouri.

The maps showed lead dust from Wednesday morning’s blast traveling southwest from the Iowa Army Ammunition Plant in Middletown into northeastern Missouri between 9 a.m. and 5 p.m. Central Time. Officials chose Wednesday because they said it had the most favorable weather conditions.

Federal officials also warned Tuesday that vulnerable individuals such as pregnant women and children should evacuate the area.

“If they are women who are pregnant, children under the age of 6, or children in general, and individuals who may have chronic disease, you definitely want to be out of that area,” Sean Kaufman, the principal deputy assistant secretary for preparedness and response at the Department of Health and Human Services, said at a press conference in Iowa, *The Washington Sun* reported.

“I would evacuate that area that’s signaled in the model. Outside of that area, if you’re close to that proximity and you’re uncomfortable, I think you need to do what you need to do to take care of your family.”

![The new maps showed lead dust from Wednesday morning’s blast traveling southwest from the Iowa Army Ammunition Plant in Middletown into northeastern Missouri between 9 a.m. and 5 p.m. Central Time](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/24/23/25/original-(1).jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Iowa residents have received an alert from federal emergency officials who said some people within five miles southwest of the detonation site may be evacuated before the controlled detonation and that people living five to 20 miles away should stay indoors with windows shut.

Locals are spending their Thanksgiving week taping up windows in their homes and buying supplies like masks and gloves instead of preparing for the holiday on Thursday.

Resident Bob Kemp was supposed to travel down south for Thanksgiving, but was compelled to stay and watch over his and his sister’s homes.

“I’m going to stay here and see how things go,” Kemp told WQAD News 8. “If they would have blown it today, I probably would have went to Texas for Thanksgiving, but since that’s not happening, it’s going to happen on Wednesday, that changed all our plans.”

![Federal officials also warned Tuesday that vulnerable individuals such as pregnant women and children should evacuate the area, telling concerned locals, ‘You definitely want to be out of that area’](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/25/0/24/original.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The blast was initially scheduled for last Friday, but the Army was held up in court over health and environmental concerns. A federal appeals court ultimately ruled that the Environmental Protection Agency had adequate protections to minimize the spread of lead dust.

But residents are still worried about the potential effects of lead exposure, which is especially harmful for children. Even low levels of lead in a child’s blood can seriously harm their health, including brain damage and slowed growth and development, according to the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention.

“I’m really angry about this. I feel like we’re guinea pigs,” Krys Plate, a retired second-grade teacher who lives with her husband and their two teenage daughters two miles south of the fence line of the Iowa Army Ammunition Plant, told *The New York Times*.

The Army has advised everyone in the affected areas — but especially pregnant women, children and people with chronic diseases — to avoid outdoor activities until further notice. State health officials in neighboring Illinois have warned residents to stay inside for 24 to 48 hours after the detonation.

People may be exposed to lead, not just from breathing it in but also from surfaces where the dust lands, the Army notes. Once it’s safe to leave their homes, the Army advised people to clean surfaces that may be touched by children, including play equipment, tables and chairs and handrails.

As the public becomes more concerned about lead dust filling the air, President Donald Trump has insisted that the detonation is necessary — even calling on Governor-elect Rob Sand to agree to it if he doesn’t want blood on his hands, despite the Democrat not taking office until January.

“The Experts, in particular the United States Military, feel that this approach will be a far safer one than allowing the situation to fester, not knowing when they will detonate by themselves,” Trumpwrote on Truth Social Monday before the appeal court’s decision allowing the detonation to proceed.

“If [Sand] or the Courts do not agree, we will have no choice but to not proceed with the controlled removal of these hazardous materials, and be forced to hold the incoming Governor responsible for any and all death or destruction caused by this very unfortunate, and longterm, situation,” the president added.

Later Monday, Sand wrote on X that the Trump administration “should make this decision based on the information they have from the people they brought in to do the job months ago and have been on the ground in Middletown for weeks.”
