Covid levels are rising around the country ahead of the winter holidays, new federal data shows.

Levels are the highest in the Southeast and Southwest, according to the U.S. Centers for Disease Control the Prevention wastewater tracker from November 8-14.

That includes Nevada, Utah, Tennessee and Florida - though levels are also deemed “very high” in Connecticut. Nearly 20 other states are shown with “high” levels from coast to coast.

“As of November 18, 2026, we estimate that COVID-19 infections are growing or likely growing in 34 states, declining or likely declining in 3 states and not changing in 12 states,” the CDC says.

Nearly 8,000 Americans have died following Covid infections this year, and hospitalizations are on the rise.

![As tens of millions of Americans travel for the holidays, Covid is spreading across the country](https://static.independent.co.uk/2025/11/25/14/25/US-HOLIDAY-TRAVEL-6o4b3pcm.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

How many Covid cases the U.S. has seen this year is unknown because the CDC no longer records that data. But the percentage of positive tests for Covid was at 3.66 percent November 14 - up from 3.55 percent the week prior.

The surge comes as more than 81 million Americans are expected to travel for Thanksgiving, per data from the American Automobile Association.

There is a heightened risk of spread at airports and on public transportation because Covid travels via respiratory droplets in the air and on surfaces.

![This map made from data collected between November 8-14, 2026, shows Covid-19 in wastewater across U.S. states in dark purple have the highest levels, states in red are high, states in orange are moderate and those in green and blue-grey are low or very low](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/25/14/20/MAP.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)
![This CDC map using data from November 18, 2026, shows where covid levels are growing across the nation. States in dark purple are growing, while light purple is likely growing and those in teal are likely declining and those in dark teal are declining](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/25/14/42/CDC-GROWING-MAP.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Getting an updated Covid shot that targets the latest strains of the virus can help to prevent severe infection. The most common strains are offshoots of Omicron, which started spreading widely in winter 2021-2022.

Just 14 percent of U.S. adults reported having received the 2026 vaccine by October 31, according to the CDC. This year’s shot is offered for free at pharmacies and doctors’ offices with most health insurance plans. Otherwise, it may cost a couple hundred dollars out of pocket.

Doctors say everyone six months and older should get the vaccine. People over the age of 65 “should receive a shot every six months,” the Association of American Medical Colleges says.

This group is at higher risk of severe outcomes, including pneumonia, organ failure and death.
