Two men are accused of stealing “the Duke of Edinburgh cufflinks” and a clock during a suspected break-in at a country estate belonging to Prince William and Kate in Norfolk.

Mirgen Doku, 34, of Courtfield Gardens in London, and Arilen Ndoci, 32, of no fixed address, appeared at Norwich Magistrates’ Court on Wednesday over the alleged incident at Anmer Hall on Monday evening.

They are charged with one count of burglary and one of trespass.

The court heard the men, both Albanian nationals, are accused of stealing six pairs of cufflinks and a clock. One of the pairs of cufflinks was described as “the Duke of Edinburgh cufflinks”.

![Anmer Hall in Norfolk, a country retreat used by William and Kate, where a break-in was reported on Monday night](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/24/14/79c7a79e707efab6443c60ec37276681Y29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzk1NjE1MzA2-2.18601745.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

During the court hearing on Wednesday, the pair, helped by Albanian interpreters, spoke to confirm their names and dates of birth.

It is understood that no members of the royal family were present during the suspected break-in.

The force said a police helicopter, drones and police dogs were deployed to assist searches, and an investigation into the incident is ongoing.

The secluded red-brick Georgian mansion is on the private Sandringham Estate in Norfolk and is a short drive from Sandringham House, one of the King’s private residences.

It was given to William and Kate as a gift by Queen Elizabeth II after their wedding, and has its own private swimming pool, a full-size artificial grass tennis court and vast grounds.

![Mirgen Doku and Arilen Ndoci appeared at Norwich Magistrates’ Court](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/25/14/01M89HTN0AB9DNZ9FN3SPKWMH0.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Since moving in, a £1.5m refurbishment has been carried out on the mansion, including a new roof to the bolt hole and a new kitchen.

Kensington Palace previously said it would not comment on security matters.

News of the suspected break-in came on Tuesday as Prince William was pictured arriving at Downing Street for the first time in a visit to prime minister Andy Burnham, as he marked another step in his role as a future head of state.

Mr Burnham’s official spokesperson declined to comment on the incident at the royal family’s home. They said it was for the police and representatives of the Prince of Wales to speak to.
