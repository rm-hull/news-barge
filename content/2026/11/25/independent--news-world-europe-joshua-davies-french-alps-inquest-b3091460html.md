A young man from London fell 200 feet to his death while trying to retrieve his mobile phone from a ski slope in the French Alps, a court heard.

Joshua Davies, 25, from Putney in southwest London, died on 18 December last year while on a trip with four school friends at Villaroger in the Savoie region of the Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes in south-eastern France, an inquest at Inner West London Coroner’s Court heard on Tuesday.

About 2.25pm that day, he and one of his friends, William, got into trouble while skiing off-piste.

Mr Davies, who worked as an asset manager for investment firm Ruffer, attempted to call for help but dropped his phone.

In an attempt to get it back, he removed his skis and sat down so he could slide down to get it on his backside.

![Joshua Davies was on holiday in Villaroger in the Savoie department in the Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/25/13/45/_Davies_TRIANGLENEWS_5.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

However, according to documentary evidence given by William, Mr Davies picked up speed about five or six metres into his descent and was unable to stop before going out of view.

He was found at the bottom of the 200-foot slope by emergency services, who aimed to resuscitate him by performing CPR, but it was unsuccessful.

He was pronounced dead at the scene.

The court heard that the gulley, or couloir, where Mr Davies fell did not have much snow cover, and that rocks and ice were visible in it.

Mr Davies died after suffering multiple unsurvivable injuries to his head, chest and abdomen, the inquest heard.

His helmet and ski glasses had come off during the fall, and the helmet was badly damaged.

At 1,200m, Villaroger is the lowest of the mountainside villages in Les Arcs and is popular with British skiers and snowboarders.

It is known as a quieter resort with no nightclubs and only a few restaurants.

In a statement, his family said that Mr Davies was a “kind” and “talented” young man who loved sport, the outdoors and cooking.

They said: “He was loyal, and he was always there for his friends and his family. He had a natural curiosity.”

Since his death, a charitable foundation has been set up in his memory.

Joshua had attended Durham University, where he graduated with a degree in Economics with Management.

Prior to that, he had attended the prestigious Oundle School in Northamptonshire, where fees can reach more than £20,000 a year, and where he had got A\* grades in A-level Economics and Maths and an A grade in A-level French.

Coroner Jean Harkin made a finding that Mr Davies had suffered an accidental death caused by multiple unsurvivable injuries.

She said: “I offer my sincere condolences to his family.”
