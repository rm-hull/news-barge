The sale of outgoing Republican Rep. Elise Stefanik’s Washington, D.C. home has prompted questions after the congresswoman struggled to find a buyer, before the property mysteriously sold under “unusual arrangements” at an above-market rate.

Trump ally Stefanik, who did not seek reelection as the GOP representative in New York’s 21st congressional district, had been trying to sell her Capitol Hill three-bedroom home since September 2025, when it was listed at $2.195 million.

The price was lowered to $1.99 million two months later, and in February, it was taken off the market entirely. In June, the home was sold while still off the market for $2.9 million — $910,000 more than the original listing, *The Washington Post* reported.

The sale was classified as a “Sale Code 4,” indicating it was sold under an “unusual arrangement between seller and buyer and price is not market value,” the *Post* reported, citing records from the D.C. Office of Tax and Revenue.

A spokesperson for the tax agency told the outlet that the designation was given because “the sale price was elevated relative to its prior listing prices, and the circumstances surrounding the transaction could not be fully verified.”

*The Independent* has contacted Stefanik’s office for comment.

Her office told the *Post* that the $2.9 million sale was “within the range of prices for comparable historic townhouse sales in the neighborhood that range from $2 million to $4 million.”

There were more questions over the identity of the buyer, reportedly a Delaware company formed a month before the purchase called AFS Real Estate Holdings. But there was little information available about them, including who owns it, according to the *Post*.

Stefanik’s office told the newspaper that the home was sold in a “commercially reasonable transaction” and the congresswoman has “duly disclosed all required information on her annual personal financial disclosure reports during her time in Congress in accordance with House Ethics.”

Her office added that the sale “complies with the law and House Ethics and will be accurately reported on Congresswoman Stefanik’s upcoming annual personal financial disclosure report as required by House Ethics.”

![Stefanik’s office said the sale was ‘within the range of prices for comparable historic townhouse sales in the neighborhood that range from $2 million to $4 million.’ The congresswoman is a close ally of President Donald Trump](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/25/17/2251631492..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Stefanik is a loyal ally of President Donald Trump. He initially nominated her in 2024 to serve as ambassador to the United Nations, but had to rescind her nomination given the Republicans’ narrow majority in the House.

In December last year, Stefanik withdrew from the New York gubernatorial race and announced that she would not seek reelection to Congress and intended to spend more time with her family.

Bruce Blakeman, the Republican nominee, lost the election to incumbent New York Gov. Kathy Hochul by a wide margin.

MAGA’s Anthony Constantino is set to replace Stefanik in January after he narrowly defeated the Democratic challenger, Blake Gendebien.
