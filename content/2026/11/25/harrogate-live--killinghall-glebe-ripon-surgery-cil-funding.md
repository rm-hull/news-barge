The Glebe playground in Killinghall and a new car park at Park Street Surgery, Ripon, are up for developer cash. The council Executive decides on 1 December.

The Glebe playground in Killinghall and a surgery car park in Ripon are among nine projects proposed for developer money across North Yorkshire. Councillors on North Yorkshire Council’s Executive will consider the list on Tuesday 1 December.

The money comes from the Community Infrastructure Levy, or CIL. The council says more than £5 million could go to the nine schemes, according to its announcement on 23 November.

## Overview

* **Killinghall:** funding to “create a safer, more inclusive playground at The Glebe”.
* **Ripon:** funding to help deliver a new car park at Park Street Surgery.
* **Total:** more than £5 million across nine projects in North Yorkshire. The council has not said how much each of the two local schemes would get.
* **Decision:** the council’s Executive, Tuesday 1 December.

## The two local schemes

The Glebe is the village recreation ground, reached by footpaths from Otley Road. It has a playground, outdoor gym equipment and a sports area, and the parish council looks after it, Killinghall Parish Council’s website says.

In September the parish council was awarded a Local Fund grant from Two Ridings Community Foundation to refurbish ageing wooden play equipment there.

The Ripon scheme is a new car park at Park Street Surgery. The council’s announcement gives no size, cost or start date for it.

## The other seven projects

The other schemes are all outside the Harrogate district:

* a new neighbourhood healthcare building in Northallerton, with £2 million earmarked
* £400,000 for repairs to Bedale Hall
* the Selby Station Gateway scheme
* 24-hour prescription dispensers at Thirsk Surgery and at Sherburn and Rillington Surgery
* a padel tennis facility at The Galtres Centre in Easingwold
* a refurbished pavilion at Sheriff Hutton Bridge Cricket Club

## How CIL works

CIL is a charge on new building. Unlike a Section 106 agreement, which pays for things tied to one development, CIL money is pooled and can be spent more widely.

It is only charged in four of the old district areas: Hambleton, Harrogate, Ryedale and Selby. The money must be spent in the area where it was raised, the council says. That is why the Killinghall and Ripon schemes are the Harrogate area’s share of this list.

In 2025/26 the council collected more than £3.7 million through CIL. Of that, £593,000 went straight to town and parish councils for smaller schemes.

Cllr Mark Crane, the deputy leader, said CIL “allows us to pool contributions from multiple developments and direct them towards infrastructure projects that are not site-specific and can benefit more residents”.

## What it means for you

Nothing is decided yet. If the Executive approves the list on 1 December, the money is allocated, but the council has given no timetable for the work. The full report to councillors is published on the council’s committee website, which could not be reached on Wednesday 25 November. We will add each scheme’s sum once it can be read.
