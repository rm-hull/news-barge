Sales of foods considered “nature’s Ozempic” have surged in popularity, according to a new report released by Waitrose.

They include products such as porridge oats - dubbed “oatzempic” - that are considered naturally filling, mimicking the satiety produced by weight-loss drugs such as Ozempic.

The supermarket’s own-brand Scottish Oats have more than doubled in sales over the last year (up by 124 per cent), according to the report, which surveyed over 4,000 customers.

Searches for overnight oats on waitrose.com have soared by 160 per cent over the same period.

The report suggests that this interest is fuelled by social media trends promoting overnight oats as well as drink recipes blending oats and water to make “oatzempic”.

![Overnight oat trends are fuelling the popularity of ‘oatzempic’](https://static.independent.co.uk/2025/12/11/11/35/iStock-2214074450.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Dr Joanne Lunn, head of health and nutrition at Waitrose, said that GLP-1 use is now “reshaping food and drink trends”.

GLP-1 medications such as Ozempic and Mounjaro are usually prescribed to treat type 2 diabetes and obesity and mimic a natural hormone by the same name, which regulates blood sugar, digestion and appetite.

“As appetite patterns change, customers baskets are shifting towards smaller proportions, nutrient density and higher satiety (feeling full),” Dr Lunn said.

“The rise of ‘nature’s Ozempic’ - fuelled by ‘oatzempic’ and overnight oat recipes - has turbocharged demand for high-protein, high-fibre foods that offer sustained energy and support long-term wellness.”

In addition, data suggests that GLP-1 users are key decision-makers when it comes to food purchases, with eight out of 10 of them saying that they are the main grocery shopper in their household.

Of those who were using weight-loss medication, 88 per cent said that they were now shopping differently as a result.

![Sales of the supermarket’s own-brand Scottish Oats have more than doubled over the last year](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/24/18/57/iStock-1264651891.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

As part of this trend, Dr Lunn added that the company is seeing a “growing shift towards savoury porridge”.

She recommends swapping sweet ingredients for alternatives such as chilli oil, bone broth and roasted veg.

Such substitutions are the “ultimate hack for a low-sugar, high-satiety boost”, she said.

In addition, “fibre maths” also appears to be a priority for customers with an “explosion” of sales of Waitrose’s Savoury Seed and Nut Topper, which shot up by 236 per cent this year.

The NHS website advises that: “Semaglutide works for weight loss by reducing your appetite and helping you to feel fuller for longer.

“For type 2 diabetes it works by lowering your blood sugar levels and helping your body to make more insulin if it's needed. It also slows digestion which keeps your blood sugar levels stable after meals.

“If you need support to lose weight your doctor will refer you to a specialist weight management service. You'll only be prescribed semaglutide if your BMI meets the eligibility criteria.”
