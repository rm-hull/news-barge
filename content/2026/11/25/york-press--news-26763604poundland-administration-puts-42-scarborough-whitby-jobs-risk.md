## Poundland enters administration as 42 local jobs hang in the balance

Poundland enters administration as 42 local jobs hang in the balance *(Image: Google Maps)*

The future of 42 Poundland workers in Scarborough and Whitby remains uncertain after administrators were formally appointed to the struggling discount retailer.

Poundland's owner, Gordon Brothers, confirmed on (November 25) that the chain had entered administration after efforts to find a buyer for the business as a whole failed.

AlixPartners has been appointed as administrator and will now seek potential buyers for all or parts of the business while efforts continue to preserve jobs and stores.

Scarborough town mayor Thomas Murray said his first thoughts were with the 42 employees at the company's Scarborough and Whitby stores.

He told the Local Democracy Reporting Service (LDRS): "There is something particularly troubling about a country experiencing a cost-of-living crisis in which even its discount retailers are struggling to survive."

Alison Hume, MP for Scarborough and Whitby, also said her thoughts were with local workers "who, through no fault of their own, now face real uncertainty just weeks before Christmas".

The Labour MP added: "I've spoken to USDAW, the shopworkers' union, to ensure staff are getting the support and advice they need during what will be an incredibly worrying time for both them and their families.

"Serious questions must be asked of Gordon Brothers."

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21689843.jpg?type=mds-article-620)

A spokesperson for Gordon Brothers said: "First and foremost, our thoughts are with Poundland colleagues. We recognise how difficult the continued uncertainty is for them.

"Efforts to preserve as many jobs and stores as possible will continue, led by the administrators."

The firm said it had "explored every avenue to secure a sale of the whole company" but that Poundland had faced "several years of instability".

It added that management had worked to improve trading and deliver £100 million of cost savings over the past year, but warned the business remained vulnerable to inflation and any downturn in sales.

The appointment of administrators comes just weeks before Christmas and follows concerns raised by local politicians about the impact any closures could have on high streets and workers.

Mr Murray, a member of the Reform-led town council, also called on the Government to consider the impact of rising costs on businesses and said the company’s owners needed to answer questions about their handling of the retailer.

Labour MP, Ms Hume, told the LDRS that hard-working local employees should not be paying the price for "decisions taken in boardrooms thousands of miles away".

She welcomed business secretary Jonathan Reynolds "holding crunch talks with Gordon Brothers following pressure from the Prime Minister to find a practical solution that protects jobs."

Ms Hume added: "Even at this stage, every option to save jobs and safeguard livelihoods must be pursued. Right now, my focus is on the workers affected.

They deserve answers. They deserve far better than this."

A spokesperson for AlixPartners said: "We appreciate this is a deeply concerning time for all involved - customers, suppliers and, in particular, Poundland colleagues.

"Our priority now is to work with all stakeholders, including representatives from government and USDAW, as we identify the best possible route forward."

The administrators have invited interest from buyers looking to acquire all or part of the business and have also appealed to retailers with vacancies to come forward with employment opportunities for affected staff.

Poundland operates hundreds of stores across the UK and remains a prominent presence on many town centre high streets, including in Scarborough and Whitby.

The future of individual stores is expected to become clearer as the administration process progresses.
