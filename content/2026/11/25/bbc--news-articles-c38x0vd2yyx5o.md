A woman who had been drugged and raped by her husband for 20 years asked police: "Is it my fault he's done this?", a court heard.

The husband, who cannot be named to protect his wife's right to anonymity, has admitted a litany of offences against her - including rape, administering a substance with intent and inviting 13 other men to join the abuse.

Nine of those men have pleaded not guilty to sexual offences against her and are standing trial at Manchester Minshull Street Crown Court.

Giving evidence in court for the first time, the woman also said claims she had been "pretending to be asleep" during the abuse were a "load of rubbish".

The victim, in her 60s, was formally interviewed by Greater Manchester Police on 7 November last year, around two weeks after officers had arrived at her family home to arrest her husband.

In the interview, played to the jury, she told Det Con Anouska Barker: "I had no knowledge of what you told me had happened and I still can't believe it's true, and I think you have made a mistake."

She told police at the time of her husband's arrest that as far as she knew, the pair had not had a sexual relationship for about nine years due to her experiencing symptoms of the menopause.

The husband, she said, had never complained about it and the pair never discussed it.

Asked how their sex life was before, the woman described it as "just normal, vanilla".

Asked by Barker what her husband had "been like" in the years they had not been in a sexual relationship, the woman said: "Is this my fault? Is it my fault that he has done this?"

"Absolutely not, I don't want you to ever think that," Barker replied.

Describing their relationship, she said they had been "a loving couple".

"We did things together, we went out with other people. We've always had quite a lot of friends and a good social circle and done things."

During the interviews she said she had never consented or agreed to anyone else being involved in their sex life, and it had never been suggested by her husband.

The woman said on a number of occasions she recalled feeling particularly "wobbly" and "weird" after drinking the previous night.

She told Barker she had messaged a friend to say: "I feel like I'm wasting the day and this is ridiculous, and I need to pull myself together and cook some dinner."

The woman said on one occasion in the summer of 2024, she felt so strange that friends suggested she "see a doctor".

Questioned about how much alcohol she drank, she said she would often drink a bottle of wine a night but was "usually fine" and would take herself to bed and remove her make-up, and would normally be up by 09:00.

Shown photographs of the men alleged to have abused her while she was unconscious, she told the detective she did not recognise any of them or remember speaking to them.

After the jury were shown the video of her police interviews, the woman was asked to take the stand in the courtroom.

Katy Thorne KC, representing 43-year-old paramedic Jonathan Kirk, said her client's case was that the victim had spoken to him and had her eyes open in the early hours of 17 August 2025 - when he allegedly raped her while she was unconscious in her bedroom.

Thorne said: "If I were to suggest to you - you were pretending to be asleep?"

The victim spoke calmly and directly and replied: "That's a load of rubbish is what I'd say."

Thorne said that Kirk's version of events was that the husband had explained that "you two as a couple had a sleep fetish; you had been pretending to sleep", asking the woman: "Is that something you recall?

The wife responded: "No."

Judge Maurice Greene interjected and asked: "Do you have a sleep fetish?"

The victim said she did not.

The jury also heard another of the accused, taxi driver Philip Wild, claimed that the victim had "greeted him at the door" on the occasions he is alleged to have sexually assaulted and attempted to rape her, in August 2023 and February 2025 respectively.

In response to Pierce Power, defending Wild, she said: "No. That's a lie".

She was also questioned by the barrister representing Jordan Wallace, 31, who is alleged to have raped her on 4 May 2025.

Rachel White, defending Wallace, said she was not suggesting the victim had been "telling lies" but said she may have "not remembered" speaking to Wallace.

"It didn't happen," she said.

Counsel representing the other six men on trial did not ask questions.

Earlier in the trial Louise Oakley KC, prosecuting, said the woman had been subjected to the "barbaric fantasies" of her husband for more than two decades.

"The sad reality of this case is that there are many, many men whose fantasy is an unconscious and unresponsive woman," she said.

"[The husband] found a large number of them."

An investigation into his offending began after a team of undercover journalists in Germany infiltrated a channel on the messaging service Telegram which was being used by some men to share images of unconscious women appearing to be raped.

One of the users was traced by police to the husband's address, where he was arrested in October 2025.

Oakley said more than a million photographs and videos of his wife being abused were found on a Samsung mobile phone, which she described as a "confession booth" of his "darkest secrets".

On trial with alleged "contact offenders" Wild, Kirk and Wallace are amateur football coach Alan Keelan, 42; recruitment consultant Mohammed Sabir, 28; sheet metal worker Richard Townsend, 37, and Robert Stewart, 70, who was retired.

Also in the dock are Daniel Rayner and David Graves, described as alleged "non-contact offenders" because they did not physically attend the home address, who deny conspiracy to rape, conspiracy to assault by penetration and conspiracy to administer a substance with intent.

Four other men; Keith Fotheringham, 59, from Dundee in Scotland; Sean Peers, 38, from Stockport; Graham Brougham, 74, from Northwich in Cheshire, and non-contact offender Karl Lindsay, 55, from Taunton in Somerset, have already pleaded guilty to a number of offences.
