A Colorado city is spending about $23 million to buy two family farms hundreds of miles away — just for the water rights that come with the land.

Utility company Aurora Water agreed to pay two families about $11.5 million each for land and water rights of their century-old farms in Colorado’s Lower Arkansas Valley, about 200 miles south of the city of Aurora.

Under the agreements, the Hirakata family will receive $11.5 million for 667 acres of land at Rocky Ford and 654 shares of the Caitlin Canal water delivery system from the Arkansas River. The other purchase pays $11.5 million to the Hanagan family farm, near La Junta, for 729 acres of land and 706 shares of the Catlin Canal, the *Colorado Sun* reported.

The deal was unanimously approved by the Aurora City Council Monday, with the sales expected to close in December and January.

Under the arrangement, both families will lease their land and water back from Aurora for 10 years, with the option of extending for 20 more years, to continue farming. Meanwhile, Aurora will only divert the water for suburban use for up to three years out of every ten, 9 News reported.

![The city of Aurora, Colorado, is paying two family-owned farms $11.5 million for land and water rights to help the city](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/25/18/1248576271..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The farmers told the city council that they were optimistic about the deal, saying they hoped the money will help them continue to operate without exhausting their water supply, Fox 31 reported.

“This will give us financial stability and actually give us different opportunities to maybe expand into other acreage around and into other possible crops,” said Eric Hanagan, owner of the Hanagan family farm.

The money to purchase the two farms comes from Aurora Water’s capital fund, according to the report.

Alexandra Davis, an assistant general manager at Aurora Water, told 9 News that farms have older water rights than cities in Colorado, so growing cities often have to rely on farms for water.

![Aurora, Colorado is currently in Stage 1 water restrictions, which are conservation measures triggered by low water supply and drought](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/25/19/24/iStock-1354103813.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Aurora, which in April went into Stage 1 water restrictions - conservation measures triggered by low water supply and drought conditions - has agreements in the Arkansas Valley that allow it to move and use that water, Davis said.

Colorado has a history of cities buying farms and removing all the water, a practice known as “buy and dry.” This deal, however, lets the families continue to farm.

“If it was a buy and dry, I would probably be more opposed to it,” Glenn Hirakata, who owns the other farm, told 9 News.

![Aurora Water’s plan to purchase the two farms was approved of by city council on Monday](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/25/18/1248577681..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Jack Goble, general manager of the Lower Arkansas Valley Water Conservancy District in Rocky Ford, said the arrangement is better than a full buy and dry, but still leaves questions.

Goble and others have raised concerns about what happens when the lease ends, and whether a future city council could reverse Aurora’s commitment to keep the land in farming.

“If our water is going to help build Aurora’s future, Aurora has a responsibility to help protect ours,” Goble said.

Goble said he does not expect water to leave the farms for several years.

City officials said Aurora will likely not buy more farms in the Arkansas Valley, but is still acquiring water rights in the South Platte basin.

Aurora Mayor Mike Coffman said he hopes the deal can help smooth over the often hostile relationship cities and farms have over water rights.

“It’s just great to hear from them that we can work together in a way that benefits both of us, that has not been the case historically, unfortunately, in the state of Colorado,” Coffman said.
