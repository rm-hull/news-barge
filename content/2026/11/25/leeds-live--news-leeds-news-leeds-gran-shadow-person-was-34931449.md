# Leeds gran 'shadow of person she was' after boiling water hurled over her on school run

## Sue Varley and her husband moved to Morley in 1991 and things were peaceful for 10 years until her neighbour Ina Priestly moved in and eventually violent chaos ensued

A grandmother attacked with boiling water outside her home in Morley has been left a "shadow of the person she once was", her daughter has said.

Susan Varley was taking her four-year-old grandson to school on February 6, 2025 when her neighbour, Ina Priestly, approached carrying a kettle. She threw its boiling contents over the boy's head and into Sue's face before calmly returning to her own home.

Sue, who is disabled, suffered severe burns to her scalp, ears, neck, forehead and cheek. Medical experts confirmed her glasses had saved her eyesight.

Priestly pleaded guilty to grievous bodily harm with intent before trial. At Leeds Crown Court, she received a two-year community order rather than a custodial sentence because of her health complications. She had already spent seven months in prison on remand.

The order included a requirement to comply with up to 25 rehabilitation activities. An indefinite restraining order also banned her from contacting Sue directly or indirectly.

Speaking on Channel 5's At War with Next Door, Sue's daughter Donna Varley questioned the sentence and described the lasting impact on her mother, who was "too traumatised" to recount the attack herself.

Donna said: "To me, that is not justice at all. I still question why she did it. I don't understand why anybody would do that to any other human, never mind to somebody who is as kind as my mum."

The attack was captured on CCTV. Sue's grandson was sitting in a pushchair when Priestly appeared and shouted: "You're f\*\*\* ing stalking me."

Police arrived within 15 minutes, while paramedics treated Sue's burns. Priestly initially denied any wrongdoing before later admitting the offence.

Describing the footage, Donna said: "It was disgusting. I can't listen to it with sound. We couldn't even believe how she didn't even have a thought for my nephew. You don't just decide to walk outside with a kettle."

The violence followed years of disputes on the Morley street where Sue and her husband Stuart had moved in 1991 to raise their three children.

Donna remembered the first decade as peaceful, with neighbours looking out for one another. She said that changed after Priestly moved next door.

Although the new neighbours "seemed nice at first", disagreements soon arose over fences, barking dogs and a cat being in the garden. Donna alleged that Priestly threw rubbish and cat faeces into Sue's garden.

When Donna tried to resolve the tension, Priestly claimed Sue was "watching her". Donna explained that her mother had disabilities and memory loss and was simply spending time in her own garden.

Donna installed CCTV because she was concerned for Sue's wellbeing, but said Priestly viewed the cameras as further surveillance. Priestly also allegedly shouted abuse at Sue while she was walking in the street.

After Sue suffered a stroke, Donna suggested selling the house and moving away. Her mother refused.

Sue told her: "But why should I have to move? I've worked hard all my life to pay for this house. Why should it be me that moves when I'm not the problem."

Donna now hopes her mother can gradually recover from the ordeal. She said: "My mum is like the kindest soul you will ever meet. She was just so happy all the time. She became a shadow of the person she once was due to all this."

She added: "I am hoping my mum does get back to the person she used to be over time. I think it is going to take quite a long time but I think eventually she will realise she is gone and she is free from her."
