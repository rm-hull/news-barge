# Myrtle Tavern: No reservations left for Christmas as Leeds pub sells out its Sunday roasts

## The head chef of popular Leeds pub Myrtle Tavern said this year was the “quickest” they had ever been booked out for Sunday roasts in the run up to Christmas.

Located near Meanwood Valley Trail, Myrtle Tavern is a popular choice for a Sunday roast in Leeds. However, this December, the demand has been record-breaking.

On November 18, the pub announced that all Sunday roasts were sold out for December.

![](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/jpim-static/image/2026/11/24/14/13/Photo-11-01-2026-12-29-57.jpeg?trim=1273,0,751,12&crop=&width=640&quality=65)


Priced between £17.50 and £21, customers have six options: roast beef, gammon, pork, turkey, a mixture of four meats, and vegetarian nut roast.

Noah Dixon, head chef at Myrtle Tavern, told The Yorkshire Post this was the fastest the bookings had ever come in.

“Beef was the most popular this Sunday,” said Noah, adding that “all meals come with roasted carrots, parsnips, potatoes, red cabbage, gravy, and Yorkshire puddings.”

Myrtle Tavern is no stranger to going viral online; last year, there was a flurry of bookings after Gemma Collins reposted their content. According to Noah, customers are particularly drawn by the pub’s themed Christmas decorations.

![](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/jpim-static/image/2026/11/24/14/45/Photo-11-01-2026-12-31-55.jpeg?trim=1099,0,925,12&crop=&width=640&quality=65)


This year, the pub transformed into Narnia: customers enter through a ‘wardrobe’ of fur coats, passing a life-sized Aslan on their way to their tables.

“People have said it’s the best decorations we’ve had,” said Noah.

In addition to its Sunday roasts, Myrtle Tavern serves food from Wednesday to Saturday. Options include the ‘Myrtle Christmas Dinner,’ with stuffed turkey breast and rosemary potatoes, and customers can order traditional mulled wine.

Most Saturdays are already fully booked, and these reservations are also disappearing.

“Each year just gets busier,” said Noah. “When weekends fill up, customers try Fridays, then Thursdays, and Wednesdays.”

“If there is space, we’ll try to accommodate you,” he added. “We’re always open for drinks, if you want to come see the decorations.”
