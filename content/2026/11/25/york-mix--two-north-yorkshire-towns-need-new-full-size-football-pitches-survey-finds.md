New full-size artificial football pitches are needed in Bedale and Northallerton to address a shortage of facilities for local clubs, according to a major review of sports provision across North Yorkshire.

North Yorkshire Council’s first countywide playing pitch strategy identifies a shortfall of 3G pitches in the former Hambleton area and recommends increasing provision to meet demand.

The strategy, which will be considered by councillors on December 1, identifies a theoretical shortfall equivalent to 1.75 full-size 3G pitches.

It recommends considering one new pitch to serve Bedale and another for Northallerton.

The report highlights issues with existing facilities, including the small 3G pitch at Bedale Leisure and Wellbeing Hub.

The pitch, last refurbished in 2014, is rated as standard quality but is considered too small to meet training demand.

The strategy recommends resurfacing the facility during the lifetime of the plan, alongside consideration of additional full-size provision for the town.

Plans for a new 3G pitch are already being developed by 3G4Bedale, a group led by Bedale JFC.

Last year, North Yorkshire Council agreed to honour a £330,000 funding commitment towards a new pitch in the town following concerns the money could be withdrawn.

## Poor quality

In Northallerton, the existing 3G pitch at the Leisure and Wellbeing Hub is rated poor quality despite being resurfaced in 2015.

The report recommends resurfacing the pitch and examining the feasibility of developing a full-size 11-a-side 3G facility at the site.

Ainderby Leisure Park, used by Northallerton Town FC and Brompton Junior Football Club, is also identified as a potential location for a new full-size pitch.

![Bedale and Northallerton need new full-size 3G pitches, review finds](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/bedale-and-northallerton-need-new-full-size-3g-pitches-1.webp)

The report notes that one of the park’s youth football pitches is overplayed and recommends further feasibility work to determine whether a 3G facility could help meet local demand.

The strategy also highlights the need to safeguard hockey provision in the area.

It recommends ensuring a long-term sinking fund is in place for future replacement of the artificial grass pitch at Thirsk School and Sixth Form College, which is used by both Northallerton Hockey Club and Thirsk Hockey Club.

The report adds that if participation at Northallerton Hockey Club increases significantly, the possibility of developing a full-size artificial grass pitch in Northallerton should be explored.

Elsewhere, concerns are raised about the condition of the artificial pitch at Wensleydale School in Leyburn.

The North Riding FA has warned that the facility, used by Leyburn FC and other local clubs, is nearing the point at which it could be taken out of use.

The strategy recommends resurfacing the pitch to improve its quality and safeguard its future.

The findings form part of what North Yorkshire Council describes as the most comprehensive assessment of sports facilities ever undertaken across the county.

## Clear picture

Alongside a separate review of built sports facilities, the playing pitch strategy aims to guide investment in leisure and sporting infrastructure over the next two decades.

North Yorkshire Council’s executive member for leisure, Cllr Simon Myers, said: “Sport and physical activity play a vital role in helping people stay healthy and active.

“We want residents across North Yorkshire to have access to good-quality sports facilities and the opportunity to take part in sport and physical activity close to where they live.

“These strategies give us the clearest picture we have ever had of sports facilities across the county. They show where facilities are meeting local needs, where there are gaps in provision and where improvements may be needed in future.”

He said the assessments highlighted significant challenges, including ageing sports facilities and growing demand for pitches.

The report was developed in partnership with Sport England, national governing bodies, local clubs, schools and other organisations.
