Andy Burnham has indicated that EU students will pay higher tuition fees than their British counterparts when studying in the UK under an aspect of his deal with the bloc that is yet to be finalised.

The prime minister last week signed agreements in Brussels on food and agriculture exports, emissions trading and a youth experience scheme.

However, negotiations on tuition fees for Europeans attending UK universities are ongoing.

Mr Burnham said in the Commons on Wednesday that his deal would boost the UK’s economy and remove “mountains of red tape” for business.

However, Conservative leader Kemi Badenoch raised concerns over whether it would go ahead after the Council of the European Union stated it would not proceed until the issue of tuition fees had concluded.

Mr Burnham said the deal was “on a separate piece of paper, a separate deal”, pointing to the words of the European Commission.

At Prime Minister’s Questions, Mrs Badenoch went on to say: “It is very clear that this deal is not done, because he cannot just wish away those comments from the Council of the European Union, and he needs to be very clear on what he is doing on tuition fees.”

She then asked: “Will he now rule out EU students paying the same tuition fees as British students?”

In reply, Mr Burnham said: “She is referring to the European negotiating position, which is not the British negotiating position.

![Conservative party leader Kemi Badenoch speaking during Prime Minister's Questions](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/25/21/01M89QV816QBSKKY5NBHA0M6GJ.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“That has not been put on the table. It won’t be put on the table.”

The prime minister also accused Mrs Badenoch of dismissing the “billions” of pounds the deal would add to the UK’s economy.

Mr Burnham continued: “The party of low growth in the last decade are standing against an opportunity to boost growth.

“They keep on making the same old mistake, over and over again.

“They want us to stick with a failed Tory Brexit deal, the failed status quo. We won’t do that.”

Mrs Badenoch retorted: “What have we learned today? No answer on paying half a billion pounds to the EU.

“No answer on putting British students before EU students. No answer on whether the deal is contingent on the tuition fees agreement itself.

“He claims that he’s offering young people hope – graduates are facing the worst recruitment crisis on record.

“Hope doesn’t get them a job. Hope doesn’t pay their student loans. Hope doesn’t pay their energy bills, which have gone up by today by £443.

![Andy Burnham signed his deal with the EU last week. Pictured with European Commission president Ursula von der Leyen and European Council president Antonio Costa](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/20/18/2026-11-20T141640Z_730372277_RC2PROAJRCS4_RTRMADP_3_EU-BRITAIN-SUMMIT.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“The prime minister keeps promising young people a brighter future, but isn’t it the case that when we ask for the details, all we get is vibes?”

Mr Burnham replied: “I remember when I first came to this House and made the statement about my approach to this whole question, and she said I’d revealed a negotiating position, we wouldn’t get a deal.

“Well, we did get a deal, and we got a deal actually, which removed the threat from Made in Europe, from the British car industry, from the British steel industry, from the aluminium industry.

“She just minimises those things because she looks for the negative in everything.

“She has not got the ability to see the bigger picture and what this deal will do for the economy.”

It comes as Mr Burnham was warned that he has ensured the next general election will be “the Brexit election” by reopening the debate on Britain’s membership of the EU.

The claim was made on Wednesday as Reform leader Nigel Farage was joined by his long standing political ally businessman Arron Banks to launch a pro-Brexit campaign - Keep Britain Out - to rival the Stay Out launched last week by Lord Michael Gove.

The rival campaigns are a rerun of Lord Gove’s Vote Leave and Mr Farage’s Leave.EU with one leaning towards the Tories and the other towards Reform UK.
