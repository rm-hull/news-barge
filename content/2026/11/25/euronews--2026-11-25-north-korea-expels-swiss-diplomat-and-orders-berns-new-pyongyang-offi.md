## Switzerland opened the office on 19 October, when Foreign Minister Ignazio Cassis travelled to Pyongyang and met senior officials, including his counterpart Choe Son Hui.

North Korea has ordered Switzerland to shut its newly opened diplomatic office in Pyongyang and given its head 48 hours to leave the country in retaliation for Swiss sanctions on a children's camp linked to Russia.

State news agency KCNA reported on Wednesday that a senior Foreign Ministry official called in Astier Sylvain, who leads the Swiss mission, and handed him a written demand for the office's closure. Pyongyang declared him persona non grata.

The move follows a warning on Tuesday from Kim Yo Jong, the sister of leader Kim Jong Un and one of the regime's leading foreign policy voices, who called the sanctions an "open hostile act" that violated North Korean sovereignty.

The expulsion comes five weeks after Benn restarted operations in Pyongyang.

## **A rare Western channel under strain**

Switzerland opened the office on 19 October, when Foreign Minister Ignazio Cassis travelled to Pyongyang and met senior officials, including his counterpart Choe Son Hui.

KCNA said the talks covered "promoting dialogue and cooperation." The office reports to the Swiss embassy in Beijing, and Bern said it would help build direct contact with North Korean officials.

Switzerland has had diplomatic relations with North Korea since 1974 and remains one of the few Western states to keep a line open. Its development agency ran a cooperation office in Pyongyang from 1997, focused largely on humanitarian work, until it closed in March 2020, when North Korea sealed its borders during the COVID-19 pandemic.

The Swiss Foreign Ministry said on Tuesday that it wanted to keep channels of dialogue open despite political differences.

It stressed that reopening the office is separate from its sanctions policy, whether UN measures tied to North Korea's nuclear programme or the EU sanctions Switzerland has adopted over Russia's invasion of Ukraine.

## **The camp at the centre of the dispute**

Bern recently added the Songdowon International Children's Camp, in the coastal city of Wonsan, to its sanctions list over Russia's war in Ukraine.

Swiss authorities say the facility is part of programmes that bring Ukrainian children from Russian-occupied territory to North Korea, where they are exposed to pro-Russian propaganda.

The UK sanctioned the camp in May, and the EU followed in September, freezing its assets and banning financial dealings with it. Given North Korea's minimal financial links with Europe, the practical effect of these measures is uncertain.

Kim Yo Jong said the camp serves "legitimate" cooperation between Pyongyang and Moscow. She did not directly respond to Western allegations that abducted Ukrainian children have been sent there for re-education.

North Korea has moved steadily closer to Russia during the war, supplying thousands of troops and large quantities of weapons to support Vladimir Putin's invasion, possibly in return for aid and military technology.

Closer relations with Moscow and Beijing have underpinned Kim Jong Un's more assertive foreign policy as he seeks to end the country's isolation and present it as part of a bloc opposed to Washington.
