**Former England footballer Raheem Sterling has been given a suspended sentence after admitting dangerously driving his Lamborghini while inhaling nitrous oxide from a balloon on a motorway.**

The 31-year-old, from Berkshire, who later crashed into a metal gate during rush hour, gave no reaction as he was handed a 12-month jail term, suspended for 18 months, at Basingstoke Magistrates' Court.

Sterling had pleaded guilty to dangerous driving of a Lamborghini Urus and possessing nitrous oxide on the M25, the M3, the A327 and Minley Road on 28 May.

The judge said Sterling's use of the Class C drug had became a "maladaptive coping mechanism during a difficult period" in his career.

Sterling, who left the court without making any comment, was ordered to complete 100 hours unpaid work and was banned from driving for 24 months.

He was arrested after crashing near the M3 in Hampshire.

District judge Kirsty Allman told the court on Wednesday that Sterling was seen at 01:10 BST at a McDonald's in Hayes driving "erratically and at speed" while inhaling from a pink balloon.

She went on: "At 7.45am, having not slept, you made a deliberate and entirely selfish decision, to drive your vehicle without adequate sleep and whilst consuming a Class C drug, nitrous oxide."

She said this exposed a "large number of road users to a significant risk of serious harm by the manner of your driving on two motorways and in peak travel time that morning".

During the September hearing, prosecutor Simon Jones said a number of calls were made between 07:45 and 08:52 BST about a black sports car and the manner it was being driven.

Footage was released of the footballer driving dangerously

Witness Christopher Jennings called 999 after driving alongside Sterling's car on the M25, and "he was shocked by what he saw", Jones said, and made clear "he sighted the defendant inhaling from the balloon".

Fiona Jennings – driving on the southbound M3 – described the driving as "absolutely horrendous" and "swerving in and out of lanes", the court heard.

The court heard Sterling strayed into the central reservation of the M3, exited at junction 4A, and veered off the A327, crashing into a metal gate near a layby.

His vehicle sustained "substantial damage to the tyres and bodywork and was no longer capable of being driven", the court heard.

Sterling was taken to Basingstoke police investigation centre by officers, where he disclosed he had a "problem with nitrous oxide", said Jones.

The substance is legally sold for catering, medical and industrial purposes but is frequently used as a recreational drug, commonly known as laughing gas.

Sterling complied with an impairment test, but for a blood sample, Sterling asked to obtain legal advice before making a decision.

In the police sergeant's view "he was deliberately failing to provide a clear answer".

Sterling was later interviewed by police with legal representation present where he accepted he had been the driver and provided no comment answers about the driving.

Defending Sterling on Wednesday, Jason Bartfeld KC said there was no dispute that this was a "top category case" because of prolonged driving and "at times" serious impairment which would have been short periods within the driving.

But the defence did not accept the damage to the car was serious and said there was "confusion" over the failure to provide a specimen.

District Judge Kirsty Allman said: "I accept that your character has been positively exemplary.

"You enjoyed a highly successful professional football career and were widely regarded as a role model for many young people through your achievements and public profile."

He was given credit for his guilty pleas.

Sterling most recently played for Feyenoord in the Dutch Eredivisie, having left Chelsea in January, but made just eight appearances in the Netherlands.

In four years at Chelsea - including a season-long loan at Arsenal - he made 59 league appearances after signing from Manchester City in 2022.

With City he won four Premier League titles, having started his senior career with Liverpool before moving to Manchester in 2015.

He is currently without a club.

In the 2021 Queen's Birthday Honours, Sterling was made an MBE for services to racial equality in sport.
