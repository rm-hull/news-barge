## Australia's Digital Duty of Care Act is the latest global effort to regulate technology platforms, following in the footsteps of the EU's watershed digital legislation.

Australia’s parliament on Wednesday passed online safety laws that could expose tech giants to multibillion-dollar fines, in line with the European Union’s landmark digital regulations.

The Digital Duty of Care Act requires Big Tech to proactively reduce the risk of harm before it occurs, according to an Australian government statement. The legislation became law in the House of Representatives, where the center-left Labour Party government commands a majority, by 97 votes to 43.

The law would stand as a “powerful instrument to protect our children and our society from online harms by placing the onus on the tech platforms to ensure a safer online environment,” Prime Minister Anthony Albanese told Australia’s parliament.

## Australian online law falls in line with EU, British legislation

The legislation is similar to the EU’s Digital Services Act (DSA), passed in 2022, and the United Kingdom’s Online Safety Act passed a year later.

The DSA is part of a raft of European regulations, including the Digital Markets Act and the AI Act, which seek to make the online world safer, fairer and more transparent.

Earlier this month, the European Commission unveiled its proposed Digital Fairness Act, setting out new rules to tackle manipulative online practices, improve price transparency and strengthen protections for children.

## Opposition leader vows to repeal ‘authoritarian’ law

Australian opposition leader Angus Taylor told the country’s parliament that he would repeal the legislation if his conservative Liberal Party wins elections in 2028, describing the law as “authoritarian.”

“This bill is a blank check for political censorship,” Taylor said. “The bill is an attack on free speech that is absolutely central to our democracy."

The government struck a deal with the minor Greens party on Tuesday to push the legislation through the Senate, where no party holds a majority.

The Australian legislation sets minimum safety standards for online services, including games, apps and artificial intelligence chatbots, requiring them to protect Australians from seriously harmful material and conduct, the government said.
