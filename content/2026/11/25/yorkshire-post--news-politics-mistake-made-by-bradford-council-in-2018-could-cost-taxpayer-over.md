# Mistake made by Bradford Council in 2018 could cost taxpayer over £10million

## A mistake made by Bradford Council in 2018 could cost the taxpayer over £10million – a new report has claimed.

Council Tax premiums imposed on owners of long empty properties between 2019 and 2024 were illegal, and need to be paid back to the owners of those homes.

Bradford Council’s current leader has now called for a prominent Labour politician to resign over his part in the matter.

But the Labour group says the policy was introduced “in good faith” – and it was up to officers at the time to make sure it was implemented properly.

The issue related to a policy introduced by the Council in 2018. The then Labour Executive approved plans to charge the owners of long term empty properties higher Council Tax rates – a policy designed to encourage owners to bring empty homes back into use.

A report going to Bradford Council’s Executive next week says the decision was not then ratified by the full Council – meaning it was never fully authorised.

The report says: “The Council has identified that Council Tax premiums applied to properties that had been empty and unfurnished for more than two, five and ten years between April 2019 and March 2024 were not authorised through the decision-making process required by legislation and are therefore considered to have been unlawfully charged.

“The Council is taking corrective action to address this position.

“Affected Council Tax accounts will be reviewed and amended as necessary. Taxpayers impacted by these corrections will be contacted and, where appropriate, refunds issued or outstanding liabilities adjusted. The estimated financial consequence of correcting these charges is currently assessed at £10.1 million.”

The report says the £10.1m is made up of “approximately £7 million of refunds, £2.9 million of account adjustments and circa £0.2 million of administration costs.”

The Executive decision in 2018 meant that from April 2019 properties that had been empty for two years or more would be charged 100 per cent Council Tax.

From April 2020 owners of properties empty for over five years would be charged 200 per cent Council Tax.

From April 2021, owners of properties empty for over 10 years would be charged 300 per cent.

These premiums will now be refunded to the owners of the long empty homes.

The report adds: “Subsequent review identified that whilst the Council had the statutory power to impose these premiums, the increases were not approved through the decision making process required by legislation (i.e. Full Council approval was required).

“Whilst annual budget decisions incorporated assumptions regarding income generated from higher premium levels, those decisions did not in themselves constitute the statutory approval required to introduce or vary Council Tax premiums.”

The report does go on to say the policy did work, pointing out that it “has contributed to limiting growth in very long-term empty properties across the district.”

The Executive will be advised to suspend the premiums before re-introducing them from April – this time having gone through the proper procedures.

Bradford’s Reform group has called the issue and “awful error” and called for Labour Councillor Imran Khan, who was Deputy Leader of the Council at the time of the error, to resign.

Councillor Stephen Place, Leader of Bradford Council, said: “It is wholly unforgivable on Labour’s part.

“Owing to his personal role in this calamity whilst he served as Susan Hinchcliffe’s Deputy, I am calling on Councillor Khan to do the decent thing and resign.”

Executive Member for Corporate Resources, Councillor Ian Eglin, further commented: “This issue once again confirms Labour’s complacency whilst they were in charge at City Hall, coupled with poor senior leadership.

“They presided over significant overspend, a structural budget gap totalling £120 million, reliance on Exceptional Financial Support, an exorbitant 9.99 per cent Council Tax rise, and now it transpires, £10 million of unlawfully collected Council Tax.

“With Councillor Khan’s ongoing involvement, we cannot be confident that Labour have changed their ways for the better. Residents should be wary about ever trusting them with power again.”

Coun Imran Khan said: “Our policy was the right one to get more empty properties back into use while generating more funds for local services. We led a range of work in which we successfully brought empty properties back into use across the district.

“So I am obviously extremely displeased to say the least that the council has now announced it made a procedural error in its implementation of our policy on empty homes premiums.

“The council has said the officers involved at the time have since left the council, but regardless, as politicians we set the policies, and council officers are employed to implement them.

“We informed officers of our decision in good faith and we instructed them to implement it in the usual way. If alternative processes were required for this specific policy, they should have advised us that was the case, but they did not.

“The council has confirmed that officers became aware of the error because of tribunal appeal cases taking place elsewhere in the country. So it’s cheap politics from Reform to pretend otherwise, as they have contradicted the council statement and pretended that they discovered this technicality.

“Importantly, we significantly strengthened the council’s governance procedures in recent years. So moving forward, when Reform deploy a version of our policy they can be assured officers will implement it correctly.”
