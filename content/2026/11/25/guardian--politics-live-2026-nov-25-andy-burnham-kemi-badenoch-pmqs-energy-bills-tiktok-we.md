Burnham dismisses Badenoch as being 'opposite' of an influencer as he declines to apologise over 'vile video'

Here is the Press Association story about the influencer row about PMQs.

Andy Burnham has said TikToker Max Balegde’s “bomboclaat pussywagon” video outside No 10 was “wrong”, but refused to apologise for “engaging with people that young people themselves are looking to for their information”.

He came under fire at PMQs for the lewd clip which emerged from a reception he hosted for online celebrities earlier this month.

In the now-deleted post, Balegde lip-synced to a song with the lyrics “bomboclaat pussywagon chicken nuggets” outside the black door to the prime minister’s official address.

Bomboclaat is a Jamaican profanity referring to a menstrual cloth while pussy is a slang term for female genitals.

Kemi Badenoch said in the Commons: “This week, we saw videos of the prime minister hosting social media influencers in Downing Street.

“The words used by one of them on the steps of No 10 were so offensive that I will not repeat them in this house.

“Disgusting and pejorative comments about women’s private parts, obscene language that referred to white women as cheap meat. This is grotesque …

“Will the prime minister now apologise for debasing his office and for allowing that vile video to be recorded on the steps of No 10?”

Burnham responded: “An inappropriate video was taken down, and the creator has rightly apologised, and she was right to call out that because it was not acceptable.

“But I won’t apologise for engaging … with people that young people themselves are looking to for their information, and I think this house – not this individual – I’m talking about the wider point.

“She sneers at TikTokers. She sneers at influencers. Young people are looking to these people for their information, and I won’t apologise for doing that. I’ve already said it was wrong what was said, and it was taken down.”

The Labour leader earlier quipped that Conservative politicians were “the opposite of influencers”.

When Badenoch said the prime minister spent too much time talking to social media personalities, he said: “It’s true I have spoken to influencers, and then I have to come to this House and speak to people the opposite of influencers.”

Tories come out against proposed restoration plan for Palace of Westminster saying cost could make it 'another HS2'

MPs have been debating an opposition day motion on proposals for a multi-billion pound refurbishment of the Palace of Westminster. The project, known as restoration and renewal, has been talked about for more than a decade, but key decisions repeatedly get postponed because the potential cost is so high.

As Peter Walker reported in a story recently, a committee has warned that, without action, the Commons is at risk of burning down in a Notre Dame-style fire.

MPs have to vote on decisions of this kind and usually they are regarded as non-partisan “house” matters.

But today the Conservatives have tabled a motion calling for a full rethink on the grounds that the proposed costs are too high. It says:

That this house expresses grave concern at the enormous projected costs of the Restoration and Renewal Programme, estimated at between £11.1bn and £39.2bn; believes that the scale of expenditure contemplated is particularly difficult to justify given the severe pressure on public finances; recognises the urgent need to address serious deficiencies in the Palace of Westminster’s fabric, infrastructure and fire safety, while expressing concern at the absence of an agreed budget or affordable scope of works to do so and the continuing failure to establish effective governance, financial control and accountability; and questions the assumptions underlying the proposals for the extensive decant of parliament, the resulting disruption to parliamentary proceedings and the substantial additional costs involved.

Andy Burnham has tabled an amendment saying the project should go ahead. It says:

Leave out from “house” to end and insert “recognises that it has a responsibility to repair, secure and protect the Palace of Westminster, part of a UNESCO World Heritage Site of global historic and symbolic significance, for future generations; notes the previous government’s failure to support parliament in implementing a credible Restoration and Renewal Programme in 14 years; further notes that this indecision has added significant costs to the remediation required and that further delay would only increase costs and risk catastrophic failure of the buildings, putting the safety of those who work on and visit the parliamentary estate at risk … notes that the Restoration and Renewal Programme will support thousands of jobs across the country; further notes that the phase one works will deliver value for money for the taxpayer by capping initial costs; and supports the government’s commitment to table a motion for debate so that individual [MPs] can take a decision in a proper and informed way before the end of the year.”

Jesse Norman, the shadow leader of the Commons who opened the debate, accused Labour and the Lib Dems of complacency.

Astonishing complacency among Labour and LibDems in the House today about the eye-watering costs of R&R. Not a budget, no defined scope, no defined schedule, just a “forecast” of £11bn to £39bn. Everyone agrees we should address fire and other safety issues, but not at the cost of another HS2.

True to form, the Lib Dems are whipping their MPs to ABSTAIN on the Restoration and Renewal of the Palace of Westminster. Perhaps something about the possible £39bn cost? They seem petrified about allowing their members a free vote on Government business.

Burnham says he is open minded about whether to introduce staggered age of consent law

Andy Burnham has confirmed that he is open-minded about moving to a staggered age of consent. This is a proposal, revived after the Russell Brand conviction and the David Walliams scandal, that would make sex with 16 and 17-year-olds lawful for young people, but not much older people. So, for example, it could be lawful for a 19-year-old man to have sex with a 16-year-old girl, but not for a 39-year-old man to do so.

Asked about this in a pooled TV interview, Burnham said:

I’m not jumping to any fixed position and I’m not saying we will or won’t [have a staggered age of consent] but I think it’s right to have the discussion because I want to lead a government that is relentless in tackling violence against women and girls from the very top.

And I know these issues cannot just be there and left ignored for many years.

We just see repeated examples in the media, in politics, in other professions where powerful people use their position for sexual purposes. And it’s wrong and it needs to be called out. And I’m afraid to say the age of consent does have to come into this debate.

Earlier, at PMQs, in response to a question from Labour’s Nadia Whittome, Burnham said he would establish a group of experts – such as judges, lawyers, academics, and campaigners – to advise the government on how courts deal with rape myths. (See 12.19pm.)

Burnham told Whittome:

Today, we have said that we are inviting survivors, parents and young people to take part in a national conversation on these issues – on false rape myths of third-party consent, the age of consent and wider abuses of power. Her expert group can sit as part of that.

Burnham looking for new EU adviser as Michael Ellam steps down

Andy Burnham is recruiting a new negotiator with the European Union, as his current top official is stepping down just as the prime minister is seeking to bolster ties with the bloc, the Press Association reports.

Michael Ellam, who secured the UK-EU deal unveiled at last week’s Brussels summit, will leave his role at the end of the year, PA says. The former aide to Gordon Brown was appointed in January last year to help steer then-prime minister Keir Starmer’s efforts to “reset” relations with the EU.

EU relations minister Hamish Falconer said:

As a former negotiator, I know what it’s like to be at the heart of critical discussions and Michael has been exemplary. His tireless work and countless hours have helped secure a better deal for Britain that will help grow our economy, cut red tape and increase opportunities for young people.

Over 20 years he’s served his country diligently, whether during the global financial crisis or taking on the prime minister’s EU agenda. His contribution has been truly outstanding.

Bridget Phillipson urges Tories to recognise 'reality' of violence against women, and stop 'weaponising' it as anti-migrant issue

Alexandra Topping

Bridget Phillipson, the minister for women and equalities, used a Commons statement on white ribbon day to tell the Tories to recognise the “reality” of violence against women, and to stop “weaponising” it as an issue against migrants.

Phillipson was speaking during a statement on white ribbon day, which marks the importance of ending violence against women and girls.

The shadow equalities minister, Joy Morrissey, asked about the safety of women in Piddington, where some residents have raised concerns following the announcement of a planned government asylum accommodation centre.

She is not the only person to have made this point. Suella Braverman, the Reform UK equalities spokesperson, has visited the village to pledge her party’s support for the women there, who she waid were “terrified” and “angry”. But she was not in the Commons today for the white ribbon day statement, and none of the other Reform UK MPs were there either.

Responding to Morrissey, Phillipson said that ministers had to root out all violence against women and girls, but pointed out that women were most likely to face such violence from men “living in the same home as them”. She said:

I take seriously the contribution that [Morrissey] raised where it comes to Piddington.

But I think it would be both wrong and dangerous to suggest that violence against women is only committed by some men from some kinds of backgrounds. We have a responsibility to root it out wherever it happens and whoever carries it out.

And the reality is that the vast majority of abuse in this country committed against women is by those who know them well, who are meant to love them, and are usually living in the same home as them.

And the sooner the party opposite got to grips with that reality and stopped weaponising violence against women and girls, we would be in a much stronger position to change and advance it.

As long as they always go on making this a question of ethnic background rather than about appalling male violence, we will do women a disservice.

Farage launches Keep Britain Out group opposing rejoining EU, saying he hopes referendum never happens

Nigel Farage and Arron Banks are speaking now at the launch of Keep Britain Out, their campaign against the UK rejoining the EU. Just as in 2016 there were two rival leave campaigns – Vote Leave, the official one, which was dominated by Tories, and Leave.EU, the Ukip-aligned one – there are now two groups campaigning to keep Britain out of the EU. Stay Out, the successor to Vote Leave, launched over a week ago. Farage and Banks are now launching their successor to Leave.EU, Keep Britain Out.

Banks said:

Rejoining or this so-called reset is not progress. It is surrender. It hands the rules that will govern AI, agri-tech, and the high-growth industries of the next thirty years straight back to Brussels – areas where Britain can and should and in some cases already does lead. It massively disadvantages our own young people, who are understandably leaving Britain in droves. We don’t want that. We want them to stay in Britain and live the British dream here that we can help build for them.”

This campaign isn’t about us, and it isn’t just about 2016. It’s about tomorrow. It’s about the future. It’s about the next generation.

Farage said that he hoped that Keep Britain Out would not be needed. He hoped a future referendum would never be called, he said. But it was being set up as an “insurance vehicle”.

Andy Burnham’s statement about the nuclear test veterans was probably more revealing than the PMQs that preceeded it. The announcement about the independent panel came yesterday, but Burnham’s opening statement was powerful and it opened a window into what motivates him as PM. He sees it, rightly, as another Hillsborough, a defining event in his life. Most PMs ultimately have a rather benign and trusting view of the British establishment. Burnham is more sceptical.

Kemi Badenoch gave a reasonably supportive response to the Burnham statement, but she always presents as someone who thinks being leader of the opposition means she has to oppose everything and she did not fully appreciate that today was a day to sideline partisan obsessions.

Earlier, in PMQs, another weakness was on display – her hyper-obsession with the priorities of the online right. She asked perfectly reasonable questions about Europe, without really making any fresh progress. Burnham says he felt full apology to nuclear test veterans should come when report published

Ed Davey, the Lib Dem leader, also welcomed the announcement. He asked Burnham if he thought nuclear test veterans should be getting an apology today.

Burnham said he had considered making an apology today, but decided it would be better coming after the report, or the interim report. He said:

I could have made such an apology today, but I would rather it to be a full, meaningful apology for the full extent of what is revealed so that it doesn’t appear glib or not serious.

I remember I worked with David Cameron, who made the apology from this government despatch box, on the publication of the Hillsborough independent panel.

And it feels to me that that is the right time to do it.

But I am absolutely certain on 3 October next year or around that time, I will be standing here doing that.

Kemi Badenoch responded to the statement. She praised the test veterans. She also asked if any money had been set aside for compensation, and she said that, given the importance of veterans, Burnham should also look again at the provisions in the Northern Ireland Troubles bill, which she said is “reopening the door to legal proceedings that remove protections against another group of elderly veterans who served our country”.

Burnham thanked Badenoch for welcoming the announcement, but said the debate about the Northern Ireland Troubles bill was for another day.
