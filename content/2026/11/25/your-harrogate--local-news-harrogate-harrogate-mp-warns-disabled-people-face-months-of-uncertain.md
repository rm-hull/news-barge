Local MP Tom Gordon has called on the Government to tell young disabled people what planned changes to Personal Independence Payment (PIP) will mean for them.

The Lib Dem MP for Harrogate and Knaresborough welcomed plans to fast-track claims for people with serious, well-evidenced conditions and end repeated reviews for those whose conditions are lifelong, progressive or unlikely to improve.

The Government accepted both recommendations after Disability Minister Sir Stephen Timms presented his review of PIP to the House of Commons. Disabled people and their organisations helped write the report.

Ministers will consider the remaining recommendations alongside Alan Milburn’s review of young people and work. A White Paper is due early next year, with legislation to follow.

The Liberal Democrats say Milburn’s proposals would cut disability support for 18- to 24-year-olds by £3.6 billion.

Tom Gordon said:

> “Ending repeated, stressful reassessments for people whose conditions will never improve, and fast-tracking those with the most serious illnesses, is the right thing to do. Credit for that belongs to the disabled people who helped write this review.
>
> “But disabled young people now face months of uncertainty, with Stephen Timms recommending one thing and Alan Milburn another. The Government must not waste time in setting out its approach.
>
> “Families in Harrogate and Knaresborough who rely on PIP, and the carers for whom PIP is the gateway to Carer's Allowance, deserve to know where they stand.
>
> "Last year the Government's own figures showed its original PIP plans would have pushed 250,000 people, including 50,000 children, into poverty. We cannot let that happen again by the back door.”

The Liberal Democrats have called for a full assessment of how the changes could affect poverty levels and access to Motability, Blue Badges and Carer’s Allowance.

The party also wants disabled people to help design the new assessment process.

It has called for Carer’s Allowance to be reduced gradually as earnings rise, so carers can take on more paid work without losing the full payment at once.
