By Donald Trump’s own admission, the president’s blatant intervention to rescue Jensen Huang from exile after resigning from Nvidia relied on his former criminal defense attorney, now serving as the nation’s top law enforcement official.

Trump, brazenly confident after Nvidia stocks rallied with Huang’s arm-twist return, declared the company’s CEO was “exonerated.” Trump is energized by well-performing stocks in which he has personally invested millions, and he sees Wall Street’s success as analogous to the American economy generally. Trump wanted Huang “exonerated” because Wall Street, he claimed, depended on it.

But to hear Attorney General Todd Blanche explain it: “I don’t know what you’re talking about.”

During a press conference Tuesday, Blanche used his platform to scold “leaks” to the press, but it was his own boss who leaked to a reporter at PBS news of Huang’s return before the markets even opened. Immediately after Nvidia’s announcement, Trump rolled out a press release of his own on Truth Social, declaring that his attorney general had “confirmed that the U.S. Government does not possess evidence that he has engaged in any unlawful conduct.”

According to *The Wall Street Journal*, Blanche told Trump and Vice President JD Vance over the weekend that Huang was not the target or subject of any Justice Department probes and determined that there was not any evidence to suggest he had broken any laws or posed a national security risk.

![Attorney General Todd Blanche rebuffed questions about Donald Trump’s reliance on the Justice Department to help install Jensen Huang as CEO of Nvidia](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/25/17/2306565417..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

If true, it’s an astonishing admission from the nation’s top law enforcement officer that seems to undermine his own administration’s investigations and disregards a mountain of intelligence fears and public criticism about Huang’s alleged role in violating U.S. trade policy.

According to Trump, Blanche assured the president — who has been publicly urging Nvidia to bring Huang back into the fold — that Huang was in the clear despite a federal criminal probe into the alleged Chinese spy he was seeing, a Securities and Exchange Commission investigation, state-level criminal investigations and consumer-protection probes into whether China illegally obtained chips and if Nvidia misled the public, and class-action lawsuit accusing Huang and Nvidia of concealing ties to Megaspeed.

By Monday morning, Trump — after speaking to Nvidia’s board — was back on the job.

Moments after Nvidia’s announcement, Trump jumped on the phone with CNBC for nearly an hour to brag about it, apparently feeling so confident in his scheme that he proclaimed Huang “did nothing wrong” and even admitted that he protected Elon Musk during his first administration.

![Trump says Blanche ‘exonerated’ Huang, whose company is the subject of national security concerns, federal and state probes and a class-action lawsuit](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/25/17/2026-11-24T133209Z_355193340_RC2SKLA3ZSOA_RTRMADP_3_NVIDIA-CEO-CHINA.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The next day, Blanche erupted at a reporter over what he called “hypothetical questions” about investigations into the embattled CEO that his own boss admitted to.

“What does that mean, ‘Had Huang been investigated’ For what?” Blanche said, sounding irritated at the question on so many Americans’ minds. “I don’t understand the question.”

Asked how the Justice Department could rule out potential criminal liability while investigations are ongoing, Blanche barked: “I don’t know what you’re talking about.”

He does, of course, but won’t say.

“To the extent that the Department of Justice had information he was committing crimes, or something like this, that’s something I would’ve certainly told the president, if it were true,” Blanche said.

But Secretary of State Marco Rubio reportedly warned top officials last year that Huang was having an affair with a woman with ties to Chinese intelligence. Still, Trump maintained close ties to Nvidia’s CEO and, upon learning about the affair, decided not to take action, calling him an “honorable guy.” Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick, meanwhile, was “doing what the president wanted” when it came to Nvidia selling chips to Chinese companies, *The New York Times* previously reported.

Administration officials and Republicans who enabled the president are now “trying to find the guy who did this.” Republican Sen. Tom Cotton called the Nvidia scandal “the tip of the iceberg,” and it’s not going away merely because “Jensen Huang has his job back.”

![Trump, who has long seen success on Wall Street as analogous to the American economy generally, boasted about his efforts to get Huang back at Nvidia moments after the company announced his return](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/24/14/2026-11-24T110016Z_703340578_RC2G8EABEPTL_RTRMADP_3_NVIDIA-CEO-BOARD.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

But there is no “deep state” cover up. Political operatives deeply embedded into the most-obscure intelligence-making recesses of the federal government aren’t manipulating the White House.

Instead, Trump himself is going out of his way to admit that he was responsible, that he marshaled an enormous amount of government resources and turned to his former lawyer for help, and that his rule by executive fiat is the cost of doing business to save the stock market.

Cotton and other members of Congress want to know who gave the president those assurances and on what basis, questions that will likely emerge in committee hearings in the coming weeks and months as Democrats take control of both chambers.

During his Senate confirmation hearing, Blanche portrayed himself as independent of the president. He later told *The New York Times* that “there was this misperception that there’s this separation and independence between the Department of Justice and the White House, and everybody likes to champion that, and they accused me of not suffering from independence.”

“I don’t,” he said.

The Supreme Court’s “immunity” decision in 2024 — stemming from a case tied to Blanche as Trump’s personal attorney — opened the door for Trump’s reliance on the Justice Department to solve his political problems. In his majority opinion for the court, Chief Justice John Roberts wrote that “the president may discuss potential investigations and prosecutions with his Attorney General and other Justice Department officials to carry out his constitutional duty to ‘take care that the laws be faithfully executed.’”

The same day Huang was brought back to Nvidia, a federal appeals court gave Trump another chance to try to move his hush money case from state court to federal jurisdiction, where the president is hoping his Justice Department — now run by the man who steered his criminal defense — can make his convictions disappear under that same “immunity” doctrine.

Trump has created an accountability-free zone of impunity for himself and a tech oligarchy that bet on his presidency. Blanche must now go out of his way to defend it, even if it means pretending he didn’t understand the question.
