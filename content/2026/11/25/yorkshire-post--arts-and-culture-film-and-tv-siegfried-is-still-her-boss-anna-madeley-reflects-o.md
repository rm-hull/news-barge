# ‘Siegfried is still her boss’: Anna Madeley reflects on Mrs Hall's feelings as All Creatures Great and Small Christmas returns

## As Christmas 1946 prepares to dawn in Darrowby, All Creatures Great and Small fans are pondering just how the relationship between Siegfried Farnon and Mrs Hall will play out.

5’s Christmas Special synopsis reveals that the friendship between the Skeldale housekeeper and head vet Siegfried has not been the same since his eleventh-hour calling off of his wedding to Dorothy.

Thanks to a circular walk with a poorly horse in season 7 episode 6, Siegfried - and viewers - now know how he feels about Mrs Hall - but what does she feel about him? Is there any reason to assume that she feels the same way and will fall into his arms under The Drovers mistletoe?

Speaking to the Yorkshire Post at the studios near Harrogate during filming, Anna Madeley reflected on the challenges Mrs Hall faces regarding her future relationship with Siegfried.

![Togetherness, whatever that means. Mrs Hall (Anna Madeley), Helen still in her dressing gown (Rachel Shenton) and Siegfried (Samuel West) at Skeldale this coming Christmas. ©5 Broadcasting Limited / © Playground](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOjIzZjZlMzZmLWMyYTMtNDZkMy04ZDVkLTNiNmY2ODVlN2IxMTo4ODAyYWZjNS02MjFhLTRiMWQtYTgxNi0yNTQ3MDJhM2UzN2M=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


She first compared her former relationship with Gerald, saying: “I think what was fun is it was also quite easy. They got on quite easily and well, and that was lovely.

“Mrs Hall and Mr Farnon, there's bridges to build and there's a lot to navigate for that to be that kind of relationship,” she said. “It's much more complicated, more history and obviously they both lost someone.

“There’s status and social background. So there's lots of areas in which, yes, they cross over and they understand, but he's still her boss. That whole world requires thought.”

At the railway station in series 6, Siegfried famously called out “Audrey”, but they have since continued to address each other as Mrs Hall and Mr Farnon.

![Mrs Hall played by Anna Madeley, who wants to see more stories showing how the post-war developments affected women in society. ©5 Broadcasting Limited / © Playground](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOjdiNDRjMDAyLWE0NTctNGUyMS1hYjQxLTk4N2I2OTA0NGE2OTo0MTU5N2Y2OS05YmY5LTRhNzktYWUzZS1mYTZjMzc3YzZlMGE=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


“I think it allows them to work together,” Anna said. “Actually, it's sort of polite and respectful. To me it doesn't feel distant as much as appropriate to what their status is with one another.

“And it is always a negotiation of, how much can you step into a situation … he is the boss of the house and you have to roll with what his decision is.

“It's quite an interesting thing on set sometimes thinking, ‘actually no, that's stepping too far, that's behaving like his wife,” she added. “It can be very subtle shifts and I think also it's a very comfortable boundary to have that there, because then whatever other feelings are going on, or affections or complications, it allows you to keep going as housekeeper and boss.

“It's also sort of the social convention at the time, isn't it? All of their clients are all Mr and Mrs. It's interesting because this did start to change after the war.

“I've noticed sometimes that backstage at work, like when you're doing theatre and they do the calls, it would be ‘Mr West’, ‘Miss Madeley’. A couple of times I've noticed them not do that, and you kind of go ‘Huh?’ Sometimes it's quite nice.”

For Mrs Hall’s future storylines, Anna said she hoped they would reflect how the post-war world affects women: “That generation of women who have had their kids, grandchildren, that's all great and lovely and now what are you going to do, when you are community-minded and you do want to get stuck in? I hope we're going to keep moving forwards on that trajectory.”

All Creatures Great and Small will return for Christmas on 5.
