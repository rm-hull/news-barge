# Fat Pigeon: This ‘secret’ Leeds bakery has gone viral - it only opens on Saturdays

## Fat Pigeon Bakery has gone viral as a “secret” spot for tasty pastries in Leeds, and I’ve been wanting to visit for months.

Located on Broad Lane, the bakery is tucked behind a residential property in Bramley. Despite its growing popularity, the bakery still feels hidden in plain sight. In fact, beneath recent Instagram videos, some residents have commented that they hadn’t realised Fat Pigeon Bakery was there, despite only living a few streets away.

Ruth Diskin opened Fat Pigeon in 2023, converting a 19th-century outbuilding into a bakery at the bottom of her garden. She only opens once a week: on Saturdays between 9am and 12pm.

Last weekend, I decided to visit and test the bakery for myself.

Parking near Bramley Baths on a frosty Saturday morning, I wondered whether it would be difficult to find.

![](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/jpim-static/image/2026/11/23/14/06/Chocolate-cake-topped-with-poached-quince-toasted-hazelnut-and-whipped-milk-chocolate-ganache.jpeg?trim=14,5,0,0&crop=&width=640&quality=65)


I shouldn’t have worried. Outside the house, there was a floor-height sign; it had been hidden by a row of parked cars when I’d driven past, but was instantly visible from the pavement.

At 9:30am, there was already a procession of customers, emerging from the driveway with thick coats and brown paper bags – presumably stuffed with baked goods. I’d read that the bakery consistently sells out, and hoped there was still enough selection. Perhaps I should have arrived earlier.

Fat Pigeon had attracted a mixed crowd: a young family, dog walkers, couples, and joggers. Following a tarmacked driveway to the back of the house, I joined a queue of approximately 10 people, waiting just under 15 minutes to reach the counter.

To my delight, there was still a large selection, from sourdough loaves to croissants and cream-stuffed cruffins, topped with wobbling marshmallows.

The menu changes regularly, and I scanned handwritten lists pinned onto large fridges. Most items were priced at around £3.

There were trays of pastries waiting to be displayed, and baskets of sourdough near the checkout, where Ruth was taking card payments. Everything arranged on the counter was unlabelled, but staff happily explained each item that caught my eye. Many of the items were inspired by seasonal produce; right now, quince is having a moment.

With family visiting, I had the perfect excuse to order a large selection. I settled on a ‘pick-n-mix’ of bread and pastries: quince chocolate cake, a s'mores cruffin, a Danish pastry topped with blue cheese, olive focaccia, and a cheese and leek pastry. It totalled at £17, which felt like an absolute steal.

![](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/jpim-static/image/2026/11/23/14/00/A-Danish-pastry-topped-with-mushroom-garlic-and-blue-cheese.jpeg?trim=0,5,14,0&crop=&width=640&quality=65)


Back at home, we split the different pastries, swapping and sharing our thoughts. Some were real conversation starters, especially the cruffin, which was stacked with toppings.

As a blend between a croissant and a muffin, ‘cruffins’ have a slightly crunchier exterior. Filled with soft cream, this cruffin combined pillowy marshmallow with gingery notes from the spiced biscuit. Between the presentation and the flavours, it went down a treat.

However, my favourite was the focaccia. Drenched in olive oil and full of plump olives, it had a spongy centre and salt-encrusted exterior, scattered with fragrant sprigs of rosemary. I had planned to eat the focaccia with homemade dips, but it was flavoursome enough to tear into as a standalone dish.

Ultimately, Fat Pigeon put a novelty twist on my Saturday morning. Between its local popularity and the ‘blink-and-you-miss-it’ opening hours, you’ve got to be organised and expect a short wait. Wrapped up in a warm coat, though, queuing just felt like part of the experience. It was fun joining the rush.

I’ll be interested to see how the menu changes across different seasons. I’ll be back for those new pastries – and a repeat order of that focaccia.
