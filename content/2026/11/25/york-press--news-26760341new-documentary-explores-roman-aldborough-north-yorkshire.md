## Mystery of missing Roman gold coin explored in Aldborough documentary

Rob in the Roman Garden *(Image: Rob Cotterill-Smith)*

A major new documentary is set to bring the story of a forgotten Roman town back to life.

The film explores Aldborough in North Yorkshire, once known to the Romans as Isurium Brigantum, and aims to introduce a wider audience to one of Yorkshire’s most important archaeological sites.

Nearly 2,000 years ago, Aldborough was the administrative centre of the Brigantes, the largest tribal confederation in Roman Britain.

The documentary is the latest project by Rob Cotterill-Smith, creator of the Rob’s Roman Britain YouTube channel.

Mr Cotterill-Smith said: "I think Aldborough is a fascinating place, and one that deserves to be much better known.

"We often think of Roman Britain in terms of Hadrian's Wall, York or the great sites in the south of the country, but Isurium Brigantum was once one of the most important towns in the north of the Roman province.

"What makes this project particularly special is being able to go beyond simply looking at the archaeological remains.

"I've had the opportunity to meet the people who care for the site, speak to experts and explore some of the stories that might otherwise go unnoticed."

With the support of English Heritage and the Friends of Roman Aldborough, Mr Cotterill-Smith was granted permission to film on location.

The documentary features interviews with experts and site volunteers, and explores the surviving Roman remains, including sections of the original town walls, public buildings and mosaic pavements.

The film also visits Aldborough’s Roman garden, where volunteers are recreating Roman horticultural practices, and St Andrew’s Church, where evidence of the ancient town survives beneath and around the present building.

One of the lesser-known stories featured in the documentary is the mystery of a Roman gold coin discovered in Aldborough in 1770.

The coin disappeared shortly after its discovery and has been missing for more than 250 years.

Mr Cotterill-Smith discusses the find with Dr Nick Summerton, who raises the possibility that a similar coin held by the British Museum could be the same one.

The documentary marks the largest on-location project to date for Mr Cotterill-Smith’s channel, which launched in February 2025.

Rob’s Roman Britain explores the history and archaeology of Roman Britain and the wider Roman world through documentaries, site visits and historical investigations.

Mr Cotterill-Smith said the documentary would not have been possible without the support of those who care for and study the site.

He said: "This has been the biggest filming undertaking I've completed for the channel so far, and I'm incredibly grateful to English Heritage, the Friends of Roman Aldborough and everyone who gave their time and knowledge to help make it possible.

"I hope the documentary encourages more people to discover Aldborough and explore this remarkable part of Yorkshire's history for themselves."

Today, Aldborough is a quiet village, but it preserves substantial evidence of its Roman heritage.

Archaeological discoveries have revealed the scale and sophistication of the settlement, which once bustled with public buildings, houses, workshops and impressive mosaics.

The documentary aims to raise awareness of the site’s historical importance and encourage more people to visit and explore Yorkshire’s Roman past.

It will be released on Saturday, November 28, 2026, on the Rob’s Roman Britain YouTube channel.
