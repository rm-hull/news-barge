Six relatives were returning from a fishing trip when they were killed in a plane crash off the Mississippi coast on Tuesday, according to their family.

The Cessna 210 the family was flying on went down between Pascagoula and Horn Island, which is part of the Gulf Islands National Seashore.

The pilot was identified as Brent Stokes, the 50-year-old president of a marine contracting company based in Fort Myers, Florida.

Also killed were his 16-year-old son, Hunter Stokes; his sister, Arlyn Knox, 43; her husband, Michael Knox, 43; and their two sons, 12-year-old Charles Holden Knox and 10-year-old Truett Knox, according to the local coroner.

![Arlyn Knox, top left, and her husband Michael Knox, top right, with their two sons Charles Holden Knox, bottom right, and Truett Knox, standing for a photo in 2026](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/25/19/10/AP26329659831047.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Arlyn and Michael Knox founded Knox Technologies, a Fort Myers-based IT company, in 2012, according to the company's website.

The Knox and Stokes families confirmed the deaths in an emailed statement provided to *The Associated Press.*

“They died doing what they loved: celebrating the start of a wonderful holiday season with a fishing trip together, before heading home to be with the rest of our family,” the families wrote.

“In the middle of our grief, we are holding on to that, and to how much love they gave us and how much love surrounds us now. We will carry them with us always.”

## Plane crash response

The air traffic control regional tower in Mobile, Alabama, reported at about 3:45 pm on Tuesday that it had lost contact with the aircraft, according to the Coast Guard.

Responders searched for hours, Coast Guard spokesperson Juan Ros said.

At first they found only three life jackets, a life raft and the plane's cabin floating on the water. Divers later found the bodies in the fuselage.

The flight tracking website FlightAware said the Cessna was en route from New Orleans to Fort Myers, Florida.

Jackson County Coroner Bruce Lynd Jr. said autopsies were being performed on Brent Stokes and Michael Knox, who was also a licensed pilot.

The Page Field airport in Fort Myers said in a statement that its community is “shocked and devastated.”

“We have lost great friends and outstanding aviators,” the airport added.

Cmdr. Trevor Blount, the Coast Guard coordinator for search and rescue in the Mobile area, called the situation heartbreaking.

“While this is not the outcome any of us had hoped for, we are grateful for the strong interagency coordination that enabled us to swiftly locate those aboard the aircraft and provide closure to their families,” he said in a statement.

Earlier this month, a Learjet 60 business jet nosedived into the ground in South Florida, killing all nine people on board.
