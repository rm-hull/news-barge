Homes and properties on a picturesque remote island were under threat on Wednesday night from an out-of-control bushfire that had sent smoke hundreds of kilometres south.

The blaze on Flinders Island in Bass Strait spread significantly on Wednesday after being started by lightning strikes.

![Police car on dirt track with bushfires burning in the distance](https://i.guim.co.uk/img/media/7db9f53cc8d54037c22c2fe78188194e742c2d63/0_0_1024_668/master/1024.jpg?width=445&dpr=1&s=none&crop=none)

As of Wednesday night, six emergency warnings were in place, including for the coastal settlement of Killiecrankie and nearby areas Leeka, Mount Tanner, Mount Boyes and Carnacs Flat.

Just before 9pm, the Tasmania fire Sservice warned that an uncontained bushfire at Palana was travelling towards Leeka and Mount Tanner.

The agency said conditions had moderated but people who were not prepared should be ready to leave for a safer place.

Some structures had been destroyed but it was too early to tell whether they were homes, the Tasmania Fire Service incident controller Steven Richardson said.

The Tasmanian premier, Jeremy Rockliff, said there would be government emergency assistance grants for people on the island who had to relocate due to the fires.

Applications for the grants – which included $250 per adult and $125 per child, with a maximum of $1,000 for any one family – would open from 2pm Thursday, Rockliff said in a social media post.

“For those on the frontline fighting the fire, thank you,” Rockliff said. “We could not be more grateful for your efforts.”

Robert Lawry, a community information officer at the Bureau of Meteorology, said the wind was expected to drop overnight and remain “really quite light” until later on Thursday, which should assist firefighting efforts.

“It’s generally a less windy situation for the next 24 hours,” he said on Wednesday night.

“The humidity will come up through the night as well, and that tends to be a positive for firefighting as well.”

Lawry urged people to check the TasALERT website for the most up to date information and instructions, and to follow authorities’ advice.

A Bureau of Meteorology post on X earlier on Wednesday featured a video showing a vast column of smoke being blown south across north-east Tasmania, as northerly winds pushed the plume across Bass Strait.

Roughly 3,000 hectares had been burned by the fire on Tuesday, before it spread south on Wednesday following a wind change.

Residents on the island, which is about 130,000 hectares in size and home to 900 people, posted photos of massive plumes of smoke on social media.

Mick Grimshaw, a farmer, helped friends move cattle from their property and said they had lost some fences.

“We were under the ashes. Right there. But we’ve got them safe,” he told ABC radio.

“The fire threat for this farm looks like it has abated a bit but certainly not further down the island.

“We can see the smoke front heading right down the guts of the island.”

![A ringtail possum near the burnt-out bush on Flinders Island](https://i.guim.co.uk/img/media/cb1ea78dcdd94a104f3ca6fa0a63fb8ca92696d7/0_0_1024_724/master/1024.jpg?width=445&dpr=1&s=none&crop=none)

Nearly 40 firefighters were working to control the bushfire, plus 12 parks and wildlife staff, five helicopters and three aircraft.

“Additional resources are being deployed and Tasmania Police is also helping evacuate people, particularly in the Leeka and Mount Tanner areas,” parks minister Nick Duigan said.

“There is a lot of smoke in the area and it’s now coming down Tasmania’s coast.”

Several roads were closed because of poor visibility due to smoke.

Flinders Island is known for high-quality exports, including beef, lamb, wool and seafood.
