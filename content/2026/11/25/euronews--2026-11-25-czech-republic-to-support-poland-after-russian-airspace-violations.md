## Prague's offer comes after Russian weapons entered Polish airspace during a large-scale attack on western Ukraine on 20 November.

After a series of Russian violations of Polish airspace, authorities in Prague have said they are ready to help Warsaw. The offer of support was announced by Defence Minister Jaromír Zůna and Chief of the General Staff Miroslav Hlaváč.

Zůna stressed that any involvement of the Czech armed forces would depend on the needs identified by Poland within NATO.

He also underlined that strengthening the security of countries on NATO's eastern flank remains one of the Czech military's most important tasks.

"This is our priority," Zůna said at a press conference. He also referred to Czech AH-1Z Viper military helicopters, which are currently deployed in Poland.

Miroslav Hlaváč outlined the military preparations in more detail. He said the armed forces had already begun assessing possible forms of support over the weekend.

"We have prepared scenarios for how we could help. I will present them to the minister, and we will wait for a Polish request and respond to it," the chief of the general staff said.

## **What equipment does the Czech military have?**

Prague has not yet said which specific forces or equipment it could send to Poland, but the Czech military operates several air-defence systems.

These include Israeli-made Spyder systems, although their operational capabilities are still being developed. They reached initial operational readiness relatively recently and are expected to become fully operational in 2028.

The Czech armed forces also continue to operate older Kub systems and Swedish RBS-70 and RBS-70NG systems designed to counter threats at very short range.

Swedish-made Gripen fighter jets are another important part of the Czech Republic's air-defence capabilities.

## **Czech government condemns Russia's actions**

Prime Minister Andrej Babiš strongly condemned Friday's violation of Polish airspace by Russia and said the Czech Republic was ready to cooperate with Warsaw.

In a statement posted on X, he stressed that the Czech Republic stood in solidarity with Poland and did not rule out providing concrete assistance.

"The Czech Republic firmly supports its Polish friends and is ready, if necessary, to provide assistance and cooperation to the neighbouring state," the prime minister wrote.

President Petr Pavel also expressed concern about the situation, warning that a similar incident could occur over Czech territory in the future.

Hlaváč said the Czech military had procedures in place for responding to this type of threat.

Prague's offer followed the events of 20 November, when Russian weapons entered Polish airspace during a large-scale attack on western Ukraine.

Polish investigators later said debris from three Russian Kh-101 cruise missiles had been found in the Lublin and Subcarpathian regions.

Another incident followed on the evening of 23 November, when a Russian unmanned aerial vehicle briefly entered Polish airspace, according to Polish officials.
