Disgraced Nvidia chief Jensen Huang is set to testify in front of Congress next week — and it turns out President Donald Trump has already given thought to a pardon if the reinstalled CEO gets himself into hot water.

In a Wednesday interview with *The New York Post*, the president said he was open to a pardon for Huang, but he doesn’t think it will come down to that because, in his opinion, the tech titan has nothing to hide.

“I don’t think he needs to take the Fifth. I think it’s an interesting story for people,” Trump said of Huang when he sits for an interview with the House Foreign Affairs Committee next Tuesday and appears before a hearing of the Senate Banking Committee next Thursday.

It has been a rollercoaster two weeks for the billionaire and his AI-chipmaking company after the tech boss admitted being unfaithful to his wife. He resigned as CEO on November 12 after Nvidia was rocked by allegations of his extramarital affairs, including with an alleged Chinese spy and another with a married subordinate Nvidia executive.

Then on Monday, Huang made an astonishing comeback after Trump declared the U.S. government “has no reason to believe that Jensen’s return to Nvidia poses any risk to national security.” Huang also had the backing of shareholders who saw Nvidia’s stock plummet and his friends in Silicon Valley, like Tesla and SpaceX CEO Elon Musk.

![Huang resigned as CEO on November 12 after Nvidia was rocked by allegations of his extramarital affairs](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/24/22/2026-11-24T114452Z_581690363_RC2TZHADFY0W_RTRMADP_3_NVIDIA-STOCKS.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The president admitted to CNBC he had leaned on Nvidia’s board of directors to reinstate him. “I spoke to them, let me put it that way,” Trump said Monday. “And I convinced them… I made the deal… The board decided to do what we wanted to do.”

Despite the White House’s backing, the decision drew immediate pushback from lawmakers on Capitol Hill, where concerns linger over national security and potential export control violations. And it is on the Hill where Huang is expected to be grilled over the international fiasco.

Only time will tell if Huang spills his guts to the committees or zips his lips — either way, it seems Trump is ready to once again help him out of a jam.
