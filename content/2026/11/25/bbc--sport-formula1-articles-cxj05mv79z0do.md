Williams have had a difficult 2026 - uncompetitive and now in breach of spending rules

**Williams broke Formula 1's cost cap during the 2025 season, governing body the FIA has found.**

The team say they "unintentionally" exceeded the cap by £627,000. Last season, the spending limit was $135m (£106m).

Alpine were also found to have broken the rules, although theirs was a procedural breach without overspending.

Both teams have reached so-called 'accepted breach agreements' with the FIA.

Williams' involves a financial penalty, the amount of which has not been disclosed, and a 1.5% reduction in permitted aerodynamic research and development time for the next 12 months.

Alpine have also been given an undisclosed financial penalty.

Both teams must foot the costs of the investigations by the FIA into their rule-breaking.

A Williams statement said: "The breach relates to administrative costs we incurred making changes to our company structure.

"We believed these costs fell outside of the cap. Following discussions with the FIA, we have agreed to resolve the matter in line with their interpretation that the costs should have been in cap.

"The FIA has noted that Williams did not gain any sporting or financial advantage, and nor did we ever seek to. It has also noted that we acted co-operatively and in good faith throughout the review process.

"Integrity is central to what Williams stands for and we can now close this case and remain focused on our future."

The FIA said in a statement: "There is no accusation or evidence that (Williams) sought at any time to act in bad faith, dishonestly or in a fraudulent manner, nor that it wilfully concealed any information from the cost-cap administration.

"The financial regulations are complex, and the breaches arose from an interpretation adopted by (Williams) in good faith.

"Williams has accepted the FIA's interpretation and the resulting breaches."

It said Williams' breach was the equivalent of a 0.48% overspend, which "falls at the lower end of the less than 5% minor overspend range".

Williams' costs were incurred as the team tried to update their operations in order to become more competitive.

Williams had targeted the new 2026 regulations as an opportunity to make a significant step forwards in the team's attempt to return to the front of F1.

Instead, the team have taken a step backwards, and after finishing fifth in 2025, are lying ninth in the constructors' championship with two races remaining this season.

Alpine said in a statement: "The team accepts that it unknowingly committed a procedural breach, which is a result of inaccuracies in the full-year reporting documentation for costs related to exclusions and adjustments.

"The errors and inaccuracies in the reporting were specifically related to non-sporting and operational costs which the CCA recognised didn't give the team any performance advantage, such as marketing activities, non-F1 activities, HR activities, employees' bonus costs, offsetting of costs, and costs of inventories."
