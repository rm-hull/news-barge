**We can bring down the cost of living for those who rent and are aspiring home owners, says** Geoff Beacon

Better lives? That means lives that are fairer and happier, which don’t ruin the only planet that supports human civilisation.

The 2025 version of the Local Plan fails to do that. It is a plan to keep property prices high to the benefit home owners and landowners. Homeowners who have benefitted from obscene rises in property values and landowners who make enormous planning gains.

They will also build developments for residents with high greenhouse emissions.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/york-emissions-carbon-footprint-graphic-ai.webp)

City of York Council is planning for 1,000 affordable homes on the York Central site next to York Station. However, to make a real difference tens of thousands of affordable homes are needed.

This will bring down the cost of living for those who rent and are aspiring home owners, ie our children and grandchildren.

If new homes are built for low carbon living this will be only a small contribution to the world’s climate crisis.

Even the high carbon residents of York’s outer suburbs do not emit as much greenhouse gases as the average American. York’s emissions are a very small drop in our world’s bucket of greenhouse gases.

The important job that the York Local Plan must do is to show the world that better lives are possible, which are fairer and happier, which don’t ruin the planet. I believe this can be done planning new settlements in York which are cheaper and ecologically sound.

## Controlling planning gain

We need a plan which is good for York and not limited to the parcels of land that landowners are willing to contribute. That could lead to a rerun of the 2025 Local Plan which benefited landowners and home owners at the expense of the rest of the population.

Increased powers given to the council will make it possible to cut the enormous burden planning gain puts on the housing market.

As noted in previous articles, a plot of land big enough for a house is valued at less than £1,000 at agricultural prices but becomes worth nearly £200,000 when the council gives planning permission for a home to be built.

This “planning gain” accrues to the landowner but strengthening of the powers of compulsory purchase has made it easier to control the landowner’s planning gain.

Google AI quotes an enormous figure for the total value of planning gain associated with the 2025 Local Plan:

*There is no single official figure for the total value of planning gain in the York Local Plan, as independent analysts and local commentators estimate the land-value uplift at roughly £150,000 to £200,000 per building plot or dwelling, translating to roughly £2.24 billion to over £3 billion across planned housing allocations depending on the housing total and period modelled. [1, 2, 3]*

I should include a disclaimer here. Google is repeating an estimate that I made in 2018 because noone else has made one. The Local Plan Inquiry refused my evidence that raised this issue.

## Environmentally sound settlements

The Local Plan should show case settlements that are environmentally sound as well as fairer – and fairer means cheaper to renters and first-time buyers, initially by taking planning gain out of the price of a house.

It will be useful to revisit the ideas of Ebenezer Howard (1850-1928), Raymond Unwin (1860-1940) and Count Peter Kropotkin (1840-1921).

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/ebenezer-howard-thoughts-on-new-cities.webp)

**Howard** combined the economic and social benefits of urban life with the space, fresh air, and greenery of the countryside.**Unwin** reshaped British housing by pairing arts and crafts design with low-density garden layouts and practical living spaces for working families.**Kropotkin** argued that housing is a basic human necessity rather than a commodity for profit or landlordism.

## Call for sites

York council has just completed it’s ‘call for sites’ asking for parcels of land to be put forward for possible developments.

Does this allow landowners to restrict development? Strictly speaking it may not but that is a likely effect. As mentioned above, this is likely to result in a rerun of the unfair 2025 plan.
