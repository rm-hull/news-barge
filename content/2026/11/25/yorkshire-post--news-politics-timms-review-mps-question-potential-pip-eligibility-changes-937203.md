# Timms review: MPs question potential Pip eligibility changes

## The Timms review into personal independence payment (Pip) said reforms could involve a “rebalancing process”.

MPs have called for greater detail around any changes to disability benefit eligibility, after it emerged that some claimants may get less money under potential reforms.

The year-long Timms review into personal independence payment (Pip), published yesterday, said reforms could involve a “rebalancing process” to ensure payments better reflect a person’s needs.

Disability Minister Sir Stephen Timms said the report did not make recommendations about eligibility, instead saying that would be up to the Government in a white paper next year.

However, review co-chair Sharon Brennan said such any “rebalancing” would likely lead to some people getting more based on their needs “and some people therefore might get less”.

Pip is currently paid to someone with a long-term physical or mental health condition or disability, and intended to help with everyday tasks and extra living costs. Many claimants are in work.

![Sir Stephen Timms. Credit: UK Parliament](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/jpim-static/image/2026/11/24/17/47/261124_Timms-Review-Final-Report-10_77122.jpg?trim=0,0,1048,0&width=640&quality=65)


The benefit was introduced in April 2013 and, under the current system, recipients can get between £30.30 and a maximum of £194.60 a week.

Total spending on Pip in 2026/27 is forecast to be £32.1bn, looks set to rise each year for the rest of the decade and is expected to reach £41.5bn in 2030/31.

Ms Brennan, who co-chaired the review with disability minister Sir Stephen Timms, told reporters: “We’ve looked at some principles around what eligibility should look like, and it should be much more tailored to real-life extra costs and the associated needs that come with that.

“As a result, it’s likely there will be a rebalancing process in which some people will get more related to their needs, and some people therefore might get less. But it’s for the Government to make decisions based on our report about eligibility and what that cost should look like.”

Leeds Central and Headingley MP Alex Sobel said: “The Pip system is not fit for purpose.

“I have long said that it is a dehumanising process, and that it needs to be as individual as the person claiming it. Therefore, I am pleased to see this first review since 2013.

“However, I need to see more detail in the coming white paper, about any potential changes to eligibility criteria, how these would be applied at the initial point of application, and if current applicants would need to reapply, before I make an overall judgment on any changes.

“Any changes must be carefully managed to ensure that people who struggle to advocate for themselves are not unfairly excluded.”

Normanton and Hemsworth MP Jon Trickett added: “The government now has the opportunity to break with that callous approach.

“But it cannot mean cuts to support, nor can it mean age-related reductions in payments.”
