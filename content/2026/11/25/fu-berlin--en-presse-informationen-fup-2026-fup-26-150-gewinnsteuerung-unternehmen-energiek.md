# Maximizing Profits in the Energy Crisis

## New study by economists shows that oil and gas companies downplayed their earnings following the start of the war in Ukraine

№ 150/2026 from Nov 25, 2026

Following the Russian attack on Ukraine in February 2022, energy costs rose sharply and many households and businesses felt the financial burden immediately. Oil and gas companies, however, were making extraordinary profits during this time. As public criticism of high energy costs grew, how did these companies handle their profits? A new study shows that European oil and gas firms likely downplayed their profits between 2022 and 2023. These earnings management practices were particularly prevalent in countries that introduced windfall profit taxes as well as in countries with strong democratic institutions. The study by Professor Jochen Bigus (Freie Universität Berlin), Professor Nadine Georgiou (University of Innsbruck), and Nils Hain (Freie Universität Berlin graduate) appeared in the *Journal of Business Ethics*(doi**:** 10.1007/s10551-026-06490-7).

“Companies can influence the profits they report because there is some room for discretion in accounting practices, for example, when calculating depreciation or provisions. Certain operational decisions can also be used to delay the recognition of profits. While this is within the parameters of lawful practices, it can lead to discrepancies between the published earnings and the actual financial situation of the oil and gas firm,” says accounting professor Jochen Bigus from the School of Business and Economics at Freie Universität Berlin.

The team of researchers found significant evidence in both forms of earnings management, namely accounting policies and operational decisions. However, they did observe a very pointed development whenever windfall profit taxes were introduced: In countries with this kind of tax, oil and gas firms reduced profits to a significantly larger extent than other businesses in the consumer goods sector, which served as the control group.

## Countering Public Criticism with Strategic Earnings Management?

Another remarkable finding of the study relates to the influence of democratic institutions. “We observed this type of earnings management much more prominently in oil and gas firms operating in countries with strong democracies. One reason for that could be that these companies may experience stronger pressure to reduce reported earnings in order to preserve legitimacy – active public discourse in more democratic environments likely heightens legitimacy concerns surrounding excessive profits,” says Nadine Georgiou from the University of Innsbruck.

They also found that oil and gas firms with low sustainability scores (based on ESG or ESGC criteria) exhibit a stronger propensity to engage in downward earnings management. Companies with stronger sustainability profiles may already benefit from a well-established ethical reputation.

## A Call for More Transparency in Profit Reporting

The researchers observed that by late 2023, by which time energy costs had dropped and public debate on especially high profits had dwindled, earnings management practices also shifted.

“Our analyses indicate that businesses do not adjust their profit reporting merely for accounting or tax purposes. Political and social expectations can also play a role,” says Bigus.

The authors also note that other explanations for these practices are also possible, such as changes to a firm’s compensation structures.

According to the researchers, their study raises important questions regarding transparency in earnings management. “When firms influence their profit reporting in times of economic crisis, it becomes more difficult for the public to judge the actual financial situation,” says Bigus. The authors therefore argue that during crises oil and gas companies should disclose performance measures that are less susceptible to earnings management, such as operating cash flow or EBITDA.
