# Halifax community kitchen won’t be allowed to cook chips

## A planned community café in north Halifax can serve cheap meals, but there’s one thing it won’t be allowed to cook: chips.

Calderdale Council approved plans to convert eight empty units on Turner Avenue South, Illingworth, into a community supermarket, meeting rooms, and employment support centre this week, but frying is off the table.

Applicant Company Shop Group (CSG) want to set up a Community Kitchen and Café “where members can enjoy good-quality meals and low prices”.

The South Yorkshire company operates membership-only community supermarkets across England selling surplus food and household goods at discounted prices to people on benefits.

![The propose scheme will take over empty offices on Turner Avenue South](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOjBjM2RkMTBkLTUyMDAtNDA2My04ZTAyLWNhYjdmZDM2ZmYwZjpkNDdjNDhjZi0wMTJhLTQ4ZDctOTUyOC02MmViM2EyNzdmNzA=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


CSG had already assured planning officers there will be no frying on-site, and that an extraction system will vent any smells out of a louvred door away from upstairs tenants.

But Environmental Health officers still had concerns about the proposed ventilation system and recommended improvements and restrictions on opening hours before the plan was accepted.

One of the planning conditions will be: “The kitchen facilities hereby approved shall not be used for any frying of food or any other cooking process that generates significant levels of grease, fumes, smoke or odours.”

According to planning documents, the site was previously occupied by the Together Housing office (formerly North Halifax Housing), but is now largely vacant with the shutters constantly down.

One supporter commented the development would be “a very worthwhile use of the building” and would make “a positive contribution to the local communities”.

In a report recommending approval, council officers said the development would bring the premises back into use, support the local economy, provide a new service for residents, and “contribute to the vitality and viability of the local shopping area”.

The applicant estimates around 140 visitors a day would use the facility, with membership generally limited to around 750 active members.

Members would typically be local and receiving means-tested benefits.

In their conclusion, officers said the benefits of bringing the premises back into use, supporting local economic activity, outweighed any limited impacts.
