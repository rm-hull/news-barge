The body of a woman has been found in the search for 20-year-old Kelsey Hunter who went missing in Dumfries and Galloway over the weekend.

The discovery was made in a wooded area in the village of Springholm, where an intensive search has been taking place over the last few days.

Formal identification has not yet taken place, but police said Ms Hunter’s family have been informed.

Police said a post-mortem examination will be carried out in due course.

![Formal identification has not yet taken place, but police said Ms Hunter’s family have been informed](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/24/19/01M87F6309PKRRWCQMZKPPKYAJ.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Ms Hunter was last seen in the nearby town of Castle Douglas at around 5pm on Saturday.

The discovery comes a day after Daniel McKnight, 21, appeared in private at Dumfries Sheriff Court charged with murder and attempting to defeat the ends of justice.

He did not submit a plea, and is due to appear again within eight days.

Extensive enquiries are continuing, with a number of resources are being used, including forensic specialists.

Detective Chief Inspector Stephen McGrath, of Police Scotland’s Major Investigation Team, said: "Our thoughts are with Kelsey's family at this extremely difficult time.

"They are completely heartbroken and continue to be supported by specialist officers. They have asked that their privacy is respected.

"A team of officers are continuing to work around the clock to get them answers.

"This is clearly a significant development and extensive work remains ongoing.

"We are building up a picture of Kelsey's movements in the days and weeks prior to her being reported missing, and I would like to thank everyone for their help so far.

![A forensic investigator at the scene near the village of Springholm, Dumfries and Galloway, during the search for Kelsey Hunter](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/24/16/6462eb40beb429f075dd426cc5b13b53Y29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzk1NjI0MzAw-2.86593685.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

"I would again appeal to any of her friends, who may have information and have not yet spoken to officers, to come forward.

"There will continue to be a police presence in the area while our enquiries continue.

"Anyone with any concerns, or any information, can approach these officers.

“Door-to-door enquiries are still being carried out and I would urge anyone who has any information, including CCTV or dashcam footage, to get in touch with us."

Local Area Commander Emma Dodds said: "We understand how concerning this will be for the local community, but we have a significant amount of resources dedicated to this investigation.

"There will be a lot of police activity in the area over the coming days as our enquiries continue.

"Anyone with any concerns, or information, can approach these officers – we are here to help."

Anyone with any information is asked to contact Police Scotland on 101, quoting incident number 2269 of 22 November.
