Proton and Windscribe are two of the most recommended VPNs available right now, but which should you buy?

Proton is one of our best VPNs: a heavily audited privacy player with an encrypted ecosystem you can use alongside, while Windscribe packs huge amounts of functionality for power users.

This year’s Black Friday deals make the choice trickier: Proton’s two-year VPN Plus plan is down to $2.49/month (75% off), while Windscribe Pro is $2.42/month with a 58% discount. Below, we compare the paid plans on features, security, speed, and price.

## Proton VPN vs Windscribe: Features

![Proton VPN on iPhone](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/SHGbmBuxgHmweVxr9SmYd6-1200-80.png)

Proton’s cheapest tier is VPN Plus, and it’s purely a VPN. Step up to Proton Unlimited, and you also get Proton Mail Plus, Proton Pass, 500 GB of encrypted Drive storage, and Premium calendar.

Windscribe takes the opposite approach with one flat Pro plan. You get R.O.B.E.R.T. – a highly customizable DNS-level blocker for ads, trackers, and malware – plus port forwarding, optional static IPs, and full custom configuration generators for WireGuard, OpenVPN, and IKEv2.

As far as core VPN tech goes, both obfuscate traffic with proprietary protocols – Proton’s Stealth and Windscribe’s WStunnel. Multihop comes in two forms: Proton’s Secure Core routes you through hardened servers in privacy-friendly jurisdictions like Iceland. Windscribe’s Double Hop lets you hand-pick any two locations. Proton additionally offers VPN Accelerator, which claims to speed up connections by processing traffic more efficiently, and alternative routing for censored networks.

On security and privacy, Proton VPN Plus puts up the stronger front: NetShield blocks ads, trackers, and malware at the DNS level, Tor-over-VPN servers let you browse the Tor network without the Tor Browser, the apps are open-source, and the no-logs policy has been repeatedly audited – all backed by Swiss jurisdiction.

Windscribe’s apps are open source too, and its VPN server infrastructure underwent independent security and penetration testing by PacketLabs in June 2024. The assessment examined its no-logs implementation, identified some logging issues, and a subsequent retest confirmed those issues had been fixed.

Its R.O.B.E.R.T. ad, tracker, and malware blocker also deserves mentioning, especially since it performed better than NetShield in our most recent testing.

Where Windscribe also leads is future-proofing: since October 2025, its desktop and mobile apps have offered post-quantum encryption on WireGuard connections. Proton has no equivalent yet, though it’s on the roadmap.

Although they rank among the fastest VPNs, our latest testing favored Windscribe at home and Proton abroad: on WireGuard, Windscribe hit 2054 Mbps locally versus Proton’s 1783 Mbps, but Proton was faster on a US-to-UK connection (1397 Mbps vs Windscribe's 1284 Mbps).

For streaming, both cracked multiple Netflix regions – including US, UK and Canada – Disney+ (occasionally on a second attempt), BBC iPlayer, and 9Now. However, Proton failed with ITVX and TVNZ+, which Windscribe handled, while Windscribe failed with YouTube US. Unfortunately, both couldn't access Prime Video across multiple countries.

## Proton VPN vs Windscribe: Pricing

![Windscribe Android app](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/8QB5cBfrXik7Cq3fyK9xEG-1200-80.jpg)

Proton VPN’s headline deal this Black Friday is VPN Plus over two years at $2.49/month, down 75% from the usual $9.99/month. This is billed as $59.76 upfront for 24 months before it renews at $83.88 every 12 months. Its Unlimited one-year plan is 50% off at $6.49/month, billed as $77.88 for the first 12 months before renewing at $119.88 every 12 months.

Windscribe’s Black Friday sale takes Pro from its usual $5.75/month to $2.42/month (58% off), billed as $29 every 12 months. Crucially, Windscribe renews at that same $29/year instead of jumping to a higher rate.

For the first year, it’s effectively a tie: $29/year for Windscribe versus $29.88/year for Proton Plus. At renewal, Windscribe stays at $29/year while Proton plans roughly double. Rather not commit long-term? Proton VPN Plus is $9.99/month, Unlimited $12.99/month, and Windscribe Pro $9.00/month.

Windscribe also lets you build your own plan at $1/month per location ($3 minimum, up to seven countries), which is great if you only need one or two countries year-round, though you’re limited to 10GB of data per location (an extra $1/month buys unlimited data and access R.O.B.E.R.T. access). The two providers offer free tiers too, but they’re much more limited.

With Windscribe, you can enjoy unlimited simultaneous connections. Proton caps simultaneous connections at 10 devices, which is nothing to sniff at, but may prove limiting for households with lots of devices.

Platform coverage is broadly matched: both ship polished Windows, macOS, Linux, Android, iOS, Apple TV, and Fire TV apps, plus browser extensions.

Proton has the much friendlier refund policy. It offers a full 30-day money-back guarantee versus Windscribe’s much shorter 7-day window, which comes with a catch: you must have used less than 10GB of data.

## Verdict

Proton VPN Plus is the winner here. Its no-logs promise is independently audited and backed by Swiss jurisdiction.

With this Black Friday deal, it’s effectively the same price as Windscribe Pro. Proton’s one cause for pause is the renewal: it jumps to $83.88/year once the deal ends, while Windscribe stays at $29/year indefinitely.

Choose Proton if you want the privacy-focused all-rounder – or Unlimited if you’ll use Proton Pass and Drive daily.

Opt for Windscribe if unlimited devices, port forwarding, or post-quantum WireGuard matter most, or if you’re settling in for the long haul and prefer to avoid a hefty renewal bill.
