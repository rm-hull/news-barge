How long would your savings last if you lost your job tomorrow?

With £2,000 in the bank and essential bills of £1,000 a month, you could cover two months. Add nursery fees and a bigger mortgage, and the same sum might last less than four weeks.

Even so, £2,000 is a useful milestone. University of Bristol researchers found that among people aged 35 to 44, the estimated risk of later falling behind with bills was just 6 per cent for those who had held at least £2,000 in either of two earlier surveys, compared with 15 per cent for those who had stayed below that level in both.

Among people aged 45 to 59, the figures were 4 per cent and 19 per cent respectively, highlighting the importance of cash savings at certain life stages.

“Our research suggests that households may need as much as £10,000 in savings to actually start to *feel* financially secure, but that holding £2,000 in savings is linked to a much lower risk of falling behind with bills,” says Jamie Evans, one of the report’s authors.

The general rule of thumb is to keep three to six months’ essential outgoings in an easy access account.

Hargreaves Lansdown’s 2024 analysis found that essential household spending was highest for people in their 40s, but your own target will depend on the bills you pay today.

The four budgets below are illustrative, so pick the one closest to your circumstances as well as your age and use your own real-life figures.

## **Age 25: start with one month’s bills**

If you share a flat and your rent, bills, food and travel come to £1,000 a month, you’d need £3,000 to cover three months.

Don’t let a single number feel out of reach though: start with what you can afford and aim to build up to one month’s bills over time. Keep money for a house deposit in a separate pot so you know what you can draw on in an emergency.

The Bristol research also found that 16 per cent of people who hadn’t saved regularly in either of two earlier surveys later fell behind with bills. Among those who saved regularly in both, the figure was just 1 per cent.

## **Age 35: count the childcare bills you’d pay**

Say your household spends £2,200 a month on essentials, including £600 in nursery fees.

You’d need £6,600 to cover three months. The nursery bill alone accounts for £1,800 of that target.

Check what you’d actually have to keep paying if you lost your job or had to stop work.

You might want to keep your child’s nursery place while you look for another job, for example. Run the sums again when fees change or your child starts school.

## **Age 45: ask how much depends on one income**

With essential bills of £1,800 a month, three months would cost £5,400. If you rely on one income and want to cover six months, the figure rises to £10,800.

Include bills you’d keep paying for older children, such as their phone or travel costs if that’s what you’d normally do.

![Different savings pots might be required for different needs](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/20/08/13114525-18783c64-8bca-46f2-8765-7f9d52558efc.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“You may also want to err on the side of caution and have closer to six months’ worth of essentials covered if there are specific risks you’re worried about,” says Sarah Coles head of personal finance at AJ Bell.

“You may, for example, be relying on a single income, or be worried about work or your health.”

## **Age 55: revisit the sums before cutting back on work**

If your essential bills come to £1,500 a month, you’d need £4,500 for three months or £9,000 for six. If you plan to go part-time and use savings to top up your pay, work out how much you’ll draw each month and set that money aside separately. That gives you a clearer view of what you’d have left for a boiler repair or a period without any work.

“If you cut back on work later in life, you should consider a bigger buffer,” says Coles. “You may already be covering part of your income from savings or investments, but if you have an unexpected cost, you’ll have less earned income to cover it, so need more of a cash cushion just in case.”

Coles suggests checking your target once a year as prices and circumstances change.

If your essential bills have risen by £100 a month since you last checked, you’ll need another £300 to cover three months.

And each time you check in, at every age stage, is also a good time to see whether your easy access account is still paying a competitive rate.
