White House staffers have a betting pool going on how long Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin will remain in the job amid mounting criticism of his leadership at the sprawling department, according to a report.

Mullin, currently on a five-day trip to the Middle East, is on shaky ground after his public response to the recent spate of technology outages and his appearance at a House committee hearing last week, according to *The Washington Post.*

While President Donald Trump said that Mullin was doing “a good job” Tuesday, he was reportedly “unhappy” with the secretary’s performance at the House Homeland Security Committee hearing last week, where he mounted a defense of his trip to an LSU-Texas college football game the day after a rare nationwide air travel ground stop.

Mullin reasoned that if he didn’t fly as planned, he risked creating “confusion” and “distrust” for Americans hoping to travel.

Days before the hearing, Mullin was also put in “primetime timeout” by White House chief of staff Stephen Miller after appearing on Fox News, where he appeared to suggest that “SI” — Trump’s preferred term for artificial intelligence — was behind the air traffic control outages, citing “one guy who is very smart with tech.”

![White House staffers reportedly have a betting pool going on how long Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin will remain in the job amid mounting criticism of his leadership at the sprawling department](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/25/15/2026-11-18T165512Z_2104720740_RC2GQOAHTD4U_RTRMADP_3_USA-SECURITY-MULLIN.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Trump administration group chats lit up with “WTFs” as staffers watched Mullin’s TV appearance, *Politico* reported at the time.

Those gaffes, along with concern that Mullin is “fairly left-leaning” when it comes to immigration in stark contrast with Trump, according to critics, have led to speculation among White House staffers about how long the former Oklahoma senator will last.

A White House official pointed *The Independent* to the “many successes” Miller and Mullin have worked together on, including eliminating fraud, spearheading a narcotics crackdown, confronting terrorist threats by arresting thousands of suspected terrorists, and dismantling foreign scam centers.

White House spokeswoman Lauren Bis touted the president for assembling “the most talented Cabinet in history who work every day to implement his commonsense agenda and deliver wins for the American people” in a statement to *The Independent. “*In just over one year, the President has made our country greater than ever before with the most secure border in history, the largest middle-class tax cut ever, trillions in investment commitments, and the lowest level of violent crime in nearly a century,” Bis added.* The Independent* contacted the Department of Homeland Security for comment.

![While President Donald Trump said that Mullin was doing ‘a good job,’ he was reportedly ‘unhappy’ with the secretary’s performance at the House Homeland Security Committee hearing last week](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/05/08/09/2275076634.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Two staffers told the *Post* that Mullin’s job appears protected for now because it is unlikely that Republicans would be able to confirm another successor before January, when Democrats gain control of Congress.

Mullin is currently in the Middle East on an unannounced tour of Jordan, Azerbaijan and the United Arab Emirates, accompanied by his wife, Christie. The trip has been planned for two months, according to the *Post*.

Trump praised Mullin in a Tuesday interview with *The New York Post*, but “appeared surprised” to learn that he was in the Middle East.

The president glanced at White House communications director Steven Cheung, who clarified to the paper: “He’s on a planned trip to meet his counterparts.”

The Department of Homeland Security told *The Washington Post* that Mullin “works 24/7 to secure the homeland, and that includes engaging with international partners for diplomatic meetings and bilateral security discussions that advance America’s national security interests.”

“This is what it looks like when DHS takes a proactive approach to securing the homeland,” the spokesperson said.

Despite the mounting scrutiny, the president appears to be standing by Mullin, according to the *Post*.

“He’s a good person. He works very hard,” the president said of Mullin, adding that he does not anticipate “much” movement in terms of his Cabinet.

“We have a good Cabinet,” he said.

Tensions between Mullin and the White House were reported in July, just a few months into his tenure at the department over decision-making and messaging with Immigration and Customs Enforcement.

Mullin took over the role from Kristi Noem, who spearheaded the administration’s immigration crackdown. She was fired by the president after he had reportedly grown weary of the “constant chaos” surrounding her and the negative press coverage she attracted.
