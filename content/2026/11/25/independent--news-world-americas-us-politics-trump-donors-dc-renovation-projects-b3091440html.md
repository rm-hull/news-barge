The Trump administration is declining to say who put roughly $300 million into a government account that the president has used to fund his renovation projects in Washington, D.C., according to a new report.

The money arrived about two months ago, but government records do not reveal its origin, including whether it came from a single donor or several, *The New York Times* reported. Officials have refused to clarify when asked.

The account was initially established to collect donations for the National Park Service. Since last year, however, President Donald Trump has drawn on it to pay for a series of large projects around the capital.

It has helped cover work on the Lincoln Memorial Reflecting Pool, a planned 250-foot triumphal arch on the Potomac River and the White House ballroom that is now under construction.

The nine-figure deposit comes as the account has raked in far more money than usual. Historically, it received less than $100 million a year, but since Trump's inauguration, it has received nearly eight times that amount, according to the *Times*.

![The Trump administration is declining to say who put roughly $300 million into a government account that the president has used to fund his renovation projects in Washington, DC, according to a new report](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/25/13/2303244316..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

When reached by the newspaper, an Interior Department spokesperson declined to provide specifics about the money. “President Donald J. Trump has done more to make our nation’s capital a shining beacon than any other president in the history of this country,” the spokesperson said.

*The Independent* has contacted the department, which oversees the National Parks Service, for comment.

Tony Irish, a former Interior Department attorney and senior counsel for Public Employees for Environmental Responsibility, informed the *Times* of the $300 million donation.

He said he was shocked at the size of the contribution and the mystery surrounding it.

![The government account has helped cover work on the Lincoln Memorial Reflecting Pool, a planned 250-foot triumphal arch on the Potomac River and the White House ballroom that is now under construction](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/08/03/18/46/Screenshot-2026-08-03-at-11-10-47-AM.png?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“Are the donors seeking certain government benefits?” Irish asked. “We just don’t know, because of the lack of transparency.”

Some previous donors to the president's projects have not been publicly identified by the government.

In October, *The Washington Post* obtained “secret” internal documents that revealed the donors behind $330 million for Trump's massive White House ballroom. They included Nvidia, Apple and the Adelson family.

A poll conducted by the newspaper earlier this month found that a majority of Americans are opposed to the Republican president’s construction projects.

It’s unclear how much these projects, some of which rely on taxpayer dollars, will cost. The Times estimated that the price tag will be upwards of $1 billion.
