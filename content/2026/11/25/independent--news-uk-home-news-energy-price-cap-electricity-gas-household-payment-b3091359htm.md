A widowed pensioner suffering with bronchitis and asthma says she can’t afford to keep her heating on overnight – and when she wakes up the morning she keeps the thermostat to 11C, well below the recommended 18C point.

Elaine Yates, aged 76, told *The Independent* she wears several layers of clothing in the day and thick pyjamas in bed at night to keep warm inside her one-bedroom bungalow in Northamptonshire.

The retired factory supervisor, who spends around £110 on energy from her £1,200 monthly income, also does not cook meals for most of the week, opting to eat heated leftovers, toast and cereal for most of the week.

During cold periods, she ensures she goes out for large parts of the day, usually to a coffee shop to warm up.

Ms Yates is one of millions of people who are set to see their household energy bills increase by 26 per cent next year after Ofgem revealed on Wednesday that the price cap will go up £2,166 for the average household using electricity and gas.

Last month, the government announced 20 million households will receive a £100 payment toward energy bills from January. There will is also a temporary 5 per cent VAT cut from electricity bills in place until June next year.

![Elaine Yates, 77, has taken drastic measures to keep the energy bills down in her home](https://static.independent.co.uk/2025/11/25/14/24/Age-UK2705-(2).jpeg?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&auto=webp)

But for people like Ms Yates, the rise in prices is a huge concern given the continued rise in inflation amidst the ongoing cost-of-living crisis.

“I don’t know how I’m going to get through it,” Ms Yates said. “Already I’m spending alot of money on energy. When it goes up, I can’t cut back my usage further, I’ll need to cut back on other things like not filling up my car with petrol or on my food shopping.

“It’s frightening, really.”

Ms Yates said she took steps to keep the energy costs down in her home, admittedly at the risk to her own health. She said she has bronicitis and asthma which comes and goes during cold weather.

The Energy Saving Trust recommends homes should be heated to a minimum of 18C in the day.

She said: “Last year was so cold, I only fear again for this winter. Because of the cost, I turn off the heating overnight, so wrap up in bed with extra large duvets on top. Then in the day I keep the thermostat to 11C. I put on layers of t-shirts and jumpers to keep warm.

![The Energy Saving Trust recommends setting the thermostat between 18C and 21C as a comfortable range for most households (Yui Mok/PA)](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/25/09/25083952-87d3d1ab-9f06-4293-8b21-5a157195779b.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“When it’s frosty in the morning, I can sometimes see my own breath when I get up.

“And as I’m spending more on heating, I cut back where I can on other energy uses like cooking. Today, I’m going to heat up a stew from yesterday. Tomorrow I’ll have toast and sandwiches. I won’t cook a proper meal till Sunday.”

As well as the £100 payment and VAT cut, the government announced last month that gas standing charges will be scrapped in England, Scotland and Wales.

But campaigners have said that current support is still enough.

New analysis by charity National Energy Action found that those with pre-payment meters will need to spend £1,000 from January to March to keep their meter topped up. It wants more targeted financial support for vulnerable and low-income households, such as a £150 payment.

Chief executive Adam Scorer said: “This January, households will need to pay vastly more for a warm home. For millions, that’s more than they can afford.”

“For people on a low income and those who are vulnerable it is a desperate situation. For those on prepayment meters, it will be impossible. They don’t even have the option to rack up huge debt. They simply can’t find over £1,000 to keep the heat and lights on this winter. They will go without. They will go to bed early to keep warm, they will not cook, some will even turn off medical equipment. And that is devastating to their health and their wellbeing.”

Simon Francis, coordinator of the End Fuel Poverty Coalition, said: “Households are set to pay more for gas than ever before with changes coming in the depths of winter. For millions of people that equals cold homes, mounting debt and having to make impossible choices.

“It means parents going without so their children can stay warm, people who rely on medical equipment rationing their electricity and households on prepayment meters left with no heat at all once their credit runs out.”

## What’s changing with energy bills

The price cap coming into force from January 1 will rise by 26 per cent, taking effect for the three month period to the end of March.

The figure would represent £2,166 for the average household if sustained for a year – the highest level for three-and-a-half years.

The rise has been particularly linked to an increase in gas prices, while electricity prices have risen more slowly.

Ofgem said it has written to energy suppliers, networks and brokers on Wednesday to remind them of their responsibilities to customers, including making sure support is easily available, this winter.

Energy Secretary Miatta Fahnbulleh said: “I know people are deeply concerned by another rise in energy bills. For too long, British families have been paying the price for conflicts overseas and volatile fossil fuel markets.

“To help this winter, we’re stepping in to give families some breathing space, so from January, the majority of households will get £100 towards their energy costs. But there is more to do to take the pressure off families and bring bills down for good.”
