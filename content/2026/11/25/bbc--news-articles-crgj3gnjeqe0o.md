Hotel bosses and women's groups were invited to the committee after a series of media reports into room intrusions

**Hotels should face national minimum standards to protect female guests, witnesses have told a parliamentary committee examining safety in the industry.**

The hearing followed an independent review into security failings at Travelodge after a woman was sexually assaulted at one of its hotels in Maidenhead, Berkshire.

The review's author, Paul Greaney KC, said room intrusions had become so common they were treated as something to be tolerated, while Travelodge's interim chief executive admitted the company had not taken guest safety seriously enough.

Committee chair and Labour MP Olivia Bailey said the evidence showed not enough was being done to protect women and called for "concrete action".

The one-off parliamentary session heard evidence from hotel chains including Travelodge, Premier Inn UK and Z Hotels, alongside women's safety organisations.

Three women giving evidence revealed they had previously barricaded their hotel room doors to feel safer during their stays.

UK Hospitality chief executive Allen Simpson also recalled inadvertently revealing the location of a domestic violence victim to their abuser while working as a hotel receptionist earlier in his career.

Greaney's independent review, commissioned by Travelodge, found the company had failed the Maidenhead victim "at every stage".

The woman had spoken to the BBC after her attacker gained access to her room by lying to reception staff.

Other guests subsequently came forward with similar experiences of security problems at Travelodge and other hotel chains.

In August, Travelodge's chief executive Jo Boydell stepped down ahead of the publication of the independent review.

Greaney told the committee: "All hotels need to have a policy designed to ensure room security and to implement it by training their staff."

Emma Lingley-Clark, chief executive of the Suzy Lamplugh Trust, argued that guest safety should be treated like other basic standards expected of hotels.

She said: "This should be considered in exactly the same light."

## 'Culture change'

Travelodge's interim chief executive Ray Reidy apologised again to the Maidenhead victim and others affected by the company's failings.

He added: "Historically, speaking for Travelodge, I don't think we have taken it seriously. I don't think we did that intentionally. I don't think we'd deliberately ignored it. But I don't think we had given it, as highlighted in the report, the focus that it needed."

"I would like to think that this is a milestone instance in the sector.

"We are still not where we want to be, but we are taking it incredibly seriously in terms of how we move forward."

Speaking after the hearing, chair Olivia Bailey, who is also MP for Reading West and Mid Berkshire, said: "There have been lots of examples that we've seen in the media that people have told the committee of women's safety just not being taken seriously enough in hotels."

"It is clear from the evidence we've heard today that not enough is being done.

"There needs to be a wholesale culture change.

"We need to make sure we don't just get warm words here. We need to make sure we have concrete action.

"What we'll be doing as a committee is considering what that concrete action might need to look like".

Kyran Smith was jailed in February for seven-and-a-half years following the Travelodge assault in December 2022.
