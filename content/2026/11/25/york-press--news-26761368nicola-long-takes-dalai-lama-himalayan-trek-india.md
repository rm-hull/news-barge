## York woman raises £2,000 for heart charity taking on Himalayan trek

Nicola Long took on the Dalai Lama Himalayan Trek in India in March 2024 *(Image: Supplied)*

A Himalayan trek has helped one woman from York rediscover hope, purpose, and new beginnings.

Nicola Long, 46, took on the Dalai Lama Himalayan Trek in India in March 2024, raising nearly £2,000 for the British Heart Foundation.

She completed the trek in memory of close relatives lost to cardiovascular disease and in support of those still living with the condition.

Ms Long said: "I gained a lot of confidence in myself and a greater appreciation of the kindness of others.

"Perhaps most importantly, the experience helped me realise that grief and joy can exist alongside each other.

"You can miss the people you've lost terribly while still enjoying life, making new memories and having adventures."

Cardiovascular disease has deeply affected several generations of Ms Long’s family.

Her paternal grandfather died of a heart attack at just 38, her maternal grandfather suffered seven heart attacks before his death when she was seven, and her step-grandfather died following a major stroke.

Her father was also diagnosed with early-onset dementia, including vascular dementia, at the age of 57.

Following her father’s death in 2018, Ms Long was hospitalised with Takotsubo cardiomyopathy, also known as "broken heart syndrome," which gave her a personal understanding of the physical effects of grief.

The idea for the trek began in conversation with her mother, who encouraged her to pursue a physically and mentally challenging adventure for a meaningful cause.

After her mother’s sudden passing in 2021, Ms Long struggled to commit to the journey.

It wasn’t until a vivid dream in October 2023, in which her mother urged her to go, that she found the strength to take part.

Arriving in India, Ms Long carried the memories of her loved ones and a determination to turn her grief into something positive.

The trek proved challenging, as she later discovered she was suffering from an infection during the journey.

She was supported throughout by her fellow trekkers, who walked beside her and offered encouragement when she needed it most.

The group shared laughter, stories, and unforgettable moments—travelling through Himalayan villages, witnessing breathtaking scenery, meeting the Dalai Lama, and celebrating Holi together.

Ms Long said the experience changed her outlook in ways she never expected.

Her efforts have had a lasting impact on those around her.

Since the trek, colleagues have told her that her story prompted them to reflect on their own heart health.

One colleague, who was reminded of Ms Long’s fundraising journey when experiencing health concerns, sought medical advice and is now scheduled for bypass surgery.

Ms Long said: "When I originally signed up, I hoped to raise money and awareness, but I never imagined that someone might remember my story years later and be encouraged to seek medical help that could potentially save their life.

"To think that something positive could come from sharing my family's experiences of loss is incredibly meaningful to me."

Now looking ahead to new adventures, Ms Long has been invited to take part in a special Charity Challenge Sahara trek in April 2027, an opportunity she describes as a "huge honour".

Charity Challenge, the adventure travel company behind the trek, has helped tens of thousands of people raise more than £100 million for charities worldwide.

More information about their overseas fundraising challenges can be found at www.charitychallenge.com.
