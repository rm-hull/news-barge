**"What does it mean to be human?" is a question that's always been at the heart of Blade Runner.**

The first film in the series, released in 1982, depicted a technologically advanced yet dystopian version of Los Angeles in the then-distant future of 2019.

While the movie's flying cars haven't - quite - materialised yet in the real world, Blade Runner's themes of technological progress and the threats it poses feel just as relevant.

At least that's what the cast and crew of Blade Runner 2099, a new TV series set in the same universe, are hoping.

BBC Newsbeat spoke to stars Hunter Schafer, Lewis Gribben and Dimitri Abold, plus showrunner Silka Luisa, to ask why they think themes of artificial consciousness and existential threats still resonate.

The original Blade Runner, directed by Sir Ridley Scott, is considered a sci-fi classic that had a long-lasting influence on the genre.

It told the story of Detective Deckard, played by Harrison Ford, who hunted down synthetic humans known as replicants.

Schafer says her first exposure to the series was actually marketing for Blade Runner 2049 - the film sequel starring Ford and Ryan Gosling in 2017.

"I never thought I'd have the opportunity to make an addition to the world," she says.

"And so when that opportunity came along, I was quite thrilled."

Blade Runner 2099 is set 80 years after the original film, and portrays a Los Angeles where replicants have taken over.

Schafer plays Cora, a fugitive human forced to team up with replicant Olwen (played by Michelle Yeoh), as she tries to track down an artefact that could save humanity.

The series lands at a time when there are disagreements about the nature of the threat AI could pose to humans.

"I think it's particularly relevant right now, especially for the youth and young people who are inheriting a world that's asking a lot of the same questions that the show asks," says Schafer.

"I think that's timely and hopefully helpful to some degree."

But Blade Runner 2099 isn't "necessarily about just AI", says showrunner Luisa.

"It's this bigger question of what is the world going to be in 15 years and how can you plan for it?" she says.

The first Blade Runner also touched on themes of corporate greed and environmental damage, which are evident in the new series.

Luisa hopes the theme of a future that "is not going to be what you were promised" resonates with viewers today.

"The idea that you were born into a world and you thought you were expecting certain aspects of your life just to roll out," she says.

"That system is collapsing. And so what will be your place in whatever comes next?"

## 'People crave human stories'

Blade Runner 2049 won awards and scored well with reviewers, but reports suggested it didn't hit box office targets.

This was partly blamed on marketing for the film trying to appeal more to existing fans than new ones - so what would the cast say to get younger viewers on board?

"It's just a very rich world," says Gribben, who plays Cora's younger brother Elias.

"It makes you want to learn more."

Gribben also points out that Blade Runner 2099, which employed "hundreds of artists and craftspeople" according to its makers, is a celebration of traditional film-making despite its themes.

"I think a lot of people crave human stories," he says.

"They crave new kind of worlds and new things.

"And I think studios will rely more on technology to make better-looking movies, but they won't have that soul."

Abold, who plays Cora's human ally Bebeto, says one of the show's key messages is about "the connections that you make".

"And the connections that you make are the foundation of humanity," he says.

"Because at the end of the day, all we have is ourselves and each other, right? And that's what feeds any human emotional experience."

Reviews for Blade Runner 2099 have been mostly positive, with a score of 72% on Rotten Tomatoes, external and an average of 65% on review aggregator Metacritic, external.

There was praise for the lead performances, the show's visual style and the way it updates the themes established in previous series entries.

The most common criticism was the show's pacing, with several critics describing it as a slow-burn and questioning whether Blade Runner worked as well off the big screen.

Schafer says she felt a lot of pressure when she signed up for the show,

"It's something that we were all aware of when making this show," she says.

"Everybody who was working on this show loves Blade Runner.

"Most people who love film-making love Blade Runner, everybody brought their best to work.

"It upped our game a bit."

*Blade Runner 2099 is available on Prime Video from 25 November.*
