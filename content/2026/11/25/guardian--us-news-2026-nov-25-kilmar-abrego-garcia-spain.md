Kilmar Ábrego García, the Salvadorian national whose mistaken deportation turned him into a symbol of Donald Trump’s mass deportation efforts, has landed in Spain.

Ábrego García arrived in Madrid on Wednesday after leaving the United States of his own volition.

“I feel free of everything evil that has followed me up until this point,” he told reporters on a video call just hours after he touched down. “I’m happy … because this time I’m leaving with my freedom, not like before when they took me out inhumanely.”

He added: “The government does not decide where my story continues. My family and I will decide our future.”

Prior to his departure, Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) agents met him at the airport and removed his ankle monitor, according to We Are Casa, a Maryland-based advocacy group.

As he made his way through the Madrid airport, Ábrego García did not speak to reporters, instead raising his fist in the air as he climbed into a waiting van.

Ábrego García became a lightning rod in the Trump administration’s immigration crackdown in 2025 after he was deported to El Salvador in what an official later called an “administrative error”.

The 31-year-old had been living in the US since 2019, after a judge ruled that he could not be deported to El Salvador due to the dangers posed by a gang that had threatened his family. The judge allowed him to live and work in the US under ICE supervision, but he was not given residency status.

Married to an American woman, the Maryland resident was part of a group of more than 200 people sent by the US to Cecot, a notorious anti-terrorism mega-prison, in violation of the US immigration judge’s 2019 order.

Amid public outcry, he was returned to the US after a decision by the supreme court. Since then, Ábrego García has been fighting government efforts to deport him for a second time to a series of African countries proposed by Department of Homeland Security officials.

On Wednesday, his lawyer, Simon Sandoval-Moshenberg, said the Trump administration had wanted to deport Ábrego García “handcuffed, on an ICE flight bound for Liberia” on Tuesday.

“And what happened? He walked out a free man, without any restrictions, without shackles on his feet. He left for the country of his choosing, and he did so on a commercial flight, alongside everyone else,” Sandoval-Moshenberg added.

He described it as a “complete victory”, adding: “Instead of flying ICE Air, he flew United Airlines. Instead of being in shackles, he was in economy plus.”

He said Ábrego García had negotiated a deal with federal authorities in which they had agreed to allow him to leave the country, removing obstacles such as the ankle monitor and taking him off the no-fly list, as long as he left by 24 November. The Department of Homeland Security said on Tuesday Ábrego García had “self-deported” to Spain.

Sandoval-Moshenberg described Ábrego García’s departure from the US as temporary, saying that he would decide where to settle for the next two years as he waited out the remainder of the Trump administration.

“Mr Ábrego García has many options to return to the United States as the husband of a US citizen spouse and the father of a US citizen child,” he said. “We don’t expect that to happen under this administration, which we know will never give him a fair shake, but we expect that the next administration will prioritise reunifying this family and bringing him back to Maryland.”

On Wednesday, Ábrego García’s lawyer, Chris Newman, said his client had landed in Spain as a tourist and that he had no plans to meet with any politicians while in Spain. “I can confirm that he’s coming here with the intention of staying for just a few days and that he already has tickets, one could say, for his next appointment,” he told Spanish media.

His departure this week was celebrated by many who had sought to push back against the Trump administration’s pursuit of Ábrego García.

“They wanted to leave him there to rot for the rest of his life. Instead, we saw our judicial system work alongside a strong legal team to produce this outcome, where Kilmar Ábrego García is able to chart his own future,” said Senator Chris Van Hollen, the Maryland Democrat who traveled to Cecot to visit Abrego Garcia in April last year.

Van Hollen, who is vying to become Senate majority leader when Democrats regain control of Congress next year, has made Ábrego García’s case and opposition to the administration’s sweeping deportation crackdown central to his vision for the party.

“Ábrego García’s journey is an example of our constitution working despite the attacks of the Trump administration,” Van Hollen added. He said he had spoken with the Spanish ambassador to the United States, Ángeles Moreno Bau, and briefed her on “all the wrongs that have been committed by the Trump administration against Kilmar Ábrego García”, adding that he was pleased Ábrego García was now in Spain and “able to continue telling his story”.
