Violence against women takes many forms: physical abuse, sexual violence but also emotional abuse such as threats and humiliation, including online.

On the International Day for the Elimination of Violence Against Women, organizations and policymakers highlight the need for protection services and support systems - as well as efforts to address the root causes of violence.

According to police crime statistics, more than 20,000 people were affected by domestic violence in Berlin alone in 2025 - a 4.9 percent increase from the previous year. Seventy percent of the victims were female and 63 percent of the perpetrators were male. The number of unreported cases is high as many incidents go unreported. "Behind every number is a person whose safety and autonomy have been violated. Violence against women is not a private matter and is not a tragic isolated incident," emphasized Susanne Buss, chair of the board of Volkssolidarität Berlin. "Anyone who threatens, controls or hits their partner bears responsibility for their own actions. Neither jealousy nor anger is an excuse," said Samira Ciyow, who leads a counseling program for men at Volkssolidarität. The program aims to teach men to take responsibility for their actions, respect boundaries and resolve conflicts without violence or threats.

Volkssolidarität also calls for work with perpetrators to be an integral part of violence prevention. The Senate Department for Anti-Discrimination recently launched the citywide campaign +BrichDasSchweigen (English: #BreakTheSilence), which is designed in particular to help men talk about violence against women and prevent it. Volkssolidarität is calling for the simultaneous expansion of protection and counseling services for women. Berlin currently has 431 family units with 946 beds available in women’s shelters, safe houses, and refuge apartments. Three new women’s shelters have opened this year alone. In Brandenburg, there are 17 women’s shelters, as well as counseling centers and safe houses.

Berlin's Governing Mayor, Kai Wegner (CDU), emphasized: "Violence against women is a men's issue. It is up to us to pay attention, to intervene when boundaries are crossed, and to take a stand in our own circles as well." He will raise the anti-violence flag in front of the Rotes Rathaus at 10:30 a.m. The so-called UN Women flag is also scheduled to be raised at the Brandenburg State Parliament. Many district offices are also organizing events and discussions around the International Day for the Elimination of Violence against Women, or raising the purple flag against violence against women. At Ostbahnhof, confidential counseling will be available from 12 noon to 4 p.m. provided by the officers for crime prevention and victim protection.
