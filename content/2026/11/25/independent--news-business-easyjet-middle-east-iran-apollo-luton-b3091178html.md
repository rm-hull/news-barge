EasyJet has revealed annual profits nosedived after being hit with an extra £244 million in fuel costs caused by the Iran war.

In its last set of full-year figures before a £5.7 billion private equity takeover, the low cost carrier revealed statutory pre-tax profits plunged 96% to £24 million in the year to September 30, down from £658 million the previous year.

Its headline pre-tax profits fell 89% to £71 million.

EasyJet saw its jet fuel bill jump 11% or £244 million over the year as the Middle East conflict sent the cost of wholesale energy and fuel soaring.

The no frills carrier also said the Iran war led to “weakened consumer demand for a prolonged period” since the conflict began in February.

The Luton-based airline said it was on track for its takeover by investment giant Apollo to complete in the first quarter of next year after it agreed to the deal in the summer, making it the latest UK firm to fall into foreign hands.

Kenton Jarvis, easyJet’s chief executive, said: “This year was challenging for airlines due to the Middle East conflict and its effect on demand and fuel prices, which materially impacted our financial performance.”

He added: “The recommended acquisition of easyJet, approved by shareholders on 11 November, reflects the quality of our business, and the underlying drivers of long-term financial performance.”
