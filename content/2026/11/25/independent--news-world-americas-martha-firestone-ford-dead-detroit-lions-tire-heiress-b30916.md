Martha Firestone Ford, an heiress to a tire fortune who connected two of the automotive industry's most prominent families and later assumed control of the NFL's Detroit Lions, has died at 101.

The team confirmed she passed away Tuesday at her home in Grosse Pointe, Michigan, surrounded by family. No cause of death was provided.

Ford was the granddaughter of tire industrialist Harvey Firestone, and her husband, William Clay Ford Sr., was the grandson of Henry Ford. He owned the Lions for half a century prior to his death in 2014.

Martha Ford subsequently acquired controlling interest in the franchise to retain family ownership. Though she rarely granted media interviews or commented publicly regarding team management, her influence was distinct. She led the franchise from 2014 until 2020, when she transferred ownership of the Lions to her daughter, Sheila Ford Hamp.

The Lions reached the postseason in 2014, but opened the following campaign with seven losses in their first eight games. In November 2015, Ford executed a midseason executive shakeup by dismissing the team's president and general manager. Announcing the moves, Ford informed supporters that the club intended to "identify and hire the very best leadership in order to produce a consistently, winning football team."

![Roger Goodell paid tribute to Ford following her death](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/25/16/2304647380..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Detroit improved over the remainder of that season and returned to the playoffs the next year after appointing Bob Quinn as general manager.

"Martha was special," NFL Commissioner Roger Goodell said in a statement. "She cared so deeply about her family, the players, their fans, and the city of Detroit. She was committed to making the Lions - and the game of football - better. Martha and her husband, William Clay Ford, led the franchise with dignity and integrity. We will miss her kindness and sweet sense of humor. We extend our deepest condolences to her children, the entire Ford family, and the Lions’ organization."

Born Sept. 16, 1925, in Akron, Ohio, Martha Parke Firestone graduated from Vassar College in 1946 and wed William Clay Ford the following year.

Their wedding generated extensive public interest—described in 2014 by the Akron Beacon Journal as "Akron’s royal wedding"—and united two families with longstanding ties.

Harvey Firestone, founder of the Firestone Tire and Rubber Co., was a close associate of Ford Motor Co. founder Henry Ford. Ford selected Firestone to supply tires for the original Model T in 1906. The two men regularly embarked on extended camping trips together, at times joined by Thomas Edison.

Martha and William Clay Ford had three daughters and a son. Their son, William Clay Ford Jr., currently serves as executive chairman of Ford Motor Co. In 2001, Bridgestone/Firestone and Ford ended their nearly century-long partnership following a dispute over fatal tire failures involving the Ford Explorer.

Sheila Ford Hamp serves as the Lions’ principal owner and chairman.

William Clay Ford’s first full season running the Lions came in 1964, seven years after the franchise won an NFL championship. Since that 1957 title, the team has recorded playoff wins in 1992 and 2023 and has never reached the Super Bowl.

![Martha was married to William Clay Ford Sr.](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/25/16/83794759..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Although Martha Ford maintained a modest public profile, the midseason personnel changes in 2015 demonstrated her willingness to act to improve the team's results. Then-coach Jim Caldwell noted that she was a constant presence within the franchise.

"I think visible ownership is great, and Mrs. Ford does it extremely well," Caldwell said in 2015. "She’s there before games and she’s there after games, and I think guys enjoy that part of it. She comes to practice and things of that nature, so it’s been fun."

Even at age 101, she continued attending games, including a victory against the Minnesota Vikings earlier this month.

Martha Ford also contributed significantly to the Detroit community. She served as a board officer for Detroit Lions Charities and held a position on the board of the Henry Ford Health System. She and her husband were also instrumental in establishing the Maplegrove Center, an addiction treatment facility in West Bloomfield.

Forbes estimated her net worth at over $1.4 billion in 2017. She is survived by four children, 14 grandchildren, and 17 great-grandchildren.
