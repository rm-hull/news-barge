The US west coast has seen record-breaking tides this week and authorities have urged residents to avoid beaches, but not everyone is heeding those warnings.

In Cannon Beach, Oregon, viral footage showed a man and his dog nearly getting swept away by a so-called king tide on Tuesday morning. The man had been standing on a staircase holding his leashed dog, who was walking in the sand, when the water suddenly surged, knocking him to his feet.

They ultimately escaped without injury, according to the Cannon Beach police department. Earlier that morning, police had cited the same man for driving on the shoreline. He had reportedly “indicated” to an officer he wanted to get a better look at the high tides.

Other footage captured around Cannon Beach on Tuesday morning showed a jogger narrowly avoiding a major wave, while another stood amid the wave seemingly filming.

King tides are the highest tide cycles of the year. This winter, conditions caused by the super El Niño have pushed these tides to record levels, contributing to widespread flooding, powerful waves and coastal erosion.

The Cannon Beach police department advised residents to stay off beaches and keep pets away from the water.

“King tides are an impressive natural phenomenon, but no photograph, video or closer look is worth risking your life, the life of your pet or the lives of the first responders who may be called upon to rescue you,” the police department said in a statement.

A coastal flood advisory is in effect for the region through Thursday, according to the National Weather Service, as “king tides, elevated seas, and the arrival of fresh swells likely lead to tidal overflow”.
