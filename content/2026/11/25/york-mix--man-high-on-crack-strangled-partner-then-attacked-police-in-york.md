A man strangled his ex-partner so violently she was gasping for breath and couldn’t speak properly for days, a court heard.

Nathan James, 37, strangled the named victim “for about a minute” during which she struggled to breathe and thought she was about to die, York Crown Court was told.

James – who had a track record for violence and assaulting police – launched the drug-fuelled attack on 23 September when the victim, who is now studying for a masters degree, picked him up from York Station to take him back to her address in Clifton.

“He told her he had taken crack and brought drugs back to her address,” said Mr Fernandez.

When they returned to the victim’s home, James, who was in a “foul mood”, subjected her to a barrage of verbal abuse after she flushed his drugs down the toilet.

He then “dived” on her head and put his knee on her chest and strangled her with one hand, added the prosecutor.

“She was unable to breathe and although she did not lose consciousness, her vision began to blur,” said Mr Fernandez.

He said that according to the victim, James was strangling her “for about a minute”, although this wasn’t conclusive.

“She was left with red marks to her neck,” added Mr Fernandez.

“Once it ended, she was so afraid of angering him that she arranged a taxi to take him back home.

“When she went outside to pay the taxi driver, the defendant shouted further abuse at her. She ran back inside and called police.”

## Attacked police

As she spoke to police, James was banging on her front door. Officers arrived but he resisted arrest as they tried to escort him to the police vehicle.

He kicked out at one officer and aimed a volley of foul-mouthed abuse at him, calling the officer “a rat” and making other deeply offensive comments. He was ultimately taken to ground and arrested.

James was charged with intentional strangulation of his ex-partner and threatening behaviour towards the police officer. He admitted the offences and appeared for sentence yesterday (Tuesday).

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/police-arrest-handcuffs-dreamstime.webp)

In a statement read out by the prosecution, the victim said: “I can’t adequately describe the terror of believing that my life was ending, while the person who was responsible blocked my attempts to call for police.”

She said that in the days following the attack, she “struggled to speak properly” because of the pain.

She had since suffered from “intense anxiety, disturbed sleep, fear, emotional exhaustion and difficulty concentrating”.

“Her confidence has been affected, as has her independence, her relationships and her studies for her Masters degree,” added Mr Fernandez.

Mr Fernandez said that, according to the victim, she had been subjected to previous violence at the hands of James during their “toxic” relationship which ended in early 2024. This led to a series of police call-outs but no charges were brought against James in those instances.

James – who had since left York and moved to Sherburn-in-Elmet, near Selby – had 11 previous convictions for 22 offences including assaulting and resisting police officers, attacking door staff and burglary.

Defence barrister Zarreen Cheetham-Alam said that James, who had long-standing alcohol and drug problems, was “disgusted” by his actions.

She said that the father-of-one, who was currently unemployed, had recently kicked his cocaine-and-alcohol habit.

Judge Simon Hickey condemned James for the “revolting” attack on his ex-partner and his “foul-mouthed” abuse towards police but noted that he had made a “fresh start” in Sherburn and there were “gaps in his offending (history)” which indicated that he could stay out of trouble.

For those reasons, he said he could “just” suspend the inevitable prison sentence.

James, of Highfield Green, Sherburn in Elmet, was given a 25-month jail sentence, suspended for 18 months.

During the suspension period, he will have to work with the Probation Service and complete a 26-session Building Choices Programme.
