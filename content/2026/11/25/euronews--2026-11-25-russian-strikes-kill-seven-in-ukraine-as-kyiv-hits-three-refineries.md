## Russia's drone attack killed people in six Ukrainian regions and hit a Naftogaz gas production facility, a DTEK thermal power plant and power networks in several parts of the country. Ukrainian drones struck Russian refineries in Samara and Rostov regions and an airbase in Nizhny Novgorod region.

Russia launched scores of drones at energy and civilian targets across Ukraine overnight on Wednesday, killing at least seven people in six regions, authorities said.

The attack involved 112 Shahed, Gerbera and Parodiya decoy drones, of which 88 were shot down or neutralised, Ukraine's air force said.

One person was killed in Bila Tserkva in Kyiv region, where the strike damaged critical infrastructure, an Epicentr shopping centre and a Nova Poshta postal branch, local authorities said.

One person was killed and seven injured in Kharkiv region after a strike hit a minibus on a highway, regional governor Oleh Synehubov said. Two people aged 76 and 69 were killed and another wounded in Sumy region.

Two people were killed in Odesa region on Tuesday evening — a 41-year-old and a 17-year-old — and one person was killed and three wounded in Zhytomyr region. Casualties were also reported in Chernihiv and Donetsk regions.

Russian forces struck a Naftogaz gas production facility in the Poltava region, the acting head of the company Serhiy Fedorenko said.

A fire broke out and the facility suspended operations. "This is not the first such attack and, unfortunately, not the last," Fedorenko said, adding that Russia was deliberately targeting gas production "to deprive Ukrainians of heat in winter."

Energy facilities in the Dnipropetrovsk region were also hit, triggering emergency power cuts across several districts.

A significant number of consumers lost power in Zaporizhzhia region, Zaporizhzhyaoblenergo head Andriy Stasevskyi said. More than 9,000 customers were left without electricity in Odesa region following Tuesday evening's attacks, regional governor Oleh Kiper said.

The energy company DTEK reported a large-scale strike on one of its thermal power plants, causing serious damage and forcing it to stop generating electricity. DTEK did not specify the location, noting it was the third large-scale strike on its plants in November.

## Ukraine strikes Russian refineries and airbase

Ukrainian drones simultaneously struck three Russian oil refineries overnight, hitting plants in Samara and Rostov regions, according to Ukrainian monitoring sources.

Two refineries in Samara region — the Kuibyshev and Syzran plants, both part of Rosneft — were struck, with fires breaking out at both facilities.

Regional governor Vyacheslav Fedorishchev confirmed the attack but did not name the facilities, saying one person was killed, power had been cut in several districts and a number of schools would operate on an adjusted schedule.

Regional authorities said 145 drones were shot down over the Samara region. Euronews could not independently verify these claims.

The Syzran refinery has a processing capacity of 8.5 million tonnes of oil per year and produces petrol, diesel, aviation kerosene, fuel oil and bitumen.

The Kuibyshev refinery has a primary processing capacity of 8.8 million tonnes per year. Ukraine last struck the Syzran plant on 15 September. The Kuibyshev refinery was also struck that month.

The third refinery struck overnight was the Novoshakhtinsk plant in Rostov region. Regional governor Yuri Slyusar confirmed the attack, saying a combined strike of more than 50 drones and missiles lasted six hours before the plant suspended operations.

Russian oil refining volumes have fallen by an estimated 45% since late October, according to analysts.

Ukraine's General Staff also reported overnight strikes on the Savasleyka airbase in Nizhny Novgorod region, which it described as one of Russia's key aviation facilities and the base from which aircraft carrying Kinzhal missiles launch strikes on Ukrainian cities.

According to Kyiv's military, three MiG-31 and two Su-34 aircraft were damaged and would be unable to fly "for some time".

Ukraine destroyed a span of the road section of the Crimean Bridge last week, halting car traffic, though train services have been restored.

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said the strike was a response to Russian attacks on Ukrainian infrastructure, describing deep strikes inside Russia as "long-range sanctions".

Ukraine attacked the bulk carrier Sparta II in the Gulf of Finland on Saturday, claiming it transported military cargo. The following day it struck the Russian corvette Merkury in the Libyan port of Benghazi. Zelenskyy confirmed the warship had been sunk.

On Tuesday, Russia's Vladimir Putin threatened Ukraine with retaliation for its strikes on refineries and complained that Kyiv had not heeded calls from US President Donald Trump to halt attacks on oil processing facilities.

Zelenskyy had said Ukraine was prepared to stop striking Russian refineries only if Moscow halted its attacks on Ukrainian energy infrastructure.
