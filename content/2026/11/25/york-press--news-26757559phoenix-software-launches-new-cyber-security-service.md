An IT security firm in Pocklington has launched a new service to help organisations manage cyber threats more effectively.

Phoenix Software, the public sector IT specialist, has unveiled Phoenix Protect - Managed Vulnerability Intelligence, a managed security service aimed at helping organisations identify and prioritise software vulnerabilities across their IT infrastructure.

The new service was developed in response to customer feedback and addresses a common challenge for IT and security teams: determining which vulnerabilities require urgent action amid a growing volume of threats.

Fred Astfeldt, head of managed security solutions at Phoenix Software, said: "The idea for this service came directly from conversations with customers.

"Time and again, organisations told us they could manage patching themselves, but what they really needed was clarity on where to start.

"When a report tells you there are 2,500 patches available, that's not particularly helpful.

"The real question is which vulnerabilities pose the greatest risk to your organisation right now."

Traditional vulnerability scans often produce overwhelming reports with little guidance on remediation priorities.

Phoenix Protect aims to change that by providing contextualised insights and risk-based prioritisation, allowing organisations to focus on the most critical vulnerabilities.

Built on an industry-recognised vulnerability management platform and supported by Phoenix's in-house Exposure and Vulnerability Insights Hub (EVI), the service offers targeted reporting and actionable remediation intelligence.

It covers on-premises infrastructure such as servers, networking equipment, and storage systems, as well as Infrastructure-as-a-Service environments in Microsoft Azure.

Mr Astfeldt said: "That's exactly what Phoenix Protect Managed Vulnerability Intelligence is designed to address.

"By combining vulnerability discovery with contextual risk analysis and prioritisation, we're helping customers focus their efforts where they will have the greatest impact on reducing cyber risk."

The launch comes as vulnerability management becomes an increasingly important part of cyber resilience strategies.
