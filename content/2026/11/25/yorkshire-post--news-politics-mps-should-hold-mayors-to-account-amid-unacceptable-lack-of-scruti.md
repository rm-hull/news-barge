# ‘Unacceptable state of affairs’: MPs should scutinise mayors as powers are rolled out to regions, report says

## Despite the rapidly increasing powers for mayors, the PAC found no clear role for Parliament and MPs to hold them to account for their use of public money.

MPs should be handed powers to hold mayors to account as the current system has an “unacceptable” lack of scrutiny, a new report has found.

The investigation, from the influential Public Accounts Committee of MPs, has probed mayoral arrangements amid a vast transfer of power from Whitehall.

Mayors will soon have far greater flexibility around spending, as a portion of business rates and income tax are handed over for them to use over the next two years.

While this fiscal devolution has been praised for ending the current “begging bowl culture”, questions have been asked around the level of scrutiny involved.

They have also been charged with playing a major role in tackling the youth unemployment crisis, with Chancellor John Healey ordering mayors to lead local apprenticeship services.

Despite the rapidly increasing powers and responsibilities, the PAC found no clear role for Parliament and locally-elected MPs to hold mayors to account for their use of public money.

PAC chair Sir Geoffrey Clifton-Brown said: “Those communities elect MPs to represent them.

“Our report provides welcome scrutiny to the envisaged system overall, but currently MPs have no role in directly holding strategic authorities and how they spend public money to account, on behalf of their constituents.

“This is an unacceptable state of affairs, and government must agree MPs’ own role in scrutinising devolved power.”

York Central’s Labour MP Rachael Maskell revealed she had already raised this issue with Andy Burnham’s administration.

![Rachael Maskell MP.  HoC](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOjEzNWRjNTdmLTE2ZDctNDAwZi1hMjg3LTIyNmZhNDM5ZGNmODo5NWEyODU3NS01OWFhLTQ3NWMtYTc0NC02NTBlN2JmYmQwOTE=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


She told The Yorkshire Post: “This is a fault line in the devolution plan at the moment … and it’s got to be addressed.

“There’s too much emphasis on the role of mayors at the moment and not enough emphasis on the role of MPs.

“We’ve got to address this huge deficit in this devolved agenda for it to work.

“It’s not written into the structures and processes around mayors so they don’t have to, but I believe they really must, to optimise outcomes for the community.”

Sir Geoffrey also said it is “currently unclear under what circumstances government are prepared to send in commissioners when things go seriously wrong”.

This has happened in Tees Valley, after the Ministry of Housing, Communities and Local Government issued Lord Ben Houchen with a best value notice.

However, the report said: “It is unclear whether the bodies involved will, between them, have the powers, resources, independence and insight needed to scrutinise increasingly powerful strategic authorities effectively.

“Nor is it clear how poor performance will be identified, challenged and ultimately corrected.”

The PAC report also found that Whitehall “is struggling to let go of its overly centralised responsibilities”.

As such, it said: “Some strategic authorities described feeling more like delivery arms of government than genuinely devolved institutions, with granular reporting arrangements and a heavy focus on national priorities.”

The Yorkshire Post previously reported how civil servants could overrule West Yorkshire Mayor Tracy Brabin and turn her plans for a tram system into a bus network.

The Ministry of Housing, Communities and Local Government did not respond to requests for comment.
