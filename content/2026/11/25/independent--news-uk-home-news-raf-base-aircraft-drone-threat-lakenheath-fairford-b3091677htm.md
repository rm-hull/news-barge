Aircraft at RAF bases are being moved into hangars and shelters under cover of darkness, according to media reports, in response to possible drone threats.

The measures were reported on Wednesday by *The Times* newspaper and Sky News, neither of which cited sources for their accounts.

The outlets also said plans were under way to deploy anti-drone netting over aircraft that are exposed.

"We take the security of our bases, equipment and personnel very seriously and are always adapting to counter any potential threats," a Ministry of Defence spokesperson said in a statement which did not specifically confirm the reports.

The reports came a day after Britain's defence ministry and MI5, the domestic spy agency, warned companies providing direct support to Ukraine's military about an increased sabotage threat from Russia.

There have also been recent incidents at military bases in Britain that appeared linked to Iran, although Tehran has denied any links to specific attacks in Britain.

That response came days after the United States moved refuelling aircraft from another airbase to RAF Lakenheath.

On Friday 13 November, it was reported US Air Force personnel had responded to "suspected unauthorised drone activity" at the RAF Lakenheath base.

That incident followed reassurance from Downing Street, which earlier that week insisted that British air bases remained safe and security was a "top priority" after US aircraft were relocated from nearby RAF Mildenhall, also situated in Suffolk.

The base disclosed back in September that it was placed on "Charlie alert", marking the second-highest threat level that can be issued by the US Department of Defence.

![A B‑1B Lancer bomber arrives at RAF Fairford airbase, after police declared a major incident at the base in September](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/20/11/2026-11-20T110553Z_1364700550_RC2ZSNAGSPD7_RTRMADP_3_BRITAIN-USA-FAIRFORD.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

RAF Lakenheath is home to the 48th Fighter Wing, also known as the Liberty Wing, with nearly 7,000 active-duty personnel, making it the largest US fighter operation in Europe, according to the facility's official website.

Last month, US President Donald Trump said a threat linked to Iran had prompted the removal of American B-1 bombers from another British airbase, RAF Fairford, after an incident in September.

Five men were arrested near Fairford after reports of suspicious activity around the base, an incident authorities say may have been linked to Iran.
