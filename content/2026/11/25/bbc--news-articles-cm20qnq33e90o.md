**Energy bills will soar by 26% for millions of households in January after a surge in gas prices sparked by the Iran war.**

Regulator Ofgem said typical bills will increase by about £37 a month in the first three months of the new year, although individual households costs will depend on how much they use at the coldest time of year.

For a household using a typical amount of energy in England, Scotland and Wales and paying by direct debit for gas and electricity, the increase would push annual bills from £1,723 to £2,166 if it was sustained for a year.

The government will pay millions of households £100 from 4 January to help cover winter costs on top of an existing VAT cut on electricity.

Charities and suppliers expect debt to rise and a wave of people to need emergency support.

Ofgem said the rise in the cap, which is set every three month, had been driven entirely by higher wholesale costs faced by suppliers owing, primarily, to the Iran war.

The warm home discount will also take £150 off bills for six million households on benefits this winter. There have been calls for this support, funded through everyone's bill, to be extended.

"We are staring down the barrel of a brutal winter," said Dame Clare Moriarty, chief executive of Citizens Advice.

Neil Kenward, Ofgem's director general for markets, said: "Conflict in the Middle East continues to drive energy costs higher, and we know this price increase will be a worry for many households as winter approaches."

He said Ofgem had written to energy suppliers "reminding them of their responsibilities" to customers this winter. They have been told to identify people who need help early, make support easy to access and ensure repayment plans are affordable and sustainable.

Suppliers said their own profits are also capped, and everyone was feeling the "impact of our exposure to volatile fossil fuel markets".

Energy UK, which represents suppliers, said the UK needed to develop more domestic clean power and accelerate the electrification of our homes and transport.

The increase is the largest seen since Russia's invasion of Ukraine drove up prices significantly. It comes at a tough time of year, when the weather is at its coldest and use is peaking.

Individual usage differs, so for every £100 spent on energy now, the new cost will be about £126.

People on prepayment meters will be hit as spending comes when energy is needed, rather than spreading the cost over the year.

Energy Secretary Miatta Fahnbulleh said people will be "deeply concerned" by another rise in energy bills.

"For too long, British families have been paying the price for conflicts overseas and volatile fossil fuel markets," she said.

Andrew Bowie, shadow energy secretary, said: "It is an outrage that, as bills soar, Labour are not only piling green levies and taxes onto bills but signing us up to an EU scheme that risks pushing them even higher.

"Labour are utterly out of touch and could fix this tomorrow if they had the backbone."

## 'We've turned the heating down by 2.5C'

![Stacey and her kids](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/800/cpsprodpb/4be2/live/fe282b50-e827-11f1-8d9d-c156c75bcdb5.jpg)

Stacey says she keeps the curtains closed "to keep the heat in" and her daughters wear thermal clothing

Stacey, 40, a single mother from Bristol, said dealing with rising energy costs has felt like a "battle" recently and she's worried about 2027.

The office worker, whose daughters are 10 and 12, is on a fixed-term tracker tariff with E.On Next, which charges rates set below the quarterly Ofgem price cap. She expects to pay £1,800 for energy this year, £400 more than last year.

Stacey, who is part of the Changing Realities project at Glasgow University, which documents life for people on low incomes, has tried a number of things to control her energy costs.

She's turned her central heating down from 22C to 19.5C, bought electric blankets and pays her bills by direct debit.

She also leaves her curtains shut to "keep the heat in", while her daughters wear thermal clothing if it gets chilly in the house.

## Support payment for millions

The rise has been forecast for a while, allowing the government time to prepare some support measures.

They include the £100 pound payment going to millions of households in council bands A to D in England to help cover the winter costs, with equivalent payments in Scotland and Wales.

Ministers have released more details, including:

* Confirmation payments will begin on 4 January
* Anyone paying council tax by direct debit will receive the money directly into bank accounts automatically
* Others will be contacted by their council and offered payment via bank transfer or as credit on council tax.

But Citizens Advice said the money will disappear quickly, particularly for those on prepayment meters, leading to a wave of requests for emergency help.

Energy UK said the funding was poorly targeted and insufficient to stem rising levels of customer debt.
