A York man died after consuming fatal levels of two drugs, an inquest heard.

Darren George Linsdell was found unresponsive in the bedroom of his supported living accommodation at Peasholme Centre in Fishergate, York, on May 6 this year.

An inquest at the Coroner’s Court in Northallerton on Monday (November 23) heard that Mr Linsdell, who was 40, had a history of drug and alcohol misuse and died a drug-related death.

The inquest record states that he had consumed fatal levels of buprenorphine together with cocaine.

Police and paramedics attended the scene, but Mr Linsdell was pronounced dead at 10.27am.

His medical cause of death was given as the effects of a combination of buprenorphine and cocaine.

Left ventricular hypertrophy was also recorded as a medical cause.

Assistant Coroner for North Yorkshire and York, Catherine Devereux, concluded that Mr Linsdell’s death was drug-related.

Mr Linsdell was born in York on October 15, 1985, the inquest heard.

An investigation into his death began on May 7, the day after his death, and an inquest was opened on July 31.

The full inquest hearing took place at the Coroner’s Court in Northallerton on November 23.

Mr Linsdell had a record of convictions.

The Press previously reported that he was found guilty of car jacking a red MG in York’s Castle car park and nearly running over its owner in 2007 when he hit a driver sitting in his car, grabbed the vehicle and led police on a chase before crashing it in Tang Hall.

In 2008, he was jailed for three years at York Crown Court after being found guilty of robbing a drunken man in a York alley of £2,500 left to him by his grandfather.

In 2016, he and his then girlfriend were both jailed for owning a white American bulldog that was responsible for a series of vicious attacks on other dogs in York.

Their pet, called Milo, was ordered to be put down and they were banned from having or caring for any other dogs for the next 10 years.

In 2017, then 32 and of no fixed address, he was jailed at York Crown Court for eight years for his part in two offences involving vulnerable people in the Walmgate area of the city.

The offences included a street robbery that took place in July 2016 during which a man with mild autism was knocked to the ground, breaking his skull, as well as a robbery in May 2017 when Mr Linsdell and another person ambushed a man who walked with a stick at his nearby flat.
