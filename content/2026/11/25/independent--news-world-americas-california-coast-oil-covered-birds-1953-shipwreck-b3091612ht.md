Wildlife rescuers have recovered 65 oil-covered birds along a nearly 200-mile stretch of the California coastline, and preliminary testing indicates that a cargo freighter that sank more than 70 years ago may be the reason why.

The affected birds were found between Monterey and Bodega Bay, according to a Monday statement from the California Department of Fish and Wildlife’s Office of Spill Prevention and Response, as reported by ABC 7 News.

Investigators are working to determine the exact scope of the spill, though early analysis points to the SS Jacob Luckenbach. According to the National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration’s Office of National Marine Sanctuaries, the vessel went down off the coast of San Francisco on July 14, 1953, following a collision with another ship, the Hawaiian Pilot.

While crews pumped out roughly 85,000 gallons of oil and sealed off the wreck in 2002, state officials noted that severe weather can still disturb the submerged hull and release trapped fuel. Similar leaks tied to shifting storms occurred in 2016 and 2024, as reported by *People*.

The rescued animals are currently being treated by the Oiled Wildlife Care Network, with many admitted to the San Francisco Bay Oiled Bird Care and Education Center. Search teams remain active along the shore to locate any additional affected wildlife.

![More than five dozen oil-covered birds have been rescued along a nearly 200-mile stretch of the California coast, and state officials believe fuel leaking from a cargo ship that sank in 1953 may be to blame](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/25/16/56/California-bird-1.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Built in 1944 in Pascagoula, Mississippi, to support World War II efforts, the Jacob Luckenbach was carrying military supplies for the Korean War and bound for Pusan, South Korea, when it sank, according to NOAA records. Navigating through heavy fog roughly 15 miles from Point Bonita, the vessel was struck by the incoming Hawaiian Pilot, whose crew mistook the ship for the San Francisco Lightship.

The Jacob Luckenbach went down within 30 minutes into 176 feet of water inside what is now the Gulf of the Farallones National Marine Sanctuary, though all 36 crew members safely evacuated onto the Hawaiian Pilot.

The wreck remained largely out of mind until late 2001, when mystery oil spills along the central coast prompted state and federal investigators to match chemical samples from the water to the sunken ship's cargo hold, leading to the 10-month, nearly $19 million salvage operation the following year.

![State wildlife crews have recovered 65 oiled birds across a nearly 200-mile stretch between Monterey and Bodega Bay — preliminary tests link the contamination to the SS Jacob Luckenbach, which previously leaked trapped fuel during storms in 2016 and 2024](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/25/17/14/California-bird-2.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Authorities are asking members of the public not to approach or handle oiled birds if they spot them on the beach.

“Attempting to help impacted animals can do more harm than good,” the Office of Spill Prevention and Response wrote on Facebook. “There are specific protocols and procedures used to properly care for impacted animals.”

Anyone who encounters oiled wildlife in the area is urged to report the sighting to the Oiled Wildlife Care Network hotline at 1-877-UCD-OWCN.
