## A truck carrying fuel reportedly overturned on a busy highway, setting vehicles ablaze and prompting a major emergency response. Authorities warned that the death toll could rise.

At least 30 people were killed and 15 others injured after a fire broke out on a major highway in the Egyptian capital Cairo on Wednesday, the country's Health Minister Khaled Abdel Ghaffar said.

The blaze erupted after a truck carrying fuel overturned on a busy highway, according to Egyptian state media.

Footage captured by passers-by showed a massive blaze and thick clouds of smoke rising from the scene.

Emergency workers pulled people from burning vehicles as firefighters battled the flames. Hundreds of people gathered near the scene in the Warraq district of Imbaba, a suburb of Cairo.

Abdel Ghaffar said 39 ambulances were dispatched to the scene, with 15 injured people taken to hospital.

Egyptian President Abdel Fattah el-Sisi expressed his condolences to the victims in a statement.

The Health Minister said he would visit the scene, adding that the death toll was preliminary and could rise further.

Traffic accidents claim thousands of lives in Egypt every year, with speeding, poor road conditions and inadequate enforcement of traffic laws among the leading causes of crashes.

In January, 21 people were killed and 16 others injured in a multi-vehicle collision on a road near the southern province of Beni Suef.
