## Alongside his wife Carla Bruni, the ex-French president will face court in late 2027, accused of conspiracy and hiding witness tampering over alleged Libyan funding of his 2007 campaign.

Just six days before the appeal court is due to announce its decision in the case over the alleged Libyan financing of the 2007 presidential campaign, Nicolas Sarkozy already has another date with the courts.

Together with his wife, singer Carla Bruni, the former president is being sent for trial before the Paris criminal court in the case over the possibly paid-for retraction by Franco-Lebanese businessman Ziad Takieddine, a source close to the case said on Tuesday, 24 November.

All those placed under formal investigation in this case, including Mimi Marchand, "high priestess of the paparazzi," are being sent before the courts.

Nicolas Sarkozy and his partner will be tried for "criminal conspiracy to commit fraud as part of an organised gang" and "concealment of witness tampering".

Mimi Marchand will be tried for "witness tampering," "criminal conspiracy to commit fraud as part of an organised gang" and "with a view to preparing acts of corruption of foreign judicial personnel". Their trial is expected to take place from 3 November to 2 December 2027.

The defendants face a maximum sentence of ten years in prison and a fine of 150,000 euros.

## The U-turns of Ziad Takieddine, key witness in the Libyan case

This new chapter in the former head of state's legal troubles is linked to the case that had earned him a **five-year prison sentence** after a guilty plea for criminal conspiracy. He had been**released from prison after twenty days** pending his appeal.

One of the main complainants in this case was Franco-Lebanese businessman Ziad Takieddine, who died in Lebanon last year. In 2016, he said he had helped deliver up to five million euros in cash from Libyan dictator Muammar Gaddafi to Nicolas Sarkozy and the former president's chief of staff in 2006 and 2007.

But in November 2020, Ziad Takieddine suddenly backtracked on his incriminating statements in an interview with French media before changing his mind once again.

This gave rise to allegations that Nicolas Sarkozy and his wife had called on *"the queen of the paparazzi"*, Mimi Marchand, to pay this key witness to retract his statements, something they have always denied.

Carla Bruni is suspected in particular of having requested a clandestine phone line at the end of 2019 so she could communicate with Mimi Marchand about the Ziad Takieddine case.

## Numerous run-ins with the law

In the case of the alleged financing of the 2007 presidential campaign, Nicolas Sarkozy became the first former French president to be jailed.

But the former occupant of the Élysée has also been definitively convicted in two other cases: the first linked to overspending during his unsuccessful 2012 election campaign, the second for later trying to obtain favours from a judge.

Mimi Marchand, for her part, was given an 18-month suspended prison sentence last year for blackmailing a television presenter, who feared she would publish images of her daughter leaving a police station.
