Nottingham Forest have suffered successive 2-0 defeats on the road to Brentford and Bournemouth

**Nottingham Forestmanager Oliver Glasner says standards at the club were not good enough when he arrived, and has questioned why some of his squad cannot speak English after being in the country for a number of years.**

Forest are one place above the relegation zone heading into Friday's home game against Chelsea, winning just two of their first 11 games in the Premier League.

"Stability in a company is having standards," said Glasner. "Everybody who joins commits to the standards. I think, very honestly, the standards were not high enough.

"We have players who are here for four years, can't speak English. If you go to Italy, after three months, every player gives his interview in the Italian language.

"For me, if I want to manage in England, I have to speak English. If I want to manage in Italy, I have to speak Italian.

"Otherwise, Oliver Glasner, if you want to speak in your dialect, stay at home in Austria, everybody understands you, but nowhere else."

Forest, who narrowly avoided relegation last season but who did reach the Europa League semi-finals, have had a succession of managers in recent years and while Glasner refused to blame the upheaval for any problems he has inherited, he feels standards need to rise.

"We want to create a stable Nottingham Forest team that can consistently play in the top half of the Premier League," added Glasner, 52.

"We are a few steps away from this but we really believe we know which steps we have to take.

"We are still very ambitious but it looks to take a little bit longer than we all hoped. The goal is getting stability into this club and we're still working on this.

"We knew this is a transition season, let's say like this, where we still stay very ambitious but with a lot of problems to solve."

Glasner held transfer talks with Forest owner Evangelos Marinakis in London on Monday as the club prepares their plans for the January transfer window, when they hope to add up to five players to their squad.

"He [Marinakis] was asking 'what do you need, what do you want here to improve in the squad, the staff and the environment'," said Glasner.

"He wants the best for Nottingham Forest, he wants the best for everyone here.

"We need to improve in the squad, in the staff, in the environment and this is our discussion. It was a fantastic meeting and it was worth spending six hours travelling."
