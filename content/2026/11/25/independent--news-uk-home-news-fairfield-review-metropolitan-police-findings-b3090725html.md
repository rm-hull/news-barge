Systemic issues of racism, misogyny and homophobia persist within the Metropolitan Police, a newly published report has found.

Evaluating Britain’s largest police force’s reform efforts following a scathing assessment by Baroness Casey in 2023, the Fairfield Review stated that the force had demonstrated "some of the best policing and some of the worst".

Dr Gillian Fairfield was brought in to assess implementation against recommendations made by Lady Casey, concluding that the force failed to achieve "good" progress on any of them.

Her team “heard evidence of behaviour, culture and organisational failings disturbingly familiar from the Casey Review”.

She added: “Some have occurred since the Casey Review’s final report.”

The Fairfield Review, published on Wednesday, found: “The Casey Review found the Met was institutionally racist, misogynistic and homophobic.

![Dr Gillian Fairfield (centre), chair of the Fairfield with Review Suzanne Jacob, Lead Reviewer and Alex Morrell, Head of Review of the Fairfield Review](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/24/14/01M86R6ZB0HJBM898J6EWH5TS0.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“Our role has been to assess the Met’s progress in addressing this; we judge these fundamental, systemic issues remain.”

## The key findings

**Lack of faith in handling misconduct**

The Met has made substantial changes to its misconduct, handling and vetting processes, but has not yet established a “fair and trusted” system, the review found.

Too many officers and staff are still “unwilling” to report misconduct because they do not think anything will happen to the perpetrator.

The review also found there is still under-reporting of bullying and harassment because of a fear of “clique-based” counter-allegations.

**Lack of action on racism, misogyny and homophobia**

The review found that “systemic issues remain” with racism, misogyny and homophobia in the force.

While the force had made some progress dealing with racism, the review said: “The Met still struggles to acknowledge racism as a structural problem rather than something people can be trained out of.”

![Metropolitan Police commissioner Sir Mark Rowley](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/06/13/101a76ad64438720a3faee9d70eacf56Y29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzk0MDU4ODg5-2.85960335.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

There has not been a fundamental reset in stop and search, as recommended by the Casey Review.

The review also found that senior leaders shy away from using the word racism when briefing staff on strategy.

Dr Fairfield said the Met needs to show “more bravery” when facing up to its relationship with racism.

Trust among the LGBT community, damaged by catastrophic failings in the case of serial killer Stephen Port, has not improved, the report said.

**Domestic abuse team resources limited**

The Met does not have enough staff working on domestic abuse work, the review found.

The force will need “help from those who determine their funding” to improve.

## Londoners’ faith in Met is low

Dr Fairfield said Londoners feel separate from their police force, with young people describing policing as something that is done to them rather than for them.

There has been limited change in public trust, and there is limited public confidence that the Met is rooting out misconduct.
