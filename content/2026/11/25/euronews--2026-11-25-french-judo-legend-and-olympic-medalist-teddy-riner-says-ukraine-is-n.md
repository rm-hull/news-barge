## Five months ahead of France's presidential elections, the question of support to Ukraine has become a major campaign topic.

Five time French Olympic gold medallist and presidential candidate Teddy Riner declared on Wednesday that he did "not understand" why France was sending troops to Ukraine.

Speaking on the sidelines of a mayoral congress in Paris, he said that the fact that troops had been sent to Ukraine without public prior knowledge "smelled bad."

When questioned about France's solidarity with Ukraine, given it is part of the European continent, he responded "is Ukraine in the European Union?"

"If tomorrow I become president, I will strive to be the person who unites people and makes them join hands", he added.

Riner's comments come more than a week after a source disclosed to French media that foreign soldiers had been deployed on a secret training mission in Ukraine for several months to train personnel and help maintain equipment.

Russia's Foreign Minister Sergei Lavrov subsequently told reporters during a press conference that this amounted to "direct participation in the war, including target designation assistance, satellite data provision, and other forms of support."

France's Foreign Minister Jean-Noël Barrot subsequently denied allegations of direct involvement, underlining that the country was not involved in a military operation, but in "training and maintenance, carried out far from the front lines, with very limited scope and no involvement in warfare".

In early November, the European Council extended Europe's Military Assistance Mission to Ukraine for a further two years. This EU framework provides non-executive military training, mentoring, and advisory support to partner armed forces.

According to a poll published by Elabe on 14 November, Teddy Riner is polling around 4.5%, trailing far behind the hard-right National Rally party's Marine Le Pen and the Jean Luc Mélenchon's hard-left France Unbowed party.
