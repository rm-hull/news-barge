The Berlin City Mission regularly runs out of sleeping bags for homeless people. "We never have enough," spokeswoman Barbara Breuer told the German Press Agency. The shelves holding sleeping bags and sleeping pads in the clothing distribution center are often empty.

On Saturday (November 28), warm, clean and undamaged sleeping bags can be dropped off at the Spandau Arcaden at Klosterstraße 3 from 10 a.m. to 4 p.m. The radio station Paradiso will be accepting donations as part of the "Wir wärmen Berlin" campaign. According to Breuer, the cold-weather bus teams distributed 600 sleeping bags last winter. The previous season, the number was 507. In addition, there are the sleeping bags distributed at emergency shelters.

Across Berlin, a total of 1,053 emergency overnight shelter beds have been available since November 20, according to the Social Services Administration. At the start of the season in October, there were 669. On November 17, occupancy stood at 96.6 percent. Six additional projects, offering a total of about 100 beds, are currently under review. The situation is particularly difficult for homeless people in wheelchairs, Breuer said. There are only a few emergency shelters suitable for them. In addition, they must be able to transfer from their wheelchair to a bed and use the restroom on their own. If the teams operating the "cold weather buses" are unable to take those in need to suitable shelter, they provide them with one or two sleeping bags.

The Berlin S-Bahn is also working with the City Mission to collect donations for homeless people and those in need. On December 2, a specially decorated Christmas train will stop at the Schöneweide, Ostkreuz and Westkreuz stations, according to the S-Bahn. Donations of money and goods can be made directly on the train at these stations. According to the S-Bahn, sleeping bags, winter clothing and hygiene items are especially needed.
