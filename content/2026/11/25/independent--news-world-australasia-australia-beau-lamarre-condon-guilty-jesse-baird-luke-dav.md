A former Australian police officer has been found guilty of murdering TV presenter Jesse Baird and his boyfriend Luke Davies in Sydney in February 2024.

A jury in the New South Wales Supreme Court unanimously found Beau Lamarre-Condon, 31, guilty of both murders, as well as a charge of breaking and entering and stealing.

Lamarre-Condon had pleaded not guilty to the killings, arguing that he acted in self-defence during a confrontation with the couple at Baird’s home in the Sydney suburb of Paddington on 19 February 2024.

The prosecutor Brett Hatfield described the killings as “brazen and shocking murders”.

The prosecution argued that Lamarre-Condon’s relationship with Baird had been marked by jealousy and controlling behaviour after the pair separated in April 2023. Mr Hatfield told the jury that losing Baird had brought Lamarre-Condon’s “greatest insecurity” to the fore, with his “wounded pride” driving him towards a plan for revenge.

During the nine-week trial, Lamarre-Condon told the court he had entered the house unannounced to collect some personal belongings when Baird “lunged” at him, prompting him to fire his police-issued pistol.

![File. Floral tributes are seen at the Paddington residence of Jesse Baird on 1 March 2024 in Sydney, Australia](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/25/07/2049538442..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

He claimed he shot Baird a second time accidentally during a struggle and that Davies had also accidentally shot himself while attempting to wrestle the gun away from him.

The prosecution argued that Lamarre-Condon had become obsessed with Baird, his former partner, and subsequently shot Davies because he was a witness to the killing.

The prosecution also alleged that Lamarre-Condon spent several days attempting to cover up the murders and dispose of the bodies.

The bodies of Baird and Davies were discovered in surfboard bags near a monastery in rural Bungonia, south-west of Sydney, eight days after the killings. Lamarre-Condon admitted transporting the bodies in a hired van and attempting to sink the bags in a farm dam using cement and weights.

CCTV footage presented during the trial showed Lamarre-Condon making several trips to hardware stores to purchase cleaning supplies and other equipment in the days after the killings.

He also visited a hospital for a minor medical procedure and met a friend for sushi while the bodies were still in his van.

He will be sentenced in March next year.
