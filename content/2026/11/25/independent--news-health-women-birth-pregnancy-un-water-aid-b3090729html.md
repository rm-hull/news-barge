Dear world leaders attending the UN Water Conference,

You owe your lives to women.

You were born of a woman. If a midwife helped deliver you, she was likely a woman too. More than 90 per cent of frontline healthcare workers are.

In and beyond hospitals and clinics, women carry much of the unpaid work that keeps families, communities and economies running

Many call it a labour of love. But it is much more than that. Without it, healthcare systems would fail. Economies would falter. The world as we know it could not run.

Yet women do all this and more, often without the most basic resources. In some maternity wards around the world, even clean water is missing

Mothers arrive at maternity wards carrying their own buckets of water. Midwives can’t wash their hands between deliveries. Women and newborns lose their lives to infections that are entirely preventable.

Every two seconds, in the world’s least developed countries, a woman gives birth in a healthcare facility without adequate water, sanitation and hygiene.

Every day, more than 2,700 mothers and newborns die from preventable infections due to unclean births.

These are not just statistics. These are women we meet, the families we work alongside and the realities we see firsthand.

As women, we do not lack the knowledge to help solve these problems. We lack equality of opportunity to shape the healthcare we use and deliver every day. Women hold just 25% of senior leadership roles in global health, despite making up almost the entire frontline workforce.

World leaders, as you gather for the 2026 UN Water Conference, we ask you to:

* **Treat water and healthcare as related, not separate, issues:** Make clean water, decent toilets and safe medical waste disposal funded requirements in every healthcare facility through national maternal, newborn and child health plans. Ensure these services reach underserved rural and remote communities, and that they are accessible to those with disabilities. Allocate adequate budgets to keep these services running.
* **Make room for women’s rightful place in healthcare decisions:** Guarantee equal and meaningful representation for women and women-led organisations, from global decision-making bodies to local healthcare facility governance boards, with real roles in planning, budgeting and oversight. This is not charity. Women’s frontline expertise is essential to making healthcare safer for everyone.
* **Show us what has changed as a result of your promises:** Report publicly every two years: Is clean water reaching maternity wards? Are infections and sepsis rates falling? Is women’s frontline expertise shaping healthcare decisions?

We have heard promises before, and we welcome every commitment to change. Now let your commitments reach the mother who can’t clean herself or her baby after giving birth. And the midwife who can’t wash her hands between deliveries.

When you leave the conference, we will return to our hospitals and communities. Let us return knowing our voices have been heard and action will follow.

With the power and privilege you hold, please remember the women who brought you into this world. And who with their ‘labour of love’ keep our healthcare systems, economies and the world running.

*In solidarity with mothers, healthcare workers and women everywhere,*
