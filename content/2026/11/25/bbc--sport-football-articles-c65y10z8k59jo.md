Manchester City are currently third in the Premier League

**The chair of English football's independent regulator has told MPs he "has the stomach" to take potential action againstManchester City.**

The club was found guilty of breaching Premier League financial rules over a nine-season period by an independent commission.

The verdict, published in September, found City's Abu Dhabi owners - a private investment company belonging to United Arab Emirates vice-president Sheikh Mansour - inflated revenues and disguised losses by £900 million through "sham" arrangements.

City deny wronging and have appealed, but if the ruling is upheld, they could face an investigation by the Independent Football Regulator (IFR).

It has the power to remove directors if they are deemed unsuitable, and even to force a sale of the club.

Appearing in front of parliament's Digital, Culture, Media and Sport select committee, IFR chairman David Kogan was clear that the watchdog would not be afraid to investigate the club's hierarchy and take action, if deemed necessary.

"Forget the name of the club, forget the size of the club, forget the scale of the issue. We cannot simply ignore our statutory duties in relation to a club because it happens to be of a certain size," he said.

"We absolutely will have to take the appropriate action as laid out in the legislation, depending on the circumstances as they come to us, which are the evidence that's been presented and collated by the independent commission of the Premier League and by any judgement that takes place on that evidence through an appeal.

"But in one sense, the evidence has already been judged by those cases having been found guilty, so we will absolutely have the stomach to do so.

"Would I, with an organisation that has a relatively new, fairly young staff, that's been going for less than a year, would I have chosen to have this particular issue in our laps this early? Well, probably not.

"But the truth is, if we don't act - assuming those circumstances are triggered - if we don't act, what is the purpose of having a football regulator?

"So I think we have to absolutely, clearly be seen to be doing the job that you as legislators have asked us to do, and we absolutely will do that, whatever that means."

The fallout from the case has prompted concern that wider UAE investment in UK projects could suffer. In September, Prime Minister Andy Burnham was criticised for saying he would be "really concerned" if the scandal led to City's owners selling the club.

Kogan added: "Does the evidence, if we come to assess it in relation to individuals, stand up in terms of the legislation as being a cause for concern? And if it is, we will have to act appropriately.

"That is the only test, not the politics around it, not the place where it came from, not the size of the club. It is the only test because without that, you cannot have an effective regulator."

When asked how any possible legal battles with City would be paid for, IFR chief executive Richard Monks said a litigation reserve of £2m was to be built up over the next two years via a levy charged to the clubs. "When we take litigation, we expect to win and we expect to reclaim costs and repay that reserve from costs," he said.

"Now clearly - and I'm not talking about a specific case - clearly, there could be a very large legal case of any particular club that would require us to spend more than £2m in terms of litigation costs and barrister costs in particular. So if that happens, we have agreed with government [that it] will effectively lend us the money in terms of cash flow.

"As I say, we'd expect to win that litigation, to claim costs and repay government. If we failed in that massive litigation, then ultimately it would be added to the levy and we'd repay government that way."
