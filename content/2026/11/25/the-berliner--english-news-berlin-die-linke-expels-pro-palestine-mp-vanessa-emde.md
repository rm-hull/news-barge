Vanessa Emde, a politician who became controversial in the German media for her strong pro-Palestinian stance, is no longer a member of Die Linke in the Berlin House of Representatives. After several hours of discussion on Tuesday evening, Die Linke’s representatives voted to expel Emde. According to their spokesperson, 33 representatives voted in favour of the motion, six against, and four abstained.

In a joint statement, the Die Linke parliamentary group affirmed, among other things, Israel’s right to exist. Emde, however, continues to argue that, from the perspective of international law, only people rather than states have a right to exist. Critics of Emde have interpreted this as an antisemitic statement.

Die Linke’s parliamentary group will shrink to 43 members following Emde’s expulsion. The projected coalition between Die Linke, Die Grünen, and the SPD would still have a relatively comfortable majority of 12 votes. In the days leading up to the vote, Emde announced that she intends to retain her seat in the House of Representatives, thereby making her an independent member of parliament.

Emde criticised her exclusion from the parliamentary group. “The decision is a watershed moment for Die Linke and has shown that the media smear campaign has had its effect and that my parliamentary group – my former parliamentary group – has obviously caved under pressure from the SPD and Die Grünen,” Emde said after the decision.

She has also experienced a great deal of solidarity in recent days. She stated, “I would like to say to the many comrades who may be struggling with the decision to leave the party. Please stay. You are not alone, we are not alone, and we will organise ourselves.”

The leader of Die Linke’s parliamentary group in Berlin, Tobias Schulze, described Emde’s expulsion as a difficult decision. “We didn’t take this step lightly. Many members of parliament expressed today that they found this step difficult, but understandable,” Schulze said.

The criticism of Emde’s behaviour was unanimous. The Berlin SPD state chairpeople, Steffen Krach and Bettina König, reacted positively to Emde’s expulsion. “She significantly hampered the coalition negotiations, and it’s good that Die Linke has now provided clarity,” they stated jointly.
