Top British defence businesses are being told to urgently step up their security arrangements during secret briefings with MI5 and other agencies who are warning that the country is under attack from Russia and that their staff risk assassination.

The warnings, being given to the chief executives of Britain’s industries in the “critical national infrastructure”, are being delivered by ministers and intelligence chiefs.

They are being told that businesses working on Ukrainian defence-related projects are especially vulnerable.

“The alert follows an increasing number of sabotage attempts against European defence-related facilities,” the Ministry of Defence said in a statement announcing the secret briefings.

“Both the briefings and the alert will set out actions organisations can take to protect their sites, people and operations amidst the changing threat landscape.

![The UK Carrier Strike Group, led by the nation's largest warship, the HMS Prince of Wales, has been deployed as part of Operation Firecrest to conduct exercises alongside US, Canadian, and Northern European allies in the the North Atlantic and High North against Russian threat](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/21/15/2283989356..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“Recommended actions include reducing digital exposure, improving the detection and reporting of hostile reconnaissance, and identifying and protecting critical assets.”

The list of companies that have been invited to the briefings, taking place on Tuesday and Wednesday, is being kept secret for security reasons.

But they are being told that they are already under attack and can expect that Russia will continue to try to undermine efforts to defend Ukraine against Moscow’s full-scale invasion of 2022.

Minister for the Armed Forces, Louise Sandher-Jones, said: “Russia and other hostile actors pose a growing threat to the companies that equip and support our Armed Forces.

“We are bringing defence industry leaders together to ensure they understand that threat and take practical action to protect their people, technology and supply chains.”

Immediate steps have been outlined for companies to take to protect themselves. This includes an increase in the personal security for people working on Ukrainian contracts.

The warnings also extend to industries that are part of Britain’s “critical national infrastructure” which includes communications, power generation, water supplies, transport, gas and oil deliveries, the National Health Service, logistics companies and many others.

The government advice includes guidance for staff to avoid being attacked by Russian agents.

“Individuals can be targets as well as organisations. It’s not just those at the top of an organisation who might be of interest – anyone with access to sensitive information, assets or people may be of interest to hostile actors. Encourage staff to proactively manage their personal security,” industry chiefs are being told.

The government website advises British staff to behave in ways that have become routine for Ukrainians under direct attack by Russia, and come amid a rising number of warnings that Russia is now a “state sponsor of terrorism” in the West.

“Staff should remain alert to their surroundings, avoid predictable routines where practicable, and consider how personal information, movements and behaviours could be used to build a picture of them, their role and their organisation,” the guidance says.

In the most dramatic warnings to industries working in the UK since the Cold War, those working with Ukraine are being told to immediately reduce their profiles online.

They need to also “detect and report hostile reconnaissance”.

To “minimise the impact of an attack”, they should: “Identify and protect your most critical assets, maintain physical protection systems and practice your response.”

This includes closely monitoring deliveries, including the mail, and: “Develop, test and rehearse procedures for responding to suspicious activity, suspicious items and attack methodologies such as arson.”

“Russia poses an enduring and significant threat to the UK and its interests around the world. The Russian state and its proxies knowingly seek to disrupt businesses and organisations that underpin the British way of life or are vital to the defence of Ukraine. They use tactics like cyber attacks, sabotage and disinformation to take organisations offline, disrupt activity, and weaken trust,” the MoD said in a statement.
