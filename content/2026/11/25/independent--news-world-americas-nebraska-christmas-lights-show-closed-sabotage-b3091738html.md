A popular Christmas light display in Nebraska remains closed after organizers discovered what they believe was an “intentional act of sabotage” to equipment powering the holiday attraction.

The Nebraska Christmas Lights Show in Lincoln shut down Monday after its generators appeared to have been tampered with, damaging the park’s electronic controls and low-voltage systems, event president Constantine Syniy said in a Facebook post.

“We are heartbroken to share that our Christmas Lights Show has suffered what we strongly believe was an intentional act of sabotage,” Syniy wrote.

The attraction features more than 1 million synchronized lights set to over 50 songs, a glowing tunnel, 15 photo spots and a Christmas Village with 10 themed rooms.

Organizers were assessing the damage and warned they might need to replace hundreds of low-voltage fuses to get the display working. They initially feared that the attraction might remain closed for the rest of the holiday season due to the extent of the damage.

![The Nebraska Christmas Lights Show in Lincoln remains closed Wednesday after suspected generator tampering damaged its electronic controls and lighting systems](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/25/19/40/484402551_661894073022578_6656336687491626085_n.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

In an update Tuesday, organizers said they had made progress restoring the lighting systems but were still unable to reopen.

“We have made significant progress in restoring our lighting systems, and things are looking promising for reopening tomorrow!” Syniy wrote in the Facebook post Tuesday.

However, Syniy confirmed to *The Independent* that the attraction would remain closed Wednesday.

Syniy said he does not have anyone he suspects of causing the damage, and organizers have not contacted police about the incident.

The damage poses no safety concerns to the public because it involves low-voltage electronics and internal Ethernet connections, Syniy added.

The display is at the Sandhills Global Event Center for the first time this year after organizers outgrew its previous location at Star City Shores.

The show also looks a little different this year, switching from a drive-through display to a walk-through experience. Tickets cost $15 per person, while children 2 and younger get in free.

The Nebraska Christmas Lights Show opened November 19 and is scheduled to run through December 27.
