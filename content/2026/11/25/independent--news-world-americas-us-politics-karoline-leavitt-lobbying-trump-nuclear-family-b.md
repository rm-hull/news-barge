Former White House press secretary Karoline Leavitt said she is lobbying President Donald Trump to push policies that “encourage the nuclear family” and that the high number of women leaving the workforce should be “celebrated.”

Leavitt quit her high-profile White House job shortly after returning from maternity leave with her second child, Vivi, and the vacancy for the role is yet to be filled.

In an interview with Fox News anchor Rachel Campos Duffy, the wife of Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy, Leavitt said that leaving the briefing room podium behind was “the best decision” she ever made, and she hopes more women follow in her footsteps.

While Leavitt acknowledged “not every woman is afforded the privilege” of being a stay-at-home mom, she railed against a study that found 240,000 women age 20 and older have left the workforce since January. Wages failing to match the rising cost of child care was a concern cited by The National Women’s Law Center.

But instead of supporting mothers who want to work, Leavitt said that the Trump administration “should continue pursuing pro-family policies to reward married couples and households where you want to not just thrive, not just survive, but thrive on a single family income.”

![Former White House press secretary Karoline Leavitt said she is lobbying President Donald Trump to push policies that ‘encourage the nuclear family’](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/25/20/2272275495..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“I'm going to continue to talk to the president about pursuing policies that encourage the nuclear family and support women and moms who want to make the decision to be home with their kids,” she said.

The MAGA mom cited the Trump administration’s Trump Accounts and middle-class tax cuts as examples of how the president is supporting women who want to stay at home with their children.

“This is a decision that women should be encouraged to do and celebrated for,” she added.

The 29-year-old, married to 60-year-old real estate developer Nicholas Riccio, also opened up about being a working mother as Trump’s press secretary. She served in the role for 18 months, and her son, Niko, was born while she was working on Trump’s 2024 presidential campaign.

“Being a working mother is so hard,” she said. “I know, I did it for the first two years of my first child's life, and it's incredibly difficult and challenging. And so women should be encouraged and supported when they have that longing in their heart to take a step back and be with their kids.”

![The 29-year-old, pictured with her son and real estate developer Nicholas Riccio at the White House last Halloween, said that ‘being a working mother is so hard’ and that quitting her job was ‘the best decision’ she ever made](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/25/21/2243685651..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Last year, *The Independent* reported on the crisis for working U.S. mothers, with women aged 25 to 44 with kids under five leaving the workforce in droves.

The reasons why women were quitting work to stay home with the kids varied, but included unaffordable childcare costs, inadequate maternity leave, and companies pulling back on pandemic-era work-from-home policies to drive employees back to the office five days a week.

Mothers saw the employment rate tick up after the pandemic, in part due to work from home flexibility.

Some of the mothers *The Independent* spoke to felt it important to push back against the Trump administration’s embrace of pronatalism and “Tradwife” movements.

Katie Miller, the wife of Trump’s White House chief of staff Stephen Miller and a friend of Leavitt’s, regularly laments America’s plummeting birthrates on social media. In March, she said that “children shouldn’t be delayed for [women’s] careers.”
