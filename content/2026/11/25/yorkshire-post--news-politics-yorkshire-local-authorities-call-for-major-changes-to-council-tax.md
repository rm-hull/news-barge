# Yorkshire local authorities call for major changes to council tax

## The Special Interest Group of Municipal Authorities has launched a fresh manifesto warning of financial issues for northern councils.

Ten Yorkshire and Humber local authorities have backed calls for an urgent reform of council tax, describing it as an “outdated” system.

This was part of a fresh manifesto from Sigoma, the Special Interest Group of Municipal Authorities, which represents 50 metropolitan and unitary councils including several in Yorkshire.

This warned that rising demand for people with special educational needs and disabilities (Send) and social care is putting growing pressure on authorities with some of the weakest local tax bases.

This is partly because of an inherent unfairness in the council tax system, based on the valuations from 1991.

Areas which have seen rapid house price growth, like London and the South East, end up with wealthy people paying a lower rate of council tax, compared to the rest of the country.

Since 1995, average house prices have risen by more than four-fold in SIGOMA areas, compared with over five-fold across England and six-fold in London.

Sigoma says the changes in property values over the past three decades mean “the system no longer accurately reflects the relative strength of local housing markets or councils’ ability to raise revenue”.

It is calling for the first national property revaluation since council tax was introduced, followed by regular revaluations to prevent the system becoming outdated again.

It argues that modernising the system would provide a more accurate assessment of what different areas can realistically raise, allowing government funding to be distributed more fairly.

This comes as the Reform UK leader of Barnsley Council Coun William Brown told BBC Radio Sheffield that his party cannot keep its election pledge to freeze council tax.

“It's everyone's dream to freeze council tax but the reality is we have looked at the budget gap we have, we have looked at the finances and it's far from what was published before we went into office,” he said.

“We have been in six months now and we've been going line by line across all departments. We are struggling like most councils across the country.

“Seventy per cent of the budget goes on social care and it's increasing year on year. There are difficult choices to make.”

Barnsley’s chief executive Sarah Norman, secretary of Sigoma, backed the manifesto, saying: “Demand for services remains significant, particularly in areas affected by deprivation and poor health outcomes, and it is essential that local government funding properly reflects those pressures.

“Sigoma's manifesto sets out a clear and evidence-led case for reform, ensuring resources are directed towards communities with the greatest need while providing councils with the stability needed to plan for the future.

“This is a constructive agenda for reform, one that would help councils to continue supporting residents, investing in their places, and contributing to the Government's growth ambitions.”
