The Left Party *(Die Linke)*, which placed first in local elections in Berlin in September, has been trying to hammer out a coalition deal with the centre-left Social Democrats (SPD) and the Greens.

Both have urged *Die Linke* to oust Emde, a newly elected local representative from the Neukölln district, which has a large Arab population.

Emde has been at the centre of weeks-long controversy over her particularly harsh condemnation of Israel and pro-Palestinian stances.

*Die Linke*hopes to make Elif Eralp, a lawyer and long-time local housing activist, the first mayor of Berlin from the hard-left party.**READ ALSO: Why is the Left Party in Berlin being accused of antisemitism?**

Emde's expulsion on Tuesday -- which was backed by more than two-thirds of *Die Linke's* lawmakers in the Berlin parliament -- clears one potential hurdle to sealing a coalition deal with the SPD and the Greens.

Local Berlin SPD leaders said Tuesday that they "welcomed" the move.

*Die Linke's* local parliamentary group leader, Tobias Schluze, said relations between the party and Emde had become "irreconcilable".

He cited Emde's decision to initially sign a party statement against antisemitism, only to later withdraw her signature, and Emde's participation in a protest against *Die Linke*.
