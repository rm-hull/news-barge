Ethan Ives-Griffiths, two, was murdered by his grandparents while he was on the child protection register

**Opportunities to protect a two-year-old who was murdered by his grandparents were missed by multiple agencies, a review has found.**

Ethan Ives-Griffiths died on 16 August 2021 after suffering a "catastrophic" head injury at his grandparents' home in Garden City, Flintshire. He had been beaten, starved and deprived of fluids.

North Wales Safeguarding Board apologised "unreservedly" after a review found attempts by his dad to raise concerns were not acted upon.

Michael Ives, 49, and Kerry Ives, 47, were jailed for life in 2025. Ethan's mum, Shannon Ives, 29, was jailed for 12 years for causing or allowing his death and child cruelty.

Flintshire council said it "failed" Ethan and was "deeply sorry".

When Ethan died, he was dangerously dehydrated and severely underweight, with 40 visible bruises or marks.

After his grandparents were found guilty, Mr Justice Griffiths said the pair had engaged in a "campaign to break him" and text messages from Michael Ives said the toddler would have to "learn the hard way".

Ethan was "a shadow of the chubby healthy child he had been" in the days before he fatally collapsed, the judge said.

The review said there had been "missed opportunities" to understand Ethan's situation and hear his "voice"

Co-chair of the safeguarding board, Nicola Stubbins, said the child practice review, published on Wednesday, made "difficult reading".

She said: "We apologise unreservedly that those multi-agency systems did not protect Ethan as they should have.

"We owe it to Ethan to ensure that the vital lessons from this case lead to lasting changes in our professional practice."

The Ives family was well known to health and education services, Flintshire council's children's services, and North Wales Police, because of issues surrounding "significant domestic violence", substance abuse and homelessness - with 36 referrals and concerns raised about Ethan or his immediate family since 2016.

The review, which refers to Ethan as Child E, noted a history of "non-engagement" with services by Ethan's mother and her family, but found there was "no escalation of concern", with a high proportion of cases closed with no further action.

It also found wider communication between agencies was impacted by IT systems which did not share all the historical information held on file regarding previous referrals.

When his mother and father, Will Griffiths, split on 9 June 2021, police in attendance noted that Ethan's father was "begging" the social worker not to take him to his grandparents, but there was no record that was shared with the children's service.

The family were initially sent to Shannon's sister, where staff thought Ethan would be safe, but later - unbeknown to the council - went to live in the home of Michael and Kerry Ives, in Garden City.

At the time, the review noted school staff, the social worker and the police officer involved thought they were instrumental in sending the children to safety, "little did they know this was the first step in [Ethan's] demise".

The "impact of this knowledge continues to be felt by these staff, who have noted the lack of support they have felt from their respective agencies since this time", the report added.

When Shannon Ives informed children's services on 7 July that Ethan was living with his grandparents, there was no record of any checks being made about his welfare and his address was not changed on the IT system, the review found.

Griffiths told reviewers he had contacted the council to express concerns about Ethan's mother and her parents, but "no one listened".

The report states it was "well documented" that there were concerns about Ethan's grandparents' care and highlighted a "missed opportunity" by Flintshire children's services to "gain valuable information".

Though national guidelines set out in Wales Safeguarding Procedures state that children on the child protection register should been seen by social workers every 10 days, the last time Ethan was seen was 22 July 2021 - more than three weeks before his death - despite a number of attempted home visits.

During a visit on 5 August, a social worker waited on the doorstep for 45 minutes, but did not see Ethan because Shannon and Kerry Ives said he was asleep. CCTV footage later revealed Ethan had already been a victim of physical abuse at this point, which his mother and grandmother were attempting to conceal.

Several attempts to see the children by a social worker and health visitor occurred over subsequent days, but access was denied each time.

Ethan's grandparents, Michael Ives and Kerry Ives, were given life sentences last year after being convicted of his murder

The review also considered the effect of the Covid19 pandemic on the provision of social services - with social workers having to isolate and some agency workers brought in from outside the local authority area.

It noted an "unprecedented increase" in referrals by 76 per cent in the three months between March 2021 and June 2021 had also placed an "additional significant pressure" on services.

## 'His voice was not heard'

However, the review concluded a repeated feature of the case was the "missed opportunities" by the various agencies to hear the voices of the Ives children, especially Ethan.

It said there was little information about him in reports and discussions, and files did not "capture his needs, his character, his likes or dislikes".

In one police report, which recorded the events of 9 June 2021 - when Shannon Ives and her children were moved from their family home, there was no mention of him, despite being present.

"From the information shared," reads the report, "it is safe to say that [Ethan's] voice was not heard."

Shannon Ives, Ethan's mum, had a history of not engaging with health and local authority services

The report set out 16 recommendations to "strengthen safeguarding practice across agencies" with Fôn Roberts, chair of the review, stating Ethan must remain "at the centre".

"He was a young boy who needed safety, care and protection," he said.

"His voice was not heard, and historical information was not consistently brought together."

He stressed new policies, training and systems "must translate into consistent practice".

"The priority must be to embed the learning and demonstrate that it makes a meaningful difference to the safety and wellbeing of children," Roberts said.

In a statement, Flintshire County Council said the "profound impact" of Ethan's death had left a "lasting effect on the professionals and teams responsible for safeguarding children in Flintshire".

It said the child practice review "highlighted areas where information recording and sharing, professional curiosity and co-ordinated intervention could have, and should have, been stronger".

The council apologised to Ethan's father and said it would work to strengthen its approach to "engaging with fathers and male carers in our work with families".

"We recognise that our services were part of the safeguarding response and accept our responsibility for the shortcomings identified by the review.

"We are deeply sorry for the areas where we failed Ethan."

Chief executive, Neal Cockerton, said the Ives made "sustained effort" to "mislead and misdirect", providing false information to professionals seeking to support and protect Ethan.

"While the responsibility for Ethan's murder lies solely with the people convicted of that crime, as a council we deeply regret that opportunities to do more were missed and that he did not receive the level of protection he deserved.

"We have never lost sight of the fact that a young child lost his life, in circumstances where opportunities to protect him were missed," he said.

"While we cannot change what happened, it has strengthened our collective determination to do everything possible to protect vulnerable children.

"However, no amount of learning or improvement can change the outcome for Ethan or lessen the grief experienced by those who love him."

Craig MacLeod, director of social services, was asked if Ethan would still be alive if there had not been the missed opportunities.

He said that "whether interventions would have made a difference I don't know..."

MacLeod was also asked why the father's concerns were not acted upon.

"We were focusing on domestic violence and emotional harm.

"We weren't fully attuned to the risk presented by the grandparents."

A Welsh government spokesperson said: "We expect all those involved in safeguarding children to carefully consider the findings and recommendations of this review and take the necessary action to strengthen practice, improve working together and keep vulnerable children safe."
