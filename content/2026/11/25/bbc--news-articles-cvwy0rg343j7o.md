**A new report has found institutional racism, misogyny and homophobia still exist in the Metropolitan Police, three years after a damning review which urged the force to make significant changes.**

The Met has failed to make good progress in all areas flagged as needing improvement.

The landmark report by Dr Gillian Fairfield has been looking at how much the UK's biggest police force has changed since Baroness Louise Casey's scathing review in 2023, external.

Met Commissioner Sir Mark Rowley said he accepted the Fairfield Review's 10 recommendations to help reform the force.

Rowley said he had been "very clear" since he started the job that prejudices including racism, misogyny and homophobia, "have got to be ruthlessly confronted".

Among other criticisms, the Fairfield Review found the Met continued to struggle to acknowledge racism as a structural problem "rather than something people can be trained out of".

It added: "None of these behaviours have changed sufficiently. Neither has the Met's institutional defensiveness."

Fairfield said she was struck by the fact that after a number of strategic briefings with the Met, no senior leader had used the word "racism" and felt the force was too defensive in its approach.

"Without more bravery than it has shown, the Met cannot fundamentally face its relationship to race and racism," she said.

"If it cannot name it, it cannot face it. The leadership team does not currently do this".

The review also raised "serious and worrying criticisms and concerns" about the treatment of bereaved families when the Met carried out investigations into deaths and murders.

Rowley said the report had "some really powerful testimony, some really humbling testimony" from victims affected by the force's handling of their case, and in some instances the Met's actions "made the whole situation worse, not better", which Sir Mark said was "just not acceptable".

The 389-page Fairfield Review, jointly commissioned by the mayor of London and the Met commissioner examined the progress made by the force after a number of recommendations made in the Casey Review.

It found in some cases the force's "first thought is how the media will react rather than how to fix problems".

Mayor of London Sadiq Khan said the Met must be "fully responsible and accountable for its failings" and "must tackle the culture that allows these behaviours to take root in the first place".

He said a deep-rooted, systemic change was needed to rebuild public trust.

Fairfield also warned further scandals were "highly likely" unless the Met's culture is addressed.

The Casey Review was commissioned after the abduction, rape and murder of Sarah Everard in 2021.

The 33-year-old was walking home in south London when she was taken by police officer Wayne Couzens.

David Carrick, an armed officer in the Met, is currently serving 37 life sentences after attacking more than a dozen women.

Another high profile scandal was later exposed in October 2025 when secret filming at Charing Cross police station by BBC Panorama revealed racism, misogyny and officers boasting about the use of excessive force, as well as jokes about rape and sexual assault.

The Met is also reviewing findings from another BBC Panorama investigation about predatory detective Wayne Arter, who sexually exploited domestic and sexual abuse victims.

His wife Rebekah died in unexplained circumstances in a luxury hotel room in Barbados in June 2024.

Arter took his own life while on remand in July 2024.

The Fairfield Review found some areas of progress including the way misconduct and vetting procedures were handles, as well as some improvements to stop and search transparency powers.

Recruitment has also improved - comparing the proportion of ethnic minority workers in the Met from March 2023 to March 2026, there was rise from 15.6% to 19.1% for officers staff and 26% to 30.1% for staff.

These figures are still significantly below London's population, where ethnic minorities make up almost half the population, at 46.3%.

The review also raised concerns about staff reporting bad behaviour.

Less than half (44%) felt confident raising concerns about a senior officer and a quarter said they were "not so confident", while 17% were "not at all" confident their concerns would be acted on.

Around half (49%) of those who felt bullied or harassed at work had not reported it, while of those who had two thirds (67%) said no action had been taken.

Home Secretary Shabana Mahmood has welcomed the Fairfield Review.

"Three years ago, when Baroness Casey completed her report, the force was facing a profound crisis.

"I particularly welcome the improvement in policing serious sexual offences as this government pursues its goal of halving violence against women and girls.

"But there are further improvements required, and I welcome the commissioner's commitment to this".
