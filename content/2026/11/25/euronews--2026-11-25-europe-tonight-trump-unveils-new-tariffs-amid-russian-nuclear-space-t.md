## Trump unveils new tariff proposals after a Supreme Court ruling, NATO pledges defense against space threats amid satellite reports, Serbia skips the Brussels summit, and Zelenskyy and Magyar signal a thaw with volleyball diplomacy.

U.S. President Donald Trump has introduced fresh tariff proposals targeting 14 trading partners—including China, the European Union, Japan, and Vietnam—following an eight-month trade probe into excess industrial capacity. Proposed rates reach up to 40% for China if trade talks fail (with a 20% baseline cap under a truce expiring in January, potentially stacking with existing duties), while other partners face lower caps or varied rates pending negotiations and public comment. The move comes after the U.S. Supreme Court struck down broader tariffs imposed earlier, prompting live debate over economic and market impacts.

### NATO Pledges Space Defense Amid Russian Satellite Reports

NATO Secretary General Mark Rutte stated that the alliance's defense commitment includes protecting against threats in space, responding to intelligence and media reports that Russia may prepare to launch a nuclear-armed satellite within weeks. Speaking alongside German Chancellor Friedrich Merz in Berlin, Rutte emphasized ongoing deterrence reviews, while Moscow denied the allegations and maintained its space activities follow international obligations.

### Serbia Absent From Brussels Western Balkans Summit

At the EU–Western Balkans Summit in Brussels, expectations remain high for Serbia's new leadership to clarify its foreign policy and EU alignment. However, Parliament Speaker and Acting President Aleksandar Čupić did not attend, citing urgent parliamentary proceedings in Belgrade. Coverage includes perspectives from Sara el Sarag, representing the Students' List, as questions linger over Belgrade's strategic direction.

### "Volleyball Diplomacy" Signals Thaw Between Kyiv and Budapest

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy and Hungarian Prime Minister Péter Magyar played a friendly volleyball match with students at a Hungarian-language high school in Ukraine's Zakarpattia region. The visit highlights a softening in bilateral relations after years of friction, using sports as an informal icebreaker—a tactic with a long history in international politics.
