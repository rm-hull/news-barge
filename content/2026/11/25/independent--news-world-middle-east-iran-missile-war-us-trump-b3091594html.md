Iran is developing new, more powerful missiles for long-range strikes, experts have warned.

Tehran is looking to create hybrids of existing weapons that will hit more accurately and be harder to intercept, it is claimed. According to the Institute for the Study of War (ISW) and the Critical Threats Project (CTP) at conservative think tank the American Enterprise Institute (AEI), the Iranian Revolutionary Guard (IRGC) has released footage which displays underground missile facilities.

“Iran appears to be developing missile variants that pair more powerful propulsion systems with warheads that can manoeuvre before impact to strike targets with greater accuracy and less chance of interception,” the report said.

“Iran can likely produce these missile variants with minimal changes to its industrial supply chain because of their similar technologies, although the Iranian defence industrial base’s current capacity for mass missile production is unclear.”

![An exhibition of missiles and drones at Tehran’s National Aerospace Park](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/25/15/2304267327..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The ISW goes on to claim the IRGC has developed hybrid missile variants that combine the Haj Qassem medium-range ballistic missile’s propulsion system with Kheibar Shekan, Kheibar Shekan-2, and Fattah-1 warheads. All three have been used in strikes on Israel and other US assets in the region during prior conflicts, the institute said.

“The Haj Qassem has a stronger propulsion system that enables the missile to attain a higher terminal velocity, according to Iranian media assessments,” the think tank explained. “This enables the missile to travel at a higher speed as it approaches its target, leaving air defence systems with less time to react and increasing the missile’s force on impact.”

The analysis comes after a report by the BBC earlier this month claimed construction work was taking place at Iranian military bases.

The construction appeared to be for new buildings, moving infrastructure underground to protect from future attacks and repair work at bases which have been targeted by US and Israeli strikes. A CIA analyst the BBC that some excavations at a site near Shahroud could also be evidence of missile production.

![US defence secretary Pete Hegseth said in September that Iran’s defence system has been “destroyed”](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/24/22/2306101344..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Within weeks of the initial US strikes launched at the end of February, President Donald Trump claimed the US had “destroyed 100 percent of Iran’s military capability”.

Six months later, US Secretary of Defense Pete Hegseth said in a speech to troops in September: “These Americans and their peers here have destroyed Iran’s navy, Iran’s air force, their defence system, their leadership and their military industrial capacity, their entire defence space”.

On November 10, Trump claimed further US attacks on Iran were “highly unlikely”. However, since then the US has launched strikes on Iran’s strategically important Qeshm island. That followed drones attacks launched by Iran on a Kuwaiti military base which hosts U.S. soldiers.

Speaking to the media in mid November, Iranian Foreign ​Ministry spokesperson Esmail Baghaei warned Tehran would “use all our tools ​to confront this U.S. aggression” as peace talks to end the nine-month conflict continue to stall.
