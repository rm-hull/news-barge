Around 15,000 York adults are not online, new research has found, as campaigners call for non-digital communication methods to be maintained for older people in the city.

Older Citizens Advocacy York said residents aged over 60’s engagement with services “dropped significantly” in almost every city ward when communication changed from in-person to purely online or phone-based services.

The charity said there are “severe digital barriers” when physical access points are closed.

Areas of York with higher proportions of older residents – such as Haxby and Wigginton, Clifton and rural surrounding wards – showed “acute drops” when services switched to digital self, the charity said.

Older Citizens Advocacy York has called for non-digital services to remain, with a spokesperson saying: “There is an assumption that everyone wants to, can, or should use digital services.

“Some people have little interest in technology, while others prefer face-to-face contact or do not want their lives managed through apps.”

Prenika Anand, a digital health professional, added: “Age UK York estimates that 15,000 York adult residents are not online, with the majority being older adults.

“Just because a service is available online does not mean it is accessible to everyone.”

Older Citizens Advocacy York, which offers residents over 50 practical support to access services and benefit, also found that older people may “miss out on financial support because they do not know what they are entitled to, feel stigma about claiming or struggle with complex processes”.

The charity said responsibility “must shift from expecting individuals to seek help towards organisations proactively identifying unmet need, communicating clearly and making entitlements easier to claim”.

Fiona McCulloch, chief executive of Citizens Advice York, said £3.7 million of pension credit is unclaimed in York each year, according to an estimate by the council.

“Come to us [Citizens Advice] early,” she said. “Debt isn't just a financial issue. It causes sleepless nights, unopened post, anxiety, and social isolation.”

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/19759490.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Fiona McCulloch, chief executive of Citizen's Advice York *(Image: Supplied)*

Ali Rogan, Older Citizens Advocacy York trustee and author of a new report by the charity about helping older people in the city, said: “We must make services easier to access, put human connection at the heart and strengthen collaboration.

“We’ve started an important conversation – now it’s time to turn those insights into meaningful action.”

Older Citizens Advocacy York’s report followed its Advocacy Forum last month which, in collaboration with the York Older People’s Assembly and Age UK, asked people to help define the future of advocacy for people aged over 50 in the area.

Wayne Dawson, the charity’s director, said: “Our heartfelt thanks to our expert panel and everyone who contributed so enthusiastically. Your insights helped shape this important conversation.

“Now it’s time to turn ideas into action, drive lasting change and build a Community Advocacy Network that gives people over 50 a stronger voice.”
