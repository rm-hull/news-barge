# Newly restored Grade II-listed Yorkshire bandstand targeted in 'arson attack'

## An alleged arson attempt on the Todmorden Bandstand has been condemned by councillors as “upsetting” and “disgraceful”.

Todmorden Town Council said it was “extremely disappointed and concerned” after damage was caused to the bandstand, a Grade-II listed venue which has hosted events throughout the spring and summer.

In a statement, the council said the incident has been reported to police.

It said the damage was minimal and was not expected to affect the Edwardian building’s use, with repairs set to be carried out as soon as possible.

![The Grade-II listed bandstand was set on fire having only just been restored](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOmU2N2FkNWI5LTM5OTQtNDRmNS1iY2UxLTk2MDlkMjA5MTYzZDozOGMxMzA4OS1lZTViLTQ1MWYtOWQ0MS04OWRhMGQzNzdjODg=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


The bandstand was built in 1914 and has hosted orchestras, football commentary, and a carnival attracting 10,000 people in 1964.

The bandstand was previously set on fire in 1999, and after years of deterioration was earmarked for demolition in 2019.

But after public backlash, including a 3,000-signature petition and an approach to Historic England to have the bandstand listed, it was restored and reopened this year.

The council said it was “incredibly disappointing” to see a community asset subjected to “mindless and potentially dangerous behaviour”.

“The council will not tolerate this type of behaviour,” the statement said.

“Deliberate damage to community facilities is not simply antisocial behaviour; it is criminal damage and a criminal offence.”

Residents reacted with anger and frustration on social media, calling the alleged vandals “disgusting” and “empty-headed”.

Some residents also called for additional security measures, including fencing around the structure when it is not in use.

The bandstand already has CCTV, and an alarm system that was allegedly targeted with bricks in May.

There was also a false alarm in March when “young people” spotted on the bandstand were moved, leaving only “a few muddy footprints”.

Councillor Jamie Furlong, who represents Todmorden West, paid tribute to everyone who has helped bring the structure back into use.

“So much time and energy has been invested by volunteers and community groups to bring the bandstand back to life,” he said.

“That’s why this attack feels particularly upsetting.”

He said he hoped those responsible would be identified and held accountable.

Coun Oscar-Seville Leach also condemned the incident, describing the attempted arson as “utterly disgraceful”.

Having grown up in Todmorden when the bandstand had fallen into disrepair, he said it had been encouraging to see it restored and actively used again this year.

He welcomed confirmation that there had been no major damage and urged anyone with information to contact police.

Todmorden Town Council said: “We are extremely disappointed and concerned to have to address an attempt to set fire to the bandstand in Todmorden.

“We want to reassure the public that we are aware of the damage caused. Thankfully, the damage is minimal and is not expected to affect the use of the building or its ongoing use by the community.

“Let’s work together to look after the places that matter to our community.”

West Yorkshire Police has been approached for more details.
