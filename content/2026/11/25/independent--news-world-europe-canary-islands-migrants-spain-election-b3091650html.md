Spanish authorities confirmed that almost 2,000 migrants reached Spain’s Canary Islands on Tuesday, setting a record high for arrivals across the archipelago in a single day.

Situated off the West African coast, the islands experienced this surge in arrivals just before Spain holds a national election later this week, when migration will be a major concern for voters.

Spain’s maritime rescue service reported that 1,990 people landed aboard 15 separate boats as rescue vessels escorted one dinghy after another into the small island of El Hierro.

"Sadly, this is a historic day," the Canary Islands' regional leader Fernando Clavijo said in an interview with Spanish radio station Cadena Ser.

Around 100 minors were among Tuesday’s arrivals, including a four-day-old baby born during the ocean journey, Clavijo said.

![Fernando Clavijo said: ‘Sadly, this is a historic day’](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/25/16/2274228442..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The Canary Islands, a tourist hotspot for many Europeans drawn to year-round mild weather, have for years been a key entry point for migrants desperate to reach Europe. The route is extremely dangerous, with thousands dying in the attempted crossing.

Although the islands lie about 65 miles (105 kilometres) from the nearest African coast, many migrants take longer routes to evade security forces. Journeys can last days or weeks, with many boats missing their target and drifting into the open Atlantic.

More than 26,000 people have reached the Canary Islands this year, with half that total arriving in November. The previous single-day record occurred just two weeks earlier, when 1,400 people reached Gran Canaria on 7 November.

In June, Pope Leo warned people smugglers that they will face God’s wrath for exploiting the desperation of migrants when the pontiff visited the islands.

Departures from West Africa are seasonal, and November often sees spikes in numbers as many leave before ocean waters become rough during winter. Calm seas recently have contributed to the high number of departures.

Most boats arriving on Tuesday originated from Senegal and Gambia, Clavijo said. Despite European Union efforts in West Africa to reduce migrant numbers, many believe leaving is their only option to find work and build a better life.

"The sailing conditions were favorable and there was a full moon, so these people looking for a better future ended up on our shores," Clavijo said.

Clavijo stated that El Hierro, the westernmost member of the archipelago with a population of 12,000, could not handle such a large influx of people, many of whom arrived exhausted and in poor condition.

He said he was satisfied overall with the transfer of adult migrants to mainland Spain, but requested help from other regions to take in unaccompanied minors who need to be cared for by authorities.

![The record-breaking day comes ahead of the Spanish election](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/25/17/2306255292..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Migration promises to be a main issue for Spain's election.

Spain’s conservative Popular Party is aiming to unseat the ruling Socialist-led central government in Sunday’s elections. To do so, it would likely have to form a government with the far-right Vox party, which is campaigning heavily on expelling migrants and social policies that favour Spaniards over recent arrivals.

Without mentioning Vox, Clavijo, who belongs to the regional Canary Coalition party, denounced any attempts to demonise migrants, calling it "a xenophobic discourse of bad people."

The Canary Island leader also slammed the recent deal by Austria, Denmark, Germany, Greece and the Netherlands to send migrants to Rwanda, calling it "ridiculous."

"Europe is on the wrong path," Clavijo said.
