Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick pulled special agents ordinarily concerned with preventing the smuggling of AI chips to China to serve as part of his personal security detail, according to a report.

*The Atlantic* writes that early on in his tenure leading the Department of Commerce, Lutnick “alienated” bodyguards initially assigned to protect him, forcing him to look elsewhere for replacements.

His solution was to redeploy three to four agents at a time from the Bureau of Industry and Security, who are primarily responsible for enforcing export controls and preventing U.S. technology from falling into the wrong hands – precisely the issue at the heart of the Nvidia scandal that recently embroiled company CEO Jensen Huang.

Huang, 63, was forced to resign on November 12 after reports of extramarital affairs – one of which was allegedly with a woman U.S. intelligence believe to be a Chinese spy.

The BIS has approximately 200 special agents on its payroll, scattered across the United States in 10 field offices, who carry weapons and have powers of arrest but are not trained in protecting Cabinet members or VIPs.

![Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick has been accused of redeploying specialist export control agents to his security detail, for which they do not have the requisite training](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/25/16/2305184677..?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&auto=webp)

“They’re not trained for protective detail,” one current official told *The Atlantic*. “These are professional investigators.”

A former official told the publication it was “completely inappropriate” for them to be protecting the secretary or managing security arrangements for his travel and at his personal properties in New York, D.C. and Florida.

The secretary did ask the House Appropriations Subcommittee in April for additional funding with which to expand BIS’s enforcement personnel, but did not mention redeployment of its agents when making the request.

A Commerce official told *The Atlantic*the redeployment of agents was temporary and said in a statement that “at a time when the secretary was facing the highest level of credible threats in the department’s history, some agents were temporarily detailed to augment executive protection.”* The Independent* has reached out to the DOC and BIS for further comment.

![The Trump administration continues to face questions about Nvidia boss Jensen Huang’s reported affair with alleged Chinese spy Alice Huang (no relation)](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/17/15/download.-4..jpg?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&auto=webp)

President Donald Trump’s administration is currently facing intense scrutiny over the Huang case, particularly with regard to the Singapore tech company Megaspeed, which is under investigation by the DOC and Department of Justice.

Lutnick, specifically, is facing questions from lawmakers about his department’s failure to add the firm to a trade blacklist before this week.

Trump, who actively lobbied the company to restore Jensen Huang to his post, has brushed aside any broader concerns about the consequences of his infidelities, writing on Truth Social Monday: “The United States Government has no reason to believe that Jensen’s return to Nvidia poses any risk to National Security.

“The Attorney General of the United States has confirmed that the U.S. Government does not possess evidence that he has engaged in any unlawful conduct.”

The scandal comes after the DOC’s own Office of the Inspector General issued a report this summer concluding that the BIS, under Lutnick’s leadership, had not done enough to stop U.S. goods from reaching China, as well as Hong Kong, Belarus and Russia.
