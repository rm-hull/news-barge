Train travellers seeking the lowest fares may have noticed that the cheapest connection from A to B on the German railway can increasingly involve riding both Deutsche Bahn (DB) and Flixtrains in combination.

Flixtrain, the lime-green branded budget railway operator, offers travel connections that can be a fraction of the price of what the same journey would cost on DB's high-speed services.

Starting in December, passengers in Germany will be able to easily purchase tickets for Flixtrain connections directly on DB platforms including the Bahn.de website and the DB navigator app.

This comes as Flixtrain is expanding its offering in Germany, and represents a significant step toward making budget train travel more convenient for travellers.

Until now, passengers searching for train tickets via DB platforms could view journeys which included a combination of Flixtrain and DB services.

These combination journeys looked markedly cheaper, but the catch was that the DB ticket did not include the price of the Flixtrain ticket which needed to be purchased separately.

This created an additional step, and sometimes a fair amount of confusion, for travellers in the purchase process. It also meant that passengers' rights were limited to the individual tickets – so a delay on one train wouldn't necessarily allow passengers to freely ride alternative trains on their next leg of the journey.

In enabling the sale of Flixtrain tickets directly on its platforms, DB is eliminating this issue for combination tickets and also making it much easier for passengers to select and purchase budget railway connections.

Cooperation between the two companies is to remain limited in other respects. Both companies continue to set prices, schedules, and offers independently of each other.

**Competition intensifies in German train travel**

Flix is currently Deutsche Bahn's largest competitor in long-distance transport.

Still, Germany's publicly-owned railway operator continues to dominate the market with a share of around 95 percent.

DB has long maintained a near monopoly on long-distance train transport in Germany, and some suggest that the lack of competition has led to a reduction in the quality of services provided.

Flixtrain plans to roughly double its existing fleet by 2028, which currently consists of 16 trains.

Starting in 2028, the Italian train company Italo also plans to enter the German long-distance transport market.
