Harrogate and Knaresborough MP Tom Gordon has called on the Prime Minister to stop energy bills rising in January.

Ofgem has announced a 26% rise in the energy price cap for the three months from 1st January 2027.

At the new rates, a household using a typical amount of gas and electricity would pay the equivalent of £2,166 a year, up £443.

Gordon has backed Liberal Democrat leader Ed Davey’s call for the government to use higher oil and gas tax receipts and windfall profits from energy network companies to bring bills down.

Tom Gordon said:

> “Families across North Yorkshire are being left to fend for themselves while bills spiral out of control.
>
> “The Prime Minister must listen to Liberal Democrat calls and stop this hike going ahead in January.
>
> "The money is there, and it should go back to the families who are paying for this crisis, not sit in Treasury coffers or shareholders' pockets.”

According to House of Commons Library figures, 14.7% of households in Yorkshire and the Humber are in fuel poverty.

The government has announced a £100 payment for households in council tax bands A to D.

The Resolution Foundation estimates that one in eight of the poorest households will miss out.

The Liberal Democrats have proposed an Essential Energy Guarantee, which would give every household a discounted rate on energy equivalent to half a typical household’s use.

The party says the scheme would save the average family £100 a year, rising to £140 for the least well-off households.

Families would receive an extra allowance for each child, and people with disabilities would pay the discounted rate on all the energy they use.
