Doubts remain over whether the Ministry of Defence can fully execute its Defence Investment Plan (Dip), the government’s spending watchdog has cautioned.

A National Audit Office (NAO) report published on Wednesday concluded that the MoD has "limited assurance" of delivering the strategy.

It highlighted that projected expenditure is set to outpace available funding, leaving an uncertain roadmap toward hitting the target of allocating 3.5% of GDP to defence by 2035.

Although defence officials consider the initial four-year period financially manageable, the NAO pointed out that Middle East hostilities have already added £1 billion to expenses for 2026/27, with potential knock-on effects for subsequent years.

Furthermore, the watchdog revealed that the department's procurement division is currently running "unsustainably", warning of a potential "bow wave" of unfulfilled programmes beyond 2030 that will compound budgetary strains.

Questions were also raised regarding the MoD's capacity to achieve £10.7 billion in intended "efficiency savings" over the coming four years, noting a distinct absence of "mature plans" to realise them.

Gareth Davies, the head of the NAO, said: “Much remains to be done to ensure the Defence Investment Plan can be delivered as intended.”

The MoD also lacks a detailed plan for the final six years of the Dip “in part because it is uncertain about its future funding trajectory”, the NAO said.

The report added: “The Government has committed to Nato that it will spend 3.5% of GDP on defence by 2035, but the MoD does not know how and when its budget will increase.

“This trajectory is important, as the MoD needs to ensure that it and industry are prepared.”

The long-delayed Dip, setting out how the UK would spend its defence budget, was finally published in July this year after a row that saw then-defence secretary John Healey resign, accusing Sir Keir Starmer of failing to fund the plan properly.

In Wednesday’s report, the NAO said the delay in publishing the Dip, which had been due to appear in late 2025, was due to the MoD “significantly” underestimating its costs.

That underestimate left a £28.8 billion gap that required decisions from the top of government to resolve.

Sir Geoffrey Clifton-Brown, Conservative MP and chairman of the Commons Public Accounts Committee, criticised the MoD, saying the gap was “no small funding error”.

He said: “This was a serious failure to accurately estimate costs, which ultimately had to be resolved at the highest level of government.

“The MoD must now get a grip on its cost data to produce robust, realistic forecasts for the future.”

In an effort to close that gap, the MoD “significantly” reduced spending on training, which the NAO said the department acknowledged would create “capability gaps or readiness reductions”.

A spokesperson for the Ministry of Defence said: “The Defence Investment Plan will transform our armed forces and step up our warfighting readiness, investing nearly £300 billion into defence.

“That investment must come with modernisation, to get the best possible value for taxpayers’ money, and to provide our armed forces with cutting-edge kit.

“That’s what we’re now delivering. In just four months since the Dip, we have signed over 400 contracts worth over £4 billion, so Britain can meet the threats we face and keep our people safe.”
