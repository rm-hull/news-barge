# Our new radio has let the sunshine in on brighter side to Britain: Sarah Todd

## News has been this reporter’s life for over 35 years. Despite a move over from chasing ambulance type hard news reporting to a slower pace, specialising in farming and country life, following the world’s happenings has remained a constant.

But recently, dear reader, an admission must be made. Self-diagnosis has been undertaken and your correspondent can reveal she is suffering from something called ‘bad news fatigue.’

There just doesn’t seem to be a day goes by when there isn’t a headline, such as Burnham riding roughshod over the European Union referendum result, that doesn’t make this writer angry. It is exhausting being constantly cranky, banging on about bonkers Britain.

It was all the current talk of preparedness that has inadvertently let a little sunshine in.

We had candles and plenty of tins of food in the cupboard but - unable to completely shake off that pretty much lifelong addiction to keeping up with the news - our survival arsenal was missing an old-fashioned radio.

While The Husband would have bought something cheap and boring to look at, his wife hunted down one made by Steepletone, which is incidentally a family-run UK brand. She is retro in style, bright pink in colour (hence the pronoun) and looks like she’s come straight out of the 1950s.

Our grown-up daughter couldn’t resist fiddling in wonder with the huge circular dial and (trying really hard to be less angry) no comment was made when her music - not the reassuring voices of the inhabitants of Ambridge - came on when the switch was turned to on just after seven o’clock in the evening. She is currently plugged in, but - when we buy some – will have back-up batteries at the ready.

Reversing back up the road to Ambridge, the following of the long-running Radio 4 series The Archers, has become much more regular since this Barbie-coloured creation took up residence in the kitchen, next to the bread bin to be precise. Previously, the radio was only sporadically listened to via the television or this laptop that your weekly missive is typed out upon.

It’s hard to explain, but there is something so reassuringly easy to get into a routine about the procedure involved in going from first crackles to, after a little fiddling with the tuner, the most wonderful tone.

This new appliance has brought back many memories about how important local radio, for her BBC Radio York, was to a late grandmother who, incidentally, lived to reach her centenary. It brought the outside world into her home and when she could no longer attend her local church, she spoke of Harry (the broadcasting legend that was the late Harry Gration) often opening fetes and other events there. She knew all about this from the station.

As her eyesight failed, the magnifying glass on the side a testament to this, the radio really was her window to the world.

The other grandmother was an avid newspaper reader and she remained mustard at spotting snippets from even the smallest column inches that might be of interest. Part of the fun of going through a bag of passed on publications was wondering what it was she wanted you to notice. Sometimes they were cut out or circled.

Getting a local evening paper, along with a national one and a magazine - something like a Women’s Weekly or a Hello - was just the same sort of lifeline as the radio mentioned above.

To try and finish on an upbeat note, it was a joy last week - at a reception held by the Yorkshire Agricultural Society - to meet some super enthusiastic young reporters who had all hailed from a local newspaper in Harrogate. One was, to my absolute and utter delight, wearing a mac.

This old hack regaled her with feeling as proud as punch getting her first reporter’s mac at the age of 18. A legendary newshound promptly put a packet of cigarettes (a matter of huge shame and worry to this young farmer’s daughter who wouldn’t dream of ever smoking) in the inside pocket. “For when you’re standing around outside court to offer around and help you get chatting.” He also told her to always carry a pencil inside another pocket, “For if it rains.”

After a significant sartorial gap, when camel coats and waxed jackets seemed to come to the fore, a new mac was a brilliant birthday present a few weeks ago. Wearing it as part of an effort to get to the races more often had been the initial aim but maybe, just maybe, it might serve as a belted beacon of the importance of good old-fashioned journalism, especially at a local level.
