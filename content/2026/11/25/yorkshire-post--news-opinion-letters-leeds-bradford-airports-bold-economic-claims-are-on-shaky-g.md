# Leeds Bradford Airport's bold economic claims are on shaky ground: Yorkshire Post Letters

## Nick Hodgkinson, Chair of Group for Action of Leeds Bradford Airport (GALBA).

Leeds Bradford Airport ’s latest public relations blitz made all sorts of claims about how the airport impacts the local economy (The Yorkshire Post, November 3). But if you look beyond the headlines and ask some simple questions, LBA’s claims are on shaky ground.

Who wrote the report? It looks very much like the airport has marked its own homework. What methodology was used and has there been any independent expert scrutiny of the airport’s claims?

Why is there no mention at all of the economic downsides of LBA to our region?

![Leeds Bradford Airport has recently unveiled a new range of routes.](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOjY2MGFlY2ViLTliNWUtNGRjOC04YWMwLTg0ODYwZjU4YWM4MTo3ZmRlMDQ0Yi1lYWM4LTQ4ODEtODIwZS0xZGU0YzEyY2RmZTM=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


As well as costs to the NHS, caused by noise related illnesses and air pollution, one of the biggest economic costs of the airport is that it flies massively more local people abroad on holiday than it brings in tourists to our region.

That means that massively more spending money is exported than imported.

No one is saying people should not go on holiday abroad but let’s be honest about the impact of LBA’s ‘bucket & spade’ business model.

Why is there no mention of the costs from the damage done by extreme weather (floods, wildfires, crop failure) that result from the greenhouse gas emissions released by planes using LBA? The report only talks about emissions from its passenger terminal and vehicles using the airport.

It makes no mention of the emissions from LBA’s planes but those emissions make up around 95 per cent of all greenhouse gases at LBA.

Have LBA’s bosses forgotten about the droughts and wildfires this summer?

Why does the report use inconsistent definitions of income and salary when talking about the level of wages paid at LBA? Could it be that a fair comparison would present quite a different picture?

Why doesn’t the report make clear the differences between the alleged existing economic impact and the alleged future economic impact? LBA's current planning application does not give any figures at all for the alleged future economic benefit of abolishing the night flight rules.

I could go on but you get the picture. Although this report is not formally part of the current planning application, it’s obviously intended to affect that decision.

Let’s be clear: this report was not an honest and independent assessment, it was hype. Don’t believe the hype.
