A suspended Reform UK councillor has indicated he will deny multiple rape and sexual assault counts during his first appearance in court.

A lawyer for George Madgwick, who was the leader of Nigel Farage’s party on Hampshire County Council and also a councillor on Portsmouth City Council, indicated he would plead not guilty during an appearance at Portsmouth magistrates court.

The 37-year-old has been charged with 35 offences, including 12 counts of rape, two counts of sexual assault, two counts of assault by penetration, two counts of making and one count of distributing indecent images of a child.

Mr Madgwick, of Portsmouth, has also been charged with one count of causing or inciting sexual exploitation of a child, eight counts of assault occasioning actual bodily harm, two counts of controlling or coercive behaviour, one count of stalking, one count of making threats to kill, one count of false imprisonment, one count of theft and one count of criminal damage.

Dressed in a blue suit and blue tie, Mr Madgwick only spoke to confirm his name, date of birth and address.

Sarah Vine, defending, indicated on behalf of Mr Madgwick that he would be entering not guilty pleas to the charges.

District Judge David Robinson sent the case to Portsmouth Crown Court for the next hearing on January 4 and released Mr Madgwick on conditional bail until then.

The allegations all relate to four victims, who were aged between 16 and 17 at the time, Hampshire and Isle of Wight Constabulary said.

It is alleged these offences took place between 2008 and 2021 in Portsmouth and Wales, the force added.

An investigation was launched on 24 July 2025, after officers receiving a report of a sexual offence, police said, with Mr Madgwick arrested in January of this year at an address in Wandsworth, London.

A Reform UK spokesperson said he had been suspended from the party “with immediate effect”.

Mr Madgwick later posted on X: “I am shocked and dismayed at the charges brought against me. Whilst the charges are extremely serious, I will say unequivocally that I am innocent of the allegations against me. Due process must take its course and the legal process must be followed without hindrance.

“I considered it responsible to resign as Reform UK leader in Hampshire Council and I will continue as an independent until I’m cleared. I will not make any further statement now, or in the future, on this in any capacity. I will deactivate my social media shortly until due process has concluded.”
