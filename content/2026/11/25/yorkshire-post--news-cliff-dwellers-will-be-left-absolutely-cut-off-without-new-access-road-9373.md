# Cliff dwellers will be left 'absolutely cut off' without new access road

## North Yorkshire Council has been given a share of £11.2 million government funding to look for solutions to problems caused by coastal erosion, including a new road to a community that could be left “absolutely” cut off.

England has some of the fastest-eroding coastlines in Europe, and the Environment Agency’s latest assessment shows that, by 2105, around 20,000 homes and businesses could be at risk.

Among the areas under threat in North Yorkshire are Filey Flat Cliffs, due to ongoing coastal erosion and landslides. It has just one road in running through Primrose Valley holiday village and its loss would isolate 45 properties and force residents to relocate.

Around 60 per cent of the homes are permanently lived in, with the rest let as holiday homes.

![Filey Flat Cliffs. Photo courtesy of Anttoni Numminen/LDRS](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOmQwM2IxNTM1LWFlN2UtNGZhNS1hNjE0LWIwMWZjYWZjMzNmZTo4YmFkYWFmMy02MTlkLTQ5NDYtOTMxMy02NTMwYzYyZDM1OWQ=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


Some people claim the private road only has five years left.

Concerns have been expressed that if the road goes, there will be no access to the sewerage works, leaving locals having to install their own septic tanks.

Independent councillor Sam Cross who represents Filey expects some of the £970,000 given to NYC, will go on a feasibility study for a new road south of the current road. “We’d like to do the job properly and by properly, if it doesn’t go on in 25 years it will go in 75.

"What we’d like to do is put a separate access down into Flat Cliffs between Primrose Valley and the Bay.

![Filey Flat Cliffs. Red Line Indicates Potential State Of Erosion By 2055](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOmE3Mjg5OGQ2LWFiMDMtNDEzMi1iM2ViLWY2YTliZDkzYTE3MDo2ODVmZTU2Ni05MDkxLTQ5Y2QtOGRmZC0xMjYxMTBlMDE4Zjc=.png?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


"There’s different opinions about when the (current) road will go – it may be five or 25 (years), but there’s no guarantee regarding the cliffs when it will actually go. We must protect residents down there. They will be absolutely cut off.

"Yorkshire Water has promised for many years to put a pumping station further up and that’s what we would like them to do. Not just sort the road out, move the sewerage station.” Funding will also be spent on drone surveys which will help spot cliff movement earlier, so planning and emergency decisions can be based on better evidence.

Meanwhile at Cayton, a modular route will be tested in case coastal access is lost.

Each coastal area has its own shoreline management plan. In North Yorkshire, towns, villages and key infrastructure are generally defended by sea defences, but many rural stretches are deemed "no active intervention," meaning natural erosion is allowed to continue.

Floods Minister Emma Hardy said: "The North Yorkshire coast is a beautiful coastline, cherished by those who call it home and by visitors who are welcomed each summer. Coastal erosion can mean uncertainty about the future of your home, your business or the walking route you've taken all your life, and as a Yorkshire resident and MP, I understand how upsetting that is.

“We will give communities the time and support they need to adapt, so that they aren’t left to face this alone."

In January East Riding Council was awarded funding from a £18 million coastal adaptation pot it shares with Norfolk and Suffolk. The Holderness Coast is considered one of Europe's fastest receding coastlines.
