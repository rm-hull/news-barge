Mexico will issue a diplomatic note protesting the United States’ refusal to extradite a former television host wanted on allegations of tax fraud, money laundering, and organized crime, Mexican President Claudia Sheinbaum said Wednesday.

Inés Gómez Mont was held at an immigration detention center in Florida from Oct. 30 until U.S. authorities deported her to Spain this week.

"Why wasn’t she extradited to Mexico?" Sheinbaum asked at her daily morning news conference, highlighting that Mexico has sent more than 90 wanted individuals to the United States since 2025.

"We ask for reciprocity," she said.

Gómez Mont gained widespread attention during Super Bowl XLII media day in 2008 by wearing a wedding dress and proposing to New England Patriots quarterback Tom Brady.

She spent years hosting television programs in Mexico before relocating to the United States.

Mexico’s Attorney General’s Office stated she is sought on charges involving organized crime, money laundering, and tax fraud.

Alongside her husband, Víctor Manuel Álvarez Puga, she is accused of receiving illicit proceeds after diverting public money through numerous bank accounts.

Sheinbaum also criticized Interpol for rescinding a red notice published at Mexico’s request for Gómez Mont’s arrest.

Confirming that U.S. officials deported Gómez Mont to Spain because she entered the United States on a Spanish passport, Sheinbaum noted Mexico has already asked Spain to extradite her.

Despite irritation over pending extradition petitions for former officials and corporate figures, Sheinbaum’s administration has collaborated closely with Washington to turn over suspects.

Between February 2025 and January this year, Mexico dispatched over 90 suspected drug kingpins wanted in American courts.
