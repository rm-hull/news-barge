# Andy Burnham government breaks silence after 26 per cent energy price cap rise

## Ofgem confirmed the January 1 energy price cap rise on Wednesday, November 25

Andy Burnham's government has responded following confirmation that energy bills will surge by 26 per cent come January. Ofgem announced the January 1 price cap on Wednesday, November 25, in the run-up to Christmas.

The Labour Party administration has now addressed the increase, which amounts to £443. Energy Secretary Miatta Fahnbulleh acknowledged the government recognised people were "deeply concerned" by another rise, reports Birmingham Live.

Ms Fahnbulleh cautioned that "for too long" households had been "paying the price for conflicts overseas and volatile fossil fuel markets". She added: "We're stepping in to give families some breathing space, so from January, the majority of households will get £100 towards their energy costs."

She continued: "But there is more to do to take the pressure off families and bring bills down for good. That means more cheap, clean, homegrown power so we're less reliant on expensive fossil fuels, and reforming our broken energy system so it's fairer for British people."

Neil Kenward, Ofgem's director general for markets, acknowledged he understood the price rises were causing anxiety for many households as the colder months arrive. He continued: "We urge anyone who is struggling to afford their energy bills to reach out to their supplier, who must offer appropriate support and advice - we are writing to suppliers today to remind them of their obligations and ensure they are ready to provide that support."

Sky News business and economics correspondent Paul Kelso has suggested that even following this "steep increase" in energy costs, there is little prospect of relief in the near term.

"This figure, this price cap, is calculated over a three-month period that finished a week ago, and that period it is, what should we call it? Murphy's law, I think, is the safe way of describing it," he said. "That increase in gas prices, which set our electricity prices, has coincided almost precisely with that. And it's why we've got higher prices now, looking to be higher into the next."
