## York facing 'travel chaos' as bus drivers prepare to strike on busiest weekend

York is facing "traffic" chaos, Unite the Union has said as 200 bus drivers go on strike weeks before Christmas *(Image: First Bus)*

YORK is facing “travel chaos" as bus drivers prepare for a two-day strike over one of the busiest weekends in the run-up to Christmas.

Two-hundred York First Bus drivers will walk out on Saturday (November 28) and Sunday (November 29), with Unite the Union, warning disruption could intensify during the Christmas period unless a deal is reached.

The industrial action will come after retailers launched Black Friday deals - and will coincide with one of the busiest weekends for York Christmas Market and the start of the the UK Snooker Championships at York Barbican.

Unite said bus services across the city, including vital Park and Ride routes, will be “severely impacted” by the industrial action, which comes as drivers demand pay parity with Yorkshire Coastliner colleagues.




Executive director of York BID Andrew Lowson said: "Anything that limits transit into York during its busiest time of year will not be welcomed by city businesses.

"Retail and hospitality need footfall and office workers are coming together for Christmas parties.

"We are hearing anecdotally that the Tuesday closures of the Christmas market has already hit business takings, so we hope this transport disruption does not have a further negative effect."

![City of York Council’s Executive Member for Transport, Cllr Kate Ravilious, said the council is urging First and Unite to “continue negotiating” so that “every effort” will have been made to reach a settlement and avert the strike](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/19384696.jpg?type=mds-article-620)City of York Council’s Executive Member for Transport, Cllr Kate Ravilious, said the council is urging First and Unite to “continue negotiating” so that “every effort” will have been made to reach a settlement and avert the strike *(Image: Dylan Connell)*

City of York Council’s executive member for transport, Cllr Kate Ravilious, said the council is urging First and Unite to “continue negotiating” so that “every effort” will have been made to reach a settlement and avert the strike.

She added: “We want to see the city’s buses operating and giving residents and visitors the option to travel sustainably, especially as the city gets more congested in the run up to Christmas.

“If it goes ahead, we’ll be helping to signpost people to alternative means of travel including trains, other bus services that continue to operate in and out of York and cycling and walking where this is an option.”

Unite the Union confirmed on Tuesday (November 24) that the weekend strike would go ahead and said the city would face "travel chaos".

The walkout will coincide with the start of the Leasing Options UK Snooker Championship, one of the sport's biggest events, as well as the Christmas shopping rush.

The tournament, at York Barbican from November 28 to December 6, will feature seven-time world champion Ronnie O'Sullivan and four-time champion Mark Selby this weekend.

![The strike coincides with the UK Snooker Championships, held at York Barbican this weekend, featuring qualifiers involving seven-time world champion Ronnie O'Sullivan](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21756232.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The strike coincides with the UK Snooker Championships, held at York Barbican this weekend, featuring qualifiers involving seven-time world champion Ronnie O'Sullivan *(Image: Supplied)*

A representative for the venue said: “We are aware of the First Bus driver strike taking place on Saturday 28 and Sunday 29 November.

“As services including the Park and Ride, will be limited, we are sending communications to our attendees advising them to plan their journey in advance and allow plenty of time to get to the venue and through security checks. Latecomers will be admitted at a suitable break.”

Meanwhile, York Designer Outlet – a major stop on the city’s Park & Ride service – said it had introduced temporary staff parking to maximise spaces available for customers to help ease potential congestion.

A spokesperson said: “We hope any disruption caused by the First Bus strike will be minimal.

“York Designer Outlet will remain open as normal, with extended opening hours across the weekend. On Saturday, we will be opening from 8am to 10pm, and Sunday from 9.30am to 6.30pm.

“We will continue to monitor the situation and keep customers updated via our website.”

![Unite said bus services across the city, including vital Park and Ride routes, will be “severely impacted” by the industrial action this weekend](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21634499.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Unite said bus services across the city, including vital Park and Ride routes, will be “severely impacted” by the industrial action this weekend *(Image: First Bus)*

The planned strike comes after Unite the Union claimed First Bus drivers in York are paid £1.32 less than Yorkshire Coastliner drivers, at £15.58 an hour.

First Bus said that its latest pay offers had been rejected by Unite members and said it was "very disappointed and saddened" by the decision to strike.

Megan Hope-Rushforth, service delivery director for First Bus, said: "Industrial action will be damaging to everyone and cause severe disruption to the daily lives of our customers who rely on the bus to get to work, education and for appointments as well as the thousands of people that want to enjoy the city in the lead up to Christmas."

First Bus said it had submitted two pay offers to Unite members.

The first, a representative for the bus company said, equated to a 4.5 per cent increase over 12 months, while the second equated to an 8.3 per cent increase over 24 months.

Both offers were backdated to April 2026.

## 'We fully recognise the strength of feeling of colleagues in York'

Ms Hope-Rushforth said: "We have met repeatedly with Unite representatives over the past eight months and made numerous offers in our attempts to find an agreement.

"We believe this reflects the vital role our drivers play while ensuring we can continue to provide reliable services for the thousands of people in York who depend on us every day."

She added: "We fully recognise the strength of feeling of colleagues in York and remain committed to working constructively with Union representatives to reach a fair and sustainable agreement.

"Our door remains open to continue discussions and avoid unnecessary industrial action."

Unite said the dispute comes after FirstGroup reported a seven per cent rise in operating profits to £110 million in the six months to September.

Unite general secretary Sharon Graham said: "First's profits continue to rise yet its hard-working York drivers are expected to put up with second-class wages.

"First can afford to put forward a reasonable offer but is choosing not to out of greed."

Unite regional officer Darren Rushworth added: "First will be entirely responsible for the disruption caused to York residents, visitors and businesses during the Christmas period.

"There is still time to avoid industrial action but that will require First tabling a deal our members can accept."
