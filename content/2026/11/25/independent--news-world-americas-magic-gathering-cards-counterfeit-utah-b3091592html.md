The makers of the *Magic: The Gathering*card game are suing a Utah family for over $28 million over an alleged counterfeit scheme.*​Magic: The Gathering* is a hugely popular trading card game, grossing $1.72 billion last year with a huge resale market for secondhand and hard-to-find cards.

However, Wizards of the Coast - the brand owner of *Magic: The Gathering -* claims in a lawsuit that a family-run provider of proxy cards - PrintMTG Enterprise - has been counterfeiting official products.

​Proxies are essentially stand-ins for official cards that are often used in casual games but are not permitted in competitions.

Wizards of the Coast alleges, though, in the November 23 lawsuit, that PrintMTG Enterprise’s activities go beyond the production and sale of proxies.

![The makers of Magic: The Gathering are suing a Utah business for over $28 million, accusing the firm of running a counterfeit scheme](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/25/16/55/Magic-The-Gathering-Card-Game.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

​“Defendants call their MTG cards ‘proxies’ to lend them a veneer of legitimacy while actually plying a business built around the sale of counterfeit cards,” the lawsuit reads.

​PrintMTG Enterprise is also accused of operating a “complex and sophisticated network of individuals and shell corporations engaged in trademark infringement, counterfeiting, and criminal copyright infringement on a grand scale.”

​Utah-based Ryan James Dunn is named in the filing as the founder of PrintMTG Enterprise, with his wife, Melissa Jayne Dunn, being named as a member of a brand linked to the company. Several other members of Dunn’s family - including his brother and parents - are also named as being linked to the firm.

​Wizards of the Coast alleges that thousands of *Magic: The Gathering* cards were “wilfully infringed” and claims that it is entitled to $28,650,000 in statutory damages.

​“These fakes are sold for profit, and our stance on them is clear: We will protect the *Magic* community from counterfeiters,” the company said in a statement given to KIRO 7. “Counterfeit cards hurt our players, collectors, and Wizards Play Network stores, and we will not stand by while this happens.”

![Cards from Magic: The Gathering are highly collectable. This card belongs to a Star Trek collection](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/25/16/07/Screenshot-2026-11-25-at-16-28-05.png?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&trim=60%2C0%2C2%2C6&auto=webp)

​The firm claims in its lawsuit that PrintMTG Enterprise’s activities amount to violations of the Lanham Act, the Digital Millennium Copyright Act, the Washington Consumer Protection Act and the Racketeer Influenced and Corrupt Organizations Act.

​Speaking to KIRO 7, card expert Otto Steckler explained that buying and selling authentic *Magic: The Gathering* cards is big business.

​“These Magic cards can be worth a tremendous, tremendous amount of money,” he said. “Some of the most iconic cards in the game, like the ‘Black Lotus,’ sell for hundreds of thousands of dollars for a raw version, or even millions of dollars for a graded version.”

*The Independent* has contacted PrintMTG Enterprise for comment.
