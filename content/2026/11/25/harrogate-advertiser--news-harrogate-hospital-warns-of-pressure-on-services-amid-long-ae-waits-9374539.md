Harrogate District Hospital is facing increased pressure on its services, with seasonal illnesses contributing to longer waits in A&E and delays to patients being admitted.

Resident Steve Riding contacted the *Harrogate Advertiser* on Monday after spending seven hours in the hospital’s Emergency Department.

He said the previous day had been even worse and that a doctor had told him he would not be kept in hospital because it was full.

He said: “I am at A&E. Seven hours so far and yesterday was worse.

“I was told by a doctor they wouldn't be keeping me in because the hospital was full and they didn't have any beds.”

Harrogate and District NHS Foundation Trust said it was experiencing increased demand because of seasonal illnesses, including flu, respiratory infections and norovirus.

A spokesperson said: “We are currently experiencing increased demand on our services as a result of seasonal illnesses, including flu, respiratory infections and norovirus, which is putting pressure on healthcare services across the region.

“While this has led to longer waits for some patients in our Emergency Department and delays in patients leaving hospital, which can delay admitting patients, our teams are working hard to ensure everyone receives safe, effective care, with those who need urgent treatment assessed and treated as quickly as possible.”

The trust thanked staff for their commitment and patients and families for their patience during the busy period.

It advised people seeking urgent medical advice to contact NHS 111 online or by phone before attending A&E, unless they have a life-threatening illness or serious injury.

Anyone with a life-threatening condition should continue to call 999 or attend A&E immediately, the trust added, saying the most seriously ill and injured patients would always be prioritised.
