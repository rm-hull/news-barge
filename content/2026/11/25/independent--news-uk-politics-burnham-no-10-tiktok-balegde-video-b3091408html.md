Andy Burnham has refused to apologise for a controversial video by a TikTok star that has created a political storm, even as he condemned it as “wrong”.

The PM said “I won’t apologise for engaging with young people” when challenged over TikToker Max Balegde’s “bomboclaat p\*\*\*\* wagon” video, which was filmed outside the door to No 10.

He did, however, add that “it was wrong” and “inappropriate”.

![Prime minister Andy Burnham said he ‘won’t apologise for engaging with young people’](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/25/12/cf0e06d11b12ce83240452bc24ac871aY29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzk1Njk2MDYz-2.86601919.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

In the now-deleted post, Mr Balegde lip-synced to a song with the lyrics “bomboclaat p\*\*\*\* wagon chicken nuggets” outside the famous black door to the prime minister’s official residence.

Bomboclaat is a Jamaican profanity referring to a menstrual cloth, while p\*\*\*\* is a slang term for female genitals.

Mr Balegde later insisted it was not intended to be offensive.

He said: “My content is always meant to be silly, fun, and light-hearted. If anybody was offended by that video, I sincerely apologise.”

But he added: “Nobody was offended for weeks until revolting right-wing people tried to put this disgusting narrative on it.

“And if anybody then looks at the video and is offended, I get that. So hence why I took it down because I think that is disgusting.”

![Max Balegde filmed himself mouthing ‘bomboclaat pwagon chicken nuggets’ outside No 10](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/24/12/34/Max-Balegde.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Despite the uproar, Downing Street has failed to rule out that Mr Balegde would be invited to another event in No 10 and also declined to say whether it was aware of the video before it was posted online.

Asked whether the influencer would be invited back, the prime minister’s official spokesperson said: “I’m not going to get ahead of future events.”

The TikTok content creator said he has been invited for tea by Mr Burnham.

Asked whether this was true, the spokesman repeated that he would not get ahead of “future receptions”.

A separate post on YouTube, filmed inside No 10, showed Mr Balegde saying there was “wine on tap” but he had been “shouted at for having a glass of wine downstairs”.

He gave a speech at the event in No 10 on 9 November, which was attended by the PM.

Part of the footage, showing him posing inside No 10, was captioned: “They hate when you serve c\*\*\* at 10 Downing Street X.”

At Prime Minister’s Questions, the Conservative leader Kemi Badenoch hit out at “disgusting and pejorative comments about women’s private parts, obscene language that referred to white women as cheap meat. This is grotesque”.

She added: “Labour MPs constantly tell us about their commitments to fighting violence against women and girls. Those MPs were silent yesterday. I won’t be. So will the prime minister now apologise for debasing his office and for allowing that vile video to be recorded on the steps of No 10?”

The Labour leader replied: “An inappropriate video was taken down, and the creator has rightly apologised, and she was right to call out that because it was not acceptable.

“But I won’t apologise for engaging … with people that young people themselves are looking to for their information, and I think this House – not this individual – I’m talking about the wider point.

“She sneers at TikTokers. She sneers at influencers. Young people are looking to these people for their information, and I won’t apologise for doing that. I’ve already said it was wrong what was said, and it was taken down.”

Earlier, the women and equalities minister defended Mr Burnham’s engagement with online influencers following the controversy.

Bridget Phillipson said the video was “totally unacceptable” and she was pleased it had been removed.

But she said the PM, who hosted a reception for content creators earlier this month, was right to try to “reach people where they are” by engaging with influencers.
