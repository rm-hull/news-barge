Donald Trump said on Tuesday he would be open to pardoning Jensen Huang, the recently reinstalled Nvidia CEO, for potential crimes but didn’t think it would be necessary.

“It’s always an option, I guess,” the US president told the New York Post in an interview. “But I don’t think it’ll be necessary.”

Trump’s comments came after he intervened in Nvidia’s leadership drama and put pressure on the tech company’s board to reinstall Huang as the CEO despite other lawmakers’ concerns over whether Huang posed a national security risk for having an affair with an alleged Chinese spy.

Huang is expected to testify to congressional committees in the coming weeks about Nvidia’s chip exports, Huang’s alleged affair and the US government’s role in having Nvidia reinstate Huang as CEO.

“I think I want [Huang] to just tell the truth, and I mean, his lawyers are going to have to advise him, but I’ve always found him to be 100% aboveboard, and you know, a very smart person,” Trump told the New York Post.

Huang has not been formally accused of any crime, and Todd Blanche, the US attorney general, told reporters on Tuesday that no evidence indicated Huang committed a crime.

“To the extent that … the Department of Justice had information that he was committing crimes or something like this, that is, that’s just not, that’s something that I would have certainly told the president had it been true,” Blanche said.

At the same time, Blanche did not confirm whether the justice department had conducted a thorough investigation into Huang, instead saying law enforcement agents “looked into” the situation.

Yet days earlier, Trump told CNBC that his administration had done “a pretty good investigation” to exonerate Huang.

But lawmakers from both sides of the aisle have questions about the US government’s determination

John Curtis, a Republican who sits on the Senate commerce committee, wrote on X: “Nvidia’s board says it relied on input from senior U.S. officials to conclude that bringing Jensen Huang back posed no national security risk. I have some questions. Who in our government weighed in? What did they tell the board? And on what basis did the board conclude there was no risk?”

Pete Ricketts, a Republican who sits on the Senate foreign relations committee, wrote on X on Monday, “Nvidia’s decision today doesn’t excuse Jensen Huang from testifying before Congress. It also doesn’t absolve Nvidia of answering for national security concerns and unaccounted-for chips.”

John Kennedy, a Republican on the Senate banking committee, told Fox News that he wanted to know more about Huang’s alleged affair for national security purposes.

“I got no interest; it’s not my place if Mr Huang wants to be a feral, if he wants to cheat on Mrs Huang. It is my interest, though, if he was having sex with a Chinese spy,” Kennedy said.

“The Trump Administration needs to explain what ‘input’ was given and by which senior officials to support Huang’s reinstatement,” Elizabeth Warren, top Democrat on the Senate banking committee, wrote on X on Monday.

Elissa Slotkin, a Democrat on the Senate homeland security committee, raised concerns about whether Trump “knew about the potential compromise with the alleged Chinese spy influencing Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang” and “did nothing about it”.
