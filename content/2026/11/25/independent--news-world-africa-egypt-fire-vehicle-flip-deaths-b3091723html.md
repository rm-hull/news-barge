At least 30 people have died and 15 were injured after a vehicle carrying petroleum products overturned and caught fire on a major ring road in Egypt, the health ministry said on Wednesday.

The road connects major highways across Greater Cairo, spanning the Cairo, Giza and Qalyubia governorates.

The fire broke out above a tunnel in Giza's Al-Warraq district, the ministry stated. Local media reported that firefighters brought the blaze under control.

Road crashes are common in Egypt due to speeding, weak enforcement of traffic regulations and poor road conditions.

![People were killed in a train crash in Egypt earlier this month](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/08/15/download.-6..jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

In recent years, Egypt expanded its network with new bridges and highways through an infrastructure programme targeting congestion, rapid population growth and urban expansion. Although traffic flow has improved in places, safety concerns persist.

Earlier this month, at least seven people were killed and more than 100 injured after two trains crashed into each other in Egypt.

Several carriages derailed in Giza province after one train rammed into the back of another, according to the Transportation Ministry.

One of the train drivers was among those killed in the collision, the health ministry said.

The crash occurred in al-Ayyat district, about 52 kilometers (32 miles) south of Cairo, between a train heading for the southwestern Fayoum province and another heading to the southern province of Aswan.

In the aftermath of the crash on 8 November, President Abdul Fattah al-Sisi said: “I am following the aftermath of the train collision with great concern and extend my deepest condolences to the families of those who lost their lives, along with my sincere wishes for a speedy recovery for the injured.”
