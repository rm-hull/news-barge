# 'We've pushed the boundaries, everything has a playfulness to it': Lotherton unveils new 12 Days of Christmas trail

## ​A new 12 Days of Christmas interactive woodland walk has been unveiled at Lotherton this year. Laura Reid meets designer Aimee Grundell and curator Rosie Heard.

Aimee Grundell creates worlds where art and engineering collide. Large-scale interactive and experiential installations inviting visitors to press buttons, move objects or watch in awe at mechanical animations. This year, she’s done so for Lotherton’s Christmas Experience, the artist and designer behind the Leeds attraction’s new festive woodland walk.

She and her team at her Calderdale-based studios were responsible for bringing Lotherton’s first 12 Days of Christmas trail to life back in 2016 - a wonderland that ran for five years. Now they have reimagined the trail, inspired by the nostalgia of children’s toys across the decades, Christmas characters and residents of Lotherton itself.

The new 12 Days of Christmas trail puts visitors at the heart of the magic, with bold, interactive installations combining traditional whimsy with a Wonka-esque world of imagination. From quirky cabins where little ones become the four calling birds, to nine giant dancing flower ladies, and 12 nutcracker ‘high-striker’ soldiers reminiscent of fairground ‘test your strength’ games, each of the stops puts play at its heart.

Aimee says: “I really wanted it to be like if you were unwrapping Christmas presents through the ages so I’ve taken iconic bits of toys or gifts as inspiration… Everything has a playfulness to it. Every single thing is interactive and playable essentially so we’ve pushed the boundaries. There’s also a lot of STEM learning in there, a lot of science.

“It was really important that it’s interactive because I think nowadays kids don’t really get to do that much because of the legalities around everything; it means play (on this scale) doesn’t really happen that much. We’re taking a risk doing this but hopefully it will pay off and everyone will have a really lovely time.”

The trail has been in the planning since last Christmas, with Aimee and her team on board since springtime. For four months, the elements were constructed and tested in the workshop, then there was a nearly two-week installation process at Lotherton before the trail opened last weekend.

“We’ve got electrics, lots of programming elements, metalwork, woodwork. So there have been many different people that have come in and done their bit,” Aimee says. “There’s a lot to contend with because you’re outdoors (for the trail), it’s freezing, and all the electrics have to somehow survive that.”

Her commission at Lotherton ten years ago was Aimee’s first “big stakes” job in her current field. She actually began her career in the fashion industry, as a stylist for magazines in London. Then, she moved back north and began work in large format printing.

For the last 15 years or so, she’s been an artist, designer and maker. But she says she’s always had a creative streak. “I come from a very academic family that don’t believe art is really a thing. So I don’t really know how I ended up doing this to be honest, it was probably just rebelling,” she laughs.

“I’ve always been like this. I insisted once on taking a drinks machine I’d made on holiday and it was taken off me at the airport. I was always tinkering with things, I used to take things apart and put them together again.”

Aimee was selected from a tender process to bring the vision for the first 12 Days of Christmas trail to life. But the idea was actually the brainchild of Leeds City Council’s Rosie Heard, who now works as part of the Breeze team, offering opportunities and events for young people in the area.

With her then four-year-old son in mind, Rosie set about bringing a big Christmassy event to one of the council’s sites and the Lotherton Christmas Experience was born. “We specified for the trail to be 12 days of Christmas themed initially because we were looking for something that everybody knew, a theme everybody could engage with, something family friendly that would engage all the generations really,” explains Rosie, who manages audience development and commercial events.

It became a popular part of Lotherton’s Christmas offering for five years, before an alternative trail then ran for another five years. Curator Rosie says visitors “still harp back” to missing the 12 Days of Christmas trail so this year, they decided to reinvent the original experience.

It forms part of Lotherton’s wider Christmas event, which also includes an elf village, the chance to meet Santa and Mrs Claus, and the opportunity to see Lotherton Hall decorated for a traditional Edwardian Christmas. But for many visitors, the trail will be the highlight.

Rosie says: “There’s a lot of Christmas event stuff that can just be bought off the shelf. We know companies where you can go to buy installations like singing reindeer for example. But we wanted this to complement the Edwardian estate and it does, with the aesthetic, the hand painted wood.

“I want the trail to be seen as something that is art but at the same time is fun and interactive and beautiful and is something that hopefully creates memories for people when they grow up. When you look back at Christmas, you won’t necessarily remember the presents that you were bought, but hopefully you’ll remember the traditions and the things that you do.

“And what we have always wanted is for Lotherton to be a family tradition. We have people who come back year after year after year…There are new Christmas events popping up all time, but I hope this will continue to engage because it’s unique and different.”
