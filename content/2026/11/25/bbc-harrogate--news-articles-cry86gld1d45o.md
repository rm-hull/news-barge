Bird flu protection and surveillance zones have been put around two commercial poultry farms in North Yorkshire

**Bird owners have been urged to tighten safety measures after cases of avian influenza were been detected in North Yorkshire.**

The disease has been found in two commercial turkey farms near Thirsk and Northallerton.

The UK Health Security Agency said risks to the public remained very low and protection and surveillance zones were in place around the farms.

Richard Foster, whose responsibilities include environmental health at North Yorkshire Council, said bird flu could have a "devastating impact on both commercial and small-scale bird keepers".

Bird flu, also known as avian influenza, is an infectious disease that affects birds and can spread through saliva, faeces or contaminated environments.

On 13 November, the Animal and Plant Health Agency introduced a national order directing all poultry keepers with more than 50 birds to ensure they are housed.

It also included smaller flocks if poultry or eggs were supplied to people outside the keeper's household.

They must follow strict biosecurity measures and restrictions on moving birds to prevent the disease from spreading.

Foster said with Christmas coming, for producers and employees the birds were "a year's worth of work".

He said he hoped that "these are the only two cases this year, but unfortunately, previous years tend to suggest that this is not the case".

The local authority urged bird keepers to check their flocks for signs of disease and contact a vet immediately if birds become unwell.

North Yorkshire Council's director of public health, Louise Wallace, said bird flu infections in people were rare.
