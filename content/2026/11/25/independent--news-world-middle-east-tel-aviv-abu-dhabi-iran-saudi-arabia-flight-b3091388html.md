An Etihad flight from Abu Dhabi to Tel Aviv had to change course multiple times, and eventually return to Abu Dhabi amid ongoing Iranian attacks in the region.

Flight EY599, which left Abu Dhabi at 9:40am on Tuesday, could be seen on flight tracker sites turning around twice, first over Saudi Arabia and then over Bahrain. The flight eventually made it to Tel Aviv on Wednesday morning at 6:30am.

The airline released a statement saying the aircraft “was required to change its flight path over Saudi Arabia.”

![Abu Dhabi-based airline changed flight path amid Iran strikes in Saudi Arabia](https://static.independent.co.uk/s3fs-public/thumbnails/image/2020/03/23/19/etihad-airways-a380-jfk-arrival.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

It has not clarified why the flight path was changed. However, on November 24 Saudi Arabia confirmed an Iran-backed Houthi attack on Riyadh resulted in a drone hitting the city’s tallest skyscraper, injuring 11 people.

The same building, which hosts the headquarters of the Saudi Public ​Investment Fund, was struck on November 19 as well.

Flight tracking data of EY599 shows the plane flying over roughly the Riyadh area of the country.

Saudi Arabian airspace has been closed on and off for the duration of the U.S, Israel, Iran war.

The airline, as quoted in the Times of Israel, went on to say: “Since the amount of fuel on board was insufficient to complete the flight on the alternative route, and continuing the flight to Tel Aviv would have resulted in exceeding the crew duty and rest time limitations set by aviation regulations, a decision was made to return the aircraft to Zayed International Airport in Abu Dhabi for refueling or replacing the aircraft with a larger one, changing the flight crew and departing again for Tel Aviv.”

Several airlines have suspended flights over central and eastern Saudi routes amid continued safety concerns.

![A damaged building in Saudi Arabia following Houthi strikes](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/09/09/08/2026-09-09T071832Z_178268819_RC26FNAJBQT4_RTRMADP_3_SAUDI-YEMEN-AFTERMATH.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

While Etihad is still running flights from Jeddah and some flights which can enter Saudi airspace, it has cancelled all of its passenger and cargo services from Abu Dhabi and both Riyadh and Dammam until the end of November, 2026, per Avi-Go News.

Emirates has suspended flights from Dubai to Riyadh and Dammam with no restart date.

European flights to the region are also impacted by suspensions. KLM has suspended its flights from Amsterdam to Dubai until March 2027, British Airways has postponed its Heathrow-Riyadh flights until mid January 2027 and permanently cancelled its flight from London to Jeddah, and the Lufthansa Group has extended its suspensions for flights to Riyadh and Dammam through 7 January 2027.
