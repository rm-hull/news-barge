## Expert reveals cheap 65p trick to prevent condensation on your windows

Condensation can cause problems in your home. *(Image: Getty)*

With the weather getting colder and the price of energy bills going up, you might be keeping your windows closed to avoid letting out any heat.

However, this can cause other problems such as condensation.

Condensation can then cause damp and mould to form, which can harm your health and damage your house.

Homeowners are now being urged to place this cheap household item next to their windows to prevent condensation from building up.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21760829.jpg?type=mds-article-620)There are cheap ways of preventing condensation in your home. *(Image: Getty)*

## Cheap household item that gets rid of condensation

Dehumidifiers are an easy way to prevent condensation in your home but can cost a lot of money if you are having to run them constantly.

A window specialist has revealed a simple and cost-effective way of getting rid of condensation.

Allan Reid, a double-glazed windows expert from Art Windows and Doors, said: “It sounds almost too simple to work, but it really does.

"A small bowl of salt acts as a natural dehumidifier, drawing moisture from the air before it can settle on your windows.

“It's an old trick, but one we still recommend to customers who want a quick, no-cost fix."

Other items such as silica gel will also work but it is more likely that you will already have some salt in your kitchen.

A 750g bottle of table salt from Aldi only costs 65p.

All you have to do is place the bowl of salt on your windowsill in the rooms that are most susceptible to condensation.

In the morning, you should open the windows for a few minutes to get rid of any trapped air and help ventilate the room.

Allan added: “Regularly replace the salt or silica gel once it becomes clumpy, as that's a sign it's absorbed as much moisture as it can.

"Keep trickle vents open where possible to maintain airflow even in cold weather.

“Make sure to also inspect and reseal older windows if needed, as damaged seals are a common hidden cause of persistent condensation.

"Think about repositioning furniture away from external walls to enhance airflow, too, which will help to minimise moisture accumulation."

Allan said the trick only takes a few minutes to do each evening but will help to prevent “long-term damage” and keep your home drier throughout the winter.
