## York restaurant faces special order after illegal workers found in two raids

Home Office immigration officers have twice found people working illegally at Sky Blue Restaurant in York *(Image: Brighton Argus)*

A York restaurant where Home Office staff twice found illegal workers during raids could be hit with a special immigration order.

The authorities are seeking an illegal working compliance order against Sky Blue Restaurant in Barbican Road, central York.

Sky Blue York Ltd, the operating company that ran the restaurant went into liquidation in September with debts including £90,000 to the Home Office in two unpaid fines.

The Home Office had imposed the fines on the business for employing an illegal worker.

York Magistrates' Court will hear the application for the compliance order on December 4.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21467977.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The premises of Sky Blue Chinese Restaurant *(Image: LDRS)*

According to Government guidance to immigration officers, the purpose of compliance orders is to prevent employers with a history of employing illegal workers from doing so in future.

An order can include clauses preventing people from going into or using the premises, making the employer carry out specific checks on whether the people they intend to employ have the right to work in the UK and making the employer comply with checks by immigration officers.

Initially a court can make an order for up to 12 months and the immigration service can apply for it to be extended to a total of 24 months.

According to the court list, the authorities are seeking to take out the order against Sky Blue Restaurant of 16 Barbican Road, York.

The restaurant was stripped of its premises licence at a City of York Council hearing on September 24 following raids by the Home Office’s Immigration Compliance and Enforcement (ICE) department in July 2025 and December 2025.

On each occasion, officers found a person working at the premises who did not have a legal right to be in the UK.

After each raid, the Home Office issued fines called civil penalty referral notices against the restaurant. The minimum for such a fine is £45,000.

Also in September, Sky Blue York Ltd went into liquidation owing £102,412.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21467978.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The premises of Sky Blue Chinese Restaurant *(Image: LDRS)*

Under the name Sky Blue Chinese Restaurant, the restaurant applied to City of York Council to transfer the enterprise’s licence from Zhong Le Chen, who had been present during the raids, to Yuk Chuan Pang.

The council decided Mr Chen had run the business when it had operated under the name The Regency Chinese Restaurant.

Home Office immigration enforcement officers objected to the transfer on the grounds that Ms Pang was not effectively checking the work status of employees and North Yorkshire Police stated it had evidence she had a business association with Zhong Le Chen.

Sky Blue Chinese Restaurant then withdrew the application earlier this month.
