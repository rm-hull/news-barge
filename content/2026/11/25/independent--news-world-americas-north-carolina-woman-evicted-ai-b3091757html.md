A North Carolina woman has been evicted after an AI assistant for her apartment complex promised her more time to pay rent.

Laura Mack from Raleigh owed nearly $4,000 in overdue rent for Colony Townhome Apartments. She was relieved when she received an email earlier this month that she thought was from a human in the leasing office, allowing her to pay at the end of November, according to an interview with WRAL News.

But she then received an eviction notice on November 16 and when she called the leasing office, she discovered the email was sent from the apartment complex’s AI assistant named Olivia.

"I'm baffled," Mack said. "I need a place to stay."

The emails Mack received on November 8 about paying her overdue rent on November 30 were never identified as being sent by Olivia. Instead, they were signed as “Colony Townhome Apartments.”

![Laura Mack received an eviction notice on November 16 and discovered the email acknowledging that she could pay back rent by November 30 was sent from the apartment complex’s AI assistant named Olivia](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/25/19/27/Screenshot-2026-11-25-at-2-51-50-PM.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

While Mack takes responsibility for the overdue rent, she said the apartment complex needs to take responsibility for what has unfolded with its AI agent.

"If you told me till the 30th, I'll get out by the 30th," Mack said. "But give me that. You at least honor that. You have to have somewhere where you're taking responsibility."

Mack was locked out of her apartment last Thursday.

The apartment complex later told WRAL that Mack had already been notified of a pending eviction.

"We recognize how difficult it is to fall behind on rent and face eviction, and we take Ms. Mack's situation seriously,” Alfred Leach, a spokesman for Colony Townhome Apartments, said Tuesday.

![While Mack takes responsibility for the overdue rent, she said the apartment complex needs to take responsibility for what has unfolded with its AI agent](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/25/19/27/GettyImages-2295567104.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Leach said a local court sided with the apartment complex on October 23, resulting in the November 16 eviction notice. A motion to appeal the eviction was denied last Thursday, WRAL reported, citing court records.

“The November 8th emails concerned her outstanding rent balance, which is owed regardless of possession. They did not address the court’s order and did not change it,” Leach said.

The spokesman also said that the leasing office “told Ms. Mack directly on Nov. 18 that a payment arrangement would not change the court-ordered lockout.”

But Mack argued that there have been similar eviction proceedings against her in the past and she avoided being kicked out after paying what was owed.

“There have been judgments against me in the past, and they've overridden them when I paid and let me stay in the apartment,” she told WRAL Tuesday. “They're still refusing to take responsibility for what their AI bot told me."
