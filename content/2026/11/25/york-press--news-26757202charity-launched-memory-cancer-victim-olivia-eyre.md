## ‘Her light carried forward’: family launches charity in memory of Olivia, 8

Olivia Eyre, who died in January 2024 aged eight years old from rhabdomyosarcoma, a rare type of tissue cancer, with her sister Elsie.

A CHARITY has been set up in memory of a "brave and fearless" eight-year-old girl to support families facing childhood cancer.

Olivia Eyre died in January 2024 aged eight years old from rhabdomyosarcoma, a rare type of tissue cancer.

In her memory, her family has launched Olivia’s Little Light Legacy, a charity dedicated to supporting families facing childhood cancer in Scarborough, Whitby, and Ryedale.

A swimathon is also being held this weekend at Derwent Swim and Fitness Centre to raise funds.

The event aims to raise funds and awareness for the charity’s work, which focuses on supporting parents from the moment their child receives a terminal diagnosis, standing alongside them throughout their child’s illness, and continuing to support them through bereavement and beyond.

Jemma said: "Olivia’s Little Light Legacy is a charity founded in loving memory of our beautiful little girl, Olivia May, who heartbreakingly lost her life to cancer at just eight years old.

"Our charity has been born out of the most devastating experience any parent could ever imagine, and a determination to make sure other families do not have to face the same journey feeling as alone as we did.

"Throughout Olivia’s illness, and especially during the most difficult stages of her cancer journey, we found that support for families in our local area was incredibly limited.

"When you are faced with the unimaginable possibility of losing your child, having the right support around you is so important.

"Yet, as parents, we often felt there was very little available to help us navigate the emotional trauma, fear, and uncertainty that came with watching our little girl suffer through such a horrific illness.

"This is why Olivia’s Little Light Legacy means so much to us.

"We want to make a difference for families across Scarborough, Whitby, and Ryedale, providing the support that we desperately needed but struggled to find.

"We want families to know that they are not alone, that their feelings matter, and that there is someone there to listen, understand, and help them through the darkest of times.

"We cannot change what happened to our beautiful, brave and fearless Olivia, and we will forever wish that her story had a different ending.

"But we can use our experiences to create something meaningful from the unimaginable pain of losing her.

"Olivia’s Little Light Legacy will be her light carried forward — a legacy of love, compassion, and support for families who find themselves walking a path no parent should ever have to walk."

The swimathon, will take place on Sunday, November 29 from 2pm.

Organiser Ollie Varey, said: "This year, we will be swimming from Whitby to Cayton — a total of 23 miles - 1,472 lengths.

"This charity is incredibly special to my family, as it has been created in loving memory of our beautiful Olivia, who sadly passed away from cancer aged just eight. We hope that through this charity, we can help other families facing the most difficult times and provide support when they need it most."

Ollie, who is a fitness instructor at Derwent Swim and Leisure Centre, added: "Everyone is welcome to come along and give us a helping hand, or even jump in and swim a length or two! Every little bit of help makes a difference.

"Your support and donations are hugely appreciated, and we look forward to seeing as many of you as possible on 29th November."

"Thank you so much for your kindness and support."
