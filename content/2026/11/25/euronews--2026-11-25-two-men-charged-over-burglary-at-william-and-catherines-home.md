## Prosecutors allege the suspects climbed a drainpipe to access a dressing room, stealing six pairs of cufflinks and a clock. Neither entered a plea and both remain in custody.

Two men appeared at Norwich Magistrates' Court on Wednesday charged with breaking into the country home of Britain's Prince William and his wife Catherine, Princess of Wales.

The pair allegedly climbed a drainpipe to gain access to a dressing room at the couple's residence in eastern England before stealing six pairs of cufflinks and a clock, prosecutors said.

Mirgen Doku, 34, and Arilen Ndoci, 32, face charges of burglary and trespass. Assisted by an Albanian interpreter, they spoke only to confirm their names and dates of birth during the hearing.

British media reported that both men are Albanian nationals.

One of the men gave an address in London, while the other had no fixed address.

Neither entered a plea at Norwich Magistrates' Court. Both were remanded in custody ahead of a further hearing on Monday.

Norfolk Police had earlier confirmed that two men had been charged in connection with a burglary on the royal family's Sandringham Estate, where William and Catherine's country home is located.

The Sandringham estate, located in Norfolk in the East of England, is the private country retreat of King Charles III and Queen Camilla.

William and Kate use the secluded red brick Georgian mansion 171 kilometers north of London as a country residence on weekends.

The couple and their three children spend most of their time at Forest Lodge in Windsor, west of London, which they have described as their “forever home.”

Sandringham House, the Jacobean-style jewel of the estate, has been open to the public since 1977. The house and gardens are open from 28 March to 9 October each year.
