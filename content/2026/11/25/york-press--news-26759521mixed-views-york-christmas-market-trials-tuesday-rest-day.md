Two friends are eating ice creams next to a mulled wine stall at York Christmas Market. But there’s no wine being offered because the stall, like all the others, is shut today.

“I enjoy the Christmas market every year … But there’s nothing to see, there’s nothing to buy. I was hoping I could buy some Christmas gifts,” says Maithili Kadam, 41, who has travelled to the market from Ripon.

Maithili didn’t realise the market is closed on Tuesdays for a ‘rest day’ before heading to York to meet her friend Gargi Deshmukh, 34, who has come from Scarborough.

The market being shut today is “disappointing”, Maithili says. “I was just telling [Gargi] how lively it is.”

But at least “the ice cream’s good”, she concludes.

What will they do instead of going to the Christmas market? “Just head back home, I guess,” Maithili says. “It’s too cold for us to hang around any longer.”

![York Christmas market shut on Tuesday (November 24) during its weekly 'rest day'](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21760083.jpg?type=mds-article-620)York Christmas Market shut on Tuesday (November 24) during its weekly 'rest day' *(Image: Dylan Connell)*

The Christmas market is trialling a Tuesday rest day this year to make the attraction more accessible, including for disabled people.

Council chiefs backed the trial, despite hearing that up to £7.8 million less could be spent in York city centre as a result.

York BID, City of York Council and Make It York, which organises the Christmas market, have said they will all monitor the economic impact of the rest day.

Two weeks into the market, traders are divided about the impact of the Tuesday closure.

“It’s definitely quieter ... We would prefer it if there wasn’t a rest day at all. We just see it as pointless,” says Nick Roberts, manning the Crystals and Fossils stall in Shambles Market.

“The footfall just isn’t there [compared to] what it usually is [when the Christmas market is open].

“… I’d really like to know how many Blue Badge holders are coming into town because I don’t think they are – we don’t see masses of them. It’s definitely better with the Christmas market [open].”

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21760084.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Nick Roberts, of Crystals and Fossils stall in Shambles Market *(Image: Dylan Connell)*

Nick says the business relies on the busy Christmas market and summer periods to stay open.

He estimates that the Tuesday closure will result in Crystals and Fossils losing about 17 per cent of its takings.

“If you’re a market trader on York market, you can stand from January up to May and not make anything,” Nick says. “You have to make it in the summer months and the Christmas months.

“So to take one day away from seven, you’re losing about 17 per cent profit.

“Now bearing in mind that trade is down for everybody – 20 to 30 per cent this year – what we really don’t need is an extra 17, 20 per cent on top of that.

“For me, personally, it’s [the rest day] a no, no. For the majority of the traders on the market, we’re all saying the same thing.”

## 'We’re still getting plenty of sales,' says trader on Christmas market 'rest day'

But Daisy Summers, working on the Lily-Betts fudge stall in Shambles Market, is more upbeat about the rest days.

“In some ways we’ve had more people than we expected,” she says. “We’ve had people looking at our stuff instead of the Christmas market.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21760085.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Daisy Summers, left, working on the Lily-Betts fudge stall in Shambles Market *(Image: Dylan Connell)*

“We’ve had a lot of people asking where it is and we’re like, ‘It’s not open today but you can buy from us’.”

“It’s a different dynamic,” she adds, “but we’re still getting plenty of sales on both days.”

Daisy also feels the rest day is “beneficial for people who live in York – it means they can have a day out and not be surrounded by big crowds, and we’re still getting plenty of customers”.

Meanwhile at the Roman Bath pub in St Sampson Square, locals are enjoying a calmer atmosphere in the city centre.

Amy Howdle, a team leader at the pub, says the area is “a lot quieter on a Tuesday, but it’s nice to have a day where it’s not so busy so we can get other jobs in the pub done rather than just constantly serving people”.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21760087.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The Roman Bath pub in St Sampson Square *(Image: Dylan Connell)*

“It’s been nice to see the regulars and locals who usually come in during the week when the market’s not on – it’s nice that there’s still a day when they can come out and do their shopping and go for a drink.

“I think it’s a good thing that there’s a day when the market’s closed.”

She feels the rest day should continue in future years. “[Locals] have been saying they don’t come in [to the city centre] during the week, but they’re glad that they can still come in on a Tuesday.”

![Artist Ric Liptrot who took advantage of the York Christmas market rest day by sketching some of the huts](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21760088.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Artist Ric Liptrot who took advantage of the York Christmas Market rest day by sketching some of the huts *(Image: Dylan Connell)*

One person taking advantage of the market's Tuesday rest day is artist Ric Liptrot, who is well-known across York for his drawings of independent shops, pubs and landmarks.

“It’s a good chance to draw some shopfronts, places in the market with less traffic,” he says.

Ric, from York, feels the market’s rest day is a “good idea – it’s getting the right mix, isn’t it? It’s hard to keep everyone happy, I guess, but I can see the benefits of having a bit of a breather through the week.”
