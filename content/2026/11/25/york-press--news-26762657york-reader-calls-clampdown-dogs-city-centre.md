**York reader calls for clampdown on dogs in city centre**

I completely agree with regular contributor DM Deamer regarding dogs being taken into town as referenced in letters to The Press (Tuesday, November 24).

I believe that this trend began in lockdown when the town was virtually empty but has continued and escalated since.

I have seen a pair of lurchers in a clothes shop, an Irish Wolfhound in food stores, owners picking faeces outside shops and have stood on the foot of a Jack Russell resulting in being barked at by both dog and owner!

I believe that there should be a clampdown on this activity, and not just at Christmas as suggested by Mr Deamer.

*W Martin,*

*Holgate,*

**I need a break from this relentless negativit** y

Peter Rickaby’s letter is a perfect example of relentless negativity. It seems he can never find a good word to say about anyone or anything. Whatever the subject, he immediately reaches for the worst possible interpretation, as though pessimism alone qualifies as political insight.

His comments about Andy Burnham are particularly telling. Rather than offering a balanced argument about the future or acknowledging that people might reasonably have different political views, he simply predicts disaster.

Perhaps Peter should remind himself of the 14 years of Conservative austerity, stagnating living standards, struggling public services and the damage that was done to communities across the country. It is rather convenient to overlook that record while criticising others and predicting doom.

Criticism is healthy, but constant negativity without balance, perspective or acknowledgement of the past is little more than cynicism. Occasionally, it would be refreshing to see him acknowledge something positive rather than assume the worst of everyone and everything.

*Jane White,*

*York*---**Programme of events for disability week**

It was great to see The Press of November 24 with its full page coverage of ‘Art gives a voice to York disability stories’ but its focus on what’s happening at Explore library meant the many, many other mostly free events right across the city and online didn’t get covered.

There are a great range of talks, workshops, a virtual pub quiz, taster events in sport, music and exercise, a chance to try out mobility equipment or find out how to improve your balance, learn about using direct payments for social care or digital exclusion, join a support group, and much more.

There’s also a big information fair on December 1 at St Sampson’s Centre.

Something for everyone! Printed programme copies are in all libraries and community centres and elsewhere and online copies are at www.yorkdisabilityweek.org.
