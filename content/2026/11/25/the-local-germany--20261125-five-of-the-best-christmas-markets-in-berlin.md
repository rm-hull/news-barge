Whether you're looking for a quieter *Weihnachtsmarkt*tucked away in a Berlin neighbourhood or a glitzy market in Mitte, here are five of the best to enjoy this winter.**Alt Rixdorfer Weihnachtsmarkt December 4th-6th**

Our guide starts with a cosy Christmas market in the historic village of Rixdorf in the southern district of Neukölln, where Richardplatz – and the surrounding cobblestone streets – is transformed into a traditional Christmas setting.

The market boasts more than 200 stalls selling homemade crafts, decorations, wooden toys and snacks. But what makes this market particularly special is that the stalls are run by non-profit organisations, charities, schools and community groups. Arguably a welcome break from the commercial side of Christmas.

While you're in the area, gaze at the old buildings such as the Bethlehemskirche church, which dates back to the 14th century. Rixdorfer Schmiede, Berlin's oldest operating blacksmith shop first documented in 1624, is also worth checking out.

The Local team thoroughly recommends getting fresh *Maronen (*chestnuts). You can watch them being prepared and then wander round eating your toasty bag of treats. Candy floss and Bratwurst are also solid options.* Berlin-Neukölln, free admission*

**WeihnachtsZauber Gendarmenmarkt, November 23rd-December 31st**

It's easy to see why this is one of Berlin's most impressive Christmas markets. Located in the stunning Gendarmenmarkt square, between the French and German cathedrals and the Konzerthaus, WeihnachtsZauber is the perfect spot to get in the mood for festive magic.

Guests can browse stalls selling traditional decorations and Christmas gifts, while foodies can enjoy a taste of classic festive snacks like Bratwurst or more elaborate dishes.

The twinkly lights and giant *Weihnachtsbaum* (Christmas tree) add to the cosy atmosphere.

![View of the Christmas market on the Gendarmenmarkt in Berlin, taken on December 13, 2025. ](https://apiwp.thelocal.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/AFP__20251213__88237WH__v1__MidRes__GermanyChristmasTraditionMarket.jpg)

There is a varied entertainment programme that includes acrobats, choirs, musicians, dancers and other performances.

If the weather gets chilly, you can make your way to one of the heated, covered dining areas and enjoy a meal or a warming drink.

*Gendarmenmarkt, Berlin-Mitte, Around €2 to enter, although admission is generally free for everyone between 11am and 2pm from Monday to Friday. Children under 12 can enter for free at all times.*

**The Weddingmarkt Wintermarkt (Christmas Edition) November 29th, December 6th, December 13th, Saturday December 19, and December 20th**

In the run up to Christmas, the flea market at Leopoldplatz in the northern district of Wedding is transformed into a winter wonderland.

This is a particularly good place to pick up Christmas gifts that are locally-made and a little different from the usual high-street offerings.

Around 70 stalls showcase work by local artists designers and farmers, with a range of ceramics, candles, porcelain, jewellery, bags, soap and food delicacies on offer.

There is also seasonal street food and Glühwein, which you can enjoy alongside entertainment, including brass bands and carol singers.

It's easy to reach from anywhere in the city: Leopoldplatz U-Bahn station is on the U6 and U9 route.

*Leopoldplatz in Berlin-Wedding, free admission*

**Lucia Weihnachtsmarkt, November 23rd-December 22nd**

For a slightly different take on the traditional Christmas market, visit the Lucia Weihnachtsmarkt at the Kulturbrauerei.

Named after Saint Lucia, known as the bringer of light during the dark winter season, this market is dedicated to the Nordic countries.

Saint Lucia is revered throughout Scandinavia and her saint's day is celebrated annually on December 13th.

The Berlin Lucia Choir marks the day with a procession through the courtyards, accompanied by Swedish and German Christmas carols.

Elsewhere in the market, you can warm yourself up with Swedish Glögg or Finnish Glögi, sample Scandinavian specialities such as elk sausage and Köttbullar, or browse the stalls for cool gifts.

![The Lucia Weihnachtsmarkt in 2021.](https://apiwp.thelocal.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/266131840.jpg)

Children can also enjoy a carousel ride – and families should definitely keep their eyes open for Santa Claus.

If you can, visit after sunset to experience the full cosy winter setting. Like all of Berlin's Christmas markets, it can get busy so stay safe and warm.

*Kulturbrauerei, Berlin-Prenzlauer-Berg, free admission*

For another classic Berlin Christmas market experience with a dose of nostalgia, visit the Rotes Rathaus.

The sprawling market has plenty to keep guests entertained, including a 50-meter panoramic Ferris wheel offering views across the city and an ice rink surrounding the Neptune Fountain.

There's also a traditional market street with stalls selling handicrafts, gifts and festive food, as well as a medieval market around St Marienkirche, where you can sample traditional treats.

This one is also open into the new year so you have plenty of time to enjoy it at your own pace.

And when you're visiting a market, remember to bring some cash. While many stallholders are beginning to allow card payments, some will still be *nur Bargeld*(cash only).* Berlin-Mitte, free admission*
