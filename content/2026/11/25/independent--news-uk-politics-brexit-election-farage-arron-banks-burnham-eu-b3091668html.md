Andy Burnham has been warned that he has ensured the next general election will be “the Brexit election” by reopening the debate on Britain’s membership of the EU.

The claims was made as Reform leader Nigel Farage was joined by his longstanding political ally businessman Arron Banks to launch a pro-Brexit campaign - Keep Britain Out - to rival the Stay Out launched last week by Lord Michael Gove.

The rival campaigns are a rerun of Lord Gove’s Vote Leave and Mr Farage’s Leave.EU with one leaning towards the Tories and the other towards Reform UK.

Already the rejoin EU campaign has got its act together with the creation of Get In!

It comes as Mr Burnham prepares to reopen a national conversation on Brexit laying out four options in his 10 year plan including the status quo, rejoining the EU or a choice of compromises of joining either the single market or customs union.

![Farage and Banks styled themselves the bad boys of Brexit](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/25/17/01M89R2MSDFB8EZKD2Z57XJ60D.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Mr Banks said: “I don't know about the date of the next EU referendum. I'm pretty sure it's going to be on the ballot for the general election, which I think will be May. We've got a slightly different view on that, but I think it's going to be a Brexit election, and that's going to be on the ballot.”

Speaking at a press conference, Mr Farage said he was confident his side would win any future referendum as it now “owned the status quo” and had “every single economic argument in our favour”.

He denied that his side would now be running “Project Fear” which damaged the Remain side 10 years ago claiming that the status quo campaign this time would be “Project Optimism” instead.

But he also insisted that the potential election of far right leader Marine Le Pen as French president in April next year would help him win a second referendum saying that she would veto British membership of the EU.

Speaking at a press conference in Westminster, Mr Farage said: “Prior to 2016, the establishment owned the status quo and people naturally have a fear of change.

“Very often human beings will accept modest decline rather than taking a risk. Well, the risk now very clearly is on rejoining.

“The risk is on being forced to join the euro. The risk is on being forced to pay £40 billion-plus a year in membership fees to the union.

“The risk is overnight destroying AI, destroying agri-tech, destroying all the areas in which we’ve shown through innovation and brilliance we can actually be world leaders.”

He denied that Keep Britain Out would be offering the equivalent of “Project Fear”, as the Leave campaign dubbed warnings from Remainers of the economic impact of leaving the EU in 2016.

He said: “This isn’t Project Fear, this is project hope. This is project we can do better.”

Mr Farage said Mr Banks, a major donor to the Leave.EU campaign, would lead Keep Britain Out.

Asked about the Stay Out campaign, Mr Banks said he would “leave that with the dripping wet Tories where it belongs”.

He added that Keep Britain Out had already been contacted by “lots of the people that backed us last time” and suggested support from the AI industry could make it “the best-funded political campaign of all time”.

The campaign intends to ask MPs to sign up to a list of 10 pledges, including keeping the UK out of the EU, opposing “mass immigration” and backing the country’s farming and fishing industries.

Mr Farage was the first to sign up to the pledges on Wednesday and suggested he expected other Reform MPs to do the same.

The two were joined by economist Catherine McBride who claimed that Mr Burnham’s deal with the EU last week was “a disaster” for the UK.

The launch came as Prosper UK, a group of Tories on the left of the party, launched their pro-EU campaign including senior former ministers like Sir David Liddington and Amber Rudd.

Earlier, Mr Burnham and Tory leader Kemi Badenoch clashed in parliament on Brexit with Mr Burnham peppered with questions over whether his deal will stand unless he gives way on allowing EU students to pay cheaper tuition fees in the UK.

The Keep Britain Out campaign unveiled a 10-point pledge card for MPs and candidates to sign including a promise to oppose the UK rejoining the EU.

The card was signed by Mr Farage and is is set to be signed by his party’s MPs.
