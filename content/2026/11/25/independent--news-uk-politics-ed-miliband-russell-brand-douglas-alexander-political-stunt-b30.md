One of Andy Burnham's cabinet ministers has revealed he tried to make foreign secretary Ed Miliband call off his notorious appearance with Russell Brand in the 2015 general election.

Scottish secretary Douglas Alexander, who was in charge of then Labour leader Miliband's election campaign, said he warned him not to go ahead with the stunt at the disgraced comedian’s flat but was ignored.

In an interview with *The Independent*editor-in-chief Geordie Greig for his series* The Talk Show*, Mr Alexander said he made it “absolutely” clear to Miliband at the time that getting involved with “unsavoury” Brand was “diminishing” for someone who “aspired to be prime minister.”**Read next:Douglas Alexander has exposed a fatal flaw in Miliband’s political judgement**

Mr Alexander said: “I disagreed with the judgement of Ed going. You can ask anyone who was there. I genuinely thought it was the wrong thing to do. I thought if you aspire to be the prime minister of the country, you should not be going to Russell Brand's flat in the east end of London.

“He seemed to me even then to be an unreliable and unsavoury character. It was diminishing for the leader of the opposition... when he should be speaking directly to the public.”

Mr Alexander said he “absolutely remembers” telling Mr Miliband it was “not a great way” to behave.

He praised Miliband's “honourable” recent acknowledgement to have got it wrong with Brand, before emphasising: “But it was wrong.”

![Ed Miliband and Douglas Alexander at the Labour conference in 2013](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/25/13/181665812..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Mr Miliband's interview with Brand took place seven years after the comedian’s obscene prank phone call – with Jonathan Ross – to Andrew Sachs, who played Manuel in *Fawlty Towers*, about Brand's relationship with Sachs' granddaughter.

Though not a household name, deeply religious Mr Alexander, 59, whose father and grandfather were ministers in the Church of Scotland, is one of the great survivors of British politics.

He first entered Parliament in 1997, has served in the cabinets of four Labour PMs and is currently in his second spell as Scottish secretary.

In a broad discussion for *The Independent*, he gave a withering assessment of Keir Starmer, saying Starmer “disdained” parts of his job as prime minister.

Alexander also revealed his “cold dispassionate fury” with former colleague Peter Mandelson over his links with convicted paedophile Jeffrey Epstein.

He said of Mandelson: “I literally wish I had never met the man... he wasn't a Labour person.”

He claimed Brexit was such a “terrible mistake” that “even Nigel Farage is running away from its consequences”.

Comparing ex Labour PMs Tony Blair and Gordon Brown, Alexander said Blair was “the most dazzling persuader in British politics in 40 years” while Brown was “driven and tough.”

He disclosed details of an unlikely personal and political alliance with U2 rock star Bono, saying he would “walk through walls for him”.

And he revealed witnessing Mr Burnham competing with students to see who knew the most lyrics of his favourite Manchester band, The Smiths.

While his condemnation of Miliband over the “ill-judged” interview with Brand is striking, his criticism of Starmer is even more sweeping.

“Keir was curiously averse to talking politics,” he said. “I started thinking maybe he's got imposter syndrome. He almost disdained aspects of the job. He didn't enjoy spending time with politicians.”

![Douglas Alexander says Starmer didn't like talking politics](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/25/17/35/s1.png?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Mr Alexander said he saw this at first hand accompanying Starmer on a trip to India last year.

“We're travelling back on the plane. There were 150 business leaders. We walk (down) the plane, and he gets a selfie taken, talks to people and comes back to the Cabinet at the front of the plane, a bottle of wine is produced, and three British Airways cabin crew join us for a drink.

“Now, if that'd been Tony Blair, he would've talked to them for a couple of minutes, totally dazzled them, and then sat down and talked politics or read a book.

“Gordon (Brown) in a rather less smooth way, would've said: ‘How are you? How are you? Thanks for all you do’.

“They would've known that he was trying to be appreciative, but then he would've talked politics. Keir stood and talked to those cabin crew for more than an hour.”

On a human level it was impressive, acknowledged Alexander, who served in Starmer's Cabinet.

But it convinced him Starmer would rather spend his time with people not involved in politics than those who were.

He characterised it thus: “Politics is the rent you pay for being prime minister. It's not an optional part of the job.”

Alexander's continuing sense of outrage over Mandelson, sacked as the UK's envoy in Washington last year over his involvement with Epstein, is palpable.

![Messages from Scottish Secretary Douglas Alexander to Lord Mandelson were contained in files released by the UK Government on Monday.](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/06/02/12/02121303-79fef71f-936f-4eea-ba0d-a3a1ef5cc1fc.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“If any of us had known for a second... that Peter Mandelson was betraying his prime minister, his cabinet colleagues and the country by sharing information with Jeffrey Epstein, all of us would have felt then what I think all of us feel now.

“Which is I literally wish I'd never met the man.

“If you look at what has emerged in terms of the crimes of Jeffrey Epstein and the horrors of what was perpetrated against women by Epstein, I feel cold, dispassionate fury with Peter Mandelson...

“Not only that association. People thought that he was a Labour person, and he wasn't.”

The harshness of Alexander's verdict may or may not have been influenced by the fact that he is an unabashed man of faith. When millions of his fellow regular churchgoets are singing *Once In Royal David's City* at coming carol concerts, they are singing lyrics written by one of his female ancestors.

She went by, to modern ears, the unusual name of Cecil Alexander, nearly two centuries ago, and also wrote *All Things Bright And Beautiful* and other hymns - “a couple of bangers as my children would say,” smiles Alexander.

Alexander, whose passionate pro European views are akin to a political article of faith, said he is “100 per cent” behind Burnham's decision to review Brexit.

It had inflicted “terrible economic damage to the UK over the last 10 years”.

![Douglas Alexander exclusively in conversation with Geordie Greig, editor-in-chief at The Independent](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/25/17/31/s9.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Throwing down the gauntlet to the Brexiteers, he said: “Who believes that Brexit has been a success? Even Nigel Farage is now running away from the consequences of his actions. The reality is that Britain would be stronger, more prosperous if we healed the broken relationship that was left behind after the EU referendum.”

The leaders of the 2016 Brexit campaign had used the same “invented grievances, flags and new borders” that had thankfully failed when deployed by Scottish nationalists in the 2014 Scottish home rule referendum, he said.

Alexander denied that by reopening Brexit, Burnham had provided a platform for Scottish Nationalists to argue that the issue Scotland's ties to the UK could be similarly reopened.

He claimed Burnham is “the Scottish National Party's worst nightmare because he is an attractive, compelling Labour politician making an internationalist case”.

Assessing friends and rivals Blair and Brown Alexander, always closer to fellow Scot Brown than Blair, said: “Tony was the single most dazzling persuader in British politics in 40 years.

“Gordon was a profoundly deep thinker, but much less comfortable in his role both as a communicator but also in running Downing Street.”

When Brown succeeded Blair in No 10 in 2007 it was never clear whether his administration represented continuity or change, stated Alexander.

Had he experienced Brown's temper? “Yes.”

![Douglas Alexander (left) with then prime minister Gordon Brown at the Labour party conference in 2007](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/25/17/77008758..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Asked if it was true that Brown would hurl telephones across the room in a rage, Alexander trod gently.

“He is a man of towering strengths and also compensating weaknesses. He is reserved and shy, so if you put him in a room, he doesn't have the natural sparkle, lightness of soul that, that Tony does.

“He brought a moral seriousness to his policy. But sure, he could be a tough boss.

“Nobody would say Gordon was an easy, relaxed prime minister. He was a driven prime minister. That absolutely had its effect on Downing Street and in terms of how the public perceived him. But I think history will ultimately be kind to him.”

There is definitely no phone throwing in Burnham's No 10 according to uber Labour loyalist Alexander.

“Andy has brought... a calmness, capability, stability, energy and optimism” to Downing Street that simply wasn't the case (under Starmer).

Alexander illustrated his point with a bizarre anecdote involving a mouse and a Whitehall mandarin.

“When I saw one of the directors of communications for a meeting under Keir, in the middle of the meeting, a mouse ran across the floor and everyone got a fright.

“As I was walked by a civil servant to the No 10 black door, the civil servant said to me, ‘We're all quite proud of that mouse. He's seen off three directors of communication’.”

![Bono has become a long-standing friend](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/25/13/2305168240..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

In the nine years Alexander spent out of the Commons between 2015 and 2034 after losing it to the SNP he formed a novel bond with Bono after attending a U2 concert in New York.

Aside from music, they have a long standing shared interest in overseas development, one of Alexander's many past ministerial portfolios.

He described his family being summoned to Irish rock legend Bono's dressing room.

“We go to a U2 show in Madison Square Gardens because it's my wife's birthday, and just before the show, someone comes and taps us on the shoulder and said, ‘Can you come and see Bono before the show?’

“I said, ‘Can I bring the kids?’ They said, ‘Yeah’.”

Bono told him boldly: 'You and I, we're gonna be partners. We're gonna work together.'

And they did, setting up climate and and impact investing fund.

“It really took off over the coming years. I ended up working with him for nine years. He is a good friend and a good man who I would walk through walls for.”

Alexander laughed as he recalled Burnham living out his own rock fantasy when they were visiting a college in Lanarkshire Scotland.

![Andy Burnham playing guitar on a visit to Ukraine](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/08/25/09/2026-08-25T082043Z_2007768415_RC255NAFQN5H_RTRMADP_3_UKRAINE-CRISIS-COALITION-BURNHAM.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

One among a group of six music students, challenged Burnham: 'I know more Smiths lyrics than you do.'

“And Andy says, "No, you don't."'They go back and forth with Smiths lyrics, and Andy said: ‘Well, you're music students. Can you play this?’

“And a guitar appears, and we go back and forth with *This Charming Man* and two or three other tracks. It was extraordinary.

“That's a key to how Andy's gonna be prime minister, he genuinely likes people, and they like him back.

“People are sick and tired of being sick and tired. They want to feel optimistic. What I glimpsed in that room gives me a real sense of optimism for the future.”
