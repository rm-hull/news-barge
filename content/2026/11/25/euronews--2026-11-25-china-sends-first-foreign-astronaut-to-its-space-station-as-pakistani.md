## The crew will carry out more than 90 scientific experiments and technology tests. according to China's state media reports.

China has launched the Shenzhou 24 spacecraft with a Pakistani astronaut on board, the first time a foreign national has flown to its Tiangong space station.

Muhammad Zeeshan Ali, 40, lifted off on Wednesday morning with Chinese crewmates Tang Hongbo, 51, and Wu Yi, 39, from the Jiuquan Satellite Launch Center in northwest China.

Hours earlier, crowds gathered for a send-off ceremony, many filming on their phones.

Following the launch, the capsule separated from its rocket and reached its planned orbit.

It is the first joint flight of Chinese and foreign astronauts in the history of China's crewed space programme.

## Pakistanis celebrate astronaut's feat

Thousands of kilometres away, Pakistanis watched the ascent on television at home and in restaurants, chanting slogans in praise of God and of Pakistan-China friendship. Scientists at the national space agency's headquarters followed the launch live.

Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif called the mission a source of national pride and an inspiration for the country's youth.

"This is not just one astronaut's journey, but the journey of 240 million Pakistanis," he wrote on X. He thanked Beijing for including a Pakistani astronaut and said the flight extended the two countries' close ties into space.

Planning Minister Ahsan Iqbal, speaking to reporters at the agency, thanked President Xi Jinping, China's leadership and its scientists and engineers, and said he hoped the flight would be the start of broader cooperation.

Asked whether Pakistan could one day send astronauts on its own, he replied: "Certainly that day will come."

Iqbal said Ali carried the Pakistani flag on his uniform, lifting his compatriots' hopes and technological ambitions into orbit. "The sky is no longer our limit; it is our launching pad," he said, calling the mission an inspiration for young Pakistanis hoping for careers in science and technology.

## Mission to carry out more than 90 scientific experiments

The flight stems from an agreement the two countries signed in February 2025. The Chinese media reported that the crew will carry out more than 90 scientific experiments and technology tests.

Ali, who is on a short-term mission, will run an experiment on photosynthetic cyanobacteria before returning to Earth aboard Shenzhou 23. Ali and Wu are making their first spaceflight. Tang has flown twice before and is set to become the first astronaut to board Tiangong three times, according to state broadcaster CCTV.

At a press conference on Tuesday, Tang said differences in language, culture and psychology had made training together harder, but that the effort was paying off.

Ali showed as much by speaking Mandarin and citing a line of Chinese poetry about climbing higher to see farther. "It's like my daily training. Day after day, reaching new heights," he said.

Switching to English, Ali said he hoped cooperation would grow from one astronaut on one flight to multiple joint missions and eventually to the Moon, Mars and beyond.

China is preparing to land astronauts on the Moon by 2030. The United States is widely seen as its main rival, with NASA aiming to put astronauts on the lunar surface in 2028.
