Free FEAST holiday clubs with a meal run from 21 December in Harrogate, Knaresborough, Ripon, Boroughbridge and Masham. Bookings opened on 23 November.

Thirteen free FEAST holiday activities are listed for the Harrogate district this Christmas, from sports camps in Harrogate and Knaresborough to a pantomime in Masham. Each one includes a healthy meal. Sessions start on Monday 21 December.

Bookings went live on Monday 23 November, and providers start confirming places from today, Wednesday 25 November, North Yorkshire Council said.

## Overview

* **What:** FEAST, North Yorkshire’s Department for Education funded holiday activities and food programme.
* **Who gets a free place:** children from Reception to Year 11 on targeted free school meals. Many families on Universal Credit do not qualify (see below).
* **When:** from Monday 21 December, with some sessions running into the first days of January.
* **In this district:** 13 activities in Harrogate, Knaresborough, Ripon, Boroughbridge and Masham.
* **How to book:** with the 16-digit voucher your school sends, at HolidayActivities, or by phoning the provider.

## Who can get a free place

This is the part that catches families out. From this autumn, every child in a household on Universal Credit can get free school meals in term time. The government has not extended free FEAST places to all of them, North Yorkshire Together explains.

There are now two kinds of benefits-related free school meals:

* **Targeted free school meals:** for households on Universal Credit earning £7,400 a year or less from work, not counting the Universal Credit itself. Families on income-related Employment and Support Allowance or the guarantee element of Pension Credit also qualify. These children get free FEAST places.
* **Expanded free school meals:** for households on Universal Credit earning more than £7,400 from work. These children get term-time meals but no automatic FEAST place.

Free meals for every child in Reception, Year 1 and Year 2 do not count on their own.

The protection that kept some children on vouchers under older rules has also ended. A child who had FEAST vouchers before may not be eligible now.

## What it means for you

* **If your child is eligible,** the school normally sends the voucher by email or text through HolidayActivities. No voucher yet? Ask the school first.
* **If you cannot book online,** phone the provider with the 16-digit voucher number. Providers should take a booking from an eligible family who does not yet have the voucher, the listing says.
* **If your child is not eligible,** many providers also sell paid places. Ask the provider directly.
* **Schools can add a limited number of children** facing serious financial hardship. Speak to the school privately.
* **Home-educated children,** or children who live in North Yorkshire but go to school elsewhere, can email [hello@northyorkshiretogether.co.uk.](mailto:hello@northyorkshiretogether.co.uk.) The team works part time, on weekdays only.

## Five years of FEAST

FEAST has supported 39,794 children and young people since it began five years ago, and served more than 200,000 free meals, the council said. More than 40 providers will run about 80 sessions across the county this Christmas.

One of them last summer was Art Camp, where nine-year-old Jaxon Pitt from Harrogate tried 3D printing and film-making. “I just love everything about Art Camp. We do fun things that I wouldn’t get to do at home,” he told the council.

Heather Phillips, the council’s executive member for corporate services, said many families “are still feeling the impact of rising household costs, particularly during the winter months”.
