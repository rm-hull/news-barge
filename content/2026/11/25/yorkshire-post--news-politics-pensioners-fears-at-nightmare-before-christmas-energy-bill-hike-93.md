# Pensioners fear wrapping themselves in blankets after ‘nightmare before Christmas’ energy bill announcement

## Pensioners have warned they will have to wrap themselves in blankets this winter amid the “nightmare before Christmas” announcement that energy bills will rise by £433 next year.

Industry regulator Ofgem revealed this morning that bills are to increase by 26 per cent from January 1 after the conflict in the Middle East drove a spike in gas prices.

It revealed that the price cap will rise by £443 per year – or £36.91 per month – to £2,166 for the average household, using both electricity and gas if this level was sustained for a year.

Experts have said bills are also unlikely to come down quickly in the following months, with early forecasts suggesting that prices will only be marginally lower in the following quarter.

Both Age UK and the palliative care and bereavement charity Sue Ryder called for targeted energy support for those who need it most in the coming months.

Age UK said its own analysis suggested around a fifth of pensioner households in Great Britain are likely to be “in fuel stress” this winter, meaning they will spend more than 10% of their after-tax household income on energy costs.

When it comes to the poorest households – in the bottom 20% of the income distribution – almost half are expected to be in this position.

The charity said the average pensioner household is expected to spend £1 in every £14 on gas and electricity at the forecast prices.

Age UK charity director Caroline Abrahams said the latest price rise “will be deeply worrying for many older people who are already anxious about how they will afford to keep warm this winter”.

While welcoming the £100 payment to households from Government towards their energy bills from January, she said it is “simply not enough”.

She added: “Another increase in energy bills risks pushing more households into hardship at a time when keeping warm is not a luxury, but an essential part of protecting their health.

“Our recent analysis suggests that this winter is set to be extremely challenging for pensioners who are struggling to afford the energy they need. That’s why we believe ministers should introduce an emergency energy support package targeted at those households most at risk of cold homes, financial hardship and worsening health this winter.”

Most households are expected to see higher bills from January even after support measures announced by the Government at the autumn Budget.

The Yorkshire Post previously revealed how the outdated council tax system means the £100 for Band A to D properties will likely disproportionately benefit people in London and the South East over the North.

Council tax bands were set in 1991, so areas which have seen rapid house price growth since then will still be in lower bands compared to the rest of the country.

Prime Minister Andy Burnham said he recognised that the new energy price cap from next year will “cause concern to many people”.

He told the House of Commons: “I do accept that there is longer-term reform needed of the energy market, and we will set that out in the plan for Britain to be published next month.”

He said the Government has taken action with the £100 support payment as well as the removal of VAT from energy bills, which will give people “some breathing space”, but added: “I accept there is more to do.”
