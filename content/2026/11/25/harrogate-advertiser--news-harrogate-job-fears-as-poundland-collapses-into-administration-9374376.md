Harrogate workers are pondering their future after Poundland collapsed into administration just weeks before Christmas.

The disappointing development puts around 10,500 to 12,000 jobs at approximately 600 UK and Irish stores at risk after the budget store chain's US owner, Gordon Brothers, rejected a series of rescue bids.

Despite a nervous last 13 months, until now, Poundland store in Harrogate had escaped the threat of closure and has continued to trade on the ground floor of Victoria Shopping Centre.

Now comes the news that Harrogate staff members - and Poundland customers - have feared during more than a year of speculation and worry.

It had been hoped that the discount retailer, which was facing down a midnight deadline giving it legal protection from creditors, could secure a solvent sale amid government pressure on Gordon Brothers to ‘do the right thing’.

But Poundland has now formally entered administration just weeks before Christmas.

Poundland's American owners held talks with several bidders over a last-minute rescue deal for the business, but no transaction was reached.

This led to Poundland's boss filing for administration, with consultancy Alix Partners appointed to oversee the process.

The US investment firm, which bought Poundland for £1 little more than a year ago, is now expected to face public and political scrutiny over whether it did enough to prevent the collapse.

The news also, potentially, leaves another empty unit in Harrogate’s flagship shopping centre.

It may still not be the end of the story, however.

Molly Monks, personal finance and insolvency expert at Parker Walsh, a UK business recovery and insolvency practice, said: “Administration does not automatically mean every employee is made redundant immediately.

“The administrators will initially assess the business, its finances and whether any parts can be sold or rescued.

“Some employees may continue working while that process takes place, but there is inevitably a very real risk of redundancies if stores close or parts of the business cannot be sold.”
