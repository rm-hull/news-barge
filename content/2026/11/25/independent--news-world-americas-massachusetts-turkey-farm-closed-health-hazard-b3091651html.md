A Massachusetts turkey farm has been forced to close just days before Thanksgiving after health inspectors uncovered unsanitary conditions and potentially unsafe poultry-handling practices.

Belwing Turkey Farm in Seekonk was ordered to suspend operations Monday after the town's Board of Health revoked its permit over multiple violations of state sanitary codes, the agency announced Tuesday.

Inspectors found open turkey packages leaking onto the grass, discarded packaging in a dumpster and what appeared to be repackaged turkeys inside a cooler, CBS News reports.

They also found plastic wrap and a heat gun, suggesting workers may have been trying to repackage the meat. No live turkeys were found at the farm.

Inside the processing shed, inspectors reported excessive flies and tables that were "in a condition not suitable for food contact." The farm was also cited for processing poultry without the required permits and failing to demonstrate proper food-handling knowledge.

![Belwing Turkey Farm in Massachusetts was ordered to shut down after inspectors found multiple sanitary violations](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/25/17/2238603133..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Belwing Turkey Farm announced the shutdown in a Facebook post Tuesday, with comments restricted.

"We have very bad news, the Seekonk board of health has shut us down in our 82nd year and we will not be able to sell turkeys Tuesday and Wednesday for the 2026 Thanksgiving season," farm officials wrote.

"This message is to our dedicated and valued customers who we are actively calling to notify you to make other arrangements for your turkey this year. We apologize for this disruption in your holiday season," the post continued.

The latest violations follow earlier concerns about the farm's food-handling practices. During a January Board of Health meeting, Assistant Health Agent Ryan Daw reported finding turkeys stored at room temperature, along with pests and a foul smell inside a farm shed.

Daw said the conditions were "the worst he has seen in 25 years in the industry," according to meeting minutes cited by CBS News.
