Ukrainian forces have damaged five aircraft during a strike on a major Russian military air base, Ukraine's General Staff reported on Wednesday.

The long-range attack targeted the Savasleyka air base in the Nizhny Novgorod region, east of Moscow, as part of efforts to cripple Moscow's war effort.

Three MiG-31 jets – used to fire hypersonic missiles – and two Su-34 fighter-bombers were knocked out of operation "for some time", the General Staff said.

Russia did not immediately comment on the report.

The operation follows a series of recent high-profile strikes by Kyiv's forces, including attacks on two Russian ships in Libyan waters and another vessel in the Gulf of Finland.

On Wednesday, the US Department of State approved a possible sale to Ukraine of F-16 aircraft sustainment and related equipment for an estimated $621.9 million.

It added that principal contractor for the sale would be selected through procurements conducted by the US government.

Meanwhile, a Russian overnight attack on Ukraine's power sector damaged energy and gas infrastructure in four Ukrainian regions, prime minister Sergiy Koretskyi said on Wednesday.

"The most difficult situation is in Zaporizhia, where all attention is now," Koretskyi said on Telegram.

Last week, consecutive attacks destroyed the road portion of Russia's bridge to the Crimea peninsula.

All train and vehicle traffic on the Crimean Bridge was suspended while specialists assessed the damage, Russia’s ministry of transport reported.

Fighting has continued since Russia launched its full-scale invasion in February 2022, despite a renewed US-backed peace push to resolve Europe's worst conflict since World War Two.

Kyiv is attempting to dent Russian military strength as it faces a renewed campaign of attacks against its own energy grid and frontline cities.

Defence Minister Yevhenii Khmara said this week that Ukrainian troops have targeted 16 Russian ballistic-missile launchers over the past several months.

Industry sources also noted this month that renewed Ukrainian drone attacks have reduced processing volumes at Russian oil refineries by 45% since late October.

Kyiv has stated it will stop striking Russian energy targets only when Moscow does the same.
