Drivers over the age of 70 will face regular eye tests in new proposals to be announced by the government within weeks, according to reports.

Under the new regulations, pensioners over a certain age will be tested on their eyesight every three years or face losing their license.

Their results will then be shared with the DVLA who will be able to revoke their licenses if they do not meet the legal standard, sources told the *Daily Mail.*

The new rules have been in discussions for months and cognitive tests for older drivers have also been considered under the government’s Road Safety Strategy.

![Drivers over the age of 70 will be reported to the DVLA if they fail to meet the legal standard of sight, according to reports](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/08/04/08/01KQ7CVJQEFSKH6V5977X5FJZB.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“We recognise the importance of driving for older people’s independence but need to balance this with our priority to keep everyone safe on our roads and avoid tragic accidents,” a spokesperson for the Department For Transport (DfT) told *The Independent* on Wednesday.

“Mandatory eye tests for drivers over 70 as part of our Road Safety Strategy are one way to do this. We are carefully reviewing consultation results.

“Older drivers who are fit to drive and have good enough vision have nothing to worry about, and hope to set out next steps soon.”

About 24 per cent of all car drivers killed in 2024 were 70 or older, and 12 per cent of casualties in car collisions involved older drivers.

![Drivers over the age of 70 will be required to meet a legal standard for sight or face losing their license (PA)](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/04/20/13/594ffc9567934e738ce2dcac11bf4f83Y29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzc2Nzc1MDY3-2.12288906.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

A government source told the *Mail:* “The fact is, there have been several arguably preventable fatalities on Britain's roads caused by drivers with failing eyesight in recent years.

“Beefed-up measures are long overdue – something which coroners and other public officials have long been calling for.”

There are nearly six million drivers over the age of 70, according to official figures.

Ministers had begun to discuss the options in January but have been reviewing the results of the consultation since May, according to the publication.

Edmund King, the president of the AA had previously said of the suggestion: “A move to make eye tests for older drivers mandatory is obviously welcome, especially as the likelihood of crashes increases once someone is over the age of 70, and markedly shoots up over the age of 80 to a peak at age 86, with eyesight often a worrying factor.

“Eye tests are free for people over the age of 60 anyway and healthcare professionals advise them every two years, given they can help with diagnosis of other underlying conditions.”

At the time of the discussions in January, the then-local transport minister Lilian Greenwood had said: “We know driving can be very important for older people’s wellbeing and help them to live independently, but we must also make sure everyone is safe on our roads.

“As the country’s older population grows, our plans in the first road safety strategy in more than a decade will preserve personal freedoms where possible with action to save lives.”

Other proposals that had been floated as part of the move to improve road safety included tightening drink-driving rules by reducing the limit from 35 micrograms of alcohol per 100ml of breath to 22 micrograms, according to reports in *The Times* in January.
