One of the leading defenders of Rotary Wood in the nine-year long battle over Harrogate Spring Water’s expansion plans has laid out the timetable for what could be the 'final showdown' between the iconic water brand and its opponents.

Pinewoods Conservation Group said that the formal appeal by the multi-national owners of the official water of Royal Ascot and Ascot Racecourse against the refusal of its proposed factory extension into Rotary Wood will be held in February by the national Planning Inspectorate.

Writing in their latest newsletter to members, the charity said the appeal was likely to be heard at the council offices at Harrogate Civic Centre and could last up to six days.

Pinewoods Conservation Group welcomed news that North Yorkshire Council has now engaged external consultants to represent it at the appeal, defending the two reasons for the refusal concerning landscaping and design.

The Harrogate-based charity, which has sought to protect Harrogate’s famous woodland and its wildlife since 2002 , is liaising with the Planning Inspectorate over whether to take up the opportunity of becoming a formal party to the appeal as “an interested party”.

This would allow it to present evidence and question the evidence put forward by others.

Harrogate Spring Water lodged an appeal on the basis that the reasons for refusal given by members of North Yorkshire Council's Harrogate and Knaresborough Area Committee did not justify departing from council officers’ recommendation to approve the scheme.

As a major employer in a growing market, the company argues it is essential to meet growing demand in a way that will boost the town’s economy, while being mindful of the environment.

Harrogate Spring Water’s Managing director Richard Hall said: “We have done everything we can to understand genuine local community concerns, mitigate environmental impact and invest for sustainable development in the future, benefitting the local economy of Harrogate.”

Taken over by the French-based multinational Danone in 2020, the company’s expansion plans include creating a new publicly accessible two-acre woodland next to Rotary Wood on land it has secured for the community.

If it wins its keenly-contested planning battle, Harrogate Spring Water says construction work on the site at Harlow Moor Road could begin as early as 2028 - if its appeal succeeds.

Pinewoods Conservation Group also welcomed news that North Yorkshire Council has engaged external consultants to represent it at next year’s appeal to defend the two reasons for refusal concerning landscaping and design.

The charity believes that professional support for the community’s own participation is essential and that campaigners against Harrogate Spring Water need to coordinate their evidence at February’s appeal so that the community’s case can be presented clearly and effectively.

Pinewoods Conservation Group is calling for people to donate to the campaign through Save Rotary Wood’s Crowdfunding page here

The fund will support specialist legal advice and representation to help defend Rotary Wood.

Save Rotary Wood is also gathering personal accounts of how people use and value the woodland.

A community update meeting hosted by Save Rotary Wood will take place this Sunday, November 29, 6.30-7.30pm, at Friends Meeting House, 12A Queen Parade, Harrogate HG1 5PP, to discuss progress, the appeal process and supporters’ views.
