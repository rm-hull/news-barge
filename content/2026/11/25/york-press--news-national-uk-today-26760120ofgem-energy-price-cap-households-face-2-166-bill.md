Household energy bills are set to soar with a 26% rise in January, adding almost £37 a month to the typical bill as the impact of conflict in the Middle East pushes up costs, in what experts are describing as a 'very serious situation'.

Ofgem has confirmed its energy price cap will increase by £443 a year, taking the annual figure for a typical household using gas and electricity to £2,166 from January 1.

The increase applies to households in England, Scotland and Wales on standard variable tariffs and represents the highest level of the quarterly cap since the second quarter of 2023.

Richard Neudegg, director of regulation at Uswitch.com , urged: “This is a very serious situation. Households need to act now to avoid the worst of this winter, as gas rates head for their highest ever level.

“This is no longer the time to wait and see. If you’re on a standard tariff, you will be exposed to the full increase and need to act before January to limit the damage."

He added: “Switching to a well-priced fixed deal is the only effective way to avoid paying these rates. The cheapest deal could save an average of £412 a year against January’s levels, but bills vary depending on where you live and how much you use, so it’s vital to run a comparison to find the right option for your home.

“We know households are already paying closer attention to their energy usage, and there are free tools available to help make this easier. When rates are high, small changes make even more of a difference.

“If you’re worried about paying your bills, reach out to your supplier as early as possible, as they can advise on payment plans, as well as any grants and schemes that may be available to you.”

## Why has it risen so much?

The new cap is in line with forecasts from industry analysts and comes after escalating violence in the Middle East sparked a fresh rise in wholesale oil and gas prices during October.

Although wholesale prices have fallen back in recent weeks, the drop came too late to feed through into household bills for the next quarter.

Neil Kenward, Ofgem’s director general for markets, said: “Conflict in the Middle East continues to drive energy costs higher, and we know this price increase will be a worry for many households as winter approaches.

“We urge anyone who is struggling to afford their energy bills to reach out to their supplier, who must offer appropriate support and advice – we are writing to suppliers today to remind them of their obligations and ensure they are ready to provide that support.”

The announcement has prompted calls for greater investment in domestic energy supplies, with ministers acknowledging the impact the increase will have on household finances.

## What help is there?

Energy Secretary Miatta Fahnbulleh acknowledged the pressure another rise would put on households.

She said: “I know people are deeply concerned by another rise in energy bills.

“For too long, British families have been paying the price for conflicts overseas and volatile fossil fuel markets.

“To help this winter, we’re stepping in to give families some breathing space, so from January, the majority of households will get £100 towards their energy costs.

“But there is more to do to take the pressure off families and bring bills down for good.

“That means more cheap, clean, homegrown power so we’re less reliant on expensive fossil fuels, and reforming our broken energy system so it’s fairer for British people.”

## Who will get the £100 energy payment?

The Government has announced a £100 energy payment for around 20 million households as part of its Budget support package.

In England, the payment is aimed at households in council tax bands A, B, C and D. It is expected to be distributed through the council tax system from January 2027.

Martin Lewis has said households are most likely to receive the money directly into their bank accounts, although the precise payment arrangements are still to be confirmed.

The scheme is expected to cost £2.6billion, with funding also being provided to devolved administrations so they can introduce similar support in Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland.

The £100 is intended to help with energy costs over the winter, but it will not prevent the January price cap increase or automatically reduce a household's energy tariff.

## What can we do longer term?

Equalities Minister Bridget Phillipson said she recognised the 26% rise would cause “real worry” for families.

Speaking to Times Radio, she said: “I think it underlines how important it is that we move away from the exposure that we have when things happen across the Middle East and in other parts of the world that then have a knock-on effect for families here.

“We’ve got to be less exposed in the future. We need to invest more in cheaper, homegrown energy, so that when those events do occur, families here are less exposed to those price fluctuations.”
