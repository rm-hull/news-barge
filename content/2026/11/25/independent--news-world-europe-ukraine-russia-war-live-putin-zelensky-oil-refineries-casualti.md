President Vladimir Putin has warned that retaliation is “inevitable” after Ukraine continued to hit Russian oil plants despite Donald Trump’s requests not to do so.

Ukrainian drones struck two more major facilities overnight into Wednesday, with local residents reporting large fires at refineries in the Samara region some 800km inside Russia.

Putin told a meeting of government officials and oil executives at the Kremlin on Tuesday that Ukraine “continues to attack our refining facilities, despite the US president's appeal and direct request to refrain from such attacks”.

“But the Kyiv authorities are ignoring this advice,” he said, adding that: “In this regard, I would like to stress that retaliatory measures on our part are inevitable.”

Ukraine’s foreign minister Andrii Sybiha said Putin could end the attacks on refineries by calling an end to the ongoing conflict.

“If Putin doesn't like Ukraine's strikes on his oil refineries, he can order an end to the war. If he doesn't want his military vessels hit, he can order an end to the war,” he wrote on X.

Russian strikes on Ukraine overnight killed one person in Kyiv and two in the Odesa region to the south.

## More than 1,000 Russian soldiers have died by suicide since war began, leaked report says

More than 1,000 Russian soldiers have taken their own lives since the invasion of Ukraine, according to a new report.

At least 1,087 military personnel ended their own life in Ukraine or in Russian border regions between 2022 and 2026, according to confidential data held by state statistics agency Rosstat and seen by *The Guardian*.

Leaked data from a corpus of 11 million death certificates shows that the trend has steadily increased since the full-scale invasion, with war and military-related deaths rising from more than 50 in 2022 to 365 last year.

## Russian attack kills one in Kyiv region

A Russian attack has killed a man near Bila Tserkva in Ukraine’s Kyiv region, Tymur Tkachenko said on Telegram.

A commercial facility was also damaged in the attack, he said.

Bila Tserkva, a city of around 200,000 people, lies some 64km (40 miles) from the outskirts of the Ukrainian capital.

## Drone attack kills one person in Russia's Samara region

A drone attack has killed one person, triggered power cuts and caused damage on the ground in Russia’s Samara region, Russian news agencies reported on Wednesday, citing regional governor Vyacheslav Fedorishchev.

The Samara region is a major oil refining hub for Russian state oil company Rosneft.

It was not immediately clear whether any oil facilities had been damaged in the attack.

## Ukraine launches drone strikes on two major Russian oil refineries

Fires have been reported at two major Russian oil refineries in the Samara region, some 800km from the border with Ukraine, following apparent Ukrainian drone strikes.

The monitoring channel Exilenova Plus named the refineries involved as the Syzran and Kuibyshev facilities, according to the Kyiv Independent, with local residents sharing footage of a large fire at the latter.

Earlier, Vladimir Putin threatened to retaliate against Ukraine's long-range strikes on its oil infrastructure, saying such attacks continued despite repeated requests from US president Vladimir Putin for them to stop.

Kyiv says the attacks are a valid response to Russia's 2022 invasion, and designed to hinder Moscow's ability to fund its war.

## Why Russia’s war on Ukraine may reach a ‘tipping point’ next year

Russia’s full-scale invasion against Ukraine may reach a “tipping point” next year that sees Moscow defeated, claims Latvian President Edgars Rinkevics.

While in New York to chair the United Nations Security Council, the politician argued that Russia is not genuinely interested in ending the war and likely viewed fresh negotiations on a ceasefire as a “decoy” to continue its aggression.

Meanwhile, Ukraine braces for a “perfect storm” of a brutal winter, increased attacks against energy infrastructure and escalating strikes against civilian targets.

## Russian strike on Ukraine kills two and wounds children

A Russian nighttime attack on the southern Ukrainian city of Zaporizhzhia killed two civilians and wounded 44 others, including four children, officials said Tuesday, as officials in Ukraine and Libya debated the legitimacy of an unprecedented Ukrainian strike on a Russian warship docked in Benghazi.

The Zaporizhzhia attack destroyed a private house and partially destroyed a five-story apartment building, according to Mykhailo Semikin, the head of the Zaporizhzhia Regional Military Administration. The youngest wounded child was less than a year old, he said, adding that the others were ages 4, 6 and 16.

The Ukrainian military is sending reinforcements to the Zaporizhzhia region, President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said Tuesday, which lies on the roughly 1,250-kilometer (almost 800-mile) front line snaking through eastern and southern Ukraine.

## Comment: Ukraine is taking its war against Russia far beyond the battlefield – and the West is worried

Blown up by a Ukrainian attack, the Russian warship Merkury, listed, capsized and sank in Libya’s Benghazi port, where only its bow is now visible in a strike Volodymyr Zelensky called an “exemplary operation” and which Libya’s government condemned.

It is the latest in an expanding number of tightly targeted Ukrainian operations, mostly at sea, involving Kyiv special forces including Spain, Malta, Turkey, Italy, Senegal, Mali, Niger, Sudan, Greece and Syria and the Baltics.

Largely unnoticed, until the latest assaults on ships off the coast of Finland and Libya, Ukraine’s secret units have been fighting a covert war against Russia, and Moscow’s mercenary forces in what was the Wagner group, since 2022.

## Norway and Ukraine to expand cooperation on anti-drone system

Norway and Ukraine will expand their cooperation on a newly developed anti-drone air defence system, the Norwegian defence ministry has said in a statement on Tuesday.

“Today, we discussed with Norway further scaling up the use of this system,” Ukrainian defence minister Yevhenii Khmara said. “Joint production is an example of how Ukrainian combat experience and expertise combine with partners' technologies and deliver concrete results.”

## Russia summons Latvia's envoy in Moscow over layoffs at Russian Embassy

Russia's Foreign Ministry said on Tuesday that it had summoned Latvia's envoy to protest against a Latvian order to terminate the employment of local staff at the Russian Embassy in Riga.

The ministry said this made it impossible to pay Russian military pensions to Latvian residents.
