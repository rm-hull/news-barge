# 'I had to make my own costumes': Sheffield circus performer Sue La La becomes wow-factor fashion designer

## As she prepares to walk tall at Sheffield’s Kelham Island Victorian Fair, circus performer Sue Fee tells how she creates costumes with the wow factor. Yorkshire Post Fashion Editor Stephanie Smith went to meet the Sheffield maker behind Sue La La Design.

Victorian stilt-walking costumes are not easy to find. As for sparkly aerial performance leotards that fit like a glove? Even harder to find. So circus performance artist Sue Fee – AKA Sue La La – decided to start making them for herself.

If you are a performer you need costumes,” she says. “It was an absolute nightmare to find them due to the expense. So I thought, okay, I'll have to learn how to make my own.”

Sue started making her own clothes on an old Singer sewing machine she found sitting on a wall near her then home in Broomhall, Sheffield. “I turned the handle and it worked,” she says. “So I shouted to the people in the garden, ‘Do you want that?’”

They didn't so she picked it up and carried it home. “It didn't even have a reverse, it didn't zigzag. I made a fairytale princess dress for going out partying. It took me three weeks but I was very proud of my dress.”

Fast-forward two decades and now Sue is an experienced and imaginative costume maker who enjoys the challenge of making fabulous, fantastical and often super-sparkly bespoke and ready-to-wear clothes, leotards, catsuits and performance wear for a range of different artistes, as well as people who want costumes or items for events and parties.

She makes costumes for circus artists such as aerial acts (including those working with rope, silks, static trapeze, web spin, swinging trapeze and aerial hoop); ground acts (stilt characters, fire dancer, roller skaters, living statues, acrobats) and party people. She even has some delightfully fun bustles to razzle up almost any outfit.

To master the craft of pattern cutting and garment construction, Sue trained at Sheffield College, earning an HND in Fashion Design (2008) and the expert ability to bring her creative visions to life in fabric. Once made, her designs take on a life of their own, travelling the globe for adventures and entertainment purposes, appearing at many circuses and festivals.

As Sue La La, Sue is also a circus performer and teacher with more than 20 years’ experience, creating spectacular entertainment that combines daring feats with elegant choreography - and, of course, those fabulous costumes.

She teaches aerial circus at Greentop Community Circus Centre at St Thomas building, a former church on Holywell Rd, Sheffield, a circus school founded in 1995 by Swamp circus and fitted out on the TV programme Challenge Anneka.

“We teach classes, kids, adults, all levels, and also private lessons,” she says. “That's where I go and train and develop new material for either use in my own routines or for teaching students.

“People see it on YouTube, on Insta, on TikTok,” she says. “They see people doing it and they think ‘that looks fun’. We have lots of regular students at Greentop, and it's great watching them develop.”

Originally from Southport, Sue is not from a circus or theatrical family, although her mum did sing, play the piano and appear in musicals when young.

Sue came to Sheffield to study Geology at Sheffield University, graduating in 1996. “I always liked being outdoors, so Sheffield's perfect, because you've got city and countryside.

“I've always been interested in landscapes and prefer to be outdoors whenever I can. To understand land formation processes you need to be able to think in 3D. This is a highly transferrable skill to pattern cutting, where you are taking the 3D form of the body and translating it into a 2D pattern.”

After graduating, she travelled to India and Nepal, where she saw fire eating and fire dancing, and started doing that, and then headed to Australia, where she went to a circus festival, saw aerial trapeze and rope performance, and was instantly hooked. “I wanted to be up there,” she says.

On her return to Sheffield in 2002, she discovered Greentop, right on her doorstep. People go there to learn aerial performance skills for many different reasons. “It's obviously very physical but also very mental,” says Sue. “People come to be challenged, they want an interesting hobby, they like the community, getting together and trying to puzzle out these things together.

“We do circus mini, for three to seven years old. And then we have the regular classes age eight to 18, and then adult classes from 18 to any age. We have students in their sixties.”

Sue is also a stilt walker, and makes her own tall costumes, and for others. Next weekend, she will be wearing her Victorian lady costume at the Kelham Island Museum Victorian Christmas Market.

“With crinoline ones, you can't actually see that far in front of you, so you have to kind of remember what the pavement was like underneath you,” she says.

Making such a costume costs from £350 (her own costume took nine metres of material alone), while custom-made leotards cost £300-£450. She buys fabrics from Fine Fabrics in Sheffield and Walton’s of Goldthorpe, and often sends off toiles to ensure the fit works, if clients order online and cannot come for fittings.

Now Sue plans to concentrate more of her time on her costume making. She makes at her house in Sheffield but hopes to have a studio so she can expand on her Etsy shop and private commissions. Earlier this year, she made a corset for the Corset Revival Project, and is now working on a corset commission, inspired by lakes, ponds, insects and dragonflies. “I can be as experimental as I like and I get to use all kinds of embellishment techniques because this is a garment that won't be laundered frequently. Much of my performance wear is designed so it can deal with repeated washing.”

Not that she is giving up aerial performance just yet. “I think it really neatly satisfies both those sides of my personality, the one that wants to be challenged and physical and grow but also the one that wants to wear the sequins and be glamorous.”
