Financial commentator and stockbroker Peter Schiff took aim at Donald Trump's trade policies Friday, calling him "the biggest loser of any president" as the U.S. goods trade deficit continues to widen despite the president’s aggressive tariffs.

Schiff's comments came after the goods trade deficit jumped 10.7 percent to $149.5 billion in October, according to the Census Bureau's latest advance report. For this year, imports totaled $365.3 billion, while exports came in at just $215.8 billion.

The gap was more than double what it was in October 2025.

“It was the third-largest monthly deficit in history, exceeded only by January and March of 2025, when importers rushed to avoid tariffs,” Schiff said. “On trade, Trump is the biggest loser of any president,” he wroteon X.

The deficit has also climbed steadily in recent months, rising from $118.9 billion in July.

![Financial commentator Peter Schiff blasted President Donald Trump's trade policies as the US goods trade deficit surged 10.7 percent to $149.5 billion in October](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/27/20/2306499377..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The latest numbers come as Trump continues to defend his tariffs, which have been a major part of his economic agenda during his second term.

Trump previously pointed to a 55 percent drop in the trade deficit in April 2026 as a sign that his tariffs were working. He celebrated the numbers on Truth Social, writing, “THANK YOU MR. TARIFF!”

But the deficit has since climbed again, putting Trump's claims about the success of his trade policies under fresh scrutiny.

Trump has continued pushing new tariffs despite mounting political and legal challenges. On Wednesday, his administration announced proposed import taxes on goods from China, the European Union and 12 other trading partners.

The administration says the tariffs are meant to stop foreign countries from making too many goods and selling them cheaply, arguing that government subsidies and other trade practices give overseas companies an unfair advantage over U.S. manufacturers.

![Tariffs have become the signature part of Trump’s economic agenda](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/27/21/download..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

China could face tariffs of up to 20 percent, while the proposed rates for other trading partners range from 15 percent to 35 percent, depending on the country. The measures are not final, and the administration is accepting public comments before taking further action.

The latest proposals also come after the Supreme Court struck down Trump's sweeping 2025 tariffs earlier this year, forcing the administration to pursue a different legal path to impose import taxes.

Schiff, who serves as chief economist and global strategist at Euro Pacific Asset Management and hosts “The Peter Schiff Show,” an investing podcast, is no stranger to criticizing Trump's economic policies.

The stockbroker also clashed with Trump last year after appearing on Fox News. Trump later called Schiff a “Trump-hating loser who has already proven to be wrong.”
