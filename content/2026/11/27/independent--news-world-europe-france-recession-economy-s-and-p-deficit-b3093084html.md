France has technically dipped into recession, official data has shown.

The country’s economy contracted more ⁠than initially thought in the third quarter of 2026, underscoring the difficulty of reducing the deficit and putting the ‌government's growth ​goal for this year of 0.5% all but ‌out of reach.

It comes as S&P revised France's credit rating outlook to "negative" from "stable" on Friday, citing growing budget pressures as weak economic growth and higher interest costs make it harder to reduce the deficit.

The ratings agency affirmed the country's sovereign rating at "A+/A-1", but said it expected larger fiscal deficits this year and next than previously forecast.

"Continued political polarization, including beyond the 2027 presidential election, could continue constraining the implementation of deficit-reducing measures and growth-enhancing reforms," S&P said in a statement.

![The minority government in France is seeking to pass its 2027 budget, which aims to cut the fiscal deficit from 5.4% of output this year to 5.0% next year](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/23/07/2300858104..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

France's benchmark 10-year borrowing ⁠costs rose to more than 5.14% last month, their highest since ​2002, ⁠and climbed more sharply than those ‌of most peers.

The rise reflected concerns about the country's ability to rein in its deficit, secure passage of the 2027 ‌budget and contain social unrest linked to ‌the cost of living.

The minority government is seeking to pass its 2027 budget, which aims to cut the fiscal deficit from 5.4% of output this year to 5.0% next year, in France's deeply divided parliament five months from a presidential election.

"(S&P's) this assessment underscores the urgent need to give the country a responsible budget without delay, that restores our public finances by bringing the deficit down to no more than 5%," Finance Minister Roland Lescure said in a statement.

"The French Government remains fully committed to pursuing along a path of fiscal consolidation," he added.

Earlier on Friday, it was revealed that French authorities were investigating whether a former Interior Ministry official leaked confidential information about judicial investigations to Marine Le Pen's far-right National Rally.

Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez summoned Hugues Moutouh, a former general secretary in the ministry, for a meeting Friday. Moutouh now serves as prefect of the Centre-Val de Loire region, representing the central government there.

Nuñez confirmed that a judiciary investigation has been opened, a day after Le Monde newspaper reported that the Paris prosecutor’s office was investigating the suspected leaks.
