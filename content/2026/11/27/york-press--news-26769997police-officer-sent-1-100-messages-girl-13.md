A North Yorkshire Police officer sent more than 1,100 messages to a vulnerable 13-year-old girl, a gross misconduct panel has heard.

The officer, who resigned while under investigation, knew the girl from a club he ran outside of his work as a police officer.

He had the consent of the teenager’s mother to contact the girl to assist with her role at the club but failed to maintain appropriate boundaries, according to a report following the misconduct hearing.

The officer, then 41, whose name was redacted in the report, messaged the girl when he knew she was at school, sent her a photograph of him holding up his middle finger and asked her about consuming alcohol, the panel found.

He also messaged the girl about her romantic relationship with a 16-year-old boy writing: “Don’t worry, I won’t tell your mum”, the report said.

The panel raised concern over the message, saying it “signals the officer is happy to conduct an exchange that is secretive in nature. It is entirely the opposite of what should have occurred.

“A police officer should have been wary about a child of 13 being in a romantic relationship with a 16-year-old.

“It should have triggered a safeguarding concern and at the very least, the officer should have proactively alerted [the girl’s mother], as opposed to telling the child he would actively avoid sharing this information with her mother.”

The panel concluded the officer committed gross misconduct and would have been dismissed had he not resigned from the force. He has been placed on the barred list for policing.

His behaviour “demonstrated that he plainly has no future in the police service”, the panel said.

## Police officer discussed sexual relationships, horror films and alcohol with girl, panel told

The report said the panel was satisfied that at least 1,126 messages were exchanged between the officer and the girl, with 530 messages exchanged in one month alone.

“The officer states he does not remember the volume of messages. The panel reject this,” the report said.

The panel also found the officer “would have known that contact with a vulnerable child without third party oversight would have been extremely unwise”.

“There were many opportunities for the officer to sensitively withdraw during the message exchanges, but this did not happen,” the report added. “He instigated conversations about adult themed matters such as alcohol, tattoos, sexual relationships and horror movies with her.

“The responsibility for maintaining appropriate boundaries in terms of volume and content of contact clearly lay with the officer.”
