British homeowners are delaying decisions to move and buy houses due to rising mortgage rates as property sales across the UK fell by 8 per cent year-on-year.

An estimated 92,310 completed transactions took place last month, representing an 8 per cent drop compared with October 2025, alongside a 2 per cent month-on-month decline.

The figures follow the Bank of England's recent decision to raise its base rate by 0.25 percentage points to 4 per cent. Inflation is also projected to pick up in the coming months as higher energy prices feed through the economy.

Homeowners are set to face further financial pressure, with data from UK Finance indicating that around 2.3 million fixed-rate mortgage deals are due to expire in 2027.

Nick Leeming, chairman of estate agency Jackson-Stops, said: “Whilst the Budget provided greater clarity, November’s interest rate rise gave those contemplating a move further pause for thought.

“As such, we are increasingly seeing a market driven by necessity rather than aspiration, with changing family circumstances and relocation for employment underpinning activity.”

Nathan Emerson, chief executive at property professionals’ body Propertymark, said: “The combination of higher borrowing costs and pressure on household finances can understandably influence decisions, particularly when committing to one of the largest financial transactions people make.”

Mark Harris, chief executive of mortgage broker SPF Private Clients, said: “Higher mortgage rates are reducing affordability and deterring some from taking the plunge.

“However, efforts from lenders to improve criteria and offer higher loan-to-value products, such as Nationwide’s 1% deposit mortgage, should generate some interest.”

Richard Donnell, executive director at Zoopla, said: “Housing transactions are slowing in response to higher mortgage rates since the spring as more households delay decisions to move home.

“This latest data tells us about sales that were agreed prior to the summer.

“The market is proving more resilient than expected as serious sellers and buyers agree sales as fast as a year ago.

“The market is still working well for sellers who price their homes realistically but it is penalising those who are too ambitious on pricing.”

Iain McKenzie, chief executive of The Guild of Property Professionals, said: “Elevated borrowing costs, stubborn inflation and geopolitical uncertainty driving up the cost of everyday living have inevitably taken some of the steam out of the housing market.”

![Experts said the report ‘underlines the importance’ of sellers getting the asking price right (PA)](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/27/10/ec27fe7b003d28af633c68b737f0f890Y29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzk1ODYyMjE2-2.85005107.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Tom Bill, head of UK residential research at Knight Frank, said: “Transaction volumes are the last thing to drop during a housing market downturn, and they are now falling at precisely the time of year when you would expect them to increase ahead of Christmas.”

Ian Futcher, a financial planner at wealth manager Quilter, said: “These figures underline just how subdued activity remains, with the combination of economic uncertainty and higher borrowing costs continuing to take much of the energy out of the market.

“The renewed pressure on interest rates has added another layer of uncertainty, with the war in Iran complicating the inflation outlook and making the future path of borrowing costs harder to predict.

“However, while the Bank of England’s latest rate rise may feel like a fresh blow to prospective buyers, markets had seen the writing on the wall for some time.

“Fixed mortgage pricing is driven more by expectations for future rates than the (Bank of England base rate) today, so lenders had already been adjusting their pricing before the decision.”

Nicky Stevenson, managing director of Fine & Country, said: “For sellers, this underlines the importance of getting the asking price right from the outset.

“Properties that require a price reduction are taking considerably longer to find a buyer, and those who come to market with overly ambitious expectations risk losing valuable momentum.”
