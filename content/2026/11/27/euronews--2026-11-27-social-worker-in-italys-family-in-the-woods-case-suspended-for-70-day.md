## The disciplinary council of Abruzzo's Order of Social Workers has sanctioned Veruska D'Angelo over her handling of the case involving the family living off-grid in Italy.

The social worker who handled the case of the so-called "family in the woods" in Palmoli, in Italy's central Abruzzo region, has been suspended from practising for 70 days.

The regional Order of Social Workers' disciplinary council imposed the suspension on 44-year-old Veruska D'Angelo, finding that her professional conduct had caused "serious harm" to those receiving her services.

The decision follows controversy surrounding the handling of the case of Catherine Birmingham and Nathan Trevallion, a British-Australian couple whose three children were taken into care following a ruling by the juvenile court in L'Aquila, which ordered their placement in a protected facility.

## **Complaints and disciplinary proceedings**

The disciplinary proceedings were launched following complaints from the regional Order of Social Workers' president and the couple's former lawyers.

The allegations against D'Angelo include breaches of professional standards relating to impartiality, respect for diversity and professional confidentiality, particularly in her dealings with the media. Her handling of the professional relationship with the family has also come under scrutiny.

The couple's former lawyers alleged that D'Angelo had displayed a hostile attitude towards the parents and failed to maintain the impartiality required of her role.

The social worker has consistently denied the allegations through her lawyer.

## **Calls for the children to return home**

Lawyer Simone Pillon welcomed the suspension, saying in a statement that the decision confirmed his concerns that the information provided to the juvenile court in L'Aquila had not always been objective and that assessments of the family's suitability had been influenced by ideological considerations.

Pillon called for a review of the handling of the case and for the court to reconsider the children's placement in care.

"Now it is up to the court to acknowledge what has happened and decide on the children's definitive return to their family," he said.

Antonio Marziale, president of the Observatory on Children's Rights, also welcomed the decision, describing it as an important step towards protecting children's rights and preserving family ties.

Marziale stressed the need for impartiality, independence and careful consideration of the views of those involved in child protection cases, while ensuring that legal safeguards are respected.

## **Social worker has 60 days to appeal**

The suspension does not take immediate effect. D'Angelo has 60 days to lodge an appeal with the national disciplinary council.

The written decision will detail which breaches of professional ethics the disciplinary panel found to have occurred.

The sanction concerns D'Angelo's professional conduct and does not automatically affect the L'Aquila juvenile court's rulings on the custody and placement of the three children.
