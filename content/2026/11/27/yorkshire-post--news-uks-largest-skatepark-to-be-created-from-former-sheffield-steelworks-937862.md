# UK’s largest skatepark to be created from former Sheffield steelworks

## A huge former steelworks in Sheffield is to be transformed into a centre aimed at tackling the problem of young people not in education, employment or training (Neets) by creating the UK’s largest skatepark.

The planned national centre for action sports & youth wellbeing will be an expansion of a successful project already running in the city.

Those behind the project say its strategy of bringing educators, employers and elite sport facilities together under one roof is exactly the model those tasked with implementing Alan Milburn’s plan for Neets should be studying.

Last week, former Labour minister Mr Milburn set out a 10-year plan to reduce the number of 16 to 24-year-olds classified as Neet from its current level of around 13 per cent – about a million people – to 5 per cent.

The new centre will be a move and expansion for the Onboard project in the city, which provides a pathway into education and work for teenagers who have had difficulties in mainstream education, using its current skate park in an old paint factory as the “hook”.

Amy Cooper, who set up Onboard more than a decade ago, described how the centre has used skateboarding, BMX and scooter riding to help hundreds of young people, often excluded or in danger of exclusion from school, to re-engage with learning and gain qualifications.

Ms Cooper said: “Every week, we see young people come into our facility and regain their confidence on a skateboard or a bike, and gradually progress into education and work.

![Onboard skate park in Sheffield. Credit: PA](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/jpim-static/image/2026/11/27/13/28/01M8EEE0B039KBHZYJ0SNX06S5.jpg?trim=0,0,0,0&width=640&quality=65)


“This centre means we can do that for thousands more young people and demonstrate what’s possible when young people are given the right opportunity.”

She said: “It was really positive to have the (Milburn) report out, because it was highlighting the things that are taking place here at Onboard, but also with our expansion project that we’re going into as well.”

Ms Cooper said the new facility is due to open in 2028 and will house an alternative provision school for 50-70 pupils, a post-16 employment academy and learning pods that open straight onto the skatepark.

![Amy Cooper at Onboard skate park in Sheffield. Credit: PA](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/jpim-static/image/2026/11/27/13/23/01M8EEE38P78T26MJD7G8SZCYH.jpg?trim=0,0,0,0&width=640&quality=65)


It is being supported by local businesses including Special Melted Products, Sheffield Forgemasters, Forged Solutions and the Advanced Forming Research Centre.

The site was bought for Onboard by Hugh Facey, chairman of the Sheffield wire firm Gripple.

Ms Cooper outlined a project with Sheffield engineering firm Special Melted Products, which saw students going into the plant to see forging operations and range of jobs, but also staff from the company join in with bunny hops on the BMXs at Onboard.

She said: “We’ve got six of the largest industrialists in Sheffield that have come on-board.

“They have come together to create this beautiful zone around what work looks like.”
