# Test home built for scientists to map indoor air pollution

Posted on Friday 27 November 2026

![The INTERIORS facility](https://www.york.ac.uk/media/news-and-events/pressreleases/2026/UoY-INTERIORS-1000.jpg)

While public concern and regulatory frameworks have long focused on outdoor pollution sources such as car emissions, researchers warn that the highest exposure to air pollution occurs indoors, where we spend 80-90% of our time in the UK.

Now, the newly unveiled Wolfson INTERIORS Facility aims to move this conversation forward by studying indoor chemistry in live, full-scale environments rather than in laboratory conditions.

The facility consists of two fully furnished three-bedroom houses built on either side of a central gas-sampling laboratory.

### **Air samples**

While visually identical to standard UK housing, one of the properties has been built to mirror future energy-efficient housing, and the other constructed to typical UK building standards, to allow for direct comparison on current and future housing stock.

A custom-built gas-sampling system extracts air samples across every room in both properties, as well as tracking incoming outdoor air, giving chemists a real-time view of pollutant transport and transformation.

Professor Nicola Carslaw, from the University of York’s Department of Environment and Geography and Scientific Director of the Wolfson INTERIORS Facility, said: “Until now, consumer products, carpets, paints, and building materials have typically been evaluated individually inside small, static laboratory chambers.

“We know, however, that individual product testing fails to reflect actual living environments, where hundreds of compounds mix simultaneously with emissions from people, such as from breath, and there are also large variations in humidity, and temperature.”

“Chemical reactions from gases generated from cooking also contribute to the composition of the air we breathe in our homes. It is these secondary chemical reactions in real environments that form new pollutants that have the potential to be harmful to health, but that we need to know more about in order to create solutions.”

### Chemical spikes

The team aims to track chemical spikes during everyday activities like cooking, frying, and using aerosol cleaning products, as well as measure how paints, adhesives, and green building materials give off gases as they age over months and years.

Professor James Lee, Technical Director of INTERIORS, from the University of York’s Wolfson Atmospheric Centre Laboratories, said: “Chemical reactions from gases generated from cooking also contribute to the composition of the air we breathe in our homes. It is these secondary chemical reactions in real environments that form new pollutants that have the potential to be harmful to health, but that we need to know more about in order to create solutions.”

Researchers will look at how these emitted gases can react within the indoor environment to potentially create novel pollutants. The data collected will allow scientists to create predictive computer models capable of forecasting indoor air risks in future housing designs.

### **House-building targets**

Professor Carslaw said: “This research is crucial as the UK government increases its house-building targets alongside tighter energy efficiency mandates.

“We know that low-ventilation buildings can cut carbon footprints, but they risk trapping indoor pollutants if chemical emissions are not properly managed, so we need to know the best methods for reducing the most harmful of these pollutants.”

In combining atmospheric chemistry, building design, and human behaviour, the researchers aim to provide the evidence required by policymakers, architects, and manufacturers to create new and safe indoor air standards.

## Further information

The Wolfson INTERIORS Facility has been made possible by the Wolfson Foundation, University of York, Dr. Anthony H Wild and the National Centre for Atmospheric Science.

The project partners are Markes International, Native Architects, Reckitt, Ricardo (WSP), and Tincture.
