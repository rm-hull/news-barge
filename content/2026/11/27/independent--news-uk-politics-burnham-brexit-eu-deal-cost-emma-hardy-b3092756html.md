UK officials have admitted that prime minister Andy Burnham’s deal with the EU will cost in the tens of millions, stepping in after a minister suggested the figure could be 10 times higher.

Agreements intended to remove trade barriers, halt most post-Brexit checks on food and drink products, and link UK and EU carbon trading schemes were finalised at last week’s summit in Brussels.

The official text of the accords, published on Thursday, outlines the calculations for annual payments the UK will be required to make to the bloc.

An "operational contribution" that broadly mirrors how member states pay into the bloc’s budget will be determined by UK and EU gross domestic product, alongside a separate "participation fee" set at 4 per cent of that contribution.

The participation fee will fund operational costs associated with running a shared biosecurity area, including IT systems.

The clarification follows an interview with *Times Radio* in which environment minister Emma Hardy put the cost to the UK at the "low hundreds of millions".

![Agreements aimed at removing trade barriers and putting a stop to most post-Brexit checks on food and drink products as well as linking the EU and UK carbon trading schemes were sealed at last week’s Brussels summit](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/26/00/cee8c78b800719a0a36a348d7a7c14ecY29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzk1NzI2Mzc0-2.86550179.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

She said this was “a contribution fee towards the cost of administering the border”.

However, UK officials said the cost will be in “the low tens of millions”.

They said this will be “massively outweighed” by the economic benefits the changes will bring to businesses.

A government spokesperson said: “There is a clear, objective formula for UK contributions set out in the agreement.”

The government estimates the deal will boost the UK economy by up to £17 billion a year by 2040.

Ministers say most British exporters already meet the bloc’s standards, but legislation that will allow the UK to align with EU rules for certain sectors was also introduced this week.

![Environment minister Emma Hardy put the cost to the UK at the ‘low hundreds of millions’ in an interview with Times Radio, which has since been clarified](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/08/27/23/01M1286T2CCBTYW3YT0RG6YQ4G.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Officials said the UK would also be “consulted early” on new rules, while the Bill would give Parliament scrutiny over changes.

In future cases where the EU seeks to impose rules that the UK refuses to accept, it is understood this would ultimately go to an independent tribunal if no agreement can be found.

The food deal also sets out a 10-year exit clause that requires any side who backs out in that time frame to pay costs.

These would be “reasonable and proportionate” costs incurred by the other party to set up border controls – for example the costs of infrastructure, equipment, initial recruitment and training, and an audit would establish how much is owed.

Downing Street said such a clause was “standard practice” and emphasised it applies equally to both sides.
