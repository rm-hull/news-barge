York Civic Trust has unveiled a new Blue Plaque commemorating the former Aldwark Synagogue, an important landmark in the history of York’s Jewish community.

The plaque was unveiled yesterday (Thursday) at 1 Aldwark, the site of the synagogue, which served York’s Jewish community from 1893 until 1975.

More than 60 people attended, including representatives of the Jewish community, York Civic Trust, faith leaders, historians and civic representatives.

The ceremony was led by Rabbi Albert Chait, Senior Rabbi to the United Hebrew Congregation in Leeds, who conducted a short service before unveiling the plaque.

Guests then gathered at Bedern Hall for an event exploring the history of Jewish life in York. Contributions came from the University of York’s Dr Louisa Hampson, York Civic Trust’s Dr Duncan Marks and the Leader of City of York Council, Cllr Claire Douglas.

![Former Aldwark synagogue in York honoured with new Blue Plaque](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/former-aldwark-synagogue-in-york-honoured-with-new-1.webp)

Rabbi Chait said: “When we tell the story of Jewish history, it is important that we not only remember the moments of darkness but also celebrate the generations of light.”

The former synagogue is included on the York Local Heritage List, which recognises its architectural, historical and communal significance.

Dating from the 18th century, for many generations its ground floor was a joiner’s workshop. The upper floor’s use as a synagogue came from a family connection of one of those joiners.

Dr Duncan Marks, planning and heritage manager at York Civic Trust, said: “While modest in appearance compared with some of York’s more prominent historic buildings, the former synagogue’s significance lies in the story of the community that used it and the contribution that community made to the city.

“This is rightly celebrated as being part of York’s Local Heritage List and through this blue plaque.”

York is widely associated with the massacre of its medieval Jewish community in 1190, but the city’s Jewish history extends far beyond that event. The synagogue at Aldwark marks an important chapter in the re-establishment of organised Jewish religious life in York during the 19th century.

The building’s closure in 1975 did not mark the end of Jewish life in the city. The York Liberal Jewish Community held its first service in June 2014 and has since become an active part of York’s religious, cultural and civic life.

It worked closely with York Civic Trust to secure recognition of the former synagogue during the planning process, including an earlier deconsecration ceremony at the building.

The plaque forms part of York Civic Trust’s longstanding Blue Plaque programme, which commemorates over 150 notable people, places and events associated with the city.

It was made possible through funding from the developer of the building, Richard and Esther Marchant.
