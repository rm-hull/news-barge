Spain’s Prime Minister Pedro Sanchez aims to secure a fourth term in Sunday’s snap election, as polling indicates voters may bring eight years of left-wing governance to an end.

Here are key pledges from the three leading parties – Sanchez’s centre-left Socialists (PSOE), the conservative People’s Party and far-right Vox – on the core issues shaping the campaign.

## Housing

Soaring home prices and rents have driven an affordability crisis. The eviction of an elderly tenant in September sparked protests nationwide, prompting Sanchez to call an election.

### PSOE

* Build 100,000 public affordable homes, mobilise public land for affordable housing construction.
* Provide interest-free loans of up to €50,000 for first-home buyers, suspend evictions of vulnerable tenants.

### PP

* Build 1 million homes, new law to mobilise public and private land for housing construction.
* Provide mortgage guarantees for first-time buyers, repeal recently approved Housing Law, which regulates rental prices.

### VOX

* Reform land laws to boost construction.
* Give Spaniards priority access to housing, introduce tax breaks for first-home buyers and evict squatters within 24 hours.

## Migration

The government's pro-immigration stance contrasts with the opposition's calls for tighter border controls, particularly after a mass migrant crossing into the North African enclave of Ceuta in July.

![A large group of migrants crowd outside the entrance of the Loma del Colmenar reception center, waiting for admission, in Ceuta on September 1, 2026](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/27/09/2292516240..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

### PSOE

* Expand legal pathways to live and work in Spain.
* Invest €500 million in immigrant integration and citizenship programmes, strengthen asylum, combat hate crimes.

### PP

* Set annual work visa quotas linked to labour shortages.
* Ban mass regularisation programmes, tighten asylum and deportations, speed up returns, also to third countries.

### VOX

* Strengthen border controls, tighten residence permit requirements.
* End mass regularisation, end public benefits for undocumented migrants, deport convicted foreigners.

## Fiscal Policy

Households are grappling with high living costs and housing prices. PSOE has combined targeted relief with windfall taxes on banks and energy companies, while PP and Vox favour broad tax cuts.

![(From L) far-right party Vox leader Santiago Abascal, Spanish Prime Minister and Spanish Socialist Party (PSOE) leader Pedro Sanchez, Spain's right wing opposition People's Party (PP) leader Alberto Nunez Feijoo and left-wing Frente Amplio candidate Ada Colau](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/27/09/2306448405..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

### PSOE

* Maintain tax exemption for low-income earners, introduce new taxes on large technology companies and luxury high-carbon travel.
* Introduce global minimum tax on large fortunes and stronger international tax cooperation.

### PP

* Cut corporate and income taxes for low- and middle-income earners, index tax brackets to inflation, exempt self-employed earning under €85,000 from VAT.

### VOX

* Cut income, corporate and energy taxes, with additional relief for families.

## Climate and Energy

Parties differ sharply on the green transition, nuclear power and water scarcity driven by climate change. The debate comes amid more frequent wildfires, record-high sea temperatures and scrutiny of data centres.

![Spain's firefighters and residents attempt to extinguish a forest fire near the hamlet of Baltar, close to the town of Xinzo de Limia in the north-west of Spain on September 24, 2026](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/27/09/2296324656..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

### PSOE

* Maintain 2035 nuclear phase-out and accelerate renewables to 81 per cent of power generation by 2030, requiring data centres to use renewable energy.
* Improve drought and flood resilience through desalination, water reuse and irrigation modernisation.

### PP

* Extend lifespan of existing nuclear power plants, scrap new rules for data centres
* Invest in water infrastructure, desalination and irrigation upgrades through national water pact.

### VOX

* Cancel nuclear shutdown plan, build new reactors at existing nuclear sites, curb large-scale solar and wind projects.
* Connect river basins, modernise irrigation, expand water infrastructure under national water pact.

## Foreign Policy

Although international affairs have received less attention than domestic issues, parties differ on multilateralism and ties to key neighbours including Britain, Morocco and Algeria.

## Gender Equality and Women’s Rights

The parties offer markedly different approaches to women's rights, gender violence and reproductive or transgender rights.

![A woman holds a Spanish flag reading 'Defend the life' during a rally against abortion](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/27/09/2278383239..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

### PSOE

* Make abortion constitutional right.
* Expand measures against gender violence, abolish prostitution, strengthen LGBTI+ and trans rights.

### PP

* Pass law that counts unborn children towards eligibility for family benefits.
* Support police, judicial and victim-support resources to combat gender violence, amend current trans law.

### VOX

* Support "right to life", protect conscientious objection against abortion.
* Strengthen police, judicial and support services for violence and sexual assault victims, defend women's sports based on biological sex.
