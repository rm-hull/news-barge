A survivor of the Huntingdon train attack has expressed her gratitude to a fellow traveller who confronted the knifeman, stating she "wouldn’t be here" without his intervention.

During the evening of 1 November last year, 33-year-old Anthony Williams stabbed 10 passengers on an LNER service bound for London. Having boarded at Peterborough, he was arrested after the train driver made an unscheduled halt at Huntingdon.

Astrid, who was 19 at the time and withheld her surname, told the BBC that she owes Stephen Crean her "life".

As the violence broke out, she had rushed down the carriage shouting a warning that a man had a knife.

She recounted Mr Crean moving from her right to shield her from the attacker, before lunging at Williams "in an attempt to take the knife out of his hand".

![Undated handout photo issued by British Transport Police of Anthony Williams who has been found guilty at Cambridge Crown Court of the attempted murders of seven people who he stabbed on a London-bound train that made an unscheduled stop in Huntingdon on November 1 last year. Issue date: Thursday November 26 , 2026.](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/26/15/38/Anthony-Williams.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

In an interview with Mr Crean and the BBC, she said: “Then I was able to shut the door with the rest of the people behind me.

“I have no idea what would have happened if you weren’t there literally in that second.”

She added: “He had a split second to make a decision and I owe him my life because of the decision he made.”

Astrid thanked Mr Crean, and said: “I just owe him everything.

“I wouldn’t be here today without you, and to put your life on the line for someone else, a complete stranger, is just complete bravery.”

Mr Crean, who was returning home from watching a Nottingham Forest game at the time of the incident, remembered running towards the knifeman.

The trial at Cambridge Crown Court was shown footage of Mr Crean confronting Williams, after telling others to shut the door and stay hidden in a cafe bar area of the train, when he was stabbed to his back, hip, fingers and head.

He told Astrid that she should be proud of herself for alerting passengers about the knifeman.

![Anthony Williams brandishing a knife on an LNER Doncaster to London service](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/26/14/01M6PDB86TREMGM53Q6CDVHFMJ.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

As they met for the first time, Mr Crean added: “It means the world to me to meet her. Meeting her was the best thing, it was worth waiting for. I’m just so happy.”

Williams stabbed a boy and attempted to stab two men in Peterborough on the evening of Halloween last year, before travelling to London by train, his trial heard.

Once in the capital, he stabbed a boy on a DLR train in the early hours of November 1, before returning to Peterborough and spending much of the day at home.

On the evening of November 1 he walked from his home to Peterborough station, boarded a London-bound LNER service and stabbed nine passengers and a member of on-board staff, after the high-speed train had departed.

Mr Crean told the BBC that the knifeman “shouldn’t have been allowed to go running around like this”.
