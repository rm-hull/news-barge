Martin Lewis has hit out at the “ridiculous” tips households are having to follow to keep warm and cut their energy bills, saying it is not the kind of society he wants to live in.

The Money Saving Expert founder said people were being forced to rely on basic energy-saving measures and even USB-heated clothing to stay warm as the cost of heating their homes continues to put pressure on household budgets.

Speaking to the BBC, Martin reluctantly said households should first look at ways to reduce energy use without affecting their quality of life.

He advised people to keep curtains closed when it is dark and open during daylight, make sure blinds face inwards and avoid hanging curtains over radiators.

He also urged households to check their radiators are properly bled and, if they have a combi boiler, look at whether its flow temperature is set too high.

But Martin said the fact people need this kind of advice in order to manage their heating costs was a sign of a much wider problem.

“Again, I don't, you know, it's not because we want to live in that society, but we do live in that society. It's ridiculous,” he said.

He also pointed to 1p an hour USB-heated clothing as another way people are trying to keep themselves warm without having to heat their entire home.

“The USB heated gilet costs a penny an hour to run. It keeps you warm. It doesn't keep your house warm, but it'll keep you going,” he said.

Martin added: “And we are in that situation, and that's not a society I want to live in, where we have to be giving out that type of information and advice. But it is there, and it is going to be happening.”

## **Martin Lewis's tips to help cut energy use**

* Keep curtains closed when it gets dark to help retain heat.
* Open curtains during daylight to let natural warmth into your home.
* Angle blinds inwards, as Martin recommends.
* Make sure curtains do not hang over radiators and block the heat.
* Bleed your radiators if they need it to help them work properly.
* Check whether your combi boiler's flow temperature is set too high.

His comments underline the pressure facing households as they look for ways to keep energy costs down, with simple changes around the home being presented as ways to make heating go further.
