**Friday's top story:Bundestag votes on record 2027 budget and new drinks taxes**

Germany's Bundestag was set to vote on the 2027 federal budget on Friday, which allows spending of around €556 billion, with €123 billion financed through borrowing. Adding in special funds, the government will have more money available than in any previous single year.

More than a third goes to labour and social affairs, while defence rises to nearly €140 billion, its highest level since the Cold War. Ukraine is due €12 billion. Two debt-funded special funds provide €57.8 billion for infrastructure and climate protection and €29.6 billion for the Bundeswehr.

To close a €34 billion shortfall, the coalition has agreed tax rises. Duty on spirits goes up by 20 percent, and the alcopop tax is extended to drinks of up to 15 percent alcohol, which could add about €1 a can. A sugar tax of 25 to 35 cents per litre takes effect in 2028.

The bill also cuts electricity tax by about 2.3 cents per kilowatt-hour, funded only through 2027. An income tax reform remains unapproved amid coalition disputes.

**Germany reaches metalworking pay deal after marathon talks**

IG Metall and the employers' association for the metal and electrical engineering sectors agreed a framework pay deal on Thursday, after talks that ran for more than 24 hours in Mannheim against a backdrop of industrial crisis.

Workers will receive a two percent rise from next May, followed by 2.3 percent a year later. They will also get a one-off payment of between €150 and €450 in March, depending on the net profit margin the group obtains. The agreement runs to the end of November 2028, covering 25 months.

The two sides also agreed a mechanism allowing collective bargaining measures to cut costs in return for commitments on investment, products or technologies that offer long-term jobs. It follows two rounds of strikes that the union says involved nearly one million workers.

This pilot accord covers two major industrial regions initially, but is due to apply nationwide to 3.7 million employees in sectors including automotive, aerospace and defence.

IG Metall, which had demanded five percent for one year, called it "a solid agreement in turbulent times". Employers described a "painful compromise on pay" but said it would "help to secure jobs and sites".

**Kimmich slams Bayern bosses over Musiala treatment**

Bayern Munich vice-captain Joshua Kimmich has hit out at the club's hierarchy for pressuring Jamal Musiala to take a pay cut as he struggles with a neurological condition.

Speaking after a Champions League defeat at Lille on Wednesday, the Germany skipper -- who is Bayern's captain with Manuel Neuer currently injured -- became the latest to criticise the German giants.

"Of course, the club always comes first and is bigger than the players, but it is not bigger than the person," Kimmich said.

"It's completely unacceptable and is not the Bayern way... What's happening now is something that must not happen in this way. I find it completely incomprehensible."

In August, Musiala collapsed twice on the field in pre-season games and then revealed he was suffering from a "neurological disorder" which can lead to "temporary but treatable seizures".

Musiala played last Friday in his first match since a two-month absence with an unrelated hamstring injury. Minutes after full-time, club powerbroker Karl-Heinz Rummenigge suggested Musiala's condition meant he might be unable to deliver on his lucrative deal and should take a pay cut.

A day later, Bayern announced record turnover of close to one billion euros at their AGM.

**Public health insurance contributions to remain stable in 2027**

Large nationwide health insurance funds are aiming for stable contributions in the new year in view of the political austerity package with spending limits set across the entire healthcare system.

DAK-Gesundheit, the third largest statutory health insurance provider in Germany with 5.4 million insured persons, announced it intends to keep the additional contribution at 3.2 percent in 2027.

Previously, the Techniker Krankenkasse (TK), the largest statutory fund with 12.5 million insured persons, and Barmer with 8.2 million insured persons had already promised stable additional contributions.

DAK CEO Andreas Storm told the Rheinische Post that stable contributions throughout 2027 are an important signal of security for insured persons and employers.

"The Contribution Stabilization Act makes a significant contribution to limiting the dynamics of expenditures," Storm said, adding that the fund has also significantly reduced administrative costs.

At the beginning of 2026, DAK had increased its additional contribution from 2.8 percent to 3.2 percent. TK had also raised its rate from 2.45 percent to 2.69 percent.

**Unemployment among people with severe disabilities hits record high**

Almost 185,000 people with severe disabilities were unemployed in 2025, the highest figure since the Inclusion Barometer for Employment was first published in 2013, according to Aktion Mensch. That was up five percent on 2024.

The charity blames the weak economy. The unemployment rate for people with disabilities averaged 12.0 percent, up from 11.6 percent and rising for a third year running. The overall rate was 6.3 percent, according to the Federal Employment Agency. In October, nearly 193,000 were jobless, four percent more than a year earlier.

Aktion Mensch said employers' willingness to lay off staff had "intensified". Requests to terminate the employment of workers with disabilities rose by more than 15 percent to just under 26,000. Spokesperson Christina Marx said employers were cutting inclusion initiatives in uncertain times.

But the organisation does see a “slight upturn” in workplace inclusion. More than 180,000 companies in Germany with 20 or more employees are legally required to allocate at least five percent of their positions to employees with disabilities. The proportion of companies that fully or at least partially meet this obligation rose slightly last year to 39 percent and 75 percent, respectively.

**Germany arrests two Iraqis over synagogue attack plot**

Two Iraqi men have been arrested in Germany on suspicion of plotting attacks on synagogues for a "pro-Iranian terrorist group", prosecutors said Thursday.

The two, named only as Mostafa Al-R. and Ameer J., were held in the western cities of Heidelberg and Cologne respectively in an operation which also saw raids at 17 locations in various regions.

According to the arrest warrant, Mostafa Al-R. is acquainted with Mohammad Al-Saadi, on trial in the United States on terror charges.

Saadi is alleged to be a key figure in a group called Harakat Ashab al-Yamin al-Islamiyah (HAYI), which emerged after the outbreak of the war in Iran this year.

HAYI is believed to be behind attacks against US, Israeli and Jewish institutions in Europe from March 2026 onwards, according to prosecutors.

Saadi and Mostafa Al-R. are accused of founding a separate organisation at the end of 2025, of which Ameer J. was also a member, to raise funds and recruit people for attacks in Germany and elsewhere in Europe.
