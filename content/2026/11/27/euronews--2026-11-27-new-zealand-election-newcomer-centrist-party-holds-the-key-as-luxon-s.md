## The final count showed more support for minor parties on the ideological flanks than before. A total of 2.8 million votes were cast.

New Zealand's final vote count has left neither bloc with a majority, handing a brand-new centrist party the power to decide who governs.

The Electoral Commission's final tally showed more support for minor parties on the ideological flanks than before. A total of 2.8 million votes were cast.

The tally on Friday, after 400,000 special votes including ballots from New Zealanders abroad, showed Prime Minister Christopher Luxon's National party dropping two seats to finish on 40.

The right-wing bloc now holds 65 seats in the 130-seat parliament, one short of the 66 needed. The left, led by Labour's Chris Hipkins, has 58, with Labour on 36.

That leaves the Opportunity party, an unaligned centrist newcomer with seven seats, as kingmaker. Its support would not be enough to put the left in power, Hipkins conceded on Friday, in an apparent admission of defeat.

Luxon, who ruled out working with Opportunity before the 7 November vote, reversed course saying he was confident of forming a government with Opportunity and his usual partners, the libertarian ACT (13 seats) and the populist NZ First (12).

All three minor parties would need to back him.

"These negotiations may be challenging and they could take some time," he said. "But getting it right matters far more than getting it done quickly."

## Qiulae Wong, the 38-year-old newcomer

Opportunity is led by 38-year-old business consultant Qiulae Wong, who has said she could work with either side. Her party's five "bottom line" policies include tougher climate action and an end to bottom-trawling, both at odds with ACT and NZ First.

Wong also repeated that she will not join a government in which NZ First leader Winston Peters is foreign minister, citing his past remarks in parliament about Asian people. She said she would consider either a full coalition or a "confidence and supply" deal, under which her MPs would back key legislation and decide other bills case by case.

Both major parties took under 30% of the party vote: Labour 29.88% and National 28.35%. Analysts pointed to anger over the cost of living and little enthusiasm for either leader.

PM Luxon survived two confidence votes among his own MPs this year, while Hipkins led Labour to defeat in 2023. Minor parties on both flanks gained ground, with the Greens on 17 seats and Te Pati Māori on five.

Opportunity's rise is striking: it failed three times to enter parliament before Wong took over less than a year ago, and none of its new MPs has served before. If no government can be formed, New Zealand could face a fresh election.
