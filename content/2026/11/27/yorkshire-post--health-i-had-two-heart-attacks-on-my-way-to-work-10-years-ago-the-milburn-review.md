# 'I had two heart attacks on my way to work 10 years ago - the Milburn Review is deeply concerning'

## A Yorkshire councillor has spoken about his health issues as his local party campaigns against young people’s disability benefits.

Green Party councillor Richard Tinsley, who has experienced health hurdles in recent years, is seconding a motion being submitted to the next full Sheffield City Council meeting.

Councillor Tom Atkins-Withers will submit the motion, heavily criticising the Milburn Review, which recommends cutting Personal Independence Payments (PIP) for more than 400,000 recipients under-25.

PIP is a monthly cash disability benefit for anyone with a long term condition or who experiences difficulty with everyday tasks or getting around.

![Councillor Richard Tinsley thinks people might not understand how essential PIP can be for those with additional needs to be able to work.](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOjZhZTgyOGY2LWJjNjAtNDZhZi1iMjhhLWY0MTRjZGNjYjQ0MjphOGUxZjdkZS1hZGY3LTQwZWItOGY2NC03MmNjOWYwNGY4OTA=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


It is not an out-of-work benefit, and is intended help with extra living costs that come with managing a condition.

Coun Tinsley said: “In 2016, I have two heart attacks on the way to work. Within a week, I went from okay, to having heart disease, diabetes, and high blood pressure. I have a box full of medication I take every morning.

“Ten years on, I’m still here. I’m a lot healthier than I was. But my lifestyle completely changed.

“Some people are born with disabilities, but others have them thrust on through illness. Dealing with that can be incredibly challenging.

![Councillor Tom Atkin-Withers said “It is important to remember that PIP supports disabled people whether in work or not, helping many to earn a living and to live an independent and fulfilling life." Credit: Sheffield Greens](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOmMxNWFkYzdkLTMxNDEtNDg3Ni04ZjQyLTgzNjFkMjAxYjk2Njo4NDEwZDE4Yy1lMDNhLTRiOWYtYTQyNS1mZWJjNjdjNWE5ZTM=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


“What you do not need on top of that is additional financial challenges that you can’t possibly meet.”

He said he has been able to manage without applying for PIP, although he has looked at applying for it on several occasions.

Coun Tinsley added: “Personal independence payment is essential for enabling people to just live normal lives.”

He said it was “deeply concerning” that the Milburn Review, released on November 17, recommended taking PIP away from under-25s and replacing it with more help to get them into work.

Coun Tinsley said: “If you’ve got mental or physical health difficulties, the right work is fantastically therapeutic. Being in an environment with supportive people, doing something that you are appreciated for and recognised for is fantastic.

“However, not being in that type of work environment is not helpful.”

Coun Tinsley is also concerned about the knock-on effects on young people who are “desperate” to get into work or training, or those experiencing anxiety and depression.

“Among the many hundreds of young people I’ve met in the last year, I’ve not found a single one of them who wanted to be not in employment,education or training, not one,” he said.

“Threatening to take away PIP is going to cause undue mental difficulties.”

Tom Atkin-Withers, who is proposing the Green motion, said cutting PIP “will just have a knock on effect elsewhere”.

They said: “Removing PIP just creates a greater need for carers, and therefore carers allowance and the carers component of Universal Credit.

“The loss of PIP would also mean losing qualification for other vital support and benefits, e.g. blue badge, rail cards and bus passes, council tax discount, potential university bursaries and specialist college access.

“Though the Government says it wants to get more young people into employment, implementing the Milburn Review could have the opposite effect.”

The motion, set to be voted on by all councillors on December 2, asks the council and Sheffield’s MPs to call on the Government to reject the policy of removing disability benefits from young people.

It also calls for the development of “innovative traineeship and volunteering programmes for young people,” including those who may otherwise face significant barriers to work.

Coun Tinsley said one “incredible thing” the council is currently doing is its ’empowering independence’ programme which enables mostly older people to live independently and continue work or community activities, preventing them moving into homes.

However, he said PIP proposals “contravene the whole purpose of our empowering independence programme.

“It’s the complete antithesis of what we’re trying to do.”
