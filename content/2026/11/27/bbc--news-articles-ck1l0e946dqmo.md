**Nearly allDepartment for Work and Pensions (DWP) services have been taken offlinedue to a "cyber incident".**

While the DWP has said benefit and pension payments are not affected and will be paid as normal, the department is continuing to investigate the cause of the incident.

Here's what we know so far and which services have been affected.

## Which services have been affected?

Nearly all of the DWP's online systems have been taken down, including those for pensions, universal credit and Pip applications, as well as the "Tell Us Once'" system used to let the government know if someone has died.

A DWP spokesperson said: "We are working closely with the National Cyber Security Centre and Government Cyber Coordination Centre to investigate and to bring services back online as quickly as possible and to minimise any disruption.

"As always, we will never message you to ask for bank details or ask you to click on links."

The DWP did not give a time frame of when services may be back online.

As of Friday morning, it appears other government departments have not been affected.

## Will I still get my pension or benefit payments on time?

The DWP has assured users that pension and benefit payments will not be affected by today's incident and will be paid as normal.

However, when users try to log in to the universal credit or pensions systems, they are met with the below error message. This could affect users' ability to check or make changes to their accounts.

Work and Pensions Committee Chair Debbie Abrahams said the DWP took "swift action" to ensure payments would still be sent out.

In a statement, she said: "That today's ongoing cyber-incident is not affecting benefit and pension payments is a great relief.

"The DWP has taken swift action to contain any potential further damage from the cyber-incident and so that service is restored as soon as possible."

She added it is "critical" the impact of the outage is investigated so advice can be given to pension and benefit claimants.

## What caused the DWP 'cyber incident'?

The cause of the "cyber incident" affecting online DWP services remains unclear and we still do not know whether someone is behind it and, if so, who they are and what their motive is.

Currently, there is no indication the DWP has been hacked, but taking services offline is part of a "Hail Mary" response we have seen in other cyber incidents, as it kicks the hackers out to prevent further intrusion.

Cutting internet connectivity can also help responders preserve evidence, which might help if this is indeed some kind of malicious activity.

The BBC's cyber correspondent Joe Tidy says the department's actions and wording are reminiscent of the Co-op hack last year.

In that incident Co-op said it had taken services offline as a "precaution". Only later was it discovered that it was already too late, as the hackers had successfully breached the firm and stolen the data of 6.5 million customers.

And in August, tech firms including Google, Microsoft, Anthropic and OpenAI signed an open letter warning countries and organisations around the world to improve their cyber defences.

Today's incident comes just weeks after the government carried out inspections on several websites after a flaw was identified in a global programming language.

The PHP security flaw prompted a worldwide race for organisations to identify whether they were impacted and fix it before it could be exploited.

So far there is no indication that those checks and today's incident are related.
