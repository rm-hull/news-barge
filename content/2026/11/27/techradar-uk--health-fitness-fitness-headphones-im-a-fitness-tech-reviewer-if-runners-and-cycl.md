## A necessary purchase

![Shokz OpenRun Pro 2](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/ZAMYuC8LRsqURfYu57bQGB-1200-80.jpg)

If you've never used bone conduction headphones before, you might not know why these headsets are on half the runners you see on the street. Bone conduction and open-ear headphones pipe sound through your bones as the name implies, using vibrating transducers to reach your eardrums while leaving your ears free.

On the street, you retain awareness, allowing you to hear cars, pedestrians and other dangers. During races, you can listen to instructions from marshals and be present, enjoying the vibe rather than isolated with in-ears. These factors alone make them essential purchases for cyclists and runners; some races *only* allow bone-conduction headsets to be worn so marshals can be heard.

Shokz is a leader in the space, and the OpenRun Pro 2s are the company's star model. I mentioned it above, but the headphones have 12 hours of listening and USB-C quick charge, allowing you to top for a few minutes before a run and get a full 2.5 hours of listening from depletion. The wind-resistant mics let you take calls, and sound is surprisingly high quality.

They're dead comfortable on a run too, not needing the elastic secure fit band that so many other headphones use thanks to its multiple size options and rigid titanium band.
