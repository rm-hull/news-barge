## 'Incredible' LNER worker gives thanks after train attacker found guilty

Samir Zitouni, LNER Customer Experience Host *(Image: LNER)*

A STAFF member of a York-based train company, who was seriously injured trying to protect those around him during a mass stabbing, has shared his gratitude.

Samir Zitouni – a LNER worker - has thanked emergency services after he was seriously injured in a stabbing aboard a train on the evening of October 31 last year.

As reported by The Press, attacker Anthony Williams, 33, was found guilty yesterday of the attempted murders of 14 people, including 10 during the attack on an LNER service that began in Peterborough and was diverted to Huntingdon.

Williams had admitted carrying out the attacks but denied that he intended to kill, and his trial was told that at the time he was suffering from an acute psychosis as a result of his schizophrenia.

Despite this, he was found guilty of all 14 counts of attempted murder by a jury after 16 hours and 52 minutes of deliberations.

Jurors were told that Samir, who works as a LNER Customer Experience Host for the York based company, taken to intensive care in a life-threatening condition, having received stab wounds including three to the back.

He suffered “catastrophic blood loss” whilst he attempted to fight off the attacker with a frying pan and required four blood transfusions.

At the time, his employer said he “did not hesitate as he stepped forward to protect those around him” – crediting him with helping to save multiple lives after passengers came under threat.

Samir said: "I want to express my deepest gratitude to the police, the NHS - particularly to Addenbrooke's A&E doctors - and to the Ambulance crews on site last year.

“I appreciate everyone’s thoughts and prayers, including from my LNER colleagues and other railway workers, during a difficult time. Thank you.”

During the incident, passengers heard Williams talking about the devil, making comments like “the devil won’t win”, and he had a bottle of fortified tonic wine with him that he drank from.

Teams at the Rail Operating Centre in York helped driver Andrew Johnson divert the train after the mass stabbing onboard allowing police to board.

Andrew Johnson, LNER Train Driver, said that the events will 'stay in the memory of all those involved for a very long time'.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21771697.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Andrew Johnson, LNER Train Driver *(Image: LNER)*

He said: "Over the past year, I have been supported by my family, friends, colleagues and especially by my partner Abbie, and I am extremely grateful for their care and kindness throughout. I’m also grateful to everyone at LNER for their continued support.

"I’d like to thank those who were involved in the response on the night itself, particularly Rob Pape, the Network Rail signaller, who worked with me to get the train swiftly into Huntingdon.

"My thanks also go to the police and the medical teams who attended so quickly and provided vital care to those who had been injured and those that have supported since, including those involved in the investigation.

"As I said at the time, the heroes of the night were my colleagues onboard, particularly Samir whose actions were incredible.

"My thoughts remain with everyone who was injured and affected by this incident. I hope the conclusion of this case brings some measure of closure and allows us all to look ahead and move forward with our lives."

David Horne, Managing Director at LNER, added: "We welcome today’s verdict from court, which we hope helps bring some closure for all those involved in this horrific incident. Just over a year on, our thoughts very much remain with everyone affected by the shocking events of Saturday 1st November 2025.

"I would like to once again recognise the bravery and swift response shown by our colleagues, customers and the emergency services that night. The remarkable courage of our onboard crew who supported customers as the incident was ongoing, particularly Samir, who chose to step forward and protect those around him.

“Also, the quick and clear thinking shown by our driver, Andrew, and Network Rail signaller, Rob, which enabled the train to be brought swiftly into Huntingdon and the station colleagues who guided customers to safety as they left the train.

“I’d also like to thank British Transport Police for their diligent investigation and their determination to ensure justice was delivered.

"We are incredibly proud of the way everyone came together to support each other and the care, compassion and unity that has resulted from such harrowing circumstances."
