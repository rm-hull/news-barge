# Calls to tackle '3-4 inch sludge of leaves' which could cause falls or flooding

## Town and parish councils should consider clearing fallen leaves from streets and pavements amid concerns over pedestrian safety and flooding, a senior councillor has said.

Councillors raised concerns about the build-up of wet leaves at a meeting of North Yorkshire Council’s Harrogate and Knaresborough area committee today.

Members warned that slippery pavements were leaving some elderly residents reluctant to venture outside, while householders in areas previously affected by flooding had resorted to clearing leaves from around drains themselves.

Cllr John Mann said: “Driving around the town, you can’t help but notice the often three or four-inch-deep sludge of leaves.

“I, for example, have parked this morning in Studley Road. The depth of the leaves by the side of the road is about three or four inches.”

He questioned whether additional street-cleaning vehicles could be deployed to tackle the problem.

A council officer told the meeting that the authority was halfway through an eight-week countywide leaf clearance programme, with resources diverted from its usual street-sweeping operations.

The officer added that resources were limited and the authority needed to retain the ability to respond to problems as they arose.

![A street in Knaresborough where residents had raised concerns about a build-up of leaves. Photo: Cllr Hannah Gostlow.](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOjQ5ZjdjYzJjLWQ3NzYtNDBmNy05MmYxLTk4MDRmYjU3ZDg0MzoyNzBhYjEwNi0xOGEwLTQ5MTAtOGViYS1hOWE1YzFjYWMyODU=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


Cllr Hannah Gostlow said residents in the Eastfield area of Knaresborough had raised concerns about the condition of walking and cycling routes.

She highlighted a footpath on Eastfield Road which was in a particularly poor condition because of wet and slippery leaves.

She added: “Some residents have been trying to keep clear the leaves themselves from around the gullies because they are so concerned that they’re going to get blocked, and those are the areas that flooded before.”

She called for areas previously affected by surface water flooding to be prioritised.

Cllr Peter Lacey argued that fallen leaves posed as much of a risk to older residents as icy pavements, increasing the likelihood of falls and social isolation.

He said: “We said last year it was unacceptable to have pavements full of ice for a week.

“If you’ve got pavements full of leaves that older people don’t walk on for a month or two, then I think that’s unacceptable.”

He questioned whether the council could better coordinate its resources to respond to the large volume of leaves falling following autumn storms.

“I just don’t think we’ve got it at all right in terms of leaves,” he added.

However, Cllr Michael Harrison, an executive member of the Conservative-controlled authority, said the sheer scale of North Yorkshire made it unrealistic to expect the council to respond immediately to widespread leaf fall.

He said: “If you say 60 per cent of the leaves are going to fall in the next three days, that’s 100 per cent of the county is going to have 60 per cent of the leaves drop in a single event.

“To then expect the council to be somehow able to fix that is just unreasonable.”

Cllr Harrison suggested town and parish councils could play a greater role in tackling the problem.

He added: “I actually think it is a challenge for the county.

“I personally think that town and parish councils need to consider what they can do, because we are never as a unitary council going to be able to achieve what you’ve described there.”
