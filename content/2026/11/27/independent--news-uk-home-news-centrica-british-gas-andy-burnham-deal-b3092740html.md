The parent company of British Gas has warned hundreds of jobs could be at risk after Andy Burnham’s government rejected proposals about its future.

Chris O’Shea, the chief executive of Centrica, told staff he is seeking urgent talks with the prime minister and the company is “doing everything we can to save your jobs” at its Rough gas storage facility off the coast of Yorkshire.

His memo, which has been obtained by Sky News, comes at a time of heightened energy insecurity given the cost-of-living crisis and the wars in both Ukraine and Iran.

Mr O’Shea has expressed frustration at repeated delays over decisions about the future of Britain’s gas storage sector, after a consultation process ended in February.

Centrica, a FTSE-100 listed utility firm, had outlined proposals for a £2bn redevelopment of Rough, which it said would create up to 5,000 skilled construction jobs.

![Centrica’s Rough facility supports directly or indirectly 400 jobs](https://static.independent.co.uk/2023/01/12/11/30104215-08878b89-73d2-466d-8ebd-b28e88acf831.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The Rough storage facility currently supports up to 400 jobs directly and indirectly, with Mr O’Shea telling staff in his memo that Centrica would be holding talks with the trade union Unite.

"The secretary of state [Miatta Fahnbulleh] has now told us that the government will not take up the short-term arrangement for this winter," Mr O'Shea wrote.

"She has also said the department needs more time to work through the consultation responses, and that its full response will now come at some point next year.”

He added the company is “extremely disappointed” by the further delay, and has said he is seeking a “minded to” decision from the government in the next month to give a signal as to how they can “plan with confidence”.

"The bigger problem, though, is that we still do not know when a decision will come, and Rough's current consent ends on 30 April 2027, and the NSTA [North Sea Transition Authority] have given their approval to cease operations,” his memo reportedly said.

Centrica has warned it will not seek a new licence for Rough when the existing one expires in five months.

Of the proposals made to redevelop the structure, Mr O’Shea said: "Our ask was clear: for [the] government to apply its own energy storage cap and floor regime to Rough, the UK's biggest energy storage facility.

![Centrica's chief executive Chris O'Shea has expressed frustration in Whitehall's repeated delays in making a decision](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/27/12/01M81G13ZJ0YTVTFJ1ZKA0J7QD.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

"Since then, world events have only made the case for Rough stronger.

"As conflict in the Middle East put pressure on global gas supplies, in April we started talking to [the] government about using Rough as a strategic reserve in the short term.

"Gas would have been injected over the summer, held for this winter, and released only if [the] government asked for it."

He reportedly stressed that any profit would have gone to the government, and that the offer was to “help keep the country as secure as possible this winter”.

"Frankly, this [decision on gas storage] has taken longer than we expected, given [the] government's stated commitment to reindustrialisation and supporting jobs," he wrote.

"A decision is now needed, and every month of delay narrows the options available.

"I have asked for time with the prime minister to discuss this directly, and hope that meeting can take place soon, so that any blockages can be removed, and a decision can be reached without further delay."

Centrica confirmed the contents of the memo reported by Sky News, but declined to comment any further.

A DESNZ spokesperson said: “National Gas’ Winter Outlook confirms we have enough gas to meet demand this coming winter - only 5 percent of our supply came from storage last winter.

“We’ve set out plans to maintain our gas security, including options for future commercial support for the sector, while ensuring all proposals provide value for money for consumers.”
