**Coventry author Jonathan Hewston’s picture book features an autistic girl navigating her library’s side entrance, accompanied by a free activity aiming to engage children visiting Harrogate’s community libraries.**

A new children’s picture book by Coventry author Jonathan Hewston is inviting families to engage with their local libraries in a fresh and creative way. The Library Door Everyone Could Open/ tells the story of Molly, an autistic girl who, faced with the main library entrance being closed, finds a quieter side door and then helps the librarian create a picture map for others.

Hewston, who has personal experience of dyslexia, ADHD, and navigating school changes, said his book reflects the challenges and perspectives of children like himself. Molly’s story encourages readers to consider how different users experience a building and the importance of inclusion.

Alongside the book, Hewston offers a free activity on his website where children can draw a two-step picture map to a side entrance of their own library or a familiar building and then invite a family member to follow the directions. The invitation offers a practical and imaginative way for families to explore local community libraries together.

The activity, which can be accessed at hewstonhiggins.com/the-library-door-everyone-could-open, is suitable for families visiting Harrogate’s community libraries, including Bilton and Woodfield Community Library, which shares premises with a local primary school.

Hewston runs his independent children’s publishing business, Hewston Higgins Books & Learning, from his home in Coventry and aims to use his work to support children who face challenges similar to those he encountered growing up.

This story is especially pertinent to families in Harrogate and North Yorkshire who are looking for engaging ways to connect with local library resources whilst encouraging children’s literacy and navigation skills.
