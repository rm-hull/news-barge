The National Energy System Operator (Neso) issued two electricity margin notices on Friday, extending a run of daily calls for back-up power that began on Tuesday.

The grid operator has now asked the market to prepare to generate more back-up power for four days in a row, an unprecedented stretch as network maintenance coincides with this week's cold snap.

Reports suggest that the sequence marks a record-breaking number of notices in a row, and for the year.

Neso said on Friday that the cushion of supply would be reduced between 7am and 10am, when it was expecting a system margin shortfall of 2,500 megawatts.

A further 2,400 megawatt shortfall in the system margin was expected between 2pm and 8pm on Friday, it said.

An electricity margin notice sends a message to the market that the operator would like more of a cushion between power demand and supply for a limited period.

![The grid operator has asked the market to prepare to generate more back-up power for four days in a row](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/16/18/2289739922..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Eleven margin notices have been made this year, including the two on Friday, which is said to be more than the previous five years combined.

But Neso insisted it was a “routine and precautionary measure” to allow for “additional generation capacity”.

A Neso spokesperson said the notice did not mean there was a risk of power cuts.

“This is not a warning of power cuts, there is no risk to customer electricity supplies and customers do not need to take action.”

The record number of electricity margin notices signal that there is unusually tight supply capacity on the power grid.

It is understood there are significant network constraints because of maintenance work, which has come at a time of surging demand given the sudden cold snap which has seen temperatures plunge across the UK.

![Temperatures have plunged across the UK in the past few weeks](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/27/18/2306044269..?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&auto=webp)

Around 6,000 megawatts are being held up due to these network constraints, according to Neso.

Laura Fleming, Hitachi Energy UK and Ireland managing director, said: “The power supply alerts this week from Neso are an important reminder of the importance of strengthening the UK's grid. This must be a national priority.

“As renewable generation expands and electricity demand rises, Britain's energy security depends not just on how much power we generate, but on how effectively we move and manage it.

“We need stronger transmission infrastructure, smarter networks and greater flexibility to balance supply and demand.

“That means accelerating investment in new infrastructure while harnessing digital technologies to unlock more capacity from existing networks.”
