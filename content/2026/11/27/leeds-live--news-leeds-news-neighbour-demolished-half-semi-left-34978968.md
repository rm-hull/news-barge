# Neighbour demolished his half of semi and left UK - now Leeds mum needs help

## "No family should have to live with their home exposed and at risk for such a prolonged period."

A fundraiser has been launched for a Leeds mum whose neighbour knocked down his half of their semi-detached home.

Shivarna Mitra, 44, has spent four years living beside an empty plot in Pudsey after the adjoining property was pulled down. She says this has left her house riddled with damp and mould and "unsellable".

Now a GoFundMe page has been set up to pay for repairs to her home, which she shares with her young son and elderly mum.

The page says: "We are fundraising on behalf of a family whose semi-detached home has been left in a devastating condition after the adjoining property was illegally demolished, exposing their internal wall to the elements."

It adds: "For the past four years, the family have endured the consequences of having part of their home exposed, facing ongoing weather damage, uncertainty, stress, and disruption to their daily lives. Despite their efforts to seek help and resolve the situation, significant repairs are still needed to make their home safe, secure, and weatherproof once again."

The money raised will go towards repairing the exposed wall and structural damage, making the property fully weatherproof, essential building and restoration works, and professional surveys. It will also cover "legal costs associated with pursuing justice and protecting the family's rights", which the page says insurance won't pay for.

The appeal reads: "No family should have to live with their home exposed and at risk for such a prolonged period. Every donation, no matter the size, will help bring them one step closer to restoring their home and rebuilding a sense of security."

Ms Mitra bought her three-bed home for £217,000 in May 2021 after saving for years. She then spent around £80,000 on renovations, including a new kitchen and a £35,000 garden.

Chris Rhodes, 47, a self-employed carpenter, bought the house next door for £280,000 in July 2022. He got planning permission for two extensions that summer, and work began that October.

Ms Mitra claims he used the planning approvals as cover to demolish the house, which he denies. She contacted Leeds City Council in January 2023, claiming the work breached planning consent, and the council issued a stop notice in May that year.

She says holes in her walls and roof let in rain, rats, squirrels and wasps. She said: "Rats got in and chewed the wires, resulting the house needing full rewiring. Water has got in through the roof and holes in the wall and has caused mould and damp."

She also said: "I have gone through cancer and had a major operation while all this was going on, so instead of recovering, I'm fighting battles from my hospital bed on emails."

Mr Rhodes, who has since left the UK, says a storm left the gable wall unsafe and that he took it down, along with the front wall, following Health and Safety Executive guidance. He told MailOnline: "I had a discussion with the HSE and it was agreed [that pulling the wall down] was necessary and the correct course of action to avoid any risk or danger to others and their properties."

He added: "There were very unfortunate circumstances that cost my wife and I a significant amount of money and stress also."

Mr Rhodes denies that his builders damaged Ms Mitra's home. He said: "We did not touch her roof as is shown in the videos and images. We left approximately two feet of our roof so as to protect the top of the wall from direct weather."

He got planning permission for a five-bedroom home in May 2024, but it was never built. He sold the plot with planning permission earlier this year for £115,000.

A Leeds City Council spokesperson said: "The building was subsequently taken down by the owner without the necessary Council permissions being in place. The owner was advised by Leeds City Council to submit a Section 80 Demolition Notice but did not do so."

The council said Building Control officers later found that Ms Mitra's home "remained structurally stable". It added: "As these are privately owned properties, the council advised Ms Mitra to contact her house insurer and take legal advice. The council will continue to monitor the site and take any appropriate action as necessary."

Ms Mitra's insurer has brought a legal claim, which she says is still around two years from reaching court.
