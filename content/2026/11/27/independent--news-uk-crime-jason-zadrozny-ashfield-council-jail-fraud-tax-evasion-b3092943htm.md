A former local authority chief accused of inventing a fictitious heritage organisation to defraud a council out of more than £9,000 has been sentenced to 27 months in prison.

Jason Zadrozny stepped down from his position leading Ashfield District Council in Nottinghamshire and resigned his seat as a district councillor following his conviction in September. A trial at Northampton Crown Court found him guilty of nine counts of fraud alongside four offences of fraudulent evasion of income tax.

At the sentencing hearing, Judge David Herbert KC described the occasion as a "day of shame" for the 46-year-old defendant, previously of Sutton Road, Kirkby-in-Ashfield, who had accomplished an "awful lot of good in the community" but ultimately "let everyone down".

Addressing the court, the judge stated: "You were elected to serve your local communities and act in their best interests, not your own, and maintain high standards of conduct.

"You have failed the electorate and harmed public confidence in the district council by your fraudulent conduct."

![Zadrozny was told by a judge that he had ‘let everyone down’](https://static.independent.co.uk/2023/07/21/12/21122339-908f9f3b-a3d3-474b-af76-4aeb0d06338f.png?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

During the four-week trial, prosecutors outlined how Zadrozny, who was first elected to Ashfield District Council and Nottinghamshire County Council in 2007, siphoned funds from the Ashfield Heritage Society (AHS), an entity he established in February 2018.

Jurors were also told that Zadrozny operated as a landlord who failed to pay income tax on earnings derived from four of his properties.

Opening the case for the Crown at the start of the proceedings, prosecutor James Varley KC stated that AHS was entirely non-existent.

Mr Varley told the court: "He told the council ‘this is a genuine organisation, they need some funds’ and they gave him some funds, but it was a scam."

Giving evidence to the jury, Zadrozny denied retaining the money for personal use, claiming he handed cash from the heritage organisation to Tony Brewer, a former Ashfield District Council chairman who died from Covid in 2020, to pay for repairs to war memorials.

However, the prosecution submitted that funding allocated to the society was extracted in cash by the defendant himself. They noted that although the Classic Cinema Club established by Zadrozny "was probably legitimate when he set it up", he subsequently helped himself to its finances.

The court was told that £9,100 was removed in cash from the account of the Ashfield Heritage Society, while a further £1,200 was taken from the cinema club account.

Both groups had received money from the councillors’ divisional fund at Nottinghamshire County Council, a grant resource intended to support local good causes.

Defending, Sam Robinson KC told the court that Zadrozny had been a "model citizen" prior to his offending, pointing out that over 60 character references had been submitted on behalf of the former councillor.

He described how an "avalanche" of testimonial letters recounted how Zadrozny helped an autistic child secure appropriate schooling, aided a resident with financial grants and home adaptations, supported a family through a baby’s critical illness, and assisted another individual in overcoming addiction.

Addressing the judge, Mr Robinson stated: "The most perplexing part of this case is, you have someone who has to be considered to have been a model citizen in the way he behaved in his public life and his personal life, then you have a situation where something happened, and from 2015 onwards, he goes sideways, he goes off the rails.

"You have a situation where, post this offending behaviour, which is now eight-years-old, you have someone like an elastic band who seems to snap back to who he was beforehand."

Mr Robinson stated that Zadrozny had repaid more than £6,000 and experienced "genuine regret and shame" regarding his actions.

He told the court that the offending had resulted in the "destruction of his life", adding: "He blew it up spectacularly in a way that on one reading is beyond comprehension."

The defence barrister noted that Zadrozny had surrendered his "livelihood, his social network, his very identity" after resigning his post on the council and relocating away from the region.

![Zadrozny was convicted at Northampton Crown Court in September](https://static.independent.co.uk/2025/09/23/12/d341824a30fbc16a2ff88a1064469bd6Y29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzU4NzEwMjU4-2.65156975.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Mr Robinson stated that the defendant had accepted responsibility for his conduct, quoting him as saying: "I have broken the public trust in the council… the hurt I have caused is devastating."

Judge Herbert informed Zadrozny that he did not consider it appropriate to suspend the 27-month prison sentence.

He told the defendant he had constructed an "elaborate series of lies" maintained throughout the trial process, acknowledging that while Zadrozny had returned a portion of the stolen funds, "had you not been caught, I am satisfied you never would have done so".

Addressing Zadrozny, the judge remarked: "I know that today is a shameful day for you, you have to be sentenced for repeated dishonesty between 2018 and 2021 that saw you abuse your position in public office.

"You have betrayed the standard that being in public office demands and you have lost your previous good name.

"You have let down your friends and all those in your community who looked up to you."

The judge told Zadrozny that his actions constituted a "gross abuse of trust and responsibility" carried out across a prolonged period of time.

Zadrozny was handed an 18-month sentence for the fraud offences and a consecutive nine-month term for the tax evasion charges.

He must serve 40 per cent of his overall custodial sentence before being eligible for release on licence.
