# High Court bid to stop ‘mini-fracking’ at East Yorkshire gas drilling site

## A CAMPAIGNER has gone to the High Court in a bid to prevent a controversial method of drilling being used at a site in East Yorkshire.

Pete Lomas, who lives in Hornsea, is trying to overturn permission granted by the Environment Agency for what the regulator has described as a "mini-frack" or low‑volume hydraulic fracturing at West Newton, near Burton Constable Hall.

Mr Lomas is also applying for an injunction to stop the mini-frack going ahead before any judicial review is heard.

A mini-frack aims to improve the flow of gas or oil from rock and involves a mixture of fluid and a granular proppant like sand being pumped into rock underground at high pressure to open cracks.

It is estimated that West Newton, which sits between Hull and the East Yorkshire coast, has up to eight billion cubic metres of gas sitting underneath it.

Rathlin Energy has held a permit to drill for oil and gas at the West Newton site since 2013.

Earlier this year, following a public consultation, Rathlin was given permission by the EA to vary the permit and carry out what the regulator described as "reservoir stimulation" to improve the flow of oil and gas.

Friends of the Earth says the case highlights a loophole in England’s fracking moratorium and is urging Labour to honour its manifesto pledge to "ban fracking for good”.

The moratorium only rules out high volume fracking in shale, leaving lower-volume operations as planned at West Newton outside its scope.

FoE said the Government’s Energy Independence Bill, which was expected to fulfil Labour’s manifesto commitment, must ban all types of fracking for fossil fuels The Bill’s second reading is expected imminently.

The group said West Newton isn’t an isolated case, with communities at Burniston, in North Yorkshire, and Wressle, near Scunthorpe, also facing low-volume fracking.

Campaigner Tony Bosworth said: “We hope Peter Lomas succeeds in his legal challenge to the Environment Agency’s permit for this fracking project in East Yorkshire.

“Ministers must amend the Energy Independence Bill to ban all types of fracking for fossil fuels once and for all."

Rathlin Energy said previously that they had a “strong track record of safe and compliant exploration” and they “remain committed to working within a robust regulatory framework and to delivering projects that contribute positively to the UK's energy needs".
