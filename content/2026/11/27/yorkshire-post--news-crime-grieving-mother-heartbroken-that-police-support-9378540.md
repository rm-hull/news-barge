# Bethany Rae Fields: grieving mum ‘heartbroken’ that new police belief is too late to save daughter from murder

## Pauline Jones was speaking as part of West Yorkshire Mayor Tracy Brabin’s refreshed strategy over violence against women and girls.

A grieving mother has praised West Yorkshire Police for showing officers her campaign to believe and react fast to women suffering domestic abuse, however said it was “heartbreaking” it was too late to save her daughter’s life.

Geography student Bethany Rae Fields was stabbed to death by her “evil” ex-boyfriend Paul Crowther in 2019 in Huddersfield.

She had recently ended their relationship and was subjected to a barrage of abuse and threats from the “manipulative, abusive and controlling” 36-year-old before the attack.

In the month before her death, the 21-year-old from Leeds told West Yorkshire Police “he has an obsession with killing someone and what it would feel like”, however officers failed to detain Crowther.

Since then Bethany’s mother, Pauline Jones, has dedicated her life towards campaigning to help domestic violence victims be believed.

![Bethany Fields was stabbed to death in 2019 by her ex-partner Paul Crowther. Her mum, Pauline Jones, has accused West Yorkshire Police of failing her daughter. (Photo family handout/Jonathan Gawthorpe)](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/jpim-static/image/2024/02/23/11/01/MixCollage-23-Feb-2024-11-21-AM-1434.jpg?trim=0,0,0,0&width=640&quality=65)


This is centred around the slogan - Believe, React, Fast - using the initials of her daughter, to prompt swift action from police when domestic abuse is reported to them.

She said: “It’s great that it’s getting out there and that people are taking the BRF campaign seriously.

“I’ve done a police training video and West Yorkshire Police have adopted it.

“I've recently come across a Facebook page where a woman has approached West Yorkshire Police, and that woman has ultimately been saved.

“She did actually use the words ‘I was believed’ and that is what is crucial.

![Bethany Rae Fields. Credit: Pauline Jones](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/jpim-static/image/2025/01/24/15/17/WhatsApp-Image-2025-01-23-at-11-06-04.jpeg?trim=179,0,221,0&width=640&quality=65)


“That is what my campaign is all about. Authorities like the police believe a person when they go in there.

“It's really good for that woman, but also it's heartbreaking for me as a mother who lost Bethany that there wasn't already a policy or that kind of attitude already within the police force.

“Not just West Yorkshire Police, but every police force that is there to serve and protect. Sadly, in Bethany's case, they didn't.”

Ms Jones was speaking as part of West Yorkshire Mayor Tracy Brabin’s refreshed violence against women and girls strategy, which is taking on BRF.

Ms Brabin said: “I can't tell you how proud and humbled I am that Pauline has found the strength to come today, after the catastrophic loss of Bethany.

![Mayor of West Yorkshire Tracy Brabin says the strategy aims to halve violence against women and girls in a decade](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/jpim-static/image/2026/11/26/13/59/7172277e-3a17-45ee-8a5f-e6557e2db316.jpg?trim=0,0,0,0&width=640&quality=65)


“As she says, Bethany was smart, she was articulate, she was afraid, and she wasn't heard or believed.

“That is exactly what we're talking about today. How can we say that in West Yorkshire you will be believed, and of course in 2019 that didn't happen for Bethany.”

Leeds North West MP Katie White said: “I know how much this moment means to Pauline and her family, and how hard they have fought for it.

“Pauline was one of the first people I met after being elected, and I’ve been in awe of her courage ever since.

![Mayor of West Yorkshire Tracy Brabin speaking at Safety of Women and Girls refresh launch](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/jpim-static/image/2026/11/27/16/03/Tracy-Brabin-at-Safety-of-Women-and-Girls-refresh-launch.jpg?trim=0,0,0,0&width=640&quality=65)


“Putting Believe, React, Fast, the initials of her beloved daughter, Bethany Rae Fields, at the heart of West Yorkshire’s strategy is about making sure that when a woman finds the courage to ask for help, she is heard, the danger she faces is taken seriously and everything possible is done to keep her safe.

“I’m so grateful to Mayor Tracy Brabin and Deputy Mayor Alison Lowe for listening to and standing alongside families like Bethany’s.”

Speaking about the wider strategy, Ms Brabin said: “Here in West Yorkshire, we have put the violence against women and girls at the heart of our police and crime plan as a real focus.

“Alison Lowe, my deputy mayor for policing and crime, and myself, we are both survivors of sexual violence.

“When I first became mayor, this was a priority and in this work so far, we have been innovators and trailblazers in our region.”

As part of the strategy, Ms Brabin launched the country’s first women’s safety unit with WYP.

The force said Ms Jones’ video had been shown to certain officers in Kirklees as part of their training.

Ch Supt Jim Griffiths of Kirklees Police said: “West Yorkshire Police fully accepted the findings of a Domestic Homicide Review published in 2024, and those of the IOPC investigation which was conducted following the appalling murder of Bethany Fields.

“We implemented all recommendations made and have amended our approach to tackling domestic abuse. As part of this we have and continue to use learning from Bethany’s death to make improvements to our training programmes and handling of domestic offending.

“Bethany lost her life in dreadful circumstances, and we want to do all we can to try and prevent another such tragedy.”
