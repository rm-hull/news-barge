The nation’s largest group of Vietnam War veterans is pulling out of a legal battle over Donald Trump’s plans for a 250-foot arch that will tower over Arlington National Cemetery in Washington, D.C.

Vietnam Veterans of America — the largest congressionally chartered national organization supporting Vietnam-era veterans and their families — was among several veterans groups encouraged by the White House to sign onto a legal brief in support of the project.

But the group is pulling its name from the brief “upon further deliberation,” according to court filings.

The White House-backed legal brief, which prominently featured Vietnam Veterans of America, explicitly argued that a group of veterans who are suing to stop construction “do not speak for veterans as a whole” and are politicizing “something that should be supported regardless of political party.”

Last month, Vietnam Veterans of America president Tom Burke issued a statement clarifying that the group’s participation in the brief was intended to reflect a distinction “between supporting the effort to honor America's veterans and endorsing every aspect of a particular monument,” but the language in the brief “went further than we intended,” he said.

![The nation’s largest group of Vietnam War-era veterans is withdrawing support for the Trump administration’s plans for a 250-foot arch that would tower over Arlington National Cemetery](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/05/21/2026-11-05T204639Z_866416228_RC2WHOAEZURV_RTRMADP_3_USA-TRUMP-ARCH.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

In a statement to *The Washington Post* that month, Burke said the group received “inaccurate information” about the brief. “Let me be clear: National VVA has not adopted a position supporting or opposing construction of the proposed arch or its design,” he wrote.

On Wednesday, the group pulled out of the brief altogether.

The group’s withdrawal from the brief “does not apply” to the groups Veterans for America First, Military Order of the Purple Heart, Korean War Veterans Association, Chosin Few and National Vet Court Alliance, which also cosigned the brief opposing a lawsuit to stop the project.

The Trump administration has appealed a federal court order that paused plans for the project moments after a federal planning commission filled with Trump’s allies signed off on the plans.

The National Capital Planning Commission, which the president has stuffed with his appointments, approved the project around 10.30 p.m. November 6 despite lengthy public testimony and demands from members of Congress and veterans who are suing the administration to shut it down.

Shortly after the plans were approved, a federal judge temporarily blocked the administration from breaking ground.

U.S. District Judge Tanya Chutkan later extended her order through at least December 3.

A second federal judge has also blocked construction after condemning what she called a “rushed job” that lacked a legal basis.

Last week, U.S. District Judge Leonie Brinkema cited multiple defects in the Trump administration’s impact assessments for the project, including an inadequate explanation about potential interference with air traffic from Reagan Washington National Airport.

“That just hasn’t been done in the case because it’s been such a rushed job,” Brinkema said.

![The president touted plans for his triumphal arch project during a speech on Veterans Day at Arlington National Cemetery, saying the 250-foot structure would ‘honor’ veterans](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/27/16/2304634297..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The latest developments follow a months-long legal battle against the project, which Trump has said he also wants to turn into a “top-grade military complex” for the nation’s capital.

Michael Lemmon, among Vietnam War-era veterans suing to stop the project, told the federal planning commission this month that construction is “flatly unlawful” without explicit congressional authorization, which it has not received.

The monument would also compromise “sacred ground” at adjoining Arlington National Cemetery, where tens of thousands of U.S. military veterans are buried, Lemmon said.

Constructing a monument that overshadows “hallowed ground” for U.S. service members is “frankly blasphemous,” he added.

The president, top Cabinet secretaries and Department of Justice officials have argued that the arch must be built to fend off drones and serve other vital “national security” purposes.

In court filings, Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth and Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin outlined plans for a militarized arch that can deploy snipers and counter drones and target other “continuously evolving” threats.

None of those plans were included in the proposal approved by the planning commission.

In remarks to service members during a national Veterans Day ceremony at Arlington National Cemetery this month, Trump said the arch will honor “our great veterans and our great militaries.”

“They’ve been waiting for it for 200 years,” he said.
