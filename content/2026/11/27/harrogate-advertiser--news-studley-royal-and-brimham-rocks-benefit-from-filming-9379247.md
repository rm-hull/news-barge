Filming at some of the region’s most prestigious tourist and natural beauty spots is helping to preserve them.

Fountains Abbey and Studley Royal, near Ripon, and Brimham Rocks, near Summerbridge have been used as locations for TV’s *All Creatures Great and Small*and Sky Arts'* Landscape Artist of the Year*and for films* 28 Years Later* and Secret Garden, among others.

National Trust Senior Communications and Marketing Officer Katie Gisbourne said: “When film and television productions choose to use National Trust places as locations, the benefits go far beyond the screen.

“At Fountains Abbey & Studley Royal and Brimham Rocks, filming brings a real sense of excitement, both during production and when the finished programme airs.

“There's always a buzz when camera crews arrive on site, and staff, volunteers and visitors alike enjoy seeing familiar landscapes and landmarks appear on television.

“As well as showing off beautiful locations, filming brings direct benefits to the places in our care.”

Katie added: “Every production generates valuable income for the National Trust, helping to fund our charitable work and support the conservation of the special places we look after.

“Location fees are reinvested into caring for historic buildings, landscapes and wildlife, ensuring they can be enjoyed both on screen and in real life for generations to come.

“At Fountains Abbey & Studley Royal, income from filming has helped support repairs to the historic river walls and landscape restoration work, helping to preserve and enhance this much-loved World Heritage Site.”

And she added that the benefits go beyond investment in repairs at the Ripon and Nidderdale sites.

She added: “Just as importantly, screen appearances help introduce Fountains Abbey & Studley Royal and Brimham Rocks to entirely new audiences.

“Visitors are often inspired to seek out the locations they've seen on screen, with some even recreating scenes and dressing up as their favourite characters during their visit.

“Often referred to as "screen tourism", this can have a lasting impact.

“From period dramas to primetime entertainment, filming sparks curiosity, encourages people to explore somewhere new and supports local economies, with visitors enjoying nearby shops, cafés, accommodation and attractions as part of their trip.

“It’s a win-win: filming helps care for these special places while allowing millions of people to experience a little of their magic, wherever they happen to be watching from.”
