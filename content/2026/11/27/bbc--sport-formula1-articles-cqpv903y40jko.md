**World champion Kimi Antonelli set the pace ahead of Mercedes team-mate George Russell in a disrupted second practice at the Qatar Grand Prix.**

Italian Antonelli was 0.127 seconds quicker than Russell on the medium tyre, as neither driver set a time on the soft compound because of two red flags that punctuated the session.

Lewis Hamilton also came to halt after his Ferrari lost power, with a virtual safety car deployed so the marshals could come to the seven-time world champion's aid.

The McLarens of Lando Norris and Oscar Piastri plus Red Bull's Max Verstappen completed the top five at the Lusail International Circuit, with the trio setting soft-tyre times that were more than four tenths adrift of the two Mercedes.

The first red flag was to clear gravel off the track after an incident for Ferrari's Charles Leclerc early on, while the other was to recover the Alpine of Pierre Gasly following a high-speed spin.

Frenchman Gasly hit a marker board before beaching himself after taking too much kerb at Turn 14 in the middle of the qualifying simulation runs.

The interruptions led to the second hour being extended by 13 minutes to allow drivers more time on track in the representative conditions.

"It was a productive day," said Antonelli. "We tried a lot of things on the car between FP1 and FP2, but in FP2, it seemed like we did a step back with the car.

"But it was very interesting to see how the car behaved after those changes. It's not easy."

Antonelli, who secured his maiden title in Mexico City earlier this month, was also fastest in the opening session on the mediums. Mercedes did fit the 20-year-old with the soft tyre in the first hour, but he failed to set a lap time on them.

He said he feels it will be close between himself and Russell for the race victory, but added: "You can never write off Max [Verstappen] and the others."

Norris, meanwhile, said Mercedes "were in a different category" to McLaren, highlighting the Silver Arrows "were quicker in every single corner by miles".

The 2025 world champion added: "We seem in a relatively decent position, similar on pace with Red Bull, and similar-ish to Ferrari. So I'd say it's OK."

Four-time title winner Verstappen said it had been a "difficult day" following his issues with heavy steering towards the end of the session.

"The balance of the car is probably too disconnected at the moment to go fast," he added. "It's costing us quite a bit of lap time."

Hamilton also had a moment with Verstappen that was noted by the race control. The Briton was on a flying lap when he got too close to the Dutchman, who was on a long run.

Verstappen said Hamilton had moved under braking, but the stewards decided no further investigation was needed.

The second Red Bull of Isack Hadjar was sixth on the timesheets, with Leclerc leading Hamilton in seventh and eighth, 1.3 seconds away from Antonelli's time.

The two Racing Bulls of British rookie Arvid Lindblad and Liam Lawson were ninth and 10th.

Leclerc had to head back into the garage after spinning and going through the gravel when Lawson was just ahead of him at Turn Nine.

The Monegasque driver said the car did not feel right, and the Italian team spent a chunk of time repairing the damage to the floor. After the session he said he lost control after going a little bit early on the throttle.

Ferrari arrived in Qatar after another on-track incident between Leclerc and Hamilton, this time in Las Vegas. The pair made contact and Leclerc was given a 10-second penalty for the incident, with Hamilton saying on Thursday the team were facing "very, very challenging times".

Leclerc thinks this weekend could potentially be another struggle for the Italian team, who can secure second in the constructors' championship if they can retain at least a 44-point lead over third-placed Red Bull.

"A very, very difficult day. Not even on high fuel were we strong," said Leclerc.

"We are working hard but for some reason, Mercedes is so much faster than I think everyone expected. So the gaps are just a little bit strange."

Drivers will be limited to a maximum of 27 laps on a set of tyres at the Lusail circuit, meaning teams will have to make at least two pit stops during the 57-lap grand prix on Sunday, which starts at 16:00 GMT.

The decision is caused by concern over the integrity of the Pirelli tyres on the high-speed, 16-corner track. Laps to the grid, formation laps and any laps completed after the end of the race will not be included.

Hamilton was told by his engineer in first practice tyre degradation was "much higher than expected" after highlighting his own issues.

Qatar and next week's finale in Abu Dhabi are going ahead as planned despite the ongoing conflict between the United States/Israel and Iran.
