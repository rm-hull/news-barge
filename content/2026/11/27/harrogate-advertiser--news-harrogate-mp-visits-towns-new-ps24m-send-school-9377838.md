Harrogate’s MP has visited the town’s new school for autistic young people in Harrogate which is already being hailed as a significant milestone for specialist provision across the district.

Based on the former Woodfield Primary School site in Bilton, Lighthouse School Harrogate is set to welcome its first students on to roll in the month ahead.

Operated by Lighthouse Education Trust, the school has been established to meet growing demand for specialist educational provision across Harrogate and North Yorkshire.

Students and families are now taking part in a fully supported transition programme, giving young people the opportunity to become familiar with the school, staff and daily routines so that they can get off to a good start in January 2027.

Harrogate and Knaresborough MP Tom Gordon has been pressing for Bilton Woodfield School to be upgraded to provide specialist educational provision for SEND pupils for the last two years.

Speaking after meeting staff on a tour of the new school, Mr Gordon said: “It was a pleasure to visit Lighthouse School and meet the team behind it.

“Every child deserves the chance to succeed, and for autistic young people that means having the right support in the right setting.

“This school will give them exactly that, and I know what a difference it will make to so many families across Harrogate and Knaresborough.”

Recent years have seen North Yorkshire Council facing rising demand for specialist education in the county with the number of children with Educational Health Care Plans (EHCPs) rising from 1,700 in 2015 to more than 6,000 in 2025.

The first cohort at Lighthouse School Harrogate will comprise 24 students across Years 7, 8 and 9, who, following transition, will begin full-time provision in January 2027.

The admissions process will continue to bring the cohort to 48 students by September 2027 and will provide up to 80 places when fully operational.

Emma Sullivan, Executive Principal of Lighthouse Education Trust, said: "We know how important access to high-quality specialist provision is for autistic young people and their families.

“Our team has worked incredibly hard to create a nurturing, ambitious, and inclusive environment where every student is supported to thrive academically, socially, and emotionally.

Building on the successful model established by Lighthouse Education Trust in Leeds, the school will offer a broad and ambitious curriculum alongside personalised support, speech and language and wellbeing provision, and bespoke interventions tailored to individual needs.

The school's approach is designed to help students thrive academically, socially, and emotionally while developing the skills, knowledge and experience needed for adulthood, employment, and independent living.

The repurposing for SEND pupils of Woodfield Community Primary School, which closed in 2022 after years of falling pupil numbers, seemed an ideal next step to North Yorkshire Council and in 2023 county councillors agreed a £2.4 million redevelopment to do just that.

Harrogate and Knaresborough MP Tom Gordon said: "This is a really good start in expanding specialist provision locally.

“I'll keep fighting for the SEND funding our area needs, so that every child gets the opportunity to thrive.

“I look forward to working with Lighthouse as the school opens and grows."
