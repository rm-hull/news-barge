The son of Hamas chief Khalil al-Hayya survived an assassination attempt by the Israeli military on Friday, senior Hamas official Basem Naim has said.

Naim told Reuters that Izzeldeen al-Hayya and his family survived a dawn airstrike. Hamas sources added that several people, including Hayya's daughter-in-law, were wounded, but there were no fatalities. The Israeli military offered no immediate comment.

Naim described Izzeldeen al-Hayya, born in 1997, as the Hamas chief's last surviving son, after four others were killed by Israeli forces. The family of one of the late sons also survived Friday's strike. Izzeldeen is the twin brother of Hamam Khalil al-Hayya, who was killed in a 2025 Israeli airstrike targeting Hamas leadership in Qatar. Hayya himself, who was named Hamas' overall leader in July, narrowly escaped assassination in that same Qatar attack.

In a statement, Hamas said the Israeli military had targeted a residential apartment, connecting the strike to threats made against Hayya by Israeli Defence Minister Israel Katz.

Accusing Israel of trying to undermine a 2025 Gaza ceasefire agreement, Hamas declared that Israel holds full responsibility for the consequences of its ongoing attacks. The strike follows warnings from Hayya on Thursday of potential retaliation after Israeli strikes killed at least 10 people in Gaza.

On Friday, medics reported five Palestinians were killed in two separate Israeli attacks.

The first killed three Palestinians and wounded two in Rafah; an Israeli military spokesperson said troops had struck three militants without providing extra detail. In the second attack, Palestinian medics reported two civil guards working at a Médecins Sans Frontières (MSF) office in Gaza City were killed.

Neither the Israeli military nor MSF made immediate comment, and Reuters could not immediately confirm their employment with MSF.

Although a broader ceasefire plan backed by US president Donald Trump remains in place, it has failed to halt violence or secure Hamas' disarmament. At least 1,600 Palestinians and five Israeli soldiers have been killed since the ceasefire was agreed in October 2025.

Israeli forces now control around two-thirds of Gaza, up from over half at the ceasefire, while Hamas controls a narrow coastal strip.

Since 2023, Israel's assault on Gaza has killed over 74,000 Palestinians, triggered a severe hunger crisis, displaced Gaza's entire population, and led to assessments of genocide from scholars and a United Nations inquiry.

Israel denies the genocide allegations, stating it acted in self-defence after Hamas-led militants killed 1,200 people and captured more than 250 hostages in October 2023.
