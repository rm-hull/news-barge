**Knife surrender bins, virtual reality experiences, and school visits form part of ongoing initiatives to raise awareness of knife crime risks and keep North Yorkshire communities safe.**

More than 11,000 knives and other sharp items have been handed in through specialist surrender bins across York and North Yorkshire since their introduction in 2025, as part of ongoing measures to tackle knife crime in the region.

The collection milestone has been highlighted during Operation Sceptre, a national week of action focused on knife crime awareness, prevention and education which ran until Sunday, November 22.

The bins, placed anonymously in multiple locations across the county, including Boroughbridge, Colburn, Eastfield, Harrogate, Knaresborough, Malton, Northallerton, Pickering, Richmond, Ripon, Scarborough, Selby, Settle, Skipton, Stokesley, Tadcaster, Thirsk, Whitby, and York, provide a safe way for individuals to dispose of unwanted bladed items.

The initiative is operated jointly by North Yorkshire Council, City of York Council, and North Yorkshire Police, with funding exceeding £15,000 from the York and North Yorkshire Office for Policing, Fire, Crime and Commissioning through its Serious Violence Duty Prevention and Early Intervention Fund.

> **Heather Phillips, North Yorkshire Council’s executive member for corporate services, emphasised the community’s response to the programme:**
>
> The response from our communities shows a growing recognition of the importance of this work.  
> While North Yorkshire remains a safe place to live, it is important that we continue to take proactive steps to raise awareness of the risks associated with carrying knives and help prevent harm before it occurs.  
> The knife surrender bins provide a safe and anonymous way for people to dispose of unwanted bladed items, while the work with young people in schools and community settings is helping to raise awareness of the potentially life-changing consequences of carrying a knife.  
> By combining practical initiatives with education and early intervention, ourselves and our partners can continue to make our communities safer for everyone.

As part of Operation Sceptre, police officers have been visiting schools countywide to engage with pupils on the dangers of knife crime. Meanwhile, young people in Tadcaster have taken part in an immersive virtual reality experience designed to illustrate the grave consequences of carrying knives.

The virtual reality headsets simulate realistic scenarios involving knives, allowing users to consider their choices in a safe environment.

> **Councillor Kirsty Poskitt, Independent representative for Tadcaster and lead youth support worker at The Barn community hub, commented:**
>
> The virtual reality headsets were brought to one of our youth clubs earlier this year, and we identified a number of young people who we felt would benefit from taking part in the experience.  
> We know young people can be particularly affected by issues relating to knives and violence, so it is important that we continue to have open and honest conversations about the risks.  
> The experience is a powerful and thought-provoking tool that helps young people see the potential consequences of carrying a knife in a way that feels real and relatable. Several of those who took part needed time to reflect afterwards, and our youth workers were on hand to support them and talk through what they had experienced.

> **Detective Chief Inspector Matthew Wilkinson of North Yorkshire Police acknowledged the relatively low number of incidents but affirmed that any knife incident is one too many:**
>
> This provides an opportunity to work closely with our communities, particularly young people, to highlight the serious risks and life-changing consequences associated with carrying a knife.  
> By encouraging open conversations, providing opportunities to safely surrender unwanted knives and helping people make informed choices, we can all play a part in preventing harm and keeping our communities safe.

Residents concerned about knife crime or weapons can report emergencies by calling 999 or non-emergencies on 101. Alternatively, information can be submitted anonymously online to Crimestoppers at www.crimestoppers-uk.org/.

The knife surrender bins, alongside education and outreach efforts, underline North Yorkshire’s commitment to proactive community safety despite its overall reputation as a safe place to live.
