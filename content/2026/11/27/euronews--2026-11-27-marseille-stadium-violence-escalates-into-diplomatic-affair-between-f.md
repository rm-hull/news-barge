## Clashes between Marseille and Bulgarian fans broke out on Thursday evening before the Europa League match OM–Levski Sofia; eleven Bulgarians were injured, five hospitalised.

The violence that broke out on Thursday evening at the Vélodrome stadium has taken on a diplomatic dimension. French President Emmanuel Macron and Bulgarian President Iliana Iotova reacted from Brussels, where they were attending the European summit. Eleven Bulgarian supporters were injured in the clashes, five of whom had to be taken to hospital.

Emmanuel Macron condemned with "the *greatest firmness*" the violence against Bulgarian supporters. He spoke with the Bulgarian president and assured her that the incidents "* would not go unpunished*".

Iliana Iotova spoke of "*horrifying*" images and "unacceptable" violence. She called on the French authorities to fully clarify the attacks and on UEFA to check the security conditions in the stadium.

The Bulgarian Ministry of Foreign Affairs also condemned "*firmly the assaults committed against Bulgarian nationals in Marseille*".

## What happened at the Vélodrome?

The causes of the incidents are still unclear, with the facts still being established. According to the newspaper *L'Equipe* *(source in French)*, around ten minutes before kick-off, scheduled for 18:45, Bulgarian fans seated in the side stand Jean-Bouin, outside the visitors' section, are said to have repeatedly provoked the Marseille ultras.

Nazi salutes were among the gestures observed. A video shared on social networks *(source in French)* shows one of these supporters making such a gesture before being restrained by a security guard.

The situation quickly escalated. Several dozen OM supporters, mainly from the Virage Sud, left their stand to move towards the Jean-Bouin and confront the roughly 500 Bulgarians who had taken their seats to watch the match.

Blows were exchanged, sometimes using flagpoles, along with missiles and flares being thrown. Some Marseille supporters were wearing balaclavas. Security staff and riot police eventually intervened to separate the two sides. Tear gas was also used to disperse those involved.

According to the Bouches-du-Rhône police prefecture, eleven Bulgarians were injured. Five were taken to hospital, notably for eye and head trauma. No arrests were initially reported.

Olympique de Marseille also condemned the incidents and announced that it wanted to establish the circumstances precisely. The team eventually won 1-0, in a match that was also marked by further tensions at half-time.
