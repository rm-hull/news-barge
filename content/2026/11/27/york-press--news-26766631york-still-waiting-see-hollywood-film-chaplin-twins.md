## York still waiting to see Hollywood film about its famous Chaplin twins

Freda and Greta Chaplin - screen shot taken from A Pair of One, documentary by Juliet Darling Image: Supplied

More than two months after a Hollywood film inspired by York's most famous twins premiered on the world stage, audiences in their home city are still waiting to see it.

Bucking Fastard, based on the extraordinary lives of Freda and Greta Chaplin from Tang Hall, made its debut at the Venice International Film Festival in September.

Yet despite attracting global attention, the film has still not been released in York – nor is it available on streaming platforms.

The film arrives more than five years after Freda's death and introduces the remarkable story of the Chaplin twins to a new international audience.

Hollywood stars Rooney Mara, known for The Girl with the Dragon Tattoo and Carol, and her real-life sister Kate Mara, who appeared in Brokeback Mountain and Fantastic Four, play sisters Jean and Joan Holbrooke, characters inspired by Freda and Greta.

![Rooney Mara, top right, and her sister Kate, inset, play York twins Freda and Greta Chaplin, main image, in a new Hollywood film Bucking Fastard](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/19426260.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Rooney Mara, top right, and her sister Kate, inset, play York twins Freda and Greta Chaplin, main image, in a new Hollywood film Bucking Fastard

Directed by acclaimed German filmmaker Werner Herzog and featuring Orlando Bloom and Domhnall Gleeson, the production tells a story that has fascinated journalists, documentary-makers and the public for decades.

Born in April 1943, Freda and Greta grew up in Tang Hall and spent virtually every moment of their lives together.

Although they appeared much like any other twins as children, their bond became increasingly intense as they grew older. By the age of 11 they had become deeply dependent on one another and later withdrew from much of the outside world, often spending long periods at home.

Their relationship captivated those who encountered them. They dressed identically, moved as mirror images and frequently spoke in perfect unison.

The sisters first attracted widespread public attention in the early 1980s when they appeared before York Magistrates' Court following a breach of the peace allegation.

👭 **Famous for speaking in unison** - Freda and Greta Chaplin became internationally known for dressing alike, moving together and talking at exactly the same time.

⭐ **Star-studded cast** - Sisters Rooney Mara and Kate Mara play characters based on the twins in the film directed by Werner Herzog.

🏡 **York roots** - Born and raised in Tang Hall, the twins' extraordinary lives fascinated the public, filmmakers and journalists for more than 40 years.

What might otherwise have been a routine local court case became international news. Reporters were astonished as Freda and Greta answered questions together, spoke the same words at the same time and insisted on their innocence in unison.

Their unusual behaviour sparked worldwide fascination and transformed the York sisters into unlikely celebrities.

That attention led to several documentaries, including Julia Darling's acclaimed A Pair of One in 1987 and The Twins by Journeyman Pictures in 1994.

Greta died from cancer in 2007 aged 64. Friends later recalled how Freda regularly visited her sister's grave at Fulford Cemetery.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/11909072.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Greta and Freda Chaplin, famous York twins, who are now buried together at Fulford Cemetery following Freda's death

Following Greta's death, Freda devoted much of her time to caring for animals and supporting causes close to her heart.

When she died in May 2020 aged 77, she left most of her £177,000 estate, including the family home in Tang Hall, to pet charity the Cinnamon Trust.

In keeping with her wishes, she was buried alongside Greta.

Today, the sisters rest together at Fulford Cemetery, just as they spent their lives side by side.

Their story, however, continues to fascinate.

What began in a modest York home has now reached Hollywood, with one of cinema's most distinctive directors bringing their lives to the big screen.

In his memoir Every Man for Himself and God Against All, Herzog recalled being mesmerised by the twins after meeting them in person.

He wrote that they wore identical clothes, carried matching handbags and umbrellas, and moved so perfectly together that "you could have folded a picture of them, and the two halves would have matched".

For now, York film fans are still waiting to discover exactly how that extraordinary story has been brought to life on screen.
