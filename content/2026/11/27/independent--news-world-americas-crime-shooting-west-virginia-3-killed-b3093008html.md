A West Virginia man is accused of killing three people, including his former girlfriend's mother and grandparents, after opening fire at their home earlier this week.

Alex Lee Fuston, 26, of Bunker Hill, was charged with three counts of first-degree murder in connection to the Monday shooting deaths of Karen Woodward, 47, and her parents, Edna Woodward, 72, and Woodrow Woodward, 75, according to court records and WV News. He was booked into the Jefferson County jail in the early hours of Friday morning without bail.

The shootings happened at a home on Lisa Lane in Kearneysville. Fuston was arrested in Berkeley County and was scheduled to appear before a magistrate Friday, although court records did not show an initial appearance had taken place, according to the outlet.

West Virginia State Police responded to the home at 6:28 p.m. Monday after receiving a report of gunfire, according to a criminal complaint reviewed by WV News.

The suspected gunman opened fire as he approached the house, striking Karen Woodward on the porch, investigators said. She ran into a bedroom, where she later died from her injuries. The shooter then went inside and fatally shot Edna and Woodrow Woodward. All three victims were shot multiple times.

![Alex Lee Fuston is accused of three people at a West Virginia home where his ex lived](https://static.the-independent.com/2026/11/27/19/52/Fuston-Alex-Lee-3724862_Mug_Front_20261127014713.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Investigators identified Fuston as the former romantic partner of a woman who lived at the home. That woman survived the shooting along with two other people.

The woman told police Fuston once told her to kill herself after she suffered a pregnancy loss in August 2023. She also said she had another child in August 2026, according to the complaint.

During the shooting, the woman and the two other witnesses heard the gunman yell, "Where is [the ex-lover's name]" and "my son is dead," the complaint reportedly states.

The woman also told police she received a Snapchat message from one of Fuston's sisters within an hour of the shooting, asking if she was OK. The message arrived before details of the killings had been made public. She said she had not spoken to the sister since the 2023 pregnancy loss.

Witness statements, vehicle descriptions and cellphone records helped investigators identify Fuston and place his phone near the shooting scene. Troopers arrested him Tuesday and searched three properties connected to him.

At his Isabelle Drive home, investigators found an empty box for a Glock 26 Gen4 9 mm pistol in his bedroom, along with ammunition from three brands: Blazer, FC 9 mm Luger and WMAVO. The same three brands of ammunition were found in shell casings at the crime scene.

The casings were consistent with ammunition fired from a Glock or Taurus firearm, the Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco, Firearms and Explosives determined, according to court records.

During a search of the Plank Bridge Road property, troopers found the Glock pistol with the matching serial number hidden inside a television stand drawer. They also found shoes that appeared to have been cleaned, including one missing a shoelace.

*The Independent* has contacted West Virginia State Police for comment.
