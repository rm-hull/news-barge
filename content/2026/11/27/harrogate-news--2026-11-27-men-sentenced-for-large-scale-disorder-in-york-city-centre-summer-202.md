**A group of men from South Yorkshire have received suspended prison sentences, unpaid work orders, and five-year city centre bans after pleading guilty to affray during violent disorder in York in July 2025.**

A number of men originating from South Yorkshire have been sentenced following their involvement in a large-scale disorder in York city centre during the summer of 2025. The offenders pleaded guilty to affray, with sentencing taking place at Leeds Crown Court on 25 November 2026.

The incident occurred on Saturday 26 July 2025 at approximately 7.45pm outside the Slug and Lettuce bar near Ouse Bridge in York. The disorder began when two members of the group, identified as Corey Hall and Thomas Salt, assaulted members of the public inside the bar without provocation. Hall was escorted from the premises by door staff, but approximately a dozen men from his group followed them outside and initiated violent attacks on door staff and bystanders.

Despite door staff retreating inside and securing the premises, the group forced the doors open by pushing against them with significant force. Other members of the group then joined in assaulting staff and members of the public inside the venue. The violence continued on Low Ousegate as police officers arrived.

Multiple door staff sustained injuries including facial cuts and bruises, while Kevin Hubbuck was hospitalised with a bruised and swollen eye, broken ribs, and punctured lungs following his arrest.

Following police investigations supported by public CCTV appeals, the following men, all from the South Yorkshire area, were sentenced after pleading guilty to affray at York Magistrates Court on 27 October 2026:

* Corey Hall, 24, Woodsetts (Rotherham): 18-month prison sentence suspended for 24 months; excluded from York city centre for five years; 300 hours unpaid work; £500 compensation; £133 CPS costs; £187 surcharge.
* Alex Newton, 27, North Anston (Rotherham): 15-month prison sentence suspended for two years; five-year city centre exclusion; 240 hours unpaid work; £200 compensation; £133 CPS costs; £187 surcharge.
* Corey Walker, 21, North Anston (Rotherham): nine-month prison sentence suspended for 18 months; five-year exclusion; 180 hours unpaid work; £500 compensation; £133 CPS costs; £187 surcharge.
* Louis Hellewell, 27, North Anston (Rotherham): nine-month prison sentence suspended for 18 months; five-year exclusion; 200 hours unpaid work; £133 CPS costs; £187 surcharge.
* Keegan Hubbuck, 24, Sheffield: nine-month prison sentence suspended for 18 months; five-year exclusion; 180 hours unpaid work; £133 CPS costs; £187 surcharge.
* Kevin Hubbuck, 48, Sheffield: nine-month prison sentence suspended for 18 months; five-year exclusion; 180 hours unpaid work; £133 CPS costs; £187 surcharge.
* Jamie Pilkington, 34, Dinnington (Rotherham): nine-month prison sentence suspended for 18 months; five-year exclusion; 180 hours unpaid work; £200 compensation; £133 CPS costs; £187 surcharge.
* Luke Gee, 24, Dinnington (Rotherham): five-year exclusion; 180 hours unpaid work; £133 CPS costs; £114 surcharge.
* Gary Bacon, 52, North Anston (Rotherham): five-year exclusion; 60 hours unpaid work; £133 CPS costs; £114 surcharge.
* Matthew Gee, 50, Dinnington (Rotherham): five-year exclusion; 60 hours unpaid work; £133 CPS costs; £114 surcharge.
* Carter Salt, 21, North Anston (Rotherham): five-year exclusion; 60 hours unpaid work; £133 CPS costs; £114 surcharge.
* Terrence Ashmore, 51, Dinnington (Rotherham): five-year exclusion; £200 fine; £133 CPS costs; £80 surcharge.
* Euan Bacon, 25, North Anston (Rotherham): five-year exclusion; £200 fine; £133 CPS costs; £80 surcharge.
* Joe Wallhead, 29, Dinnington (Rotherham): five-year exclusion; £100 fine; £133 CPS costs; £40 surcharge.
* Thomas Salt, 33, Dinnington (Rotherham): 15-month prison sentence suspended for 24 months; five-year exclusion; 240 hours unpaid work; £200 compensation; £133 CPS costs; £187 surcharge.

> **Assistant Chief Constable Ben Moseley of North Yorkshire Police praised members of the public for their assistance following CCTV appeals, stating:**
>
> Firstly, I would like to thank everyone who contacted us following the release of our CCTV appeals. The information provided by members of the public was instrumental in helping us identify a number of those responsible.
>
> This shocking outbreak of violence took place on a key street in York city centre, in full view of members of the public, including children and families. The impact on those who witnessed it was clear, with people left distressed and fearful as the disorder unfolded around them.
>
> Some individuals were forced to put themselves at risk to protect others from harm, while parents had to quickly move their children away from the violence. No one should have to experience scenes like this while enjoying a day or night out in our city.
>
> York remains a safe city, and today’s sentencing sends a clear message that this type of violence has no place on our streets. We will continue to work tirelessly to identify offenders, bring them before the courts, and ensure those who threaten the safety of our communities are held accountable.

The sentences include a mix of suspended prison terms, unpaid work requirements, compensation payments, fines, and five-year exclusions from York city centre to prevent further disturbances. This case reflects ongoing efforts by police and courts to maintain safety in popular public spaces across North Yorkshire.
