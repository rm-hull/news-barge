## ‘Concerning’ update on future of iconic cliff lift

Spa Cliff Lift. Copyright Hayley Green, Geograph. *(Image: Hayley Green, Geograph.)*

BRITAIN’S oldest cliff lift is unlikely to reopen before next summer’s Spa season, councillors have been warned.

Hopes that Scarborough’s Spa Cliff Lift could be repaired and reopened by next spring have been cast in doubt after an update on the various issues that could slow down work to bring the Victorian-era attraction back into service.

At a meeting on Friday, councillors were told that any reopening could still be months away, and repairs are unlikely to be completed by the Spa’s next summer season, which runs from July to September.

Members of the Scarborough and Whitby Area Committee backed a motion calling on North Yorkshire Council to allocate funding for the repair of the lift as well as Whitby’s West Cliff Lift, which has been shut since 2022.

However, members were told to manage expectations because of the scale of investigations required and the planning and heritage processes involved with a Grade-II Listed structure.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21356991.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Scarborough'S Spa Cliff Lift. Courtesy North Yorkshire Council.

The Spa Cliff Lift was closed in August after an independent engineering assessment identified problems affecting tracks, sleepers, braking rails, carriages and other safety-critical infrastructure.

The council-run funicular is separate from the Central Tramway near the Town Hall, which continues to operate.

Council officers said that a proposal setting out how the lift could be reopened through a phased programme of inspections, testing and repairs, had been received.

However, head of harbours and coastal infrastructure Chris Bourne warned that significant uncertainty remains, and told councillors that specialists would need to carry out tests on key components before the extent of the required repairs could be fully understood.

“The question is how much work have you got to do to reopen it?” he said.

Under the proposed approach, work would begin with the highest-risk elements first, including the lift’s carriages and safety systems.

Mr Bourne said costs could not yet be disclosed publicly because the council was still considering procurement options.

He added that the listed status of the attraction could also create lengthy delays.

“It’s a listed structure and may have to go through some quite rigorous processes with Historic England and others,” he told the meeting.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21774604.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Scarborough And Whitby Area Committee 27.11.26. Numminen, LDRS

While there was strong support among councillors to see the attraction reopened as soon as possible, committee chair Cllr Liz Colling said residents should be realistic about the timetable.

She told the meeting: “To manage expectations, if we thought Scarborough Cliff Lift might be open for this summer’s performances at the Spa, I think it’s unlikely.”

Mr Bourne agreed, warning that any heritage approvals alone could take several months.

The issue of funding is also yet to be decided. A council report states there is currently no capital funding allocated for repairs to the lifts in Scarborough or Whitby.

Mr Bourne said officers were pursuing a staged approach designed to get the lift operating again as soon as possible, but stressed that the process would take time.

He said all of the concerns raised by councillors and members of the public would be fed into a briefing for North Yorkshire Council’s executive, which is due to receive an update on the project in December.
