# Drivers urged to put household staple on dashboards to help escape possible fine

## Drivers are being urged to place the item in their car to help tackle car condensation

Motorists are being advised to place tea bags on their car dashboard as a means of avoiding potential fines. Ben Pitcher from DPF Experts recommends positioning tea bags around your vehicle, such as on the dashboard or in the door pockets, to cut down on the time spent waiting for windows to demist.

He explained: "Tea bags, especially black or green tea, are natural moisture absorbers. Placing them in your car can help reduce condensation, especially in colder months. Though they aren't as powerful as professional dehumidifiers, they're a cheap, eco-friendly solution for tackling minor dampness."

Both green and black tea are capable of soaking up the moisture responsible for condensation that forms when there's a temperature difference between the inside and outside of a vehicle. By distributing tea bags or small quantities of loose tea throughout the car's interior, including on the dashboard or in door pockets, drivers can significantly reduce the likelihood of their windscreen misting over, reports Birmingham Live.

Beyond their effectiveness in combating condensation, tea bags can also serve as a natural air freshener, potentially enhancing the smell of your vehicle by absorbing unpleasant odours and replacing them with the inviting scent of a freshly brewed cup of tea.

Ben further suggests that drivers can achieve comparable de-fogging results by making use of small silica gel packets. Commonly discovered in the packaging of electronic devices, these small sachets can also help prevent moisture build-up on car windscreens during those frosty mornings.

"Silica gel packets, commonly found in packaging, are excellent at absorbing moisture," he explained. "Collecting a few and placing them around your car can help control condensation. While they won't clear foggy windows instantly, they can help reduce moisture and keep your windscreen clearer for longer."

It's essential for motorists to ensure their vehicle's windows are completely free from condensation before setting off. Under Rule 229 of the Highway Code, drivers must maintain clear visibility by eliminating all obstructions that could create dangerous blind spots.

Motorists face increased danger of causing a collision if they drive with misted-up windows, potentially failing to notice other road users, and could be hit with penalties between £100 and £2,500 if apprehended in such circumstances.
