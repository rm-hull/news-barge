A Formula 1 boss and his mum are suing for £1.1m after tons of “sludge” from nearby roads ended up in the lake at their £2.2m manor house - smothering their prized lily pads and preventing them boating on the waters.

Pamela Oakes, 71- mum of former racing driver and Alpine F1 team boss Ollie Oakes - along with husband Billy, 69, moved into idyllic Hopton Manor, near Great Yarmouth, in 1993, converting the existing coaching house and stables on the site to create a home for them and their four kids.

The house came with a large lake known as “the fish pond” complete with tench, pike and roach, and a cluster of elegant lily pads - which the family used for boating when their children were young.

But the family claims the lake has since been ruined by repeated discharges of “silt and pollutants” into the water - which they blame on “highway runoff” - and are now suing National Highways Ltd and Norfolk County Council as managers of the nearby roads.

![Aerial view of Hopton Manor Lake as it appears now](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/27/8/04/HoptonManorLakeAfter4.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

With the property now held in the names of Mrs Oakes and the children - former racing driver and Alpine F1 team boss Ollie Oakes and siblings Jordan, Shankara and William - they are demanding more than £1.1m to cover the cost of cleaning it up and restoring it to its former beauty.

Speaking outside court, forklift firm boss Billy Oakes - who is not a party to the claim - said the lake had been a source of great enjoyment for the family in the past, but now, "nobody wants to use it because we know there’s contamination in there”.

"We are now unable to boat on the lake, or let our dogs and pets swim in the lake, or family or guests to fish in the lake," he explained.

![Billy Oakes outside High Court after hearing in dispute over pollution at Hopton Manor Lake](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/27/8/02/BillyOakes1.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

But both National Highways and Norfolk County Council deny liability to compensate the Oakes family, with lawyers claiming they were entitled to discharge surface water into the lake and that much of the silt in the lake is caused by "naturally occurring vegetation."

Mr and Mrs Oakes purchased Hopton Manor and moved in in 1993, converting the old coach house and stables to provide a home for the couple and their young children.

Photographic evidence put before the High Court shows the children when they were young, playing beside the idyllic lake, which was home to swans and lily pads.

But the family say the waters later began to silt up, with Mr Oakes explaining outside court that they consider 100 metres of it to be blighted by the effect of “polluted surface waters," including algae and reeds growing on top of the silt.

![The Oakes family's Hopton Manor Lake as it appeared before significant silting](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/27/8/28/HoptonManorLakeBefore3.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“When we moved in there were lots of lily pads there, but they’re now completely swamped with reeds," he said, adding that fighting the case had already cost the family £650,000 in lawyers' bills.

Before Mr Justice Thompsell, Mrs Oakes and the children are suing for trespass and nuisance, blaming polluted water from surrounding drains for the condition of the lake.

Their lawyers say that silting in the lake was the result of rainwater - containing hydrocarbons and heavy metals - running off local roads, particularly the A47, which runs near to the property.

![Pamela Oakes outside High Court after hearing in dispute over pollution at Hopton Manor Lake](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/27/8/50/PamelaOakes1.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“The aforesaid roads and, on the claimants’ case, their verges and certain associated areas, drain their surface water through the culverts of the A47 and into the lake,” said their barrister, Clifford Darton KC.

He said the highway authority had a clear duty to properly maintain the drainage system near the lake and any “silt traps” that could block pollutants seeping into the lake, which they failed to do.

“Put simply, the claimants contend that the defendants must stop discharging polluted surface water into the lake and pay for the costs of clearing up the damage that has been done by these discharges,” he said.

The family is suing on the basis that polluted drain overflow into the Hopton Manor lake is an “unlawful interference" with their property, also arguing that the runoff breached their rights to respect for privacy, family life and the “peaceful enjoyment of possessions” as safeguarded by Articles 1 and 8 of the European Convention on Human Rights.

Experts testifying for the Oakes family say removing the silt debris from the lake could cost over £1m, while the sale value of their £2.2m country home has also been slashed to just over £1m by the sludge scourge.

![The Oakes family's Hopton Manor Lake as it appeared before significant silting](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/27/8/18/HoptonManorLakeBefore1.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Part of the solution suggested by their experts is the creation of an “attenuation” lake nearby to hold excess rainfall water and guard it from running off onto Hopton Manor land.

As well as claiming a damages payout, the family are seeking court injunctions against both the council and highway authority, forcing them to prevent future runoff and carry out works to prevent surface water seeping onto their land.

The highway authority is being sued in relation to alleged failure to control water runoff from the main road, while the council is being sued in relation to its upkeep of the smaller access roads on a housing estate.

Both deny liability to compensate the Oakes family, with the council also arguing that the case against it is time-barred as the family left it too late to sue.

In addition, the highways authority insists it had a right to discharge into the lake in line with the terms of a land conveyance dating back to the 1970’s.

Additionally, much of the material in the lake is either "lawfully deposited highway silt" or "naturally occurring organic matter," for which it is not responsible.

It also disputes that it was under a legal duty to “purify” the surface water before its discharge into the Hopton Manor lake.

"The discharge of surface water from the A47 into the lake, including the ordinary suspended solids and road residues which such water carries, is authorised by the express terms of the 1973 conveyance and, independently, by statute," said barrister Aaron Walder.

"Lawful user of an easement, and alternatively the exercise of a statutory right, is neither a trespass nor an actionable nuisance.

"For the avoidance of doubt, National Highways has admitted that the water discharged through the pipe contains suspended solids and hazardous substances in the general sense pleaded.

"What National Highways denies is that the presence of those substances, at the levels which ordinary road run-off carries, is outside the scope of the rights granted, or gives rise to any cause of action."

For the council, barrister Simon Vaughan says it is "lawful and permissible" for surface waters from its roads to flow onto the A47 and then discharge into the lake via its drains, from which the highways authority has a right to discharge runoff.

The case has been brought to court by Pamela Oakes, together with her kids, former racing driver and Alpine F1 team boss Ollie Oakes, 38, and siblings William, Shankara and Jordan.

His dad, Billy Oakes, boss of a major forklift and construction aids company, was in court to support the claim, although he is not a named party, since Hopton Manor is jointly owned in his wife and children’s names.

The judge is expected to give a ruling on the case at a later date.
