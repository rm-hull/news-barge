The good news for Howard Lutnick and Jensen Huang is Republicans will hold the gavels when they testify to Congress over the next two weeks. The bad news is that Republicans have plenty of questions for them, too, and Democrats are expected to haul the duo right back in January.

But Donald Trump’s Commerce Secretary and the recently bailed-out CEO of Nvidia will first appear before members of Congress as protected members of the president’s inner circle: one as a barely tolerated longtime Trump ally at the center of the Nvidia scandal who coughed up millions of dollars for Trump’s library, and the other a disgraced tech executive who Trump helped become one of the most powerful chipmakers on the planet.

Huang is set to testify behind closed doors before a group of House committees on December 1. Nvidia’s general counsel Tim Teter is also scheduled to testify before the same committees on December 2.

Huang is on the agenda to testify publicly to the Senate Banking Committee on December 3 for a hearing called “Protecting America’s AI Advantage: Oversight of U.S. Export Controls.” He is the only witness.

Lutnick is then scheduled to testify to that same committee on December 10.

![Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick and Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang are set to testify to members of Congress in several hearings in the wake of an alleged spy scandal and Donald Trump’s intervention to bring Huang back to the company after his resignation](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/27/21/2212184816..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Their testimony will likely only scratch the surface. Company employees and little-known figures within the administration and elsewhere should also be prepared for testimony in the coming months and begin sharing communications, emails, calendars and text messages with lawmakers who are eager to dig into Nvidia’s records and find out more about Huang’s relationship with an alleged Chinese spy and the sale of Nvidia chips to China.

Democratic Senators Elizabeth Warren and Chris Coons have already sent letters to Lutnick, the State Department and the White House ordering officials to preserve their records ahead of expected investigations when Democrats take control of both chambers of Congress next year.

## **Who knew about Huang’s affairs and when?**

The salaciousness is driving the headlines, but Congress is eager to find out whether the president and other top administration officials as well as Nvidia’s board were aware of Huang’s relationship with Alice Huang (no relation) and what, exactly, he shared with her.

Can the government be sure that Jensen Huang, or administration officials or the president, for that matter, were not being blackmailed by the Chinese government?

Alice Huang is a former executive at Megaspeed, the Singapore-based company accused of diverting Nvidia chips to China in violation of U.S. trade restrictions. She reportedly left the company last year, but her relationship with Nvidia’s CEO has put them at the center of a scandal with major implications for the international AI arms race and a volatile American economy that depends heavily on it.

![Lawmakers will need to grill Huang and Trump administration officials about whether the president and Cabinet members were aware of allegations against the Nvidia CEO and why the US continued to approve chip sales despite them](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/24/14/2026-11-24T110016Z_703340578_RC2G8EABEPTL_RTRMADP_3_NVIDIA-CEO-BOARD.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

If intelligence officials were warning people close to Trump more than a year ago, why was Huang never given a defensive briefing, and why did the administration continue approving chip sales to China? What did Huang tell Trump when they were repeatedly meeting at high-profile events over the last year?

## **What was Lutnick up to?**

Lutnick’s Commerce Department was reportedly investigating Megapseed last year. Teter, Nvidia’s general counsel, reportedly reached out to investigators after getting permission from one of Lutnick’s deputies. The Department of Justice then took control of the probe.

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent told CNBC this month that his agency, the Department of Defense and others all wanted Megaspeed blacklisted over risks of diverting advanced computing hardware, “but Commerce has the pen.” Commerce did not move until November 23.

Lutnick reportedly took efforts to unwind restrictions on Megaspeed out of concern over escalating trade friction with China, but Rep. Jamie Raskin, the incoming chair of the powerful House Judiciary Committee, has alleged that Lutnick and other administration officials have converging and overlapping personal, political and financial interests involving Huang and Nvidia’s success.

![Lutnick is under fire for allegedly refusing to enforce trade restrictions against Megaspeed until Jensen Huang’s relationship with a former CEO accused of working for Chinese intelligence was disclosed](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/25/18/2026-11-24T192933Z_70575736_RC2JUOAC1D5Z_RTRMADP_3_USA-TRUMP.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

How did Lutnick’s relationship with Nvidia, and Nvidia’s relationship with Megaspeed, influence decision-making at either company as well as the inner workings of the federal government?

Republican Senator Dave McCormick of Pennsylvania, a member of the Banking Committee, said lawmakers need more information about Nvidia’s compliance with export restrictions.

“Congress needs answers on whether any laws were broken, whether national security was compromised, and whether export controls in place were circumvented,” he wrote this week.

## **How did Trump bring Huang back to Nvidia?**

By Trump’s own admission, the president’s blatant intervention to rescue Jensen Huang from exile after resigning from Nvidia relied on advice from the Department of Justice and his former criminal defense attorney Todd Blanche, who is now U.S. Attorney General.

Trump declaring that Blanche “confirmed that the U.S. Government does not possess evidence that he has engaged in any unlawful conduct.” Nvidia’s press release also claimed that the decision was based on “input from senior levels of the United States government.”

The president later declared the company’s CEO was “exonerated.” But Blanche has refused to address whether the Justice Department ever investigated Huang or whether he believes Nvidia violated export controls.

“What promises were made to flip the board?” Democratic Senator Lisa Blunt Rochester asked this week. “How can this administration prove there is no national security risk?”

Senator Andy Kim, another Democrat on the Banking Committee, also shared reporting that Trump may have made as much as $15 million on Nvidia shares after tech stock rallied with news of Huang’s return as CEO.

“We hear the president’s motives loud and clear,” Kim wrote.
