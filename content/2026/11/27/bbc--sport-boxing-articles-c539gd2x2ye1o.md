**Tyson Fury has been medically cleared to fight Anthony Joshua in December after the bout was put in jeopardy by claims of a schizophrenia diagnosis.**

Fury, 38, spoke of his mental health struggles in a Netflix documentary which was released on Friday, with his wife, Paris, saying he had been diagnosed with schizophrenia.

But Paris has since retracted that statement, saying he "doesn't have this condition" and Netflix have edited the remarks out of their film.

The British Boxing Board of Control (BBBoC) told BBC Sport: "Tyson Fury has been cleared to box on 11 December."

Fury faces Joshua, 37, in a long overdue all-British heavyweight contest at Cardiff's Principality Stadium in the early hours of 12 December.

Originally, the Netflix documentary featured the Furys recalling his admission to hospital in June 2016 with a suspected heart attack.

Doctors said he had suffered an anxiety attack. He was diagnosed with schizophrenia after he left hospital, according to the film.

The NHS describes schizophrenia as a form of psychosis, and symptoms can include hallucinations, delusions and muddled thoughts and speech.

Under regulation 8.1(b) of the British Boxing Board of Control's 2026 rules and regulations, schizophrenia is among the conditions that "may preclude" a boxer from obtaining a first licence, while for an existing licence holder the board "may take such action as in its absolute discretion as it sees fit".

On Thursday, the BBBoC general secretary Robert Smith told BBC Sport that it was "in the process of dealing with the matter" and that it was "confident he [Tyson Fury] will be absolutely fine".

With just two weeks until the British rivals meet for the vacant WBA 'super' heavyweight world title, Fury has been fully cleared to compete.

Fury, who has retired from professional boxing on five occasions, first stepped away from the sport following his world title win over Wladimir Klitschko in November 2015.

In the Netflix documentary, Fury describes a "turning point" in 2017 when he was on a night out for Halloween and had spent two years drinking and taking drugs.

"I went out and I was embarrassed by how I looked," Fury said.

"I was a fat mess and I had a glow-in-the-dark skeleton suit with full face mask on.

"I looked around and could see all the young people and I thought, 'I had the world in my hands and I threw it all away, and now I'm a fat mess addicted to alcohol'."

He says that was the beginning of a comeback which led him to focus more on his family and to return to training.

Fury is a two-time heavyweight world champion and has lost just two of his 39 professional contests.
