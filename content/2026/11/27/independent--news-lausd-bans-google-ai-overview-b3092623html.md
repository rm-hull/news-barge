America's largest school districts are moving to shut off Google's artificial intelligence features in the classroom, and some are weighing whether to abandon the company's products altogether unless it provides a permanent way to remove AI from its tools.

The standoff sets the districts against a company whose Chromebook laptops and education software are woven into daily school life across the United States. Los Angeles school officials have taken the most significant step so far, turning off AI-generated summaries, known as AI Overview, from Google searches on school-issued devices.

The decision makes Los Angeles Unified the largest district to sideline the company, which has said it will not separate its search product from AI tools for education. The district, which enrolls half a million students, has not made a formal announcement.

![Los Angeles school officials have disabled AI-generated summaries in Google searches on school-issued devices](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/12/15/AI-Lawsuit_94174.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The move caught parents and students by surprise, said Bridie Lee, a leader of Schools Beyond Screens LA, a group that has pushed for screen-time limits and tech oversight in the district. Several members of the group spoke at a meeting of the Los Angeles school board's A.I. committee on Friday, Nov. 13.

By that Monday, AI Overview was gone. “It was the talk of the playground,” Lee said. Google's position in America's classrooms is dominant.

Its low-cost Chromebooks are the most widely used student laptops, and its suite of education apps, including Google Docs and Classroom, has become integral to classwork, homework and communication among students and teachers. Concerns about AI's impact on learning have ignited a battle between schools and the company.

In September, the country's two largest school districts, New York and LA, announced one-year moratoriums on student use of generative AI. Google has said it can't remove AI from its search products, and schools are considering other internet search providers.

![Google’s Chromebook laptops are the most widely used among students in America](https://static.independent.co.uk/2025/09/12/08/Artificial_Intelligence_Cheating_82502.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

LAUSD said in a statement it temporarily suspended AI Overview to ensure student experiences are “age-appropriate and safe.” The district also said it continually evaluates its digital safeguards, including third-party tools. It likely used such a tool to disable AI Overview.

Schools Beyond Screens LA asked parents to send videos of what laptop screens looked like. The clips they received showed that AI Overview was no longer an option in Google search on district devices. Activists then reached out to the district, which confirmed AI Overview was disabled.

Lee said they had no other details on how it was done or how long it is intended to stay disabled. “We’re thrilled LAUSD did this,” said Lee. “AI is seeping into everything. It’s dangerous. It’s hurting learning. And it’s a corporate overreach into public schools. It’s Big Tech attempting to strong-arm tech policy without anyone’s consent.”

Other districts have taken similar steps, according to multiple published reports. Fairfax County Public Schools in Virginia has developed plug-ins to remove AI summaries for students searching on Google.

The backlash against technology in schools has been growing, starting with cellphones and screen time and now focusing on AI, amid concerns that students are using AI to think for them.

![Google CEO Sundar Pichai reacts as President Donald Trump speaks at the White House driveway following a luncheon in the East Room](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/09/30/09/2026-09-29T215630Z_1763166875_RC29TNA0XX9U_RTRMADP_3_USA-TRUMP.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The Mountain View, California-based company found itself at the center of controversy in August. That was when it said it had turned on its Gemini chatbot for many K-12 students using Google Classroom, its learning management system.

Weeks later, New York City and LA schools banned generative AI. In New York, the ban applies to all elementary and middle school students. In Los Angeles, all students K-12 are banned from using AI on school devices. Both districts said the ban would allow officials to develop AI policies this academic year.

Then, in early November, Google backtracked. It published a blog post saying it would instead turn off Gemini and all search tools by default for K-12 schools in the coming months. Administrators would have to turn on each tool if they wanted students to access them.

Google said this was to give schools more choice. But the choice to turn off AI altogether is not there.

While the blog post doesn't explicitly say search can't be used without AI, Chris Phillips, Google's vice president for education, told *The New York Times*: “Search and AI are deeply integrated.” If schools ban AI, students “will not have access to search, and they’ll see a message from their school that indicates it’s not available.”

AI integration in search isn't new. Google introduced AI Overview in 2025, and anyone who uses Google Search will get AI answers for many searches. Google insists AI Overview is an integral part of its search product now and cannot be separated.

![Google introduced AI Overview in 2025, and anyone who uses Google Search will get AI answers for many searches](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/14/16/2026-10-13T160324Z_1336090057_RC29YJADA4C6_RTRMADP_3_GOOGLE-AI.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Earlier this month, hundreds of Google employees signed a petition asking the company's leadership to allow schools to turn off AI features “without disrupting their ability to use search as a whole.”

“The way the decision has been reported in the press is poor, and makes Google, and our Education and Search products as a result, look like we’re punishing schools for not using AI,” the petition said. “Many students, educators and parents have concerns about AI in the classroom, as is shown by many school districts who have placed moratoriums or bans on such technology. It is our job as Googlers to respect the user, and listen to them when they have concerns about our products.”

Google says it hears parents' and teachers' concerns about students using AI to “shortcut learning” and that it shares the same goal: helping students learn.

But what many parents and teachers want- the ability to bypass AI entirely and allow students to use Google search like it was in, say, 2024- is not something Google offers. This has forced districts across the U.S. to choose between restricting students' access to AI and maintaining access to the popular search engine.

Instead of offering an AI-free search product for schools, Google announced a pilot “guided learning” tool that walks students “step-by-step through a range of complex problems, instead of just giving quick answers.”

“We are actively building these experiences in partnership with educators, listening closely to ensure our products support students and teachers,” the company said in a statement.
