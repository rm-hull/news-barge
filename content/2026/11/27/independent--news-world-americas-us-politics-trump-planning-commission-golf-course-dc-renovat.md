A federal planning commission stuffed with Donald Trump’s allies is expected to hold a meeting on New Year’s Eve to rush approval for the president’s Washington, D.C. construction projects before Democrats take control of Congress next year.

Trump has packed the 12-person National Capital Planning Commission with six of his appointees. Four others represent D.C., and two were appointed by Republican members of Congress.

When Democrats control both chambers next year, they’ll get two seats currently held by Republicans. Nothing on the committee’s agenda can move forward on a tie, giving Democrats an opening to derail the president’s projects.

But Mark Paoletta, a top White House official who Trump appointed to steer the 12-member panel, has called a special executive session December 31, days before Democrats take control of Congress, according to an email sent to members Friday and first reported by *The Washington Post.*

The meeting’s agenda has not yet been released, but the panel is expected to finalize approval for the president’s plans to redevelop the East Potomac Park golf course and repaint the Eisenhower Executive Office Building, among other projects, all of which have drawn high-profile lawsuits and sparked heated courtroom battles.

![Before Democrats take control of Congress and add members to the panel, the National Capital Planning Commission is expected to try to rush approval for Donald Trump’s Washington, DC, construction projects, which have faced a wave of protests and high-profile lawsuits](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/27/21/2026-11-15T181312Z_2062225118_RC2HOOAQHQ4W_RTRMADP_3_USA-TRUMP-WASHINGTON.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Incoming Democratic members of Congress have signaled plans to block the president’s D.C. projects, none of which have received congressional approval, with their new majorities next year.

The committee is scheduled to hold its next regular meeting on December 3, when the panel is expected to review plans for East Potomac Park golf course and Trump’s National Garden of American Heroes.

Trump has overseen a dramatic transformation of the nation’s capital with a multi-billion dollar package of gilded renovations and new construction, including the demolition of the East Wing of the White House to make way for a massive, heavily fortified ballroom that could cost as much as $600 million.

The president is also in the middle of lawsuits over plans to rename the Kennedy Center after himself, construct a “triumphal” arch that will loom over Arlington National Cemetery and close the East Potomac Links public golf course to build a “world class” facility in its place.

A federal judge has temporarily blocked the Trump administration from constructing the 250-foot arch, which the National Capital Planning Commission swiftly voted to approve this month despite roughly seven hours of public testimony urging the panel to reject it.

![The commission is expected to review plans for East Potomac Park, which Trump wants to transform into a ‘world class’ golf facility. A federal judge has blocked the plans from moving forward, for now](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/27/21/2304972660..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Another federal judge has blocked the president from closing and performing work on the golf course through at least December 12.

But what began as yet another lawsuit to stop one of the president’s D.C.-area renovations has turned into a volatile legal battle between the executive branch and the judiciary.

Preservation groups and golfers have sued the administration to stop the golf course closure have argued that the administration’s rationale — park safety — is merely a pretext for closing the popular public space to transform it into an exclusive, high-end course.

District Judge Ana C. Reyes, who Trump has called a “lunatic,” is opening an investigation into whether government lawyers lied to her court or violated ethics rules in the legal battle over plans for the course.

The Trump administration has insisted for weeks that there were no imminent plans to close the park despite the president's Truth Social announcements outlining his vision for the project.

The Department of Justice finally admitted in court filings this week that the president’s plans to close East Potomac Park on November 15 were, in fact, in the works, though the court has blocked the plans from moving forward, for now.
