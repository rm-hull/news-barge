# "Why we recruited a small army of knitters to help yarn bomb Yorkshire with Bettys festive post box toppers"

## In the run-up to Christmas, some very special postbox toppers will start appearing across Yorkshire as yarn bombers strike. Since the summer, knitters and crocheters from Yorkshire and as far afield as Lincoln and Daventry have been busy creating toppers all inspired by iconic Bettys products. From Fat Rascals to afternoon tea, a small army of crafters have been hard at work.

The idea came from this year’s Christmas window theme, which is a nod to the days when we sent cards, letters and gifts by post, with each of the branches’ windows featuring handmade ‘Royal Mail’ post boxes, decorated by hand by Bettys confectionery team.

“We were looking at ideas for our Christmas window campaign this year, and Robyn Cox, our visual merchandising manager, who designs the window displays, was looking back at a time when sending and receiving things by post was the traditional way to do things and it was always really exciting,” explains Bettys PR manager Ruth Burke-Kennedy.

“Our founder Frederick Belmont would also have been making beautiful window displays at this time of year, and he won awards for them. Harking back to that era, Robyn came up with the idea of a postbox and the idea of ‘From Bettys with love’ and how it would all be about sending things to far-flung friends and family and connecting people in an old-fashioned nostalgic way.”

Robyn drew a sketch for his window of a big traditional 1920s-style red post box. “The postboxes have been hand-iced by Bettys cake decorator Danni Grieg, including the robin on the top. When we were talking about what Christmas meant to people and the importance of Bettys at Christmas to people. Somebody came up with the idea that it would be really nice to make postbox toppers. We had no idea how to make toppers, so the obvious place was to go to the Cone Exchange.”

The Cone Exchange is the flagship community project of Bettys & Taylors Group. They repurpose waste materials from businesses, alongside donations from the local community – literally turning trash into treasure, raising funds for local good causes and creating a sense of community and new skills for all involved. Last year, a record-breaking £75,000 was raised and donated to good causes.

As well as being supported by members of Bettys and Taylors’ staff, the Cone Exchange is run with the help of more than 50 community volunteers. And so when Ruth approached Sarah Wells, Bettys and Taylors Community Manager, who oversees the Cone Exchange, she knew where to go for help.

Jane Hull, one of the volunteers at the Cone Exchange, also volunteers for the Leeds branch of The Knitting and Crochet Guild; she got her group leader, Kelly Ferguson, involved.

“Kelly loved the idea and told me what she’d need. I had no idea how much work went into making them – they take months to make. The brief was so simple, and they have been so imaginative. They all knew Bettys and brought their own experiences to the task, and the results are amazing. I thought we’d be lucky to get four – but in the end they have made ten.”

Kelly, whose grandma taught her to knit, reached out to her members, and about half a dozen came forward. “It’s been a real group effort,” says Kelly. “I then reached out to a couple of other branches.” She recruited people not only from her own Guild but also from Lincoln and as far as Daventry – places that don’t even have a Bettys.

Linda Phillips is from the Lincoln branch of the Guild, which makes bollard toppers for Waitrose. “I’ve been doing that for five years, and we do three or four different seasons throughout the year. So Kelly contacted me, as she knew I had experience making bollard toppers, and asked if we would help. We agreed to make two.” Even though there are no Bettys in Lincoln, Linda knew of the brand. She wanted to make a Bettys gift box to connect with the idea of sending things through the post and put that to her members, who agreed.

“We also decided to do fondant fancies with the Christmas design, and then everyone shouted that we had to have Fat Rascals, so that decided the content.” One of the biggest challenges was recreating the Bettys logo in embroidery. “It took me three or four attempts, as I didn’t want to represent Bettys in any other way than if I worked for them.”

The Knitting and Crochet Guild started in 1978 to preserve the skills and heritage of hand-knitting, machine-knitting and crochet in the UK. “Today we promote and support these crafts through our branch network, Collection, annual convention, publications and digital channels,” says Kelly. “We are a membership organisation, run entirely by volunteers with branches up and down the country. Each of the branches is different, and our members range from beginners to experts, and anyone with an interest in any of the three crafts is welcome.”

Although the likes of Tom Daley have raised the profile of knitting and crocheting among the younger generation, membership of the Guild tends to be an older demographic, and they are trying to recruit younger members. “We just don’t want those skills to be lost.”

Tracy Watkins is a member of the Leeds branch of the Guild as well as the charity Hookers and Clickers and has been making postbox toppers for three years and is something of an expert.

“When you choose a postbox, you tend to stick to that one, and you don’t poach other people’s,” she explains. “I’ve made about 12 over the years, but with these ones for Bettys it is a combination of lots of people’s efforts.

“The main challenge is designing them so that they stand up and endure the harsh weather. You don’t want to make it in cotton, or else it will become very heavy and start to lean. Natural wools and dyes won't keep their colour. Although it’s acrylic, it really is the best material for this sort of thing.” Most of the toppers will have some sort of frame to maintain their shape. “You’ll be surprised what some of them are made of,” says Tracy. “It’s all about using your imagination and having a go.”

Members of staff at Bettys and Taylors have also been involved. Some have made full toppers; others have made small elements that are then included in the end design. Among them are mother and daughter Heather and Emily Ong.

“We came up with the idea of Santa having a Bettys afternoon tea,” explains Emily, whose mum taught her to knit. “The presents are supposed to look like the Christmas fondant fancies. But it became too crowded, so we split it into two. We have a take on the chocolate robin, a little cake that Bettys make at Christmas, and the Rudolph macaron from the afternoon tea and fondant fancies.

“It takes a lot of hours. The little stuff is so fiddly, and there’s a lot of trial and error, as we have no patterns.”

“The whole story is one about crafting and how central that is to us as a business,” says Ruth. “Bettys is all about craft, from the vision to the craft skills in the bakery to the Cone Exchange.”

They plan to ‘bomb’ post boxes in various spots in Yorkshire in the lead-up to Christmas. “There will be some around each of the Bettys branches, also in iconic places in Yorkshire and possibly further afield,” says Ruth. “We’ll put some hints on social media and ask people to take photographs when they spot them and send them in to us, and we will put them on our social media channels.”
