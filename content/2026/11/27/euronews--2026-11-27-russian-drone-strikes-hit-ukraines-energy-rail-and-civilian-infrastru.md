## After a series of Russian strikes and with the onset of winter cold, Ukraine was forced to halt all electricity exports, while imports reached their highest level since mid-June.

Since the evening of Thursday 26 November, Russian forces have attacked Ukraine with 155 strike UAVs of various types (51 of them jet powered), as well as Parodiya-type decoy drones. Ukrainian air defences managed to shoot down *(source in Ukrainian)* 110 drones, but impacts were recorded at several locations.

Prime Minister Sergey Koretsky said that during the night the Russian Armed Forces carried out strikes on civilian infrastructure in nine regions of Ukraine.

"Tonight Russia carried out strikes on the railway, the energy sector, civilian enterprises and residential buildings in nine regions," he wrote *(source in Ukrainian)* on Telegram.

As a result of the Russian attacks, by morning part of consumers in Dnipropetrovsk, Zaporizhzhia, Kirovohrad, Sumy, Kharkiv and Kherson regions were left without electricity, according to Ukraine's First Deputy Energy Minister Artem Nekrasov.

In particular, he said *(source in Ukrainian)* that in Dnipropetrovsk region engineers have been forced to carry out emergency shutdowns because equipment has been damaged and voltage has fallen in the high-voltage grid.

According to the authors of the November economic overview *(source in Ukrainian)* by the Centre for Economic Strategy (CES), after the Russian attack on 20 November Ukraine completely halted electricity exports, and imports reached their highest level since mid-June.

Since September, Russia has carried out more than 1,000 strikes on Ukraine's energy system, targeting not only generation facilities but also high-voltage substations through which electricity reaches consumers.

As a result of the overnight strikes, two people were killed at an enterprise in Nizhyn in Chernihiv region; people may be trapped under the rubble.

One woman, aged 86, was killed and another six people were injured after Russian Armed Forces attacks on Izium in the Kharkiv region. Blocks of flats and private houses, administrative buildings and vehicles were damaged, and fires broke out in various parts of the city.

One person was killed in Kryvyi Rih and another two were wounded after more than 20 UAV strikes on five districts of the Dnipropetrovsk region.

Three people were injured on the morning of 27 November as a result of strikes by Russian drones on the Shevchenkivskyi and Kyiv districts of Kharkiv.

## Strike by Ukrainian forces on chemical plant producing fuel for Iskander missiles

On Friday night Ukrainian drones struck a combined heat and power plant that is part of the Rosatom state corporation, as well as a synthetic rubber plant involved in producing components of solid rocket fuel, in Yefremov in the Tula region.

This is at least the third strike on the chemical complex over the past year.

It produces polymers for the rubber industry, electrical engineering, adhesives, sealants and lubricants, as part of the enterprise's output is dual-use.

In September the General Staff of the Ukrainian Armed Forces claimed *(source in Ukrainian)* that the Yefremov plant is involved in producing components of solid rocket fuel, including for ballistic missiles of the Iskander operational-tactical complexes. The plant has been under Ukrainian sanctions since May 2023.

Also overnight there were reports *(source in Russian)* of a massive drone attack on Saint Petersburg and Leningrad region. The authorities of the northern city said that 45 drones had been shot down over the region.

In Novorossiysk three people were injured in a drone attack, the operational headquarters of Krasnodar region reported. Another two people were killed in Bryansk region by a drone strike and an explosive device that was dropped.

The Russian Defence Ministry reported that 318 Ukrainian UAVs had been shot down in 10 regions, as well as over illegally annexed Crimea.

Ukrainian troops also reported *(source in Ukrainian)* destroying a camouflaged oil depot in occupied territory in Luhansk region, which Russian forces were using to supply fuel to their units.

Ukraine's President Volodymyr Zelensky summed up the impact of Ukrainian strikes on Russian refineries and defence industry facilities, saying there were "results" in Samara, Nizhny Novgorod, Krasnodar, Rostov, Volgograd, Stavropol, Bryansk, Perm and Tula regions, as well as in Bashkortostan and Tatarstan, and in the Black Sea.

"Ukraine is absolutely right to respond to every Russian strike. And I thank our Ukrainian defence forces for ensuring that the responses are accurate."

"Russian oil facilities, enterprises involved in producing explosives and other sites that serve the needs of the war, military airfields and aircraft, our range covers all of this," the president said.
