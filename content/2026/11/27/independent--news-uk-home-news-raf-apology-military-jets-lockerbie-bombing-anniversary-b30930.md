The Royal Air Force has issued an apology to Lockerbie residents after military planes carried out low-level flights near the town close to the anniversary of the 1988 bombing.

A Pan-Am jumbo jet crashed in the Scottish town in December 1988, causing the deaths of 259 people on board and 11 residents on the ground.

Local MP David Mundell noted that an informal agreement was established after the attack to prevent low flying over the area out of respect for the local population.

According to reports, that understanding was breached on multiple occasions earlier this month.

The RAF stated it was sorry for any distress caused and confirmed an investigation is under way.

![David Mundell noted an informal agreement was reached to prevent low flying over the Lockerbie area (Jane Barlow/PA)](https://static.independent.co.uk/2023/02/09/13/041d1a4f91404becc2fbfb748c1c8765Y29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNjc2MDM1OTM2-2.41324463.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The flights are believed to have involved Swiss Air Force F/A-18 Hornet jets conducting night-flying training alongside RAF aircraft.

An RAF spokesperson said: "We completely understand the concerns from the Lockerbie community and are sorry for any distress caused by the recent flying activity.

"This is currently being investigated and we take concerns raised by local community very seriously.

"Wherever possible, we publicise low-flying activity in advance and minimise disruption, while delivering the training required with our partners for international defence operations."

Speaking in the Commons on Thursday, Mr Mundell, MP for Dumfriesshire, Clydesdale and Tweeddale, said: "Following the bombing of Pan Am Flight 103 over Lockerbie on December 21 1988, there was an understanding that there would not be low flying over that community.

"In recent weeks, unfortunately, there have been reports of low flying as we approach the 38th anniversary of the bombing, which is always a very difficult time in the community."

He requested a meeting with the Ministry of Defence "to find out what has happened in relation to these low-flying incidents and to ensure that they don’t happen again?".

The Lockerbie atrocity remains the deadliest act of terrorism recorded in UK history.

Those killed spanned 21 nationalities, including 190 Americans.
