A man who rang a London nursery and threatened to “murder the staff and children” there has been jailed for 20 months.

Joshua Ajai, 32, caused lockdowns and a “great degree of panic” at Plumcroft Primary School and Star Day Nursery when he made phone threats to both within a week of each other this summer.

Doctors’ notes suggested that Ajai had been “rejected” from jobs he applied for at nurseries, which may have been the “catalyst” for making the calls.

Ajai admitted two counts of sending communications threatening death or serious harm, and one offence of fear of violence by harassment.

Sentencing him at Woolwich Crown Court on Friday, Judge Christopher Grout said that “threats of murder and violence” against nursery and school children can “only” be met with immediate custody.

In Judge Grout’s sentencing remarks, published after the hearing, the timeline of Ajai’s threats were laid out.

![Ajai said that children should ‘watch their backs’ as they left Plumcroft Primary School, pictured](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/27/17/18/plumcroft.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&auto=webp)

Just after 11.30am on 7 July, Ajai called Star Day Nursery and told the manager: “I’m going to come and murder all the members of staff and the children.”

He repeated the threat as the manager rushed to lock the back door, before telling her he would be outside in a black hoodie at 5pm.

Ajai then called again and asked if he could “come and meet the children” because he “wanted to haunt them in their nightmares”.

The manager called the police and placed the nursery on lockdown, and Ajai was arrested at his home address the next morning.

A week earlier, on 30 June, Ajai had called Plumcroft Primary School and said “when your children leave school tell them to watch their backs”.

That led to the school placed on lockdown, and children kept inside.

The receptionist who took the call said in her witness statement: “This triggered a great degree of panic in the parents and resulted in many of them calling the school worried.”

Judge Grout said: “Members of the public place huge trust in nurseries to look after their young children, which is precisely what the staff did.

“However, actions such as yours risks undermining the good and important work that people in the childcare and education sector perform because parents will undoubtedly become anxious and concerned about leaving their children in the care of others when incidents such as this occur.”

The judge expressed his admiration to the staff at both schools and made justice awards to the four most involved members of staff.

Ajai, who told doctors he had “no intention” of carrying out the attacks, was sentenced to 20 months’ imprisonment.
