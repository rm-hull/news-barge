The wife of Graham Thorpe, the former England cricketer who took his own life, has started legal proceedings against an NHS trust after an inquest identified “failings” in his mental health care.

Thorpe died aged 55 in August 2024 in Surrey. His wife, Amanda, has lodged a personal injury claim at the high court against Surrey and Borders Partnership NHS foundation trust which was involved in his care, the Telegraph reported.

The former batsman and coach was diagnosed with anxiety and depression and had tried to kill himself in 2022, the inquest at Surrey coroner’s court in Woking heard last year. The inquest found the trust was culpable of “failings” and “shortcomings”.

Thorpe, who played 100 Tests for England, had “spiralled into depression” after losing his job as a batting coach in 2022 at the conclusion of the Ashes series in Australia, the hearing heard.

Surrey coroner’s court was told there had been a gap of more than four months in his being seen by the local NHS trust before his death despite a “crisis” call in June 2024 when he told his care coordinator that he had “given up”. Thorpe was offered a follow-up appointment by the community mental health team but no home visit was made.

Jonathan Stevens, the assistant coroner, said at the inquest: “In my judgment, someone should have been going to see him to properly monitor and assess him, to understand his care needs. Saying ‘come and see me in two weeks’ was not an appropriate response to the crisis.

“I don’t accept that when Graham was constantly asking his wife to help end his life, which was a new presentation that hadn’t been seen before, he was not at that moment in crisis. There were shortcomings in the care that should have been provided to Graham in the last four months of his life. There were failings in the provision of his care.”

Stevens did not conclude those shortcomings were negligent, adding: “I cannot say whether it would have made any difference or not.”

Amanda told the inquest her husband was constantly asking for help to end his life and that he was sure nothing would work. She also told the inquest of Thorpe’s desire to go to Switzerland to end his life, although he did also tell his care coordinator that he had “no immediate plans” to act on his suicidal thoughts. A coroner recorded a verdict of suicide.
