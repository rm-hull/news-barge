US president Donald Trump is taking steps to replace a judicial nominee in Texas after outgoing Republican Senator John Cornyn blocked the choice from advancing before Democrats take control of the Senate in January.

Senate Judiciary Committee Chairman Chuck Grassley stated late on Wednesday that his panel obtained partial nomination paperwork for Steve Fahey, a former prosecutor backed by Cornyn for a Dallas judgeship. Trump had previously chosen US Justice Department official T. Elliot Gaiser.

Last week, Cornyn withheld a blue slip of support for Gaiser, preventing the original pick from moving forward in the latest intra-party dispute over home-state judicial vacancies.

Cornyn, who lost a primary to a Trump-endorsed challenger, objected to Gaiser, pointing out he was not from Texas and arguing the White House "was trying to jam him down our throat without adequate vetting for a lifetime job."

![Outgoing Republican Senator John Cornyn blocked the pick ahead of the Democrats taking control of the Senate](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/18/21/2026-11-18T212355Z_560106722_RC2JQOALWQDC_RTRMADP_3_USA-CONGRESS.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The dispute complicates administration efforts to confirm remaining judicial nominees before Democrats take control of the Senate on January 3 following a 56-seat win in the recent election.

The White House was previously forced to replace another candidate opposed by home-state Republican senators.

Grassley's office said the Iowa senator will consult with colleagues to determine if Fahey and North Carolina nominee Jim O'Neill possess realistic paths toward confirmation.

The White House did not respond to a request for comment.

Across two terms, Trump has appointed 295 judicial nominees to lifetime seats, including three members of the US Supreme Court's 6-3 conservative majority.

Under Senate custom, home-state senators retain substantial power over federal court selections and can effectively block trial court choices by refusing to return a blue slip.

Trump nominated Gaiser to the US District Court for the Northern District of Texas post-election with backing from Texas' other Republican senator, Ted Cruz.

Gaiser, a former Ohio solicitor general who clerked for conservative US Supreme Court Justice Samuel Alito, currently directs the Justice Department's Office of Legal Counsel, which reviews the legality of executive actions.
