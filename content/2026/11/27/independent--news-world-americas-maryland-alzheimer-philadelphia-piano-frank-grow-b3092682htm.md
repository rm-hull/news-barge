A missing Maryland man with Alzheimer’s was found more than 100 miles away in Philadelphia playing the piano.

​Frank Grow, 78, traveled more from his home in Bowie, a city about 15 miles from Washington, D.C., after he went missing last week.

​Bowie police issued a missing persons report on November 19, noting that he was a “critical” missing person.

​Maggie Henkin, Grow’s 40-year-old daughter, told NBC4 Washington that she had left the car running while she stepped out to get a cup of coffee.

​“I was gone for literally two minutes and, when I came back out, the car was gone,” she told the outlet. “I just panicked. I didn’t know how far he had gotten.”

![Frank Grow was found playing the piano in Pennsylvania after vanishing in Maryland](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/27/11/57/Screenshot-2026-11-27-at-10-59-26.png?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&auto=webp)

​Speaking to *The Washington Post*, Henkin explained that her father had not driven since his diagnosis six years ago. It is difficult for him to write his own name, and he sometimes does not remember members of his family.

​Henkin said that she panicked when she realized that her father had often asked to return to where he had grown up, Norristown in Pennsylvania, given how far it was from Bowie.

​Meanwhile, Grow had driven to Philadelphia but stopped in the northeast of the city. That is where local resident Angelo Torres found the 78-year-old.

​“I noticed a gentleman standing on the corner,” Torres told NBC10 Philadelphia. “So I pulled up to him and asked, ‘Sir, are you ok?’ And he said, 'No, I’m not ok.’”

​Standing in Torres’ front yard was a piano, and Grow eventually sat down to play.

![Grow (pictured) was diagnosed with Alzheimer's six years ago, but his daughter says that he still loves to play the piano](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/27/10/44/Screenshot-2026-11-27-at-10-50-21.png?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&trim=55%2C0%2C51%2C1&auto=webp)

​Alzheimer’s is a progressive disease that can impair a person’s memory and their cognitive abilities.

​According to physicians at UCLA Health, scans of patients’ brains have shown that music works with a more diverse neural network in the brain than speech. That means patients can often recognize songs from their youth, even if they cannot remember other important details.

​Henkin explained that her father still loves to play music despite his diagnosis.

​“Angelo happens to have a piano in (sic) front yard. My dad is a musician,” she told NBC10 Philadelphia. “He’s had Alzheimer’s for 6 years now. But it’s something he does all day. He still remembers how to.”

![Angelo Torres says that he found Grow in northeast Philadelphia](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/27/10/40/Screenshot-2026-11-27-at-10-53-46.png?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&trim=142%2C4%2C67%2C0&auto=webp)

​Eventually, Torres found Henkin’s cell phone number inside an invoice from a Nissan dealership in her car and managed to get in contact with her.

​Later on November 19, the Bowie Police Department confirmed that the 78-year-old had been found.

​“Mr Frank Grow has been found and is in good health,” a spokesperson wrote on X. “We thank the public for their assistance.”

​Torres told NBC10 Philadelphia that he felt the encounter was a sign from his own late father.

​**“** All week I was thinking about my dad, and then all of a sudden he shows up, and I’m thinking my dad came in this form,” he said. “That’s what I’m thinking.”
