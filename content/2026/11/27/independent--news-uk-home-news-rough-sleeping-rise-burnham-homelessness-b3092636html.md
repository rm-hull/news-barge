More than 3,000 people in England have so far been offered a bed or support as part of Andy Burnham’s promise to give all rough sleepers a route off the streets for Christmas.

The prime minister vowed in his first speech in the job that his government would “end rough sleeping” across the country, saying it would be his “first instruction”.

Newly released data on Friday revealed the scale of the problem, showing that in September – before the scheme began – the number of people sleeping rough in England was one per cent higher than this time last year at 9,369.

People sleeping rough long-term is at a record high since data collection began in May 2023 and has been increasing month-on-month since February 2026, the data shows.

The government said Mr Burnham’s Rough Sleeping Programme is providing £874m over three years to give people experiencing street homelessness a route off the pavements and support going forwards.

![The number of people sleeping rough long-term is at a record high](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/27/10/2287425698..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Rough sleeping data released on Friday from the Ministry of Housing, Communities, and Local Government (MHCLG) is from July to September 2026, while the first data published on the prime minister’s homelessness scheme corresponds only to October 2026.

Of the estimated 9,369 people sleeping rough over September, 31 per cent were new to rough sleeping, and 6 per cent had recently left an institution – mostly prison – the government said. They added this was a decrease of 22 per cent when compared with September 2025.

The figures also show that while the number of long-term rough sleepers has risen, the number of both new rough sleepers and people returning to the streets has decreased when compared with the same time last year.

![Andy Burnham pledged to end rough sleeping as he took on the premiership earlier this year](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/27/10/2306951550..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

London continues to have the highest levels of rough sleeping, the data shows, but the city also saw a decrease in those sleeping rough for just one night, which fell by 5 per cent.

Four per cent of those sleeping rough in September were under 25 years old, figures show.

But 82 per cent of areas reported that they are confident that by 1 December they will be able to provide everyone sleeping rough with an offer of a route off the street following the new funding.

Alicia Walker, assistant director of activism and advocacy at homelessness charity Shelter, said: "These figures show the government has made a good start in helping everyone off the streets by Christmas. But with nearly two-thirds of people still waiting for an offer of a bed, it needs to put its foot on the gas to make sure no one risks their lives on the street this winter.

“It’s appalling that rough sleeping remains shockingly high across the country, hitting a new record in July. To end this injustice for good, the government’s offer must go beyond a bed in a hostel and provide people a permanent home and wraparound support.

“Rough sleeping is only a tiny fraction of overall homelessness – our services are supporting some of the 86,000 families stuck in miserable temporary accommodation who also desperately need a safe and secure home. If the government truly wants to end homelessness, we need an iron-clad guarantee that it will deliver a new generation of social rent homes.”

*The Independent* is campaigning to tackle youth homelessness this Christmas through Project Nest, our innovative partnership with youth charity EveryYouth.

Our campaign is aiming to fund the renovation of three dilapidated houses in Fife, Sheffield and Basingstoke, which will be transformed into accommodation for young people struggling with homelessness.

It has received backing from scores of high-profile names, including Mr Burnham.

Writing in *The Independent*, he said: “I’m proud to support* The Independent*’s Christmas appeal for Project Nest, and the brilliant work EveryYouth does for young people with nowhere safe to call home.

“My first instruction as prime minister was to end rough sleeping. We have to stop accepting it as a fact of life. But government can’t do it alone, and ending homelessness for good means making sure young people never reach the streets in the first place.

“That’s what Project Nest is about: not just putting a roof over young people’s heads, but giving them the support to get into education, training or work and build the lives they deserve.”
