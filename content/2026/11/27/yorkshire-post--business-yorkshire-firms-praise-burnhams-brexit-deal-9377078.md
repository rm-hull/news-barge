# ‘Reducing red tape will help sell Yorkshire Wagyu to Europe’ - firms praise Burnham’s Brexit deal

## Since Brexit, agri-food exports have dropped by a third, according to the National Farmers’ Union, due to additional costs and forms to fill out.

Yorkshire beef and tofu businesses have backed the Government’s food and drink deal with the European Union, as it was laid in Parliament.

Since Brexit, agri-food exports have dropped by a third, according to the National Farmers’ Union, due to additional costs and forms to fill out.

The new deal will see those costly export health certificates scrapped and allow British favourites, like sausages, burgers and seafood, to be sold once again in Europe and Northern Ireland.

Officials said the UK would also be “consulted early” on new EU rules which would impact British businesses.

![Andy Burnham speaking at the UK-EU summit](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/jpim-static/image/2026/11/26/17/cs-6a2f9cc9-88aa-4cd4-830f-2a854a0c4259.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


Prime Minister Andy Burnham introduced the agreement as part of the European Partnership Bill to Parliament yesterday, with MPs set to get a vote next month.

The deal has been backed by British supermarkets like M&S and groups like the NFU, while Brexiteers such as Nigel Farage slammed it and predicted farmer protests in the new year.

However, a number of small Yorkshire exporters praised it for enhancing their opportunities on the continent.

Tom Richardson, managing director of Warrendale Wagyu, based just outside of Pocklington, in the East Riding, said that “reducing administrative burdens … will improve our competitiveness”.

"We welcome the conclusion of negotiations on a UK-EU SPS agreement, which represents a significant and positive step forward for British food and agricultural exports,” he explained.

"For Warrendale Wagyu, and the wider UK red meat industry, reducing administrative burdens, certification requirements and border delays will improve our competitiveness and create greater opportunities to develop trade with our European partners.

![Warrendale Wagyu is aiming to spread the word about high quality beef.](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOmU2YzNmMDdlLTc5YmQtNDAwYy1hYTVmLWM0ODI4ZWNmZDA1NzpkYzY4ODNmNS05ZGI5LTQ3YmEtYTQ3OC0yOTQxMGI5YjI5MTk=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


"The UK produces some of the finest beef in the world, and ensuring our farmers, processors and exporters can access international markets efficiently is essential to supporting long-term investment, growth and the future of British agriculture."

David Knibbs, chief executive of North Yorkshire Tofu producers, Tofoo and Co added: "This food and drink deal with the EU will greatly enhance our ability to export where we have significant opportunities in EU markets like France, Holland, Belgium and Germany.”

Last year, more than two-thirds of Yorkshire’s food and drinks exports - some £1.1bn worth - went to the EU.

Environment Secretary Dame Angela Eagle said: “Businesses in every corner of the country stand to benefit from the deal and be able to focus on growing their businesses and not costly paperwork.

![Wagyu Beef](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOmQ1MzVhNWI0LTFjZDUtNDgzNC1iZTE1LTA1MTkwZWE0ODI3MDo5YzY0MWQ4OC00NTEyLTQ3NDUtYjgyMS1jODQ5ZDBmN2E3Yzg=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


“The agreement marks a new era for Britain's relationship with Europe and will deliver a trade boost for home-grown brands and a better deal for customers."

A debate on the draft legislation to implement the deal will be held in the House of Commons on December 7, with a vote expected to follow.

Mr Burnham has faced questions over whether the agreements he secured in Brussels, which also include a youth experience scheme, will even go ahead.

Negotiations on a further deal on tuition fees for Europeans studying at UK universities remain ongoing, with Conservative leader Kemi Badenoch suggesting the issue could derail last week’s agreements.

At Prime Minister’s Questions on Wednesday, Mrs Badenoch said the Prime Minister had provided “no answer on whether the deal is contingent on the tuition fees agreement itself”.

But Mr Burnham insisted it was “on a separate piece of paper, a separate deal” and that a reduction in tuition fees for EU students to UK levels “won’t be on the table”.
