A former GP who was jailed for trying to poison his mother’s partner with a fake Covid jab has failed in a legal bid to withdraw a guilty plea to a previous plot to murder him with poisoned bottles of spirits.

Thomas Kwan was already serving 31 years for the attempted murder of Patrick O’Hara when he was charged with a separate attempt to kill him with alcohol he sent his victim from a fake wine club.

The plot came to light during his first trial, when a member of Mr O’Hara’s family realised what the prosecution at that point thought was an aborted plan to send him poisoned alcohol had actually happened, sparking a new police investigation.

It emerged that Mr O’Hara had drunk some of the alcohol he received and, unwittingly, had passed on a bottle to his son-in-law, Torquil Gundlach, who had also become ill.

Tests subsequently revealed that Kwan had injected the poison thallium into the bottles of spirits and then resealed them and sent them to his mother’s partner over a period of months prior to the fake Covid plan.

![Thomas Kwan was already serving 31 years for the attempted murder of Patrick O’Hara when he was charged with a separate attempt to kill him with alcohol he sent his victim from a fake wine club](https://static.independent.co.uk/2024/11/06/13/ead8df01-af0c-45cb-b185-daf61e28125b.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Kwan initially admitted the poisoned alcohol attempted murder charge, then tried to withdraw his guilty plea, claiming his legal team – including the respected barrister Paul Greaney KC – had put undue pressure on him to admit the offence.

But Mr Justice Cotter, sitting on Thursday at Newcastle Crown Court, rejected an application to vacate his guilty plea, finding the legal advice Kwan received was proper.

Mr Justice Cotter said Kwan will be sentenced for the alcohol plot at a later date by a different judge.

He lifted reporting restrictions which had prevented the media from publishing details about the ex-GP’s bid to change his plea.

Investigators believed it was a lack of patience with the alcohol plot that led Kwan to adopt a different plan, and in January 2024 he disguised himself as a nurse and injected Mr O’Hara with a poisoned, fake, Covid jab.

Kwan originally contacted Mr O’Hara via the fictitious Northern Wine and Drinks Tasting Gentlemen’s Club and sent him up to 21 bottles of alcohol, some of which were poisoned and some were unadulterated to avoid suspicion.

Mr O’Hara drank some of the bottles and gifted one to his son-in-law.

In a highly unusual case, Kwan appeared in court to claim his guilty plea to the alcohol plot had followed “enormous, oppressive pressure” to admit the charges to the fake Covid jab plan.

Kwan claimed he had been seriously assaulted in prison prior to the original trial and he was left feeling confused by the advice given around the time.

In claims that were firmly rejected by Mr Justice Cotter, Kwan suggested his legal team had tricked him, and, at one point, said that the police and prison service had colluded against him and also that he had been framed.

![Photo ID Thomas Kwan used while posing as a nurse to try to kill his mother’s partner](https://static.independent.co.uk/2024/11/06/12/0b9331edf81f450f14c91497120bf5c7Y29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzMwOTgyMDQz-2.77689735.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Kwan even claimed he feared being murdered in prison if he did not make admissions.

But the judge said Kwan’s legal advisers, Mr Greaney and Brian Hegarty, had given him “clear, cogent and correct” advice about his options and the likely sentence he would face if he made early admissions.

Kwan, formerly a GP in Sunderland, also admitted administering a noxious substance to Mr Gundlach, who also consumed some of the alcohol which was laced with thallium, when Mr O’Hara was the intended target.

In October 2025, when Kwan originally admitted the alcohol plot, Peter Makepeace KC, prosecuting, said two bottles recovered contained poison and there was evidence that a third, which was laced with thallium, caused Mr O’Hara to fall ill.

Mr Makepeace said not all of the bottles were found to contain poison as Mr O’Hara would have swiftly fallen ill, “quickly bringing the scheme to a halt”.

He added: “Genuine bottles were sent to lure the victim into a sense of security.”

He told the court that the wine club Kwan created “does not exist”.

Mr O’Hara’s daughter, sitting in the public gallery at the original trial, heard prosecutors mention the wine club concept which detectives did not realise had come to fruition.

She told police about her suspicions and officers immediately went to recover any bottles they could find.

![The Hong Kong-born doctor was first jailed in November 2024 for what the sentencing judge, Mrs Justice Lambert, called an ‘audacious plan to murder a man in plain sight’](https://static.independent.co.uk/2024/10/03/14/SEI224290917.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

After his legal team were alerted to the development, Kwan was to confess to his legal team that he used thallium but later backtracked and tried to claim that he guessed that was the poison used from watching “movies”.

He also claimed his mother did not drink and he wanted to cause problems in their relationship by sending Mr O’Hara alcohol in the hope she would become annoyed at his behaviour.

The Hong Kong-born doctor was first jailed in November 2024 for what the sentencing judge, Mrs Justice Lambert, called an “audacious plan to murder a man in plain sight”.

Kwan sent two fake letters with NHS logos, hyperlinks and even a QR code, offering Mr O’Hara a home visit from a community nurse which he carried out in January 2024.

Kwan turned up in disguise at the couple’s home in central Newcastle and carried out a health check before administering a fake Covid vaccine, which was in reality the iodomethane, a poison used in pesticides and which is difficult to detect.

Mr O’Hara felt a sharp pain and Kwan quickly fled.

His victim went through a horrific hospital ordeal as medics fought to save his life without knowing the make-up of the poison.

Mr O’Hara needed to undergo plastic surgery after he developed a flesh-eating disease and the attempt on his life left him “a shell of an individual”, he said in a victim statement at the time.

Sentencing, Mrs Justice Lambert said: “His emotional reaction is due in part to his disbelief that this terrible act could be perpetrated by the son of his partner under the guise of a trusted health professional.”

Kwan was effectively estranged from his mother, Jenny Leung, after falling out over money.

He was angry after he found out that his mother had made a will which allowed Mr O’Hara to stay in her home should she die before him.

The couple have split up since her son’s attempt on his life.

Officers scoured CCTV and were able to track Kwan, still disguised as a nurse, back to a city centre hotel and then to his home in Ingleby Barwick, Teesside.

In his garage, they discovered an array of dangerous chemicals which the GP had amassed.

On his computer they found the instructions on how to make the chemical weapon ricin.

It was first thought he had used ricin on Mr O’Hara, but a poisons expert said iodomethane was more likely.

Kwan was struck off the medical register.

Outside court, Christopher Atkinson, head of the Crown Prosecution Service Complex Casework Unit in the North East, said the investigation showed Kwan had tried to kill Mr O’Hara for more than a year longer than previously thought.

It also risked other people being poisoned, if the bottles were shared or given away, he said.

“This additional attempt was even more reckless, with the contaminated alcohol presenting a grave and all but certain exposure of third parties to highly toxic chemicals,” he said.
