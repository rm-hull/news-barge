# Leeds Bradford Airport's economic findings are based on evidence: Yorkshire Post Letters

## Joselyn Jones, Director of External Affairs, Leeds Bradford Airport

I was disappointed to read claims questioning the findings of “Driving West Yorkshire’s Economy”, the independent report produced by York Aviation for Leeds Bradford Airport (The Yorkshire Post, November 11).

York Aviation is an established independent consultancy with extensive experience advising airports, governments and transport authorities on aviation economics and regional connectivity.

Robust debate is important, but it must be based on factual evidence, not simply a disregard of the report's purpose or findings. The report establishes the airport's current and potential contribution to the regional economy and the barriers to realising that potential.

![Leeds Bradford Airport has responded to criticism from GALBA](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOmVhY2Q3ZGRmLTk1YTUtNGY3ZC05NTI0LTgzMzkzMzQ4ODQ1Zjo4MDQ5NmIxMi1mZWZmLTRiM2EtYTNkYS1mOTAyNDcxMTMxYTc=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


Importantly, this contribution goes hand in hand with LBA’s environmental responsibilities. LBA has a clear commitment to more sustainable operations, with a published roadmap to achieve net zero carbon emissions from activities under its direct control by 2030.

LBA is also working with industry partners to support aviation's wider decarbonisation.

The report does not suggest the airport creates economic value in isolation. Using established industry methodologies, it considers employment, supply-chain activity, business productivity and inbound tourism.

Its findings show that LBA contributes around £737 million annually to the West Yorkshire economy and supports more than 9,100 jobs. Critics are entitled to challenge the finding of the report, but it is important to recognise the wider evidence it’s based on.

LBA’s contribution to the West Yorkshire economy matters, particularly when around 9.5 million passengers from Yorkshire and the Humber use airports outside the region each year, almost half via Manchester. This represents lost economic activity, visitor spending and business connectivity.

The report's message is about making better use of an existing strategic asset. Improved connectivity can attract investment, support businesses and strengthen tourism. The proposed LBA Parkway Station would also improve public transport access, helping reduce reliance on cars and supporting more sustainable journeys to the airport.

Airports are far more than leisure facilities. They are essential economic infrastructure, supporting businesses, exporters, investors, universities and tourism organisations.

Yorkshire deserves a balanced debate about its future connectivity and economic competitiveness. That debate should start with the independent evidence, not a selective interpretation of it.
