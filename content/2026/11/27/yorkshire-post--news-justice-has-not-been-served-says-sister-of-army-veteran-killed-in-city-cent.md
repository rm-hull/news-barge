# Justice 'has not been served' says sister of Army veteran killed in city centre fight

## The sister of an Army veteran who was killed in Hull city centre claimed “justice had not been served” after three people were sentenced to a total of nine years for manslaughter.

Martin Chadburn, 53, who was an employee of the Hull 4 Heroes charity, was taken to hospital after suffering fatal head injuries in Queen Victoria Square in the early hours of April 19.

Britney Naylor, 20, Lukasz Matusiak, 27 and Shaytoyia Farrow, 21, were convicted of manslaughter, but cleared of murder, following a six-week trial. Two others, Bethany Naylor, 20, and Kristjan Suits, 35, were found not guilty of manslaughter.

Mr Chadburn had been on a night out with his friend Steven Grayburn when an altercation developed.

![A man and two women have been sentenced over the death of an army veteran who suffered fatal head injuries after being punched to the ground during a night out.](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOmIwNmY0MzM3LWFhNGMtNDg4Yy1hYjg1LWQzNjc4MzQ5ODA3Nzo4NTAyOTI4YS1mMjE5LTQwYWYtOTZlYi05ZmJlMTlmN2IzMzk=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=620&quality=65)


Judge John Thackray KC said the incident started after what the judge described as “vile and disgusting” racist abuse directed at the women by Mr Grayburn. Mr Chadburn did not make any of the racist comments.

The confrontation escalated and Matusiak, who was standing nearby with Suits, intervened, believing the women were being attacked by the older men.

Matusiak, who had been drinking and taken cocaine, landed a flurry of punches. A heavy blow fractured Mr Chadburn’s jaw, causing an arterial tear "which made his head twist and tilt, leading to an unsurvivable brain bleed".

Mr Chadburn, who had served in the British Army for over eight years, died two days later in hospital.

Matusiak was sentenced to five years in jail, with 18 months to run concurrently, for a breach of a suspended sentence for drugs offences. He was transferred to another prison after being attacked at Hull jail after his connection with the case became known.

Sentencing Naylor, the judge said when her sister Bethany reacted badly to the slurs and pursued the men, Naylor tried to persuade her to ignore it. Naylor was punched in the face repeatedly in the melee.

Naylor, who has previous convictions of violence including assaulting by beating an emergency worker in 2025, threw a punch to Mr Chadburn before the fatal attack – the evidence being a mark left by her ring. She was sentenced to two years.

Farrow, who "gratuitously" stamped on Mr Chadburn, as he lay unconscious, was also handed a two-year jail term.

In court, Mr Chadburn’s sister Sharon described the impact of the sudden loss of her brother, from walking onto the intensive care ward and discovering him helpless and lifeless, to the investigation and subsequent trial.

She said: "The trial has ended but our grief has not. It will be there when we wake up tomorrow, every family gathering, every birthday, every Christmas. We will instinctively think of Martin and think he is no longer here."

Afterwards, she said she felt there had been too much emphasis on the racial element of the case. She said: "Justice has not been served for a life that was thriving.

"He was a guy who gave to his community. He wasn’t racist in any shape or form. It has gone against Martin even though he had no part in it.”
