## £85,000 India trade mission yields ‘secret’ investment opportunities

David Skaith, Mayor of York and North Yorkshire. *(Image: YNYCA)*

Details of some business opportunities and investment discussions arising from York and North Yorkshire’s £85,000 trade mission to India are being kept secret despite the trip being funded by taxpayers.

The York and North Yorkshire Combined Authority has withheld information about some commercial opportunities identified during the visit – documents released under the Freedom of Information (FOI) Act have revealed – arguing that disclosure could damage the interests of businesses involved and undermine future trade missions.

The October mission, led by David Skaith, cost £85,102 and involved a 21-person delegation comprising nine Combined Authority staff and 12 external delegates from business and education organisations.

The FOI request was submitted by the Local Democracy Reporting Service (LDRS).

The Combined Authority released details of several outcomes from the visit, including agreements to explore closer cooperation with the Indian state of Telangana, the launch of a Mayor’s Gateway Scholarship at the University of York Mumbai, and a series of education, research and innovation partnerships.

The mayor previously said the visit could help the sale of fertiliser created on the North Yorkshire coast and help to boost crop yields globally.

However, other information has been withheld under Section 43(2) of the Freedom of Information Act, which relates to commercial interests.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21774969.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Page 1 Of Outcomes And Benefits Document For Mayor Skaith'S India Mission By YNYCA. Obtained Via FOI By The LDRS.

The authority said disclosure could harm participating businesses by revealing their “market intentions and partners to competitors” and argued that discussions were still at an early stage.

In response to questions from the Local Democracy Reporting Service (LDRS), a spokesperson said: “The benefits of the trade mission will be felt for many years to come.

“As many discussions are at an early stage, delegates may not wish to disclose any commercially sensitive information at this moment.”

Officials also said disclosure could make organisations “less willing to engage openly on future missions, weakening its ability to secure inward investment and trade for the region”.

Internal planning documents obtained by the LDRS show officials considered the reputational risks of a publicly funded mission before it took place.

An options paper prepared ahead of the visit warned that trade missions funded with public money could attract criticism if they failed to deliver clear and measurable outcomes.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21774971.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Page 1 Of Official Itinerary For Mayor Skaith'S India Mission By YNYCA. Obtained Via FOI By The LDRS.

When asked what evidence existed that the trip had delivered against those expectations, the Combined Authority said: “The trade mission has been hugely positive for our county.

“This is the first time that York and North Yorkshire has been able to undertake such a mission.

“We will continue to report on the benefits as they develop further.”

**Calls for ‘full transparency’**

Keane Duncan, who stood in the 2024 mayoral election against Mr Skaith for the Conservatives, said taxpayers deserved “full transparency so they can judge whether this £85,000 trade mission was a worthwhile investment – or simply an expensive jolly for the Mayor and nine of his staff”.

Cllr Duncan, who is a member of North Yorkshire Council, said that while trade missions had an important role to play, he questioned the value of staying in luxury hotels, adding: “You don’t need five-star luxury to fly the flag for Yorkshire overseas”.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21774968.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Coun Keane Duncan Outside The Grand Hotel In Scarborough.

The FOI request revealed that the 21-person delegation led by the mayor spent £34,707 on accommodation at a cost of around £1,600 per person for 10 nights, including at the luxury five-star chain of Taj hotels.

Cllr Duncan told the LDRS: “Mr Skaith must recognise just how out of touch this looks to the people of York and North Yorkshire.

“Local businesses are being squeezed by escalating costs and families are struggling to make ends meet, yet their Mayor is enjoying five-star hotels in India while proposing new and higher taxes back home.”

Labour’s Mr Skaith has defended the mission, arguing that new links had already been forged with businesses, universities and potential investors.

He said: “The free trade agreement between the UK and India that came into force in July has opened the door for northern mayors to form direct links with one of the world’s fastest-growing economies.

“In York and North Yorkshire, we are already seeing the benefits of the Great North’s trade mission with mayors to India, with new relationships emerging between businesses, universities and potential investors.

“This is devolution delivering, as our county will benefit from direct relationships being forged to unlock new opportunities for our businesses, create high-quality career pathways, and support leading research and innovation.”

In response to Cllr Duncan’s comments, a spokesperson for the mayor added: “Any public spending is approved through a rigorous process that assesses value for money and is not led by politicians.

“The Investment and Business Promotion Fund, which this money came from, was approved unanimously by our cabinet, including North Yorkshire’s Conservative Leadership.”
