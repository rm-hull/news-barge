Organised criminals are suspected of having targeted people to commit a series of robberies on the night an “unknown substance” was released into the crowd during rapper Don Toliver’s concert, Greater Manchester Police said.

The Co-op Live Arena was evacuated during rapper Don Toliver’s show and a major incident declared on Sunday night, with concertgoers describing a “horrible smell”, which police initially believed to be tear gas.

According to Greater Manchester Police (GMP), 89 people displayed symptoms believed to be linked to the exposure. One woman was taken to Manchester Royal Infirmary for further assessment and treatment.

In an update on Friday, Greater Manchester Police said the force is working to identify the suspects and the substance involved during the incident at Co-op Live Arena on Sunday evening.

Greater Manchester Police assistant chief constable Tom Harding said the release of an unknown substance during rapper Don Toliver’s concert in Manchester is linked to a group of organised criminals attempting to commit robberies on the night.

![Police officers outside the Co-op Live in Manchester](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/24/19/01M87EN26VY64Y63T0EXEZQSH8.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Harding said there had been similar incidents at two of the US musician’s gigs in Europe, adding the force was in contact with agencies investigating. No arrests have been made but mobile phone footage is being reviewed by officers, a police spokesperson added.

Mr Harding said: “We’re working to identify those suspects and the substance that we believe was released in the crowd as part of these incidents. All of this work is ongoing at pace.

“There are similar incidents at two Don Toliver gigs in Europe that are also relevant to our enquiries. We’re in contact with law enforcement involved in those investigations.

“No arrests have been made, but our detectives are working through footage captured on mobile phones and CCTV.

“There are over a hundred people at the gig that we are working to try and contact to potentially assist with our enquiries. We thank all those that have assisted us so far.”

Mr Harding said the force was aware some people had been affected psychologically during the incident at Co-op Live because of the 2017 terror attack at Manchester Arena where 22 people were killed by suicide-bomber Salman Abedi.

![Police suspect the release of an unknown substance during rapper Don Toliver’s concert in Manchester is linked to a group of organised criminals attempting to commit robberies on the night](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/23/10/2306466792..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

He added: “We also know many more people were impacted on the night and afterwards, physically and psychologically.

“What happened on Sunday night and the imagery on our TVs and online will bring back painful memories of 2017.

“Thankfully, we are not dealing with an incident of such a devastating nature or motive; however, we understand the impact this is likely to have caused.”

88 people underwent dry decontamination at the scene and were subsequently accommodated within the Etihad Stadium while the incident was managed. The major incident status ended in the early hours of Monday, police said, and the scene had been lifted on Tuesday.

The rapper’s show at London’s O2 Arena – which had been scheduled for Monday night – was postponed.

Posting on Instagram on Monday, the rapper said: “I’m very sad to sit here and say I have to reschedule the O2 London show to December 15 due to the unfortunate incident last night in Manchester.

“I just wanna make sure everyone is safe and the next show goes on properly. My heart and prayers are with everyone that was injured last night at Co-op Live.
