The brother of an autistic Briton who has been detained in a Turkish prison accused of drug smuggling says he is terrified his twin will not make it out alive.

Conor Colegate, 33, says his twin brother Louis Malletier was targeted by criminals while he was queueing up for freebies at a pop-up event in London.

Mr Malletier, who is described by his family as having a “mental age of eight”, believed he had won a free holiday and flew to Thailand secretly, knowing his family would try to stop him.

But his plane ticket home was swapped for one to Spain, and he was pressured to carry a suitcase which he did not know the contents of, according to his family.

He was stopped by authorities during a layover at Istanbul Airport after 15kg of cannabis was discovered in the suitcase and taken to Maltepe prison on 15 October. His family say Mr Malletier “would not know what drugs are” and believe his vulnerabilities were exploited by criminal gangs attempting to smuggle drugs into Spain.

![Conor Colegate says his brother Louis Malletier is facing daily attacks in prison in Turkey](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/27/12/41/881b45ec-e641-460c-872d-c86ea4bebd62.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

They say that in Turkish prison he has suffered regular beatings, abuse, and “psychological torture” from other inmates, and are calling on the government to urgently intervene in his case.

“I am so worried about him; he is extremely vulnerable and wouldn’t have known what he was doing,” Mr Colegate, who works as a mental health nurse, told *The Independent*. “We need to protect him. My biggest fear is that someone is going to call me and say something tragic has happened to him in the prison.”

Mr Malletier’s family believe he told his carer he was going to visit them in Kent, something he regularly did by himself, before travelling to Heathrow airport under the impression he had won a free holiday on 25 September.

They said he had believed he would be provided with accommodation and money upon arrival in Thailand, leaving him at the mercy of the traffickers, who gave him a ticket to Malaga and forced him to take a suitcase with him.

![Mr Colegate said his brother is 'extremely vulnerable'](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/27/12/35/21cc0e80-808e-49b5-a76f-e38491be75f1.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Because both his family and his carer believed he was safe with the other, neither were aware he had left the country until Mr Malletier’s dad received a panicked call from him after he was detained in Istanbul on 15 October. Soon after, they say his phone was taken and they were unable to contact him.

Mr Colegate said it took the family several desperate weeks to find out where Mr Malletier had been taken. Once they had located him, they enlisted the help of a Turkish lawyer, who was able to visit the 33-year-old in prison and understand the circumstances leading to his detention.

He travelled to visit his twin himself on 8 November, and said he “broke down” after he saw the condition his brother was in.

“He has never cuddled me before, but he ran at me, grabbed me, laid his head on my shoulder and just cried,” he said. “He soaked my shoulder in snot and tears and didn’t want to let go.

“He told me he feels like he is in hell.”

Mr Colegate said his brother told him he is living in a cell with 70 other men and is forced to sleep on the concrete floor with just a small blanket and pillow. He even showed him a pair of orange earbuds he had been trying to push so far down his ears he would make himself deaf because he was so traumatised by the noises in the prison.

![Mr Colegate is calling on the British government to intervene in his brother's case](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/27/12/20/90ccb8c8-1afa-4b7f-8ff4-e8cd4e172072.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“He is literally living off peanuts and raw onion,” he said. “The thing that sounded most appealing to me that he has eaten in there is potato soup.”

He told *The Independent* his brother has not been charged but is being investigated under drug importation charges, which could leave him facing a prison sentence of 20 to 30 years.

“It’s just so heartbreaking because I have no control to be able to stop it,” he said. “If anything, I would rather take his place.”

He described his brother as a “lovely” man who “can’t wait to help everyone” and believes he was targeted because of his specific vulnerabilities.

“Tell him the words free and he’s all over it,” he explained. “He is still a 33-year-old man that knows how to get around and navigate, but he doesn’t sense the danger the way a 33-year-old would.”

Mr Colegate is now calling on foreign secretary Ed Miliband and prime minister Andy Burnham to protect Mr Malletier, arguing they have a duty to safeguard him as a vulnerable British national.

“Knowing him personally and as a professional, he is extremely vulnerable,” Mr Colegate said. “He would not have known what he was doing.

“We need to protect him and make sure that he doesn’t fall through the cracks of the justice system in another country. There is a language barrier and they probably don’t fully understand his disabilities and his vulnerabilities.

“I want Ed Miliband and Andy Burnham talking about it. I want them involved. I want them taking action because ultimately they’re the leaders of our country and they’re the ones that need to be safeguarding their vulnerable British national.”

He has also launched a fundraiser to support the family with their legal costs and to pay for travel to visit Mr Malletier.

An FCDO Spokesperson said: “We are supporting a British national detained in Turkey and are in contact with the local authorities”.

The Turkish embassy has been contacted for comment.
