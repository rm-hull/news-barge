**As the UK Government promotes its WaterSure scheme, Independent Age highlights the need for wider assistance for pensioners struggling with water affordability.**

Independent Age has responded to the UK Government’s recent efforts to improve take-up of the WaterSure scheme, a tariff designed to help households with high water use linked to medical conditions or large families. The national charity, which supports older people facing financial hardship, has emphasised that the scheme’s narrow eligibility excludes many older individuals on low incomes who are struggling to pay rising water bills.

> **Joanna Elson, Chief Executive of Independent Age, CBE:**  
> There is no doubt WaterSure is an important scheme which brings much needed support to the relatively small group of people on low incomes it is designed to help – those whose high use is linked to a medical condition, or families with multiple children. But the scheme’s very specific eligibility criteria mean it isn’t available to many older people on low incomes who desperately need support now to pay rising water bills.

The charity’s own research, based on a YouGov poll conducted in early November 2026, estimates that nearly 750,000 pensioner households in England and Wales currently experience water poverty, a figure that could rise to one million by 2029-30. The research defined low income as individuals earning under £15,000 annually.

Independent Age regularly hears from older people who are forced to ration their water use to reduce costs, sometimes at the expense of their health. Examples include reducing toilet flushes, skipping showers, and washing clothes simultaneously with personal hygiene to conserve water.

> **Elson:**  
> Independent Age is calling on the UK Government to end the national shame of water unaffordability once and for all, through broader and more consistent support. A single social tariff for water in England and Wales is the best way to ensure everyone on a low income who is struggling with rising water bills has access to the same level of support from their water company. All low-income households must be able to use water without the fear of being hit with unaffordable bills.

Independent Age continues to campaign for wider assistance and policy change to support older people facing financial hardship, including water poverty, highlighting the pressing need for equitable access to essential utilities.

For more information on Independent Age and their work, visit www.independentage.org.
