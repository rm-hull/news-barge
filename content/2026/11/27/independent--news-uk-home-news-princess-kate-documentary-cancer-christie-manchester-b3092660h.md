The Princess of Wales has said she is 'delighted' to become the new patron of a major cancer hospital.

Kate, herself a former cancer patient, is the new figurehead of Manchester-based centre The Christie and its charity, after visiting the Withington site in June. The princess called the facility an "extraordinary place".

One of Europe's leading cancer centres, The Christie treats over 60,000 patients annually.

It serves a population of 3.2 million people from Greater Manchester and the surrounding areas.

Among its holistic therapies, all free of charge to patients and carers, are drop-in art classes, a wellbeing garden and a chaplaincy service.

![Kate, herself a former cancer patient, is the new figurehead of Manchester-based centre The Christie and its charity](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/27/10/01M8E4SENKZ4C59A7XS44VMB6S.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Roger Spencer, chief executive of The Christie, said: "We were honoured to welcome Her Royal Highness to our Withington site in June and are delighted that she has now become patron of The Christie and the charity that supports it.

"The care we deliver is centred on the individual, not just their cancer. Our person-centred approach is something Her Royal Highness is also an advocate for, and it was a pleasure to show her the holistic support available to patients and their loved ones during their visit.

“Her interest and support were a real inspiration for our patients and staff and we look forward to working with her to shine a spotlight on the expert and compassionate care that our colleagues deliver every day.”

![The Princess of Wales meets Claire Lorente after she rang the bell marking the end of her treatment](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/27/10/01M8E4S9HGT0FKNCYD4YTV0VKQ.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

In a social media post, the princess wrote: "During my visit to The Christie NHS Foundation Trust, I saw first-hand how exceptional care extends beyond treatment alone.

"When people face serious illness, it is often the compassion, relationships and support that surround them which help sustain hope and resilience through the most challenging times.

"The Christie's commitment to caring for the whole person, alongside it's world-leading clinical expertise, makes it an extraordinary place.

“I am delighted to become Patron and look forward to supporting the remarkable people who make such a difference to the lives of patients and their families every day – C.”

During her June visit, Kate joined in a hospital cancer ward celebration for a young mother who completed her chemotherapy treatment.

She hugged Claire Lorente, 30, then joined well-wishers in encouraging a tearful Ms Lorente to ring a bell, a tradition that marks the end of treatment, saying: “You can do it.”

![During her June visit, Kate joined in a hospital cancer ward celebration for a young mother who completed her chemotherapy treatment](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/27/10/01M8E4S9JTQ1KZWJWM6AR895CD.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Kate revealed at the start of last year she was in remission after receiving chemotherapy treatment for an undisclosed form of cancer.

Her cancer journey is the subject of a new documentary called Heal, which features the princess saying how being “faced with your own mortality” means accepting life is not infinite which “softens your heart”.

A trailer released for the 30-minute documentary, which premieres on BBC iPlayer and BBC One on Sunday, shows Kate undertaking the Three Peaks Challenge – climbing the highest mountains in Scotland, England and Wales within 24 hours – and reflecting on her cancer experience alongside others navigating their own health journeys.

In it, she was seen saying “cancer does change you” and heard in a voiceover saying: “It’s clear we’re not meant to carry life’s challenges alone.”
