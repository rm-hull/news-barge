A charity set up by the family of renowned Covid-19 pandemic fundraiser Captain Sir Tom Moore has applied to be struck off.

The charity was previously known as The Captain Tom Foundation, but is now known as the 1189808 Foundation after dropping Sir Tom’s name in January 2025.

On Friday, an application to have it struck off appeared on the Companies House register.

The dropping of Sir Tom’s name came after the Charity Commission found that there had been “repeated” misconduct and mismanagement on the part of Sir Tom’s daughter, Hannah Ingram-Moore, and her husband, Colin Ingram-Moore, in their running of the foundation.

The report found a “repeated pattern of behaviour” through which the couple made private gains and which the regulator said would have left people feeling “misled”.

![Hannah Ingram-Moore and her husband Colin were banned from being charity trustees in 2024](https://static.independent.co.uk/2024/11/21/00/0b189ed19f1add5a2a230a756bf8d551Y29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzMxMDAwNzQw-2.63516456.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The Ingram-Moores enjoyed a number of financial benefits through their family links to the Captain Tom Foundation, the report found, including a £1.4 million book deal and an £18,000 awards ceremony appearance fee.

It was found that Ms Ingram-Moore had set out expectations for a £150,000 salary as CEO, and that the couple had used the charity’s name “inappropriately for private benefit” to erect a spa building in the garden of their Grade II listed home in Bedfordshire.

The couple have already been banned from being charity trustees.

Ms Ingram-Moore was prohibited from being a trustee or holding a senior management role in any charity in England and Wales for 10 years, and Mr Ingram-Moore for eight years.

They both resigned from the foundation.

Ms Ingram-Moore has since rebranded as a TikTok “resilience coach” under the name “Moore Moments”.

![Captain Tom raised millions for the NHS during the first Covid lockdown](https://static.independent.co.uk/2025/07/08/00/76420eaefd6f84f38a2ff49ce9033975Y29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzUxOTgyOTQ4-2.57865919.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Sir Tom made headlines during the Covid pandemic by walking 100 laps of his garden during the first lockdown in the lead-up to his 100th birthday.

The Second World War veteran originally had the aim of raising £1,000, but his efforts ultimately raised more than £32 million.

That money, which was raised before the foundation was formed, were not part of the commission’s inquiry.

Sir Tom was later given a Pride of Britain award and was knighted by Queen Elizabeth II.

He died, aged 100, in February 2021 after contracting Covid and being treated for pneumonia.
