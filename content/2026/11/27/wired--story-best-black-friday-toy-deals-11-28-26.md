With the weather worsening, taking advantage of Black Friday toy deals and picking up discounted board games or Lego sets could be a very smart move. The WIRED Reviews team has a bunch of kids of all ages between us, and most of us are still big kids at heart, so we’ve tried a lot of board games and toys over the years.

The best Black Friday deals are well and truly on, and that includes some of the best family board games, best STEM toys, and best gifts for Lego lovers. I have handpicked a selection of WIRED-tested and approved options for you right here, so whether you’re buying gifts or just treating yourself, there’s sure to be something to put a smile on your face.

## Best Black Friday Toy Deals

What kid doesn’t love a marble run? This clever release from ThinkFun combines the fun of dropping marbles with logic puzzles that challenge kids to build a 3D maze that can guide the marble to the target end spot by building connected towers. It’s one of the best STEM toys we’ve tried, and it goes down a storm with most kids aged eight years and up.

This irresistible construction kit from our best STEM toys guide is a wonderful gift for kids of all ages. Clixo’s colorful, versatile magnetic pieces snap together, letting you build all kinds of three-dimensional shapes, from crowns to cats. The pieces stack neatly and are very compact when packed away. Clixo sets are also durable and washable, and you can play with them anywhere. Clixo kits are suitable for kids aged 4 and up.

The tough, translucent Magna-Tiles click together easily to form two-dimensional shapes and three-dimensional objects. Kids love building houses, castles, vehicles, and whatever else they can imagine with these triangular and square magnetic tiles. The classic set is a great start, but be warned: There are themed sets, and you will end up buying more! I recommend the Undersea Adventure set.

One for older kids, the Camp Snap CS-8 is a (relatively) inexpensive video camera modeled on a 1960s home movie camera. Think of it as a digital Super 8 camera, a kind of antidote to the spec-chasing rat race of modern videography which also makes a great starter camera for children. It'll shoot 2.7K video, but that's not really the point, this is a camera that you pass around at parties, throw in your backpacks for day trips, and use without a second thought. —*Scott Gilbertson* Sphero's Bolt coding robot is for any kid interested in programming. It's got a gyroscope, accelerometer and magnetometer, ambient light sensor, and the LED matrix for feedback. Kids can tap into all this through a visual programming interface, or older kids can dive into JavaScript or Python to control the Bolt. —*Scott Gilbertson* This 175-piece kit takes you through the process of building a 4-cylinder engine. As someone who has built both this and rebuilt an actual engine (technically a 6-cylinder), this is very realistic, right down to all the tiny screws and springs you'll need to get it all together. And yes it “runs” or rather the pistons and valves move and will definitely give kids a very good working sense of how and internal combustion engine works.*—Scott Gilbertson*

## Best Black Friday Board Game Deals

This Bluetooth-enabled chess board connects to an app on your phone, and you can play against remote players or yourself, or get AI coaching to improve your game. You can scale the assistance you want, with different-colored lights that advise you of moves as you pick up each piece. It’s perfect for beginners and kids. Particula also makes a Harry Potter-themed version that appears in our best smart chess boards guide.

Azul is about making mosaics by matching tiles with different patterns and colors to get the maximum possible score. It’s easy to grasp but supports many different tactical approaches and has surprising depth. The tiles and other components are high quality, and filling your board is deeply satisfying. Play moves quickly, and you can complete a game in less than an hour, but you will want to play again.

This beautifully themed game casts players as villains from the Star Wars universe, such as Darth Vader and Moff Gideon. If you’ve played other games in the Villainous series, you’ll know every character gets a unique set of victory conditions, and you can meddle with the other player’s attempts to win. There’s a definite learning curve, and some characters are easier to win with than others. This game is best for four players aged 10 years and up.

This immersive strategy game is all about gathering and trading resources to build roads, settlements, and cities on a randomly generated board of hexagonal tiles. Collecting enough points to win requires the right strategy and a bit of luck with dice rolls. My kids love the trading and the robber mechanic, which allows you to steal cards from your opponents and block their resources (though it can cause arguments). The game is perfect for three or four players and usually lasts around an hour.

The perfect spooky board game for classic horror fans, you must work cooperatively to defeat old horror-movie monsters like Dracula, the Wolf Man, and the Mummy. Cooperative games are perfect for family board game night, and this one is challenging enough that you really have to communicate well and work together if you want to avoid an untimely death and save the townsfolk.

Some of the best family board games are cooperative, so you can avoid those arguments that sometimes lead to board flipping and huffs, and Pandemic is a solid example. There’s a worldwide pandemic, and you must work together for the survival of mankind by jetting around the board and trying to halt the spread. Each player assumes a role, like a scientist or medic, with unique special powers, and you must strategize and coordinate to emerge alive.
