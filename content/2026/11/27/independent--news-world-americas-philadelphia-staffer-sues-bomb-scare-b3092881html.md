A Pennsylvania legislative staffer whose gym bag sparked a bomb scare and evacuation at Philadelphia City Hall is suing the city and its Sheriff's Office, claiming deputies held him for hours without justification.

Jawad Pullin, legislative director for Pennsylvania state Sen. Sharif Street, filed a federal lawsuit Wednesday over the September 17 incident. He claims deputies kept him in custody for more than two hours after officials searched his bag, which contained a laptop, boxing gloves, sneakers and a headphone cord, and found nothing dangerous.

Pullin brought the bag to City Hall for Philadelphia City Council's first meeting of the fall session. After passing through security, he placed it on an empty chair in the council chamber to save a seat for Street, according to the lawsuit.

When Street had not arrived as the meeting began, Pullin stepped outside to call him. By the time he returned, someone had taken the seat and moved his bag to the floor. Pullin left it there rather than interrupt the meeting, the lawsuit states.

Shortly after 11 a.m., a council staffer spotted the unattended bag and alerted the Philadelphia Sheriff's Office. Two sheriff's office K-9s alerted to the bag, prompting officials to evacuate City Hall and call the Philadelphia Police Department's bomb squad.

Sharif Street, is suing Philadelphia after deputies detained him for hours over an unattended gym bag that prompted a bomb scare at City Hall](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/27/15/2221660584..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Pullin approached authorities and said the bag might be his. But deputies handcuffed him behind his back, questioned him and led him out of City Hall in front of reporters, according to the lawsuit.

“[Pullin] did not attempt to flee, conceal his identity, or avoid the authorities,” the complaint says. “He sought them out.”

Authorities cleared the bag around noon, finding only personal belongings and no explosives. Philadelphia Deputy Police Commissioner Frank Vanore told reporters at the time that the dogs may have detected residue from a firearm or fireworks that had previously been inside the bag.

But deputies did not release Pullin. They took him to a holding cell at the Criminal Justice Center before taking him to police headquarters, where officers searched him, removed his shoelaces and placed him in another cell.

An investigator eventually apologized and acknowledged that authorities had overreacted because Pullin had done nothing wrong, the lawsuit alleges. He was released around 3 p.m., nearly four hours after the evacuation began.

The lawsuit argues deputies had no legal reason to keep Pullin in custody after confirming his bag was harmless. It accuses the defendants of unlawful search and seizure, false imprisonment and prolonged detention. It also claims Sheriff Rochelle Bilal failed to properly supervise her deputies.

Pullin is seeking compensatory and punitive damages and a jury trial. The lawsuit names the city, the Sheriff's Office, Bilal and several unnamed deputies as defendants.

The complaint also says the incident damaged Pullin's reputation by linking him to a bomb scare. Footage of him being led away in handcuffs circulated in local news coverage and on social media.

Pullin has not been charged with a crime related to the incident.

*The Independent* has contacted representatives for the City of Philadelphia and the Sheriff's Office, as well as Pullin, for comment.
