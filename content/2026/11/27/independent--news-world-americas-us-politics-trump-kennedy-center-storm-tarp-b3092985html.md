Scaffolding and a massive white tarp have covered the entrance to the Kennedy Center for six months, blocking the building’s signage so the public can’t see where Donald Trump’s name used to be after the president’s failed attempt to rename the building after himself.

But powerful wind gusts and rain in Washington, D.C., on Thanksgiving were strong enough to loosen the tarp, exposing the venue’s blank marble facade for the first time since Trump’s name was stripped from it.

Video from NBC’s News4 shows most of the tarp remained attached to the scaffolding, but an upper corner became detached, revealing the blank space where Trump’s name was stamped in metal letters.

Construction crews began removing the president’s name from the building during an overnight operation in June, hours after a federal district court judge and panel of appeals court judges denied the Kennedy Center’s 11th-hour attempts to rename the venue.

But the scaffolding and tarp has been in place ever since while the Trump administration fights a legal battle over its attempts to close the building altogether for two years for renovations.

![Storm gusts loosened up a massive tarp that has been covering up the entrance to the Kennedy Center for more than six months after workers were ordered to strip Donald Trump’s name from an institution he wants to rename after himself](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/27/18/2292011242..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Lawyers for the Department of Justice previously argued that the scaffolding needs to stay there while workers perform “water testing and structural repairs to the roof.”

In December, after appointing himself a trustee and reshaping the board with his allies, the Kennedy Center’s board voted to rename the institution the memorial to President John F. Kennedy as the “The Donald J. Trump and John F. Kennedy Memorial Center for the Performing Arts.”

Trump’s name was added to the exterior facade the next day.

In May, a federal judge ruled that Congress made it “crystal clear” that the memorial is only to be named after the assassinated former president, “and it cannot bear any other formal name or public memorial” based on a “unilateral say-so.”

The next month, construction crews erected scaffolding and covered their work in a tarp in an overnight operation to remove Trump’s name.

In September, the board voted to close the building for repairs after the Trump administration repeatedly argued that the venue was in dire financial straits and could not continue to operate without the president’s name on the building.

A federal judge is currently mulling a decision on whether to indefinitely block the closure while a legal battle is ongoing.

![Democratic Rep Joyce Beatty, who is leading the legal battle against Trump’s proposed changes, has accused the Kennedy Center of trying to close down for two years to get around court orders that block the president from renaming it](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/08/27/11/2281284410..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

During a hearing on November 19, lawyers for Democratic Rep. Joyce Beatty, who is leading the legal battle against Trump’s plans, argued that the Kennedy Center’s emergency closure is merely an attempt to accomplish what it can’t do under the court’s injunction.

Lawyers for the Justice Department called that idea “offensive,” claiming that poor conditions and imminent risk of collapse require its emergency closure.

In September, Judge Christopher R. Cooper ordered the administration to provide at least 30 days’ notice before making any physical changes to the Kennedy Center — including demolishing it.

His order followed a flurry of court documents in response to the president’s suggestion that the memorial could be “ripped down” after the board’s vote to shutter the venue for repairs.

Kennedy Center officials insisted in court filings that the venue is only “temporarily” closing to address “advanced deterioration,” though the intensifying legal battle to close it for years has only fueled speculation that the president could try to tear it down altogether.

The Justice Department also wants to prevent the judge from forcing the government to share details about the president’s role in the Kennedy Center’s planned two-year closure.

Beatty’s legal team accused the Justice Department of “stonewalling” requests for information.

Unless the judge intervenes, the Trump administration “will be allowed to curate the record and hide the truth,” Beatty’s lawyers wrote last month.
