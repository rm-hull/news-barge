Approving the Rosebank oil field would “permanently damage” the UK’s climate credentials and threaten Labour’s chances of re-election, backbench MPs have warned Andy Burnham.

In a letter to the Prime Minister, dozens of backbenchers urged Mr Burnham to refuse permission to develop the oil project 80 miles north west of Shetland.

Monmouthshire MP Catherine Fookes, who helped organise the letter, told the Guardian it had been signed by 92 MPs, enough to make it difficult to carry on Labour votes alone.

![Catherine Fookes (House of Commons/UK Parliament)](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/26/22/6668a3ee49f68a96b4a137046d9ba82bY29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzk1ODE5Njc2-2.78340733.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

In their letter, the group said they were “increasingly concerned that the approval of the Rosebank oil field would permanently damage our climate credentials as a party and as a country”.

They added: “Polling is clear – in order to stand a chance of winning parliamentary majorities, maintaining our ‘progressive electoral base’ is imperative… approving Rosebank risks becoming an irreversible ‘point of no return’ for the party’s climate credibility, and risks alienating members of the electorate who have put their trust in our message of hope and positivity.”

Following the collapse of the Cop31 climate talks in Turkey last week, the backbenchers said the UK “must be wary about what signals we are sending our counterparts about our commitment to transition away from fossil fuels”.

They cast doubt on the argument that Rosebank would be a significant boost for the Treasury, describing it as “not a serious revenue raising measure” and claiming it would provide “less than 0.1% of our annual UK tax revenue”.

![Energy Secretary Miatta Fahnbulleh (PA)](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/26/22/8cf9b91231fa0032ad3251a3d297c955Y29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzk1ODE5NzE1-2.86442537.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The Government is yet to say when Energy Secretary Miatta Fahnbulleh will decide whether to approve Rosebank, although one of the companies with a stake in the project has said it expects a decision by the end of the year.

The decision threatens to become a major political problem for Mr Burnham, who has already come under pressure from environmental charities to reject the project.

But the Conservatives have called for the Government to “get Britain drilling” and approve Rosebank, claiming it would secure jobs, tax revenue and energy security.

Last month, the Government approved production at the Jackdaw gas field, also in the North Sea, despite opposition from environmental campaigners.

Writing in the Guardian, Ms Fookes said she and her fellow backbenchers had felt “uneasy” about Jackdaw but “understood the reasons for that decision” as it was a “relatively small, short-lived” project.

But Rosebank is “a different proposition”, she added, that would “do nothing to reduce energy bills for our constituents”, with the oil produced being sold “to the highest bidder”.

![(PA Graphics)](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/26/22/71e1c41669ecfc578012d69c70fa3002Y29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzk1ODE5NzQ2-2.85399371.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

She said: “If Rosebank is approved, champagne corks will pop in the offices of oil companies. But it will not make an iota of difference to our constituents’ energy bills.

“It will accelerate the climate crisis, which is already pushing up food bills, causing devastating floods in winter and in summer turning our green and pleasant land into a parched, burnt-out expanse.”

Labour MP Fleur Anderson, who signed the letter, said Ms Fahnbulleh would need to weigh the economic case for Rosebank against the “economic damage done by climate change and the lives, the wildfires that we saw” in the summer.

Asked about Chancellor John Healey’s reported support for approving the field on the basis of the potential tax take, she told BBC Radio 4’s Today programme: “Our understanding is tax revenues are very low, and the (Office for Budget Responsibility) itself says that they will fall to nearly zero by 2030, and yet the oil will still be being pumped out from Rosebank until the 2050s.”

![Green leader Zack Polanski welcomed the letter (PA)](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/27/09/ef2d29c434fedd5e47e865f837b30415Y29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzk1ODU3MTUw-2.86583485.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

She also suggested that Labour opposition to the potential expansion of Heathrow Airport is at a similar level, telling Today there are “significant concerns”.

But the MP said Mr Burnham and his ministers are listening to backbenchers, adding: “I’ve met with the Secretary of State on this and we’ve talked to Andy Burnham. He does listen.

“I’ve seen this many times, and not just listen, but take action as a result of listening… and we take that in absolute good faith, and that’s why we’ve joined together to write this letter.”

Green Party leader Zack Polanski piled pressure on to the Prime Minister, welcoming “the growing number of Labour MPs making a stand against Rosebank”.

He said: “The Prime Minister is giving every impression of wanting to see it go ahead – just over a year after he signed the Fossil Fuel Non-Proliferation Treaty.

“From cutting warmer homes funding to watering down electric vehicle mandates, this Labour Government cannot claim to be a climate leader while engaging in climate vandalism.”

A spokesperson for the Department for Energy Security and Net Zero said: “Any decision on Rosebank will be made through the quasi-judicial process, taking into account all relevant evidence, including environmental assessments and public representations received as part of the regulatory process.”
