STRUCTURAL defects have been discovered on a key North Yorkshire bridge.

The northbound carriage of the Selby Bypass Swing Bridge is currently closed, with two-way temporary traffic signals in place, with repairs set to take an indeterminate amount of time.

The issues were found were during an overnight closure of the A63 between 7:30pm last night (November 26) and 6am on Friday (November 27).

Karl Battersby, North Yorkshire Council’s director for environment, said: "Following an overnight closure of the A63 to assess Selby Bypass Swing Bridge, structural defects were identified on a transition plate on both the northbound and southbound carriageway.

"These plates allow vehicles to pass safely from the swing bridge deck to the adjoining road and are essential to the safe operation of the crossing.

"As a result, temporary traffic management measures have been put in place to ensure the bridge can remain safely open to traffic."

The northbound lane is currently closed, with two-way temporary traffic signals in place, and on the southbound side, a chicane system is being used to divert vehicles away from the damaged area.

Mr Battersby said: "While these measures are not ideal and may cause delays, they provide a safe interim solution and avoid the need for a full road closure

“We are now sourcing specialist contractors to undertake repairs. At this stage it is too early to confirm exactly how long the repairs will take, but the aim is to restore normal two-way traffic flows as soon as possible.

"We would like to thank motorists for their patience and apologise for any disruption while these essential repairs are carried out."
