# Rapist attacked woman after Sheffield night out then fled to Iraq

## A sex attacker who raped a woman in Sheffield before fleeing the UK the next day has been jailed more than two years later.

Sultan Al-Zireg, 26, attacked the woman after she’d been on a night out with a friend at an address in Sheffield city centre in the early hours of January 27.

The rape victim, who is in her 20s, reported the attack the following day.

Al-Zireg was charged in his absence, but then flew back to the UK on January 4, 2026, and was arrested at Manchester Airport.

![Sultan Al-Zireg, 26, has been jailed after raping a woman in her 20s and then fleeing the country.](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/jpim-static/image/2026/11/27/14/10/Sultan-Al-Zireg.png?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


He denied two counts of rape, two counts of sexual assault, and one count of assault by penetration, but was convicted after a five-day trial.

During the trial, the victim’s personal statement was read out on her behalf.

It read: “For months after (the rape), I barely left my house except to go to work.

"I have felt overwhelming shame, embarrassment and a complete loss of dignity… I found myself constantly questioning my own actions, thinking, 'I should have been firmer. I should have fought harder’.

"Those thoughts have been relentless and have caused me to doubt myself in ways I never had before.

"They left me feeling invalidated, despite knowing that every person's response to trauma is different.

"What has been taken from me is not just my sense of safety, but also my confidence, my independence and my trust. I miss the person I was before this happened—the fun girl who was excited for her first night out with a new friend. While I continue to rebuild my life, the effects of what happened remain with me every single day.”

Al-Zireg was jailed for eight years and six months on November 26.

Detective Constable Kevin Sibley, the officer in charge of the case, said: "I would like to pay tribute to the victim’s bravery in pursuing prosecution in this case.

"Al-Zireg’s decision to flee after his crime, and then to deny his guilt when he returned to the country, showed him to have no remorse.

"We were determined to achieve justice for the victim and repay the trust she put in us, and I am very happy that we have done so.”

Following the sentencing, the victim said: "This has been one of the hardest things I’ve ever had to go through, and I don’t think I’ll ever be able to put into words the emotions I’ve felt along the way.

"I’m just so relieved it’s over, and so glad I found the courage to report it

"I will forever be grateful to Kevin, for the compassion and support he has shown me throughout this process, and for the incredible work he has done to help get justice for me.”
