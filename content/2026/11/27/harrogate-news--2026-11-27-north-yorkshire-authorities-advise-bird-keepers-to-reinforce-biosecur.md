**With avian influenza detected at two commercial turkey farms near Thirsk and Northallerton, North Yorkshire Council is urging all bird keepers to comply with housing and biosecurity requirements to prevent further spread.**

Bird flu has been confirmed at two commercial turkey farms near Thirsk and Northallerton in North Yorkshire, prompting local authorities to call on bird keepers across the county to implement strict biosecurity and housing measures.

Protection and surveillance zones have been established around the affected farms. These zones include restrictions on bird movements and additional measures designed to curb the disease’s spread.

The UK Health Security Agency maintains that the risk to the general public is very low, with properly cooked poultry, poultry products and eggs remaining safe for consumption.

A nationwide Avian Influenza Prevention Zone remains active, mandating all bird keepers to rigorously follow biosecurity protocols. Housing requirements apply to flocks of 50 birds or more, and to smaller groups if poultry or eggs are supplied beyond the keeper’s household.

North Yorkshire Council’s executive member responsible for managing the environment, Cllr Richard Foster, emphasised the importance of adherence to these measures:

These measures are vital to protect birds across North Yorkshire.

Avian influenza can have a devastating impact on both commercial and small-scale bird keepers, and we urge everyone to follow the requirements, maintain good biosecurity and ensure their birds are registered.

He also highlighted the shared responsibility of bird owners:

Whether you keep thousands of birds or just a few hens at home, everyone has a role to play in preventing the spread of disease.

By following the housing and biosecurity requirements, we can help protect our poultry sector, rural communities and local businesses while keeping North Yorkshire free from further spread of the disease.

Bird keepers have been urged to monitor their flocks for any signs of illness and to contact a veterinary professional promptly if birds become unwell.

Louise Wallace, North Yorkshire Council’s director of public health, cautioned that although bird flu infections in humans are rare and the public risk remains very low, precautions remain necessary:

However, avian influenza can be serious, and residents are advised to follow national guidance by not touching sick or dead wild birds, avoiding contact with feathers or droppings and washing your hands thoroughly if contact cannot be avoided to help reduce any risk.

We are working closely with partners, including the UK Health Security Agency, the Animal and Plant Health Agency and Defra, to monitor the situation and provide advice to protect both public and animal health.

Any suspicion of bird flu in kept birds must be reported immediately to the Animal and Plant Health Agency (APHA) on 03000 200 301.

The council also reminds bird keepers that registering captive birds is a legal requirement. Those who have not yet registered are encouraged to do so at www.gov.uk/government/collections/register-as-a-bird-keeper.

Members of the public are advised not to touch sick or dead wild birds. Any sightings of dead wild birds should be reported by telephone on 03459 33 55 77 or through an online reporting system.
