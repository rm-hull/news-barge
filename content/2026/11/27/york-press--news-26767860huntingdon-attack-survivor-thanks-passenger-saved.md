A SURVIVOR of the Huntingdon train knife attack has told a fellow passenger who tried to stop the knifeman that she owes him her life.

Astrid, who was 19 at the time of the attack and has chosen not to share her surname, publicly thanked passenger Stephen Crean in an interview with the BBC.

The attack took place on a London-bound LNER service on November 1, 2023, when 33-year-old Anthony Williams stabbed 10 people after boarding the train at Peterborough.

The train made an unscheduled stop at Huntingdon, with the help of teams at the Rail Operating Centre in York, and Williams was arrested at the scene.

He was found guilty of all 14 counts of attempted murder by a jury after 16 hours and 52 minutes of deliberations.

Astrid said: "I just owe him everything. I wouldn’t be here today without you, and to put your life on the line for someone else, a complete stranger, is just complete bravery."

Astrid recalled Stephen coming from her right-hand side, placing himself between her and Williams, and then throwing himself at the attacker "in an attempt to take the knife out of his hand".

She said: "He had a split second to make a decision and I owe him my life because of the decision he made."

Stephen, who was returning home after watching a Nottingham Forest football match, confronted Williams and was stabbed multiple times in the back, hip, fingers, and head.

He said meeting Astrid meant a great deal to him, adding: "It means the world to me to meet her. Meeting her was the best thing, it was worth waiting for. I’m just so happy."

Stephen also told the BBC that the knifeman “shouldn’t have been allowed to go running around like this”.
