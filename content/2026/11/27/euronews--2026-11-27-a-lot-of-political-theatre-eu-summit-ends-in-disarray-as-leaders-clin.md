## António Costa's attempt to prod EU leaders into ditching their red lines on the budget fell flat on Thursday as talks turned messy and bitter.

A summit specifically convened to find a breakthrough on the European Union's next budget ended in disarray after leaders, split in opposing camps, refused to abandon their red lines and openly questioned whether a deal could be reached by December.

The meeting was plagued by tensions, reproaches and even boycotts, raising serious questions on the leadership of European Council President António Costa, whose credibility now rests on whether these negotiations succeed or fail.

Costa's **compromise proposal** worth €1.78 trillion in current prices across seven years – a 10% cut from the original draft – was met with fierce pushback from all sides, taking the Council presidency aback.

"This meeting was a necessary step in the process. This is a negotiation process. It is normal that the different parties are starting to underline and mark their starting points. Everybody knows that we cannot fail Europe, and we need to have a new budget from the 1st of January 2028," Costa said at the press conference at the end of the summit.

The summit appeared doomed from the outset.

Leaders showed virtually no willingness to compromise as they made their way into Thursday's gathering and huddled with their respective groups.

On one side, the seven "Frugals", bent on slashing the budget. On the other side, the 17 "Friends of Cohesion", determined to push back against further cuts.

The tone was set by Italian Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni, who told reporters upon arrival that a deal on the seven-year budget was "light years away".

Meloni questioned Costa's much-repeated objective of reaching a deal by the end of the year to prevent the process from spilling over into the 2027 electoral cycle.

"I don't have a time limit. I have specific conditions," she said. "When the agreement is one that I consider valid and fair for Italy, then I'll sign it."

Dutch Prime Minister Rob Jetten, a staunch "frugal", was equally dismissive.

"There's no need to hurry," he said. "For me, there's no deadline."

The budget negotiations began around midday after leaders settled the matter of Christine Lagarde's succession as **European Central Bank president**. They first met all together in the main room, where they shared their well-known positions.

Nobody caved in during this session, waiting for others to move first, diplomats said.

Then, Costa split the summit into small groups of leaders. First, the "frugals", then the "cohesion friends", and then the six largest member states. After that, he began breakout sessions with geographically mixed leaders.

The proceedings increasingly frustrated the "frugals", who saw it as an attempt to pile pressure on them in favour of the "cohesion friends".

Feeling unfairly targeted, the "frugals" closed ranks and decided to boycott the small-group format, which Costa continued without their participation.

Meanwhile, French President Emmanuel Macron raised the stakes, calling a press conference to deliver a stark ultimatum: reach a deal by December or walk away empty-handed.

Macron warned that protracted negotiations risked becoming dangerously entangled in the French presidential campaign. Far-right firebrand Marine Le Pen, who is leading opinion polls, has vowed to slash France's contribution to the EU budget.

"I intend to make it very clear to our colleagues that, for political and democratic reasons, it will not be possible to reopen or conclude negotiations on the budget after 31st December," Macron said, decrying Costa's proposed cuts as a "strategic mistake".

"We are not prepared to reach a deal at any cost. We can conclude by 31st December, but that is the absolute limit."

Other member states consider Macron's threat of a future Le Pen presidency a tactic to extract concessions and eventually get his way, despite Costa's compromise widely seen as being overly French-friendly.

"That's quite rich," a diplomat quipped about the French reaction.

Macron's mid-summit appearance, a rare occurrence, wiped out chances of a breakthrough and precipitated the collapse of the talks.

In his own press conference, German Chancellor Friedrich Merz adopted a hardline "frugal" stance and vowed to slash the budget even further.

The deep political divide between Paris and Berlin, the EU’s two main powerhouses, has cast a shadow over the negotiations.

"Are we any further ahead? Yes and no. We are trying to define which direction it is going. I think I may say that," Merz told reporters.

The cuts to the budget, he said, will not stop at the €204 billion proposed by Costa. "It will become less," he added. "How much it will be then is open."

The fact that Costa announced early in the summit that the talks would not continue on Friday as originally planned dampened the energy among national delegations and immediately lowered expectations for a breakthrough, a diplomat said.

A second diplomat was more positive on the outcome of the summit, despite the messy proceedings. "You need to have a bad European Council to have a good one," they said. "It was a lot of political theatre. We did not have detailed negotiations."

Forgotten in the talks was Ukraine.

The summit was supposed to discuss a **€45 billion support loan** for the country, which faces a ballooning wartime deficit amid Russia's relentless attacks. But the budget fight sucked in all the energy and left Ukraine relegated to an afterthought.
