## EU budget talks collapse early in Brussels as Germany and "frugals" walk out, pushing a deal to December. Meanwhile, Spain braces for Sunday’s snap election that could unseat PM Pedro Sánchez, and Britain unveils details of its new EU cooperation deal.

European leaders left Brussels without a deal after a special two-day summit on the bloc’s next seven-year budget (2028–2034) ended a full day early on Thursday night. Talks broke down after midnight when German Chancellor Friedrich Merz and the fiscally conservative "frugal" nations walked out on European Council President António Costa. Leaders have set a final deadline for reaching an agreement at an upcoming summit one week before Christmas.

The deadlock exposes deep divisions over spending priorities. Berlin is pushing for expenditure cuts and wants to link Ukraine funding directly to the broader EU budget, with Merz threatening a veto unless all 27 member states sign on.

By contrast, the "Friends of Cohesion" bloc (including Italy and Greece) is resisting spending cuts. Italian Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni maintained a firm stance, echoed by Greek Prime Minister Kyriakos Mitsotakis, who warned: "We cannot talk about a strong Europe with a weak budget." French President Emmanuel Macron also warned that failing to reach an agreement before the end of the year would stall negotiations until after France's presidential election in April.

### **Spain’s snap election could shift the EU’s political balance**

Spanish Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez skipped the Brussels summit to campaign ahead of Sunday’s snap general election. Following Thursday night's final televised debate between the candidates, polls suggest Sánchez’s Socialist-led coalition could be unseated. A victory for a right-wing coalition could bring the far right into government for the first time in decades and significantly shift the EU’s political balance ahead of December's decisive budget talks.

### UK publishes details of new EU reset deal

In London, the government has officially published the legal texts of Prime Minister Andy Burnham’s new package of agreements with Brussels—covering shared food and agricultural standards, linked emissions trading schemes, and a youth mobility programme. Hailed by Burnham as a "win-win," the pact comes as polling shows a majority of Britons favour rejoining the bloc, sparking debate over whether the deal is a pragmatic reset or a first step towards full EU membership.
