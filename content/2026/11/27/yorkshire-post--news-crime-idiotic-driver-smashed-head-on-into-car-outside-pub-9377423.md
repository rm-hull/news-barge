# ‘Idiotic’ teen driver smashed head-on into car outside pub leaving victims with horror injuries

## A teen who drove a vehicle “like an F1 driver” ploughed into an oncoming vehicle after losing control of his car at more than twice the speed limit.

The crash left another driver and passengers with catastrophic injuries, Leeds Crown Court was told.

The court heard Lewis Dyer’s conduct was “idiotic”, “extremely dangerous” and likened to that of a Formula 1 driver when he veered into the path of a Ford Mondeo on a suburban road in Leeds.

The driver and passengers in the car, in some cases, were left needing months of hospital treatment.

Dyer, who is now 20 but was 17 at the time, admitted three counts of causing serious injury through dangerous driving and was jailed.

![Dyer (inset) smashed into the car head-on on Cross Green Lane outside the Leodis pub.](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/jpim-static/image/2026/11/26/13/05/Dyer.jpeg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


He was behind the wheel of a Ford Fiesta and carrying two passengers shortly before 5pm on May 31, 2024, along Cross Green Lane in Halton towards Cross Gates.

He was described as driving well in excess of the 20mph zone. He lost control on a left-hand bend outside the Leodis pub and smashed into the Ford Mondeo coming the other way.

It was later found that the Fiesta was travelling at almost 50mph moments before the impact. The Mondeo driver had no time to avoid the collision, prosecutor Emily Jenkins told the court.

Dyer had to be be cut free from the Fiesta, along with a passenger.

Three people from the Mondeo, now aged 58, 64 and 80 suffered serious injuries. One had a broken collarbone, fractured spine and pelvis and punctured lung.

Another suffered “shattered” leg bones which needed two rounds of surgery to pin them back together. She requires round-the-clock care.

He later told police he has no memory of the crash.

Mitigating, Sarah Barlow said Dyer was now in employment and pointed to his lack of previous convictions as well as no subsequent offending.

The court heard that Dyer had been aggressive towards the emergency services after he was cut from the Fiesta, but Ms Barlow said it was consistent with the traumatic head injury he had suffered and that the aggression was “out of character” for him.

Judge Simon Phillips KC told Dyer that had he been an adult at the time, he could have expected a starting point of up to five years in prison.
