Belgian authorities have ordered Google to halt construction work on a large datacentre in the south of the country because the US tech firm’s requests to modify the original terms of the planning permission did not have approval.

“Inspectors visited the site and observed that the building was almost finished, with the structural work completed, even though the permit had been refused,” Raphaël Stokis, a Wallonia regional official, told the public broadcaster RTBF.

Stokis said the 300 workers had been given a verbal order to stop work that had been confirmed in writing to Google. The company said in a statement it was “committed to maintaining an open and collaborative dialogue” with the regional authorities.

Google’s 2025 application to change its plans, notably including a switch from air to water cooling, was initially approved by local authorities but later revoked after protests. The company has appealed and a ruling is due early next year.

The vast building, which occupies about 3 hectares (7.4 acres) of a 50-hectare business park on the site of a former coalmine at Aiseau-Presles, about 15km (9 miles) west of Charleroi, is one of three originally planned by Google on the site.

Nicolas Yernaux, a regional government spokesperson, told Agence France-Presse on Friday that a report was being submitted to public prosecutors alleging “a major offence: the illegal construction of a building covering 3 hectares”.

The environmental group Greenpeace earlier this week published a report including drone footage that showed Google was carrying on construction work at the site despite lacking the correct permits.

“The tech giant is continuing work, even though in August the latest version of its planning permission to build this datacentre was revoked,” Greenpeace said, adding that it had filed an emergency appeal with local planning authorities.

“The climate crisis will only intensify in our region,” the environmental group said. “Against this backdrop, the construction of energy-guzzling behemoths cannot be allowed to go unnoticed.”

Google’s 2024 permit was to construct three smaller air-cooled datacentres, but in 2025 applied instead to merge two of them into one much larger facility and switch to liquid cooling by drawing 600m3 of water an hour from the nearby Sambre River.

The regional administration initially approved the amendments in April, but residents and protest groups appealed and in August the Wallonia infrastructure minister, François Desquesnes, revoked the modified permit over drought concerns.

Google appealed against that decision to Belgium’s council of state and a ruling in the case is expected on 7 January. The legal row is the latest confronting the company in Europe as large tech companies push to build datacentres to support the AI boom.

In October, Finland opened a criminal investigation into possible breaches ⁠of environmental ⁠laws ​at a planned Google datacentre in the rural north of the country. Work has been halted until environmental impact assessments are completed.
