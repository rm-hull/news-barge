**Euronews Persian**

## Iran's Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi called the accusations baseless and the incident "suspicious" and offered to cooperate on a joint investigation. Kuwait called it "a heinous Iranian aggression". Saudi Arabia, Bahrain, Qatar and the UAE condemned the attack.

Iran denied responsibility on Thursday for drone strikes on Kuwait that killed two Italian nationals, with Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi calling the accusations baseless and proposing a joint investigation with Rome and Kuwait City.

Araghchi spoke by telephone with his Kuwaiti and Italian counterparts, Sheikh Jarrah Jaber Al-Ahmad Al-Sabah and Antonio Tajani, and later wrote on X that the incident was "suspicious and looks like a preecooked recipe for war."

"We stand ready to cooperate with Kuwait and Italy to determine the cause of the incident," he added.

Tajani had earlier told the Italian Senate that several Iranian drones struck Kuwaiti military facilities at around 2 pm local time (12 pm CET), including an air defence radar centre where two Italian specialists employed by arms company Rheinmetall were working.

Kuwait's Al-Sabah condemned the attack as "a heinous Iranian aggression" and offered condolences to Italy.

Senior spokesperson for Iran's armed forces Abolfazl Shekarchi flatly denied any Iranian military operation in the region and accused the US and Israel of staging a false flag attack to destabilise the area.

IRGC-affiliated Fars news agency went further, citing an unnamed security source who alleged that Ukraine had deployed a network of thousands of drones across the region, including in the Kurdistan Region of Iraq. It provided no evidence.

The Iran embassy in Rome separately issued a statement categorically rejecting any Iranian role and warning against attempts to widen the war.

## Gulf states condemn Tehran

Saudi Arabia called for an immediate halt to Iranian attacks and said it held Tehran responsible for the consequences of its actions. Bahrain asked the UN Security Council to hold Iran accountable and demanded compensation for the damage.

Qatar warned that repeated strikes on Kuwait risked undermining diplomatic efforts to reduce regional tensions. The UAE, Syria and Jordan also condemned the attack.

European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen called for increased pressure on Tehran and said Gulf security was directly linked to Europe's security.

Iran has repeatedly struck Arab Gulf states with drones and missiles since the start of the war, saying it has targeted military bases and US-linked facilities, with Kuwait and Bahrain among the most frequently targeted countries.

Tehran has urged its neighbours not to allow their territory to be used for attacks against Iran.

Iran's army confirmed a separate drone attack on Kuwait's Ali Al Salem base earlier in November.

Tajani had informed the Italian parliament on 8 October of a decision to relocate most of its troops from Kuwait, with only around 10 military personnel remaining in the country at the time of the attack.
