Several services run by the Department for Work and Pensions (DWP) have been taken offline following a cyber incident.

The department, which is responsible for administering most UK benefits, said it is working with GCHQ’s National Cyber Security Centre (NCSC) and government experts to investigate the incident.

Affected services are understood to include those used to manage Universal Credit, Personal Independence Payment (PIP), and pension applications.

The DWP said benefit and pension payments should go out as normal despite the disruption.

The UK’s 8.4 million Universal Credit claimants are also currently unable to access their online journal as a result of the issue, with the login portal failing to load.

This digital service is essential for claimants with work requirements, who must meet deadlines to report changes in circumstances and complete ‘to-do’ tasks.

![DWP has taken some of its services offline as a precaution](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/06/03/15/2214408292..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Officials have not confirmed what support is in place for those unable to meet deadlines because of the issue, although leniency is likely in these cases. They can also call the Universal Credit helpline on 0800 328 5644 for further guidance, although longer wait times can be expected.

The ‘tell us once’ service that allows people to report a death to most government organisations via a single form is also reportedly offline.

A statement on the DWP website said: “We have taken some DWP online services offline as a precaution after we identified a cyber incident affecting our systems.

“We are working closely with the National Cyber Security Centre and Government Cyber Coordination Centre to investigate and to bring services back online as quickly as possible and to minimise any disruption.

“Regular benefit and pension payments are not affected and will be paid as normal.

“If an online service you need is unavailable, please try again later.

“As always, we will never message you to ask for bank details or ask you to click on links.”

It is understood there was no evidence on Friday morning that any personal data from DWP claimants had been stolen, although investigations are ongoing.

The NCSC said it had nothing to add to the DWP’s statement.
