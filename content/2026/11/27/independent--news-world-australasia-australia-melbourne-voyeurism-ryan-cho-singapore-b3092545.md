A 29-year-old trainee doctor in Melbourne has admitted to secretly filming more than 800 colleagues in toilets at three Australian hospitals.

Ryan Cho was taken into custody on Thursday after a three-day plea hearing in a Melbourne court.

Cho pleaded guilty to secretly recording patients and hospital staff at three Melbourne hospitals and in a shared accommodation.

He concealed a mobile phone inside a mesh bag to film people using toilets and bathrooms, and reportedly obstructed other facilities and tampered with locks to direct people towards the locations where he had hidden cameras.

Cho will return to court on 12 February next year for sentencing.

Cho’s lawyer Belinda Franjic said that her client had expressed “genuine remorse” and that he has taken steps towards rehabilitation.

“He got caught up in his own voyeuristic fantasies and it seems he simply didn’t turn his mind to the impact on the victims,” she said. She added that while there were cases of people surrepticiously recording others, there was “nothing of this nature and nothing of this scale”.

Although Cho initially faced more than 900 charges following his arrest last year, prosecutors and his lawyers agreed to combine them into 13 charges after he pleaded guilty. These included charges covering the secret filming of 541 people at Austin Hospital, 277 at the Royal Melbourne Hospital and 50 at the Peter MacCallum Cancer Centre.

Producing an intimate image carries a maximum sentence of three years in prison per charge.

Judge Gabriele Cannon said sentencing Cho would be difficult because the maximum penalty for producing an intimate image was three years in prison. She said he was entitled to a sentence reduction for his early guilty plea and that other mitigating circumstances would also be considered.

Cho admitted to four counts of producing intimate images, two counts of stalking, offences involving the production and possession of child abuse material, four counts of installing surveillance devices and failing to comply with police directions.

The child abuse material charges related to footage he had secretly recorded and kept of a hospital employee’s daughters.

Prosecutor Mark Gibson KC defended the decision to combine the charges, saying Cho would otherwise have faced hundreds of separate charges in the County Court.

Court proceedings also revealed that Cho had previously stalked a housemate in 2021, recording more than 3,000 videos of the woman and her friends.

His lawyer, Ms Franjic said the crimes “beggars belief”. She told the court that Cho had been diagnosed with schizoid personality disorder and voyeuristic disorder.

However, Mr Gibson said Cho’s prospects of rehabilitation were “guarded”, with a psychiatrist assessing him as having an above-average risk of reoffending.

“It is extraordinary to think that having spent years studying and being on the precipice of a successful career and future … that he would risk it all in this way,” Mr Gibson said.

One of the victims, nurse Courtney Whelan, said she opposed combining the charges because each recording represented a separate decision by Cho. She said the crimes had caused her stress, depression and anxiety.

“I found it really hard to keep seeing him in the media and him getting the right to cover his face … when we didn’t get that choice as victims,” Ms Whelan said.

Judge Cannon also questioned whether Cho could genuinely have been unaware of the harm he was causing.

“He knew through his training the importance of privacy and dignity and treating people in a respectful way,” she said.

“He must have known the victims finding out about this would be very harmful to them.”

Cho, a Singaporean citizen, could face deportation after completing his sentence. He had remained on bail for more than a year following his arrest and was taken into custody at the end of Thursday’s hearing.
