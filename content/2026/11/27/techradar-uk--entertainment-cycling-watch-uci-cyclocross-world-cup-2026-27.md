Like cycling crossed with Mario Kart, Cyclocross is gloriously unpredictable, as in-thrall to the wildly disparate off-road circuit characteristics, weather and ground conditions as the immense skill of the riders themselves. Mathieu van der Poel and Lucinda Brand enter the 2026/27 UCI Cyclocross World Cup as reigning champions, but the title tends to change hands most years.

Van der Poel, who also won out eight years ago, had to respond to Thibau Nys' fast start a year ago, but managed to string four victories together not just once but twice, in order to pip the Belgian and his compatriot Niels Vandeputte to glory. Big things are expected from youngster Tibor del Grosso for the new campaign.

Brand, meanwhile, became the first rider since herself three years previously to successfully defend her crown, which she did by comfortably seeing off the challenges of Ceylin del Carmen Alvarado and Amandine Fouquenet. There are hopes that Italian national champion Sara Casasola will give the veteran Dutch rider more of a run for her money this time.

With races taking place on mud, sand, grass and gravel, in torrential rain, snowstorms and – with a bit of luck – blazing sunshine, it's not the fastest, the most powerful or the most technical riders who win out over the 12 rounds of the Cyclocross World Cup, but the most adaptable and, indeed the most daring.
