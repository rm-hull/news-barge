## US tariffs brought the EU and Indonesia closer, Indonesia's Coordinating Minister for Economic Affairs Airlangga Hartarto said in an exclusive interview with Euronews. After a decade of talks, the two sides signed a deal last week, and the minister expects it to lift trade by 50% in three years.

US tariffs pushed the EU and Indonesia closer as they negotiated a trade deal, Indonesia's economy minister told Euronews in an exclusive interview.

Airlangga Hartarto expects the deal, signed in Brussels on Saturday after a decade of talks, to lift trade between the two sides by 50% within three years.

Asked whether US President Donald Trump's sweeping tariffs had helped bring the talks to a close, Hartarto said: "I think there is an impact of the parallel negotiation with the US". Jakarta has been negotiating tariffs with Washington alongside its talks with Brussels.

The EU and Indonesia struck a political agreement in July 2025, two days before Trump announced a deal setting a 19% US tariff on Indonesian goods. On Wednesday, Washington proposed new tariffs on some goods from 14 trading partners, including the EU and Indonesia.

EU-Indonesia goods trade was worth €28.9 billion last year, according to the European Council. The minister said the 50% goal had been discussed with EU partners and business groups from both sides.

He called the two economies "not competing but compatible", since they make different products and can meet each other's needs.

## Investor confidence

Hartarto also played down a downgrade warning from MSCI, whose stock indices are tracked by funds worldwide.

In October, the index provider proposed downgrading Indonesia from "emerging" to "standalone" status, which would remove Indonesian shares from its emerging markets indices. It plans to decide by the end of June 2027.

Jakarta's benchmark stock index, the IDX Composite, has fallen roughly 38% this year.

The minister likened the warning to a broken window. "So basically this is an issue of a window that is broken by somebody, right?" he said.

Asked whether he meant that everything looks broken to investors through that window, he agreed and said Indonesia's economy was in better shape than the MSCI warning suggested.

He described Indonesia's growth as "one of the highest" in the G20. The economy grew 5.45% in the first nine months of 2026 from a year earlier, according to Statistics Indonesia, the national statistics agency.

Jakarta is reforming its capital market to improve transparency, he added.

Hartarto also said next week's New Asia Forum, an investment conference in Bali, could yield deals worth up to $30 billion (€27.4bn). "I think the potential of $30 billion is there," he told Euronews.

That figure is the forum's target for the whole region. Danantara, the Indonesian sovereign wealth fund backing the forum, expects that about 40% to 50% will go to Indonesia.

Indonesia plans more of these events to promote investment, including one with the EU that the minister has discussed with EU trade commissioner Maroš Šefčovič.

## Chip ambitions

Mathias Cormann, Secretary-General of the Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD), launched two reports in Jakarta this week as Indonesia aims to join the Paris-based group of mostly wealthy economies by 2028.

According to the minister, one report looks at how Indonesia can benefit from the digital economy by developing semiconductors. The other, the OECD's latest economic survey, found Indonesia would need growth averaging 5.8% a year for 20 years to become a high-income country by 2045.

On current productivity trends, though, growth would slow to about 3.5%, the OECD warned. The minister said the 5.8% figure was in line with the government's plans, in which chips play a big part.

"Indonesia has already made a contract with Arm," he told Euronews as an example of the moves the country is making to become a bigger player in the global AI economy.

Danantara signed a framework agreement with the British chip designer in London back in February.

Through the agreement, Indonesia is training up to 15,000 engineers in AI and semiconductors and will acquire six pieces of chip-design intellectual property, the minister said.

## Year-end deadlines

Indonesian exporters face two deadlines at the turn of the year.

From 30 December, the EU's twice-delayed deforestation regulation (EUDR) will require large and medium-sized companies to prove that palm oil, coffee, cocoa, rubber and other goods they sell in the EU were not produced on land deforested after 2020.

According to the minister, the rules were discussed separately from the trade deal.

Indonesia is ready to comply, he said, but has asked for schemes such as its own national Indonesian Sustainable Palm Oil (ISPO) certification to count towards compliance. That request is still being discussed, he added.

On 1 January, Indonesia also leaves the Generalised Scheme of Preferences (GSP), under which the EU cuts tariffs for developing countries. Exports that currently benefit from the scheme will then face standard EU duties until the trade deal applies.

Hartarto is confident the agreement will be in place in time. He said it was being brought to the European Parliament "so that on the first of January next year it will be implemented".

Indonesia's embassy in Brussels has been in contact with several MEPs, who have given strong support, according to the minister.

The Parliament's legislative tracker lists 14 December, the first day of its last plenary session of the year, as a provisional date for its vote. The file is still at the preparatory stage, and the Parliament said on signing day that the exact timeline had not yet been agreed. MEPs cannot amend the deal, only approve or reject it.

Once both sides have completed their internal procedures, the deal will apply provisionally, likely in early 2027, according to the European Commission.
