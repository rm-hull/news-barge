## Ukraine's military intelligence said Kim Jong-un personally ordered the suspension after Russia's deputy foreign minister said it would help ease tensions between Seoul and Pyongyang.

North Korea has suspended military assistance to Russia following a diplomatic dispute over Moscow's statements on Seoul, Ukraine's military intelligence directorate said on Wednesday, in claims neither Moscow nor Pyongyang has confirmed or denied.

The Ukrainian Defence Ministry's Main Intelligence Directorate (HUR) said North Korean leader Kim Jong-un personally made the decision after Pyongyang took offence at remarks by Russia's Deputy Foreign Minister Andrei Rudenko, who said in October that the Kremlin would help de-escalate tensions between Seoul and Pyongyang.

North Korea's Foreign Minister Choe Son Hui characterised the statements as "not consistent with the spirit of the allied agreements between the two states" and announced a temporary suspension of military support, the HUR said.

The suspension includes a ban on Russian military transport aircraft using North Korean airspace, a halt to deliveries of KN-23 ballistic missiles and a stop to further artillery munitions shipments, according to Ukrainian intelligence.

North Korea is also holding back from fulfilling a Russian request to expand its military contingent on Russian territory from 8,500 to 50,000 troops, the HUR added.

Rudenko made no public statement in those terms, but told Russian state media on 20 October that Moscow was ready for a "normalisation of relations" with Seoul.

"We do not link our relations with any country to what is happening with Ukraine. Therefore, if our South Korean partners take any practical steps, then of course they will be welcomed on our part," he said.

On 16 November, a senior aide to Kim, Politburo Presidium member Cho Yong Won, met Russian President Vladimir Putin in Moscow, on the eve of the APEC summit.

Moscow and Pyongyang had made no official statements in response to the HUR report at the time of publication.

Putin and Kim signed a mutual defence pact in June 2024, after which North Korea became one of Russia's closest military partners in its ongoing all-out war in Ukraine. Russia deployed North Korean troops in an offensive later that year against Ukrainian forces in the Russian border region of Kursk.

Russia remains heavily dependent on North Korean weapons and materiel. In exchange, Pyongyang is understood to be seeking military technology, raw materials and financial assistance, as well as operational experience from Russia's war in Ukraine.

North Korea's army numbers more than 1 million personnel, making it one of the largest in the world.
