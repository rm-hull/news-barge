## The RN presidential candidate accuses the European Commission of seeking to "rush" adoption of the next EU budget before the 2027 French election.

Talks over the EU budget have sparked a new clash between Emmanuel Macron and Marine Le Pen.

The National Rally candidate accuses the European Commission of trying to hurry through an agreement on the next multiannual EU budget before the French presidential election. She says this strategy is intended to prevent "the democratic expression of the French people".

Emmanuel Macron did not appreciate the remark, recalling that he "was elected until 13 May 2027" and therefore "remains president until 13 May 2027".

The head of state retorted during a press conference in Brussels on Monday: "It is a strange kind of arrogance to think that she is already in charge of France's destiny. Because, until proven otherwise, she has not been elected".

The 27 member states are struggling to reach agreement on the Union's next budget, after most EU countries rejected the Commission's initial proposal, set at €2,000 billion.

The President of the European Council, António Costa, has tried to ease divisions by cutting the budget by €204 billion, but the latest negotiations in Brussels have produced no decisive breakthrough.

The German chancellor, Friedrich Merz, is insisting in particular that aid to Ukraine should be guaranteed by all 27 EU countries, even as funding for Kyiv faces growing opposition across Europe.

European Union leaders will meet again on 17 December, a last chance to reach an agreement before the end of the year.
