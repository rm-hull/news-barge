Damit seien nun 164 neue Wagen der neuen Baureihe auf der Linie U5 unterwegs. Wenige ältere Züge blieben als Reserve.

A train from the new J class. (File photo)

Berlin Transport Authority (BVG) has fully converted the U5 line to new trains. The BVG announced that the last J-series carriages for this line, for the time being, were delivered and put into service a few days ago.

Next, the roll-out on the U9 is set to begin. Since the start of October, two trains comprising a total of twelve carriages from the new series have already been in service here. By the end of the year, there are expected to be up to 30 new carriages. Over the course of the coming year, they are also due to be deployed on the U6. “The fleet of new carriages is growing, and it is growing faster than announced,” said BVG Chief Executive Henrik Falk. On the U5 and U9 lines, there are already more new carriages in service than had been planned by the end of the year.

In total, the BVG has ordered 344 J-series carriages from the manufacturer Stadler. A further 166 have already been approved by the supervisory board and are due to be delivered once the current order has been completed. Together with the trains from the sister JK series for lines U1 to U4, they are intended to modernise the BVG’s ageing underground fleet and stabilise operations. The new vehicles are urgently needed. Reliability has declined sharply in recent years due to the high maintenance costs associated with the old trains.
