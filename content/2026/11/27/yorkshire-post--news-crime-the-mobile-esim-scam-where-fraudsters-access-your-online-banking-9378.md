# The mobile eSIM scam where fraudsters access your online banking as fraudster is jailed

## Police have issued a warning over a mobile eSIM scam which sees criminals take over victims’ phones after a fraudster was jailed in Yorkshire.

Imad Islam, from East Ham, London, targeted 28 victims across the UK and gained financial benefit after scamming people into accessing their devices remotely.

Islam accessed compromised personalised information from his victims and ordered eSIMs in their names, which sent a one-time password to their phones.

From there he was able to take over their devices, including their banking and emails, which he used to purchase items from high street stores using their online bank accounts.

Islam was sentenced at Leeds Magistrates Court on Nov 26, where he was given a 12-month community order, 120 hours of unpaid work and ordered to pay a £114 victim surcharge.

Detective Inspector Rachel Greenfield, of the Yorkshire and Humber Regional Organised Crime Unit’s Proactive Economic Crime Team, said: “Islam was clearly an offender with some technical ability, but we were able to trace him and build a clear evidential picture of his involvement in this fraud through a detailed and comprehensive investigation.

“It should serve as a reminder to people that criminals are becoming increasingly sophisticated in the way that they carry out frauds and that people should take all necessary precautions around online crime prevention, including not sharing one-time passcodes and creating a strong email password.”

Police have now urged people to be mindful of their mobile phone security and the potential fraud threats they could face.

West Yorkshire Police said phone users should lock down their mobile account by asking their network for extra account PINs, passwords or eSIM change protections.

They also suggest using authenticator apps or passkeys instead of text message security codes where possible, and to use strong, unique passwords on their email.

Police issued a final warning to watching out for suspicious activity, such as sudden loss of signal, unexpected messages or sudden password resets - and to contact their mobile network immediately if they suspect fraud.
