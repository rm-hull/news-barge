Highlights: Ireland 38-14 Australia

**Nations ChampionshipIreland (17) 38Tries:** Henshaw, Timoney, Baloucoune, McNabney 2**Cons:** Prendergast 5**Pen:** Prendergast**Australia (7) 14Tries:** Williams, Thomas**Cons:** Gordon 2**Ireland secured a fifth-place finish in the inaugural Nations Championship thanks to a 38-14 victory over Australia in London.**

While last week's 20-17 win over South Africa was the undoubted highlight of their campaign, victory on Friday night ensured Ireland won all four games they played in November.

After Italy narrowly beat Fiji in the first game of the day, Ireland started slowly in a sparsely populated Allianz Stadium with the Wallabies taking a deserved lead through Jeremy Williams' try.

Andy Farrell's side took control of the contest through 24 unanswered points either side of half-time with Robbie Henshaw, Nick Timoney and Robert Baloucoune crossing for scores, while Sam Prendergast converted all three and added a penalty.

Kalani Thomas briefly stalled Ireland's momentum, but replacement back row James McNabney marked his second cap with a pair of tries in the final seven minutes to give the number two side in the world a comfortable, if not always convincing, victory.

After what was their final Test match of 2026, Ireland finish the year with a record of 10 wins and two losses from 12 internationals.

After making only two forced changes from the side who beat the Springboks six days ago - the banned Dan Sheehan and ill Mack Hansen the ones to miss out - head coach Farrell had called on his squad to back up their performance against the reigning world champions.

The early stages, however, had none of the tempo or accuracy from last week in the Aviva Stadium. Sloppy knock-ons, coupled with a pair of scrum penalties, saw Ireland struggle to gain a foothold in the contest.

For all Australia's territory and possession, the Wallabies took until the 23rd minute to press home their advantage on the scoreboard.

Previously undone by a couple of kicking errors, Les Kiss' side looked most dangerous when keeping the ball tight and it was lock Williams who burrowed over for the game's opening score.

Australia's deserved lead, however, lasted just three minutes. A midfield penalty allowed Prendergast to kick for the corner and give Ireland their first visit to the opposition 22.

After a sharp attack down the short side was thwarted before the line, Ireland came back inside and Stuart McCloskey spun out of a tackle well to put his centre partner Henshaw over for the try.

Three minutes before the break, again aided by an Australia penalty, Ireland doubled their lead.

Tadhg Beirne did well to gather a line-out with one hand and, with Ireland's centres joining the maul, number eight Timoney broke off the base for the simple score.

Prendergast converted both scores and added a penalty with the last kick of the half to put Ireland in a commanding 10-point lead at the break despite their shaky start.

Ireland finished the autumn with four wins from four games played

If Ireland started slowly in the first half, they flew from the traps after the restart.

Jacob Stockdale had already almost executed a fine chip-and-chase reminiscent of his best days in the green jersey before his Ulster team-mate Baloucoune scored Ireland's third in the 43rd minute.

Baloucoune, 29, gathered Prendergast's measured cross-field kick over the head of James Ramm to score his fifth try of the autumn.

Australia threw everything at getting back into the game but a penalty for coming in at the side, which halted a 19-phase attack under the posts, summed up a frustrating evening with ball in hand for next year's World Cup hosts.

Scrum-half Thomas did score his first international try to reduce Ireland's lead to 24-14 with 25 minutes to go, but a comeback never felt likely from an Australia side who used Kurtley Beale from the bench for the utility back's first cap in five years.

While the game lost further shape in the final quarter with the arrival of a glut of replacements, Ulster's McNabney caught the eye in a short cameo.

Having had his debut against Fiji cut short by a head injury two weeks ago, the back row was the beneficiary of a fearsome Irish scrum shortly before his first try and then finished things off with another close-range score in the final minute.
