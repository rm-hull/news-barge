# Restoring Skipton to Colne line will benefit both Yorkshire and Lancashire: Jonathan Hinder

## On Lancashire Day, the need to revive the rail link connecting former mill towns in my constituency directly with Yorkshire has never been greater and would benefit communities across the North.

Constituents in and around West Craven are fond of reminding me that their heritage lies firmly in Yorkshire. Some still deeply resent the 1974 boundary changes that landed them in Lancashire.

Tragically, one of the area’s most important ties with “God’s own country”, the Colne-Skipton train line, was axed in 1970. A scandalous decision, made far away in London, it must be reversed as soon as possible.

It’s not just about reconnecting Colne and Skipton. It’s about reconnecting so many other places too - Burnley and Keighley, Brierfield and Bingley, for example.

![There are renewed efforts to reopen old Skipton to Colne rail line. PIC: Gerard Binks](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOjBiYThhODY2LWRhOTctNDJlOS1hNTcyLTI0NzlkYWZmNTU4ZjpkOTQ5MTAzYy0zZTExLTQ3MWYtODVlZi0yM2Y5MjI0YzMwMTY=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


The reopening of the 12-mile missing link, which even Dr Beeching recommended retaining, would benefit communities on both sides of the border.

As the White Rose Rail Plan 2025 highlighted, people do not “live their lives within regional government boundaries.” Cross-border connectivity must be a higher priority when it comes to planning public transport.

The focus should not be just on improving the economies of big cities, such as Manchester and Leeds.

It’s a simple question of fairness. Struggling families in my constituency deserve good access to jobs, education and leisure opportunities, just as much as their metropolitan counterparts.

They should be able to work or study in booming places like Leeds, without having to up sticks. Having more of them staying close to home would also be a shot in the arm for Pendle’s local shops, pubs and restaurants.

Many of my constituents also rely on Airedale Hospital, just a few miles over the border in Steeton, so this link would also improve access to healthcare.

In case old rivalries make you think otherwise, the reopened line would not be a one-way street.

Consider how many Yorkshire people currently have no other option but the car, when travelling west to Lancashire - to work, to study, to shop, to visit relatives, to watch Burnley FC play at Turf Moor, to join the audience at theatres, such as Colne Muni, and to climb Pendle Hill.

In October, I was pleased to join my neighbouring colleagues, Robbie Moore and Sir Julian Smith, supporting the launch of a petition calling on ministers to back the Colne-Skipton Line’s reopening.

Founded in 2001, SELRAP (Skipton East Lancashire Rail Action Partnership), the petition’s organisers, have recently attracted a wave of younger supporters.

They have also engaged with students at Craven College, a quarter of whom commute by road to Skipton from East Lancashire. It’s a campaign focused on the future, not the past.

It’s also a campaign backed by local councillors from across the political spectrum, from the Greens to Reform. Consensus like that is a rare thing these days.

Business is enthusiastically supporting the reopening too, with the East Lancashire Chamber of Commerce and the Skipton Group, owners of the Skipton Building Society, both welcoming the economic benefits it would generate.

The tourism sector would be a particular beneficiary. SELRAP’s work is endorsed by leading attractions, including Skipton Castle and the Keighley & Worth Valley Railway.

The elephant in the room for many is can we afford the cost of reopening the line in these financially challenging times?

My answer is we simply can’t afford not to get the line back in action.

It would have a transformational, positive effect. Every pound spent on the reopening would generate £1.50 for the wider economy.

The price tag would also be miniscule, compared to the money being poured into Northern Powerhouse Rail, the Transpennine Rail Upgrade, not to mention HS2 – all of which do little to help the long-overlooked towns of the central Pennines.

It’s a good job that the builders of the Ribblehead Viaduct, the Leeds-Liverpool Canal and other key assets across the North, did not listen to sceptics who suggested their plans would be too costly, too disruptive or little used.

All recent evidence indicates the reopened line would be well used and cover its operating costs. Half of Lancashire’s population lives within two miles of the existing Blackpool-Colne Line.

You can sign a petition on Change.org. Regardless of which side of the Pennines is home for you, please don’t miss this opportunity to make your voice heard in Westminster.

Jonathan Hinder is Labour MP for Pendle & Clitheroe
