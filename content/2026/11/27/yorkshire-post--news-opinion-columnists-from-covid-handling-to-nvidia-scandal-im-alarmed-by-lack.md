# From Covid handling to Nvidia scandal, I'm alarmed by lack of consequences for failure: Daxa Patel

## Accountability and integrity must be non-negotiable aspects of all leadership, whether one leads a team of four, a public listed company or a nation. The latest report from the UK Covid-19 Inquiry, chaired by Baroness Heather Hallett, laid bare the lack of preparedness in the handling of the pandemic, and with it came massive criticism of the then government.

Fundamental errors cost lives and resulted in avoidable deaths. But who has been held to account? Nobody.

The key findings included the fact that our borders were left open for longer, and that efforts to contain coronavirus "were doomed to fail". If that is not a damning verdict, I am not sure what is. Yet nobody stands with their head in their hands in shame. In this country we do not have accountability. That is a sweeping statement, but those in public office essentially receive a tiny rap on the knuckles, and that is it. Telling the bereaved that lessons have been learnt is like saying sorry after the event. It is neither meant, nor will it be a turning point. That is a sorry state of affairs, and an apathy I would so wish to be proved wrong on.

The Inquiry said the Test and Trace system was launched two months later than it should have been, and that in the early stages the four UK nations were "effectively blind". Another damning verdict. School closures and lockdown could have been handled better and sooner had we copied the best practice modelled by Hong Kong, South Korea and Singapore.

Instead, our then Prime Minister openly spoke of how he continued to shake hands with people to reassure the public, and that life was business as usual. He failed abysmally to lead us safely through the pandemic. Yes, it was an unprecedented pandemic, but unprecedented things happen in life. The key is how those in charge help those who rely on their decision making to feel safe and protected. The then government failed badly, and not a single political leader has been held to account in a meaningful way, so there is little solace for those left behind who lost loved ones through the mismanagement of the pandemic.

The Inquiry commented on how those self-isolating were forgotten, and that flights and border controls had no clear strategy for implementation, which would have made the difference between saving lives and more avoidable deaths. The timing of border controls left the nation in an "extremely vulnerable position", yet another damning verdict, and still nobody's head is held in shame. When 93 per cent of countries had put measures in place by April 2020 to suspend flights, why did our government only require travellers from high-risk areas to self-isolate for fourteen days? We did not have border controls in place until June 2020.

Those in government may hold positions of power, but being a minister in defence, health or even in charge of the country does not mean one is qualified or equipped with the sound judgement needed to emulate best practice from other countries. If a doctor had acted negligently, resulting in a patient's avoidable death, they would face a civil claim for compensation, and their professional body would suspend them from practice. If a teacher's actions resulted in the avoidable death of a pupil in their care, the matter would be subject to investigation, and the teacher could face suspension by their employer and regulatory action by the Teaching Regulation Agency.

Professionals in positions of trust are held to account for their actions, yet those in government often appear to operate above such scrutiny, with scant regard for the lives entrusted to their care.

A system that appoints ministers to powerful roles based on loyalty rather than expertise is a mockery of the task they are entrusted to perform. It was not just the Prime Minister who failed the standards of his high office, but also his Home Secretary, Health Secretary and Education Secretary. Yet these people continue to hold status-filled positions with no consequences for the decisions they took, or failed to take, when they were in charge. That should set alarm bells ringing and warrants serious national introspection into how our politicians lead us through crisis.

But this is not simply a collective failure of government in the UK. The reappointment of Nvidia’s CEO raises equally serious questions about integrity and accountability. By his own admission to staff, he had been unfaithful to his wife of over three decades, causing hurt to her and their two adult children. The board also says it received input from senior US government officials regarding the reinstatement. Another telling example of how easily integrity can be compromised and accountability set aside.

If those in power are allowed to act with impunity and tread on the values that make us caring members of a civilised world, what signals are we giving to those around us? Essentially, we are saying it is acceptable to have the Nolan Principles, but not to apply them to ministers who partied while the late Queen and much of the nation were in mourning. We are saying that if a CEO of a well-known company admits he let his family down, it is perfectly fine to hand him his job back, and that this is a standard of behaviour we are comfortable with.

I want to offer some hope, and it is this. Ordinary people are wise enough to see through the tinted glass of double standards.

Daxa Patel is an author, leadership coach and solicitor.
