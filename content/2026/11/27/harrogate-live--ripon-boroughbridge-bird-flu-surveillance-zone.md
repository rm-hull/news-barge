Bird flu was confirmed near Thirsk on 18 November. Ripon, Boroughbridge and Dishforth are inside the control zones, so anyone keeping birds there faces movement rules.

Most of Ripon and all of central Boroughbridge are inside a bird flu surveillance zone, after the disease was confirmed in commercial poultry near Thirsk. Dishforth and Rainton are closer still, inside the inner protection zone.

Highly pathogenic avian influenza H5N1 was confirmed at the premises on 18 November. All the poultry there will be culled, according to the Defra case notice.

Defra declared two zones around the farm from 6.45pm that evening. They stay in force until Defra withdraws or changes the declaration:

* a **3km protection zone**
* a **10km surveillance zone** beyond it

The rules apply to anyone keeping poultry or other captive birds inside the zones, from a commercial flock to a few hens in a back garden.

## Which places are in the zones

Defra’s map of the zones names the villages inside each circle (declaration and map, PDF). We measured the distances from the centre of the zones that the declaration gives.

| Zone | Places on Defra’s map |
| --- | --- |
| Protection zone, 3km | Dishforth, Rainton, Topcliffe, Asenby |
| Surveillance zone, 10km | Ripon (most of the city, including the centre), Sharow, Copt Hewick, Bridge Hewick, Marton-le-Moor, Littlethorpe, Skelton-on-Ure, Langthorpe, Kirby Hill, Boroughbridge, Melmerby, Baldersby, Cundall, Norton-le-Clay, Hutton Conyers, Thirsk |

The outer edge of the surveillance zone runs through the west of Ripon, about 10km from the centre. Ripon’s city centre is about 9km away, and so is the centre of Boroughbridge. Bishop Monkton and Roecliffe are just outside.

If you live near the edge, check your address on Defra’s interactive disease control zone map rather than relying on a village name.

A second case was confirmed in commercial poultry at Northallerton on 22 November, with its own zones (Defra case notice). Northallerton is about 25 miles north of Harrogate, and that case’s zones stop well short of Ripon. North Yorkshire Council said both cases were at commercial turkey farms.

## What the rules say

In the surveillance zone, the declaration says keepers of poultry or other captive birds must:

* keep a record of everyone who visits the part of the premises where birds are kept
* record all poultry coming in or going out
* report any rise in deaths or illness, or a big drop in egg laying, straight away
* not move poultry, other captive birds or mammals on or off the premises without a licence
* not spread or remove used poultry litter or manure without a licence

The declaration also bans:

* taking birds to any fair, market, show or other gathering in the zone
* releasing game birds such as pheasants and partridges

In the protection zone, the same rules apply, and birds must also be housed, or kept isolated where a vet has agreed that housing is impractical. Moving poultry, eggs or poultry meat into, within or out of the protection zone needs a licence, with exceptions for table eggs going to shops and wholesalers. Movement licences are applied for through GOV.UK.

Drivers carrying poultry or eggs can pass through the zones on a main road or by rail, so long as they do not stop.

## What the council and health officials said

A nationwide Avian Influenza Prevention Zone is also in place. North Yorkshire Council said its housing rules apply to flocks of 50 birds or more, and to smaller flocks if poultry or eggs go to people outside the keeper’s household.

Cllr Richard Foster, the council’s executive member for managing our environment, said: “Whether you keep thousands of birds or just a few hens at home, everyone has a role to play in preventing the spread of disease.”

The council’s director of public health, Louise Wallace, said bird flu infections in people are rare and the risk to the public remains very low. The council said properly cooked poultry, poultry products and eggs are safe to eat.

## What it means for you

* **If you keep birds anywhere in the Ripon and Boroughbridge area**, check whether your address is inside the zones on Defra’s map, and follow the rules above if it is.
* **Registering your birds is a legal requirement**, the council said, however small the flock.
* **If you think your birds have bird flu**, report it to the Animal and Plant Health Agency on 03000 200 301.
* **Do not touch sick or dead wild birds.** Report dead wild birds on 03459 33 55 77.
* **Shows and sales are off inside the zone.** Anyone planning a poultry sale or show in the area needs to check the zones first.

For everyone else, nothing changes: the council and the UK Health Security Agency say the risk to the public is very low.
