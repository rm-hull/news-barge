## More than 16 years after the Smolensk disaster, prosecutors have released the findings of international experts who ruled out an explosion as the cause of the crash. Former Polish Defence Minister Antoni Macierewicz has rejected their conclusions.

An international team of experts has identified four factors it believes led to the 2010 Smolensk plane crash, which killed 96 people, including Polish President Lech Kaczyński and his wife.

The experts identified an unstable approach to landing, an incorrect assessment of the aircraft's actual altitude, a continued descent below the minimum altitude and a delayed decision to abort the landing. They also ruled out an explosion as the cause of the crash.

Poland's National Prosecutor's Office spokesperson Przemysław Nowak presented the findings of the comprehensive report on Friday, stressing that they reflected the experts' conclusions rather than an independent assessment by prosecutors.

A total of 22 experts from the United States, the United Kingdom, Ireland and Hungary contributed to the report.

"These are the findings and assessments of the experts. So they are not the findings and assessments of the prosecution or the prosecutors," Nowak said.

## Five years of work and 66 questions from prosecutors

The international team of experts was tasked with preparing a comprehensive report on the circumstances, sequence of events and causes of the crash. The experts drew on all the evidence gathered and on earlier expert assessments.

The disciplines covered in the analysis included aviation, medicine, chemistry, engineering, computational mathematics and photogrammetry.

The first report was issued in August 2024. The document, drafted in English, ran to several hundred pages including annexes. After it was translated into Polish the prosecutors carried out a detailed analysis.

They concluded at the time that the material was incomplete and unclear, so they submitted 66 questions to the experts. A supplementary report was issued in May 2026. Once it had been translated the investigators considered the overall report "complete and clear".

Among the experts were specialists who had previously analysed other major aviation and aerospace disasters, including the Lockerbie bombing, the Airbus emergency landing on the Hudson River and the Space Shuttle Columbia disaster.

## **What caused the Smolensk crash, according to experts?**

According to Nowak, the experts concluded that the Smolensk crash was caused by a chain of events during the final approach to landing, including an unstable approach, an incorrect assessment of the aircraft's actual altitude, a continued descent below the minimum altitude and a delayed decision to go around.

"The experts assessed as an absolutely key circumstance of the Smolensk crash the continuation of the descent below the minimum altitude despite the absence of conditions for a safe landing," he said.

## **Experts ruled out an explosion**

The spokesperson for the National Prosecutor's Office said the experts had ruled out an explosion as the cause of the crash, explaining that the hypothesis had been tested using various methods.

"The results of all these tests" led to "identical conclusions ruling out an explosion as the cause of this crash."

The experts analysed cockpit voice recordings and found no sound corresponding to an explosion. They also examined damage to the aircraft's structure, computer simulations, the victims' injuries, the distribution of wreckage and bodies, and the results of physico-chemical analyses.

## **Political reactions to the findings**

Former Polish Defence Minister Antoni Macierewicz challenged the experts' findings, questioning their authority and pointing out that none of them had visited Smolensk in person.

Macierewicz also reiterated his claims regarding explosive materials, arguing that the experts had failed to properly consider evidence concerning the aircraft wreckage and earlier tests.

Meanwhile, Polish Prime Minister Donald Tusk, speaking at a press conference in Brussels, said the causes of the crash had already been established by a commission in 2011 and that its findings had subsequently been challenged "for purely political reasons".

"There is nothing worse than making such cynical political fuel out of death, especially the death of loved ones. I will never forgive them for that," Tusk said.

## **Investigation remains ongoing**

The presentation of the experts' report does not mark the end of the investigation, which was launched on 10 April 2010 and handed over to the National Prosecutor's Office in 2016.

Investigators have obtained several hundred expert assessments, interviewed more than 1,000 witnesses and compiled nearly 2,000 volumes of case files.

Prosecutors are still examining several possible scenarios, including errors by Russian air traffic controllers, crew errors, potential aircraft malfunction and various forms of sabotage.

According to the National Prosecutor's Office, three Russian officers, including air traffic controller Lieutenant-Colonel Paweł P., are suspected of causing an air traffic disaster and are wanted on arrest warrants.
