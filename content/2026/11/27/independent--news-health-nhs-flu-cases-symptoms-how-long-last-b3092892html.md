Health officials have reported a surge in flu cases across the UK heading into December, with hospitalisations increasing substantially.

According to the UK Health Security Agency (UKHSA), hospital admission rates in England reached 9.0 per 100,000 people last week, up from 5.0 per 100,000 during the prior seven days. Figures from the agency indicate that rates have not hit such elevated levels at this point in November in any year this decade.

"Flu levels are now rising sharply with hospital and intensive care admission rates increasing by almost 80 per cent this week," said Dr Ellen Fragaszy, lead epidemiologist at UKHSA.

Data from the UKHSA indicates that about one in three people in high-risk categories have taken up their flu vaccine so far this season.

Dr Mohammad Bakhtiar, clinical lead at Medical Express Clinic, has shared his advice on how to spot flu symptoms and when to seek medical attention.

## Why do flu cases tend to rise this time of year?

“Every year during the winter flu cases spike and the major factor driving this is the change in weather,” says Bakhtiar.

“Many viruses [such as influenza] survive better in the winter months.”

People also tend to socialise indoors at this time of year and Bakhtiar explains that this helps flu spread more easily.

![People also tend to socialise indoors at this time of year and Bakhtiar explains that this helps flu spread more easily](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/27/15/01M8EPNCXJ2G7MWBD5MVV4PN00.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“In winter people tend to get together in the same room indoors because it’s warmer, which is one factor behind the spike in flu cases during this time of year,” says Bakhtiar.

“Every year there are new strains of the virus because it mutates. You cannot put every single type of strain into the flu vaccine, but people do become naturally immune to some strains through the years.”

## What are the main symptoms of flu to look out for?

“The main symptoms of flu include body aches, a cough, headaches and a lack of energy,” says Bakhtiar.

A high temperature is also common.

“Look out for a spike in temperature that goes up and comes down for a few days,” advises Bakhtiar.

The symptoms are similar for children, but they can also get pain in their ear, sore eyes and swollen glands, according to the NHS website.

## How long does flu typically last?

Duration and recovery depend on both immunity and age, as well as overall health.

“For example, if somebody is elderly and has an underlying disease, then recovery is likely to take longer,” says Bakhtiar.

![Duration and recovery depend on both immunity and age, as well as overall health](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/27/15/01M8EPN3Z25GY5054N4JRX1WSN.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“Whereas young people who don’t have any underlying diseases are much more likely to recover from it much quicker.”

He adds that speed of recovery can also depend on how much rest a patient has.

“The more rest you get, the more rapidly you can recover because the body and immune system need every ounce of energy to combat these viruses,” says Bakhtiar.

## What are the best ways to recover from the flu?

“Rest, take paracetamol regularly, have plenty of fluids and very simple food,” recommends Bakhtiar.

## When should someone see a doctor about their symptoms?

“If you have a persistent cough, are short of breath, have a severe headache, are vomiting or have diarrhoea or a temperature over 41 and your symptoms don’t get better after two or three days or get worse instead of better, contact your doctor,” advises Bakhtiar.

![The NHS website also advises asking for an urgent GP appointment or calling 111 if you’re 65 or over](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/27/15/01M8EPMRSP8HWXBQ4WRBHJFKJ5.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The NHS website also advises asking for an urgent GP appointment or calling 111 if you’re 65 or over, you’re pregnant or have recently given birth, have a long-term medical condition, a weakened immune system, or are worried about your baby’s or child’s symptoms.

## Is there anything we can do to reduce the risk of spreading flu?

Flu is very infectious and easily spreads to other people. You’re more likely to give it to others in the first five days after getting infected, according to the NHS website.

“Try to avoid close contact with other people,” advises Bakhtiar. “Sleep in the spare room and just stay at home and isolate yourself until you recover.”

The NHS website also recommends washing your hands often with warm water and soap, covering your mouth and nose with a tissue when you cough or sneeze, and binning used tissues as quickly as possible.

“Also, make sure you get your flu vaccine if you are invited and are eligible,” advises Bakhtiar.

## Should people carry on working if they have flu symptoms?

“Flu is very contagious and if you go to work you could give it to your work colleagues. So, if you have a temperature, a bad cough and other symptoms, it’s better not to go to work,” says Bakhtiar.

He recommends listening to your body.

“You know your body better than anybody else,” says Bakhtiar.
