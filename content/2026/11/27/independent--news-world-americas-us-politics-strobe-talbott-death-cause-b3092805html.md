Strobe Talbott, who reported for *Time* magazine before serving as deputy secretary of state under President Bill Clinton and later leading the Brookings Institution, has died at the age of 80.

His son, Devin Talbott, said that he passed away peacefully after a long illness at his East Hampton, New York, home.

Talbott was educated at Yale and Oxford, where he bunked with Clinton.

While at the British university, after interning for *Time* in Moscow, the magazine asked Talbott to help translate the leaked memoirs of Nikita Khrushchev, the former first secretary of the Communist Party of the Soviet Union who was ousted in 1964.

Talbott did, and it gave him some notoriety and a job at the magazine.

![US President Bill Clinton (top left), US National Security Advisor Sandy Berger (top center), and US Deputy Secretary of State Strobe Talbott (top right) sit across the table from Russian President Boris Yeltsin (lower right) and his staff during their bi-lateral meeting on 20 June 1999](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/27/14/1733617836..?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&auto=webp)

He went on to spend 21 years at *Time*, where he focused on Soviet-American relations and held positions including Washington bureau chief and a foreign affairs columnist, according to his U.S. State Department biography.

The newly elected Clinton then came calling.

Talbott joined the U.S. State Department in 1994, swiftly becoming deputy secretary of state.

In that role, he was the architect of the Clinton administration's approach to Russia and other formerly Soviet countries and conducted negotiations between the U.S. and India after the latter's nuclear tests in 1998.

Clinton said in a statement that he and wife Hillary were “deeply saddened” by the death of “a wonderful friend, and a world-class human being”.

![Strobe Talbott, right, with US Secretary of State Madeleine Albright and National Security advisor Sandy Berger](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/27/14/2210817036..?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&auto=webp)

The statement said: “Strobe was a good and brilliant man who made our world more peaceful and secure.”

After serving in the Clinton administration, Talbott was the president of Brookings from 2002 to 2017.

In that time, he also chaired the U.S. State Department's Foreign Affairs Policy Board.

Talbott also authored over a dozen books.

His last book was titled *Our Founders’ Warning: The Age of Reason Meets the Age of Trump*, which was published in 2020.

![President-elect Bill Clinton and Strobe Talbott of Time magazine talk privately as they walk along the beach on Hilton Head Island, S.C., on 2 January 1993](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/26/21/Strobe_Talbott_56139.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The book contrasts Donald Trump with the first six U.S. presidents and their values of honesty, civic duty, and empathy.

Talbott was born in Dayton, Ohio, in 1946.

He is survived by two sons, Devin and Adrian; stepdaughter Rebecca Ascher-Walsh; siblings Kirk, Marjo and Page Talbott; and eight grandchildren, according to the *New York Times**.*

His first wife, Brook Shearer, died in 2009.

He later married author Barbara Lazear Ascher.
