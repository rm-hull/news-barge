The first students at Lighthouse School Harrogate are getting to know their new school ahead of starting full-time in January.

The specialist school for autistic young people aged 11 to 19 is based on the former Woodfield Primary School site in Bilton.

Its first intake will be 24 students in Years 7, 8 and 9. They and their families are taking part in a transition programme to meet staff, explore the school and get used to daily routines.

![](https://mmo.aiircdn.com/409/6b0956ec2fa597a7aaf.png)

Harrogate and Knaresborough MP Tom Gordon visited yesterday (Thursday 26th November) to tour the school and meet the team preparing for the January intake.

Emma Sullivan, Executive Principal of Lighthouse Education Trust, said:

> “It is wonderful to see students now beginning their transition into school and becoming familiar with the environment, staff and opportunities available to them.”

The trust, which also runs a specialist school in Leeds, expects student numbers in Harrogate to reach 48 by September 2027.

The school will eventually offer up to 80 places.

Alongside the curriculum, students will have access to speech and language support and help with their wellbeing.

They will also develop skills for work and independent living.

Tom Gordon MP said:

> “It was a pleasure to visit Lighthouse School today and meet the team behind it. Every child deserves the chance to succeed, and for autistic young people that means having the right support in the right setting.
>
> "This school will give them exactly that, and I know what a difference it will make to so many families across Harrogate and Knaresborough.
>
> “This is a really good start in expanding specialist provision locally. I'll keep fighting for the SEND funding our area needs, so that every child gets the opportunity to thrive.
>
> "I look forward to working with Lighthouse as the school opens and grows.”
