A former rough sleeper has rebuilt his life with the support of York’s housing navigators and is now looking for work.

Damien Goudy, a qualified construction worker, became homeless after losing his job, home, and car.

He spent time living on the streets and later in temporary accommodation before moving into supported housing at York’s Peasholme Centre.

Mr Goudy said: "When you’re trying to deal with everything yourself, and someone who knows everything helps you, that makes all the difference.

"The Housing navigators are really good at relating to you: not everyone can."

Eighteen months ago, he got sober and moved into his own flat with ongoing support from City of York Council’s housing navigators.

He is now applying for jobs and sorting out a dentist with their help.

Councillor Anna Perrett, executive member for housing at City of York Council, said: "As Damien’s story shows, people experience knocks in life and sometimes need a helping hand to get back on track.

"This year’s allocation of almost £500k will be spent on continuing to do what works well: offering accommodation, including tenancies, and tailored support to meet each individual’s needs."

The funding comes from a new three-year Government scheme.
