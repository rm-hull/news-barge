Veteran BBC journalist John Simpson said it is “shameful” that his restaurateur and friend has been charged with attempting to murder his 100-year-old mother, a court has heard.

Browns Brasseries founder Jeremy Mogford, 79, and his sister Sarah Pickering, 72, are on trial at Oxford Crown Court accused of trying to kill their mother Pamela Mogford in Bledlow Ridge, Buckinghamshire, in February 2022, by administering morphine.

On Friday, Mr Simpson took to the witness box and told a jury how he had arranged his reporting for the BBC on the Ukraine war from Kyiv so that he could be available to give evidence in the case.

Asked why he wanted to come to court, the journalist said: “Firstly, because I admire him greatly and, secondly, I thought it was shameful that he would be charged over this.

“And, so, I was prepared to do quite a lot to come to give evidence on his behalf.”

Mr Simpson said: “I feel really, really strongly that somebody needed to speak up about all these things.”

![Browns Brasseries founder Jeremy Mogford, 79, and his sister Sarah Pickering, 72, are on trial at Oxford Crown Court accused of trying to kill their mother Pamela Mogford](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/23/14/01M84A3BTPVFPETC1AGHZSH9J2.jpg?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&auto=webp)

The reporter described how he had known Mogford for many years, sees him “every month, or every other month” and is a regular at his restaurant in Oxford.

He said he had never met Pamela Mogford but “knew all about her” because of what his friend had said.

Mr Simpson said Mogford was “a man who clearly loved his mother and admired her”.

He told the court: “I remember talking to him about her and how proud he was that she was coming up to 100.

“He absolutely had a great, deep affection for her.”

Mr Simpson said that, with his restaurants and hotels, the defendant had “single-handedly lifted it (Oxford) to a different plane”.

And he said he had an “excellent, excellent character”, describing how he supported many causes without many people knowing.

A statement was also read from the novelist William Boyd, who said he had known Mogford and his family for almost 50 years.

Mr Boyd said the family had to deal with a very difficult situation when Pamela Mogford was at the end of her life.

He said the defendant was “someone I know I can rely on absolutely – a true friend, which is a rare category in anyone’s life”.

![Pamela Mogford, who died in Bledlow Ridge, Buckinghamshire, in February 2022](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/23/14/01M84A3AFD7JF1F4QQFA1Q7070.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Prosecutors say the defendants gave their mother the equivalent of “at least four times” as much morphine as her GP had given her the evening before she died in an effort to “speed up” her death.

Mogford and Pickering maintain they only gave their mother morphine after the GP left as pain relief.

Earlier on Friday Julian Christopher KC, prosecuting, asked Mogford about when he was interviewed by police and whether he was aware at the time they were alleging that “you and your sister tried to hasten her death” with the administration of Oramorph, which is a liquid morphine medicine.

Mogford told Oxford Crown Court: “That is complete and utter nonsense.”

The defendant said: “We gave her Oramorph because she was in pain.”

The prosecutor said to Mogford: “In the interview, when you were asked questions, you couldn’t tell the truth because the truth is that you and your sister tried to shorten your mother’s life on February 14.”

The defendant told the jury: “Honestly, that is such utter nonsense.

“Why would we shorten our mother’s life? Why? She was dying.”

Mogford told the jury: “We’ve been absolutely transparent here when we’ve said we gave our mum some Oramorph because she was in pain, and that was the go-to solution for extreme pain in the house.”

The defendant became visibly upset at the end of his evidence when he told the jury how the worst aspect of all he has been through was the exhumation of his mother for a post-mortem examination.

He said he found out this had happened through a media report, although prosecutors read out his police interview when officers told him about the exhumation.

![Mogford and Sarah Pickering (pictured) maintain they only gave their mother morphine after the GP left as pain relief](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/27/16/01M7SMA2CA2H90SH9D1T52GDXN.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Mogford appeared to be in tears when he said: “What was the worst thing… it was taking her out of the ground. Cutting her up. In a hospital that she loathed. It’s appalling.”

The jury has heard how the siblings were arrested after texts were discovered from Pickering on the evening of her mother’s death which included: “I am actually killing her”, and: “I shall wait until Foyle is over then I can go to my grave knowing I have topped someone. It’s certainly on my bucket list.”

Mogford opened the first Browns Brasserie in 1973 in Brighton with then-business partner John Mayhew.

He sold the Browns chain, which has a large presence in university towns such as Oxford and Cambridge, to Bass Brewery for £35 million in 1996.

He is also the founder and chairman of The Oxford Collection, a hospitality group which comprises two luxury five-star hotels – The Old Bank and The Old Parsonage – and three restaurants – Quod, Gees and Parsonage Grill – in Oxford.

The prosecution and defence both agree there was “absolutely no financial motive to kill Pamela Mogford”, jurors previously heard.

The court also heard experts cannot conclude that the level of morphine given to Mrs Mogford was enough to kill her.

An ambulance had been called on 14 February, 2022 as it was thought Mrs Mogford might have had a stroke, and although paramedics wanted to take her to hospital she refused.

Jurors also previously heard that Mrs Mogford’s GP visited her three times that day, giving her injections of morphine on the second and third visits as he thought “she might be dying”.

The court further heard Mrs Mogford’s wishes not to be taken to hospital, and not to be resuscitated, had been recorded for years prior to her death.

The trial continues.
