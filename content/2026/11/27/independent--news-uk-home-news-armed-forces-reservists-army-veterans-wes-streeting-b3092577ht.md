The Ministry of Defence (MoD) has been unable to contact tens of thousands of British veterans who could be called upon to fight for Britain in the case of war.

Letters and emails have only been sent to about half of the 95,000 former troops who form part of the country’s “strategic reserve”, a defence source told *The Times*.

The newspaper reported the MoD has failed to contact around 45,000 to tell them to be prepared for a call-up, as it does not have up-to-date addresses.

The government has launched a push to reconnect with the reservists, who have a legal liability to return to service in an emergency.

In addition to direct emails to those it has contact details for, open letters have been published on the armed forces’ websites.

Former troops can be compelled to take up arms during a national crisis under new legislation, if they are younger than 65 and have left the service within the last 18 years.

The contact with veterans is the first of its kind to be sent since the Cold War, and offers opportunities to volunteer for training and exercises.

Those who have left within the past four years are being offered a £50 grant if they respond.

![Wes Streeting has said we ‘need to build our national resilience’](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/26/08/01M86NJHAD5Z6YS64Q8ZDXVHKN.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Speaking to Times Radio, defence secretary Wes Streeting said: “We need to keep up to date with them. We need to make sure they have access to further development opportunities with us. In some ways, what I’m doing is what should have been done for a long time.

“We do need to build our national resilience, build our readiness and make sure that we are prepared for the worst – even as we’re taking steps every day that make that worst-case scenario less likely.

“Doing it now, when there isn't a looming threat, is the right thing to do. It's much better than finding yourself caught short later.

“We saw that lesson writ large in the pandemic, when the government was criticised for under-preparedness. I'm not going to make the same mistake, which is why we're taking this step. It should be routine business and will now be regular practice."

![General Sir Roly Walker, head of the army, has written to former troops](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/16/14/2228926185..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

General Sir Roly Walker, head of the army, wrote another letter to former troops saying they wished to know what skills and experiences veterans had accumulated since leaving that might be of relevance.

He wrote: “As we so often tell ourselves, when it comes to the hard business of soldiering, our point of difference to other armies has [been], is and will always be the quality of our people: so we want to know what talent we have in our part of the strategic reserve in case we need to call upon it in the years ahead.”

Mr Streeting told Sky News on Thursday: “We have written out to our strategic reserve.

“These are people who previously served in our armed forces.

“We’ve written to them to make sure their details are up to date, and also we have an understanding of the experience and the skills that they’ve developed since leaving the armed forces.

“This should be information that I think the government ought to hold as standard, whatever the threat environment.

“But there’s no doubt, and I’ve talked about this – as have all of my counterparts across Nato in recent days, weeks and months – we are operating in a heightened threat environment.

“And so this degree of readiness, I think, is a sensible, necessary precaution.

“People should find it reassuring rather than alarming.”
