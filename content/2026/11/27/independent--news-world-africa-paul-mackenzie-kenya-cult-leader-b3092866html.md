A Kenyan court has found a starvation cult leader guilty of manslaughter on Friday for the deaths of 123 of his followers, the latest conviction over one of the deadliest cult-related massacres ⁠in modern times.

Paul Mackenzie, already serving a 200-year jail sentence from earlier this month for the murder of nearly 200 children, has also been convicted of child cruelty, assault, radicalisation and terrorism-related offences.

Mackenzie, who was once a taxi driver, founded Good News International Church in 2003 and began preaching increasingly apocalyptic sermons and encouraging his followers to starve themselves and their children to death so they could reach heaven before the end of the world.

In a March 2023 YouTube video, Mackenzie — who had thousands of followers in person and online — said that he was guided by "the voice of Christ" when he preached messages about the world coming to an end that year.

He led an apocalyptic Christian church in a forest near Kenya's Indian Ocean coast.

His suspected 500 victims included children buried alive or dumped in latrines.

Delivering his judgement in a court in the city of Mombasa on Friday, magistrate Alex ‌Ithuku said Mackenzie "gave the directions to fast ‌to death... the order in which people were to die, directed the faithful move to secluded fasting places, and took part in what ⁠followed these deaths".

Mackenzie had pleaded not guilty to manslaughter, and one of his lawyers said he would appeal it after sentencing.

The scale of the killings in the Shakahola Forest, which gradually emerged from April 2023 onwards, has shocked Kenya and prompted calls for tighter oversight of religious ‌groups.

![Workers take shelter while digging the ground to exume bodies from the mass grave site in the Shakahola forest of Malindi, Kenya](https://static.independent.co.uk/2023/06/15/16/newFile-5.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Police searched Mackenzie's Malindi compound in 2017 and found dozens of children living there without attending school. He was charged with offering education at an unregistered facility but continued his teachings until 2019, when his church was shut down, and he relocated to the Shakahola Forest.

People were lured there by the promise of affordable arable land but found armed militia who enforced the fasting and eventual deaths of followers, KNCHR found. Those who defied orders to fast or tried to escape were strangled or clubbed to death, it added.

In 2023, President William Ruto said he ​took responsibility as head of state for the government's ‌failure to prevent the deaths.
