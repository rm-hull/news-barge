# University of York wins national award for tackling PhD equity gap

Posted on Friday 27 November 2026

![The YCEDE team pose for the cameras after winning their award](https://www.york.ac.uk/media/news-and-events/pressreleases/2026/Gemini_Generated_Image_6na91o6na91o6na9.jpg)

The award celebrates the Yorkshire Consortium for Equity in Doctoral Education (YCEDE) - a York-led collaboration with the Universities of Bradford, Leeds, Sheffield, and Sheffield Hallam, alongside external partners. Together, the consortium has pioneered a sector-leading programme to address ethnic underrepresentation at PhD level.

**Equity models**

Higher education has long faced a stark equity problem in postgraduate research. While postgraduates are largely excluded from traditional access and participation plans, data from

2021 showed that ethnically minoritised students made up 30% of undergraduates across the five Yorkshire universities, yet dropped to just 21% at doctoral level.

By pooling resources and learning from established graduate equity models in the US, YCEDE has driven systemic cultural change at a scale and speed, impossible for a single institution to achieve alone.

Focusing on nurturing the talent pipeline, reforming institutional processes, and empowering on-course support, the initiative has delivered transformative results:

* Closing the gap at York: By 2025, the University of York completely closed its 17.4 percentage-point offer gap - meaning white and ethnically minoritised candidates are now equally likely to be offered a PhD place.
* Surge in applications: Home PhD applications from ethnically minoritised candidates across the consortium jumped from a 21% baseline to 31.2% in 2024/25, representing more than 350 additional applicants.
* More offers made: Across all five partner universities, the proportion of ethnically minoritised applicants receiving a PhD offer rose from 19% to over 24%.

The judges said: “The evidence tells us that doctoral education has an equity problem, and the Yorkshire Consortium for Equity in Doctoral Education collaboration tackled this through nurturing the pipeline, reforming institutions and empowering on-course support. It shows what is possible when universities work together to make change at pace. It is a project that sets the standard for postgraduate widening participation."

**Structural barriers**

Kathryn Arnold, Professor of Ecology and Project Lead, Yorkshire Consortium for Equity in Doctoral Education, said: “As a University of Opportunity, York is committed to ensuring that talent - not background - determines who gets to shape the future of research.

"Winning this prestigious award is a testament to what we can achieve when organisations collaborate to dismantle structural barriers."

The YCEDE win reflects York’s broader mission as a University of Opportunity to widen access and participation across the institution.

Through flagship programmes like Generation Research, the University is giving a vital helping hand to students from all backgrounds looking to gain hands-on studentship experience.

The scheme offers fully funded technical and research placements at universities, companies, charities, and research institutes - helping demystify postgraduate study and build the skills needed to succeed at PhD level and beyond.

York was nominated in two other categories at the awards ceremony held in Birmingham on 26 November.

SAINTS, the UKRI AI Centre for Doctoral Training in Safe Artificial Intelligence Systems, part of the Institute for Safe Autonomy at the University of York, was nominated for Outstanding Contribution to Equality, Diversity and Inclusion Award.

The University was also nominated in the Outstanding Entrepreneurial University category.
