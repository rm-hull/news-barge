Proton VPN and ExpressVPN sit at the premium end of the VPN market, but they appeal to slightly different buyers.

Proton leans hard into privacy pedigree and a broader encrypted ecosystem. At the same time, ExpressVPN pairs its impressive global server network with a wider suite of bundled extras, from password management to travel data.

Right now, both are running highly competitive Black Friday discounts – Proton VPN Plus is 75% off, and ExpressVPN’s tiers are discounted by as much as 81% – which makes this a very good moment to settle the debate.

Below, we compare two of the best VPNs available on features, pricing, and streaming performance, and crown an overall winner.

## Proton VPN Plus vs ExpressVPN: Features

![ExpressVPN home page on Windows](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/mSEVrAs4FJf4mcddGkJZLH-1200-80.png)

Both services run their own custom protocols. Proton VPN builds everything on open-source foundations: WireGuard and OpenVPN, plus its Stealth protocol which disguises VPN traffic as ordinary web traffic – invaluable on restrictive networks.

ExpressVPN counters with Lightway, an open-source protocol praised for staying quick on unreliable connections. Its latest upgrade, Lightway Turbo, is noticeably faster again, and obfuscation is handled automatically on every server.

Locally, Proton VPN's WireGuard protocol measured 1783 Mbps against ExpressVPN’s 1810 Mbps with Lightway Turbo in dual-tunnel mode. From the US to a UK server, Proton VPN held up impressively at 1397 Mbps, with ExpressVPN’s dual tunnel close behind at 1184 Mbps. Ultimately, the two are evenly matched on speed, with Proton VPN holding a slight edge over long distances.

Security-wise, both are seriously well equipped. ExpressVPN runs its entire network on RAM-only TrustedServers and has been independently audited more times than most VPNs.

Proton VPN is Swiss-based, open source, and regularly audited, and has two tricks that ExpressVPN lacks: Secure Core, a two-hop route through hardened data centers in Switzerland, Iceland, and Sweden, and port forwarding for torrenting.

One key difference is post-quantum encryption. ExpressVPN has already rolled out post-quantum encryption by default across Lightway and WireGuard, future-proofing traffic against attacks. Proton, despite building post-quantum encryption into Proton Mail, hasn’t shipped it for its VPN yet.

Extra features reflect each company’s ecosystem. Proton VPN Plus is a pure VPN plus NetShield ad-and-malware blocking. Step up to Proton Unlimited, and you get premium Proton Pass (Proton’s password manager), 500GB of encrypted Proton Drive storage, Proton Mail, Calendar, and Wallet.

ExpressVPN layers its extras differently: every tier includes ad, tracker, and malicious website blocking, plus ExpressAI (a private assistant with credits varying by tier) and ExpressMailGuard email aliases. Advanced adds a password manager, identity monitoring, and free eSIM data, and Pro a dedicated IP and Identity Defender (US-only).

As for streaming, Proton VPN was the stronger Netflix performer. Both unblocked the US, UK, and Australia libraries, but ExpressVPN had trouble with Netflix Canada and Japan.

Yet ExpressVPN accessed ITVX and TVNZ+ where Proton struggled, and reached Prime Video almost everywhere while Proton VPN missed multiple countries. ExpressVPN also stumbled with YouTube in several countries. BBC iPlayer and 9Now posed no problem for either.

## Proton VPN Plus vs ExpressVPN: Pricing

![Proton VPN website download page on windows laptop](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/SJtS4QJrmUdgrg55RYwNJc-1200-80.png)

Proton VPN offers two paid subscription tiers, compared to ExpressVPN’s three. Proton VPN Plus costs $2.49/month on the two-year Black Friday deal – 75% off its usual $9.99 – billed $59.76 for the first 24 months, then renewing at $83.88/year.

That pits it directly against ExpressVPN Basic, the VPN-only entry tier, at $2.79/month over a 28-month term (2 years plus 4 months, 81% off), billed $78.12 before renewing at $99.95/year. ExpressVPN’s deal runs four months longer, but Proton VPN Plus is cheaper upfront and at renewal.

ExpressVPN Advanced adds the Keys password manager, identity monitoring, and eSIM data for $3.99/month over a 28-month term, billed at $111.72 and renewing at $119.95/year. Pro is ExpressVPN’s all-in-one bundle, adding a dedicated IP and the full identity-theft toolkit. It costs $6.99/month over a 28-month term and is billed at $195.72 before renewing at $199.95/year.

On shorter commitments, prices rise across the board. ExpressVPN spans $4.99–$8.99/month on a one year subscription, jumping to $14.99–$22.99 for a single month. At $9.99/month (VPN Plus) and $12.99/month (Unlimited), Proton VPN’s one-month plans are the better value if you don’t want to commit long-term.  
Proton Unlimited, which swaps in an entire privacy suite (premium Pass, Mail, and Drive), is priced at $6.49/month on the one-year deal ($77.88 for the first year, renewing at $119.88/year).

Proton VPN Plus is the standout bargain; Unlimited is tremendous value if you’d use Proton’s bundled services. ExpressVPN Advanced is an affordable route to a lightly bundled, VPN-focused plan. Keep in mind that listed prices don’t include sales tax. However, each VPN offers a 30-day money-back guarantee, so you can try either risk-free.

Proton VPN Plus and Unlimited allow 10 simultaneous connections which will be plenty for most. That said, ExpressVPN starts at 10 on Basic, rising to 12 on Advanced, and 14 on Pro.

On apps, both cover Windows, macOS, Linux, Android, iOS, Fire TV, and Apple TV, and offer browser extensions. Proton VPN’s recent app updates have narrowed the ease-of-use gap, but ExpressVPN’s beginner-friendly interface still edges it. However, Proton’s apps pack in more granular controls tinkerers will appreciate.

## Verdict

Overall, Proton VPN edges this contest on value and privacy depth, while ExpressVPN narrowly wins on streaming access and beginner-friendly polish.

Budget-conscious and privacy-focused buyers should pick Proton VPN Plus – cheaper now and at renewal, with Secure Core and port forwarding thrown in.

Anyone wanting Proton’s entire privacy suite in one subscription should grab Proton Unlimited.

Streaming fanatics, first-time VPN users, and anyone who wants post-quantum encryption or a dedicated IP out of the box should choose ExpressVPN. Both Black Friday deals – 75% off Proton VPN Plus and 81% off ExpressVPN Basic – are worthwhile.
