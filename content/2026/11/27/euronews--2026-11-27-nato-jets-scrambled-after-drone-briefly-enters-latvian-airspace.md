## Latvia's army hasn't said where the drone came from, and it warns more incursions are likely.

NATO fighter jets and a Latvian interceptor drone were deployed overnight after an unidentified drone briefly entered the country's airspace near the Russian border, Latvia's National Armed Forces (NBS) said on Friday.

The drone was detected moving close to the border in Ludza municipality, eastern Latvia, and "briefly flew over Latvian territory", the army said in a statement.

According to the NBS, an air threat was declared in Ludza at 2.30am local time (00.30 GMT) and residents were sent an orange alert. That level signals a confirmed threat that requires immediate action.

The alert was lifted at 2.55am, when a lower-level yellow warning was issued for neighbouring Balvi municipality in Latvia's north-east. That warning ended at about 3.30am, public broadcaster LSM reported, citing the armed forces.

"Fighter jets from NATO's air-policing mission in the Baltic States were scrambled, and an interceptor drone from the national armed forces was deployed," the army said. The interceptor returned to its launch site after the flight, and air defence units were ready to respond immediately.

The NBS did not say where the drone came from. It said it had sent extra air defence units to the eastern border, and warned that foreign drones could again enter Latvian airspace while Russia's war in Ukraine continues.

Several countries near the war have reported drone incursions or crashes since Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine in February 2022.

Drones have been shot down over Latvia twice this year, the NBS said at the time. French jets on NATO's Baltic Air Policing mission downed one in June, and allied fighters shot down another over Balvi in August.
