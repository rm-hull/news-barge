North Yorkshire Council has suspended a scheme allowing leisure centre members to pay extra to book exercise classes ahead of other customers following a backlash.

The move comes as a petition signed by 512 people accusing the authority of creating an unfair divide between customers who can afford to pay more and those who cannot is set to be debated by councillors.

Under the new membership structure introduced at Active North Yorkshire leisure centres, customers paying an additional £5 a month for Plus membership were given the opportunity to book exercise classes two days earlier than standard members.

However, the changes have prompted significant criticism, particularly at Skipton Leisure and Wellbeing Hub, where popular exercise classes are already oversubscribed.

The petition calls for the council to restore equal booking rights for all members.

It states: “Many members are deeply concerned about this policy shift.

“These are local authority-run facilities, meant to promote communal access to health and wellness resources.

“It feels unjust and contrary to the spirit of a community-driven environment to prioritise those who can afford to pay more to get better access.”

The petitioners say some longstanding members had already faced substantial increases in membership fees in September.

They added: “Local leisure centres are intended to serve the entire community, not just those who can easily afford it.

“By introducing this payment barrier, North Yorkshire Council is creating a divide in accessibility of exercise and wellness resources.”

In a report prepared for next week’s meeting of the Skipton and Ripon area committee, council officers acknowledge there has been a significant volume of negative feedback following the introduction of Plus membership.

They say the problems have been compounded by technical issues with the authority’s new leisure management system.

The report states: “We fully appreciate the impact on customers and this is not the customer journey we want.

“Having considered the feedback and as a result we have temporarily suspended the ten-day advance booking benefit whilst further work is undertaken to investigate and resolve the underlying issues.”

Councillor Simon Myers, executive member for culture, arts and housing, said the problems appeared to be largely confined to Skipton, with few similar complaints reported elsewhere in North Yorkshire.

He added: “I think it’s really to do with the system not working and there being a general problem with capacity, with people unable to get on classes.

“We’ve heard what people have said, so we’ve paused it.

“We’re going to review it in the future when everything has settled down and the leisure investment strategy is a bit further on, and see what we can do about it.”

Officers say the new membership and pricing structure was introduced to provide a consistent approach across the county, with considerable differences in charges previously in place.

While acknowledging that some customers are now paying more, they say others have seen a reduction in membership fees.

The authority is also looking at ways to address demand for exercise classes, including reviewing class capacities, improving waiting lists and introducing measures to reduce unused bookings.

Longer-term investment in leisure facilities is expected to create additional space for group exercise classes.

Councillors will consider the petition when they meet on Thursday, December 3.
