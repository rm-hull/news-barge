# Don’t let train knifeman remove feeling of safety on railways, urges detective

## A senior police officer has urged rail passengers not to let Huntingdon train knifeman Anthony Williams “remove that feeling of safety” of travelling on the railways after his “one night of carnage”.

Detective Superintendent Sam Blackburn, of the British Transport Police’s Major, Serious and Organised Crime Unit, said it is “incredibly safe to travel on trains”.

Williams, 33, stabbed 10 people on a London-bound LNER service from Doncaster on the evening of November 1 last year, boarding at Peterborough before the quick-thinking driver made an unscheduled stop at Huntingdon and Williams was arrested.

The “marauding” knifeman has been found guilty of the attempted murders of 14 people, and will be sentenced in February.

All of those attacked survived their injuries.

Mr Blackburn said: “I’m incredibly frustrated by the fact that Anthony Williams has eroded that feeling of safety on trains, but I do want to reiterate that the train network is incredibly safe.

“We have BTP officers patrolling in high visibility, unpredictable patterns, station visits and patrolling on trains.

![LNER train attacker Anthony Williams](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/jpim-static/image/2026/11/26/16/35/71ba9b40-e754-11f1-88dc-f746e0cde621.jpeg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


“But not only that, we’re utilising technology. We have over 100,000 CCTV cameras.

“We have drones in a box, utilising technology to make sure people feel safe.”

He stressed it is “incredibly safe to travel on trains”.

He added: “It’s safe and secure. Don’t let Anthony Williams remove that feeling of safety from that one night of carnage.”

He said he believed that after the attack, “we lost some sense of security in using the railway, which is a frustration of mine and still is because the railway is incredibly safe”.

![Anthony Williams](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/jpim-static/image/2026/11/26/16/42/anthony-williams.png?trim=18,2,124,0&width=640&quality=65)


Mr Blackburn continued: “Millions of people take journeys every single day with no threat of violence and it’s so frustrating for me personally that one individual, Mr Williams, took away that feeling of safety that people would regularly have travelling on a train.

“Hopefully the result that we’ve got and the investigation that we’ve thoroughly carried out has given that reassurance back to individuals.”

Asked how much work he believed it would take to restore the image of safety on trains to previous levels, he said: “I think it’s really important that we consider what we do now.

“It’s incredibly rare for individuals to be involved in violence on the railway.

“Millions of passengers use the railway every single day.

“It is a safe and secure network, one that BTP manages every single day, working closely with partners.”
