Markets rallied on Friday after Brazilian President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva confirmed he had chosen a veteran banker to lead his finance ministry in 2027, a move seen as a sign of his commitment to fiscal discipline.

Brazil’s currency, the real, and its stock market had tumbled after Lula’s surprise victory over Flávio Bolsonaro in last month’s presidential election. Investors had been counting on a win by Bolsonaro, who had pledged to slash spending.

But the announcement late Thursday that Bradesco chief Luiz Carlos Trabuco had accepted Lula’s invitation to oversee the country’s finances during his upcoming fourth term, which begins Jan. 5, triggered a sharp rebound.

The real strengthened from 5.88 to the dollar on Wednesday to 5.53 on Friday morning, while stocks surged between Thursday evening and Friday morning.

The currency had already begun strengthening after Brazil’s lower house approved a government plan earlier this week to control spending and increase revenue. Although many investors considered the plan too timid, they viewed it as a step in the right direction.

The package has drawn criticism from some of Lula’s supporters, who accuse his government of bowing to market pressure. They see the measures as a threat to health, education and welfare spending, as well as to his campaign promises.

Ahead of the runoff, Lula unveiled costly initiatives, including a 15% increase in payments under his flagship welfare program, Bolsa Família, or Family Grant. That measure alone will cost 5.8 billion reais ($1 billion) this year and about 22 billion reais ($3.7 billion) next year, according to Brazilian government figures.

Other measures introduced by Lula’s administration include subsidized credit and debt-relief programs for small businesses and low-income earners, as well as temporary federal fuel tax cuts. The government has also proposed free public transportation on weekends, while Congress has passed legislation shortening the workweek without cutting pay.

Last week, students and left-wing activists protested against the fiscal package on São Paulo’s Brigadeiro Faria Lima Avenue, home to many banks and brokerages, and in Rio de Janeiro’s historic Candelária Square.
