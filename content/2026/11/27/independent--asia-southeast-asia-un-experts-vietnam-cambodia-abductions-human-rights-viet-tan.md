UN human rights experts have called on Cambodia and Vietnam to investigate the “transboundary abduction” and forced transfer of three human rights defenders from Cambodia to Vietnam.

The three activists – Nguyen Duc Thuan, a Norwegian citizen; Tran Hiep, an Australian citizen; and Thai Cong Truong Son, a Vietnamese citizen – disappeared in Cambodia on 18 September.

Their whereabouts remained unknown for 10 days before Vietnamese authorities announced they had been arrested and charged with terrorism offences.

They are reportedly being held in pretrial detention in Ho Chi Minch City.

Mr Thuan and Mr Hiep, who were involved with the pro-democracy group Viet Tan, had travelled to Cambodia on the morning of 18 September to meet fellow members of the organisation. Viet Tan is an international pro-democracy organisation designated a “terrorist” group by Vietnam in 2016.

UN experts said witness accounts contradicted official claims that the men had been arrested while crossing into Vietnam, raising questions about whether Cambodian officials had facilitated or allowed their alleged abduction and transfer.

They called for independent investigations by both countries.

In a statement, the UN experts said: “We are alarmed by these alleged transboundary abductions, which violate international human rights law, including the prohibitions on arbitrary detention, enforced disappearance and torture and cruel, inhuman and degrading treatment.”

They added: “Cambodia has an international obligation to investigate the men’s apparent enforced disappearance, including whether Cambodian officials knew of, facilitated, or tolerated their alleged arbitrary detention and transfer.

“Cambodia must protect everyone in its territory from human rights violations, including interference by foreign authorities,” the statement said.

“We urge Vietnam to disclose the circumstances and legal basis of the arrests, investigate any involvement of its officials in the alleged abductions, transfers and enforced disappearances and cooperate fully with Cambodia’s investigation,” the experts said.

They also questioned the basis of the terrorism charges. “This designation does not appear to be justified, or to meet the requirements of due process and judicial protection under international human rights law,” the experts said.

“Counter-terrorism measures must comply with international law and should not criminalise the peaceful exercise of human rights, including freedoms of expression, association, religion or belief, and the right to take part in public affairs.”

“We are concerned by the growth of transnational repression in the region and prevailing impunity surrounding it. We call on States to combat it, including through the Association of South East Asian Nations.”

The statement said: “All options must now be considered, including the formal invocation of Vietnam’s legal responsibility for internationally wrongful acts against our clients, with a formal demand for their release to end this human rights violation.”
