## The committee formally backed the bill, but six environmental groups say its objections should stop it in its tracks.

A committee of Italy's lower house has questioned whether key parts of the government-backed overhaul of the country's hunting law comply with EU rules, prompting six environmental and animal welfare groups to declare the reform dead.

The Chamber of Deputies' EU Policies Committee gave a formally favourable opinion on bill AC 2984, which rewrites Italy's 1992 hunting law. The committee's rapporteur, who drafted the opinion, was Stefano Candiani of Lega, part of Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni's governing coalition.

But the groups, ENPA, LAC, LAV, Legambiente, LIPU and WWF Italia, said the opinion was clearly negative in substance on essential points.

"The Chamber of Deputies' EU Policies Committee has issued a resounding and solemn rejection of the proposed hunting law, specifically on key points. The reform ends here," they said in a joint statement on Friday.

According to the groups, the committee cited concerns the European Commission had raised in a letter of 18 December 2025, and identified three main problems.

First, the capture and use of live birds as decoys is already criticised by the European Commission in an ongoing EU Pilot procedure, the Commission's informal step before possible infringement proceedings.

Second, allowing hunting on agritourism and hunting farms after the season ends appears to conflict with the EU's Habitats and Birds Directives.

Third, Article 11 of the bill appears to breach the Birds Directive on hunting during the pre-nuptial migration, when birds return to their breeding grounds.

The groups said the committee had asked for the text to be revised on live decoys, on hunting on farms after the season, on allowing regions to depart from the advice of ISPRA, Italy's national environmental research institute, and on extending the season beyond the first ten days of February. In their view, those provisions represent "the hunting world's greatest desires and, at the same time, the most flagrant violations of European law".

"It is unthinkable, at this point, that the Government, Parliament, and the relevant ministries would ignore it," they said.

The bill went before the full Chamber on Friday for its general debate, but detailed scrutiny and voting will not take place until January, La Stampa reported. The delay comes as the governing coalition remains divided over the bill.

Laura Cavandoli, a Lega MP and one of the bill's rapporteurs, told the Chamber that the committees' observations "do not rule out an amendment to the text", according to the Italian hunting magazine Caccia Magazine.

The pressure is also coming from within the coalition. Forza Italia, part of Meloni's coalition, has tabled 14 amendments that would, among other things, restrict where and when hunting is allowed and ban the use of live decoys, La Stampa reported.

Any change would send the bill back to the Senate, which passed it on 23 June. Caccia Magazine noted it was unclear whether there would be time, with the budget session, the last of the current parliament, coming first.
