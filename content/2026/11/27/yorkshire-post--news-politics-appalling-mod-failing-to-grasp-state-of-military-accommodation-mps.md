# ‘Appalling’: MoD failing to grasp state of military accommodation, MPs say

## The state of military accommodation is putting off recruits and pushing families out of the forces.

The true gravity of "appalling" housing conditions for the Armed Forces is not appreciated by the Government, a new report into military accommodation has found.

The investigation published today by the influential Public Accounts Committee of MPs finds that the Ministry of Defence needs to turn into a housing developer, yet it “severely lacks the skills and expertise”.

Satisfaction rates with service accommodation have dropped to 39 per cent in 2026, with the PAC saying much of it is in an “appalling condition”.

This is “unacceptably low, particularly given the impact of poor housing on service personnel's quality of life and the risks it poses to recruitment and retention”, it said.

A recent survey from the MoD, published in August, found around a third of the members of the Armed Forces said they lived in damp or mouldy barracks.

Catterick Garrison, near Richmond, is the largest British Army base in the world, and has a population of around 13,000 people.

Yet in recent years, reports have claimed that there have been issues with hot water and showering facilities at some of the barracks in the base.

The PAC put much of the current issues down to the “disastrous” 1996 sale of the service family accommodation (SFA) estate to Annington Homes.

This has now cost the taxpayer some £14.5m. Sir Geoffrey Clifton-Brown, chair of the PAC, said: “The decision to farm out the estate’s maintenance for short-term gain is at the root of the appalling housing conditions that our service men, women and their families have had to put up with, and our report makes clear that this happened in part due to a lack of financial expertise on the part of government.

“We therefore congratulate the MoD for finally getting out of this poor deal, which was carried out with all the skill of civil servants working at their best.

“The government must therefore be alive to the fact that it now has on its hands a gigantic estate that needs all the skills of a major property developer to bring to an acceptable condition for its occupants - skills which, with the best will in the world, the MoD currently does not possess.”

The MPs have called for the establishment of military housing standards, which makes public the quality of service accommodation.

They also say the MoD needs to publish its Defence Housing Strategy as soon as possible.

As defence secretary, John Healey made improving military accommodation one of his key aims, and pledged to spend £9bn over a decade on this, including building 100,000 new homes on surplus land.

However, earlier this year it was revealed that the MoD deferred almost £2bn that was due to be spent on refurbishing mouldy military barracks to the next Parliament.

This includes £800m that was due to be spent on SFA, which is subsidised housing for service men and women’s families, and £950m for single living accommodation (SLA).

Last month, current Defence Secretary Wes Streeting said the Armed Forces were being asked to “live within their means”.

He told the Commons that there were historic issues of “routine overspending” that had required “reprioritisation”.

An MoD spokesperson said: “We expect a lot better for those families who make enormous sacrifices and experience a lot of disruption for what they do, and we are determined to deliver the standard of housing our Armed Forces and their families deserve.

“That is why we brought more than 36,000 homes back into public ownership and launched a £9.2bn, 10-year Defence Housing Strategy, to deliver the biggest transformation of military housing in more than 50 years, including modernising nine in ten forces’ family homes.”
