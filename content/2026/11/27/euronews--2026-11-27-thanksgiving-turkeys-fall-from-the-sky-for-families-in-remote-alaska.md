In south-central Alaska, two friends are taking to the skies to deliver the traditional holiday meal to families living in remote wilderness communities.

For the past few years, pilot Heidi Hastings and her friend Esther Keim have carried out the annual operation. While Hastings flies the aircraft, Keim drops the turkeys through the plane's window.

Some of the communities they reach are completely off the grid, around 50 miles from the nearest road. With snow and ice making access difficult, residents can struggle to get supplies, including a turkey for Thanksgiving.

Known as the “turkey bombers”, the two women have turned the flights into a yearly tradition, making sure families in some of Alaska's most remote areas can also enjoy a Thanksgiving meal.
