An Arabic-language news channel has lost its UK broadcasting licence after livestreaming a speech by the Houthi leader Abdul Malik al-Houthi.

Ofcom found that Lualua TV was “not fit and proper” to hold the licence after it broadcast a 45-minute speech by the Houthi leader on its website, which Ofcom found glorified antisemitic hate speech.

The regulator began its investigation in October 2026 after viewing an English translation of the speech by al-Houthi, which it said featured a banner on screen with the statement: “God is Greater. Death to America. Death to Israel. Curse the Jews. Victory to Islam.”

The regulator's report states that he repeatedly characterised the October 7 2023 attack on Israel by Hamas in positive terms.

Referring to the attacks carried out by Hamas, he described them as an “important, great and blessed operation”, a “historical milestone”, a “major leap forward”, and a “successful, powerful, meticulously planned, and expertly executed operation”.

He also expressed support for Hezbollah, a proscribed terrorist group.

Ofcom said it considers that the speech as livestreamed contained material that constituted hate speech and abusive and derogatory treatment towards Jewish people.

![The regulator’s decision comes after a 45-minute livestream on the channel’s website of a speech by Abdul Malik al-Houthi](https://static.independent.co.uk/2024/01/24/08/11099221-1.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The regulator requested a copy of the livestream from Shell For Media Productions.

It identified differences between the first recording provided to Ofcom and the full version of the livestream, including that the banner had either been removed or obscured.

It stated: “Upon review Ofcom identified significant gaps in the material supplied, including recordings whose durations were substantially shorter than the periods indicated by their filenames and several periods for which no recordings had been provided.”

Ofcom also raised concerns about posts on the channel's social media accounts which it felt encouraged support for “proscribed terrorist organisations”.

The regulator also noted social media posts by the channel which expressed support for Hezbollah leaders.

Lualua TV is primarily focused on Bahrain.

It is available to view in Britain via livestream on its website, and its licence was provided by Shells For Media Productions Limited.

Although Lualua TV did not broadcast on British TV, the removal of its licence means it will not be able to commence broadcasting in the UK under that licence in the future.

It does not prevent the continued livestream of Lualua TV to audiences online.

Cristina Nicolotti Squires, Ofcom's broadcasting and media group director, said: "Freedom of expression is something we guard fiercely in this country, and the bar for action against broadcasters is rightly set very high."

“Following a detailed investigation and taking account of a wide range of evidence, we have concluded that Lualua TV is not fit and proper to hold a UK broadcasting licence, and so have revoked it with immediate effect.”

Shell For Media Productions could not be reached for comment.
