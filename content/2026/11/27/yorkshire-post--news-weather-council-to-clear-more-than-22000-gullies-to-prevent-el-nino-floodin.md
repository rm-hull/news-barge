# Council to clear more than 22,000 gullies to prevent El Niño flooding

## Wakefield Council plans to clean more than 22,000 gullies as part of efforts to prevent flooding caused by El Niño, a meeting heard.

Councillors were told the authority’s highways department had stepped up its drain cleansing and road sweeping targets due to “the increased risk” caused by the predicted weather event.

The council’s regeneration and communities scrutiny committee heard the measures were also deemed necessary due to this year’s lack of rainfall.

Joe Jenkinson, the council service director for planning, transportation and highways, said: “Because we have had such a dry summer you can get a build up of debris, and obviously with the El Niño and potential storms during the winter we want to make sure the gullies and drains are in a decent condition.

![A council worker cleaning a gully](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOjk3ODVhNTk4LWVjZTctNGQyYy04OTI4LTFkODQ2N2M4ODk2Yzo2NmZmZjRjOC1lNzM4LTQ5OTUtOWE4Yy1hODU5ZGIxOTA4YTI=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


“So there has been a demonstrable increase in gully cleansing and that will remain the focus.”

A report to committee members also stated: “Two thirds of highway drainage schemes are complete and we have revised upwards our target for gully cleansing.

“It was originally to cleanse 5,413 gullies but, given the increased risk of a wet winter (due to El Niño), we have already cleansed 13,702 and plan to cleanse a further 8,598.”

The council is responsible maintaining around 67,000 gullies across the district.

As part of an ongoing maintenance programme, at least a third are cleaned each year, with known flood hotspots cleaned twice per year and main roads cleaned once per year.

Earlier this month, the council said the number of mechanical road sweepers operating across the district would be doubled to 12 and the number of gully cleansing vehicles increased from three to five in preparation for severe weather.

A statement released at the time said: “With very little rainfall over the summer, leaves, silt and other debris have built up on roads and in gullies.

“Road sweepers remove leaves, silt and debris before they can enter the drainage system.

“That helps to keep gullies working effectively while also improving the appearance of our streets.

“One sweeper recently collected more than 26 tonnes of leaves and debris from a five-mile stretch of road in a single week, highlighting the scale of the challenge at this time of year.

“While no drainage system can completely prevent flooding during periods of extreme weather, regular maintenance helps the district to be prepared for wet weather and keeps the network operating as efficiently as possible.”
