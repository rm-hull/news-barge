The Department for Work and Pensions has taken some services offline after “identifying a cyber incident affecting our systems”.

In a statement, the DWP said it was working with GCHQ’s National Cyber Security Centre and the Government Cyber Coordination Centre to investigate the incident.

It said it had taken some services offline “as a precaution” with users unable to login to their accounts to carry out tasks such a checking pensions or making a benefits claim.

The department, which is responsible for administering the welfare system, said regular benefit and pension payments have not been affected and will be paid as normal.

It reminded users that the department will never ask them for bank details or to click on links.

![](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/27/8/45/The-DWP-login-page-on-Friday-morning.png?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“We have taken some DWP online services offline as a precaution after we identified a cyber incident affecting our systems,” a statement published on Friday said.

“We are working closely with the National Cyber Security Centre and Government Cyber Coordination Centre to investigate and to bring services back online as quickly as possible and to minimise any disruption.

“Regular benefit and pension payments are not affected and will be paid as normal.

“If an online service you need is unavailable, please try again later. As always, we will never message you to ask for bank details or ask you to click on links.”

Those attempting to login to their DWP accounts on Friday morning were met with an error message following the incident.

The department has not confirmed the nature of the cyber incident or whether personal data has been accessed.

![The Government is forecast to spend £32.1 billion on personal independence payments in 2026/27](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/23/14/23140852-4891c169-6864-4557-9e65-bd88ee039eab.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Work and Pensions Committee Chair Debbie Abrahams said DWP’s decision to take “swift action” was “absolutely the right thing to do”.

She said: “That today’s ongoing cyber-incident is not affecting benefit and pension payments is a great relief. The DWP has taken swift action to contain any potential further damage from the cyber-incident and so that service is restored as soon as possible.

“Although this may interrupt some services this was absolutely the right thing to do.

“It’s critical that the impact on service users from the outage is investigated and analysed so that timely and accurate advice can be given to them.”

It comes amid increased concern over cyber threats facing the country.

Earlier this month, the UK’s financial regulators have proposed new guidance to help financial services firms prepare for and recover from cyber incidents, amid concerns that attacks are becoming more frequent, complex and dangerous.

Dr Richard Horne, National Cyber Security Centre (NCSC) chief executive, said at the time: “We know cyber threats are becoming increasingly frequent and sophisticated, making it more important than ever that organisations can withstand and recover from cyber incidents.

“The National Cyber Security Centre welcomes this proposed guidance, which will help firms better understand, assess and strengthen their cyber resilience, in line with our Cyber Assessment Framework.”

It comes as top British defence businesses have been told to urgently step up their security arrangements during secret briefings with MI5 and other agencies who are warning that the country is under attack from Russia and that their staff risk assassination.

The warnings, being given to the chief executives of Britain’s industries in the “critical national infrastructure”, are being delivered by ministers and intelligence chiefs.

Andy Ward, from security systems company Absolute Security, said: “We are seeing a concerning pattern of cyberattacks that go beyond financial gain, with criminals targeting the services which people rely on daily. Prolonged downtime of these services may have serious consequences to users and unfortunately, this incident is just another reminder of the growing cyber threats businesses are facing today.”
