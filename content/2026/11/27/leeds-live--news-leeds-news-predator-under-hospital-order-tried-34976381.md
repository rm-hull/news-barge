# Predator was under hospital order when he tried to meet teenage girl for sex

## Luke Rees has appeared in court

A predator who left a secure hospital to meet a teenage girl for sex has narrowly avoided prison.

Luke Rees, 37, was the subject of a hospital order and was on the low-secure ward at the medium-secure Cygnet Hospital in Stevenage when he tried to meet up with the 14-year-old at Headingley Train Station.

Unbeknown to Rees, however, the profile he had been messaging for three months actually was the creation of a member of the Angels of Innocence group.

Prosecutor Zanisha Herbert told Leeds Crown Court on Friday, November 27: "The group is involved in tracking, locating and exposing those who use social media to prey on children."

The profile, under the name "Amelia," received a friend request from Rees, who began talking to it.

Ms Herbert told the court: "He began to chat and called her gorgeous. She told him she was 14. The defendant continued with the conversation, which soon turned sexual. It then progressed from Facebook to WhatsApp. He had sent messages to her saying she was 'cute' and he loved her. He called her 'babe' and referred to her as 'little wifey' and said he wanted her to be his girlfriend and said he would love to see her naked.

"He told her he wanted to shower with her and have intercourse with her and described contraception, unprotected sex and pregnancy. He sent video messages of him dancing and planned and communicated that they were to meet."

In September last year, Rees attended at Headingley Train Station to meet "Amelia," but was instead met by members of the Angels of Innocence group before he was arrested.

Rees' phone was seized by officers and demonstrated an "interest in young children."

The court heard he had a search engine designed to keep searches private installed on his phone.

Mitigating, Ian Hudson, told the court a pre-sentence report and psychological report had been prepared for Rees, who had been convicted of attempting to engage in sexual communication with a child and attempting to meet a girl after grooming.

The court heard he was placed on a hospital order in 2015 after being convicted of arson.

Mr Hudson said: "It is right to say he was made subject to the hospital order with restrictions...He still remains under those terms. It is the Secretary of State that will have to decide if he is safe to be released."

Recorder Jason Pitter made Rees the subject of a 20-month sentence suspended for 18-months. During that time he must complete a probation requirement. He was also made the subject of a sexual harm prevention order.

The judge told Rees: "You're 37 and have a lengthy history for offences, not directly relevant to offences of this type. In 2015, you were subject to a hospital order with restrictions and you're still operating under them. These offences took place while you were under that hospital order."
