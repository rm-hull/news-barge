The son of a British mother languishing in an Iranian prison has hit out at the government’s “wait and see” approach as she spends another birthday behind bars.

Lindsay Foreman and her husband Craig were detained in January 2025 as they travelled through the country on a motorbike tour and later sentenced to 10 years on spying charges in what their family describe as a “sham trial”.

The British government has said it is obvious the couple are innocent tourists but has refused to declare that they are being arbitrarily detained.

In an open letter to foreign secretary Ed Miliband as Lindsay turns 54 on Friday inside Tehran’s notorious Evin prison, her son Joe Bennett has demanded answers.

“My mother has endured prison conditions no British citizen should have to imagine,” he wrote. “She and my step-father Craig have been sentenced to 10 years in Iran on false spying charges. They have faced serious health concerns, long stretches without contact with home, and the terror of conflict reaching the prison where they are held.

“Yet when I ask Britain to formally recognise their detention as arbitrary, I am told there are risks. Risks of what, exactly? And how do they compare with the risks they face every day, held in that cell in Evin prison?”

![Lindsay and Craig Foreman have spent nearly two years detained in Iran](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/03/26/06/973dee7819c3e2fd326e96c6c7120bb8Y29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzc0NTY4NTM5-2.81377771.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Mr Bennett posed the same question to Mr Miliband in a meeting a month ago, but is yet to receive a response.

He challenged the government’s position given it has recently ramped up diplomatic tensions with Iran, including designating the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps as a national security threat under new legislation and condemning a string of state-backed attacks in Britain.

The government has taken “firm action” against Iran on matters of national security but is “yet to explain why formally challenging the detention of two of its citizens, supposedly carries unacceptable additional risk”, Mr Bennett said.

“The cost of continuing the present wait-and-see approach is not zero,” he added. Lindsay and Craig are still recovering from spending 84 and 93 days respectively on hunger strike over their treatment, which left them so weak it was described as a “medical emergency” by the UN.

![The couple spent months on hunger strike earlier this year](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/06/05/17/01KS2K2A14S61Y91T2SERRW490.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

In an appeal to Mr Miliband, Mr Bennett continued: “For almost two years, caution has been presented as protection. But caution is also a choice, with consequences.

“If the government believes its present course gives Lindsay and Craig the best prospect of coming home, it should be able to explain why, at least in principle. If it believes a different course would endanger them, it should explain what evidence supports that conclusion. It has done neither.

“There is a particular cruelty in watching people you love lose years of their lives while the debate about what might happen next remains theoretical and unresolved. Mum and Craig do not have the luxury of waiting for a risk-free moment. No such moment may exist. For us, every unanswered question becomes another day in which uncertainty is mistaken for progress.”

He concluded: “Britain has shown it is prepared to confront Iran more generally. Now please explain why it will not take these steps for its citizens, my mother and Craig.”

![Joe Bennett, son of Lindsay Foreman, has appealed to foreign secretary Ed Miliband](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/28/07/956ee7027b087aaf5b81ee29c435531bY29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzkzMjU3MDUw-2.84684319.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Earlier this year, *The Independent* revealed that Iran is seeking the return of an Iranian national who has spent 23 years in a maximum-security British prison, as the couple urged British officials to consider an exchange.

Mr Foreman believes that Richard Jan, an Iranian stalker held in Britain, could be the “only avenue” to negotiating their release after his case was raised by their jailers in a consular meeting.

Jan’s incarceration was also raised in negotiations for the return of British-Iranian mother Nazanin Zaghari-Ratcliffe, who was detained for six years in Iran before she was finally freed after the British government settled a £400m arms debt with the country.

A UK government spokesperson insisted there was “no truth whatsoever to the claims of a potential exchange arrangement”. However, former deputy prime minister David Lammy admitted that “arrangements can be made” when he was questioned about the issue on Sky News.

A Foreign Office spokesperson said: “Craig and Lindsay’s health and welfare is a top priority. Since their arrest last year, Britain’s ambassador to Tehran, along with diplomats and officials in London, have been working to provide consular assistance. This includes the ambassador visiting them in prison.

“On 27 October, the family of Craig and Lindsay met with the foreign secretary, alongside the minister for the Middle East, Stephen Doughty, and the UK’s Envoy for Complex Consular Detentions, Alistair Burt. The foreign secretary set out how unjustified and appalling we consider Lindsay and Craig’s incarceration to be, and the action that the UK government is taking to try to secure their release.”
