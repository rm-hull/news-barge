A group of organised criminals are believed to have carried out robberies during Don Toliver’s Manchester concert, where an “unknown substance” was released, police have said, with similar incidents having occurred at another two of the US rapper’s gigs in Europe.

Greater Manchester police said they were working to identify the suspects and the substance involved during the incident at the Co-op Live Arena on Sunday evening.

No arrests have been made but mobile phone and CCTV footage is being reviewed by officers, a police statement said. The force is working with other law enforcement agencies across Europe after similar incidents at two Toliver gigs, which officers believe are “relevant” to their inquiries.

Eighty-nine people displayed symptoms believed to be linked to exposure to the substance during the performance in Manchester. The venue was evacuated and a major incident declared as those in the crowd described a “horrible smell”, which police initially believed to be teargas.

![Armed police officers stand guard behind security tape outside Co-op Live at night](https://i.guim.co.uk/img/media/ab539c22f62a96794ceb38ace1bf1aeb5e81f23e/0_0_5697_3798/master/5697.jpg?width=445&dpr=1&s=none&crop=none)

Footage shared on social media appeared to show concertgoers coughing and covering their mouths as they left the venue, and medical officials urged anyone who had become unwell after leaving to get in touch. Some of the dozens assessed by doctors reported eye and respiratory symptoms.

Tom Harding, Greater Manchester police assistant chief constable, said: “We’re working to identify those suspects and the substance that we believe was released in the crowd as part of these incidents. All of this work is ongoing at pace.

“There are similar incidents at two Don Toliver gigs in Europe that are also relevant to our inquiries. We’re in contact with law enforcement involved in those investigations.

“No arrests have been made, but our detectives are working through footage captured on mobile phones and CCTV. There are over a 100 people at the gig we are working to try to contact to assist with our inquiries. We thank all those that have assisted us so far.”

Harding said the force was aware some people had been affected psychologically during the Co-op Live incident because of the 2017 terror attack at the Manchester Arena where 22 people were killed by the suicide-bomber Salman Abedi.

He added: “We also know many more people were impacted on the night and afterwards, physically and psychologically. What happened on Sunday night and the imagery on our TVs and online will bring back painful memories of 2017.

“Thankfully, we are not dealing with an incident of such a devastating nature or motive; however, we understand the impact this is likely to have caused.”
