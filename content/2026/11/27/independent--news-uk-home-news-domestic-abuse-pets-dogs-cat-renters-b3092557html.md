Individuals escaping domestic abuse encounter major obstacles to reaching safety when perpetrators exploit pets to exert coercive control and the animals cannot be housed with them, a charity specialist has cautioned.

Look Ahead, a domestic abuse charity operating across London and the South East, supported more than 1,000 women, men and families fleeing abuse over the past year. Across Kent, the organisation provided housing for over 10 pets, including dogs, cats, guinea pigs, rats and a tortoise.

The charity is organising a community walk this Friday to highlight the ways abusers manipulate animals and to stress the necessity of safe refuge that accepts pets alongside their owners.

Organiser Rebecca Swaine highlighted that although one of their refuges accepts animals, this remains "quite rare", with very few temporary housing options prepared to take pets.

She said: “We identified that the need for safe accommodation, especially for those who are in the process of fleeing domestic abuse and being able to take their pets with them, is actually quite challenging.

“There are very few providers of temporary accommodation that allow you to take your pet, and leaving your pet behind is like leaving a member of the family.

“It isn’t as easy as just walking out of a door to leave abuse, there’s a lot of considerations, and our pets are members of the family, so we believe no-one should get left behind.”

She urged private landlords to consider taking someone seeking safety and their pets.

Of the Renters’ Rights Act on pets, she said: “I don’t think it’s gone far enough for those who are fleeing abuse.

“Often it’s immediate, it’s urgent and if you haven’t got somewhere safe to go, then people stay.”

Under the new laws introduced earlier this year, tenants must request in writing if they want to keep a pet, and landlords have 28 days to respond and cannot unreasonably refuse.

Ms Swaine, who has worked in supporting survivors for 17 years, said threatening to harm animals or put them on platforms to rehome them are some of the “many different ways” animals are used by abusers as a form of coercion.

“We’ve also seen the opposite, where abusers will continually buy people pets, and actually (it’s) really hard for you to then find accommodation when you’ve now got three, four, five dogs or cats,” she said.

“We also know a lot of people leaving their pets behind is a huge traumatic event for them. What’s going to happen to that pet when they’re left behind?”

Ms Swaine said in one example, an abuser had a large breed dog who was trained to attack, and the victim was not allowed to know what the attack word was.

“They never knew that this animal in the house was going to cause them harm or not,” she said.

“That instils fear completely at all times.”

“Anyone living in fear, no-one deserves to be in that situation.”

Look Ahead’s Kent and Medway domestic abuse services will raise awareness of barriers survivors can face seeking safety during the Walk Away From Abuse event on Friday.

The walk will start at Calverley Park in Tunbridge Wells at 1.30pm, with free treats for pets and “doggy bandanas”, and end at Town and Country Housing.

Key discussions will be held around how to get support, how domestic abuse affects the whole family, and how no-one should have to choose between living in safety and their pet.

Ms Swaine added: “Domestic abuse thrives in silence. If you’re living with an abuser, or you’re experiencing abuse, or you’re concerned about somebody, please reach out.

“It’s not your fault, but there are people there to listen.”

The 24-hour National Domestic Abuse Helpline can be reached for free on 0808 2000 247.
