Dutch authorities ​seized 26 critically endangered mud turtles at Amsterdam’s Schiphol airport last week, the Dutch Food and Consumer ⁠Product Safety Authority (NVWA) said on Friday.

The Vallarta turtles were brought in from Mexico, where a mere 300 of them are believed to remain in the wild.

Vallarta turtles are one of the world’s smallest turtle species, and have been linked with illegal trade due to their considerable financial value.

According to the NVWA, the turtles were hidden in the checked baggage of ​a ⁠man who was travelling ‌to Hong Kong.

The man was subsequently arrested, while further investigations are ongoing.

Most of the turtles were packed inside of a box alongside a toy car, while a handful of others were placed inside of a sealed sock.

The turtles had been wrapped in duct tape to prevent them from moving.

![View of Vallarta mud turtles (Kinosternon Vogti) in a laboratory of the Guadalajara Univertsity, in Puerto Vallarta, Jalisco State, on June 9, 2018](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/27/10/971756568..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

All of the turtles survived and were taken to a specialist animal rescue centre, the NVWA said.

According to the US-based ⁠Center for Biological Diversity, Vallarta mud turtles are sold for large sums in China.

“Vallarta mud turtles sell for up to $30,000 each in China, providing a major financial incentive that drives illegal trade,” it said in a statement last month, calling on ‌the Mexican ​government to do ‌more to protect the ​animals.

“Experts believe wildlife authorities only seize a small portion of illegal traded wildlife.”

A similar incident occurred in December 2025 at Los Angeles International Airport, the Center said, where “approximately 20 Vallarta mud turtles bound for Asia” were seized.

Based on US Fish and Wildlife Service data, Tara Easter, a University of Michigan doctoral candidate who has studied the trade, previously estimated the commercial export trade for mud turtles in the United States increased from 1,844 in 1999 to nearly 40,000 in 2017.
