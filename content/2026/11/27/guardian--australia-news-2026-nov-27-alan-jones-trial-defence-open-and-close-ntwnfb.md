Alan Jones’s defence in his indecent assault and sexual touching trial has lasted only a few hours, with closing submissions in the long-running case due next week.

Jones’s barrister Gabrielle Bashir SC opened the defence case on Friday by stating, “I am not calling the defendant,” ending months of speculation about whether the former broadcaster, 85, would give evidence.

Jones has pleaded not guilty to 19 indecent assault and two sexual touching charges brought by five men who allege the offences took place between 2003 and 2020.

On Friday afternoon, after a break for the crown to read the defence submissions, Bashir presented two medical reports and a restaurant receipt, along with a corresponding bank statement.

After half an hour of objections to the documents by the crown, Bashir said: **“** Your honour, that closes the defence.”

No witnesses were called on Friday although some defence witnesses were called by the crown during the four-month trial.

The prosecutor Georgina Wright objected to the restaurant documents on the basis they carried no weight “whatsoever” – but judge Glenn Walsh admitted them.

Bashir argued the documents were relevant to the “credibility and reliability” of complainant C, who alleged he was assaulted by Jones at an end of year lunch at a north shore restaurant.

The alleged victim, a former driver for Jones, earlier told the court that Jones had instructed him to sit next to him and then spent the entire lunch with his left hand under the table either on his leg or on his penis over his pants.

The defence has argued the end of year lunch took place at a different north shore restaurant and presented a receipt for that establishment for $1,247.

Jason Morrison, a former 2GB colleague of Jones, earlier told the court he recalled a different name of the restaurant to complainant C and other witnesses.

“This has been a big trial,” Walsh said. “There are something like 290 or so exhibits and about 5,000-odd pages worth of transcripts.”

The judge gave the parties four days to prepare closing submissions, which are scheduled to begin on Thursday.

The judge-only trial has sat for 70 days at Sydney’s Downing Centre local court and the defendant has been present for all the evidence. Jones has sat in the front row next to his solicitor Bryan Wrench.

The youngest complainant, known as complainant A, alleged that Jones touched him on the bottom when he stayed overnight at Jones’s Fitzroy Falls farm when he was 17.

The trial has heard from a prominent athlete, known as complainant M, who told the court Jones would welcome him with a hug and a kiss on the cheek and use flattering terms like “champion” and “beautiful man”.

M told the court the defendant kissed him and pressed his genitalia against his thigh causing him to shout out “OK mate” and flee Jones’s harbourside apartment. He alleged that on another occasion Jones kissed him on the side of his mouth at an awards night.

Jones has denied all wrongdoing and said the allegations were either baseless or distorted the truth.

Bashir has said the alleged victims were egged on to complain by media rivals, and that their reliability and credibility were questionable.

Media rivals, Bashir alleged in her opening statements, included shock jock Ray Hadley and Sydney Morning Herald columnist Peter FitzSimons.
