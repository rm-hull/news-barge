# Farm on the M62: ​Tractors make light of my restive season - Jill Thorp

## It’s that time of year again when the shops are filled with every conceivable festive biscuit, chocolate and party platter imaginable. Shop windows are quick to advertise the coming of Christmas and the second the ghosts, wizards and witches of Halloween have vanished and pumpkins are rotting away in gardens, the shelves are filled with selection packs, tinsel and chocolate reindeers.

Christmas has spread and crept like ivy, now occupying November, impatiently lurking around bonfire night, desperate to fill every TV advert, glossy magazine and supermarket shelf. I stubbornly refuse to allow it in until we’re at least in December, despite the twinkling lights now springing up in every village and house window. The Christmas tractor runs, which I of course look forward to have also arrived early this year. The annual parade of the Holme Valleys finest tractors, from the cab-less David Brown 880 to the huge all singing and all dancing John Deere 6140, are the absolute highlight of the Christmas lights celebration passing through Holmfirth and surrounding villages. I’m always filled with overwhelming pride when they appear, bedecked in a sparkling array of multi-coloured lights, driven by the most hardworking and passionate people I know. The streets are crammed; people wrapped up warm against the cold, rosy cheeks beneath woolly hats, children bouncing for joy as the farmers wave from their tractors, horns blaring. Despite it being in November, it really does signal the beginning of the festive celebrations and feels like a necessary step away from the overwhelming commercialism seen in the big towns and cities. It’s community spirit at its best. Following the parade, we headed up to the Wills O’Nats pub for some singing, a long held tradition in these parts but more poignantly to remember Paul’s brother, who was not only a passionate supporter of our local foot packs but a talented singer. Both Paul and his youngest brother have every known hunting song stored in their heads and will happily stand in front of a roaring fire and silence a pub with their voices. It turned out to be a superb night with some of West Yorkshires finest filling the old pub.

It was a bitterly cold night that greeted us as we spilled out from the warmth of the pub; dense, freezing fog making our drive home a painfully slow one. Boo, our Teckel had of course sat the fire out so we all headed to bed with “Drink puppy drink” still ringing in our ears.

The following evening, the cattle wagon decided to give up the ghost at the end of a long, cold and tiring day. Just as Paul was making the right turn onto our drive, it died. Shuddering to a halt it stretched across the narrow lane, the trailer hitched behind blocking the entire road. We unhitched the trailer which set off at an alarming pace down the road with Paul frantically trying to steer it into a passing place. He eventually parked it up, of a fashion and then headed up to the yard to get the tractor, a string of expletives lingering in the frosty air. Thankfully there was no traffic and we managed to tow it off the road and up to the cow shed where it sits abandoned whilst we wait for a new alternator!
