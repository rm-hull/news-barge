Britons are growing unhappier and sicker at a faster rate than almost any other major European nation, new global rankings have revealed.

The UK plunged from 13th to 26th place for happiness in the Global Healthy Ageing and Prevention Index, published by the International Longevity Centre (ILC) think tank.

Across all 147 countries assessed, Britain slipped from 20th overall in 2019 to 22nd in the latest edition, having been overtaken by Japan.

The index ranks nations on six equally weighted measures: life span, healthy years lived, secure employment, income, clean air and happiness. Its findings draw primarily on comparable international data from 2023, alongside happiness figures averaged across 2023 to 2025.

![Britain has tumbled down in global rankings for happiness, health and job security](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/27/15/2242196210..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The report suggests Britain’s worsening health and sliding morale, at least in part, reflects the toll of a series of short-lived governments in the past decade.

While the UK saw modest improvements in air quality – rising two places to 18th – its overall standing was pulled down by deteriorating health, stagnant living standards and falling job security.

Britain’s overall ranking now trails four fellow G7 nations: Germany, Japan, Canada and France. Only the United States, in 25th, and Italy, in 26th, sit lower among the group.

Average UK life expectancy fell by six months between 2019 and 2023, dropping from 81.5 years to 81 years and pushing Britain down two places to 27th globally. By comparison, the European average edged up by 0.1 years over the same period.

Healthy life expectancy, which adjusts life span for the severity of illness and disability, saw an even sharper contraction. It fell by 1.1 years from 69.4 to 68.3 years, dropping the UK five spots to 33rd in one of the steepest declines recorded across 31 European countries.

![Life expectancy in the country has seen a concerning backslide](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/27/16/01M7YHYS1721JPESJHK5Z9J7XP.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Job security also slipped in Britain, with the UK’s secure employment rate edging down from 52.6 per cent in 2019 to 52.3 per cent, dropping the country three places to 31st globally.

The newly introduced measure tracks the share of the population in stable, contracted roles rather than precarious or informal work, which researchers say is closely tied to financial security and better health in later life

But the report warns that simply spending more on healthcare is no guarantee of better outcomes, noting that the UK devotes 11 per cent of GDP to health yet ranks well below the Netherlands, which sits in 10th place while spending 9.8 per cent.

Ben Franklin, deputy chief executive of the ILC and author of the report, said governments were failing to turn promises on preventative healthcare into real-world results.

“While governments talk about taking a prevention-first approach, we are seeing little evidence that this is feeding through into healthy ageing outcomes,” he said. “The UK has suffered a particularly severe decline in health over the period – but it is not alone.”

Mr Franklin warned that unless work, welfare and health systems adapt to help people with long-term conditions stay active, rising rates of chronic illness risk “crippling economies and public finances in the process”.

Elsewhere in the index, Norway claimed first place overall ahead of Iceland and Switzerland, while Finland, Iceland, Denmark, Costa Rica and Sweden topped the happiness standings.

India recorded the biggest overall jump of any nation, climbing 12 places from 117th to 105th on the back of a 25-place surge in happiness, while Ukraine and Libya suffered the steepest falls.

Across all 153 countries tracked since 2019, the proportion of life spent in less than full health widened in every single nation, rising from 13.7 per cent to 14.3 per cent – a larger increase in four years than across the previous three decades.
