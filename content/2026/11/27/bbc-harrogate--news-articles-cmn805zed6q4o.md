Mayor David Skaith's trip to India last month was part of a wider effort to boost trade and investment

**The cost to North Yorkshire taxpayers of a mayoral trade mission to India was more than £85,000, recently released figures show.**

David Skaith and eight staff from the York and North Yorkshire Combined Authority (YNYCA) were accompanied by 12 external delegates on the trip to Hyderabad and Mumbai last month.

The combined authority spent a total of £85,102 on flights, stays at five-star hotels, and meals on the trip, a Freedom of Information (FOI) request has revealed.

The Labour mayor said that York and North Yorkshire was already "seeing new relationships emerging between businesses, universities and potential investors".

The 200-person trade mission by Northern political leaders, businesses, universities and industry representatives sought to strengthen trade links following the new UK-India free trade agreement.

Other Yorkshire mayors, including South Yorkshire's Oliver Coppard and West Yorkshire's Tracy Brabin, also accompanied their own delegations to the country.

The FOI request, submitted by the Local Democracy Reporting Service (LDRS), revealed the delegation, led by the mayor, spent £34,707 on accommodation at a cost of around £1,600 per person for 10 nights.

Skaith, who went to try and secure a share of what was described as a potential £170m annual economic boost for the region, stayed at the luxury Taj hotels chain during the visit.

Travel, including economy class flights with Virgin Atlantic and internal flights with low-cost airline IndiGo, cost the YNYCA £27,122 in total.

The third largest expense category was national and regional contributions at £12,333, while professional fees amounted to £5,000 and incidental expenses £2,443.

The mayors of South, West and North Yorkshire travelled to India

Skaith said that the free trade agreement between the UK and India, which came into force in July had "opened the door for northern mayors to form direct links with one of the world's fastest-growing economies."

He said York and North Yorkshire "was already seeing the benefits" of it, "with new relationships emerging between businesses, universities and potential investors".

The mayor previously said the visit could help the sale of fertiliser created on the North Yorkshire coast and help to boost crop yields globally.

A spokesperson for the combined authority said: "The trade mission has been hugely positive for our county.

"This is the first time that York and North Yorkshire has been able to undertake such a mission.

"We will continue to report on the benefits as they develop further."
