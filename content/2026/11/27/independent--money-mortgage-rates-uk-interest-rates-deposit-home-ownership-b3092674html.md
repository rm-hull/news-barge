When it comes to hurdles home buyers face, saving for a house deposit is right up there as a significant headache, making affordability for some consumers near impossible.

According to the Building Societies Association’s (BSA) property tracker report in October, 59 per cent of 2,005 adults polled said raising a deposit is most likely to stop them buying a residential property.

Sarah Harrison, chief executive of the BSA, says saving a deposit remains one of the biggest hurdles for first-time buyers. But she adds that for those that are able to save a little more, “it can make a real difference to monthly mortgage repayments”.

Now, new research shared with *The Independent* sheds some light on just how much borrowers could save on their monthly repayments if they are able to sweeten their deposit figure.

The figures are particularly relevant after fresh Bank of England data showed many more households than expected are set to suffer big hikes in mortgage repayments starting in 2027.

## How much cheaper are mortgage repayments with a bigger deposit?

Estate agency Yopa did the analysis and chief executive Verona Frankish says it shows buyers who are able to significantly boost their deposit amount can potentially reduce their mortgage repayments by more than £20,000 over five years.

That is by increasing their deposit from 5 per cent to 20 per cent on the average UK property.

As Frankish puts it though, finding an additional £40,000 is no small undertaking.

But even for those who can make more modest increase in deposit size, a meaningful difference is still possible.

Frankish says increasing a deposit from 5 per cent to 10 per cent could reduce monthly mortgage repayments by around £175, equivalent to more than £10,000 over five years.

Much of this reduction comes from borrowing less in the first place, rather than securing a better interest rate alone, adds Frankish.

“For those in a position to save a little more before purchasing, understanding the different mortgage thresholds could help them secure a more manageable monthly repayment and put themselves on a stronger financial footing for the years ahead.”

The table above uses suggested rates, while real-life rates may of course vary. The average rate for a five-year fix across all lenders on the market on 27 November is 6 per cent, per Moneyfacts.

## Current mortgage market conditions

Mortgage rates heading toward the end of 2026 remain higher than borrowers have been familiar with in recent years.

Take the average two-year fix on 27 November, which is 5.99 per cent - compared with 5.39 per cent and 2.43 per cent for the same month in 2024 and 2020 respectively, Moneyfacts data shows.

Daniel Clarke, founder of Homebuyer.Agency says the size of a deposit has become an increasingly important factor in determining the overall affordability of a home.

![House price isn’t the only factor to consider - there are legal fees, stamp duty and more to pay too afterwards](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/27/11/13/iStock-2273219547.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

He explains: “Lenders generally reserve their most competitive rates for borrowers with lower loan-to-value ratios, so increasing your deposit enough to cross into a lower mortgage band can potentially deliver a double benefit.

“Not only are you borrowing less, but you could also qualify for a more competitive rate on the money you do need to borrow.”

However he says buyers shouldn't automatically assume that putting every available penny into their deposit is the right approach.

Ranald Mitchell, a director at mortgage brokers Charwin Private Clients, agrees. “Legal fees, moving costs, repairs and unexpected bills still need paying, and many buyers want money left for decorating or improving the property once they move in,” he points out.

“The sweet spot is usually the deposit that gets you into the next meaningful LTV band while still leaving a sensible cash buffer. The right LTV bracket can save you thousands, but becoming mortgage-rich and cash-poor is rarely a good trade.”

## First time buyer deposits

Getting on the property ladder can feel out of reach for many, not helped by high rent costs some would-be buyers are currently paying. But comments from the BSA’s Harrison may be worth considering.

“Building societies offer mortgages for people with small deposits, and even no deposit at all. The Government's Your First Home scheme will also help buyers with just a 2.5 per cent deposit. So don't assume homeownership is out of reach because of the deposit saved. Explore your options,” she urges buyers.

Marc von Grundherr, a director at estate agents Benham and Reeves, adds: “The reality is that saving even an additional 5 per cent can take considerable time, during which property prices and mortgage rates may also change.”

He outlines that existing homeowners are often in a stronger position, as they may have built up equity through years of mortgage repayments and house price growth. This equity can then be carried forward into their next purchase.

It’s not all stacked against first-time buyers, though. “Getting onto the property ladder with a smaller deposit can still be the right decision, provided the monthly repayments are affordable and they have sufficient financial breathing room,” von Grundherr said.
