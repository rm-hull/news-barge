A man has appeared in court accused of setting a fire to an historic York church which caused £30,000 worth of damage.

Michael Robert Peter Evans appeared in the dock at York Magistrates’ Court in connection with the fire at Holy Trinity Church in Micklegate on Wednesday (November 25).

Evans, 42, made no indication of plea to a single charge of arson after the blaze at the church, parts of which date back to the 13th century.

He is accused of causing £30,000 of damage to the church by committing arson.

Evans, of Lowther Terrace in Holgate, agreed for the case to be sent to York Crown Court where it will be heard at 10am on Tuesday, December 29.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21768729.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Holy Trinity Church in Micklegate *(Image: Newsquest)*

Magistrates agreed to release Evans on bail after hearing from his lawyer, Liam Hassan.

Evans, who appeared in court wearing a grey tracksuit, thanked the magistrates after they granted him bail.

Revd Simon Askey, priest-in-charge at Holy Trinity Church, told The Press two artificial flower arrangements behind the altar were set on fire at about noon on Wednesday.

He said the fire “quickly took hold” but a regular visitor entered the church, saw the flames and called for help.

“She called the emergency services (who responded in minutes) and enlisted the support of a young man from a shop in Micklegate to put out the fire using one of the church fire extinguishers,” Revd Askey said.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21768687.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Holy Trinity Church in Micklegate *(Image: Nadia Jefferson-Brown)*

He said the altar piece is “badly damaged and two panels are reduced to charcoal”.

The altar piece, he said, was carved by master craftsman George Walker Milburn of York in 1894 and dedicated in December 1898 in memory of members of local families.

Revd Askey said other than the damage to the altar piece, the only other issue is dust.

A working party will clean the church at 10am on Saturday, he said, with volunteers welcome.

Revd Askey said the church will reopen on Sunday and its nativity scene, Journey to Bethlehem, consisting of more than 150 pieces, will be in place from Thursday, December 10.

Most of the surviving older stonework at Holy Trinity Church is from the 13th century, according to the National Churches Trust.

The charity said parts of the church decayed after the dissolution of the monasteries in the 16th century and had to be rebuilt in the 19th century.

A church has stood on the site since Holy Trinity was originally founded during Anglo-Saxon times, possibly as early as the seventh century.
