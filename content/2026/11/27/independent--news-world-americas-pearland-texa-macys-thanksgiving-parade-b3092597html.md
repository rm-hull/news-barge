A Texas high school marching band raised $350,000 in hours to extend its stay in New York after bad weather forced the Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade to be postponed for the first time in its history.

The band, from Pearland, Texas, had traveled to Manhattan for the annual festivities with roughly 500 hungry kids to feed, only for organizers to move the parade from Thursday to Saturday.

Quick donations from businesses and almost 2,000 others helped the band cover the cost of staying on a few more days in a city where accommodation is notoriously expensive, even without a holiday pushing up Midtown hotel prices.

![Workers deflate a balloon after the Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade was postponed](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/26/13/Macy%27s_Thanksgiving_Parade_Postponed_36_12.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“I am still shocked at it, to be honest with you, I'm still shocked,” said Ryan Demkovich, the band's director, who added that the news of the postponement broke while he and the band were watching the second act of a matinee showing of “Wicked” on Broadway.

“We're just very thankful for everybody that has just come together to really go above and beyond to make this a positive experience for everybody.” The postponement left the parade's expansive list of performers changing plans, checking flight schedules, and extending hotel stays so they could stick around for the weekend.

Macy's said it was the first time the parade had ever been postponed, though it was canceled for three years during World War II. The rescheduling came as many wondered how the parade's iconic balloons would handle the powerful winds and rains bearing down on the city.

![A float of a turkey from the Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade, in the Manhattan borough of New York City](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/27/07/2306420885..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The retail giant said it was working with performers to piece together the complex logistics of putting on the parade two days later, including deflating balloons and shelving them until Saturday. Not every act will be there for the new date. The University of Alabama's Million Dollar Band will instead be heading home to perform at a football game.

But representatives confirmed that performers for Broadway shows such as “The Rocky Horror Show,” “Wicked” and “Hamilton” will still appear, as will the University of Connecticut's marching band, which is set to perform in the parade for the first time.

Another group facing a scramble is the Nā Koa Aliʻi Hawaiʻi All-State Marching Band, which is hoping to scrape together around $700,000 so it can also remain in the city through Saturday. Getting back home would come during an always hectic and costly travel season.

“We are building that chest as we speak and it is trending and progressing, and we're hopeful at this point, but you know, we're just waiting,” said Rickey H Badua, the band's director, who said they're trying to accommodate a few hundred band members and staffers. “We're waiting to see if people wake up with their families at home, if they see the message of need.”

![People brave the bad weather in the Manhattan borough of New York City on the Thanksgiving in the wake of the postponement of the iconic parade](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/27/07/2306438355..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The city has measures in place, following a severe balloon accident in 1997 that left a woman in a coma, that dictate whether to lower balloons or nix them entirely if the wind is too strong. Weather has grounded the parade balloons only once before, in 1971.

The parade has long drawn tons of tourists to New York, and this year was no different. Jess Rydell, 37, made her way to the Big Apple, only to have her dreams of attending the event for the first time dashed in what she described as “a big letdown.”

“Well, it is quite windy this morning, so I think the balloons would have been grounded anyway, so it's probably best for the safety of everyone, just disappointing when you’re here for it,” said Rydell, who instead tossed on a raincoat and strolled around Central Park.
