After more than 10 years, Amelia Dimoldenberg announced Chicken Shop Date was shutting up shop

**For better or worse, everyone recalls their first date. But how will the last one be remembered?**

After more than a decade of serving up her awkward, off-the-wall questions, Amelia Dimoldenberg's Chicken Shop Date is no longer looking for love, with the YouTuber calling time on her hit series.

From a youth club project to a full-blown media empire with A-listers asking *her* out, the series has influenced the interview landscape and carved a new path for the next generation of content creators.

"To start my next chapter, I have to close this one," Dimoldenberg told the BBC earlier this month.

"I'm focussing on making sure I say goodbye in the perfect way so I feel ready to do my next thing."

**This article contains spoilers for the final episode of Chicken Shop Date.**

After much speculation as to who would be the final date, fans were never going to be able to agree as to whether the farewell was indeed perfect.

Names like Harry Styles and Beyoncé had been floated around, but it was Drake who was the favourite with some bookies after Dimoldenberg previously described the Canadian rapper as her dream date.

So when the camera panned to psychotherapist Esther Perel, "everyone was a bit surprised", says fan Sarah Almond, who watched an early cinema screening on Tuesday.

Relationships expert Perel is also a bestselling author and podcast host

"I wasn't familiar with Esther Perel myself and I don't think anyone else in the cinema was aware of who she was," the content creator tells BBC Newsbeat.

And when the episode went live on YouTube, while most viewers reacted positively to the choice of guest, some said they did not recognise her and asked "where's Drake?!".

"I did hear a few people say they were disappointed, but I wasn't disappointed at all," Almond says.

"Even though I was expecting a bigger name to go off with a big bang, it was exactly what the show needed."

The episode played out like a counselling session, becoming emotional at points, Almond says.

"She's treating the end of Chicken Shop Date like it's a relationship break-up.

"It served her really well. It was a nice goodbye."

![Sarah Almond, a 27-year-old woman with long brown hair and brown eyes. She is pictured outside wearing a white blouse and looking over her shoulder, with fields and greenery behind her. ](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/e58e/live/fcc2fb00-ea61-11f1-ad88-915444d789fa.jpg)

Sarah Almond says the final episode was "bittersweet" but a fitting end for the series

Signing off with an interview with a relationships expert is a sign of the journey Dimoldenberg has been on with her pioneering series.

With more than three million YouTube subscribers, Chicken Shop Date has climbed the pecking order to become a key stop on press tours for even some of the world's biggest names, including the likes of Cher and Sir Paul McCartney.

But it wasn't always that way.

Rachel Adeolu watched the series from the very beginning, when Dimoldenberg would mostly date emerging rap and grime artists.

"A chicken shop is somewhere that's very familiar to a lot of people who grew up in London," the 21-year-old says. "It's kind of like a third space where a lot of us built friendships.

"So seeing content creators who also looked like us being interviewed in that same kind of setting with this unconventional interview style, which was also very funny, was unheard of."

Rachel Adeolu was a fan of how the series represented black culture when it first started

By 2021, seven years on from the first episode, the show was starting to look different.

As well as rappers and content creators, Dimoldenberg was bagging interviews with pop stars like Ed Sheeran and Finneas, as well as footballers Tammy Abraham and John Stones.

It was about that time producer Liv West joined the team, and her background at a major music publisher helped the series secure an impressive roster of guests.

"That summer we had a really big line-up," she says, with the likes of Keke Palmer, Charli XCX and Rosalía looking for love at first bite.

"It was then we started to think the tides are changing," she adds.

![Liv West is a woman in her 20s with blonde hair slicked back in a bun. She's pictured smiling outside at a sports pitch, wearing headphones while at work. ](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/c27b/live/9ac63100-ea71-11f1-a828-d384c0290f6b.jpg)

Liv West was a producer on Chicken Shop Date for almost five years

As the profiles of the dates grew though, so did their teams, with West saying it became a challenge to squeeze everyone in for shoots - with some being put into the kitchen.

The producer feels things ramped up after the episode with documentary maker Louis Theroux, as he was not a typical guest and of "legendary status".

For earlier fans like Adeolu, the take-off came at a cost.

"It feels like the audience who are more interested in seeing A-list celebrities be interviewed, they have definitely been favoured over the people who made the show popular in the first place," she says.

"In terms of just the initial charm of what the show was, I think that has probably had a negative impact on it."

Dimoldenberg has collaborated with Theroux multiple times since their date in 2022

As Dimoldenberg reached new audiences, her success started to balloon and before long she was appearing on major red carpets, in films and magazines - as well as a guest on other people's talk shows.

It also allowed her to work on a passion project: Dimz Inc Academy.

Launched in 2025, the scheme aims to upskill and mentor young people from underrepresented backgrounds who want to get into creative industries.

"It was Amelia's big dream to really make the creative industry, and particularly digital entertainment, accessible from every walk of life for every young person," West says.

Now in its second year, the scheme will be a legacy for the series away from screens - but as for what's next for the series' star, that's yet to be revealed.

"I really want to spend more time writing, that's what I'd like to focus on in the future," Dimoldenberg told the BBC.

"But who knows, the possibilities are endless."
