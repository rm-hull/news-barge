![The headline on the front page of the Guardian reads: “Scores of Labour MPs call on PM to reject Rosebank oilfield project”.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/624/cpsprodpb/29ee/live/0ea106e0-e9e6-11f1-a094-5fdc692f3e33.jpg)

The Guardian leads on "Labour's internal divisions" over the Rosebank oilfield project, reporting that nearly 100 MPs have signed a letter to the prime minister expressing their opposition to the project. It says it is the "clearest sign yet" of the deep rift the issue has caused within the party, with sources telling the paper that more than one frontbench ministerial aid is considering resigning if it is approved. The government has remained tight-lipped - both publicly and privately - about what it is likely to do.

![The headline on the front page of the Mirror reads: “Shameless Huw back on the box”.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/800/cpsprodpb/b615/live/644bcaa0-e9e4-11f1-a094-5fdc692f3e33.jpg)

Disgraced former BBC News presenter Huw Edwards is pictured on a number of front pages, including the Mirror, after giving his first interviews following his 2024 conviction for making indecent images of children. Edwards will feature in an hour-long Channel 4 documentary, the paper reports, adding that his appearance has been "slammed" by a victims' support charity. The Mirror's headline reads: "Shameless Huw back on the box".

![The headline on the front page of the Daily Star reads: “Huw: I lost the plot”.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/800/cpsprodpb/0782/live/6b7dae10-e9e4-11f1-99b6-a35a62fa4264.jpg)

Edwards also dominates the Daily Star, which says he told Channel 4 director Patrick Forbes he "lost the plot" when he was questioned about his offending.

![The headline on the front page of the Sun reads: “Shameless”.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/1e81/live/f01b7570-e9e5-11f1-a094-5fdc692f3e33.jpg)

The Sun says Channel 4 has been at the centre of "massive backlash" for giving "vile Huw" a platform for his "poor me" documentary. The broadcaster has defended the programme, with a spokesperson telling the BBC that a "highly experienced director" holds Edwards to account with "no questions off limits, revealing his account of what led to his offending and very public downfall".

![The headline on the front page of the i Paper reads: “Proof: Mandelson files show No 10 did cut corners on his business links”.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/800/cpsprodpb/1ac4/live/73b05fb0-e9e4-11f1-99b6-a35a62fa4264.jpg)

Lord Mandelson is the focus of the i Paper, which says government files have revealed how the former US ambassador benefited from "light-touch" checks on potential conflicts of interest. Lord Mandelson has yet to comment on the most recent release of papers relating to his appointment.

![The headline on the front page of the Independent reads: “Millions face pain of huge mortgage rises, warns bank”.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/57bc/live/3a95e5b0-e9e9-11f1-99b6-a35a62fa4264.jpg)

The Independent carries a warning from the Bank of England, which says millions of Britons are set to see a jump in their mortgage repayments next year. The "sharpest" £500-a-month increase will affect around 400,000 households, the paper says.

![The headline on the front page of the Times reads: “Banks 'likely to pass tax raid cost to customers'”.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/800/cpsprodpb/fbc8/live/0811a500-e9e6-11f1-99b6-a35a62fa4264.jpg)

Similarly, the Times warns banks "are likely to try and pass on the cost of Labour's tax raid on their profits to customers". The paper says city analysts have predicted that banks will seek to offset their high tax burden via more expensive home loans, or less generous interest rates on deposits. It notes that the warning comes as British households are under "mounting financial pressure".

![The headline on the front page of the Daily Telegraph reads: “Record flu season sweeps the NHS”.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/9849/live/0f5a0130-e9e7-11f1-a094-5fdc692f3e33.jpg)

"Record flu season sweeps the NHS" reads the Daily Telegraph, writing that hospital admission rates for this time of year in England are now the highest since records began 15 years ago. Senior medics have told the paper of "really grim" scenes in hospitals as doctors struggle amid emergency departments that are "brimming over". NHS Chief Sir Jim Mackey has warned of a "horrible spike" ahead, it adds.

![The headline on the front page of the Financial Times reads: “Brussels levies extra 'pay-to-play' fee as price of Burnham's Brexit reset”.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/9f01/live/596802c0-e9e4-11f1-99a1-253766e7ad8b.jpg)

The Financial Times considers Andy Burnham's "reset" of post-Brexit trading relations, after legal texts were released on Thursday. It reports the UK will pay an additional "participation fee" to the European Union as the price of its deal to remove border checks for food and drink exports, and re-link carbon markets. The fee will be 4% of the annual operational contribution, the paper says.

![The headline on the front page of the Daily Mail reads: “Brexit sell-out foils Labour's foie gras ban”.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/800/cpsprodpb/5331/live/4e6a8670-e9e6-11f1-a094-5fdc692f3e33.jpg)

Burnham's "reset" is also the main story for the Daily Mail, which says it will see the Labour Party forced to drop its election pledge to ban imports of foie gras. The paper says animal welfare campaigners have responded with "fury", and condemned the "shameful decision". A No10 spokesman told the paper the government would "keep sharing the UK's views and experience as we work with the EU on protecting and improving animal welfare."

![The headline on the front page of the Daily Express reads: “250,000 arrivals in a year allowed to stay in year”.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/800/cpsprodpb/a5c3/live/91cde080-e9e4-11f1-a094-5fdc692f3e33.jpg)

Nearly 250,000 migrants were given indefinite leave to remain in the UK for a single year, according to the Daily Express. The paper says the analysis was done by the Conservative Party, with shadow home secretary Chris Philp using the numbers as evidence to call for an "annual binding limit on inward migration". The report comes a day after migration statistics were released, showing that net migration to the UK had fallen to 77,000 in the year to the end of June. Burnham said the figures showed there was now "a sufficient degree of control" over the migration system.

![The headline on the front page of the Metro reads: “Return of the Tardis”.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/800/cpsprodpb/937c/live/d7c4e850-e9e3-11f1-a094-5fdc692f3e33.jpg)

"Return of the Tardis" declares the Metro, after a Doctor Who-esque police box was placed in a part of Watford town centre, where more than 500 crimes were reported in a year.
