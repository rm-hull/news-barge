Hundreds of thousands of households are missing out on a water bill discount averaging over £400 a year, the government has confirmed, as it launches a new drive urging those eligible to act.

Up to 250,000 households who have not yet claimed support under the WaterSure scheme are set to be contacted by water companies, after figures revealed fewer than half of those entitled are receiving the help.

The scheme caps annual water bills for low-income metered customers who have high essential water use, usually due to being a larger household or having specific medical needs.

WaterSure currently supports around 300,000 households across England and Wales, saving claimants an average of £428 a year.

Ministers said take-up has remained low because many eligible residents are unaware the discount exists or face hurdles proving their entitlement to suppliers.

![Up to 250,000 households have not yet claimed support under the WaterSure scheme](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/18/13/2305701940..?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&auto=webp)

Under the new initiative, water companies will use records from the Department for Work and Pensions (DWP) to directly identify and contact the 250,000 additional households missing out.

The drive comes as energy bills are set to jump by 26 per cent in January, pushing the typical annual household bill up by £443 to £2,166 under Ofgem’s latest price cap.

“If one part of the state knows someone needs help, another shouldn’t leave them in the dark. Using [DWP] data to tell people they’re entitled to WaterSure is a welcome initiative. Help is only effective if it actually reaches those who need it.”

A Water UK spokesperson said: “Water companies have long called for government to automatically provide us with the information needed to give people heavily discounted water bills. As a result, hundreds of thousands more people will now benefit from WaterSure.”

Those targeted include families with three or more children under the age of 19, as well as people with medical conditions that require significant extra water usage.

Separate eligibility reforms scheduled for early 2027 will also extend the WaterSure scheme to a further 53,000 low-income households, including more disabled customers.

The government says it is also launching a new Water Watchdog, replacing the Consumer Council for Water (CCW), with stronger powers to investigate poor customer service and intervene before widespread failures occur.

Environment secretary Dame Angela Eagle said: “Hundreds of thousands of the most vulnerable people aren’t getting help with their water bills because they don’t know how to access it. That’s not right.

“By sharing data across government and water companies, we’re fixing that problem – meaning getting that help will be far easier for those who need it. It’s just common sense.”

Anyone who believes they may qualify does not need to wait to be contacted and can check their eligibility and apply directly for free through their water supplier or via the advice pages on GOV.UK.

The push comes as Andy Burnham ramps up his crackdown on water firms, having pledged to bring essentials like water under stronger public control since taking office.

The prime minister has vowed to reform the system in which he says “shareholders never lose and customers never win”.

Alongside the bill support, ministers are preparing a strengthened Water Bill that will scrap the regulator Ofwat, introduce criminal liability for water bosses who cover up sewage spills, and lift legal limits on state ownership to allow struggling firms to be taken into public hands.
