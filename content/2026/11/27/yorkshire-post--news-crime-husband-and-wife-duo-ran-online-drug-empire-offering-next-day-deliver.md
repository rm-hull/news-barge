# Husband and wife duo ran online drug empire offering next-day delivery

## A husband and wife duo have been sentenced for their roles in a online drugs empire which offered next-day delivery of cocaine and heroin on the dark web.

The operation, which saw large scale quantities of Class A drugs sold through dark web marketplaces in exchange for cryptocurrency, was run by Huddersfield-based Christo Ollewagen.

The 49-year-old had been running the operation for seven years when police uncovered the scale of the sophisticated operation.

Heroin, cocaine and crack cocaine was distributed across the UK on a platform which offered next-day delivery and even leave reviews.

The platform had hundreds of reviews praising the service he had created.

Officers from West Yorkshire Police arrested Christo and his wife Jennifer Ollewagen, 37, in July 2025 and seized around 3.5kg of class A drugs, with a suspected street value of almost £320,000.

![Christo Ollewagen, 49, ran the online operation on the dark web](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/jpim-static/image/2026/11/27/11/24/christo_ollewagen_custody_photo.jpg?trim=252,0,206,4&width=640&quality=65)


Officers also found hundreds of postal bags, packaging materials and a laptop with detailed records of drug sales, orders and transactions.

Christo pleaded guilty to possession with intent to supply and being concerned in the supply of class A drugs, as well as possessing and removing criminal property in January 2026.

Bank statements and cryptocurrency transaction records showed how Jennifer would convert cryptocurrency into fiat currency, which was then transferred to overseas bank accounts.

She pleaded guilty to converting criminal property in September 2026.

Christo was jailed for 14 years at Leeds Crown Court on Thursday (Nov 26), while Jennifer was handed a three-month jail term suspended for a year.

Detective Inspector Richard Wallis, Senior Investigating Officer of the Yorkshire and Humber Regional Organised Crime Unit's Cyber Crime team, said: “This was a sophisticated and highly profitable criminal enterprise operating behind the perceived anonymity of the dark web, using multiple online identities, cryptocurrency and overseas financial channels to avoid detection.

“Through specialist cyber investigations and digital forensic work, we were able to uncover the full scale of the operation and bring them both before the courts.

“The quantity of drugs recovered, the evidence of nationwide distribution and the significant financial gain demonstrate the serious harm caused by this offending.

“The case highlights the growing role of specialist cyber investigators in tackling organised crime and disrupting drug trafficking networks operating in the digital space. It sends a clear message to those using the dark web to facilitate criminality that there is no safe haven online.

“We have the capability, expertise and determination to identify offenders, dismantle their operations and hold them accountable.”
