Four Jehovah's Witnesses have been convicted of "extremist" activity in Russian-annexed Crimea and sentenced to up to eight years in prison.

The sentences are among the longest jail terms given to members since the Russian government banned the faith group in 2017.

Jarrod Lopes, a New York-based spokesperson for the Christian group, said that the case, which concluded on Wednesday, had been based on secret witnesses and wiretaps of conversations between believers.

Alexander Kopylets was handed an eight-year sentence, while Alexander Voronchikhin was given seven-and-a-half years and Dmitriy Zakharevich was sentenced to six-and-a-half years.

A fourth defendant, Yekaterina Demidova, received a three-year suspended sentence, he said.

![Clockwise from top left: Aleksandr Kopylets, Yekaterina Demidova, Aleksandr Voronchikhin, and Dmitriy Zakharevich](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/27/12/45/jehovahs-witnesses-in-russian-annexed-crimea.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&auto=webp)

Over the years, six other members of the Jevohah’s Witnesses have received eight-year sentences, so far the harshest punishment Russia has handed out linked to the group, he added.

Comment has been requested from the court.

The Jehovah's Witnesses is a Christian denomination that was founded in the 1870s.

Members are known for door-to-door evangelism, distribution of literature, and a literal approach to studying the Bible.

They abstain from military service and blood transfusions and do not celebrate birthdays, Christmas, or Easter.

Russia has outlawed the group as an "extremist organisation".

The European Court of Human Rights, whose authority Russia no longer recognises, ruled in 2022 that the ban violated the group's rights to freedom of religion, expression and association. It has been similarly condemned by the UN Human Rights Committee.

![The Assembly Hall of Jehovah's Witnesses in St. Petersburg](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/27/12/28/zk-00_hu_b82991a72bec76a9-copy.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&auto=webp)

In statements to the court in Crimea, which Russia annexed from Ukraine in 2014, the defendants said that they were innocent and had been peacefully practising their faith, Mr Lopes said.

"Locking people up and separating them from their families for peacefully reading the Bible and singing to their God is anything but law enforcement – it is a mockery of the rule of law," Mr Lopes added.

The Orthodox Church, which is championed by President Vladimir Putin, is dominant in Russia.

A total of 185 Jehovah's Witnesses are currently imprisoned in the country, Mr Lopes said.

In July, a Moscow court seized 123 properties belonging to members of the faith and handed them over to the Russian state.

They include 63 residential and commercial properties covering a combined total area of over 11,000 square metres, as well as 60 plots of land with an overall area of 5.6 hectares located across the country.

In February, four Jehovah’s Witnesses were jailed for extremism after spies allegedly infiltrated their prayer sessions and made covert video recordings.
