Joe Biden’s latest memoir, "Promise Me, America”, opened with approximately 21,000 sales during its first week, according to industry tracker Circana.

While the figure secured the top spot on The New York Times nonfiction bestseller list, it falls significantly short of the debut figures achieved by predecessors such as Barack Obama and Bill Clinton.

Circana monitors about 85 percent of all physical book sales across the United States.

Released last week, the book received a muted critical reception. Reviewers noted a lack of fresh insight into Biden’s presidency or his decision to withdraw from the 2024 race following public debates over his age and health. A review in The New York Times carried the headline "Reflecting on 2024, Joe Biden Finds No Faults of His Own."

Promotional efforts were curtailed as the 84-year-old continues treatment for stage 4 prostate cancer, likely affecting commercial momentum.

![Biden’s sales fell well short of the figure achieved by Barack Obama when he released his memoir](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/05/04/19/2178039845.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

By contrast, Barack Obama’s 2020 memoir "A Promised Land" cleared 800,000 copies inside 24 hours and surpassed one million by week's end. Bill Clinton’s 2004 release, "My Life”, also crossed the one million mark within two weeks.

Commercial figures for books connected to the Biden family have routinely trailed those of other recent occupants of the White House. Former first lady Jill Biden’s June release, "View from the East Wing”, has recorded around 46,000 sales to date, per Circana data.

Meanwhile, memoirs by Michelle Obama and Hillary Clinton each exceeded one million copies within their first month.

Addressing the sales, publisher Little, Brown and Company issued a statement expressing that they were "immensely proud" to see the title reach number one on the New York Times list.

Earlier this month, multiple people were arrested when Biden made a surprise visit to Trinity College in Dublin.

Biden was accompanied by his wife to a ceremony where their son, Hunter Biden, accepted the Gold Medal for Outstanding Contribution for Public Discourse.

Demonstrators and spectators gathered outside the venue during the event hosted by the College Historical Society (HIST). As Biden departed, some people stepped forward and threw objects.
