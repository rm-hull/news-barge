Russian hybrid attacks could cause a mass-casualty event in Europe, a report has warned, as Moscow steps up sabotage operations across the continent.

The study, carried out by the International Institute for Strategic Studies (IISS), found that sabotage operations across the continent had risen by 16 per cent and focused on countries that supported Kyiv.

Poland suffered 24 per cent of all the attacks while Germany faced 20 per cent and the Baltic states - including Latvia, Estonia and Lithuania - faced 20 per cent.

Instances of violent attacks have also increased significantly, with a rise in arson attacks and the use of improvised explosive devices (IEDs).

Russian proxies have demonstrated increased capabilities with more complex IEDs deploying timed and remote detonators.

![Russia is increasing violent hybrid attacks across Europe according to a new report](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/27/09/2306510922..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

It comes as Europe has faced cyber-attacks across supermarkets, blood banks and airports.

“Sabotage has not yet achieved a strategic effect, but a mass-casualty incident is possible as the space for miscalculation narrows,” the report said.

“A single operation that caused fatalities or prolonged disruption would generate demands for retaliation and likely expose divisions between European governments.”

It predicted that European governments would be likely to continue responding to Russia’s attacks “one incident at a time”.

![An Ukrainian "Antonov" cargo plane is pictured on August 5, 2026 at the German airport Leipzig-Halle in Schkeuditz, eastern Germany, after an explosives-laden drone was found there the night before.](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/27/09/2288384601..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“Despite recent attacks and attempted attacks in Estonia, Lithuania and Romania, none has set out red lines, with concern about escalation the primary instinct,” it continued.

It warned: “The effect is that the first mass-casualty attack (deliberate or by miscalculation) would set the terms of Europe’s response, with governments acting under pressure and possibly divided.”

This approach has done little to impact the Kremlin’s strategy and the report offered suggestions on how to improve the chances of changing the Kremlin’s approach.

“Publicly signalling, at least among Nato member states, what level of harm triggers which response would give Europe a better chance of changing the Kremlin’s calculation,” it said.

![A satellite image shows the port of Benghazi following reported Ukrainian drone attacks](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/24/08/2026-11-23T165327Z_99355042_RC2JTOAU4HQW_RTRMADP_3_UKRAINE-CRISIS-SHIPS.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The study assessed attacks between October 2025 and September 2026.

The fallout from the war has spread beyond Europe too, with a Russian warship sunk in Benghazhi, Libya following a Ukrainian attack.

The explosion took place on board Merkury, a Russian navy warship docked at the Benghazi naval base.

Satellite images by Vantor showed the ship was a Russian Navy corvette, or small warship with pictures showing that most of the vessel had capsized and sunk, with only its bow still visible above water while smoke rises.

It came a day after Finland’s prime minister Petteri Orpo warned that the Russia-Ukaine conflict was “spilling over” after a Russian ship was reportedly on fire in Finnish water on last Saturday.
