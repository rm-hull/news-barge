Volodymyr Zelensky has hit out at Donald Trump for cosying up to Russian president Vladimir Putin - comparing it to befriending Adolf Hitler during the Second World War.

The Ukrainian president discussed a photo of the Russian and US leaders posted by Trump in which he described the Russian president as “my friend”. A video of the same meeting in Shenzhen, China, earlier this month showed Trump jokingly saying “who is he?” before entering the room and giving Putin a hug.

"Of course it's up to the President of the United States, but... it's strange," Zelensky told the BBC in an interview in Kyiv.

"He's an aggressor, and a killer…He can't be a friend. Just cannot be. It’s the same if somebody said about Hitler during the Second World War that he is a good friend,” he continued. “No, he’s not a friend.”

![Mr Zelensky made the comments after Trump described the Russian president as his 'friend'](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/27/8/59/Screenshot-2026-11-27-080548.png?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The meeting has sparked further concern among Europe and Nato, appearing to be another development in the warming of relations between Washington and Moscow.

Trump’s attitude to Zelensky has been far more dismissive, at times hostile and deeply critical of the Ukrainian president, who he has previously blamed for the war which was started by Russia in February 2022.

Recent attempts by peace envoys Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff have made little progress in forcing Moscow into agreeing a ceasefire, but they have suggested that Washington appears to be more aligned with Moscow than with Kyiv.

Kushner, who is also Donald Trump’s son-in-law, accused Europe of having “no plan” to end the war, and said negotiations should focus on the post-war period rather than looking at an immediate ceasefire. The Kremlin has argued the same.

The US envoy was also been accused of being like Putin’s “spokesman” after showering the Russian president with praise in an interview with Axel Springer CEO Mathias Döpfner.

![Trump described Putin as ‘my friend’ as he entered the room](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/26/17/2026-11-26T162433Z_1015617438_RC2TQOANQQ5V_RTRMADP_3_UKRAINE-CRISIS-TRUMP.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“Going into the meetings, President Putin is very organised. People can like what he’s done, they can not like what he’s done, but he’s definitely a grand master of politics,” Kushner said in the interview.

Maria Popova, director of the Jean Monnet Centre Montreal who focusses on the Russia-Ukraine war, said on X, said Kushner spoke “as if he’s Putin’s spokesman rather than a mediator or a US envoy”.

Zelensky, in his interview, made a similar criticism of the US president.

“They have to pressure the situation,” he said. “The situation is that Russia is the aggressor. That’s why they’re mediators, it’s not about comfortable talks, it’s about a strong country who can pressure because this country has power and respect.”

He added: “We need the American position to be the American position. We don’t need signals from the United States which we can get from Russia,” he said.

Asked if the US is pressuring Russia as much as he would like them to, the Ukrainian president added: “No, no of course.”
