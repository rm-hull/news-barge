**I'm a Celebrity... Get Me Out of Here host Declan Donnelly has said tensions between Mariella Frostrup and AJ Odudu over cooking duties on the show "became a much bigger deal than it should have".**

Co-host Ant McPartlin said "cooking makes things very tense in camp", adding: "If they could zoom out a little bit in there, they'll realise it's not that deep. But they just can't, once they're in there".

Donnelly and McPartlin discussed on their podcast how relations became fractious, when Odudu attempted to help Frostrup cook on Saturday's episode - the next day Odudu was distressed after Frostrup accused her of being "aggressive".

On Wednesday 887 people complained to Ofcom about Sunday's episode in relation to "alleged bullying behaviour" by Frostrup towards Odudu.

![A still from I'm A Celebrity showing AJ Odudu and Mariella Frostrup looking frosty and Wayne Rooney in the middle doing up a shirt button and facing Frostrup](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/f850/live/5ea1e540-e8bc-11f1-9e64-bddd508b9881.jpg)

Wayne Rooney tried to repair relations between AJ Odudu (left) and Mariella Frostrup on Sunday

Frostrup apologised during an amicable conversation on Tuesday, and Odudu accepted, explaining it was "triggering" because "it's a strong word to use in casual scenarios, particularly towards black women".

On their podcast, Hanging Out with Ant and Dec, Donnelly said tensions in camp started "surprisingly early".

"From day one cooking was tense and then it obviously came to a head with AJ and Mariella, and it spilled over to breakfast the next morning," he said.

McPartlin added: "You have 12 hungry mouths to feed and you and your sous chef, the other chef, are cooking and everyone is stood around looking at them. The pressure is quite a lot."

Donnelly continued: "When you are there, and so much of your life revolves around that mealtime, it's the only meal of the day you really get that isn't rice and beans.

"There is a lot of pressure and obviously Mariella was feeling that pressure.

"AJ wanted to come in and help and it all came to a head, and maybe because there was so much pressure and because it was so important, Mariella maybe didn't handle it very well around the campfire when she was cooking, and it spilled over into the next day - and it became a much bigger deal than it should have done."

He said the "most important thing" was that "the two ladies were able to sit down and drew a line under it, they were adult enough to sit there, talk it through, and move on together. If more people did that the world would be a much better place".

McPartlin agreed, saying: "If some of our leaders did that, the world would be a better place."

AJ Odudu said being called aggressive was "very harsh" and it was "an insane word" to use about her

On Wednesday, an Ofcom spokesperson told BBC News: "We are assessing the complaints against our rules, but are yet to decide whether or not to investigate."

On Thursday night, Frostrup completed her fourth bushtucker trial, which is voted for by the public.

Many viewers defended Odudu after the encounter.

They included broadcaster and comic Judi Love who said she felt "triggered by Frostrup's comment, while Britain's Got Talent judge and Strictly Come Dancing judge Motsi Mabuse also gave their support to Odudu.

BBC Radio 1 DJ Clara Amfo said: "Calling a black woman 'aggressive' when she is being anything else but is a classic microaggression."

Speaking on spin-off show I'm A Celebrity... Unpacked on Sunday, former campmate Jordan Banjo said Frostrup had handled the situation "poorly", but urged people to "give a bit of grace to Mariella, and everyone in the camp" because "you can't judge an abnormal situation by normal standards".

It is "a really weird situation that every single person's in, and I'm not sure if people go at stuff with the clearest head", he said, explaining: "You get these weird breakdowns, and I think that's what we're seeing."

An ITV statement said: "The production monitors everyone in camp 24/7 and if at any time we feel someone has overstepped the line by using inappropriate language or displaying unacceptable behaviour, we would take steps to intervene and speak to everyone involved.

"The duty of care for our contestants is of the utmost importance to us. We take the welfare of the celebrities very seriously and have measures in place to ensure they are supported at every stage of the process: before, during and after filming."
