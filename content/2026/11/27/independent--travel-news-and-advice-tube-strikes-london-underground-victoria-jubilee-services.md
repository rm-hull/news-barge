London Underground managed to operate more Tube services than anticipated on Friday morning, despite a walkout by drivers.

The 24-hour strike, called by Rail, Maritime and Transport union (RMT) members, began at midday on Thursday over a dispute concerning a four-day week.

LU stated that slightly above 55 per cent of the network ran on Thursday, exceeding output from previous industrial action in the same disagreement.

Although expected disruption occurred on Friday morning, the Victoria and Jubilee lines started on time, with all lines running before 6.30am — earlier than predicted, LU said.

Up to 9am on Friday, the network maintained just over 40 per cent of services, hampered by a suspended Waterloo & City line and reduced Piccadilly line trains.

Transport network usage reached 90 per cent of normal levels on Thursday, while Tube passenger numbers hit roughly two thirds of typical figures, according to LU.

The strike officially ended at 12pm today, but Tube lines are not expected to return to normal service again until 6pm.

The RMT says a proposed four-day week will lead to longer and more unpredictable shifts and end agreements negotiated over years.

The Aslef union has accepted the proposals and its members have worked normally during the strike.

Nick Dent, director of customer operations for London Underground, said: “We apologise to customers and businesses for the disruption they are experiencing this morning due to the strike action by the RMT.

“We were able to offer more service than anticipated yesterday and that trend has continued into this morning despite this unnecessary action.

“The number of drivers who have come to work over the last few days shows that there is a clear appetite from drivers to have their say on our four-day week proposals.

“We urge the RMT to put the proposals to their members before they attempt to subject Londoners to more disruption.”

More strikes are planned next month and in the new year unless the dispute is resolved.
