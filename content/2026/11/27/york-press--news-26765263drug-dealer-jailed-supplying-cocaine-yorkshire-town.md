A drug dealer is liable for deportation after being jailed for three years for supplying cocaine in a North Yorkshire town.

Amir Migkint, 23, was caught dealing the Class A drug in an alleyway in Scarborough after being followed by police, prosecutor Victoria Ball told York Crown Court.

The police officer first saw Migkint, who had been living at the Green Gables Hotel, cycling along Castle Street to North Marine Road at about 3.30pm on October 15.

Ms Ball said the police officer followed Migkint after suspecting him to be a drug dealer.

Migkint was seen approaching Woodall Avenue where he met another man who the police officer knew to be a drug user, Ms Ball said.

She said Migkint and the other man then went into an alleyway where they were met by a third man.

As the police officer approached the alleyway, Ms Ball said he saw what appeared to be a drug deal.

The police officer detained Migkint and the other two men ran away, Ms Ball said.

She said police found 8.9 grammes of cocaine and £71 in Migkint’s jacket pockets.

Officers then searched Migkint’s hotel room and found £1,000 in cash hidden between a men’s shaving kit and a sweets container.

“Officers also spoke to hotel staff who confirmed the defendant was using an alias and that the defendant had paid for his room in cash, at a cost of £200 per week,” Ms Ball said.

“In custody, the defendant was interviewed where he denied the offence in a prepared statement.

“He then answered no comment to the further questions.”

Migkint, of no fixed address, pleaded guilty to being concerned in the supply of a Class A drug.

## 'What you are doing here and where you come from is all opaque,' judge tells defendant

The Recorder of York, Judge Sean Morris, told Migkint, who followed his sentencing hearing on Thursday (November 26) through a Greek interpreter: “Amir Migkint, you’re 23 years of age and a bit of a mystery.

“What you are doing here and where you come from is all opaque.

“But what is clear is you have been dealing drugs, and Class A Drugs, which has to be an immediate sentence.”

The judge said Migkint would have faced up to four and a half years in jail had he not pleaded guilty and been convicted after trial instead.

“But,” Judge Morris told Migkint, “you were also a drug user.

“There was no firm evidence to show you had been bang at it for months and months – the amounts recovered indicate that this was not dealing on any huge scale.

“But you were obviously making money.

“So I come down from the starting point of four and a half years to four for these mitigating factors, and because of your age.

“Giving you 25 per cent credit for your [guilty] plea brings it [the jail sentence] down to three years, which you will serve 40 per cent of.”

Thomas Harris, defending, said Migkint, who has no previous convictions, was a drug user who had an “operational function in the [drug dealing] chain”.

Mr Harris said: “The defendant has asked me to tell the court how sorry he is.

“The defendant knows he is liable for deportation.”
