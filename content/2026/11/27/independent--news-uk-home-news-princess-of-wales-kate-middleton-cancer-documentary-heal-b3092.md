The Princess of Wales has spoken about the “lonely road” she faced during her battle with breast cancer in a clip from a new documentary about her treatment and recovery.

Kate’s new film *Heal* follows the princess as she undertakes the Three Peaks Challenge and reflects on her cancer experience alongside other survivors.

Footage released ahead of the premiere on Sunday shows the royal joining patients and survivors from charity Shine Cancer Support.

During a meeting at a pub in Richmond-Upon-Thames, she is heard telling the group: “Genuinely meeting you all here today really means a lot to me because it’s just lovely to be able to share my personal experience, but also hear your stories and be able to connect with others who’ve gone through something similar.

“I haven’t really explored this outside my family walls, really. So thank you for allowing me the time to do that and the open space to explore that. Really appreciate it, so thank you.”

![The Princess of Wales said her cancer journey was a ‘lonely road’](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/27/11/02/Princess-of-Wales-opens-up-about-cancer-journey-in-new-documentary.png?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Ceinwen Giles, the co-founder of Shine Cancer Support and a cancer survivor, who was at the table with Kate, echoed the princess’s sentiment.

She added: “I remember someone saying to me, ‘Oh, you can’t take cancer patients to the pub’. And I was like, ‘No. That’s where they wanna go’.”

The Princess of Wales was diagnosed with cancer in 2024 after undergoing major abdominal surgery. She had chemotherapy at the Royal Marsden Hospital and announced she was in remission at the start of 2025.

On Friday, she announced she was the new patron The Christie, a Manchester-based cancer centre.

During the documentary, the princess tells viewers: “Navigating cancer or any long-term illness is a lonely road. But it’s clear we’re not meant to carry life’s challenges alone.

“My illness has brought me alongside so many others navigating their own journeys too. This has become so much more than a personal or solitary experience but really a collective one.”

![Kate’s new Heal documentary will air on Sunday](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/25/12/88e2b0b6c92f31cbe1c2ffbbf2f6cb3bY29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzk1NjkxOTYw-2.86560940.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

In previously released clips from the documentary, Kate opened up about facing her own mortality.

She said: “Cancer does change you. It changes your body. It changes you physically.

“You have to go through treatment and surgeries… and everything. But there’s a much deeper part to what a cancer diagnosis shifts in you.

“I think when you’ve had to confront your own vulnerabilities… You know, when you’re faced with your own mortality, you have to accept that life is not infinite, and in those moments, yes, a shift does happen.”

The documentary follows Kate as she climbs the highest mountains in Scotland, England and Wales within 24 hours to raise money for the Royal Marsden Cancer Charity.

It will premiere on BBC iPlayer and BBC One on Sunday, before it is released internationally via NBCUniversal on 30 November 30, Channel 9 in Australia on 1 December and CTV in Canada on 7 December.
