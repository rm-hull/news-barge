The Princess of Wales is moved to tears listening to voice messages from her children as she opens up about her “really tough” battle with cancer in a new documentary.

The film *Heal* is set to be released on Sunday and follows Kate as she takes on the Three Peaks Challenge, climbing the highest mountains in Scotland, England, and Wales.

At one point, her brother, James Middleton, reveals that Prince Louis, Prince George, and Princess Charlotte have left messages for her to listen to as they climb Scafell Pike.

“Oh, no. Don’t. You’re gonna make me cry. No, please don’t,” Kate tells her brother.

Prince Louis, eight, is first heard saying: “Hello, Mummy. I really love you, and I hope you do well going up the mountain. Remember, I’m always in your heart with you, and I love you very much.

“And I’ll be following behind your back with you, and I’ll always be with you. And remember, I love you really lots and lots, and I give you lots and lots of love. Bye.”

![The Princess of Wales opens up about the challenges she faced while undergoing cancer treatment](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/27/11/02/Princess-of-Wales-opens-up-about-cancer-journey-in-new-documentary.png?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Mr Middleton then plays the message from Prince George, 13, who tells her: “Hi, Mamma. Hope you’re doing well. We’re all thinking of you right now, and we all love you so much, and you’re gonna do a great job.”

He adds: “I’m tracking you on Flightradar up the mountains and you’re looking… you look really, really strong. Keep going, and we’re right behind you.”

Princess Charlotte, 11, says in her message: “Hello, Mummy. I love you so much. We are so proud of you doing your walk and your challenges. We really wish you good luck, and I’ll be cheering you on. See you soon.”

Kate is seen brushing away tears as she listens to the messages, with Mr Middleton then telling her: “Sorry – William sent them to me and I said I’d play them when we got to the top.”

She then tells her brother that her children gave her the “reason to get out of hospital. They’re going to give me the reason to finish this challenge.”

“This is for the kiddies,” the princess adds.

![Kate was diagnosed with cancer in 2024 and was in remission at the start of 2025](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/26/17/19/5874312-37522185.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Kate was diagnosed with cancer in 2024 after undergoing major abdominal surgery. She had chemotherapy at the Royal Marsden Hospital, which will receive all the proceeds from the documentary’s distribution.

At another point in the film, the royal opens up about facing her own mortality and how her diagnosis changed her both physically and mentally.

“You have to go through treatment and surgeries… and everything. But there’s a much deeper part to what a cancer diagnosis shifts in you,” she says.

“Something like cancer strips everything away. Yes, it’s physical, but it’s emotional too. The things you thought you knew are no longer there. And you do, in a way, lose yourself. I’m having to learn again to live life fully, finding joy in simple things, feeling connected to my body in an inner sense of self.”

The princess describes her time in hospital as feeling “stark and alien and stripped back”, and reflects on the importance of having someone by her side.

![‘Heal’ is premiering on BBC on Sunday](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/27/12/f084a03203e9218c278d56b993eb5264Y29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzk1ODY2MjE2-2.86560940.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Kate adds “The darkness was a really scary, disorientating experience. I think I was sort of in and out of consciousness. It was a really dark time.

“But also then, somewhere through that too, I became aware of someone holding my hand. They weren’t talking to me. They weren’t trying to fix anything. They were just there.

“And the presence of someone that stood by me in that time, I’ll forever be grateful for. And it really made me realise the importance of simply being alongside somebody through their darkest times.”

Speaking ahead of the screening of *Heal* on Tuesday afternoon, Kate said Three Peaks was not only a physical challenge for her, but also a way of giving back to a community “that holds great meaning for me and whose care and expertise are life-changing for so many people”.

She said: “Every year, hundreds of thousands of people receive the words no one wants to hear.

“Cancer doesn’t just affect the body. It changes how you think and feel and profoundly affects every aspect of life. I know this personally and that the journey through and beyond treatment requires more than medicine alone.

“But it's often the people, the places and experiences that can help people through the darkest times.”

The documentary will premiere on BBC iPlayer and BBC One on Sunday, before it is released internationally via NBCUniversal on 30 November, Channel 9 in Australia on 1 December and CTV in Canada on 7 December.
