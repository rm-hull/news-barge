## Hopes rise for Whitby Cliff Lift after ‘optimistic’ engineering report

Councillors And Whitby Cliff Lift Campaigners in March 2026. Numminen/Ldrs

HOPES of reopening Whitby’s Cliff Lift have been boosted after engineers concluded the attraction may not require a feared £4 million package of major structural repairs.

The future of the West Cliff lift has been uncertain since it was closed in 2022 after severe corrosion, water ingress and structural defects were discovered.

On Friday, November 27, councillors backed a motion calling on North Yorkshire Council to identify funding to repair and reopen both the Whitby Cliff Lift and Scarborough Spa Cliff Lift.

The move came after officers confirmed no funding has yet been allocated for either project and amid discussions over the council’s future budgets.

It follows months of campaigning by residents, businesses, councillors and community groups seeking answers over the future of the historic transport link and tourist attraction.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21522642.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Whitby Cliff Lift. Google Maps.

Earlier this year, North Yorkshire Council backed away from plans to permanently decommission the lift following a public backlash and instead commissioned a fresh engineering assessment to examine whether it could be restored.

Now, councillors have been told the long-awaited report has been received by the authority and paints a more optimistic picture than some had feared.

Speaking at a meeting of North Yorkshire Council’s Scarborough and Whitby Area Committee, head of harbours and coastal infrastructure Chris Bourne said investigators had not found evidence that water was entering through the lift’s main casing joints, potentially avoiding the most expensive repair option.

He told members: “The recent inspections haven’t found any water ingress through the joints of the outer casing.”

The finding is significant because the most expensive repair option involved extensive grouting around the structure to stop water entering through those joints.

Mr Bourne said the previous worst-case scenario would have involved back-wall grouting around the entire structure.

“The worst-case scenario, which I’ve been transparent about, was the potential for having to spend in the region of £4 million,” he said.

Instead, engineers are expected to recommend a package of mitigation measures, including repairs at the top station, improved ventilation and corrosion-resistant materials, alongside a new lift, mechanical systems and electrical equipment.

Mr Bourne said: “It is possible to bring the lift back into use, and that is what the executive is going to look at.”

The update was welcomed by councillors and campaigners who have argued the lift is a vital accessibility link rather than simply a tourist attraction.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21522641.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Whitby Cliff Lift. Google Maps

Cllr Phil Trumper, who represents Whitby West Cliff and has been one of the leading voices calling for the lift to be saved, said he was “so happy” permanent closure had been halted earlier this year.

He told the meeting: “Having it repaired is so important for the community. Now we need to make sure it can be reopened.”

Members backed a motion calling on North Yorkshire Council to make sufficient funding available to return both cliff lifts to service, reflecting what councillors described as strong local support for the attractions.

A report to the committee states there is currently “no capital funding allocated” for the repairs and that a funding source would need to be identified before any restoration works could proceed.

While the engineering findings were welcomed, councillors were warned that planning and conservation processes could still take time before any repair programme begins.

North Yorkshire Council’s executive is set to receive a briefing on the report on December 8 before deciding when to consider the future of the lift in public.
