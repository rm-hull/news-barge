Millions of households could receive their £100 energy payment automatically if they pay their council tax by direct debit, under the Government's plans to help families cope with rising bills.

Chancellor John Healey announced the one-off payment in the Autumn Budget, promising support for around 20 million households in council tax bands A to D in England.

Councils are expected to begin making payments from January 2027, with eligible households who have verified direct debit details held by their local authority receiving the money straight into their bank accounts.

Around 13 million households are expected to receive the payment automatically, without needing to fill in a form or make a separate application.

The Government has said households that do not pay their council tax by direct debit will also be able to get the £100, but will be contacted by their local council with details of how to receive it.

## How to get the £100 energy payment

Households in council tax bands A to D in England are expected to qualify for the £100 payment.

If you already pay your council tax by direct debit and your council holds the necessary verified bank details, the money should be paid automatically into your account.

This means around 13 million eligible households will not need to do anything to receive the payment.

Those who do not pay by direct debit will be contacted by their council and given a choice of receiving the £100 through a bank transfer or having it credited to their council tax account.

The Government has not said that everyone must set up a direct debit to receive the money, so households without one should wait for instructions from their local authority.

## When will the £100 payment arrive?

Councils are expected to start making the payments from January 2027.

The money is intended to help households with energy costs through the coldest months and into spring.

The payment comes as millions of households face another increase in energy bills. Ofgem has announced that the energy price cap will rise by 26% from January, adding £443 a year to the bill of a typical household paying by direct debit on a standard variable tariff.

Announcing the £100 support in the House of Commons, Chancellor John Healey said: “I know rising energy bills are a real worry and people look to government to give them a bit of breathing space.

“So this winter for 20 million households in council tax bands A to D, I am announcing a £100 energy payment for households. That’s a £2.6bn scheme to take the pressure off in the coldest months and see people through to the spring.”
