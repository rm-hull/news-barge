Sunderland will unite in silence to honour those who served and sacrificed.

Remembrance events will take place in communities across the city on Sunday, November 8, with the main parade and service at the War Memorial on Burdon Road.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21651329.jpg?type=mds-article-620) *(Image: Sunderland City Council)*

Armed forces personnel and veterans will join community groups, dignitaries, and members of the public in paying tribute to the fallen.

Councillor Robert Hutchinson, Mayor of Sunderland, said: "We are all incredibly proud of our strong connections with the Armed Forces in Sunderland."

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21651332.jpg?type=mds-article-620) *(Image: Sunderland City Council)*

"Remembrance Sunday is an important opportunity for the whole city to come together to pay tribute to all those who fought in conflicts past and present and those who made the ultimate sacrifice for their country.

"This year’s key anniversaries include the battles of the Somme and Jutland in the First World War, 70 years since the Suez Crisis and 25 years since UK forces entered Afghanistan after the September attacks of 9/11.

"Every year it is inspiring to see people of all ages come together to remember those who gave so much in service to their country.

"As well as reflecting on the past, we should also take time to acknowledge the servicemen and women serving today and thank them for the contribution and sacrifices they make.

"I would urge anyone who can to attend a local Remembrance Service or Parade and join their community in paying tribute."

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21651330.jpg?type=mds-article-620) *(Image: Sunderland City Council)*

The parade, featuring personnel from the 4th Regiment Royal Artillery and the 3rd Battalion The Rifles, will begin on Burdon Road at 10.35am, heading north towards the War Memorial.

Music will be provided by the Eleventh Hour Band.

Captain Joe Giles of 3 Rifles will read the First World War poem For the Fallen before two field guns are fired from Mowbray Park to mark the start of the two-minute silence at 11am.

The wreath-laying ceremony will follow, honouring those commemorated on the War Memorial and Memorial Wall.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21651331.jpg?type=mds-article-620) *(Image: Sunderland City Council)*

The Memorial Wall, dedicated in 2011, pays tribute to those who have died in active service since the end of the Second World War.

The event will conclude with a march-past by veterans, serving personnel, emergency services, and community groups.

The Lord-Lieutenant of Tyne and Wear, Lucy Winskell OBE, representing The King, and the Mayor of Sunderland will take the salute from the steps of the Museum and Winter Gardens.

Members of the public are advised to arrive by 10.15am and dress appropriately for the weather.

City centre parking will be available, and two public viewing screens will be set up on Holmeside and Burdon Road.

A disabled viewing platform next to the War Memorial will be accessible from 9.30am.

Road closures on Burdon Road and Park Road will be in effect from 7am until approximately 2pm.

For those unable to attend in person, the service and parade will be live-streamed on the Sunderland, UK Facebook page.

Remembrance services will also take place in Castletown, Easington Lane, Fencehouses, Hetton, Houghton, Fatfield, Ryhope, Silksworth, South Hylton, Southwick, and Washington Village.

A two-minute silence will be observed at the War Memorial on November 11 to mark Armistice Day, signalled by maroons fired from Mowbray Park.

In tribute, landmarks including the Northern Spire Bridge, Penshaw Monument, Fulwell Mill, Silksworth Pit Wheel, and High Street West will be illuminated in red from Remembrance Sunday until the close of Armistice Day.
