A holiday park company with sites across the UK was placed into administration owing £244 million.

The extent of the debt of the company that owned sites in England and Scotland, which have remained in operation, is revealed for the first time as creditors backed the continuation of the sale process.

Cove Communities Holiday Park UK HoldCo Limited (HPUK) was placed into administration towards the end of last year with four companies including one based in Argyll with eight locations and nearly 197 staff, one in the Scottish Borders employing 20, a Cumbria site with 46 staff and a Cornwall location with 97, still operating.




Now, creditors have agreed to an extension of the administration to October 2027, new documents show.

The Herald reported that a fifth site, Medmerry, was recently separately placed into administration.

![road](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21654021.jpg?type=mds-article-620)UK holiday park firm placed into administration owing £244m *(Image: Google)*

This column also revealed that the administrators were seeking to sell Scottish sites, which include Drimsynie Holiday, Hunters Quay Holiday Village, Loch Awe Holiday Park, Loch Eck Caravan Park, Loch Eck Country Lodges, Loch Lomond, St Catherine’s Caravan Park and Stratheck Holiday Park.

Administrators Adam Paxton, of Alvarez & Marsal, said in their most recent update, from June, that their strategy is to trade four of the operating subsidiaries of the company “then maximise value by marketing the business and assets of the company and all of its subsidiaries in a coordinated sales process”.

Sites south of the Border are also to be sold: “During the period, we invited several agents to tender for the role of sales agent in relation to a sale of the business.

“Following consultation with the secured lender, we instructed Colliers to assist with the sale of business process.”

Colliers has undertaken site visits to the parks while administrators have been liaising with lawyers around legal documentation required for the sale process.

It is uncertain whether a secured lender owed £70m will receive a distribution, with this “dependent on the value achieved for the sale of business process”.

Administrators also said that, based on current estimates, it is not clear whether the preferential creditors will receive payment, including potentially His Majesty’s Revenue and Customs, which is owed £240,000, a situation which again depends on money raised from sales.

It is the same position for unsecured creditors, which totalled £174m, although much of this was inter-company debt and loan notes.

The administrators described events leading to the administration, saying finances were hit by “high interest rates and consumer spending contraction” along with a flooding that closed the Medmerry site, and wider rising costs.

The administrators said: “This led to an inability to continue to service the loan. As a result of the above, the group requested the secured lender provide additional funding.

“The secured lender did not agree to the group’s request and exercised its powers under its qualifying floating charge to appoint administrators in order to protect value within the company.”

The Scottish group in Argyll, which was family-founded before being sold, owed £103m at the point it was placed into administration, while recent documents for Springwood in Kelso show uncertainty over creditor returns.

Cove acquired Argyll Holidays for £100m in 2022, when director Keith Campbell said: "We as a family have put heart and soul into developing our group of parks.

"Cove is the ideal custodian of Argyll Holidays, sharing as we do, the same missions and values and commitment to the preservation and investment in the destinations we own."
