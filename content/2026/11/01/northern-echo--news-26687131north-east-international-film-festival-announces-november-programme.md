The North East International Film Festival (NEIFF) is returning with a packed programme of screenings, workshops, and new creative additions.

The festival will take place from November 16 to November 22, featuring a week-long celebration of independent filmmaking and creative storytelling across venues in the North East.

This year’s festival will introduce two new strands: a competitive games programme and the region’s first dedicated Screen Dance festival and award.

Lisa-Marie Tonelli MBE, festival director, said: "We want NEIFF to be a place where audiences discover extraordinary stories, filmmakers make meaningful connections and the next generation of creative talent can see a future for themselves in the industry.

"This year’s programme reflects how far the festival has come and our ambition for what it can achieve.

"With the introduction of Games and Screen Dance alongside our international film programme, we are opening up new opportunities for collaboration and showcasing the incredible breadth of creativity across the North East and beyond."

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21654707.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The festival will welcome audiences, filmmakers, industry professionals, students and creatives for a packed week. *(Image: NEIFF)*

The festival will officially open on November 16 at Tyneside Cinema with a special screening of the Australian black comedy fantasy film The Fox, introduced by director Dario Russo.

The film features the voice of the late Sam Neil as Magpie (his last film role to be released in his lifetime), and Olivia Coleman as Fox.

Venues across the region will host screenings and events throughout the week, including Everyman Newcastle, Newcastle College, and Gateshead College.

The core of the festival remains its official selection, which features a diverse mix of short films, features, documentaries, animation, and experimental work from the North East, the UK, and around the world.

Audiences will have the opportunity to engage with filmmakers through Q&As and gain insight into the stories behind the films.

NEIFF 2026 will also debut a competitive games programme in partnership with Gateshead College and Acid\_Gamelabs, reflecting the growing intersection between film and interactive media.

Additionally, the festival will launch its first Screen Dance programme, hosted in collaboration with Newcastle College’s Performance Academy, which explores storytelling through the fusion of movement, choreography, and film.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21654708.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The festival will also introduce its inaugural Screen Dance programme. *(Image: NEIFF)*

Education and industry engagement remain central to the festival’s mission, and NEIFF’s partnership with Newcastle College will provide students with hands-on experience, professional networking, and direct interaction with filmmakers.

Newcastle College has been announced as a headline sponsor for 2026, strengthening NEIFF’s commitment to developing future talent and helping make the North East a thriving destination for film and screen-based creativity.

Alongside screenings, the festival programme includes educational and professional development opportunities designed to bring together established industry figures and emerging creatives.

The festival will also include an industry networking breakfast at The Vermont Hotel and an evening party at ChaChaBuchi, both on November 21.

The week will conclude with a Closing Awards Gala, honouring achievement across the festival and recognising the filmmakers helping to shape the future of independent cinema.

As a volunteer-led Community Interest Company, NEIFF continues to champion inclusion, accessibility and diverse voices.

Ms Tonelli said: "NEIFF is about more than watching films.

"It is about bringing people together, creating opportunities and helping our region take its place on the national and international creative stage."

The festival will run in partnership with the BFI North East Talent Hub, offering members a special festival pass.

NEIFF will also continue its collaboration with Newcastle College, providing students with opportunities to engage directly with filmmakers and explore careers in the screen industries.

Individual tickets, event passes, and the full festival programme are now available, offering audiences the chance to curate their own festival experience or immerse themselves fully in the week-long celebration of film and creativity.
