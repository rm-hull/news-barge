Reviews of maternity services at Harrogate District Hospital have identified areas for improvement in documentation, escalation processes, risk assessment and workforce resilience, according to Harrogate and District NHS Foundation Trust.

The trust outlined the findings in response to questions from The Harrogate Informer about maternity safety reviews, outstanding actions, performance monitoring, the handling of concerns and referrals to external regulators.

The trust said it maintains a continuous programme of maternity safety monitoring, review and improvement, including internal governance reviews, participation in national maternity safety programmes, external assurance processes and service improvement plans arising from incident investigations, audit findings and quality reviews.

## Reviews and maternity safety findings

Harrogate and District NHS Foundation Trust completed a Care Quality Commission (CQC) maternity inspection process that began in July 2025. The inspection report was published in January 2026.

The trust also said information had been submitted to NHS England following the recent Amos Review to provide assurance. Regional assurance processes covering the whole maternity service are ongoing.

According to the trust, the principal findings from its reviews demonstrated that maternity services provide safe and compassionate care for the vast majority of women and families.

However, the reviews also identified opportunities for improvement, particularly in documentation, escalation processes, consistency of risk assessment in the Maternity Assessment Centre (MAC) and workforce resilience.

The trust said these findings had informed ongoing quality improvement work and service development programmes.

## Maternity safety improvements and outstanding actions

The trust said recommendations arising from reviews and investigations had focused on strengthening governance arrangements, improving multidisciplinary training, enhancing fetal surveillance and risk assessment processes, embedding learning from incidents and further developing the maternity workforce.

It reported that the majority of actions had been completed and embedded into routine practice.

A small number of longer-term actions remained in progress, primarily those relating to workforce development, national maternity system changes and ongoing quality improvement initiatives.

The trust said these actions were subject to regular monitoring through maternity governance structures and were progressing in line with agreed implementation plans.

However, its response did not identify each outstanding action individually, provide specific reasons for any delays or give expected completion dates for the remaining work.

## How Harrogate maternity services monitor safety

The trust said it uses a range of measures to monitor maternity safety and assess the quality of care. These include:

* Maternity safety intelligence and quality dashboards.
* NHS Resolution Maternity Incentive Scheme standards.
* Compliance with multidisciplinary training requirements.
* Clinical audit programmes.
* Patient experience and feedback measures.
* Staffing and workforce indicators.
* National and regional benchmarking data.
* Perinatal mortality reviews.
* Clinical incident reporting and investigation outcomes.

Performance is reviewed through regular maternity governance meetings, trust committees and board assurance processes.

The trust said its most recent performance indicators continued to demonstrate positive outcomes in the majority of safety measures. It said the indicators were routinely reviewed to identify emerging trends and opportunities for improvement.

However, the trust did not provide specific figures for the indicators in its response, so the results could not be examined in detail from the information supplied.

## How maternity concerns are investigated

The trust said patients, families and staff could raise concerns through a number of routes, including formal complaints processes, incident reporting systems, the Maternity and Neonatal Voices Partnership, Maternity and Neonatal Safety Champions, Freedom to Speak Up arrangements, Professional Midwifery Advocates and direct feedback mechanisms.

Concerns are reviewed by appropriately trained clinical and managerial teams, according to the trust.

The trust said learning from concerns and incidents is shared through governance meetings, staff briefings, multidisciplinary training and quality improvement programmes.

Actions arising from investigations are tracked to completion through a Maternity Quality Assurance Meeting. The trust said audits are then used to assess whether changes have resulted in measurable improvements.

Its response described these processes but did not provide individual case examples or specific audit results demonstrating the impact of particular changes.

## External reporting of maternity safety issues

Asked whether significant maternity safety issues had been referred to an external regulator or other oversight body during the period, the trust said: “No ‘significant maternity safety issues’ have been reported to an external regulator or oversight body.”

The trust added that it had processes to ensure incidents meeting national reporting criteria were shared with relevant external organisations and regulators in accordance with NHS requirements.

It said these arrangements included statutory and regulatory frameworks involving NHS England, the Care Quality Commission, NHS Resolution and Maternity and Newborn Safety Investigations where the relevant criteria had been met.

The trust said reviews had been undertaken in accordance with national guidance, with learning identified and actions implemented to strengthen practice and improve patient safety.

Its response did not identify specific incidents referred to external bodies or detail the outcomes of individual referrals.

## CQC inspection report

The trust’s maternity inspection process began in July 2025, with the CQC report published in January 2026.

The report is available on the Care Quality Commission website.
