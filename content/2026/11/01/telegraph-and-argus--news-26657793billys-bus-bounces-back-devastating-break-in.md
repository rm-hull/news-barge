A dog bus business that was left devastated after vandals targeted one of its vehicles is celebrating its first birthday.

Billy's Bus, founded by Laura Mohan and named after her own dog Billy, marked the milestone with a special anniversary gathering, bringing together dogs, owners and volunteers who have helped make the venture a success.

Every day the bus picks up around 15 passengers waiting at stops across areas like Ilkley, Skipton, Otley, Shipley and Guiseley.

It comes months after one of the business's buses was broken into, leaving the team facing repair costs, disruption and uncertainty.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21613730.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Penny, one of Billy's Bus's four-legged passengers enjoys an outdoor play session. *(Image: T&A)*

The incident was reported to police, but the case has since been filed pending any new enquiries.

## 'Absolutely heart-breaking'

Ms Mohan said she initially believed there had been a mechanical fault when she arrived to start work one morning.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21613720.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Dogs board Billy's Bus as the business celebrates its first birthday. *(Image: T&A)*

She said: "I started the engine and realised something had gone wrong, but I couldn't understand how all the wires had been cut,"

Ms Mohan added: "I thought something must have happened in the engine, but then the garage told me somebody had done it on purpose. It was absolutely heart-breaking."

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21613725.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Canine passengers enjoy a ride on Billy's Bus during the business's first anniversary celebrations. *(Image: T&A)*

## More than just a business

Ms Mohan said the impact of the break-in stretched far beyond the vehicle itself.

"The bus is not for profit. I don't make any money out of it," she explained.

"It all goes back into training, support and canine first aid courses for our volunteers and the adults with learning difficulties who work with us."

Ms Mohan added: "When something like that happens, not only do we let down all of our dog owners, some of whom rely on us, but volunteers had to be stood down as well. For some of them, this is the best day of the week."

The damage also meant the team faced an unexpected repair bill while trying to keep services running.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21613728.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Billy's Bus takes dogs to secure outdoor fields for games, exercise and socialisation. *(Image: T&A)*

## Hundreds rally behind Billy's Bus

Following the incident, Ms Mohan shared news of the break-in online, but what happened next took her by surprise.

She said: "We had hundreds and hundreds and hundreds of messages of support...people were offering to pay for the garage bill and set up fundraising pages. I didn't ask for any of it, but just knowing people cared was lovely."

The overwhelming support helped the team bounce back quickly, and the bus was back on the road within 24 hours.

Ms Mohan said: "The next day we were just beeping our heads off, as if to say, 'Nice try, but we're still here.'"

A year on, the idea has expanded far beyond what she initially imagined.

"We've now got three buses on the road...and built something really special."

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21613722.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Dogs enjoy a trip aboard Billy's Bus, which has expanded to three vehicles in just 12 months. *(Image: T&A)*

## Bringing smiles beyond dog owners

As well as transporting dogs, the buses have become a familiar sight at schools, nurseries and care homes.

Ms Mohan said some of her favourite memories have come from visits to older residents, particularly those living with dementia.

She recently visited a care home in Otley, where residents spent time stroking and interacting with the dogs.

Ms Mohan said: "It was wonderful, the ripple effects of Billy's Bus are kind of everywhere to be seen."
