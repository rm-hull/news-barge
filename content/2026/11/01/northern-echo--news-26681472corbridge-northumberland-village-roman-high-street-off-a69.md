Just off one of the North East's busiest roads lies a Northumberland village where visitors can still walk along a street built by the Romans almost 2,000 years ago.

Corbridge sits close to the A69, between Newcastle and Hexham, but its historic buildings, independent shops and riverside setting feel removed from the traffic.

The village, beside the River Tyne, has become a popular destination for visitors exploring the Tyne Valley.

However, its most extraordinary attraction lies just outside the centre, where the remains of an ancient Roman settlement reveal what life was like on the northern frontier of the Roman Empire.

At Corbridge Roman Town, visitors can walk along the original surface of a Roman high street, surrounded by the remains of buildings.

Corbridge's Roman history stretches back to the first century AD.

The settlement, known in Roman times as Coria, developed at an important crossroads where major routes met near the River Tyne.

The Romans established a series of military forts in the area from around AD 85.

Over time, the site developed into a thriving town serving soldiers, traders and civilians.

The town stood at the meeting point of Dere Street, a major Roman road running north to south, and the Stanegate, which connected settlements across the region.

Today, visitors can explore the surviving remains of this once-bustling community.

One of its most striking features is the Roman high street, where the original road surface can still be walked upon.

Corbridge is also home to one of Britain's most important Roman archaeological discoveries.

The Corbridge Hoard was unearthed during excavations in 1964.

Archaeologists discovered a wooden chest containing a remarkable collection of objects, including Roman armour, weapons, tools and personal possessions.

The items had been buried during the second century AD and remained hidden for centuries.

Among the most significant discoveries were pieces of segmented Roman armour, which helped historians better understand how Roman soldiers' equipment was assembled.

The objects are now displayed at the Corbridge Roman Town museum, alongside other finds from the site.

While Corbridge's Roman remains attract visitors from across the country, the modern village has plenty to offer.

Visitors can browse independent retailers, stop for refreshments or explore the surrounding streets.

The mixture of old buildings and independent businesses gives Corbridge a different atmosphere from the larger shopping centres of Newcastle and other nearby places.

St Andrew's Church stands at the heart of the village, while the surrounding buildings reflect centuries of settlement in the Tyne Valley.

The surrounding countryside provides opportunities for walking and exploring, with the nearby village of Riding Mill and the historic town of Hexham also within easy reach.

Hadrian's Wall lies a short distance to the north, making Corbridge a convenient stopping point for visitors exploring Roman Northumberland.

The wider area is home to some of the country's most important Roman remains, alongside historic villages and scenic countryside.
