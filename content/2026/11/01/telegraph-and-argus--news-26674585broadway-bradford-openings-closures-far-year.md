A changing mix of shops is reshaping one of Bradford city centre’s most important retail destinations.

Bradford’s Broadway shopping centre has seen a mix of closures and new arrivals throughout 2026, reflecting wider shifts in the high street retail landscape and ongoing efforts to refresh its offering.

## What has closed and opened at The Broadway in 2026?

One of the most notable departures was **The Body Shop**, which closed its doors on October 24 after 11 years at the centre.

The closure followed the retailer’s entry into administration in 2024, though the store had initially survived the restructuring before being selected for closure later.

Despite the loss of **The Body Shop**, The Broadway has continued to attract new businesses and refresh its tenant mix.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21637479.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The Broadway

Bodycare returned to Bradford with a new-generation Studio Store, which opened on September 19 and was launched by BBC presenter Anita Rani.

Other recent additions include **Starbucks, BOO Burger, Dough Club**, and** Jamaica Blue**, all of which have expanded the centre’s food and drink options.

Fashion retailer **YOURS** also joined the line-up, strengthening the centre’s fashion offering.

## General manager on changes

Earlier this year, Ian Ward, general manager at The Broadway, said: "Bradford shoppers deserve variety, quality and a vibrant experience - and that's exactly what we're delivering.

"With exciting new brands arriving and much-loved favourites moving into the centre from across the city, the buzz has never been greater.

"And there's even more on the horizon, with fresh announcements set to make 2026 an unforgettable year for Bradford's shopping scene."

Those early 2026 openings included jeweller **F.Hinds, Berries, Timpson, Max Spielmann** and**Big Jack Potatoes**.

## Previous openings and closures

The Broadway has seen significant changes since it opened in November 2015.

Major names such as **Debenhams, Marks & Spencer and WH Smith** have exited over the years, but others like**Primark** have filled key units, helping the centre adapt to evolving shopping habits.

By the end of 2025, The Broadway had recorded more than 100 million visits since opening.

The centre’s strategy now combines national brands with specialist retailers and a growing focus on food and leisure.

While closures like The Body Shop reflect the broader challenges facing high street retailers, The Broadway’s ongoing investment and new arrivals suggest it remains central to Bradford’s regeneration and retail future.

The management team has said more announcements on new retailers are expected in the coming weeks.
