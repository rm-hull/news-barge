**This is our new column Wayne's World written by Wayne Chadwick, managing director of YO1 Radio and owner of The York Roast Co.**

As a young lad I used to visit Blackpool on a regular basis to see my grandparents and my cousin.

You can imagine the joy for a lad of eight having several visits a year and the chance to go to the Pleasure Beach and the other fabulous attractions.

Times move on, though, and I hadn’t been to Blackpool for nearly 40 years until just a couple of weeks ago when I was invited to attend an awards ceremony at a prominent hotel.

The event started at 7pm.

It was dark, wet and windy and I was running late.

A few miles from Blackpool I got my first glimpse of the famous tower lit up in green in all its glory welcoming me back as if it was yesterday.

The sight was a little emotional as all my fond childhood memories came flooding back.

I was excited to be back but that emotion rapidly subsided as I drove along the promenade and onto some of the side streets.

Multiple shops closed and boarded up and what appeared to be countless drunk, or worse, people walking the streets with no one to care for them on this dreadful evening.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21260625.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Blackpool Pleasure Beach. *(Image: Newsquest)*

The town has become like a ghetto and a far cry from my memories.

When I arrived at my seafront hotel the weather couldn’t have been worse.

The wind coming off the Irish Sea was relentless and the rain was drenching.

I had my jacket on ready for the event as I knew I had little time, so I went straight into the awards ceremony and joined my table.

The next day I decided to avoid going for my planned stroll along the prom as I think it would have tarnished my childhood memories.

Blackpool seems so unlike our wonderful Whitby, Scarborough and Filey for example.

As I drove away and glanced again at the tower behind me in the rear-view mirror, I could only thank God for our North Yorkshire, no matter how much we moan and groan about it at times.

The grass is rarely greener on the other side.
