A 14-year-old boy has handed himself into police after a viral video appeared to show a Coventry fan taunting Sunderland supporters with a photo of Bradley Lowery.

An investigation has been launched after an incident at the Black Cat’s away match against Coventry City on Saturday (October 31) sparked outrage online.

Inspirational Bradley, from Blackhall Colliery, brought the football community together across the country before his tragic death from terminal childhood cancer neuroblastoma in 2017.

West Midlands Police has this afternoon (November 1) confirmed that a teenage boy has handed himself in and will be spoken to in due course.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/17283089.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Bradley Lowery, whose infestious smile and spirit captured the hearts of football fans not only of *(Image: The Northern Echo)*

A spokesperson said: “We are aware of a video circulating on social media showing an incident at Coventry City’s Premier League match with Sunderland yesterday afternoon.

“A thorough investigation has been launched, and a 14-year-old boy has since handed himself in and will be spoken with in due course.

“We understand the upset and outrage this video has caused in the community and to the victim’s family and our officers are keeping them up to date with the progress of our investigation and working closely alongside both football clubs.”

Bradley’s mother Gemma Lowery has already spoken out about the incident, saying she is heartbroken that a “beautiful memory” has been “used in such a cruel way”.

"We are a football-loving family who follow Sunderland home and away,” she said. “I’m all for football banter, but surely mocking a child who died from cancer is not banter?

"I hope the club takes appropriate action and the young lad’s family help him understand how hurtful his actions were.

"I don’t want hatred directed towards him, so please please don't, just for him to learn from this."

Coventry has confirmed that it is aware of the video and will liaise with authorities.

Bradley’s memory now lives on through the Bradley Lowery Foundation, which was established by Gemma in August 2017 following her son’s death.

The foundation's mission includes supporting families fundraising for treatment or equipment not covered by the NHS, encompassing all illnesses and conditions.
