If you are looking for somewhere to live, you might have a few different priorities ranging from the area’s choice of restaurants to house prices.

To help you out, Muddy Stilettos releases a list of the best places to live around the UK each year.

For the North East, the village of Corbridge made it onto the best of the rest list thanks to its “Roman ruins, riverside rambles and ridiculously good restaurants”.

## Why was Corbridge named one of the best places to live?

On the village, Muddy Stilettos said: “Pretty, historic and just the right side of posh, Corbridge is one of those villages that delivers good living by the bucketload.

“Perched on the River Tyne, its honey-stone streets are packed with excellent indies, buzzy cafés, restaurants and proper pubs, with hidden yards and little passageways adding to the charm.

“There’s 2,000 years of history underfoot – the Romans were rather keen on it too – but this is no sleepy heritage village.

“Newcastle is around 15 miles away, glorious Tyne Valley countryside surrounds you and Hadrian’s Wall is practically on the doorstep.

“No wonder families and city escapees are fighting over the For Sale signs.”

If you are looking to move to Corbridge, the average price of homes over the last year was £500,197, according to Rightmove.

The majority of properties sold in the area were detached homes and cost around £693,525.

Terraced properties cost an average of £377,833 while flats cost around £184,444.

The area has a few schools, including the Corbridge C of E First School and the Corbridge Middle School.

Children can then move to the Queen Elizabeth High School in Hexham.

Corbridge is in a great spot for visiting nearby cities and towns with Newcastle just a 30-to-40-minute train away and Hexham is an easy drive away.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21651119.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The streets look very quaint. *(Image: Getty)*

For those who love history, you can easily reach the Corbridge Roman Town from the village where you can walk along the Roman high street.

There is also Hadrian’s Wall where you can enjoy a peaceful walk and Aydon Castle, which is just a mile away.

There are plenty of restaurants and food shops to explore with the Corbridge Larder being a favourite for those wanting to pick up some cheeses and deli meats.
