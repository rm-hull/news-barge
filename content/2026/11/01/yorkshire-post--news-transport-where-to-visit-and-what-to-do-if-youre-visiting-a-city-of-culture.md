# Where to visit and what to do if you're visiting a City of Culture nominee - including Sheffield

## Camilla Foster looks at what we can visit in the latest shortlisted cities of culture. These wonderful bustling cities are full of museums, art galleries and music venues that visitors can explore.

The UK has a wide variety of cultural cities, each with its own distinct history, accent, arts scene, and regional identity. But only one can claim the coveted UK City of Culture title.

Blackpool, Milton Keynes, Sheffield and Wrexham were announced as the four cities shortlisted to compete for the prestigious 2029 title last week.

The Department for Culture, Media and Sport confirmed that each contender will receive at least £125,000 to develop its bid, with the winning city set to benefit from the title and a £10 million prize fund.

![Sheffield Cathedral. Picture credit: Alamy/PA.](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOjMwY2JmNWMwLWQ2NjctNDU4NS1hOWI1LWNkZWVmZGI1NDhhMDo4NDlhY2E5OC02MjRlLTRjNjctOWUxZi1lMjI2YzM5M2IxYWQ=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


As part of the bidding process, each city council was asked to demonstrate how it would harness culture to transform its communities, boost economic growth, create new opportunities and foster a greater sense of local pride.

Awarded every four years, the UK City of Culture title is currently held by Bradford. For the shortlisted cities, securing the accolade would bring valuable investment, increased visibility and an opportunity to showcase what makes them special.

Each contender has made a compelling case, but what sets these four cities apart? From cultural attractions and local experiences to hidden gems worth discovering, here’s some suggestions on what to see and do if you’re planning a visit…

**Blackpool**

![Bletchley Park in Milton Keynes. Picture credit: Alamy/PA.](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOjcxY2ZmYjA0LWIwMDAtNDU5ZS05MjU0LTZmNzllMDE4NTZiYzpjYWFiY2ZjOC00M2QwLTQ0YmMtOTNjNy0yNDNhYjZmOTU4OWE=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


When someone mentions Blackpool, neon lights, piers and donkey rides along the beach are probably the first pictures that immediately spring to mind.

Although the dazzling Blackpool Illuminations along the promenade draws crowds in throughout the autumn and winter season, the city’s increasing diverse cultural offerings are what encourage people to extend their stay.

Often referred to as “the capital of dance”, Blackpool in Lancashire is not only home to magnificent ballrooms, but also hosts several lively dance festivals and competitions throughout the year.

Meanwhile, Blackpool Tower remains a must-visit landmark, packed with dramatic dungeon experiences, stunning panoramic views, and world-famous cream tea dances.

The renowned British seaside resort also boasts a thriving arts scene and a packed music festival calendar.

You can catch live music at historic landmarks like the Winter Gardens, the Empress Ballroom, and Grand Theatre, or dive into the local subculture at alternative, high-energy venues like Bootleg Social and The Waterloo Music Bar.

Furthermore, a vast array of fun family attractions – including free-admission amusement arcade and indoor entertainment complex Coral Island, Blackpool Zoo, a skate park and the Showtown Museum – provide children with hours of entertainment.

**Milton Keynes**

Milton Keynes in Buckinghamshire is arguably the underdog contender for the City of Culture accolade, but its bid highlighted this city’s strong contemporary arts and museum scene.

Among it’s fun cultural venues, MK Gallery is a contemporary gem. Many exciting exhibitions and events, such as the Lowry The Theatre of Life exhibition, take place within this shiny community hub.

Bletchley Park is another major local highlight with a fascinating history and is celebrated as the top-secret home of Britain’s Second World War codebreakers.

The intriguing city is also home to a plethora of green spaces, such as Willen Lake and Campbell Park, which are perfect for a relaxed stroll.

However, if you find yourself visiting the city on a rainy day, head to Milton Keynes Theatre and lose yourself in a play or delve into some local history at the Milton Keynes Museum.

Milton Keynes also hosts a variety of cultural events throughout the year, including the summer music festival Reggae Land and the Autumn Light Festival.

**Sheffield**

Known as the “Steel City”, Sheffield in South Yorkshire has a rich industrial heritage, but is also celebrated for its music and sporting history.

The northern city has produced a long line of iconic global acts such as Arctic Monkeys, Pulp, Def Leppard and Bring Me The Horizon. It remains a bustling hub for grassroots and independent music with plenty of fun venues to choose from such as Sidney & Matilda, The Leadmill and Yellow Arch Studios.

From music to snooker, lots of lively events unfold in the hilly city every year, including indie-alt and rock music festival Tramlines, Sheffield Food Festival and the World Snooker Championships.

Football is also deeply embedded into the DNA of this proud city, as Sheffield FC is recognised by FIFA as the oldest existing independent football club in the world. The Sheffield Home of Football Charity run 90-minute guided football heritage walking tours through the city centre for visitors seeking to boost their football trivia.

In addition, Sheffield is widely recognised for its vibrant performing arts scene, anchored by major landmarks like The Crucible and Lyceum theatres. It also holds international documentary festival Sheffield DocFest every year.

Plus, if you are a big history buff, don’t forget to pop into Kelham Island Museum, Sheffield Cathedral and Weston Park Museum to get fascinating glimpse into Sheffield’s past.

**Wrexham**

The release of the docuseries Welcome to Wrexham, following Hollywood stars Ryan Reynolds and Rob McElhenney’s purchase of Wrexham AFC in 2020, undoubtedly put the Welsh city on the global map.

Football fans can soak up the atmosphere at a home game at the Racecourse Ground or visit the Football Museum of Wales in Wrexham to discover more about the nation’s rich footballing heritage.

For those who love shopping and exploring independent businesses, the Independent Quarter is well worth a visit, with its eclectic mix of shops, galleries and markets.

Families can also keep the kids entertained at Xplore! Science Discovery Centre, while Ty Pawb brings together independent businesses, art galleries, a theatre and a vibrant food court in the heart of the city.

Beyond the city centre, Wrexham is an ideal base for discovering some of Wales’ finest landscapes and outdoor adventures.

One of the region’s standout attractions is Pontcysyllte Aqueduct, one of Wales’ four UNESCO World Heritage Sites.

Visitors can admire this remarkable feat of engineering from a train, take a leisurely canal boat trip across the aqueduct or enjoy a more adventurous perspective by canoeing along the waterway.
