**The Reform UK Harrogate and Knaresborough branch has called for a fresh assessment of a northern relief road, tighter scrutiny of council spending and changes to housing development as part of its proposals for the area.**

In a response submitted to The Harrogate Informer in October, branch chair Jonathan Swales outlined the party’s priorities for infrastructure, local government finances, housing, the town centre and accountability.

The branch said Harrogate faced increasing pressure on infrastructure, rising local government costs and the challenge of protecting the town’s character and prosperity.

### Northern relief road assessment

Reform UK said it would support a fresh assessment of a northern relief road, examining alternative routes that avoid environmentally sensitive areas such as Nidd Gorge.

The submission argued that the previous study relied on traffic data dating back to 2015 and said further housing development and changing traffic patterns justified reassessing the economic case.

The branch also called for future development to take account of roads, schools, healthcare and drainage before further expansion takes place.

### Council finances and spending

The submission highlighted North Yorkshire Council’s financial position, citing a forecast budget shortfall of £17 million in the current year, rising to approximately £25 million annually by 2028/29, despite a 4.99% council tax increase.

Reform UK said the council’s reliance on reserves to balance its budget was not sustainable in the long term.

The branch called for projects such as Harrogate Station Gateway, discretionary 20mph restrictions and traffic-calming schemes to be assessed for necessity, affordability and value for money.

It said essential services, support for vulnerable residents and road maintenance should remain priorities, alongside efforts to improve procurement and identify efficiencies.

### Housing and development

Reform UK said Harrogate needed more affordable homes, particularly for young people and families, but argued that new housing should be accompanied by improvements to infrastructure.

The branch advocated properly planned communities incorporating housing, employment, schools, healthcare and transport links.

It cited Maltkiln, centred on Cattal railway station, as one example and said it would also examine whether Flaxby Park could accommodate a mix of homes and employment uses. The site had previously been proposed for housing but is now earmarked for employment development, according to the submission.

The branch said good rail connections, appropriate infrastructure and environmental protection should form part of any such development.

### Town centre and businesses

Reform UK said independent businesses, shops, restaurants and hospitality venues were central to Harrogate’s economy and character.

It proposed reviewing parking charges and arrangements, including at Victoria and Jubilee car parks, and working with local businesses to improve accessibility, cleanliness and the town-centre environment.

The branch also called for the impact of major transport schemes on trading conditions to be examined and for practical action to address antisocial behaviour.

It said business feedback, pedestrian footfall, commercial vacancy rates and new business openings should be used to assess the success of town-centre initiatives.

### Calls for greater accountability

The submission raised concerns about the costs and performance of major projects and council-owned businesses.

It cited more than £2.3 million in consultancy costs associated with Harrogate Station Gateway before construction, cost increases at Kex Gill and a £7.5 million loss recorded by council-owned Brierley Homes.

Reform UK proposed a publicly accessible register of major council projects and businesses, setting out original budgets, expenditure, forecast costs, delivery milestones and emerging risks.

It said quarterly reporting would help residents identify problems and scrutinise decisions before additional money was committed.

The branch also pledged to publish an independent assessment of selected local projects within six months of the May elections, followed by quarterly updates.

The proposals were submitted by Jonathan Swales, chair of Reform UK Harrogate and Knaresborough, in response to questions from The Harrogate Informer.
