A planning dispute over a rural plot of land where goats and chickens are reared has led to a retrospective planning application.

In April 2025, Bradford Council issued an enforcement notice following “unauthorised development” on a section of field off Whitehill Road in Oakworth.

There are currently three outbuildings, two polytunnels, and a CCTV system including three masts on the site, as well as a blue material spread out over much of the ground.

Now a retrospective planning application to keep these structures on the Bronte Country site has been submitted to the Council by Nnagbo Aniaha, and it claims the works are “sympathetic to the countryside setting.”

The plans argue that the site is use for “small scale agricultural use” and is home to “modest structures.”

The land is currently used for keeping goats and chickens, growing vegetables, fruit trees and other produce and for storing tools and feed.

The CCTV poles were fitted for security purposes, and there are several birdhouses on the site.

The site is part of an area of land divided into 30 smaller parcels, which are managed by different owners.

![The site in Oakworth](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21650603.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The site in Oakworth *(Image: T&A)*

The planning application says: “The applicant is the freehold owner of the land and has used the plot for a smallholding since 2021.

“The purpose of this application is to regularise the existing use and modest structures following the Enforcement Notice issued by Bradford Council on April 15 2025.

“The Council confirmed via correspondence shared with the MP’s office that they will validate and consider a planning application despite the existing Enforcement Notice and will suspend active enforcement while the application is determined, provided it is submitted promptly.

“The structures are small and logically arranged to serve agricultural needs. No structure obstructs the shared access route or interferes with neighbouring plots.

“This evidence contradicts any assumption that development is excessive or visually harmful.

“The use of the land for keeping small livestock and growing food crops is fully in keeping with the historic character of the wider area.

“All structures are modest in scale, low in height, rural in appearance (timber construction), sited centrally on the plot, not visible in a harmful way from Whitehill Road and consistent with the appearance of adjacent smallholdings.

“The development remains low-impact, proportionate, and sympathetic to the countryside setting.”

A decision on the application is expected in December.
