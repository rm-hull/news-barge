Whitby has swapped its usual seaside sights for something a little darker, as hundreds of goths, punks and alternative characters descend on the town for its famous Goth Weekend.

From elaborate Victorian-inspired outfits and towering hats to dramatic make-up, corsets and plenty of black lace, visitors have been bringing their own unique style to the North Yorkshire coast.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21655991.jpg?type=mds-article-620) *(Image: Simon McCabe Photography/Northern Echo Camera Club)*

This year's autumn Whitby Goth Weekend ran from Friday, October 30, until Sunday, November 1, with Halloween falling right in the middle of the celebrations.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21655938.jpg?type=mds-article-620) *(Image: Shane Dew/Northern Echo Camera Club)*

The annual gathering has once again given visitors the chance to show off their creativity, catch up with fellow enthusiasts and soak up the unmistakable atmosphere around the town.

And, as these pictures show, there has been no shortage of striking outfits and characters out and about in Whitby.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21655941.jpg?type=mds-article-620) *(Image: Shane Dew/Northern Echo Camera Club)*

Whitby Goth Weekend was founded in 1994 by Jo Hampshire and has since grown into one of the world's best-known events celebrating goth and alternative culture.

What began as a much smaller gathering, with just 40 enthusiasts, has developed into a twice-yearly event, with the town welcoming visitors from across the UK and further afield.

The event takes place in spring and autumn, with Whitby's atmospheric streets, historic buildings and coastal setting providing a fitting backdrop for the occasion.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21655942.jpg?type=mds-article-620) *(Image: Shane Dew/Northern Echo Camera Club)*

The town's connection with Bram Stoker's Dracula is another reason it has become such a natural home for the gothic community.

Stoker visited Whitby in 1890, and the town's famous abbey ruins, graveyard and 199 steps have since become closely associated with the novel and its enduring legacy.

Away from the outfits and photo opportunities, the weekend also features live music and alternative events, alongside the Bizarre Bazaar Alternative Market.

The market is a major part of the celebrations, with more than 100 indoor stalls offering everything from gothic clothing and jewellery to artwork and unusual accessories.

Whether visitors arrived for the music, the shopping, the social side or simply to show off an outfit they have spent months putting together, Whitby Goth Weekend has become a fixture of the town's calendar.
