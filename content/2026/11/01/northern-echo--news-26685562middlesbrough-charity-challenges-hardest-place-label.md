A North East youth charity has hit back at a report branding its town the hardest place in England for children, saying it ignores young people’s strength and potential.

Middlesbrough-based Linx Youth Project responded after the Children’s Commissioner for England published The Childhood Lottery.

The report called Middlesbrough the hardest place in England for children to grow up.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21653605.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Young people gather with Mayor Chris Cooke at the Linx 33rd Anniversary *(Image: Supplied)*

Wayne Mason, chief executive of Linx Youth Project, said: "We always talk about deprivation and poverty and crime, but we don't talk about community.

"For Linx, this is a vital part of the conversation.

"Across Middlesbrough, young people are developing friendships, discovering interests, building confidence and finding places where they feel safe, supported and valued."

He said the charity’s youth clubs give young people space to see friends, join activities, build life skills and form positive relationships with trusted adults.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21653601.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Young people in McDonalds for 15+ session learning about money management with Youth Worker Hannah *(Image: Supplied)*

He said these experiences matter most to young people facing difficulties.

Mr Mason said the report has sparked debate about Middlesbrough’s problems but overlooks the positive experiences and opportunities available to its young people.

He said statistics can identify where support and investment are needed, but cannot tell the whole story.

Eilís Sexton, deputy of Linx Youth Project, warned that the report’s findings could affect young people and families.

She said the label could make young people lose faith in a better future.

Ms Sexton said: "Young people deserve honesty about the challenges they may face, but they also deserve encouragement, opportunity and the confidence to believe their circumstances do not have to determine their future.

"At Linx, the organisation sees the pride young people have in their town and the importance they place on friendship, belonging and community.

"These are not always reflected in national rankings, but they are fundamental to how young people experience their lives."

Linx Youth Project has worked with young people and communities for more than 33 years, helping them connect, learn and grow.

The organisation accepts that youth work cannot solve deep-rooted inequalities alone, but says accessible youth services are vital.
