Nearly half of CalMac’s permanent fleet of lifeline ferries serving Scotland's island communities is past their 'sell by date' set by the Scottish Government for replacement with 15 of 33 vessels now at least aged 30 or over.

The Herald can reveal that the ageing ships include vessels which have suffered engine, propulsion, electrical and mechanical faults, adding to the pressure on an operator already struggling to maintain reliable services.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21654597.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The 15 CalMac ships, above *(Image: Newsquest)*

CalMac’s own replacement-planning benchmark is 25 years, while the Scottish Government’s 2012 Ferries Plan put the expected working life of a vessel at around 30 years.

The plan launched by then transport secretary Keith Brown stated: "Our policy will be to replace vessels once they reach the end of their working lives. "

Critics say the continued reliance on these older ships highlights the scale of the renewal challenge facing Scotland’s lifeline ferry network with just one ferry, Glen Sannox actually online to replace an ageing ferry - Isle of Arran at least six years late.

Mr Brown said in launching the plan: "We are fully committed to delivering first class sustainable ferry services to our communities, stimulating social and economic growth across Scotland. We believe that the planned improvements set out in our Ferries Plan will enable our rural and remote communities to thrive, and continue to make a significant contribution to Scotland’s economy. "

**The 15 vessels aged 30 or over**

## 1. Isle of Cumbrae — 49 years old

Entered service in 1977, it was originally earmarked to be replaced by 2016 and remains a vital lifeline backup vessel for Scotland's west coast.

![MV Isle of Cumbrae](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/18380964.jpg?type=mds-article-620)MV Isle of Cumbrae *(Image: NQ)*

However, its recent utility has been heavily compromised by chronic engine breakdowns and mechanical faults stemming from its advanced age.

Compounding these technical failures, severe crewing shortages have frequently left the vessel sitting idle and unable to provide relief during network disruptions.

## 2. Isle of Arran — 42 years old

Introduced in 1984, it remains a critical, heavily relied-upon workhorse in the CalMac fleet, frequently redeployed across the network to cover for other vessels.

![MV Isle of Arran](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/19740746.jpg?type=mds-article-620)MV Isle of Arran *(Image: Newsquest)*

However, its advanced age has triggered significant mechanical vulnerabilities, highlighted by a severe bow thruster failure that took it out of service for over a month.

Because of its obsolete parts, repairs require custom fabrication, compounding wider fleet shortages and leaving island communities exposed to severe route disruption. It was due for replacement by 2019.

## 3. Loch Linnhe — 40 years old

The vessel entered service in 1986. On January 30, 2025, the MV Loch Linnhe suffered extensive structural damage after grounding on a charted rock in the Sound of Gigha due to sailing without a legally required voyage plan.

![MV Loch Linnhe](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21413737.jpg?type=mds-article-620)MV Loch Linnhe *(Image: CalMac)*

Deemed a constructive total loss by assessors, the vessel was knocked out of service for 218 days before returning on 6 September 2025.

To protect network capacity ahead of the Small Vessel Replacement Programme, CalMac bypassed its minor insurance policy to fund the £1 million hull repair bill entirely out of pocket. Its replacement was due by 2019.

## 4. Loch Riddon — 40 years old

Also introduced in 1986, it has seen its reliability compromised by notable mechanical incidents, including a major holiday-weekend engine gearbox breakdown in July 2021 and an unexpected vehicle ramp fault in August 2023.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/12216187.jpg?type=mds-article-620)MV Loch Riddon *(Image: Newsquest)*

Because of widespread network delays and systemic vessel failures across the aging CalMac fleet, she has been continuously shuffled away from her primary stations to provide emergency cover.

It was one of the small vessels earmarked for replacement by 2019.

## 5. Loch Striven — 40 years old

The 1986 vessel has faced recurring technical challenges on the Oban–Lismore route, heavily impacted by an aft generator exhaust failure on June 1, 2026.

This mechanical vulnerability was compounded just two weeks later when a critical vehicle ramp fault pulled the vessel completely out of service for ten days.

These consecutive summer breakdowns highlight the aging ferry's fragility, adding strain to a tightly scheduled small vessel network that relies on her heavily during fleet overhauls.

## 6. Loch Ranza — 39 years old

Introduced in 1987, it has seen its reliability on the Tayinloan–Gigha route repeatedly tested by age-related technical faults, most notably a severe breakdown of its bilge pump system in autumn 2026.

This sudden mechanical failure stranded dozens of vehicle passengers on the island overnight, exposing the limitations of CalMac’s foot-passenger-only emergency charter replacements.

The ageing vessel's fragile status is further strained by complex network reshuffling, such as her temporary displacement during winter overhaul windows in January 2026.

## 7. Isle of Mull — 38 years old

The ferry entered service in 1988 and was due to be replaced by 2019. It has faced persistent technical disruptions on its vital Oban–Craignure route, highlighted by a sudden bridge window wiper breakdown on September 14, 2026 that cancelled key morning crossings.

![MV Isle of Mull](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21117780.jpg?type=mds-article-620)MV Isle of Mull *(Image: NQ)*

Originally slated to be retired at the end of 2026, the veteran ship received an emergency reprieve in October 2026 to remain active until 2031 because replacement vessels had failed to arrive on schedule.

To make the vessel survive another five years, CalMac has been forced to commit to major reliability overhauls, following an exhausting winter period where she was constantly shuffled to plug emergency gaps across the wider network.

## 8. Lord of the Isles — 37 years old

Built in 1989, it has been pushed to its absolute limits as a network gap-filler, undergoing relentless route reassignments between Lochboisdale, Tiree, and Colonsay throughout the winter of 2025/2026.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/19646494.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Lord of the Isles *(Image: Newsquest)*

Her scheduling volatility intensified in March 2026 when she had to be hastily deployed to run emergency support sailings for Mull to combat cascading fleet failures. While the veteran ferry continues to run, this intense over-deployment across multiple fragile west coast lifelines leaves her primary South Uist route highly exposed to wider network disruptions.

Moves are only just being made for her replacement, even though it was scheduled to be replaced by 2025, and procurers are expected to formally award and sign the shipbuilding contract after March 31, 2027.

## 9. Loch Dunvegan — 35 years old

The 1991 vessel continues to provide a vital link on the Colintraive–Rhubodach route but was due to be replaced by 2025. Its reliability was hit by a notable mechanical vehicle ramp fault in October 2026.

This specific technical issue forced the ship into restrictive "single-ended" operations, complicating loading and unloading procedures for passengers.

It remains under immense operational pressure, having carried an exhaustive deployment load throughout the winter of 2025/2026 to compensate for a backlog of breakdowns across CalMac's wider small-boat fleet.

## 10. Loch Fyne — 35 years old

Introduced in 1991 it was due to be replaced by 2025. It has faced repeated disruption due to critical engine overheating issues, which twice forced it off the water and caused severe route delays in June 2024.

Following unscheduled repairs to its aging propulsion units in September 2024, the veteran ferry was knocked out of action again in February, 2025 by a major forward main engine failure.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/17466316.jpg?type=mds-article-620)MV Loch Fyne *(Image: Newsquest)*

These escalating breakdowns highlight the heavy mechanical strain placed on the vessel as it is continuously deployed to cover high-volume commuter lifelines.

## 11. Loch Buie — 34 years old

The vessel entered service in 1992 and was due to be replace by 2025. It continues to maintain the critical Fionnphort–Iona lifeline crossing, but its aging systems have triggered multiple service suspensions, including a notable fleet-wide disruption window in April 2024.

The vessel's mechanical fragility was brought to the forefront in April 2026 when it suffered a sudden complete power blackout and died immediately after pulling away from the slipway.

This latest failure underscores the systemic vulnerability of Iona's island transit, which relies heavily on a vessel plagued by historic steering seal failures and electrical switchboard degradation.

## 12. Loch Tarbert — 34 years old

Also introduced in 1992 and due to be replaced by 2025, it has faced notable service interruptions, highlighted by a sudden engine failure on the Tobermory–Kilchoan route that cancelled vehicle crossings and required an emergency passenger charter while engineers hunted for parts.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/20763698.jpg?type=mds-article-620)MV Loch Tarbert *(Image: CalMac)*

The 34-year-old vessel has also been subjected to tightly squeezed operational windows, including a frantic winter overhaul rotation in October 2025.

This mechanical vulnerability is continually aggravated by network-wide disruptions, which forced the small ferry to sustain gruelling, high-capacity summer coverage on the high-volume Largs–Cumbrae crossing throughout 2026.

## 13. Caledonian Isles — 33 years old

The 1993 vessel was forced into a massive 18-month service blackout starting in January 2024 after inspectors discovered catastrophic hull degradation that required a £12 million structural steel rebuild.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/20903843.jpg?type=mds-article-620)MV Caledonian Isles *(Image: CalMac/Rachel Keenan)*

Her highly anticipated return was further delayed by a sudden propeller pitch instability failure, prolonging the crisis before she finally completed successful sea trials. This unprecedented double-breakdown heavily destabilised the network, forcing CalMac to rely on expensive chartered ships and causing severe economic disruption to the Arran commuter route.

It remains in service despite being due to be replaced by 2025.

## 14. Isle of Lewis — 31 years old

Introduced in 1995, it is entering its final months of service, with CalMac confirming the veteran vessel will be permanently retired from the fleet by March 2027.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/19414169.jpg?type=mds-article-620)MV Isle of Lewis in Castlebay, *(Image: Rachel Connolly)*

In the lead-up to her retirement, the ferry has been subjected to relentless winter route shuffling across Ullapool, Stornoway, and Castlebay to plug severe capacity gaps left by other vessels.

## 15. Loch Bhrusda — 30 years old

The vessel which entered service in 1996 is heavily relied upon as a small relief ferry across the Western Isles, but its small size triggers automatic one-third vehicle capacity penalties whenever it is drafted to cover for larger broken-down vessels.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/17967622.jpg?type=mds-article-620)MV Loch Bhrusda *(Image: Newsquest)*

Her vulnerability to wider fleet failures was evident during August 2026, when she was pulled from her normal routines to run a highly restrictive, amended service on the Sound of Harris.

This operational strain worsened in October 2026 on the Mallaig–Armadale crossing, where its technical limitations and multi-route tasking forced CalMac to cancel heavily utilised daytime sailings.
