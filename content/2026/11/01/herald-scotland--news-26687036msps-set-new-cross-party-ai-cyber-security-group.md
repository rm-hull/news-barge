MSPs have launched a new cross-party group on artificial intelligence and cyber security, as an expert warned that no laws exist to stop criminals selling stolen personal data on the dark web.

The Cross-Party Group (CPG) on AI and Cyber held its inaugural meeting at Holyrood on Tuesday, bringing together MSPs with experts from the private sector and academia.

The SNP MSPs Zen Ghani and Calum Kerr were elected as co-conveners. Other members include Reform's David Kirkwood, Scottish Tory Stephen Kerr and Patrick Harvie from the Scottish Greens.

The group will be supported by Cyber and Fraud Centre Scotland, a social enterprise that specialises in helping organisations stay secure.

Jude McCorry, the centre's chief executive, said the group would discuss the need for laws to protect data from being sold and shared on the dark web.

She said: "I am delighted that this CPG has been established around AI and Cyber, as we need to look at both in tandem.

"From a cyber perspective I know the group will be discussing the need for laws to protect our data from being sold and shared on the dark web.

"At the moment there are no laws to protect against the selling or sharing of our personal data which criminals are sharing on the dark and clear web after cyber attacks.

"As a nation we have got away lightly on this aspect so far, even after some high-profile attacks that have occurred and personal data has been breached.

"But as we head into an era of speed and innovation around AI, we need to ensure that we are looking at the basics and ensuring our data is protected by law."

NHS Dumfries and Galloway had a "large volume" of stolen patient and staff data published on the dark web by a ransomware group after a cyber attack in March 2024.

In 2023, the Rhysida gang advertised data stolen from the University of the West of Scotland, including staff bank details and national insurance numbers. It demanded 20 bitcoin, about £452,640, and said the data would be sold to the highest bidder.

Last year, West Lothian Council confirmed that data had been stolen from its education network after the Interlock group claimed responsibility, and that the data included personal and sensitive information.

The new Holyrood group aims to strengthen MSPs' understanding of artificial intelligence and cyber security, and will "provide a forum for open, evidence-based discussion of what these technologies mean for Scotland".

![Members of the CPG on AI and Cyber at its inaugural meeting](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21654554.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The Cross Party Group held its inaugural meeting last week *(Image: CPG on AI and Cyber)*

Mr Ghani said: "AI is already having an impact in Scotland, bringing opportunities for our public services and economy, alongside challenges we need to understand.

"As one of the youngest MSPs in the Scottish Parliament, I'm particularly conscious that the decisions we make today could shape the lives of future generations for decades to come.

"As this technology develops, it is essential that we as MSPs build our knowledge of the role AI could play in Scotland's future.

"We have a responsibility to consider its wider implications for people across Scotland. That means understanding the risks as well as the potential.

"This group is here to bring together different perspectives, examine the evidence and help parliamentarians understand the choices Scotland faces."

Mr Kerr said that, given technology is "now so central to our lives", it was "incumbent on us as MSPs to go a bit deeper".

He added: "We do not all need to become experts, but we do need to understand enough to ask the right questions.

"I worked in technology for 28 years, including on AI. The right questions now include how it could be used, who would benefit and what safeguards would be needed.

"Cyber security matters just as much, and AI is changing it. It can accelerate the attacker, but it can accelerate the defender too.

"This group will be a place to explore these issues openly, hear a range of views and challenge assumptions. We won't always agree, but that's healthy. What matters is that we approach these discussions with an open mind."
