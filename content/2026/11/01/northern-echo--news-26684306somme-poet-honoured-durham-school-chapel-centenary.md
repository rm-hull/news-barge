The words of a young soldier killed on the first day of the Battle of the Somme have been brought to life in a new piece of music as a County Durham school marked the centenary of its historic chapel.

Durham School hosted the world premiere of composer and former pupil Will Todd's setting of Before Action, written by Old Dunelmian William Noel Hodgson shortly before his death in 1916, during a special centenary service on Saturday, October 24.

At the heart of the service was Todd’s new anthem, a musical tribute to Hodgson’s poem.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21651394.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Current pupils and alumni joined forces for the premiere, conducted by Will Todd during a special centenary service at Durham School *(Image: Supplied)*

Dr Michael Alderson, headmaster of Durham School, said: "For 100 years, our Chapel has stood at the heart of Durham School; it is a place where generations of pupils have gathered to worship, reflect, and mark important moments in school life.

"It remains so today.

"It was a privilege to welcome so many members of our community to celebrate this milestone.

"The premiere of Will Todd's beautiful setting of Noel Hodgson's words was a particularly moving reminder of the Chapel's origins and the connections which bind generations of Dunelmians together."

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21651395.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The Rt Revd Nigel Stock reflected on the chapel’s enduring legacy as generations of the Durham School community gathered to celebrate its 100th anniversary *(Image: Supplied)*

The chapel, built in 1926 under Headmaster Richard Budworth, stands as a memorial to Old Dunelmians who died in the First World War.

Hodgson himself is commemorated in its stained-glass windows, and his loss was one of those that inspired the creation of the chapel.

The anthem was performed by a combined choir of current pupils and alumni, conducted by Mr Todd.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21651393.jpg?type=mds-article-620) *(Image: Supplied)*

Fellow Old Dunelmian Ralph Woodward played the organ, with conducting and organ duties shared by the school's director of music, Simon Walker.

The service also included a performance of John Ireland’s Greater Love.

The Rt Revd Nigel Stock, a former pupil and retired Church of England bishop, gave the address, reflecting on the chapel’s legacy and its role in helping young people explore questions of faith.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21651391.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Durham School's historic chapel *(Image: Supplied)*

His family’s connection to Durham School predates the chapel itself.

His grandfather attended the school before the chapel was built, while his maternal uncles worshipped there in its early years.

Bishop Stock also won the school’s Hodgson Prize for Poetry, adding a personal resonance to the centenary tribute.

Among the guests were the High Sheriff of Durham, Dr Arnab Basu MBE; the Mayor of Durham, Cllr Amanda Hopgood; and the Revd Canon Michael Hampel, Precentor of Durham Cathedral.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21651390.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Durham School marked its chapel’s centenary with the world premiere of a moving anthem inspired by First World War poet William Noel Hodgson *(Image: Supplied)*

Following the service, celebrations continued with the Old Dunelmian Society’s Annual Dinner in Big School.

Former pupils aged from 18 to 93 gathered to reconnect and celebrate their shared history.

Durham School is part of the Durham Cathedral Schools Foundation, which provides co-educational independent education for pupils aged 3 to 18.

Its chapel remains a central place of worship and community life.

William Noel Hodgson, often referred to as "the poet of the Somme," was educated at Durham School.

He wrote Before Action shortly before his death on July 1, 1916.

The school continues to honour his literary legacy through the Hodgson Prize for Poetry.

The poem gained wider recognition during the centenary commemorations of the Battle of the Somme in 2016, when it was read by Prince Harry at the Thiepval Memorial.

Will Todd, who attended Durham School from 1981 to 1988, is an internationally recognised composer, conductor, and jazz pianist.

He is best known for Mass in Blue and The Call of Wisdom, which was commissioned for Queen Elizabeth II’s Diamond Jubilee.

Ralph Woodward, who attended the school from 1985 to 1990, is an acclaimed conductor, organist, and arranger.

He previously served as assistant director of music at King’s College, Cambridge.

The Rt Revd Nigel Stock attended Durham School from 1963 to 1968 and later served in a range of senior roles within the Church of England.

These include Bishop of Stockport, Bishop of St Edmundsbury and Ipswich, Bishop to the Forces, Bishop for the Falkland Islands, and Bishop at Lambeth.

The Old Dunelmian Society connects alumni of Durham School, Chorister School, and Bow School, and supports lifelong engagement with the foundation.
