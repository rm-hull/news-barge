A new mural celebrating 35 years of Teesside Park has been unveiled, capturing everything from Toys R Us and Primark to some of Teesside's biggest landmarks.

Created by Stockton-based artists Abby and Owen, the mural is on display opposite Hobbycraft and charts the park’s history.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21653837.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Artists Abby and Owen, who created the Teesside Park mural *(Image: Teesside Retail Park)*

Teesside Park officially opened in 1991, with boxer Frank Bruno among those at the launch, but its story began several years earlier.

Plans to transform the former Stockton Racecourse site were announced in 1987, with Toys R Us becoming the first store to open at Teesside Park three years later.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21653835.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Teesside Park anniversary mural *(Image: Teesside Retail Park)*

Over the last three decades, Teesside Park has changed considerably, welcoming scores of new shops, restaurants and leisure attractions.

The mural features those arrivals alongside other memorable moments from across Teesside.

Among the milestones featured are Showcase Cinema’s arrival in 1993, followed by two major Teesside landmarks, the Tees Barrage in 1994 and Middlesbrough FC’s Riverside Stadium in 1995.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21653836.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Teesside Park anniversary mural *(Image: Teesside Retail Park)*

Other nostalgic highlights include Millennium nightclub, which opened in 1996 and was known for its distinctive funfair-inspired interior.

Fast-forward almost two decades, and Mary Berry officially opened Barker and Stonehouse in 2015, before Primark became another major arrival in 2024.

Abby and Owen created the mural from scratch with hand-drawn illustrations and handwritten notes, giving it inky textures inspired by printed archive material.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21653833.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Teesside Park anniversary mural *(Image: Teesside Retail Park)*

For the Stockton-based artists, there was also a personal connection to the project, with Abby having childhood memories of visiting Teesside Park.

They said: “We wanted the design to feel like a journey through the centre’s history, while also capturing some of the wider moments and memories that make the region what it is.

“As artists based in Stockton-on-Tees, it was particularly special to create something celebrating our local area.”

Catherine Furlong, centre director at Teesside Park, said: “Reaching our 35th anniversary is a real milestone for us, and we wanted to mark it with something that belongs to the whole community and celebrates the people who have made the centre what it is today.

“The mural tells our story including how we've grown, the brands we've welcomed, and the local organisations and community groups we’ve been privileged to support.”

The mural is on display opposite Hobbycraft, inviting visitors to spot familiar places, people and moments from the past 35 years.
