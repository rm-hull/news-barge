Anti-social behaviour (ASB) has fallen by almost a third across Teesside, new Cleveland Police figures show.

Cleveland Police recorded 30 per cent fewer reports of anti-social behaviour in September than in September 2025, but admitted the figures may not reflect residents’ experiences.

Problems reported to police range from youths riding electric bikes and scooters and groups causing intimidation around parks and shops, to public drug use and suspected dealing from homes.

Officers also believe ASB is significantly under-reported, with many residents reluctant to come forward because they believe nothing will be done.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21481268.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Cleveland Police officers conduct a raid on a property in South Bank, as part of Operation Artemis *(Image: PA WIRE)*

But police say the figures show why reporting ASB matters, with officers able to use dispersal orders, Criminal Behaviour Orders and closure orders to tackle persistent problems.

Recent cases across Teesside have highlighted the impact persistent ASB can have on communities.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21274681.jpg?type=mds-article-620) *(Image: Cleveland Police)*

Earlier this week, a property on West Lane in Middlesbrough was ordered to close for three months following multiple reports of drug activity and ASB.

Police had previously seized Class A drugs at the address before working with Middlesbrough Council to secure the closure order.

In June, a house on Ayton Drive, Redcar, was closed for three months after evidence of repeated nuisance, ASB and criminality.

Police called closure orders an "integral part" of their strategy to protect communities.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21653325.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Ayton Drive closure order, Redcar *(Image: Cleveland Police)*

The crackdown forms part of wider work being carried out by Cleveland Police, including Operation Artemis - a force-wide operation targeting criminals which brings officers and regional partners together for targeted days of action across different areas.

Since its launch, the operation has tackled crime in Middlesbrough through arrests, the recovery of a stolen motorcycle, the crushing of 21 off-road bikes, the seizure of 11 abandoned vehicles and the recovery of more than 500 illegal vapes.

Its latest major outing in Stockton on October 21 resulted in 29 arrests for alleged offences including drugs, violence and theft.

The initiative has also targeted fraud, wanted suspects and violence against women and girls.

Despite the fall in reported incidents, Cleveland Police acknowledged the improvement may not be felt by everyone, particularly those still dealing with persistent problems close to home.

Officers are urging residents to continue reporting ASB, helping police and partner agencies identify problem areas and take action as quickly as possible.

Incidents can be reported to Cleveland Police by calling 101 or through its online reporting service.
