Britain's Got Talent star Myra DuBois believes adults could learn an important lesson from children, arguing that young people are naturally curious about differences rather than prejudiced by them.

The comedian, drag performer and television personality made the comments ahead of her return to Bradford's Alhambra Theatre in this year's production of The Pantomime Adventures of Peter Pan.

Reflecting on the show's themes of childhood, imagination and never growing up, Ms DuBois suggested children often approach the world with far fewer assumptions than adults.

## 'Children don't see gender'

Speaking about what adults could learn from younger generations, Ms DuBois said children possess a natural openness that many lose as they get older.

She said: "Children walk through life not assuming everyone they meet has an ulterior motive, they're just like, 'Hello, who are you?' and that's the beginning and end of it."

Ms DuBois added that children are often curious about differences without attaching judgement to them.

She said: "If they meet anyone who's different to them, let's say, for example, they're a different gender or a different ethnicity, they'll show a curiosity that is void of bigotry,"

Ms DuBois added: "They want to know who that person is and why they're different, and it begins and ends there. I think that's beautiful."

## Returning to Bradford in a brand new role

Myra DuBois is returning to the Alhambra after appearing in last year's panto, but insisted she did not want to simply repeat what audiences had seen before.

She told the T&A: "They originally wanted me to play Captain Hook because I was the Wicked Queen last year, I said, if I'm coming back to Bradford, I do not want to do the same thing.

"Audiences don't want me to do the same thing. If they've come again, they want to see the same people doing different things."

Instead, she will take on the role of the Magical Myrmaira, a brand-new character created specifically for her.

## A character created just for Myra

The larger-than-life mermaid is a new addition to the world of Neverland and gave the performer the chance to help shape the role from scratch.

"It's a huge honour and an exciting blank canvas," she said.

"A lovely opportunity to create something new. There are mermaids in Neverland, so she's a Neverlandian and a mermaid."

Speaking about her extravagant costume, Myra joked that audiences should expect even more glamour as the show progresses.

She said: "This is my opener frock.it just gets bigger and more glamorous from here on. This is my casual look."

The performer, whose career has included appearances on Britain's Got Talent, UK comedy tours and West End stages, will star alongside Bradford panto favourite Billy Pearce in The Pantomime Adventures of Peter Pan at the Alhambra Theatre this Christmas.
