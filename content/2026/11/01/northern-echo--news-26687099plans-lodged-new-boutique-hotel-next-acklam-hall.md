Plans have formally been lodged for a new boutique hotel next to Middlesbrough's Acklam Hall.

The 59-bedroom hotel, which has been described as a “major new investment opportunity”, would be located to the right of the town’s only Grade I-listed building, off Hall Drive.

The project - hoped to support and strengthen the hall’s wedding, conference and events business - was first announced back in August.

The U-shaped hotel, which features a courtyard, central garden, and three ranges of accommodation, has been designed to “preserve the significance” of the historic hall.

And now, following a consultation period, proposals have formally been lodged with Middlesbrough Council for the project.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21344980.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Plans for a 59 bedroom boutique hotel in Middlesbrough *(Image: PYRAMID ARCHITECTURAL DESIGNS)*

It comes just two years after a previous bid for a 55-bedroom hotel and spa were rejected after councillors argued it was “not in keeping with the historic building”.

Landowner James Watson, who runs Over Ice Business Consultants and Teesside Restaurant Week, previously told the Echo how talks were ongoing with national hotel brands, as well as potential investors and operators from the Far East.

He argued there was a gap in the market, saying the hotel is “about investing in the long-term future of Acklam Hall and creating a sustainable destination”. 

## **Hotel plans**

If approved, the new hotel would have three floors and wrap around a central garden, intended to deliberately echo the U-plan of the hall itself.

The lower ground floor would boast 19 bedrooms, storage, and housekeeping while the upper ground floor would have the entrance, reception, and office space as well as 19 rooms.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21344979.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Plans for a 59 bedroom boutique hotel in Middlesbrough *(Image: PYRAMID ARCHITECTURAL DESIGNS)*

The first floor of the building would have 21 bedrooms, which would be serviced by a passenger lift and two stair cores.

The planning application describes how scale has been considered in the design due to the previous refused plans, with the lower ground floor being concealed.

The building would be made up of red brick, with details taken directly from the hall’s chimneys, and tall vertical window bays.

The garden would have a circular water feature and formal planting, “reinterpreting the designed-landscape character of the hall's grounds”.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21344978.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Plans for a 59 bedroom boutique hotel in Middlesbrough *(Image: PYRAMID ARCHITECTURAL DESIGNS)*

Vehicles would use Hall Drive to access the hotel, with 25 car parking spaces provided to the front as an extension of the existing parking area.

“The proposals would preserve the significance of the Grade I listed building and its setting, and would preserve and enhance the character and appearance of the conservation area”, the application said.

The plans have taken on board feedback from previously refused 2019 plans for the site, which was described as “inappropriate” and also included a spa.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21345043.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Mayor Chris Cooke, land owner James Watson and architect Ben Wears at the site of a potential hotel next to Acklam Hall in Middlesbrough *(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)*

Historic England also objected to the previous scheme, which was described as looking “like an annexe” to the new building.

James told the Echo he hoped construction could begin in late 2027.

Acklam Hall was built in 1683 by William Hustler, a cloth merchant from Bridlington - and for the next two centuries it was occupied by generations of his family.

The hall was sold to the Middlesbrough Corporation in 1928, which opened it as a grammar school for boys in 1935.

By 1967, it merged with Kirby Girls' Grammar School to form Acklam High School before becoming a sixth form and latterly Middlesbrough College.

The college moved to a new purpose-built campus in Middlehaven in 2008 and the land was then sold by the council in 2014 for £907,000.

From 2008 until to 2015, the venue stood empty, with Acklam Hall Ltd finally starting work on its restoration in 2014 before it reopened as a wedding venue and restaurant in 2016.

Tomahawk Steakhouse then opened in the hall in 2020, taking over the Brierley restaurant, which just months ago was transformed into The Brasserie.
