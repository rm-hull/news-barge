Ok, I admit it. Before this week, I'd never been to Toby Carvery.

It's something I'm deeply ashamed about, particularly as Bradford's branch is just a stone's throw away from the T&A offices - a gravy-soaked beacon in an otherwise grey and dreary industrial estate.

I grew up outside the UK, on an island where roast dinners existed but franchised carvery restaurants didn't.

Somehow, despite living here for years, Toby Carvery remained a glaring gap in my cultural education.

Once my colleagues discovered this, there was only ever going to be one lunch destination.

Plans were swiftly rearranged. Sandwiches were abandoned, the Morrisons salad bar remained untouched and, before I knew it, I was crossing the threshold of a place my colleagues regard as a sacred institution.

## No Yorkshire Pudding!

My inexperience in the franchised carvery world was soon exposed when I picked up a menu.

"What are you reading that for?" a colleague asked.

"So I know what to order."

I was laughed at for this response. Apparently, you just go up and pick.

I joined the queue and was asked if I wanted a Yorkshire Pudding.

I said no.

At the time this seemed entirely reasonable. I've never been a huge Yorkshire pudding fan and there was plenty more food to come.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21653577.jpg?type=mds-article-620)My colleague's considerably less controversial selection. *(Image: T&A)*

## What did I get?

With that decision made, I moved along the line and onto the main event.

I was informed by another colleague to get "all the meats", which sounded like a good idea at the time.

When confronted by "all the meats", I got scared and, after a brief internal debate, landed on turkey and beef.

The turkey selection would later prove controversial.

Moving along the line, I loaded up on vegetables, potatoes and a mysterious potato-and-parsnip creation that looked far better than it tasted.

Content with my selections, I headed back to the table convinced I'd just navigated a complex social situation like a seasoned pro.

## Turkey selection proves controversial

What I hadn't appreciated was declining a Yorkshire Pudding in Yorkshire is apparently somewhere between a social faux pas and a crime tried at The Hague.

The reaction from colleagues was swift and venomous.

"No Yorkshire?!"

"I wanted room for veg."

The explanation did not help.

It was at this point I realised there are certain rules that exist beyond my understanding.

My choice of turkey didn't fare much better.

"Turkey is just dry chicken," said the same colleague.

Despite the criticism, I pressed on.

## My Toby Carvery verdict

And honestly? I enjoyed it.

The beef was good, the gravy did exactly what gravy is supposed to do, and whilst the potato-parsnip thing tasted vaguely like Play-Doh, most of the food was hearty and filling.

And I understood the appeal.

For around £11, you get a substantial meal, plenty of choice and enough food to make you question whether you'll ever need to eat again.

Would I do anything differently next time?

Absolutely.

The turkey may not survive the cut.

And after receiving what felt like a county-wide intervention, I'll definitely be getting a Yorkshire Pudding.

Overall, I'd give my first Toby Carvery experience a **7/10.**

I went in a complete novice and left slightly wiser, considerably fuller and with one major regret.

Never say no to a Yorkshire pudding in Yorkshire.
