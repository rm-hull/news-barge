The former head chef of Northumberland's first Michelin-starred restaurant is bringing a Polish twist to the traditional Sunday lunch after opening his own County Durham restaurant earlier this month.

Marek Dziedzic, who spent four years at Restaurant Hjem and played a key role in helping the restaurant retain its Michelin star, has launched a new three-course Sunday Special at Restaurant Biedronka, based at The Farmhouse, South Causey Inn.

The £50 set menu is designed as an alternative to the traditional roast dinner, with dishes inspired by the chef's Polish heritage and seasonal British ingredients.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21650876.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Traditional Polish Rosół chicken broth with handmade noodles, carrots and shredded chicken served at Restaurant Biedronka. *(Image: sean elliott photography)*

The launch comes just weeks after Mr Dziedzic fulfilled a long-held ambition by opening Biedronka, a restaurant named after the Polish word for ladybird.

The venue reflects both his upbringing in Poland and the culinary techniques he developed during a career that has taken him from kitchens in Krakow to Michelin-starred restaurants in the UK.

Speaking ahead of the restaurant's opening, Mr Dziedzic said he wanted to create something personal that offered diners a genuine taste of Polish cuisine while celebrating produce from the North East.

Now, after a successful first month in business, he is hoping the restaurant's Sunday offering will become a regular part of customers' weekend plans.

"Sunday lunch has always been about getting people together, enjoying good food and taking your time over it," he said.

"That's exactly what I'd like Biedronka to be part of. Somewhere you can come with your family or friends, enjoy a really lovely meal and head home feeling relaxed, happy and well fed."

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21651027.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Anna Petrea of the kitchen team prepares dishes at Restaurant Biedronka, The Farmhouse at South Causey Inn. Picture: Sean Elliott Photography *(Image: sean elliott photography)*

Since opening on October 1, Mr Dziedzic said he has been overwhelmed by the response from diners, particularly to the Polish elements of the menu.

"I've been blown away by the response over these first few weeks," he said.

"People have been so open to what we're doing, especially the Polish dishes. It's been lovely seeing people try different types of dumplings, sometimes for the first time, and really enjoy them.

"The food is very personal to me, so seeing people embrace it in the part of the world I now call home means a lot."

Unlike Biedronka's regular menu, where customers can choose individual dishes, the Sunday Special is served as a fixed three-course menu, allowing the kitchen team to adapt the offering to the seasons and experiment with different recipes.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21650874.jpg?type=mds-article-620)A selection of Polish-inspired dishes served at Restaurant Biedronka at The Farmhouse, South Causey Inn. *(Image: sean elliott photography)*

One recent menu featured Rosół, a traditional Polish chicken broth with homemade noodles, followed by slow-braised pork with potatoes and beetroot salad.

Dessert was Karpatka, a classic cream-filled Polish cake.

Mr Dziedzic said the changing nature of the menu would encourage customers to return throughout the year.

"I like the idea of people coming back on different Sundays and finding something new on the menu," he said.

"It gives us the freedom to cook what we think will be really good, while keeping the food comforting and generous. That's what I want a Sunday at Biedronka to feel like."

The chef added that he wants the restaurant to offer more than just good food.

"There's something lovely about finishing a Sunday knowing you've had a really good meal, spent time with people you care about and haven't had to do the washing up," he said.

"That's the feeling I'd like people to associate with Biedronka."

The Sunday Special is served at Restaurant Biedronka, The Farmhouse at South Causey Inn, with sittings from noon and 2.30pm, priced at £50 per person.
