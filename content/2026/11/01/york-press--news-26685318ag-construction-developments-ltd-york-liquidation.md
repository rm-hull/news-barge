A York-based construction company has gone into liquidation with reported debts topping £500,000.

AG Construction and Developments Ltd of Front Street, Acomb, was incorporated in December 2018 and it went into liquidation on October 29, with Parker Walsh of Stockport appointed liquidator.

Companies House records its sole director as Graham Walker.

A report known as a statement of affairs outlines the financial situation and the circumstances behind the liquidation.

Such a report has also been sent to the creditors as part of the liquidation process.

An explanatory note has also been sent to the creditors to supplement the estimated statement of affairs, which outlines the financial situation of the company.

The note says the company employed 11 staff.

Annual accounts, it continued, had been prepared but not filed to Companies House for the year ended March 31, 2025.

They showed a turnover of £978,955, with a pre-tax profit of £69,740.

This compared with £698,052 turnover the previous year and a pre-tax profit of £21,271.

The report notes ‘difficulties’ with the company’s demise attributed to cost increase in materials, general decline in turnover over a significant period, and jobs being delayed or running over expected end times.

It said in August, it became apparent the company could not settle its debts and sought advice from Parker Walsh on the financial situation of the company and the options available.

In September, Parker Walsh was instructed by Graham Walker to help in prepare an estimated statement of affairs, convene company members and seek a decision with creditors regarding the appointment of a liquidator.

The statement of affairs says that on October 16, AG Construction and Development had assets consisting of £9,025.67 cash in the bank.

It had 11 preferential creditors – staff owed £1 each as a preferential creditor – someone paid first.

The company also had secondary preference creditors – someone paid next – owed £131,563.55.

This consisted of HMRC owed £100,711.55 in PAYE and £30,852.00 in VAT.

In addition, there were unsecured creditors – those who are paid last – who were owed a further £389,260.94 between them.

This consisted of 23 trade and expense creditors owed £380,817.94 between them.

Employees were owed £11 between them and HMRC was owed a further £8,432 in industry levies.

This gave a total shortfall or deficit of £511,809.82.

With the company having issued and called up capital of £100, this gave a final shortfall of £511,909.82, according to the estimated statement of affairs.
