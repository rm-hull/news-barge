People in Middlesbrough are likely to have two votes for mayor in May 2027.

On Thursday, May 6, voters across the town will head to the polls to elect their ward councillors as well as a mayor to lead the local authority.

The current Middlesbrough Mayor is Labour’s Chris Cooke – not to be confused with Tees Valley Tory Mayor Ben Houchen, who represents five local areas, including Middlesbrough, and has different responsibilities at Tees Valley Combined Authority (TVCA), compared to functions carried out by local councils.

The next regional mayoral election is scheduled for May 2028.

An ongoing government policy change would see a return to the voting system used up until four years ago, when the Elections Act 2022 came into effect and saw mayoral elections undertaken using the same voting system as in general elections.

While it is the stated ambition of the government to see a return to the supplementary vote system – where voters have a first and second preference vote – the ministry for housing, communities and local government (MHCLG) is not yet certain that it will be in operation by next May.

While the English Devolution and Community Empowerment Act 2026 provided the primary legislation for a change in the voting system at a mayoral level, secondary legislation still needs to be drawn up to see it implemented for local mayors.

The previously used supplementary vote system has already returned at a regional mayoral level, as seen with this summer’s by-election in Greater Manchester.

The return to supplementary vote is replacing first past the post, which is used in parliamentary elections and only permits voters one choice.

Middlesbrough Council is one of the few local authorities in the land where residents elect not only their local ward councillors, but also a mayor, who leads the council.

Once the mayor is elected by voters from across the town, they then have the power to select councillors to sit on their Executive.

This differs from the Leader and Cabinet model, used by neighbouring authorities, where councillors select a leader amongst themselves.

Currently, eight Labour councillors sit alongside Mayor Cooke to form the Executive. Labour has a majority of councillors in Middlesbrough, with 24 out of 46. When it comes to votes in the council chamber, Mayor Cooke also has one, meaning there is a maximum total of 47 votes.

Middlesbrough voted decisively in favour of having a mayoral model back in 2001, with the first elected mayor, Ray Mallon, being voted in during May 2002, before being re-elected twice in 2007 and 2011. On all three occasions, he gained an outright majority of ballots that were cast.

In 2015, however, no individual candidate received more than half of the votes, meaning that second preference votes were counted – the first and only time that this has happened.

On this occasion, after all the first preference votes were counted, Dave Budd had 33.8 per cent of the vote, compared to Andy Preston’s 28.9 per cent. As the top two candidates, they went into their own run-off – all other candidates were eliminated.

Those voters who didn’t vote for either of the top two candidates with their first preference votes, then had their second preference examined. If their second preference vote was for either of the top two candidates, then it was added to that candidate’s total.

Eleven years ago, this nearly flipped the result. Although Mr Preston was trailing by more than 2,000 votes, after first preference votes were counted, he gained many more second preference votes than his rival, meaning that Mr Budd only just clinched victory, with a 256 vote majority.

Four years later, in 2019, Mr Preston won a landslide victory, so second preference votes were not counted, and then a change in legislation from the previous Conservative government meant that all mayoral elections, at the combined authority and local council level, were to be held using first past the post, meaning no one needed a majority and the candidate with the most votes wins, just as it is when electing MPs.

This means that in the 2023 local election in Middlesbrough, current Mayor Chris Cooke was elected with 40.2 per cent of the vote, beating then-incumbent Mr Preston by 800 votes.

If the supplementary vote system was still being used at this time, then second preference votes would have been counted and could have altered the result.

Now, the Labour government has reverted back to the supplementary vote system for regional mayoral elections, and after introducing the necessary secondary legislation early next year, it is anticipated that the supplementary vote system will come into force on February 18, 2027.
