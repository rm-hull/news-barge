A County Durham strongman has called for answers after claiming a rival who beat him to a world title should never have been allowed to compete in the novice category.

Martin Taylor, 35, from Consett, travelled thousands of miles to Phoenix, Arizona, for the Static Monsters World Championships after months of gruelling training.

But despite finishing second in the log lift and joint first in the deadlift, he claims the competition's organisers have refused to explain why the eventual winner was allowed to enter the novice category despite previously finishing second in another strength competition.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21653368.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Martin Taylor, 35, from Consett at the Strongman Static Monsters competition in Phoneix, Arizona *(Image: PROVIDED)*![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21653316.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The results from Martin's event*(Image: PROVIDED)*

Martin competed in the men's novice under 95kg category at the September 19 event, where he recorded personal bests in both lifts.

He was announced as runner-up, with Jacob Kotz taking the overall title.

However, after discovering Kotz had previously finished second in the novice male lightweight category at the 2024 Static Siege competition, Martin questioned whether he had met the published eligibility criteria for the 2026 event.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21653318.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The previous results where the winner had previously placed in the top three in the same competition *(Image: PROVIDED)*

The rules stated that athletes who had previously finished in the top three of any strength competition, including qualifiers, were not eligible to compete in the novice category.

Martin said he initially had no intention of challenging the result but wanted organisers to explain how the rules had been applied.

He said: "This isn't just a case of I've come second and I'm not happy about it."

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21653348.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The 2026 rules on Static Monsters website *(Image: PROVIDED)*

Martin had spent months preparing for the competition, training around four times a week while balancing life as a new father after welcoming a baby in January.

He also spent thousands of pounds travelling from Newcastle to Arizona, spending six or seven days away from his wife and eight-month-old child.

He said: "It's the training, it's the time spent committed to doing that, but also it's the money involved as well. I paid thousands of pounds to go to Phoenix, Arizona from Newcastle to go and compete expecting, you know, the rules to be properly applied."

Martin contacted Static Monsters on September 20 to raise his concerns.

The organisers initially acknowledged his complaint and said they would investigate.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21653314.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The response Martin received from Static Monsters *(Image: PROVIDED)*

However, in a response on September 24, they reportedly told him that had they been informed of the eligibility concern before the competition, they would have acted, but would not change the results after the event.

They said: "To do this after the event is not in anyone’s benefit."

Martin said the response left him questioning why the timing of a potential rules breach should determine whether action was taken.

He also pointed to the case of a friend from his gym who had been disqualified from the competition before the event after previously finishing in the top three of another competition.

Martin said: "You've done it for one of my friends. So how, like, you know, what's going on?"

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21653315.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Static Monsters has since blocked Martin on social media *(Image: PROVIDED)*

The organisers also told him they would no longer hold a novice category at Static Monsters because checking every athlete's eligibility on a global scale was too difficult.

Martin said he would have accepted an explanation if organisers had confirmed why Kotz was eligible, but claims his repeated requests for clarification went unanswered.

He also said he appeared to have been blocked from the organisation's Instagram and Facebook accounts after raising the issue.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21653317.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The waiver all participants received and signed *(Image: PROVIDED)*

A formal complaint followed on September 28.

In response, Static Monsters offered to refund Martin's competition entry fee, but the proposed settlement included confidentiality and non-disparagement conditions.

Martin said the agreement, which he had reviewed with a solicitor, was too restrictive and did not address the issue at the heart of his complaint.

He rejected the offer and instead submitted his own settlement proposal, seeking an explanation of Kotz's eligibility and a correction of the result if the rules had not been correctly applied.

Martin also offered to sign a confidentiality and non-disparagement agreement on what he considered fairer terms.

He said: "All I want is an answer to my question of why he was allowed to compete if he was breaking the rules. And if he isn't eligible, then they should change the results to reflect that."

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21653365.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Martin Taylor, 35, from Consett at the Strongman Static Monsters competition in Phoneix, Arizona *(Image: PROVIDED)*

The dispute has also raised questions over the competition's own participation waiver, signed by athletes before taking part.

Martin's signed document included provisions allowing participation to be revoked or suspended for breaches of the competitive rules.

He said this made the organisers' refusal to reconsider the result particularly difficult to understand.

Despite the dispute, Martin stressed he had enjoyed competing in America and had congratulated Kotz on the day.

He said his complaint was not about being unhappy with finishing second, but about ensuring the same rules applied to everyone.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21653367.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Martin Taylor, 35, from Consett at the Strongman Static Monsters competition in Phoneix, Arizona *(Image: PROVIDED)*

Martin said: "I shook the guy's hand on the day and I said, like, congratulations, you know, well done.

He added: "It's more about wanting them to take some accountability for their mistake instead of brushing it under the rug."

Finishing second also earned Martin an invitation to next year's world finals in South Africa, but he now questions whether he would take part again.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21653366.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Martin Taylor, 35, from Consett at the Strongman Static Monsters competition in Phoneix, Arizona *(Image: PROVIDED)*

He said: "Why would I want to go now, train and spend lots of money again when I could go and then someone who's not even supposed to be competing alongside me goes and then wins and they just don't take any action."

Martin said he wanted organisers to provide a straightforward answer over whether the winning competitor met the published eligibility requirements and, if not, why the official standings had been allowed to stand.

The Northern Echo contacted Static Monsters for a comment, but did not receive one in time for publication.
