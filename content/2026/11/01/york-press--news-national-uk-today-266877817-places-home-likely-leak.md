## People urged to check 7 areas around the home in danger during the winter

Lofts, roofs and ceilings are among the areas of the home most at risk of leaks during the winter >(Image: Getty Images)

Homeowners have been advised to check various areas of their property to make sure there aren't any leaks during the winter months.

When autumn transitions into winter, the weather tends to take a turn for the worse, with an increased chance of storms and rain.

As a result, it also increases the chance of areas of the home leaking.

Neil Mckenzie, a home safety expert from Halton Stairlifts, said: "Water finds the weak spots in a home very quickly.

"A loose roof tile or a dodgy seal around a window might not seem like much, but after strong winds and driving rain, it can let a surprising amount of water in.

"The good news is that most of the common problem areas can be checked in under an hour."

Mckenzie has outlined seven areas that should be regularly inspected to prevent water damage during the wetter months.

## 7 areas of the home you need to check for leaks

### Ceilings

Brown or yellow stains, bubbling paint, or a sagging ceiling are warning signs that water may be entering from above.

Top-floor ceilings and areas around light fittings are particularly vulnerable, as water can travel along frames and appear far from the source of the leak.

A bulging ceiling should never be ignored.

If it appears to be holding water, keep everyone out of the room and call a professional immediately.

### Roof spaces

From ground level, check for damaged or missing tiles and pay attention to areas around chimneys and where the roof meets the walls.

Blocked gutters and downpipes are major culprits, as overflowing water can be forced under tiles or down exterior walls.

If you spot a problem, do not climb ladders in wet or windy conditions.

It’s safer to call a roofer or someone you trust to investigate.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21655933.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Damaged or missing tiles could lead to leaky roofs *(Image: Getty Images)*

### Windows

Inspect the seals and mastic around window frames for cracks or gaps, and ensure drainage holes aren’t blocked.

While some condensation is normal, water on the sills after rain or damp patches below the window often indicate a failed seal.

### Doors

Front and back doors are exposed to the elements and can easily let in water during a storm.

Check for worn draught excluders, gaps around the frame, and damaged thresholds.

Wind-driven rain can seep under doors, creating wet patches that are a slipping hazard.

### Lofts

If it’s safe, use a torch to inspect the loft.

Look for damp insulation, water marks on the timber, or daylight shining through the roof.

Ensure that the cold water tank and any exposed pipes are properly lagged, as the first cold snaps often arrive with seasonal storms.

### Conservatories

Conservatory roofs are prone to leaks due to their multiple joints and seals.

Check for cracked panels, gaps where the structure meets the house, and blocked gutters.

Leaks here often drip onto hard floors, creating a slip risk.

### External walls

Cracked render, damaged mortar, and blocked air bricks can all allow water to penetrate the home.

Watch for damp patches on interior and external walls, especially in corners or behind furniture.

Also ensure that overflow pipes and outdoor taps aren’t dripping onto brickwork.
