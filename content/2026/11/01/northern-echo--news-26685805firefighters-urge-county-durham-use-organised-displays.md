Firefighters are urging people across County Durham and Darlington to attend organised Bonfire Night displays after crews tackled more than 90 incidents on November 5 last year.

The service attended 91 callouts on Bonfire Night in 2025, prompting warnings about the dangers of fireworks and bonfires at home.

County Durham and Darlington Fire and Rescue Service (CDDFRS) has joined police and both local councils to launch a safety campaign ahead of this year’s celebrations.

Kev Bell, station manager at CDDFRS, said: "The best way to protect your loved ones this Bonfire Night is by attending an organised fireworks display.

"Organised displays have appropriate safety measures in place and trained staff handling safety-regulated fireworks to protect spectators.

"Whereas having fireworks or bonfires at home increases the risk of people suffering injuries such as burns and fires breaking out, which can put lives and property at risk.

"We cannot stress enough how important it is that only an adult handles fireworks and that these are bought from a licenced retailer, bearing the CE or UKCA mark on the box."

Mr Bell urged anyone setting off fireworks at home to follow the Firework Code and keep bonfires away from sheds, fences and trees.

He also advised keeping a bucket of water or a garden hose nearby, and warned that children under five must never use sparklers, which should be handled with gloves.

Police have also warned about the legal consequences of misusing fireworks.

Superintendent Dean Haythornthwaite, head of neighbourhood policing, said: "Our number one priority is making sure everyone stays safe and enjoys Bonfire Night.

"We would like to remind everyone that it is illegal to set off or throw fireworks, including sparklers, in the street or other public places.

"Anyone found using fireworks illegally could receive a fine of up to £5,000 and a prison sentence of up to six months.

"We would encourage people to attend an organised event, as this is the safest way to enjoy fireworks.

"Please have fun but stay safe."

Police and fire crews will carry out joint patrols to prevent crime, disorder and anti-social behaviour on Bonfire Night.

Councils are also urging residents not to build unauthorised bonfires on public land and to report any they spot.

Councillor Jim Garner, cabinet member for stronger communities at Darlington Borough Council, said: "Bonfires can be dangerous and may result in incidents that place unnecessary pressure on emergency services.

"Responding to these incidents can take fire crews away from critical emergencies elsewhere.

"We urge residents to think carefully before building a bonfire and help us protect our communities by reporting any bonfires being built in public spaces.

"If you are planning on using fireworks, remember to always follow the Firework Code."

Owen Cleugh, Durham County Council’s acting head of community protection, said: "Organised firework displays are the safest way to enjoy Bonfire Night.

"Unauthorised displays lead to avoidable incidents and divert fire service resources from people who need them most.

"If you plan to buy fireworks, use a licensed seller and follow all safety precautions, particularly when storing them."

He urged people to report unauthorised bonfires while they are being built so the council can work with landowners to remove them quickly.

Report unauthorised bonfires in County Durham at www.durham.gov.uk/fireworksafety.

In Darlington, report them by calling 01325 406999 or emailing [customerservices@darlington.gov.uk](mailto:customerservices@darlington.gov.uk) during office hours.

In an emergency, dial 999 and ask for the fire service.
