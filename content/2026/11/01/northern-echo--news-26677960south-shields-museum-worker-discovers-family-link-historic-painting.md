A museum worker has discovered an unexpected family connection to a 17th-century painting.

Rory Brady, who works at South Shields Museum & Art Gallery, found the link while researching his genealogy on Ancestry.

The portrait of Catherine, Marquise de Seignelay, by French artist Pierre Mignard, is currently on display as part of the National Gallery’s Masterpiece Tour.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21641798.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The Marquise de Seignelay *(Image: South Shields Museum & Art Gallery)*

Mr Brady said: "I recently discovered that I’m a distant cousin of Charles de Lorraine, Count of Marsan, the second husband of Catherine, Marquise de Seignelay.

"It’s quite remarkable that I’ve been invigilating the painting, without knowing until now that the Marquise was a distant relative."

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21641796.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Rory and the Marquise de Seignelay *(Image: South Shields Museum & Art Gallery)*

His research revealed a family connection to Charles de Lorraine, providing a personal link to one of the figures linked to the portrait.

The painting is part of Mignard: The Power of Posing, an exhibition exploring how portraiture communicates identity, status and personality through pose, clothing, expression and setting.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21641803.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Mignard The Power of Posing *(Image: Colin Davison)*

These elements offer clues to the lives and aspirations of the sitters.

Pierre Mignard’s The Marquise de Seignelay (1691) is more than a portrait of a French aristocrat.

**Read More:**

It reflects family, legacy and social ambition in 17th-century France.

Catherine-Thérèse is depicted as the sea goddess Thetis, with her son as Achilles, linking their lineage to classical heroism.

By portraying mother and son through the stories of Thetis and Achilles, Mignard celebrates the bond between parent and child while linking their family identity to ideas of heroism and greatness.
