Kate Forbes has urged the Scottish Government to support the controversial Rosebank oilfield.

Writing in the Sunday Times, the former deputy first minister said it was "the right decision" and wholly in line with SNP policy.

The call will add to the pressure on First Minister John Swinney, who has maintained a studiously neutral stance on the future of the largest undeveloped oil and gas field in UK waters.

There are also growing Cabinet tensions in Westminster around the project, with Energy Secretary Miatta Fahnbulleh reportedly being left increasingly isolated, with Prime Minister Andy Burnham and Chancellor John Healey keen to push ahead, according to the Mail on Sunday.

Treasury documents published after last week's Budget priced in future production revenues from the field. And in the House of Lords, Energy Minister Baroness Curran admitted that the Office for Budget Responsibility would "have to republish the figures" if Rosebank does not go ahead.

At First Minister's Questions on Thursday, Mr Swinney was repeatedly pressed by both Scottish Labour leader Michael Marra and Scottish Conservative leader Russell Findlay to state whether he supported Rosebank.

The First Minister refused four times to say whether he backed the field.

"The process of consideration of the Rosebank application has got to be applied," he told MSPs, "but it's got to be applied by the quasi-judicial process that the Prime Minister talks about, where the implications of the consenting of that development has got to be compared with the importation of oil and gas."

![Kate Forbes and John Swinney during her time in government](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21504143.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Kate Forbes and John Swinney during her time in government *(Image: PA)*

In her column, Ms Forbes pointed to research by Robert Gordon University which warned the north-east could lose up to another 18,000 offshore energy jobs by 2035 if investment in new opportunities fails to keep pace with diminishing oil and gas activity.

She wrote: "There has been some welcome progress. The decision to approve Jackdaw reflects the continued importance of domestic oil and gas production.

"Rosebank, meanwhile, still awaits a decision, even as the Office for Budget Responsibility's forecasts published alongside the Budget include future production from the field.

"It is the right decision to approve Rosebank, for more reasons than I can adequately list. And doing so is entirely consistent with the motion recently passed at SNP conference, allowing the Scottish Government to back Rosebank, for the benefit of Scotland."

While no final version of that strategy has yet to materialise, the First Minister signalled a shift in his party's position in May, saying energy security should be considered in any new decisions.

Delegates at the last SNP conference backed a motion brought by energy minister Stephen Gethins, which supported domestic energy production as "clearly preferable to importation".

It added that the "SNP should champion policies that safeguard Scottish jobs and skills, reduce reliance on imported fuels and use the economic benefits of Scotland's remaining oil and gas resources to help fund the transition to a net-zero economy".

According to the Mail on Sunday, senior UK Government officials are bracing themselves for higher energy prices if, as expected, Donald Trump suffers heavy losses in this week's US mid-term elections.

US diplomats have reportedly told several Middle East countries and regional allies to be prepared for potential renewed military action, as well as the possibility that Iran will attempt to retaliate against them.

A source told the Mail on Sunday: "A lame duck Donald promises to be challenging, so the priority is 'power sovereignty' and 'cheap energy'. We obviously want clean energy, but when it is also cheap energy."

The source added: "Even Ed [Miliband] is starting to see the logic of this."

![Scottish Secretary Douglas Alexander, Energy Secretary Miatta Fahnbulleh and Chancellor of the Exchequer, John Healey during a visit to Shell UK's St Fergus Gas Terminal](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21613897.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Last month, Ms Fahnbulleh told The Herald there would be "no delay" on Rosebank *(Image: Paul Reid/PA)*

Ms Fahnbulleh's parliamentary aides circulated a memo to MPs insisting she would have the final say on the project. The memo said: "No decision has been made. The Energy Secretary is the only decision maker and will consider all relevant factors as part of the quasi-judicial process. No other Cabinet member or minister has any role in this decision and does not see the advice on it."

Last month, Ms Fahnbulleh told The Herald there would be "no delay" on the Rosebank decision.

Meanwhile, Mr Healey has said the UK Government will stop buying and building new power generation unless it brings down bills.

The Chancellor was speaking to BBC One's Sunday with Laura Kuenssberg. He said: "There's big changes that mean an end to gas standing charges for households next year. An end to the government policy costs simply loaded onto consumer bills through levies. And an end to this business of buying and building new generation unless it adds to cheaper power."

In Wednesday's Budget, Mr Healey announced a new cost control framework under which the government will only buy and build new generation projects that can connect to the grid and lower bills.

He also said the framework would cover all aspects of energy bills, and that he would scrap the upcoming Gas Shippers Obligation levy, backing hydrogen projects directly from government instead.

Older renewable projects are to move onto fixed-price contracts, to weaken the link between gas and electricity prices.

In a column for the Times on Saturday, he said the same test would apply at the next renewables auction: "We will take that approach into our next renewables auction, only buying projects that can lower bills while strengthening our energy security."

"This is not a retreat from clean power. It is the credible route to it," he wrote.

The Mail on Sunday reported that ministers are increasingly alarmed by the cost of so-called "wasted wind", where wind farms are paid to switch off because the grid cannot carry their power.

It said the National Energy System Operator has admitted the cost of disconnecting turbines will reach nine billion pounds by 2029, adding up to £100 to domestic bills. According to the paper, almost £7 million was paid to wind farm owners to switch off on Tuesday alone, with more than £40 million paid to gas plant owners to cover the shortfall.

On BBC Scotland's The Sunday Show, Energy minister Martin McCluskey said: "If you were designing an energy system from scratch today, you wouldn't design it in the way we've got it at the moment, which is why the Prime Minister said that energy is going to be a key part of his 10-year plan to show that we can change the way that the energy system works and make sure that we can give people cheap, clean power."

In her column, Ms Forbes described the decision to change Acorn's funding as the “most bewildering development” in the Budget.

The carbon capture project in Aberdeenshire was due to receive £200m in grant funding from the UK Government, but the Chancellor changed the commitment to the same amount in loans or an equity stake.

Acorn proposes storing emissions from across Scotland under the North Sea seabed and could support about 15,000 jobs at its peak.

UK Government energy minister Martin McCluskey told BBC Scotland’s The Sunday Show: “We awarded £200 million in grant funding at the last spending review. Some of that has already been drawn down by the developers, and the money that they’ve already drawn down remains in place. For the remainder, and for any future support, instead of it being through grant funding, it will be through loans and equities.”

According to the Scotsman, just £12m in grant funding has actually been drawn down.

The Minister insisted the change was better value for the taxpayer because it could give the UK Government a stake in the project, and that budgets had been reprioritised to fund the defence investment plan.

Mr McCluskey said: “We want that project to succeed, and the developers who I’m speaking to later this week will come forward with proposals, and that funding is on the table.”
