**Plans for the temporary use of the Union Street fire site are "close to completion", the council has confirmed.**

Concept designs for 'meanwhile use' of the space, at the corner of Union Street and Gordon Street, are set to be announced in the coming weeks.

At a Merchant City and Trongate Community Council meeting on Tuesday, October 27, residents asked attending councillors what was happening with the site.

Cllr Angus Millar confirmed work was ongoing to develop plans for the site, which is under multiple ownership.

![builders at sight](https://www.glasgowtimes.co.uk/resources/images/20653087.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The scene after the Union Street fire *(Image: Colin Mearns)*

A Glasgow City Council spokesperson said: "Concept designs for the meanwhile use of the site are being developed and are now close to completion, and details will be announced in the coming weeks."

The Glasgow Times reported in July that plans were being drawn up to have the site of the fire back in use before the end of this year.

Space for the businesses that were displaced, a public event area, temporary retail, and leisure are all being considered.

A ‘Boxpark style’ development with shops and bars could be installed while a permanent solution is found.

![](https://www.glasgowtimes.co.uk/resources/images/21652021.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Site of the Union Street fire pictured on Friday, October 30, 2026. *(Image: Colin Mearns)*

The area was previously occupied by several businesses including Sexy Coffee, Blue Lagoon, Shelter Scotland, and many others, such as tattooists, make-up studios, and hairdressers.

Glasgow City Council, through the Union Street Recovery Group, has appointed O'Donnell Brown architects to produce a design concept for a ‘meanwhile use’ for the site.

Susan Aitken, former leader of Glasgow City Council, told the Glasgow Times it was a sign of the willingness to make progress on returning the site to active use.

The 'meanwhile use' is expected to be in place for up to three years.

![The height of the fire on Glasgow's Union Street](https://www.glasgowtimes.co.uk/resources/images/20635470.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The height of the fire on Glasgow's Union Street on Sunday, March 8, 2026. *(Image: Supplied)*

The Union Street fire on Sunday, March 8, saw the blaze spread from a vape shop and through the historic B-listed structure at the corner of Union Street and Gordon Street, next to Glasgow Central Station.

Only the facade of the Victorian building was left standing, but it was later demolished due to safety fears.

Central Street, Union Street and Gordon Street were fully reopened in July.
