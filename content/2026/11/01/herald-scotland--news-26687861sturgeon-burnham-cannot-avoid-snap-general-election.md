Nicola Sturgeon has said she cannot see how Andy Burnham avoids a snap general election, despite the Prime Minister's repeated denials.

The former First Minister was speaking on BBC One's Sunday with Laura Kuenssberg, two days after Mr Burnham insisted there would be no early poll.

Asked about the Prime Minister's position after Wednesday's Budget, Ms Sturgeon said: "He has very, very limited, almost no fiscal room for manoeuvre. But he also has no political mandate for the big reforms he wants to make and I find it really difficult to see how he avoids a general election in the relatively near term."

On Friday, Mr Burnham was asked directly when the next election would be. He said: "I said yesterday, no election in 2027. I said that really, really plainly yesterday.

"I'll say it again today to you and the reason I say that is because I am not coming into this job full of political calculation in terms of 'what do I need to do for how it benefits me or the party?'"

Those remarks appeared to mark a shift. Before Labour's party conference in September, the Prime Minister said the vote would be in 2029.

Ms Sturgeon told the programme she wants Mr Burnham to succeed because the whole UK needs it, but she warned that the Prime Minister's political capital would not last.

She said: "Burnham's got a lot of goodwill right now. I think people are giving him the benefit of the doubt, but they want to see him start to deliver on the promises that he made."

She said the Budget had been worthy but very small, and that it would either prove a down payment on the fundamental reform Mr Burnham promised or the sum of what he had to offer. If it was the latter, she said, public anger would "pretty quickly kick in".

Speculation about the timing of an election has only intensified after John Healey delivered his first Budget.

Many of the voter-friendly measures have a short shelf-life, while some of the toughest decisions have been pushed back to next year's spending review.

The Liberal Democrats say there is a 30% chance of a general election on December 10 and party chiefs have put activits on a snap election footing.

Reform UK leader Nigel Farage has said he thinks there could be an election in May.

Some Labour MPs are so convinced a poll is coming that they have told their staff to prepare for one in the first half of next year, the BBC has reported.

She told The Herald: "This budget, if you're a new Prime Minister or a new Chancellor, coming in with all these opportunities at your disposal, it's quite a restrictive budget. It's about maintaining spending limits.

"It's about really I suppose not rattling the markets. It's not about helping folk necessarily. It's not about turning on the tax for public services. So I think that's interesting in many ways."

She added: "You could also argue that the PM might be deciding to hold things back for an early general election."

Ms Gilruth said: "I do think today's budget there's so much that's not there. If you compare the content of today's budget with the PM's speech at Labour Party Conference, the two do not match. So there's an incoherence there which suggests perhaps that something might be around the corner."
