There were a few seconds of deathly silence at the end of the screening of NAZA I attended at Glasgow’s GFT last week – seconds in which 99% of the audience was trying to absorb the gravity of the testimony it had heard.

There was much to take in. For 80 minutes, 24 Israeli military insiders had sat on rooftops in Tel Aviv giving chilling, damning interviews on the erasure of Gaza. NAZA is short for Nezek Agavi, the Hebrew for collateral damage; it is the term the military uses for the “acceptable” number of civilian victims from each attack it launches.

I will write more in a minute about the details of the film. But for now, suffice it to say that what it exposes is the way in which AI and algorithms are used to justify death tolls that would otherwise be unconscionable. And that the “acceptable” level of collateral damage would appear to be the annihilation of every man, woman and child on the 25-mile strip.

Horribly for the vast majority of cinema-goers who had come in good faith – to listen, to learn, to broaden their understanding – those seconds of deathly silence were broken by a middle-aged white man who launched into an antisemitic tirade. I won’t repeat exactly what he said for obvious reasons, but the gist was that Jewish people were genetically predisposed to the kind of violence we have seen let loose on Palestine under Benjamin Netanyahu’s leadership.

I should point out, here, that the man was jeered and booed, and told to “fuck off out of the cinema” by the rest of the audience. A few days later, the GFT sent out an email apologising for the outburst (which they could scarcely have foreseen), and clarifying that the police had been called and were now investigating.

Still, I am sure those who were forced to listen to this hate speech (some of whom were likely Jewish) continue to feel disturbed by the incident. That what the man said was abhorrent and fundamentally wrong is a given.

But his opinions were also so at odds with the thrust of the film we had just watched you had to wonder if he had engaged with its contents at all.

**Gaza genocide**

BLAMING ordinary Jews for the actions of a government is no more legitimate than blaming every Palestinian for Hamas’ s October 7 massacre, which is, paradoxically, how Benjamin Netanyahu is attempting to legitimise genocide.

NAZA exists to confront that paradox. It was made by a Jewish duo so outraged by the violence being inflicted on Gaza they were prepared to suffer the inevitable backlash, which has included death threats. Its directors, Yuval Abraham and Rachel Szor, are Israeli dissidents who lost family members in the Holocaust. Its Jewish executive producer, Jonathan Glazer, made the phenomenal Zone of Interest – a movie, which is set almost entirely in Auschwitz commandant Rudolf Höss’’ domestic quarters and explores Hannah Arendt’s concept of the “banality of evil”.

The “banality of evil“ is at the heart of this film, too. In Nazi Germany, the demonisation of Jews to the point where they were seen as inhuman and expendable was achieved through propaganda. There is no shortage of propaganda where Gaza is concerned either. But what is new and devastatingly effective is the process of othering by AI.

One of the systems the film examines is called “Lavender”. It uses surveillance data – call patterns, social media activity and address changes – to come up with a probability score for any given individual being a militant. Once it has identified a “target”, the same system calculates the maximum number of civilians it would be reasonable to sacrifice in order to take him or her out.

One military insider suggested this was a more effective, because less emotional, way of operating — much better, he said, than leaving such decisions to someone who had just lost a loved one to Hamas. But outsourcing moral judgements to technology is both deeply unreliable and a form of moral cowardice that leaves no one accountable for the consequences.

Calling innocent victims “NAZAs” is supposed to relieve those who press the button on the AI-flagged up airstrikes of the guilt that would normally accompany the razing of residential areas. Yet another programme called “Where’s Daddy?” – which eavesdrops on family members – means many of those interviewed had been listening to the voices of the “NAZA” children for several weeks before they sanctioned their slaughter.

**Hamas ‘operatives’**

ACCORDING to the military insiders, Lavender has flagged up tens of thousands of Hamas “operatives” (some of whom may just be tradesmen with militants in their contacts books). If you take a NAZA factor of 20 for junior Hamas operatives and up to 500 for senior ones – well, you do the maths: it’s the entire population.

The insiders varied in their reaction to their own revelations. Despite having come forward to be interviewed, some seemed relatively sanguine or defensive. Others were clearly experiencing self-disgust. One talked of how he had visited Auschwitz and wondered how it was possible to dispose of people in such a way, then realised he was doing the same. “The Nazis were the devil," he said. “But what are we?

The irony is that the film-makers have also been accused of being antisemitic though every part of their film undermines the claims the man at the GFT was making. Its aim is to dismantle the concept of collective guilt, not enforce it. It demonstrates that the ability to kill without compunction – or even to tolerate such killing as a bystander – is not the preserve of one specific ethnicity or nationality or demographic.

It is something that, in a given set of circumstances, human beings of all colours, creeds and countries are capable of. NAZA, then, is not only an exposé ; it is a call for universal self-examination.

Self-examination is something we seem increasingly incapable of as a society. We prefer to adopt crude political positions (often dictated by the side we have chosen in the so-called Culture War) than to chart a course through moral minefields.

Earlier this week, the pro-Palestinian, Jewish stand-up comedian Menachem Silverstein was heckled in a US comedy club by a women who shouted “I am against all Jews.”

He responded with a good deal more grace than I would have mustered. “Why am I on the hook for explaining to you why one religion of people aren’t all evil at the individual level?” he said. “I care about being Jewish. I think what’s happening in Israel is horrible, but those are two different things.”

**Closet Jew-haters**

EQUALLY, there are those who dismiss all those who take a stand against the atrocities being carried out in Gaza and the West Bank as leftist extremists, potential terrorists and closet Jew-haters when most are ordinary people responding in an entirely appropriate manner to an onslaught that ought to devastate anyone still in possession of a conscience.

Just as Donald Trump has licensed previously covert racists to air their opinions in public, so Netanyahu has legitimised the worst Israeli extremism. A recent Channel 4 news team interviewed a woman in an encampment next to the Gaza Strip who was waiting for the moment she could move in and occupy it. Humanitarian catastrophe as relocation opportunity.

At the same time, so many Israelis are opposed to Netanyahu that his bloc just lost its parliamentary majority in the recent elections. Tens of thousands are said to have returned home so they could vote against him. It is true that some of these people were motivated primarily by the security lapses that allowed October 7 to happen, and that the outcome was also influenced by the mobilisation of Palestinian citizens in Israel voting for Arab-led parties.

But there are plenty of Jews – inside and outside Israel – who are repulsed by what their country has been allowed to become, and gentiles who are at pains to make it clear their anger is directed only at the Israeli government .

Those who use Gaza as a cover for antisemitism, and those who indulge in indiscriminate and unsubstantiated accusations of antisemitism to silence protest, are two sides of the same coin.

They are steeped in prejudice and pursuing agendas which are not driven by compassion for those caught up in this conflict. While they go on promulgating hate, and causing a distraction, the senseless, algorithm-justified destruction of a people and their homeland endures.
