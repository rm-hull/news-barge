# Leeds bar in 'red zone' seeks 4am licence but hotels, police and council against plan

## Bridge Inn's bid to extend its late-night licence in Leeds' Freedom Quarter faces strong opposition

A well-known bar in the city's LGBTQ+ quarter faces potential refusal to serve alcohol until 4am following objections.

West Yorkshire Police and the council have challenged an application from the Bridge Inn to extend its Thursday-Saturday closing times by an additional two hours. Leeds City Council will examine a variation to the premises licence for the pub on Bridge End.

A licensing report noted the bar sits within a cumulative impact area (CIA), designated as experiencing elevated levels of alcohol-related anti-social behaviour. The premises also falls within a CIA "red zone" where the most stringent licensing controls are enforced.

A licensing report stated: "The council's CIA identifies this area as one experiencing the highest levels of alcohol-related crime, disorder and nuisance within Leeds city centre. Applications received from premises located within the red zone and operating during peak hours can expect representations from West Yorkshire Police and the licensing authority, recommending refusal."

The council also received objection letters on behalf of neighbouring hotels Indigo and Malmaison. Hotel Indigo's letter noted disruption was already occurring due to loud music in the vicinity after midnight. It stated: "This has resulted in disappointed guests and complaints about their inability to sleep or enjoy their accommodation without disturbance."

Star Pubs Trading Ltd stated the Bridge was managed by an experienced team and had procedures in place to prevent nuisance behaviour. These included CCTV measures and protocols for the safe dispersal of patrons at closing time.

The licence application stated: "Bridge Inn is an established licensed premises within Leeds' Freedom Quarter, rather than a new entrant to the late night economy." It added: "The variation is intended to support the responsible operation and improvement of an existing LGBTQ+ venue."

Councillors are set to review the application at a licensing sub-committee hearing at Leeds Civic Hall on Tuesday, 10 November. The pub, which is also known as Bridge Bar, sought new management after facing closure last year.

Its website stated: "Bridge Bar Leeds is back, bringing fresh flavour to the thriving LGBTQ+ scene."
