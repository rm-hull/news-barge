Located in the North Yorkshire countryside is a historic market town with two famous breweries, a vast Georgian marketplace and a mysterious stone temple hidden in nearby woodland.

Masham sits beside the River Ure, within easy reach of the A1(M), and has long been known for its brewing heritage, independent shops and picturesque surroundings.

The town, situated between Ripon and Leyburn, is home to Theakston and Black Sheep breweries, both of which have helped put Masham on the map.

Its cobbled marketplace, historic buildings and surrounding countryside make it a popular destination for visitors exploring the region.

However, one of its most intriguing attractions lies several miles outside the town, where a collection of standing stones resembles a smaller version of Stonehenge.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21646448.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Masham Sheep Show *(Image: Stuart Boulton/NORTHERN ECHO)*

At first glance, its standing stones, unusual formations and secluded setting could suggest an ancient ceremonial site.

However, the temple is not a prehistoric monument.

It is actually a Grade II-listed folly, built around 1800 for William Danby, a wealthy landowner associated with the Swinton Estate.

The structure was designed to resemble the stone circles and monuments associated with ancient Druids.

According to local history, the construction of the temple may have helped provide employment for people in the area.

Today, visitors can explore the woodland surrounding the temple, with walking trails and views across the Yorkshire countryside.

The attraction lies around four miles from Masham, making it an unusual addition to a day out in the historic market town.

Masham's brewing heritage is another reason the town attracts visitors.

Theakston Brewery traces its history to 1827, when Robert Theakston began brewing at the Black Bull.

The business went on to become one of Yorkshire's best-known brewing names, producing beers including Old Peculier.

The town is also home to Black Sheep Brewery, which was founded in 1992 by Paul Theakston.

The two breweries have become an important part of Masham's identity, with their history closely linked to the town's independent character.

At the centre of Masham is its large marketplace, surrounded by Georgian buildings, independent shops and traditional pubs.

The town has held a market charter since 1250, with its trading history closely connected to sheep farming.

During the medieval period, nearby Fountains and Jervaulx abbeys were heavily involved in the wool trade.

Masham became an important place for buying and selling sheep, helping explain why its marketplace is so unusually large.

St Mary's Church and its Norman heritage provide another reminder of Masham's long history.

Visitors can also explore the independent shops, galleries and cafés clustered around the marketplace.

The River Ure flows close to the town, while the surrounding countryside offers opportunities for walking and exploring.

The nearby Swinton Estate includes woodland, parkland and access to some of the area's most picturesque landscapes.

Other nearby attractions include Hackfall Woods, known for its dramatic woodland scenery and historic landscape features.

For motorists travelling along the A1(M), it can be easy to overlook the historic communities lying a short drive from the main road.
