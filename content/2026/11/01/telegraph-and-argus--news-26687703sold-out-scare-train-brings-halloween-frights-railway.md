When a popular tourist attraction billed a Halloween event as “Adults Only”, I got the sense it might be something more than your average spooky evening.

Others obviously shared my viewpoint, as tickets for the “Scare Train” sold out quickly after being put on sale by the Keighley & Worth Valley Railway.

People’s interest had been piqued by a Saturday night that promised a journey into the unknown for thrill seekers – but did it live up to expectations?

![Passengers on a packed platform at Oxenhope for the Scare Train](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21655747.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Passengers on a the platform at Oxenhope for the Scare Train *(Image: T&A)*

We gathered on a packed platform at Oxenhope Station as darkness began to fall across the Worth Valley.

Anticipation grew as the steam-hauled train drew into the station, with many of the passengers dressed in suitably frightening outfits.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21655752.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Passengers donned scary outfits for the experience *(Image: T&A)*

No sooner had the journey begun than the lights were dimmed and the ghastly noises began, courtesy of an audio system rigged up in the carriages.

As the train eased through Haworth Station, the first of the terrifying live performers burst into our carriage and handed out scares aplenty to onlookers.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21655750.jpg?type=mds-article-620)One of the actors entering a carriage on the train *(Image: T&A)*

A series of horrifying characters appeared as we progressed through Oakworth and Ingrow, including a man with a chainsaw, a sinister-looking clown and a girl holding a doll.

Having started the journey in a bit of a mickey-taking mood, my outlook on the whole experience changed when a screaming woman in a blood-stained wedding dress lurched towards me in a somewhat unnerving manner.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21655748.jpg?type=mds-article-620)A woman screaming at the top of her voice as she runs through the carriage *(Image: T&A)*

After a similar experience with one of the actors, one passenger shouted “leave me alone you horrible creature.”

By the time we reached Keighley, I was feeling a tad jittery after the onslaught of spine-chilling effects and immersive live actors.

At that point, the drama was ramped up to a new level as one of the actors launched a vicious attack on another, amid blood-curdling screams.

All the while, the audio system continued to issue chilling warnings of impending doom amid sinister-sounding laughter.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21655754.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The Scare Train draws into Oxenhope Station *(Image: T&A)*

Organisers warned this wasn’t for the faint-hearted – and so it was proving.

The journey back up the valley proved less eventful, with loud rock music being played and a party mood breaking out in our carriage.

Having been haunted in Haworth and daunted in Damems, I arrived back at Oxenhope feeling fulfilled by the experience.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21655751.jpg?type=mds-article-620)One of the Keighley & Worth Valley Railway workers in costume *(Image: T&A)*

I heard a couple of grumbles from fellow passengers, such as the speakers being too loud if you were sat close to them, and there not being enough spooky lineside displays to look at out of the windows.

However, the mood seemed generally one of enthusiasm and satisfaction.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21655753.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The Exhibition Shed at Oxenhope *(Image: T&A)*

Many of the passengers took the chance to head into the Exhibition Shed at Oxenhope, where a very enjoyable variety of live music was provided by Night Patrol.

To calm the nerves, I worked my way through a very pleasant pint of Farmers Jack O' Lantern, from Bradfield Brewery.

Perhaps I should have had a whisky, but I’d seen enough spirits by that point!
