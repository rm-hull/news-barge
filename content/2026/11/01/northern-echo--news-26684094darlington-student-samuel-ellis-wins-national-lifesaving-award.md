A Darlington student who helped save a cyclist suffering cardiac arrest at the roadside has won a national lifesaving honour.

Samuel Ellis, 16, a student at Queen Elizabeth Sixth Form College in Darlington, received a Royal Humane Society Resuscitation Certificate after the cyclist collapsed near Tideswell, Derbyshire, on April 10.

Samuel, a Northallerton Air Cadets sergeant and aspiring RAF pilot, was returning from a family break in the Peak District when he saw the cyclist fall from his bike and become unresponsive.

He rushed over and found the cyclist breathing, but the man soon stopped.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21650432.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Samuel Ellis *(Image: PETE BARRON)*

Samuel told a bystander to call 999 and immediately began CPR.

A passing dental nurse and a Community First Responder joined him, and the trio continued CPR for about an hour until two ambulances and an air ambulance arrived.

Paramedics worked for another hour before restarting the cyclist’s heart.

The cyclist was taken to hospital and later made a full recovery.

The Royal Humane Society said Samuel’s quick thinking and determination helped keep the cyclist alive until emergency crews took over.

Andrew Chapman, secretary of the Royal Humane Society, said: "Samuel, who was on his own at first, did a brilliant job.

"The prolonged bout of CPR ensured that the man was in the best possible condition for recovery when the paramedics arrived at the scene.

"Samuel richly deserves the award he is to receive."

The honour is the latest recognition for Samuel, who was named Young Citizen of the Year at Queen Elizabeth Sixth Form College earlier this year.

Years of caring for his family helped prepare Samuel for the lifesaving response.

Samuel has supported his mother, Vicky, since childhood as she has faced serious health problems including epilepsy, strokes and a brain tumour.

Alongside his studies and Air Cadets commitments, Samuel helps care for his family and supports their award-winning chocolate business in Leeming Bar.

Speaking about the rescue, Samuel said he did not see his actions as extraordinary.

"I didn't think I'd done anything special because I'm so used to being a carer. I just knew what to do," he said.
