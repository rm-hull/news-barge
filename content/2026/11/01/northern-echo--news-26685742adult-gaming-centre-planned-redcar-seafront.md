Vacant ground floor premises on Redcar’s Esplanade could be turned into an adult gaming centre if planning permission is approved.

Filey based XO Gaming is seeking change of use consent for an empty unit next to the Palace Hub.

The entire building, which contains flats above, was home to an amusement arcade at one time.

A previous application granted in 2020 sub-divided the ground floor into separate units with a view to a pub/café use and one of these would be converted.

A planning statement accompanying the new application submitted to Redcar and Cleveland Council said: “The proposal is a small scale leisure use in an existing building in the centre, on the seafront, in a building that was an amusement arcade for many years.

“It reuses a vacant unit, creates jobs and adds daytime activity to the seafront frontage.

“The proposed adult gaming centre would be adult only, would not sell or allow alcohol, would play no amplified music and would close at 23:00.”

It said the proposal accorded with various planning policy elements of the council.

XO Gaming is also applying to the council, as the licensing authority, for a premises licence under the Gambling Act 2005.

In a separate submission, the council’s licensing team said it did not object to such an application.

Between 25 and 40 gaming machines for over 18s are planned with the Redcar venue opening at 9am and being staffed by up to two people.

The planning statement said a company director would act as the premises manager and also be responsible for a noise management and litter management plan, which forms part of the submission.

Staff would be trained before opening and at regular intervals on age checks, safer gambling and the management plans.

Referring to the operation of the planned venture, there would be ‘Challenge 25’ age checks at the door with no cash machine or credit available.

CCTV coverage would be monitored from the front counter and available to police and the council on request.

The statement said the applicant recognised that there were other gambling premises on the seafront, including a licensed family entertainment centre – Nobles Amusements – but did not believe the proposal would result in an over-concentration of such outlets.

Various planning conditions were also suggested, including self-closing customer entrance doors to be kept closed except for immediate access and exit, and restricted delivery hours.

The plans are subject to a public consultation which ends on November 11.
