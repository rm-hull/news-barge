It was a night of spooky surprises, scary sights and plenty of Halloween fun as Aycliffe Bus Museum threw open its doors for a special evening event.

The Aycliffe and District Bus Preservation Museum hosted its 'Night at the Museum – Halloween Horror Spooktacular' on Saturday, October 31, welcoming visitors for an evening of family entertainment with a frightening twist.

The event brought a Halloween makeover to the Newton Aycliffe museum, with a packed programme of activities designed to give visitors something a little different for the spooky season.

Among the attractions advertised were a scare walk, a Halloween-themed disco and the chance to climb aboard the specially themed Scare Bus.

There were also children's treat bags provided by Shake 'n' Cake, refreshments and a can bar, while fancy dress was encouraged – with prizes on offer for the best costumes.

Adding to the entertainment were special performances from students at Bishop Auckland College of Performing Arts.

Aycliffe and District Bus Preservation Museum is run by the Aycliffe and District Bus Preservation Society, which works to preserve the area's public transport heritage.

The museum has a particular focus on the history of bus services in the North East, including vehicles associated with United Automobile Services and local municipal operators.

Its Halloween event offered visitors the chance to experience the venue in a rather different way, swapping the usual transport-history setting for an evening of scares, costumes and seasonal entertainment.
