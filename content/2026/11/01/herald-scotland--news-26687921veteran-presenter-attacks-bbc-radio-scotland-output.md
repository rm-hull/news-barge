**Broadcaster and outdoors presenter Cameron McNeish has launched a scathing attack on BBC Radio Scotland, claiming the station is driving away listeners with poor programming decisions and an increasing focus on commercial-style output.**

"I've always had a soft spot for BBC Scotland," he wrote, recalling a broadcasting career with the corporation stretching back to the early 1980s, when he presented programmes including *Leisure Trail*,* Weekend Break*and* In The Country*.

Writing on Facebook, McNeish said he was not surprised by recent audience figures showing a sharp fall in listeners, arguing the broadcaster had lost sight of its traditional audience.

**Read More: BBC Radio Scotland weekly audience drops by more than 90,000**RAJAR figures released on Thursday show** BBC Radio Scotland** had a weekly reach of 753,000 listeners between July and September this year. This was down from 844,000 the previous quarter - a fall of 91,000, or 10.8%.

Turning to Radio Scotland's current output, however, he said: "It doesn't surprise me that folk are abandoning what should be Scotland's own radio channel."

McNeish argued the station's attempts to attract younger audiences were misguided, adding: "I'm not sure young people listen to radio, as we oldies know it. Certainly my own granddaughters don't."

He reserved particular criticism for recent schedule changes, claiming the broadcaster had dismissed some of its most popular presenters while replacing established programming with what he described as "teenage giggling, in-house jokes, eighties pop music and stand-up comedians no-one has ever heard of".

"In my opinion it represents the very worst of commercial radio - cheap, poorly edited and badly presented," he wrote. "It reminds me of children's programmes in the seventies."

The presenter also criticised changes to the station's weekday phone-in programme after Kaye Adams left, while praising fellow broadcaster Stephen Jardine.

"Well, Connie, let me tell you something - I wanna hear less from you and more from Stephen Jardine who presents the show much more professionally on a Thursday and Friday," he wrote.

McNeish said Radio Scotland still employed talented broadcasters and editors, but questioned why it was replacing experienced presenters.

"BBC Radio Scotland has presenters and editors of talent, Stephen Jardine is certainly one of them, so why are such talented professionals being replaced by DJs from commercial radio?" he asked.

Suggesting the broadcaster had adopted too much of a commercial radio mindset, he added: "Looks like she brought a bunch of her pals with her to replace some experienced and capable presenters?"

The comments appeared to be directed at Victoria Easton Riley, BBC Scotland's head of audio and events, who joined the broadcaster from Bauer Media, where she served as director of content for Scotland and Northern Ireland. Easton Riley has overseen a series of programming changes at BBC Radio Scotland and previously worked for the company behind stations including Clyde 1 and Forth 1.

McNeish concluded with a warning about the station's future, arguing it required a "drastic overhaul" and a better understanding of its audience.

"I want to see a Scottish national radio station that genuinely reflects the interests and cultures of Scotland," he wrote. "We have broadcasting talent in Scotland, we have some brilliant programme makers and presenters."

He added: "I just don't understand why the current hierarchy is determined to plunge to the depths of meaningless, pop-driven commercial radio."

Questioning the broadcaster's long-term prospects, McNeish said: "I just wonder how long it will be before BBC Radio Scotland goes the same way as the BBC Scotland Channel?"

Responding to the RAJAR analysis, a BBC Radio Scotland spokesperson said: "This latest RAJAR report shows that in an increasingly competitive market, BBC Radio Scotland remains the biggest Scottish station, and has seen a quarter-on-quarter rise in the average amount of time listeners spend with us.

"We're delighted that audiences continue to come to us in significant numbers for their favourite shows from agenda-setting news and sports programmes, to entertainment shows such as Breaking the News through to our night-time music offering which includes Travelling Folk, Another Country with Ricky Ross, and Roddy Hart's Mix Tape."
