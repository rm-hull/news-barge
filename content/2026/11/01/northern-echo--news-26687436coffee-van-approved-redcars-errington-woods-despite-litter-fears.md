A licence has been granted allowing coffee and snacks to be sold to visitors to a wooded area in Redcar and Cleveland.

The application for the car park at Errington Woods, in New Marske, was opposed by local councillor Vera Rider who complained of the potential for discarded rubbish.

But the street trading licence sought by Kevin Grady was approved when the application came before the council’s regulatory committee for determination.

Cllr Rider said she had spoken to residents who agreed that, what was already a problem, could become worse.

She also described how litter picks that took place in the vicinity would regularly fill several bags worth of rubbish.

The councillor maintained her objection despite assurances being given by council licensing officers, who said the permit holder would be responsible for removing waste and litter arising from the business.

They would also be required to provide a litter bin and undertake a litter pick of their own each day.

![The vehicle involved in the venture. Picture/credit: RCBC. Free for use for all media partners.](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21655260.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The vehicle involved in the venture. Picture/credit: RCBC. Free for use for all media partners.

Mr Grady said his ‘Fuel’d By Coffee’ venture, set up last year, which promises to “fuel your daily grind”, involved a mobile Volkswagen van from which he sold coffee, tea, hot chocolate, cans of pop, tray bakes and crisps.

In correspondence with the council prior to the successful outcome, he said he was “disappointed” by the councillor’s continued stance.

He said: “We completely understand the concerns around keeping the area clean and tidy, and I just want to make it clear that this is something we take seriously.

“We walk our dog Skye in the woods regularly ourselves, so it’s an area we already enjoy and care about.

“The last thing we would want is to run a business there and see the area become untidy.”

Mr Grady, whose proposed hours of operation are from Monday to Sunday 8am to 3pm, added: “We aren’t looking to just turn up, sell coffee and leave.

“We want to be part of the local community and hopefully encourage more people to get out and enjoy the woods and the surrounding area.”

He said there would be bins next to the van for customers to use and discounts would be offered for folk using their own reusable cups.

The aim was to “leave the car park cleaner than we found it”, while litter picking would also take place into the forest.
