A historic landmark lit up in purple to mark a global health campaign.

Bolton Castle in Wensleydale was illuminated as part of World Polio Day, which took place on October 24 to raise awareness of the ongoing fight to eradicate polio worldwide.

Polio is a highly infectious disease that primarily affects children under five and can cause paralysis.

Rotary launched its global eradication campaign in 1985 when the disease was present in 125 countries, with more than 1,000 cases reported daily.

Richard Pennell, president of Wensleydale Rotary Club, said: "Every £1 donation to the End Polio Now campaign is matched by £2 from the Bill and Melinda Gates Foundation.

"We are so close to removing this terrible disease from the world.

"We are very grateful to Bolton Castle in allowing us to raise the profile of the End Polio Now Campaign."

The purple lighting represents the dye used to mark the fingers of children who have received the polio vaccine.

**Read More:**

Today, only Pakistan and Afghanistan remain polio-endemic, and even there, case numbers have dropped significantly.

However, recent funding cuts from governments worldwide threaten to undermine progress.

Efforts must continue not only to eliminate the virus in the remaining countries but also to maintain high immunisation levels globally to prevent a resurgence.
