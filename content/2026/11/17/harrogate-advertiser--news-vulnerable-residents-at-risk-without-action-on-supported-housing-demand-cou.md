Failure to meet growing demand for supported housing in North Yorkshire could put vulnerable residents at risk and place further pressure on public services, a council report has warned.

North Yorkshire Council says failing to plan effectively for the housing needs of people requiring care and support could increase risks to their wellbeing and safety, as well as creating problems with housing standards and safeguarding.

The warning comes as the authority prepares to consult on a new five-year strategy aimed at addressing gaps in specialist accommodation across the county.

The proposals will be considered by the council’s executive member for culture, arts and housing on Friday, November 20.

The report states: “Failure to effectively plan for and respond to the growth in demand for supported housing in North Yorkshire could increase risks relating to resident wellbeing and safeguarding, housing standards, regulatory compliance and service pressures.”

The strategy covers a wide range of residents, including older people, care leavers and young people at risk, people with learning disabilities and autism, those with physical disabilities and people experiencing multiple disadvantages or complex needs.

It aims to increase the supply and range of supported housing, improve the quality of accommodation and help residents live as independently as possible.

The council also wants to prevent people reaching crisis point by providing earlier support and making it easier for residents to move into, through and out of supported accommodation.

However, the report acknowledges shortages of affordable housing more generally can make it harder for people to leave supported accommodation and move into independent homes.

It says the availability and affordability of ordinary housing can influence both demand for specialist accommodation and the ability of residents to move on successfully.

The council’s wider health and wellbeing strategy has already identified shortages of specialist housing, particularly for working-age adults with care and support needs.

The new strategy is intended to address these gaps through closer working between housing services, social care, health organisations and accommodation providers.

It will also prepare the council for new national regulations aimed at improving standards in supported housing and tackling poor-quality provision.

However, the financial implications of delivering the strategy have not yet been fully calculated.

The Government has allocated North Yorkshire Council £179,353 over four financial years to support its additional responsibilities, including preparing and implementing the strategy.

The council says the costs of individual measures will be assessed as detailed delivery plans are developed.

If approved, a public consultation on the draft strategy and supporting housing needs assessment will run from Monday, November 30 to Friday, January 8.

The final proposals are expected to be considered by the council’s executive in February before going to full council in March 2027.
