**Campaigners aiming to bring a tidal sea pool back to Scarborough hope the move would attract triathlons to the town.**

North Yorkshire Council backed plans to build a three-sided seawater pool at the top of South Bay last year, with a similar facility in the town closing almost 40 years ago.

The Scarborough Sea Swimming Community Interest Company (CIC) said the sea pool could open the door for triathlons along with other organised swimming events.

This year marks the 400th anniversary of the resort's official designation as a spa town, with those backing the campaign hoping the addition would "keep that Scarborough magic going".

Cathy Foreman, from the CIC, said the pool will be "big enough to get triathlons back in Scarborough" and also attract further visitors.

"What we'd like to do is have events so the RNLI can use it, get school swimming lessons, just any sort of events people want to do," Foreman said.

She added: "It would mean a lot to Scarborough residents because then they can swim safer in the sea, they would be hugged by a sea pool wall.

"When it's rough and the tide comes in you think it's a bit too dangerous to go in and out."

![Campaigners Lizzie Marriott, Cathy Foreman and Karen Chiverall from the Scarborough Sea Swimming Community Interest Company with the arms aloft at a fundraising table. ](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/686e/live/5228b900-e269-11f1-900c-895604014268.jpg)

Lizzie Marriott, Cathy Foreman and Karen Chiverall are part of the group pushing to develop the facility

Last year, Foreman and fellow campaigners Karen Chiverall and Lizzie Marriott described the town without a pool like 'fish and chips without the chips'.

"As a really quite deprived coastal town, we feel by building this kind of unique landmark we will be inviting lots of regeneration into the area," Marriott said.

"We will be providing a place for local people and visitors to swim for free and it's just about putting a different face on Scarborough."

The town did have a similar facility from 1915 to 1989, but it had to close due to financial difficulties.

After conversations with councillor Rich Maw last year, a community engagement session found sea swimmers were keen to see the town have a tidal pool again.

The council previously commissioned a feasibility study which pinpointed the proposed site at Children's Corner, at the top of South Bay.

The plan also included a sheltered beach, changing area and bathing area for children.

The three-sided seawater pool is set to be built at the top of Scarborough's South Bay

The town hosted a number of events marking the 400th anniversary, including an exhibition at Scarborough Art Gallery, a giant deck chair trail and the unveiling of a new sculpture.

"We just want to keep that Scarborough magic going," said Chiverall.

"If we had a sea pool here we would be a resort which would take us through the next 400 years."
