Tom Gordon MP wants any visitor levy raised in Harrogate and Knaresborough kept for the area, as the Mayor consults on a bus charge on council tax.

Every penny of any visitor levy raised in Harrogate and Knaresborough should be ring-fenced for the area, its MP Tom Gordon told the Commons on Monday 16 November, warning that the town must not become “a cash cow” for a mayor in York.

The Liberal Democrat MP spoke in the second reading debate on the Visitor Levy (England) Bill. Under the Bill, the Mayor of York and North Yorkshire, Labour’s David Skaith, could charge a percentage on every overnight stay. Council leaders could object, but the final say would rest with the Mayor alone.

## Overview

* **What:** the Visitor Levy (England) Bill, which would let the Mayor charge a percentage on overnight stays.
* **When:** second reading in the Commons on 16 November 2026.
* **Mr Gordon’s ask:** money raised in Harrogate and Knaresborough is spent in Harrogate and Knaresborough.
* **Also on 16 November:** the Mayor launched a consultation that includes, for the first time, a transport element for buses.

## “That isn’t devolution”

“Local businesses tell me they support a visitor levy in principle,” Mr Gordon said. “What they can’t accept is a far-off mayor in York funnelling the money away. Harrogate cannot be used as a cash cow to pump money into other areas and a mayor’s pet projects.”

He set out where he wants the money to go: “If ministers want this levy to work, every penny raised in Harrogate and Knaresborough must be ring-fenced for Harrogate and Knaresborough. That means dualling the Knaresborough to York line, fixing our pothole-riddled roads, and backing the town and parish councils that run festivals and events and look after our toilets and flower beds.

“Local leaders can object, but the Mayor has the final word. That isn’t devolution. Quite frankly, it’s a con.”

## The bus charge consultation

On the same day as the debate, Mr Skaith launched his consultation on the 2027/28 police and fire precepts, which for the first time includes a transport element to fund buses. Mr Gordon’s office says the charge, added to council tax, could raise up to £2 million a year, and that it comes despite a pledge the Mayor made before his election that he had no plans for extra mayoral taxes.

“Now he wants a bus tax on top,” Mr Gordon said. “If buses are being paid for by a transport levy, where will the visitor levy really go? It’s yet another tax on working people and the visitors who keep our local economies alive, and that simply won’t do.”

## No impact assessment

Mr Gordon also criticised the Government for publishing no impact assessment for the Bill, although any mayor who wants a levy would have to produce one first. The Bill’s published papers include explanatory notes and memoranda on human rights and delegated powers, but no impact assessment.

He returned to the Bill’s offences, which carry up to 14 years in prison for fraudulent evasion, and called for the justice impact test to be published so the effect on courts and prisons is known. He had raised the missing test at Justice Questions earlier this month.

“In France, a hotel that fails to collect the tourist tax gets a fine. In Spain, you only face jail if the fraud tops €120,000. Italy used to lock people up and switched to fines. Nobody should get off scot-free, but this is about proportionality,” he said.

His office gives the detail: in France, failing to collect or pass on the taxe de séjour carries a fine of €750 to €2,500; in Spain, tax fraud is a crime only above €120,000 a year; and Italy decriminalised hoteliers’ non-payment of tourist tax in 2020.

“Ministers demand impact assessments from every mayor but won’t do the sums themselves,” Mr Gordon said. “Our hospitality sector needs a VAT cut, not a new tax.”

Sir Julian Smith, the Conservative MP for Skipton and Ripon, spoke in the same debate and asked ministers to put the Bill on hold. The Bill’s progress can be followed on its Parliament page.
