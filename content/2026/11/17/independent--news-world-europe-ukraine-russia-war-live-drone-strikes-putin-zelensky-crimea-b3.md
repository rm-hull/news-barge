A section of the bridge connecting occupied Crimea with Russia has collapsed into the sea following a coordinated missile and drone attack by Ukraine, according to images of the aftermath.

One of the Crimean Bridge’s main support arches has completely collapsed, alongside a significant chunk of the bridge’s road surface, according to a picture shared by Ukrainian sanctions commissioner Vladyslav Vlasiuk.

Ukrainian media reports said a domestically-produced Flamingo missile was used in the strike and carried on-board camera footage purporting to be from the missile.

The bridge cost around £3bn to build and was a key pet project for Vladimir Putin, who illegally annexed Crimea from Ukraine in 2014.

Earlier, parts of Kyiv were left without electricity and water after Russian forces struck two major bridges across the Dnipro River and other critical infrastructure in a massive overnight missile and drone attack on Ukraine.

Loud explosions rocked the Ukrainian capital late on Monday as Russian missiles hit the North and South bridges, according to Kyiv mayor Vitali Klitschko.

The bombardment extended beyond Kyiv, with at least three people injured in a missile strike in the central Cherkasy region. Kharkiv, Ukraine’s second-largest city, was also targeted, local officials said.

## Damage to Ukrainian bridges pictured

Russian strikes have damaged bridges in Kyiv, following Ukraine’s attacks on the strategic Crimea Bridge.

Both the city’s North Bridge and Southern Bridge were struck during an overnight missile attack by Putin’s forces.

Workers were seen inspecting the damage to the North Bridge on Tuesday, following the attack that brought traffic to a half.

## Ukraine offers humanitarian mission similar to Oleshky

Ukraine has offered to carry out a humanitarian mission similar to the one in Russia-occupied Oleshky for 10 settlements in the southern Kherson and Zaporizhzhia regions, President Volodymyr Zelensky said.

“Our team will discuss this with the American mediators,” he said on X.

Hundreds of Ukrainians returned home from Oleshky last month after a rare, localised ceasefire was brokered by the US.

## Zelensky posts video appearing to show damage to bridge

A video posted on X by Zelensky appeared to show the bridge's main road arch collapsed into the Kerch Strait, blocking navigation.

*The Independent* could not immediately verify the footage.

Zelensky said the strike followed an unprecedented operation that methodically destroyed Russian air ⁠defences, radar systems and electronic warfare capabilities.

Ukraine's General Staff said the arch and a span of the bridge's road section had collapsed after being dealt powerful blows, but that the rail part of the bridge was still standing albeit with a damaged arch.

There was no official Russian confirmation of the extent of damage to the Crimean bridge, a flagship project closely associated ⁠with President Vladimir Putin after Russia annexed the peninsula from Ukraine in 2014.

## Taiwan, Japan and South Korea flag incursions by Russian aircraft as Putin set to arrive in China

Taiwan's defence ministry said it detected two Russian military aircraft flying off its east coast on Tuesday, while Japan protested to Moscow after two aircraft believed to be Russian violated its airspace.

The reports come ⁠ahead of Russian president Vladimir Putin arrival in China's southern city of Shenzhen for this week's summit of APEC leaders. China and Russia have close diplomatic and military ties.

Taiwan's ministry said that after flying through airspace to the island's east, the Russian aircraft, whose type it ⁠did not identify, exited its air defence identification ​zone ⁠south of the eastern county of Taitung.

The ministry used joint intelligence, surveillance, and reconnaissance means to monitor the full course of the activity and "responded appropriately", it ⁠added in a brief statement, without giving details.

Earlier, Japan lodged a strong protest ​to ⁠Moscow after two aircraft believed to be ‌Russian violated its airspace off the island of Okinoshima in southwestern Fukuoka, chief cabinet secretary Minoru Kihara said.

"The violation of Japan's airspace by Russian aircraft is a serious ‌infringement of Japan's sovereignty," Kihara said, adding that the ‌safety threat was completely unacceptable.

"We lodged a strong protest with Russia and urged it to prevent a recurrence."

The Japan Self-Defense Forces scrambled fighter aircraft and took necessary measures in response, such as issuing warnings and ⁠notifications, the country's defence ministry said.

The suspected Russian aircraft were subsequently confirmed to have transited the Tsushima Strait, passed between Yonaguni Island and Taiwan, and proceeded into the Pacific Ocean, it added.

Yonaguni is the Japanese island closest to Taiwan, about 100km (62 miles) off its east.

A map issued by Japan's defence ministry showed a flight path to Taiwan's east, heading in a southerly direction.

South Korea’s Joint Chiefs of Staff said it scrambled fighter jets after several Russian military aircraft entered its air defence identification zone over the East Sea.

## Russia strikes two key bridges in Kyiv

Russian forces struck two bridges over the Dnipro River in Ukraine's capital Kyiv late on Monday and some other infrastructure, Mayor ​Vitali Klitschko said.

Moscow also launched drones and missiles across ‌other parts of the country.

"According to preliminary information, there have been hits on the North and South bridges in the capital," Klitschko wrote on Telegram.

The attack partially damaged the ​subway system, prompted some water and power cuts and traffic restrictions.

## Zelensky says attack on bridge was part of operation underway for several weeks

Ukrainian president Volodymyr Zelensky said the strike on the bridge connecting occupied Crimea with Russia was the "seventh phase of an operation that has been underway for several weeks".

"Our forces systematically destroyed air defence systems, radars, electronic warfare assets, and other designated targets along the path to the final objective," he said in a post on social media.

## Russia accused of using Japanese journalist as ‘geopolitical leverage’ after arrest on spying charges

Press freedom groups have condemned Russia’s detention of a Japanese journalist on charges of espionage, calling for his immediate release and warning against the criminalisation of reporting.

Yuki Imai, a 34-year-old correspondent for the Japanese newspaper *Hokkaido Shimbun*, was detained on 14 October.

## Zelensky holds fresh peace talks with US envoys as Russia plans major strikes

Ukrainian president Volodymyr Zelensky said Kyiv had held fresh talks with US envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner, alongside European representatives, as diplomatic efforts to end Russia's war continued.

The virtual discussions on Monday came less than a week after US, Ukrainian and Russian officials held their first three-way talks in nine months in Abu Dhabi.

"Diplomatic processes are actively underway and we hope that these efforts will not be in vain," Zelensky said in his nightly address, describing the discussions as "not easy".

Representatives from France, Germany, Britain, Italy and Poland participated in the talks, with Zelensky saying they had agreed to meet again in Berlin alongside countries from the "Coalition of the Willing".

However, Kremlin aide Yuri Ushakov said Moscow had not agreed to European participation in the next round of negotiations.

Zelensky also warned that Ukrainian intelligence indicated Russia was preparing major strikes overnight and into Tuesday.

He said North Korea was supplying Russia with dozens of ballistic missiles each month, warning that Pyongyang was using the war to improve its weapons capabilities.

"This is extremely dangerous and not only for Ukraine," he said.
