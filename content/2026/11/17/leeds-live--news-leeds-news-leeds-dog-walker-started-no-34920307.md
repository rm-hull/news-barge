# Leeds dog walker started with no customers - now she's up for national award

## Sophie Johnson said she can't work out who nominated her

A Leeds woman who started her own dog-walking business with no customers has been named a finalist in a national small business awards.

Sophie Johnson, 28, from Bramley, launched Wag & Wuff Co. in July 2025 after leaving her job as a van driver, turning a childhood dream into a growing business.

Just over a year later, she is looking after around 15 dogs across Leeds and has been shortlisted in the Best Pet Business category at the 2026 UK Small Business Awards.

And while Sophie admits taking the leap into self-employment was terrifying, she's glad she did.

Sophie said: "I've loved dogs for as long as I can remember, I was always begging my mum for one growing up, but she insisted they were too much responsibility.

"Once I'd left school and started earning my own money, I decided to take matters into my own hands and brought home a Staffie cross called Lola without telling her!

"Luckily, we both fell completely in love with her, and she's still part of our family today at nine years old."

Despite always dreaming of working with dogs, Sophie never quite knew how to turn that passion into a career. Before launching her business, she worked at Motor Parts Direct in Dewsbury as a van driver, delivering car parts.

Sophie said: "Don't get me wrong, I genuinely loved my job, but deep down I knew it wasn't what I wanted to do for the rest of my life. I left in January 2025, which gave me the opportunity to think about what I really wanted to do next."

The idea for Wag & Wuff Co. came out of nowhere, with Sophie remembering the moment she decided to take the plunge.

She continued: "It was actually quite spontaneous, I remember waking up one morning and telling my partner, 'I'm setting up my own dog-walking business!', he's supported me ever since."

Sophie spent the following six months researching self-employment, designing her logo and learning everything she needed to know about running a business.

She also completed canine first aid training and obtained a DBS check to make sure she was properly prepared before welcoming her first four-legged customers.

Asked what it was like starting from scratch with no clients, Sophie said: "Honestly, terrifying! You're stepping into the unknown, not knowing whether your business will take off or whether you'll be left with nothing.

"Luckily, my first client came through Facebook within roughly the first week of launching.

"I remember feeling so excited that someone was putting their trust in me and my little business!"

Since then, the business has gone from strength to strength, with Sophie now regularly looking after around 15 dogs and becoming almost fully booked during her main working hours.

She offers solo walks, walks for dogs from the same household and pop-in visits across LS13 and surrounding areas.

She said: "No two days are ever the same, which is something I absolutely love! I generally start around 10am, offering solo walks, household walks and pop-in visits. Every dog has its own personality, and I love getting to know them individually.

"I love being outdoors, helping owners while they're at work and, of course, being my own boss!"

One particular dog, Walter, an ex-racing greyhound, holds a special place in Sophie's heart.

When she first started visiting him, he was understandably nervous and would barely acknowledge her.

She said: "He had a difficult past with his previous owners, but he's now with the most wonderful family who absolutely adore him.

"When I first started visiting Walter, he wouldn't even look at me. He'd just lie upstairs on his owners' bed, although we all call it Walter's bed now because he's completely claimed it!"

After around a year of visiting him three times a week, Walter has become a completely different dog around Sophie.

She said: "He now comes straight downstairs when I arrive. We go into the garden for his toilet break, I set up snuffle mats for him, and we spend time playing and having cuddles. His owners even call me 'Aunty Sophie', which I absolutely love!"

She added: "Watching him go from barely acknowledging me to building such a lovely bond has honestly been one of the most rewarding parts of my job.

"It's moments like those that remind me why I started Wag & Wuff Co. in the first place. It's not always just about walking dogs, sometimes it's about earning their trust and becoming a little part of their lives."

However, building a business entirely on her own hasn't always been straightforward. Sophie invested her own savings into getting the company off the ground, without knowing whether she would ever make that money back.

She said: "There was a point when I was getting bookings, but nowhere near enough to make a living from. Wag & Wuff Co. was my only source of income, so it was quite worrying not knowing whether things would pick up."

Alongside looking after the dogs, Sophie has had to learn every aspect of running a business, from finding customers and managing bookings to finances and marketing.

She credits her supportive partner with helping her through the more difficult moments.

She added: "There have definitely been stressful days, luckily, I've got an incredibly supportive partner who always encouraged me to stick at it, even when things were difficult. Having someone believe in me when I was doubting myself made such a difference."

And now, all that hard work has been recognised after Sophie received an unexpected email informing her she had been shortlisted for a national award.

She said: "I actually cried when I opened it! I couldn't wait to tell my partner, family and clients. I genuinely wasn't expecting it, especially as I've only been running the business for just over a year and I'm still growing."

Sophie has no idea who nominated her, despite asking around, but says she would love the opportunity to thank whoever put her forward. She is hoping to attend the awards ceremony in Birmingham on December 19.

The recognition is particularly meaningful for Sophie, who is also studying for a Bachelor of Laws (LLB) degree with The Open University.

She said: "I'm honestly so, so proud of myself. I had quite a difficult childhood, so looking at where I am now and what I've achieved means more than I can put into words.

"Alongside running Wag & Wuff Co. full-time, I'm also studying for a law degree. Balancing a business and a law degree can be incredibly stressful, and some days I feel like there just aren't enough hours!"

Looking ahead, Sophie has ambitious plans to grow the business further, with group dog walks set to be introduced in January 2027.

She is also hoping to take on her first employee, allowing her to lead the group walks while someone else takes over some of the solo bookings.

"It's exciting to think that something I started entirely on my own could eventually provide employment for someone else," she said.

Despite her ambitions, Sophie is keen to remain hands-on and continue doing what she loves.

She said: "I'd love Wag & Wuff Co. to grow into a bigger business with a team of employees, but I always want to remain hands-on.

"I know some business owners eventually want to sit back and watch their business grow, but that's not me!

"I started this because I genuinely love working with dogs, and I never want to lose that."

Asked her advice for anyone else dreaming of turning a passion into a career, Sophie said: "My advice to anyone dreaming of starting their own business would be to just go for it!

"It's scary stepping into the unknown, but you'll never know unless you try. If I'd let fear stop me, none of this would have happened."
