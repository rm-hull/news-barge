"Russia is intensifying its air attacks because it is not making progress on the battlefield," minister Boris Pistorius said at an EU meeting in Brussels.

Pistorius said that Berlin would contribute $300 million to buying the latest tranche of US weaponry – with the focus of the package on air defence, and in particular Patriot interceptor missiles.

Russia has stepped up its bombing campaign on cities and infrastructure around Ukraine in what Kyiv says is an attempt to freeze the country into submission this winter.

Ukraine has desperately been urging its backers to help find more Patriots as they are the only system capable of shooting down Russian ballistic missiles.

Kyiv has been facing a dire shortfall as US stockpiles have been drained by the war with Iran.

The announcement of fresh support comes ahead of a meeting of Ukraine's military backers at NATO headquarters in Brussels later on Tuesday.

The United States under Donald Trump has stopped giving weaponry to Kyiv – but allows other countries to purchase American arms for Ukraine.

NATO says that Washington's allies have so far spent more than $10 billion on US weaponry for Kyiv.

The push to shore up Ukraine's defences comes as there appears to be renewed momentum in US-led efforts to halt the fighting in Ukraine.

But Kyiv and its backers say Moscow continues to escalate its attacks and is showing little sign that it is genuinely interested in peace.
