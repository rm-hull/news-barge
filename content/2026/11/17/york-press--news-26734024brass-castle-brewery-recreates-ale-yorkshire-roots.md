A historic ale is making a return in a modern brewery’s latest creation.

Brass Castle Brewery in Malton has revived the centuries-old Yorkshire beer tradition of 'Stingo' with its latest release, Wallop Stingo, an 8.4 per cent ale aged in bourbon barrels and brewed with black treacle.

Stingo, a term for strong, well-matured ale dating back to the 17th century, is most closely associated with Yorkshire, though it was once brewed across the UK.

The style all but disappeared in the 20th century, likely due to punitive duties, rationing and the need of the state to draw heavily upon its population for engagement in total war.

Mike Antonacci, head brewer at Brass Castle, said: "We're very proud to present this year's version of Wallop Stingo, and delighted at the flavours of oak, raisin, treacle toffee and Christmas pudding that it has developed.

"It's exactly the sort of cosy sharing beer that helps people come together warmly in these colder months.

"We love making beers that connect our modern brewing processes to the styles and vocabularies that would have been familiar in Malton's former brewing powerhouse.

"It's not an exercise in nostalgia, but a rediscovery of some well-founded craft."

Brass Castle has brewed Wallop Stingo in Malton frequently since 2013, with various barrel-ageing programmes incorporating numerous flavour taints.

The latest version includes black treacle for a light liquorice note and has been matured in former bourbon barrels to enhance its depth and complexity.

Stingo has no single historical recipe, but it is generally described as a strong, aged ale—often with dark fruit and caramel notes.

Some brewers believe the name also hints at a slight tartness or "sting," once a prized characteristic of matured beers.

The ale’s legacy extends beyond Yorkshire.

Belgian brewers reportedly visited Yorkshire to study the techniques behind Stingo, a testament to its influence in the wider brewing world.

Brass Castle’s Wallop Stingo is bottled in 750ml format, reflecting its intended role as a beer to savour and share.

The brewery describes it as a “bottle-worthy” ale that honours a long history while offering something distinct in today’s fast-paced beer market.

Stingo’s fall from favour is often attributed to the impact of the World Wars, when punitive duties, rationing and the need of the state to draw heavily upon its population for engagement in total war made strong ales less viable for breweries to produce.

Brass Castle’s revival of the style offers drinkers a taste of brewing heritage, reimagined for a new generation.
