President Donald Trump on Tuesday offered praise for Philippines President Ferdinand “Bongbong” Marcos Jr.’s infamous parents, telling him he was the beneficiary of “good genetics” and calling the notorious kleptocrats “great” as he dined with leaders at the Association of Southeast Asian Nations annual summit.

Trump made the strange remarks as he spoke at a dinner for ASEAN heads of state at a resort in Parañaque City after being introduced by Marcos, who was elected president of the Philippines in 2022.

“I just don't want to date myself. But I knew your parents very well, and they were great. I believe in genetics, and they did a good job. You have very good genetics. I can tell you that,” Trump said.

Marcos’ parents, Ferdinand Marcos Sr and Imelda Marcos, ruled the Philippines from 1965 to 1986 — the last 14 years of which were under martial law. During that time, they stole what the Philippines authorities have estimated to be as much as $30 billion from the country’s treasury — setting a Guinness World Record for "Greatest Robbery of a Government" — and used it to fund a lavish lifestyle during periods of economic crisis and unrest.

The dictator and his wife fled the Philippines for exile in the United States after they were toppled by a populist revolution in 1986, leaving behind Imelda’s now-infamous wardrobe which was said to include as many as 15 mink coats, 508 gowns, 888 handbags, and at least 1,000 pairs of shoes, all paid for with public funds. American customs documents showed they arrived in the U.S. with millions of dollars worth of clothes, jewelry, and gold bars.

![Trump purchased the 40 Wall Street skyscraper from Philippine president Ferdinand 'Bongbong' Marcos Jr's kleptocrat parents, Ferdinand Marcos Sr and Imelda Marcos](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/17/13/download.-1..jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Marcos died in exile in Honolulu, Hawaii in 1989 without ever facing trial. Imelda Marcos, 97, lives in New York City.

Some of their ill-gotten funds were even used to purchase American real estate, including the leasehold for the 40 Wall Street skyscraper in New York. It was later sold to Trump, whose company still owns it.

Trump’s fond recollection of getting to know Marcos’ kleptocrat parents in his younger years came as he praised ASEAN leaders for having entered into what he called “vital agreements” with the U.S. concerning “energy, critical minerals, and technology” and other sectors.

He said he hoped the assembled heads of state would be “working very hard on critical minerals, rare earths, and magnets,” referencing raw materials that are key ingredients in advanced electric vehicles and battery technology. These are currently sourced mainly from China, providing Xi Jinping’s communist government with a key leverage point in Beijing’s trade relationships.

Trump is in Manilla for the first day of a four-day visit to Asia that will include a trilateral summit with Xi and Russian president Vladimir Putin Thursday in Shenzhen, China.

![Philippine President Ferdinand Marcos Jr., seated next to U.S. Secretary of State Marco Rubio, at the U.S.-Association of Southeast Asian Nations summit in Manila, Philippines, November 17](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/17/14/2026-11-17T135226Z_36502203_RC2PPOAAKE06_RTRMADP_3_ASEAN-SUMMIT.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The trip comes as the Trump administration has been rocked by a national security crisis linked to the ouster of Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang after he publicly admitted to an extramarital affair with a Chinese businesswoman and suspected spy, Alice Huang.

In particular, U.S. Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick, a longtime friend of Trump, refused to implement Biden-era export controls on advanced AI chips made by Nvidia at the behest of Jensen Huang. Lutnick also declined to place sanctions on Megaspeed, a company run by Alice Huang, despite concerns that it was operating as a front for Chinese interests to evade U.S. export controls.

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent wasted no time pointing the finger Friday when he told CNBC that it was the Lutnick-led Commerce Department who resisted putting Megaspeed on his department’s “Entity List” of companies not allowed to purchase American products.

Asked whether the Trump administration considered adding Megaspeed to the list, Bessent replied: “I can tell you there was coordination. It was Treasury, War, [the U.S. trade representative], and State. They were in favor of putting Megaspeed on the entity list for months. But Commerce has the pen.”

He added: “You will have to ask them about their internal process.”

Trump has thus far defended Lutnick, who he said had sat on his hands to avoid “a blowup with China.”

In his remarks at the summit in the Philippines Tuesday, Trump singled out both Bessent and Lutnick, praising both by name as part of what he called “a great team of people.”

Earlier in the day, the president said he, Putin and Xi would “have a chance to accomplish a lot” when they meet later this week. He also teased the possibility that North Korean dictator Kim Jong Un could join them.
