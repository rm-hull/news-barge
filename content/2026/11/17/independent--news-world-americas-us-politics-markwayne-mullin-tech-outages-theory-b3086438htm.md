Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin was mocked for pushing a theory he heard from “one guy” about the series of major tech outages that have caused chaos in the U.S.

In the last few days, Microsoft was plagued by technical issues that prevented users from accessing its services, thousands were left stranded over the weekend when the nation’s air traffic control system malfunctioned Friday, and major banks suffered online problems Monday, preventing some customers from accessing their online accounts.

Mullin was asked by Fox News host Sean Hannity what message he wanted to share with the country, particularly those who are “beginning to panic” about the outages.

He relayed a conversation he’d had with an anonymous expert “that’s very smart with tech,” and stressed that it was “not Elon,” referring to the world’s richest man and former special government employee, Elon Musk.

“I said, what could be causing all these separate outages? And he says, ‘I believe that it's the SI,’” Mullin said, referring to President Donald Trump’s preferred term for artificial intelligence, “super intelligence.”

![Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin pushed a theory about ‘SI’ he heard from an anonymous tech expert that may have caused the recent tech outages](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/17/13/43/Screenshot-2026-11-17-at-8-21-37-AM.png?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“He said because these programs aren't designed to work as fast as SI is, and so when they contribute or when they put in a program that's able to write codes and it goes in, it self-fixes itself.” Mullin continued. “As it starts self-fixing itself, it's like doing a change order on a house. When you change one thing in a house, everything else starts changing.”

“That's a ripple effect, and it causes these systems to be overran that weren't designed to run as fast as the SI technology is today,” Mullin concluded, saying that the theory “kind of makes sense to me.”

He ruled out a cyber attack and tried to reassure Americans that “the homeland is secure.”

But Mullin was swiftly ridiculed online for floating the theory of the anonymous expert and for his repeated use of “SI.”

“How about we get some facts before just saying stuff? Maybe outages are caused by AI (no normal person calls it SI),” California Democratic Rep. Ted Lieu said in a post on X. “Maybe they’re caused by non-AI software bugs. Maybe it’s old equipment.” He added that the Trump administration “needs to deal with facts.”

“So if the government thinks AI\*is behind all of these outages, what is it doing about it?” asked polling analyst Nate Silver. “\* Super f\*\*\* ing stupid not to use the more legible term when you're trying to communicate clearly.”

“The Homeland Security secretary just passing along speculation from ‘one guy’ to millions of Fox viewers,” added CNN’s Brian Stelter.

“Is there nobody in the U.S. government agency he runs who might have given him a take on what happened?” asked journalist Laura Rozen. “He is citing some guy, not Elon?”

![Thousands of passengers were left stranded after the FAA issued a rare nationwide ground stop. The glitch arose from a contractor replacing old copper wire, it later emerged](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/16/17/35/GettyImages-2304497343.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

National security expert Ryan Goodman blasted Mullin’s remarks as one of the “least reassuring brain mush responses from a USG leader responsible for Americans’ safety” in a post on X.

Mullin spoke after a new outage that affected online banking, prompting fresh concern.

Thousands of customers at major banks, including JPMorgan Chase, Bank of America, Citigroup, Capital One and Wells Fargo and the peer-to-peer payment platform Zelle reported difficulty accessing online accounts.

“JPMorganChase has experienced a temporary issue related to a software bug in on-premise hardware systems – there is no evidence this is cyber-related. It is taking longer than usual to service some customers,” Chase wrote on X.

“Where we were party to a transaction involving other banks, those banks may have been impacted, but this is not a systemic issue. Customers still have access to their accounts and funds. We’re working diligently to resolve this by the end of the day today,” the bank said.

Elsewhere, flight schedules were returning to normal following Friday’s chaos. Airlines, which were already affected by the Microsoft outage, were then told by the Federal Aviation Administration to ground all their flights after an issue arose from a contractor replacing old copper wire with fiber optic cable at a radio facility in El Dorado, Arkansas.

Trump did not acknowledge the air traffic control crisis until Sunday, when he blamed his predecessor, former President Joe Biden.

“The Biden Administration was an FAA disaster. Everything they touched turned bad. Big money spent unwisely, making an old and broken system worse than when they began,” he posted on Truth Social.
