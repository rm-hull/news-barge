A public consultation has been launched to gather views on next year’s council tax precept.

David Skaith, Mayor of York and North Yorkshire, announced the consultation on Monday, 16 November, inviting residents, workers, and students across the region to share their views on the funding that supports North Yorkshire Police, North Yorkshire Fire and Rescue Service, and, for the first time, improvements to local bus services.

The consultation asks residents how council tax precepts should balance affordability with necessary investment in public services.

It includes options that range from maintaining current service levels to enabling further investment in key areas.

Mr Skaith said: "We want to keep precepts as low as possible, while making sure that our public services have the funding to deliver for you and to keep our county safe.

"For the first time, we're looking at adding a small amount to invest in our bus services, focusing in particular on making it easier for young people to travel across our county.

"Before I make any decision, I want to hear from you.

"Please share your views on what this means for our police, fire, and transport services."

Recent Home Office data shows North Yorkshire continues to have the lowest crime rate of any police force area in England and Wales.

This is supported by ongoing investment in neighbourhood policing, anti-social behaviour reduction, and services for victims and vulnerable people.

The fire and rescue service has also benefited from funding that has protected frontline services and improved prevention, road safety, and operational capacity.

This year, the mayor is considering adding a transport element to the precept to support improved bus services across York and North Yorkshire.

Feedback from the consultation will help shape the final proposals, which will be presented to the Police, Fire and Crime Panel and the Combined Authority early in the new year.

The survey is available at yorknorthyorks-ca.gov.uk/precept.
