President Donald Trump has spent years promising that he alone can fix America's problems. He was going to restore order, bring back technological supremacy, stop all those people supposedly laughing behind their hands at the U.S. — and he’d do it all by surrounding himself with the toughest, smartest people in the business.

His administration, we were told, would be a crack team of world-beaters, unencumbered by the dead weight of bureaucratic incompetence. Red tape be damned! Swamp be drained! Greatness is just around the corner!

It is an impressive sales pitch. But it is looking rather less impressive in practice, especially after this week.

In the space of a few days since Republicans were handed a bruising set of midterm results, Trump 2.0 has found itself confronted with a succession of rather serious tests of its ability to govern. America's aviation infrastructure suffered a spectacular failure, grounding thousands of flights last Friday. A string of outages, from Microsoft to Chase Bank to Apple, has caused widespread disruption. And now the highly-classified internal communications network used by the National Security Agency has reportedly gone down — while the president is in Asia.

There’s apparently “no evidence” that any of these multiple outages are the result of a cyber attack. But is that better or worse? Either Trump 2.0 prompted a bunch of hackers to successfully target U.S. infrastructure by starting a war nobody wanted or the country is falling apart at the seams. Take your apocalyptic pick.

![President Donald Trump speaks during an official dinner at the 49th Association of Southeast Asian Nations Summit in Manila, Philippines, November 17. The Trump administration has found itself confronted with a succession of rather serious tests of its ability to govern, while the president is in Asia](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/17/20/2305045213..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Neither option reflects well on an administration that just spent two years in charge of both houses of Congress as well as the executive branch. The growing list of failures is raising legitimate questions about the resilience of the systems on which the country depends. It is also providing a rather extraordinary demonstration of what happens when a government built around bombastic bravado is asked to demonstrate some actual competence (or when it wanders into a war it doesn’t actually know how to end which, again, comes back to basic competence.)

On Friday, a nationwide ground stop brought U.S. air travel to a complete halt for four hours. The apparent cause was a problem with telecommunications infrastructure, during a contractor's transition from old copper wiring to fiber-optic cables, at a radio facility in Arkansas. If this is true, it’s extraordinary that it was allowed to happen. Was infrastructure in such a bad state that it was liable to break nationwide air traffic control comms at any time? If so, why wasn’t this a more pressing emergency than the “emergencies” declared at the southern border or in the name of tariffs last year?

Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy and Trump himself have both tied themselves in knots trying to blame former President Joe Biden. Vice President JD Vance appeared on Fox News to suggest that the real issue is Democrats’ “ridiculous obsession with men in women's sports.”

And yes, perhaps it *is*the secret cabal of transgender athletes - widely-known for leaning on air traffic controllers at times of high capacity - who are to blame for all of this. But much more likely is that the GOP-led Congress, headed up by the Republican president, did not invest in creaking aviation infrastructure. In fact, it allowed Elon Musk’s pet project DOGE to cut hundreds of federal aviation jobs. And —* once again —* it started a war that heavily incentivized state-sponsored hackers to target American infrastructure.

![Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy and Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin have both drawn scrutiny following the FAA ground stop last Friday](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/15/20/2026-11-14T015400Z_1007661329_RC2DNOAKE3N1_RTRMADP_3_USA-TRUMP.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

While thousands of Americans were stuck in airports during an airlines ground stop of the length not seen since 9/11, Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin took a government aircraft to Louisiana to attend an LSU college football game, by the way. He was subsequently spotted in a luxury box belonging to a shipping executive whose company does business with his department, getting his priorities straight.

When Mullin was eventually asked about the growing number of technology outages, his contribution was even more reassuring. Appearing on Fox News, he explained that he had consulted an unnamed technology expert, whom he described as “one guy” who was very smart with tech. This individual had suggested that the culprit might be what Trump calls “super intelligence” — or AI, as literally everybody else calls it — and its ability to write and rewrite code was overwhelming older systems. Mullin said the explanation “kind of makes sense to me.”

It is not entirely clear why the DHS secretary, who oversees the department responsible for protecting the country against cyber threats, was relying on an anonymous acquaintance for an explanation of a potential national infrastructure problem. Nor was it clear what evidence supported the theory. He nevertheless used the interview to rule out a cyber attack and assure Americans that “the homeland is secure.”

Well, if it’s good enough for “one guy,” I guess it’s “kind of” good enough for me!

![Vice President JD Vance at a Marine Corps event New Bern, North Carolina, November 12. The administration's other great technological headache right now is Nvidia, the semiconductor giant whose chips underpin much of the global AI industry. The company is so central to the current AI boom that even a hint of trouble around its business can send shockwaves through the technology sector and financial markets. Yet here, too, the administration has been struggling to present anything resembling a coherent strategy after Jensen Huang’s leather jackets proved themselves to be the harbinger of doom we should have all seen coming.

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent has publicly criticized the Commerce Department, overseen by Howard Lutnick, for dragging its feet on restrictions designed to prevent advanced Nvidia chips reaching Chinese interests through third-party companies. Bessent said his department and other national security agencies had been pushing for action for months, while Lutnick's department controlled the process.

The result is a rather extraordinary spectacle: a government that claims to be engaged in an existential technological contest with China, while senior officials appear unable to agree on how to stop sensitive American technology reaching China.

Oh, and the president was advised by U.S. intelligence months ago that Jensen Huang was probably sleeping with a suspected Chinese spy but he brought him onto Air Force One and invited him round for dinner at the White House anyway. A little dose of presidential willful ignorance to help the intra-departmental chaos go down. Lucky this doesn’t go to the heart of America’s national security and economic future, otherwise we’d really be in trouble!

![As thousands of Americans were stuck in airports around the country and America was experiencing a ground stop of the length not seen since 9/11, Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin took a government aircraft to Louisiana to attend an LSU football game](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/14/08/2026-11-14T011443Z_347789350_RC2CNOAWLHV1_RTRMADP_3_USA-FAA-GROUND-STOP-MEXICO.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

And now, just as Americans are digesting the latest reports of technology failures, CNN has reported that the NSA's highly classified internal communications network, known as NSA Net, has gone down. The network allows agency personnel to communicate classified information internally. Officials are reportedly working to establish the scope of the problem, with staggered arrivals introduced for personnel.

Trump's own contribution has been to shrug. Asked about the recent outages Tuesday by a reporter during his visit to the Philippines, he replied: “It's like when you're big and powerful, and you have all the money. Things like that happen. It's fine.”

So here’s the president who has made a career out of promising that everything in America can be made bigger, stronger and better, simply by putting him in charge, and yet mysteriously, everything seems to be disintegrating. Standing among the ruins, the MAGA lackeys then start finger-pointing at literally anyone: Biden, trans athletes, pronouns in the flight deck. But don’t worry, *one guy* they know has a viewpoint!

Establishing the facts, explaining how you intend to fix things and assuming responsibility (remember “the buck stops here,” anyone?) used to be a thing. But that was back when woke commie beardo weirdos were in charge, I guess. These days it’s all about “things like that happen,” which is truly the sort of insightful leadership America has been craving all along.

To round it all out, Tuesday saw the U.S. lose its measles elimination status after 26 years of being measles-free. Another fantastic administrative effort, contributed to by the genius of Health Secretary Robert F. Kennedy, Jr. Outbreaks across the country over the past two years have seen multiple people — mainly children — sickened. Nine have died.

This is an administration that has spent enormous amounts of time and political capital dismantling expertise, attacking civil servants, sidelining scientists and treating government institutions as hostile territory to be conquered. It has promised that the private sector instincts of its appointees, the technological genius of its billionaire allies and the force of Trump's personality would be more than enough to replace the supposedly dysfunctional machinery of government and workers.

And yet! It turns out that the dysfunction was coming from inside the house. Who would’ve thought? While Trump spends time in southeast Asia, we’re left with a guy who loves ball games, a vice president stuck on culture wars and a man whose main concern when it comes to flights is whether you wear pajamas onboard - while planes stop flying and basic infrastructure goes down, company by company, day by day, advent calendar-style.

God bless America, indeed — or at least, please look kindly on us as we veer unsteadily into a ditch in a clown car, driven by circus performers yelling about how the problem just has to be the road.
