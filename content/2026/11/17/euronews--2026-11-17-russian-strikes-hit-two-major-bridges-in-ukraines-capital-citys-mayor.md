## The 19-kilometre bridge connects Krasnodar Krai in Russia with the Crimean peninsula across the Kerch Strait and was built by Moscow following the 2014 occupation.

Russia will hold an emergency government meeting, state media reported on Tuesday, after a Ukrainian attack on the bridge linking Russia to the annexed peninsula of Crimea caused an entire section to collapse.

"A meeting of a government commission for dealing with the consequences of the attack on the Crimean bridge, chaired by Deputy Prime Minister Marat Khusnullin, will be held shortly," state news agencies reported the government saying in a statement.

The 19-kilometre bridge connects Krasnodar Krai in Russia with the Crimean peninsula across the Kerch Strait and was built by Moscow following the 2014 occupation.

The bridge has been a frequent target of Ukrainian attacks since the beginning of the 2022 full-scale invasion.

"Crimean bridge. Sanctioned," Ukraine’s Presidential Sanctions Commissioner Vladyslav Vlasiuk wrote in a post on X.

The bridge was reportedly hit by Ukraine’s newly-developed Flamingo cruise missile.

The strike on the Crimea bridge comes after Russian strikes hit two major bridges in Kyiv late on Monday, part of an overnight attack that damaged critical infrastructure and disrupted heating and water in parts of the capital, the city's mayor said.

Ukraine's President Volodymyr Zelenskyy had warned of an attack by Russia in the coming days even as negotiators representing Kyiv as well as Europe and the United States held talks on future negotiations with Russia aimed at finding a path to end the war.

"Tonight, overnight, and throughout tomorrow, please pay attention to missile danger alerts," he said.

"And they there, in Russia, must know that for every one of their evils there will be our just response," he added.

"Preliminarily, hits to the North and South bridges in the capital," Kyiv's Mayor Vitali Klitschko posted on Telegram just before midnight local time (2300 CET), shortly after the Ukrainian air force warned of incoming missiles.

"As a result of the enemy attack, critical infrastructure systems have been damaged," he said in a later post, adding heating and water supplies in some districts were affected.

## **Missiles from Pyongyang**

Zelenskyy also said Monday that Russia was receiving "dozens of missiles per month" from North Korea, calling on European allies to send more weapons to respond "on equal terms" to the threat.

"Russia is unfortunately receiving more ballistic missiles from North Korea. We are talking about dozens of missiles per month," Zelenskyy said. He added that "Europe has missiles that can deliver truly effective strikes" and asked allies to help in "countering Russian global banditry".

North Korea has sent thousands of troops to support Russia's nearly four-year full-scale invasion of Ukraine, according to South Korean and Western intelligence agencies.

At least 600 have died and thousands more have sustained injuries, according to South Korean estimates.

Analysts say North Korea is receiving financial aid, military technology and food and energy supplies from Russia in return.

## **Getting Russia to peace talks**

Meanwhile, Ukrainian, US and European negotiators held their first talks on future peace negotiations with Russia, Zelenskyy said on Monday, adding that US envoys Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff joined the online talks and that "for the first time", Europe was represented by "those who will represent it in negotiations with the Russians".

France, Britain, Germany, Poland, Italy and European Union negotiators have been chosen to represent the continent at future talks with Russia.

Zelenskyy said the five countries, the EU and a NATO representative had been involved in the latest talks with the US envoys.

"The conversation was not easy, but there is a result," the Ukrainian leader added, without giving details.

Kushner confirmed the talks, which he said included national security advisers from the European nations, and insisted that "progress" was being made to end the war.

He said much of the focus of talks had been on ways to "facilitate direct engagement" between Ukraine and Russia.

"While much of the public discussion focuses on the differences between the parties, we continue to see positive and constructive ideas emerging through serious discussions away from the public spotlight," Kushner said in a post on X.
