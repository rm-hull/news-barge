## EU economy chief Dombrovskis warns France to rein in debt as growth outlook improves

EU Economy Commissioner Valdis Dombrovskis has urged France to stick to its commitments on reducing its budget deficit, even as the European Commission upgraded its growth forecasts for the European economy.

Speaking exclusively to Euronews on Tuesday, Dombrovskis played down the immediate risk of a sovereign debt crisis in France, arguing that rising borrowing costs reflected a broader global trend, with US ten-year borrowing rates at comparable levels to those of France.

The Commission raised its growth forecast for the EU to 1.4% for both 2026 and 2027, and to 1.2% for the eurozone, despite a persistent energy price shock that is expected to keep inflation elevated at around 3% next year.

However, mounting public debt remains a concern, particularly in France, where it is projected to reach 123% of GDP by 2028.

Dombrovskis said financial markets were increasingly differentiating between EU countries, making fiscal discipline all the more important.

“We do not see any immediate market risk,” he said, adding that demand for French government bonds remained strong.

“But obviously, it's important to send the right signal.”

With France facing a potentially turbulent presidential election campaign, Dombrovskis stressed the importance of adopting the government's “ambitious” draft budget for 2027, which would bring the country into line with its fiscal commitments to Brussels.

He said fiscal discipline would be a central message when the Commission presents its European Semester autumn package next week.

Dombrovskis also confirmed that the Commission would unveil a proposal on Wednesday to provide Ukraine with a further €45 billion in financial assistance, covering roughly two-thirds of its estimated funding shortfall for 2027.

He said Russia's intensifying attacks on Ukrainian infrastructure had increased Kyiv's military and budgetary needs.

Dombrovskis also reiterated that the EU reserved the right to use frozen Russian sovereign assets to cover Ukraine's loan repayments if Moscow failed to pay war reparations.
