A senior member of the United States army who was arrested in the UK on suspicion of spying for Russia has been taken into custody by the US military.

British counter-terror police last week arrested the 41-year-old soldier near Gloucester.

Lieutenant Colonel Brandon Carroll Tyner was based at the Imjin Barracks, near Innsworth in Gloucestershire, which is is home to the headquarters of Nato’s Allied Rapid Reaction Corps.

As part of the investigation, officers carried out searches at two locations in Gloucestershire.

Lt Col Tyner joined up in 2008 as a senior human resources officer, and has since deployed overseas to Iraq, Qatar and Afghanistan.

Counter Terrorism Policing said the soldier was detained on suspicion of offences under the National Security Act 2023. The allegations related to sections on obtaining or disclosing protected information and assisting a foreign intelligence service.

The handover was done in line with international protocols, the Metropolitan Police said.

![The suspect was based at Imjin Barracks in Gloucestershire](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/13/21/2304128748..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Assistant Commissioner Laurence Taylor, head of Counter Terrorism Policing, said the case was handed to US authorities after discussion with the Crown Prosecution Service.

“These are extremely serious allegations, and our collective response should send a strong message that we will act swiftly and robustly where we suspect this kind of espionage activity taking place,” he said.

“I’d like to praise the excellent work of the investigation team here in the UK to get us to this point and we will continue to support our US colleagues as they take the matter forwards.”

FBI director Kash Patel said: “This FBI has zero tolerance for anyone who betrays their oath to the United States or puts our national security at risk.”

He said the FBI’s Philadelphia Field Office and Legat London had worked with British partners to “identify a potential threat to national security and take swift action”.

US Brigadier General Sean Stinchon said: “This case should serve as a stark warning to any service member suspected of violating the oath we all take to protect and defend the Constitution.

“Whenever we suspect somebody of betraying the trust of their fellow soldiers and jeopardising our collective defence, we will identify you, and we will ensure justice is served. This effort is a great example of the remarkable partnership Army Counterintelligence Command has with Army prosecutors, the FBI, DOJ, and the UK.”

A Kremlin spokesman has denied Russia has any links to the US officer.
