# BBC doctor warns common habit you don't realise you're doing could trigger headache

## Experts highlights the surprising, unconscious behaviour that could be the missing link behind your recurring headaches

A BBC health expert has warned that headaches may be caused by an unexpected culprit - a habit people often do without noticing. Appearing on BBC Morning Live today (November 17), Dr Ranj Singh said teeth grinding could be to blame, adding that cases are increasing and it’s becoming more common among younger people.

He noted that it "affects about one in five people globally, but we’re getting reports from the British Dental Association (BDA) that it is on the rise, particularly among younger people."

Not only is it said to be on the rise, but "it’s a bit worse at this time of year", Dr Ranj added. He said experts don’t yet fully understand what’s driving the rise, but suggested it may be linked to higher stress and anxiety levels, seasonal money worries in the run-up to Christmas, and even colder temperatures, which can make muscles tighten.

### How do I know if I am grinding my teeth in my sleep?

Often, people don’t notice they’re doing it until symptoms appear — or until someone else mentions it - because grinding can be loud enough for others to hear. The health guru explained that when you chew, "there are a group of muscles in your jaw that spring into action." When these muscles activate, if they’re under increased tension or are more active than usual, it can lead to grinding and clenching. This can happen while you’re awake or when you’re asleep.

### What are the signs I'm grinding my teeth when I sleep?

Headaches could actually be a sign that you grind your teeth in your sleep. If there is no apparent cause for your headache, you should see a GP - but this is one cause worth considering.

The doctor explained: "You could have headaches, particularly in the morning, because you are clenching. And because the muscles [involved] extend towards the head, that could cause headaches."

You may also notice:

* **Pain** - You could have soreness in your jaw area
* **Teeth** - You may have a chipped tooth or other damage to fillings
* **Noise** - Your partner may notice this
* **Teeth marks** - This could be on the inside of the cheek or on the tongue

### You could develop TMJ

Dr Ranj said that if it continues, it could develop into temporomandibular disorder (TMD). This is a condition that affects how the jaw moves. It’s usually not serious and generally improves on its own.

Symptoms of TMD include:

* Difficulty opening your mouth fully
* A headache around your temples
* Your jaw locking when you open your mouth
* Clicking, popping or grinding noises when you move your jaw
* Pain around your jaw, ear and temple

The pain may be worse when chewing and when you feel stressed.

### Can I prevent teeth grinding?

There are ways to help reduce the risk, though they don’t work for everyone. Getting enough sleep can make a big difference. He also recommends trying relaxation techniques and cutting back on smoking, alcohol and caffeine.

He adds that it may also help to avoid chewing gum or hard foods, as these can put extra strain on the jaw muscles. In some cases, Botox can be injected into the muscles - usually a few injections on each side to ease symptoms. The condition, known as bruxism, can be severe enough in some cases to qualify for NHS treatment.

### This could make it worse, says Dr Ranj

While face massages can help relieve tension, he says that some face wraps that wrap around your jaw and over your head "could make this more problematic" and "increase" the strain you are trying to avoid. These, according to the expert, are more targeted towards shaping the jaw rather than teeth grinding.

Instead, try a dental splint or bite guard, which can protect your teeth when you grind. According to tmj.org, a "splint is a removable dental appliance that covers several or all of the upper or lower teeth. Constructed in a dental lab, splints are typically made of hard acrylic resin and moulded from an impression of your teeth."

If you’re worried, or if the pain is getting worse or isn’t improving, speak to your GP or dentist, the doctor concluded. He stressed that dentists are the experts in this area.
