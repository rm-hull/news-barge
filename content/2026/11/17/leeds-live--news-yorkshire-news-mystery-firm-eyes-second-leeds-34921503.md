# Mystery firm eyes second Leeds AI data centre 1.5 miles from Microsoft site

## A Leeds councillor has warned that a tech firm is in talks with the council over plans to build a second AI data centre

A technology company is reportedly in discussions with the council regarding the potential construction of a second AI data centre in the city, according to a local councillor.

Leeds Green Party leader Penny Stables revealed it would be situated just 1.5 miles from a data centre already planned by Microsoft in Stourton.

Environmental campaigners have previously staged protests concerning the substantial energy and water consumption the facility at the former Skelton Grange power station site could require.

Leeds City Council's plans panel approved the Microsoft project in April.

Councillor Stables confirmed that preliminary discussions have commenced with an undisclosed firm about a second data centre proposal.

She stated: "I am extremely concerned that another planning application is coming forward for a data hyper-centre only 1.5 miles away from that agreed at Skelton Grange.

"In particular, I have serious concerns about where the enormous amount of energy will come from and whether there will be any potential effect on the river Aire and its ecosystem."

The council explained that pre-application planning consultations are frequently offered to deliver early guidance for developers seeking to build within the city.

A council spokesperson commented: "Many pre-application discussions take place and do not always result in an application being submitted.

"All pre-application discussions are commercially sensitive and are treated as confidential, so we are not able to provide any further detail at this stage.

"However, should a formal planning application subsequently be submitted, the relevant information will be made publicly available through the normal planning process."

Over 45,000 people have put their names to a petition opposing the Microsoft development, which would accommodate computer servers and cooling chimneys.

Microsoft stated that its Stourton complex would power digital services for communities, businesses and the public sector.

The company previously said it was committed to becoming "carbon negative, water positive and zero waste by 2030".

The Skelton Grange development would comprise three data centre buildings, alongside a warehouse offering up to 161,000 sq ft of floorspace.
