Pete Buttigieg has pushed back after President Donald Trump blamed him for the weekend’s air travel chaos.

Trump targeted the former transportation secretary over Friday’s rare nationwide ground stop, which led to more than 12,000 flight cancellations around the U.S.

The Trump administration has attributed the ground stop — the longest since 9/11 — to communications problems triggered by a routine equipment upgrade. Officials have since called for more congressional funding to modernize what they described as an old and outdated air traffic control system.

“We’ve been trying to get the money for two years to fix it,” the president told reporters outside the White House Monday. “And Buttigieg ruined the system. He totally ruined it. The Democrats — really, it’s very old — but what they did really set back the system a long way.”

Buttigieg, who served under Joe Biden and is widely viewed as a contender for the Democratic presidential nomination in 2028, responded with a lengthy X post a few hours later.

![Pete Buttigieg clapped back after Donald Trump blamed him for Friday’s nationwide ground stop, writing on X: ‘As secretary, I made sure to leave air travel in better condition than when we arrived’](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/17/14/27/GettyImages-2304502548.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“As secretary, I made sure to leave air travel in better condition than when we arrived,” the former South Bend mayor wrote. “We reversed a decade of decline in air traffic controller staffing, drove down runway safety incidents, and when I left office we were experiencing the lowest flight cancellation rates in years.”

“As part of that work, I launched a project to transition the FAA's communications system from copper wire to a modern network,” he added. “I am glad the Trump administration has continued that work, but they have so far failed to explain why their execution of it triggered the worst FAA communications breakdown this century.”

Friday’s ground stop was linked to a routine router replacement during a copper-to-fiber upgrade in Arkansas. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy has said a contractor may have been responsible. The FAA is still investigating.

As part of a modernization effort, 69 percent of copper communications used by air traffic control have been replaced by fiber, which can transmit more data over long distances, according to the Department of Transportation.

“I support more funding for FAA upgrades, but this episode is another blow to the public’s confidence that the administration can deliver,” Buttigieg added.

“The Trump administration owes Americans an honest accounting of how they have spent the billions of dollars already authorized, clearer answers on why their repair work brought down the entire system on Friday, and an explanation of what they are doing to make sure this failure is not repeated,” he wrote.

Aviation experts previously told *The Independent* that Friday’s episode should have been preventable.

The former transportation secretary’s remarks come amid a debate over whether the Federal Aviation Administration needs additional funding to prevent similar disruptions.

Congress approved $12.5 billion for modernization last year, which Duffy has described as a down payment rather than the full cost of replacing outdated systems. By Friday, he increased that request to $50 billion, calling the need an “emergency.”

“This is bipartisan. This is American. I want $50 billion,” Duffy said Friday.

President Donald Trump took a more partisan approach in Sunday Truth Social posts, accusing the Biden administration of making an “old and broken system worse than when they began.” He urged Congress to approve funding rather than modernizing in “drips and drabs.”

Some Democrats say their objections are not to modernization itself, but to the administration’s management of the effort.

Rep. Rosa DeLauro, the top Democrat on the House Appropriations Committee, said previously approved funding largely remained unspent and accused Duffy of demanding “a $50 billion ransom without a real plan to deliver.”

“Congress has shown its willingness to modernize the FAA’s systems and support its workforce as part of annual bipartisan legislation, but we need a competent partner at the Department of Transportation to carry this out in a sustainable, transparent manner,” she said in a statement.
