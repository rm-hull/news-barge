Head office employees at Santander Bank were handed a back-to-office policy two years ago requiring them to attend 12 days a month on average - but it appears they may be able to be in significantly less.

More than 15,000 people work for the Spanish-owned bank in the UK, which in 2024 was one of the ranks of financial firms to order staff to be more frequently back in the office.

The likes of Lloyds, HSBC, Morgan Stanley and JPMorgan Chase all placed similar requirements on different members of staff across that year and the next, reversing the hybrid or remote working policies which became commonplace during and after Covid.

But for Santander staff, a loophole seems to allow the target number to be significantly lower than expected.

Officially, Santander UK policy dictates that impacted employees must attend a head office location 12 days each month, averaged over a rolling period of three months.

However, it is understood this is not a strict metric, as it is adjusted for notable absences including annual leave.

Sick leave and public holidays are also included in the same flexibility around office attendance, which the bank believes allows for strong collaboration, staff development and meeting individual needs.

As such, it’s feasible that a staff member may not actually be required to attend the head office at all in a given month, should they have holidays booked in during the same few weeks they are are off ill or there are bank holidays in the calendar.

All the same, Santander has faced problems with integrating new staff into the policy, with TSB employees preparing legal action to fight being included in the mandate following Santander’s £2.6bn takeover deal.

TSB staff had previously been allowed to work remotely, with the return to office policy presently expected to come into force by April 2027.

A Santander UK spokeswoman told *The Times*: “The 12 days a month requirement is adjusted for periods of leave, including public holidays, annual leave and sick leave, meaning if a colleague is not well enough to work, they do not have to make up their in-office days.”

The policy doesn’t apply to branch staff, who may have different requirements, but instead those who are head office-based. Santander has offices in London and Milton Keynes.

Recently, Santander released data showing that nearly half of Brits (44 per cent) were unprepared and unable to pay an unexpected bill, while more than seven in ten (71 per cent) have faced one in the past year - costing £535 on average.

Car repairs (43 per cent), broken household appliances (43 per cent) and boiler breakdowns (34 per cent) were the expenses that Brits dread the most, according to their survey.
