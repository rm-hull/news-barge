The former head of the UK's therapist body has been struck off after accidentally sending details about a vulnerable six-year-old patient to their ex school headteacher.

Therapist Margaret Ellis breached confidentiality rules by sending a 'large amount of confidential information' about the child in an email, a tribunal heard.

Without parental consent, Ms Ellis sent an email about the child to their former school headteacher, a solicitor, and the leader of a council.

After being taken to a misconduct hearing, she has now been struck off for breaching the 'fundamental tenets of the profession'.

The misconduct hearing found that Ms Ellis 'was in denial' and that her email showed 'a clear risk of emotional and psychological harm'.

Ms Ellis had been chair of the College of Occupational Therapists between 1987 and 1989.

On September 13, 2022, she sent an email to various people containing a large amount of confidential information regarding Child A, who was one of her patients, and her family.

The recipients included the headteacher of the child's previous school, a Special Educational Needs and Disability (SEND) officer, a solicitor and the Lead of Suffolk County Council.

The email contained information around the child's occupational therapy, personal needs and details of other multidisciplinary teams concerned in their care.

The child's father reported the incident saying that Ms Ellis was no longer involved in providing Child A's care and that he had not given consent for the information to be disclosed.

The case was taken before the Health & Care Professions Tribunal Service (HCPTS) and an interim suspension was put in place.

Ms Ellis unsuccessfully appealed the decision and has now been struck off.

The chair of the HCPTS panel concluded: "There was a clear risk of emotional and psychological harm being caused to a vulnerable child and their family.

"The unauthorised disclosure of personal information is a matter which would bring the profession into disrepute.

"Members of the public are entitled to expect that the professionals trusted with their personal data respect that data and do not disclose it without authority to do so.

"[Ms Ellis] is under a duty to protect the public and manage the risks of harm.

"Further, [Ms Ellis'] apparent hostility and lack of respect for the HCPC as a professional regulator is something which brings the profession into disrepute.

"The Panel also noted the substantive hearing panel’s conclusions that [Ms Ellis'] actions had breached fundamental tenets of the profession.

"Due to the lack of engagement of [Ms Ellis] and her failure to attend the review hearing, the Panel concluded that [Ms Ellis] had failed to discharge the persuasive burden to demonstrate that she had fully acknowledged the deficiencies which led to the original finding and has addressed that impairment sufficiently.

"The Panel considered that [Ms Ellis] was in denial, and that it was highly unlikely that [Ms Ellis] would engage during a further period of suspension, to the extent where her fitness to practise would be found to be no longer impaired."
