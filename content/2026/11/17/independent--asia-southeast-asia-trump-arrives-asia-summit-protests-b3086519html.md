Hundreds of anti-Trump protesters have been arrested in demonstrations against the US president’s whirlwind trip to Asia.

Donald Trump touched down in the Philippines on Tuesday to meet with the leaders of Japan, Australia and India, a group dubbed ‘The Quad’, ahead of his trilateral summit with Vladimir Putin and Xi Jinping on Thursday.

But while Trump was pictured holding US and Filipino flag and serenaded by a performance of his signature song ‘YMCA’ upon his arrival at Ninoy Aquino International Airport, crowds of demonstrators were gathering in the capital Manila for a fifth day of protests.

Organisers claimed as many as 10,000 people would be taking part, calling for an end to the Middle East war and the scrapping of the US-led Pax Silica supply chain initiative. The latter scheme will see an industrial hub established on the Philippine island of Luzon, but activists say the development will drain local resources.

![Police have filed charges against more than 200 protesters in Manila](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/17/16/download..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)
![Demonstrators brought effigies of the US president](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/17/16/2305521944..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The protesters burned an effigy of the US commander-in-chief, while others brought placards and even a makeshift coffin demanding ‘Trump out’.

Police have filed charges against more than 200 protesters arrested in Manila, according to local media. Two police personnel sustained injuries to their arms and hands on Tuesday due to effigy burning, the Philippines National Police (PNP) force added.

However, despite the arrests PNP spokesperson Police Brig. Gen. Allen Rae Co said the situation had remained “generally peaceful” from Sunday through Tuesday while the ASEAN summit of Asian heads of states took place.

![The protesters called for an end to the war in Iran and](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/17/13/download.-2..jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Trump held his first meeting in two years with the Quad group of Indo-Pacific democracies, including India’s prime minister Nahendra Modi, Japan’s Sanae Takaichi and Australia’s Anthony Albanese.

In a joint statement, the group announced plans for joint military exercises next year featuring maritime patrol aircraft, and reaffirmed a commitment to coordinate and ‌fund the development of artificial intelligence.

“We want to ensure that the future of this critical technology is dominated by ‌nations that share our values and our common interests and make sure that it turns out to be as good as it can be,” Trump said, referring to the AI plans.

![Riot police stand and protesters at a demonstration during the 49th Association of Southeast Asian Nations (ASEAN) Summit](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/17/16/2305032094..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The Quad also expressed “grave concerns” about “economic coercion”, including through export restrictions.

While the statement did not name any country, the US and Japan have complained about China’s restrictions on exports of rare earths ⁠vital to the manufacturing of everything from electric cars to cell phones.

The ASEAN summit will be followed on Thursday by the main focus of Trump’s trip, a gathering of the 21-member Pacific Rim grouping APEC in China.

China’s president Xi will host ⁠Trump and Russia’s Putin at the event, among others. Trump has also suggested that North Korea’s leader Kim Jong Un could also attend the already historic gathering of the three world leaders.

![Photos were released of Kim Jong Un in Samjiyon, not far from the Chinese border](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/17/12/download..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

On Tuesday Kim was photographed visiting a tourism venue with his daughter, who was skiing, near Samjiyon which is close to the Chinese border. The leader was sat on a large armchair on the ice and snow, smoking a cigarette.

Trump has said the Kim’s presence at APEC was ultimately up to Chinese President Xi Jinping.

“He would like to,” the US president remarked on Tuesday. “We’ll see what happens.”

![The Quad: US President Donald Trump poses with Japan's Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi, India's Prime Minister Narendra Modi and Australia's Prime Minister Anthony Albanese](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/17/11/2305027680..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

When they meet Thursday, Trump, Putin and Xi are expected to discuss their nuclear arsenals, AI development and the Iran war.

Nine months into the conflict, the US president could not escape questions about the war in the Middle East as he was questioned about a CNN report claiming Iran was putting together plans to attack Washington with a dirty bomb or chemical weapons.

Trump warned: “Well, if there is, they will pay a price the likes of which no country has ever paid before. I hope it’s not. But we take it very seriously because they happen to be crazy.”
