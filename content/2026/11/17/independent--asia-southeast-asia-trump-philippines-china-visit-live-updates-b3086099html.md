Donald Trump has warned Iran would pay “a price the likes of which no country has ever paid before” if reports of a plot to attack Washington DC with a dirty bomb were true.

The US president landed in Manila on Tuesday on his first stop on an Asia tour that will include a historic summit with China’s Xi Jinping and Russia’s Vladimir Putin.

Reporters questioned the president about a CNN report that Iran was putting together plans to attack Washington with a dirty bomb or chemical weapons. A White House spokesperson said “at this time there are no credible threats to the homeland.”

Nevertheless, Trump responded to the report with a stark warning: “Well, if there is, they will pay a price the likes of which no country has ever paid before.

“I hope it’s not. But we take it very seriously because they happen to be crazy.”

Much of the focus of the trip will be on the first trilateral meeting between leaders of the US, China and Russia where they are expected to discuss their nuclear arsenals, AI development and the Iran war.

## Trump says US trade deal with Thailand to be signed soon

Thailand will eliminate ​all tariffs on US exports and remove substantial non-tariff barriers under a trade agreement with the United States expected to be finalised in the coming weeks, President Donald ⁠Trump said on Tuesday.

Appearing alongside Thai Prime Minister Anutin Charnvirakul at the signing of a statement about the "substantial conclusion" to negotiations on reciprocal trade, Trump said the agreement, when completed, "will be a wonderful deal for both ⁠parties."

"Thailand will eliminate all tariffs ​on ⁠US exports, which is something that we've been after for a long time," Trump said, hours after his arrival ⁠in Manila for the end of the Association of Southeast Asian ​Nations ⁠summit.

"This is an incredible ‌victory ... I look forward to finalising the details of our formal agreement in the coming weeks."

## Trump announces 'wonderful deal' with Thailand on tariffs

President Donald Trump has announced Thailand will eliminate all tariffs on US exports and remove substantial non-tariff barriers as part of a trade agreement expected to be finalised soon.

Trump said at the signing a joint statement on reciprocal trade that it would be "a wonderful deal".

Thailand's Prime Minister Anutin Charnvirakul joined Trump to sign the agreement at the Solaire Resort on the sidelines of the 49th Association of Southeast Asian Nations (ASEAN) Summit in Manila.

## 'Pretty confident something's underway': Odds improve on Trump-Kim meeting

The odds of US president Donald Trump meeting North Korean leader Kim Jong Un on the sidelines of the Apec summit have improved after the Kremlin said a meeting between Kim and Russian President Vladimir Putin was possible, analysts said.

Trump has repeatedly expressed interest in meeting Kim again and said on Monday it might happen in China this week.

However, previously mooted meetings have not taken place and US and South Korean officials have said the chances were low.

But a report by Russia's Interfax news agency quoting the Kremlin saying Putin could meet Kim if the North Korean leader attended this week's Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (APEC) summit in Shenzhen has shifted bets.

The Kremlin "wouldn't do that unless they were certain he was going to meet", said Sydney Seiler, a former US national intelligence officer for North Korea, now at the Center for Strategic and International Studies.

"I'm pretty confident something's underway."

A series of visits by North Korean officials to Russia and China in ​recent weeks, ⁠as well as by Russian and Chinese delegations to Pyongyang, could also have been for summit preparations. Kim also made a surprise visit to the Chinese border city of Dandong last month.

## Japan to play more proactive role in boosting regional economic and energy resilience

ASEAN's leaders held ​meetings on Tuesday involving counterparts from Japan, South Korea, China and India.

Japanese prime minister Sanae Takaichi pledged to work with ASEAN to boost regional economic and ‌energy resilience, to address export restrictions and vulnerabilities from rapid AI development, and to ⁠strengthen supply chains, including on critical minerals.

"While the international order is experiencing a period of great instability, Japan will play an ever more proactive role," Takaichi said.

Trump ⁠was due to sign a joint statement with Thai Prime Minister Anutin Charnvirakul on Tuesday on an agreement on reciprocal trade. Thai media have reported the two would hold talks and the statement would be about progress on ‌negotiations.

The US is Thailand's largest export market, accounting ​for 24 per cent of its total shipments this year. The US designated Thailand as a major non-NATO ‌ally in 2003.

Trump will meet Philippine president Ferdinand Marcos Jr on Wednesday, before ​heading to Shenzhen.
