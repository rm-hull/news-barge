If President Donald Trump had a mental timer going for when he was going to discard Susie Wiles, as he’s done so many senior-level staffers throughout his terms, it was sped up when he learned she had cancer, a veteran reporter has revealed.

According to *The New York Times’*Maggie Haberman, who joined the* Raging Moderates* podcast with host Jessica Tarlov on Monday, Trump began “picking” on his then-chief of staff after Wiles announced her early-stage breast cancer diagnosis in March. His reasoning is as simple as it is cold: illness is weakness, so says Haberman.

Trump replaced Wiles as chief of staff after the midterms when she became a lightning rod for his rage in the wake of the blue tsunami. She was promptly supplanted by Trump’s steadfast MAGA hardliner, Stephen Miller.

Tarlov asked if the scandal involving Wiles’s daughter Katie allegedly “trading influence” in her position with the lobbying firm Continental Strategy, and the subsequent fallout with Trump, was the catalyst for her departure. Haberman said yes and no.

![In mid-September, Wiles announced she was cancer-free, but there were already too many cracks in the foundation of her working relationship with Trump at that point, according to reporter Maggie Haberman](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/06/21/2026-11-06T194237Z_1618674378_RC26JIA430SD_RTRMADP_3_USA-TRUMP-MILLER.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“In terms of why she left, there were a lot of reasons,” Haberman said. “The main one is that everybody has an hourglass timer with Trump on their own timeframe of how long they’re going to last. You know, the egg timer turns, and the sands start falling — it is literally true for everyone. And then they can end up coming back in some fashion.”

In mid-September, Wiles announced she was cancer-free, but there were already too many cracks in the foundation of her working relationship with Trump at that point to withstand the shellacking the GOP endured earlier this month, per Haberman.

“A couple of people have said to us privately that you can trace the beginning of chaos becoming more visible — doesn’t mean it wasn’t there — but more visible and reverting to the Trump mean, around her diagnosis,” the *NYT* veteran said. “She wasn’t physically as present; she tried working through it. People knew that she wasn’t there, and Trump does not like…illness. He sees it as weakness. And so, he started picking on her.”

Haberman added: “So, this was always going to end poorly this way.”
