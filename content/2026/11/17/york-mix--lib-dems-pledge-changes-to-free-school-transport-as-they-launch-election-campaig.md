The Liberal Democrats have pledged to restore free school transport to catchment schools and give communities more control over local decisions as they launch their campaign to take control of North Yorkshire Council.

The party, which is currently the council’s official opposition, has also promised tougher scrutiny of public spending and action to tackle sewage pollution as it sets out its priorities ahead of the May 2027 local elections.

The campaign sees the Liberal Democrats announcing the first three commitments in their Six to Fix programme.

One of the headline pledges is to restore free school transport to catchment schools through the development of ‘integrated local transport solutions’.

North Yorkshire Council’s decision to only offer free transport to a child’s nearest school in a bid to reduce the annual school transport bill of more than £50m has proved controversial.

The party says its approach would end uncertainty for families while improving access to essential services for the wider community.

It has also promised to give town and parish councils greater control over local services, assets and budgets, alongside the introduction of citizens’ panels to give residents a stronger voice in decisions.

Its third commitment focuses on the environment, including establishing a countywide Water Watch scheme to monitor water quality and sewage pollution.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/Cllr_Peter_Lacey_N_Yorks_Lib_Dems_Eveline_Stephenson_2026.jpg)

Cllr Peter Lacey, leader of the North Yorkshire Liberal Democrat group, said the party wanted to move beyond short-term pressures and focus on the long-term wellbeing of communities.

He said: “Residents want reliable services, from well-maintained roads and public spaces to fair school transport.

“But their aspirations go further. They want communities where children can flourish, families can access the support they need, and people have a genuine voice in shaping their future.

“Protecting green spaces, cleaning up rivers and responding to climate change are part of that same ambition for a healthier place to live and an improved legacy for the next generation.”

The party has also criticised the council’s financial management following revelations about its housebuilding company, Brierley Homes.

The council-owned company recorded a £7.8m loss in 2025/26 despite previously forecasting a £350,000 profit.

The party also pointed to £3.4m in council loans needed in 2025 to keep the company financially viable, with total borrowing from the authority standing at £27.54m.

Cllr Lacey said a Liberal Democrat administration would strengthen transparency and scrutiny, avoid unnecessary financial risks and consider the long-term consequences of council decisions.

He said: “Responsible stewardship of public money is essential, not an end in itself, but a way to sustain the services and investment that help residents, businesses and communities thrive.

“A Lib Dem-led council would strengthen transparency and scrutiny and seek to add greater value from major investments and contracts, avoid unnecessary risks outside its core responsibilities and consider the long-term consequences of its decisions for residents, the environment and future generations.”

The three pledges form part of a wider campaign covering community empowerment, council performance, public spending, access to services, local businesses and environmental protection.

Cllr Lacey added: “We have listened to people across North Yorkshire. Our first three promises bring that listening into action by giving communities more control, making local transport networks accessible for all and taking responsibility for our rivers, nature and climate.

“Our approach is collaborative and forward-looking. We want to bring people together around a long-term vision, with practical action now and a clear responsibility to those who come after us. That is the change we are ready to lead.”
