A former drug dealer has successfully spent six months persuading a judge he can go straight and is looking forward to a new crime-free life with his own business, York Crown Court heard.

Shaun Turner, 39, had bags of cocaine on him when he went through a red light and was stopped by police, said Benjamin Bell, prosecuting.

Officers found £19,000 had gone through his bank account, most of which was drug profits.

The Recorder of York, Judge Sean Morris, said Turner, who had no previous drug convictions, had started using drugs to cope with a ‘series of tragedies’ and then had started dealing to fund his habit.

Turner was living in sheltered accommodation when he first appeared for sentence in May.

After hearing about his offence and background, the judge said he took an ‘wholly unusual step’ of deferring sentence for six months so that he could turn his life round and set him a series of conditions.

When he returned to court, his barrister Nick Peacock, said he had fulfilled all but one condition – and the one he had failed to fulfil was because he had helped his son buy tools to start his apprenticeship in a legal trade.

Turner, who still lives in sheltered accommodation in Scarborough, pleaded guilty to being concerned in the supply of cocaine between January and July last year.

After reading reports on Turner’s actions in the six months since May and his current attitudes to offending, the judge said: “It is highly unlikely you will be back in court. I think you have turned a corner.

“No doubt you will soon be able to get normal, not supported accommodation.”

He gave him a two-year prison sentence suspended for two years on condition Turner does 240 hours’ unpaid work.

The conditions Turner had to meet during the six month deferment were: stay out of trouble, get a job, maintain stable accommodation, stop using illegal drugs and raise £500 towards prosecution costs.

His barrister Nick Peacock said he had fulfilled all of them apart from using the £500 to buy tools for his son.

He had had a job for four months in packaging items which he found so monotonous he had now set up his own business and had a contract to lay a patio.

Mr Bell said the cocaine found on Turner when he was arrested was worth about £230 in street sales.
