A petition has been started by a leading Wetherby ward councillor to try to reduce the number of HGVs travelling through Boston Spa.

Coun Norma Harrington, Chair of Outer North East Community Committee, says the move follows complaints from residents about congestion on the A659 High Street and options include re-classifying the route.

“I have been approached by residents of Boston Spa, in increasing numbers, over the past eight years, complaining about the number of HGVs in the village,” Coun Harrington told the *Wetherby News*.

“Congestion in the village is an issue for several reasons.

“But the presence of large commercial vehicles exacerbates the situation with parked cars the length of the High Street meaning there is no safe place to move to, to accommodate them.

“There have been several near misses reported.

“The safety of all of our residents, road users and pedestrians on our roads is paramount.”

Coun Harrington added: “It is difficult to say where exactly the HGVs are coming from, but many are contractors’ vehicles using the village as a through route to Tadcaster, as some residents have actually followed them through the village.

“The bridge at Thorp Arch has been a problem for many years, with several instances of extremely long vehicles struggling to get up the hill and then complete the turn at the top of Bridge Road.

“One such vehicle blocked the whole road for the best part of a working day.”

She added: “The petition is being carried out to gauge wider public opinion, not only those who have written to me and my Ward colleague Alan Lamb, or raised the issue at Parish Council meetings.”

Coun Harrington explained that Highways Officers are carrying out roadside counts to identify the exact number of HGVs travelling through the village as part of evidence to change the classification of the route.

She added : “After discussions with Senior Highways staff at Leeds City Council, it has become clear that there is a formal process available to re-classify the A659 from an A road to a B road.

“Reclassification would open the door to applying for an HGV ban within the village (with exemptions for buses and local deliveries).

“This change would help reduce congestion, lower air pollution, and create a safer, cleaner, and more pleasant environment for everyone who lives in or visits Boston Spa).

Highways staff are also looking at the feasibility of alternative routes using the A1 and A64 with improved signage.

“There would, of course, always be exemptions for genuine deliveries to shops and businesses, as well as buses and emergency vehicles,” Coun Harrington added.

The petition has already received comments on social media.

Simon Hawkin said: “Driving through Boston spa is bad enough in a van let alone a HGV.

“There's a weight restriction, so any HGVs are likely to be delivering to or collecting from Boston Spa.

“The issue is the awful on road parking, and some extremely poor driving skills (the latter not just a problem in Boston Spa but everywhere).

“It’s going to take longer for any HGVs to fight their way through Boston Spa than it is to carry on and use the A64 to access Tadcaster.”

John Smith added: “It's cars parking on both sides of the road that is the problem.”
