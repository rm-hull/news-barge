# Andy Burnham says Brexit deal is like ‘Emperor’s new clothes’

## Prime Minister Andy Burnham said the Brexit deal hashed out by the Conservatives had “burnt our bridges” with the EU as he pledged to forge new ties with the bloc that improve the lives of people in the UK.

It comes ahead of the UK-EU summit on Friday, at which Mr Burnham will seek to lock in mini-deals on food, emissions trading and youth mobility.

The Prime Minister defended his push for closer ties with Brussels during a major speech at the Lord Mayor’s Banquet last night.

He said: “It’s like the Emperor’s new clothes – where no one is prepared to say the obvious truth. Well, I say it as I see it. And to be frank, the Emperor has nothing on.

“The Brexit we were given has not worked. We have lost more than we gained.”

After fuelling questions about the parameters around his vision for deeper partnership with a call for an “honest discussion” about free movement, he reiterated that he was ruling out a deal that crosses Labour’s manifesto red lines.

This means no return to the single market, customs union or freedom of movement, though Mr Burnham has said he wants to explore options for Britain’s longer-term relationship with the bloc, on which voters would ultimately decide.

He added: “The referendum gave voters a chance to send a message and demand change. Ten years on, it is clear that the change promised by the promoters of Brexit has not materialised.

“Far from taking back control, we are in a weaker position on immigration. The small boats crisis has reached our shores. Household bills and business costs have been soaring.

“The truth is Britain ended up with a much harder Brexit than the one promised at the referendum. Rather than back Theresa May’s deal, Boris Johnson burnt our bridges. His Tory Brexit deal has done more harm than good.”

He said the proposed deal would spare the UK’s steel, car and aluminium industries from extra taxes and further talks on lower electricity prices, as well as opening the door to greater cooperation on lower electricity prices.

He said any new agreement must improve the Brexit deal reached by the Tories, and pass the “Makerfield test”.

The test was set out by the Prime Minister following his by-election win in the Greater Manchester seat, on the premise that measures which work for the constituency will work for others like it across the country.

“Any deal I seek to agree in Brussels must first pass what I call the Makerfield test: will it make life better for people in the parts of Britain that have felt forgotten?” he wrote.

Downing Street defended plans for a scheme that would open the doors for EU youngsters to come to the UK to work at the same time as the Government is grappling with high levels of youth unemployment.

Former prime minister Boris Johnson said he believed that Britain would back Brexit again if Mr Burnham forced another vote, despite polls suggesting a majority now favour either rejoining or getting closer to Europe.

Echoing Lord David Cameron’s advice last week to avoid returning to the bloc, he told the Telegraph he hoped the Prime Minister “has the wisdom not to do this”.

He added: “I think that when he goes to the country, one way or the other, people will wake up to what is going on. They won’t want it, and he won’t succeed.”
