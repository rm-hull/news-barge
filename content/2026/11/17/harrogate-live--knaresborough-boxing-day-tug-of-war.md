Knaresborough’s Boxing Day tug of war is listed for **Saturday 26 December 2026 at Low Bridge**, with the pulling starting at** 12 noon**. Drinkers from the Half Moon on one bank and the Mother Shipton Inn on the other pull against each other across the River Nidd, each side trying to drag the other into the water (Visit North Yorkshire).

## Overview

* **When:** Saturday 26 December 2026. The listing runs from 11.30am to 2.30pm, and the competition begins at noon.
* **Where:** Low Bridge, Knaresborough, on the B6163 (sat nav HG5 8HY).
* **Who pulls:** teams from the Half Moon and the Mother Shipton Inn, on opposite banks of the Nidd. There are men’s and women’s contests.
* **Tickets:** none listed. It is a riverside spectacle you watch from the banks.

## Is it on this year?

Yes, on the evidence so far. North Yorkshire Council’s tourism service, Visit North Yorkshire, lists the 2026 tug of war on Saturday 26 December, with the same noon start as last year. Neither pub has published its own 2026 notice yet. We will add any change, and anything the teams announce, to this page.

## When and where

The rope goes across the river at **Low Bridge**, where the B6163 crosses the Nidd, between the Half Moon on one bank and the Mother Shipton Inn on the other. The listing describes it as a time-honoured tradition and invites spectators to head down to Low Bridge, “pick a side and cheer”. Arrive before noon if you want a place at the water’s edge.

## Parking and getting there

Knaresborough’s council car parks, including Waterside by the river (about 70 spaces) and York Place (about 240), are listed with their charges on our parking guide. For Boxing Day trains and buses, check our Christmas trains page and the roadworks and travel page before you set off.

## More on Boxing Day

The tug of war fits neatly into a riverside walk: our Boxing Day walks page has the route from the castle down to Low Bridge, and the Boxing Day pilgrimage from Ripon Cathedral to Fountains Abbey runs the same morning. Six days later, Knaresborough’s New Year’s Day duck race uses the same river.
