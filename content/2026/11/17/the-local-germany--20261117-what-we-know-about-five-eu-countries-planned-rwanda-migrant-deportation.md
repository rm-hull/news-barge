The five have been exploring sending migrants to centres outside Europe for months, with the pilot project planned to be launched next year.

**Who and how?**

During a joint press announcement in Vienna, ministers from the five EU countries and Rwanda remained evasive on the project's scope.

But according to people involved, the centre will be for around 100 migrants.

They will be men and women whose asylum applications have been rejected but who refuse to return to their countries of origin despite having exhausted all avenues of appeal.

Nationals of any country are eligible, without exceptions, according to those involved in the plan. Minors are excluded.

NGOs are worried the initiative could ultimately target all asylum seekers -- a claim denied by the five countries involved.

The five European countries' national authorities will be responsible for transferring the rejected asylum seekers to Rwanda.

**Where?**

Once they arrive in Rwanda, authorities of the east African country will take charge of them, and they will receive residence permits.

They will be placed in a centre, with Kigali inclined to name it a "welcome centre".

The five European countries insist that it will be open, without any deprivation of liberty or barbed wire.

A site has already been identified. The European countries, however, refuse to specify where it is.

They only stress that it is not one of the centres the United Kingdom had planned to use.

London had announced plans in 2022 to send asylum seekers to Rwanda, but the project was ultimately abandoned because of legal obstacles.

**How long?**

The idea is for the migrants to stay in the centre for a few months, with the aim that they then integrate into Rwandan society.

"These illegal migrants who will be relocated to Rwanda are not going to be kept in a camp or in a dedicated area. They will be integrated in our society," Rwandan Interior Minister Vincent Biruta told the press conference.

Vocational training and language courses are to be offered to them.

The five EU countries also say an independent monitoring mechanism -- the details of which have not been spelled out -- will be put in place to ensure respect for human rights.

Another option is for the exiles to return voluntarily to their country of origin, but not to the EU.

**Why, and is it legal?**

Those involved in the plan hope this will discourage migrants from trying to settle on the European continent, besides getting those asylum seekers whose applications have been rejected out of the EU.

Danish Prime Minister Mette Frederiksen said the project aimed to send a "signal... that you can no longer enter the European Union illegally and think that you can just stay around".

The EU states have refused to say what Rwanda would receive in return.

The five countries claim that the legal basis is solid, relying on EU legislation on returns adopted a few months ago.

They have dismissed fierce criticism and concern about the treatment of migrants.

But the plans are expected to face court challenges.
