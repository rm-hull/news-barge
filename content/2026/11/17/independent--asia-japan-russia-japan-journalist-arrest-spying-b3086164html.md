Press freedom groups have condemned Russia’s detention of a Japanese journalist on charges of espionage, calling for his immediate release and warning against the criminalisation of reporting.

Yuki Imai, a 34-year-old correspondent for the Japanese newspaper *Hokkaido Shimbun*, was detained on 14 October.

Television footage showed officers barging into Imai’s hotel room in the city of Yuzhno-Sakhalinsk on Sakhalin Island while he was sleeping, pinning him to the floor and handcuffing him.

He was swiftly transferred to the Lefortovo Prison in Moscow after a court ordered his pretrial detention till at least December.

Imai faces up to 20 years in jail if convicted on the espionage charges.

His detention has drawn condemnation from press freedom organisations, which have described it as part of a pattern by the Russian government to target foreign journalists.

The International Press Institute said Russia had provided no evidence for the charges slapped on Imai. “The arrest of Yuki Imai follows an established pattern by the Russian government of treating journalists doing their jobs as spies to be used as geopolitical leverage,” the institute’s director Scott Griffen said.

“Journalism is not a crime. The IPI global network stands in solidarity with Imai as he faces these baseless charges. We forcefully demand his immediate release.”

![Yuki Imai remains in Lefortovo Prison in Moscow](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/17/07/2299589819..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The *Hokkaido Shimbun,* which released the detained reporter’s name on 12 November in the hope of gathering public support for his swift return, rejected Russia's allegation that Imai was involved in espionage.

The paper said a lawyer representing Imai had confirmed he was being held in Moscow's Lefortovo Prison.

The lawyer, who had visited the detainee several times to deliver food and clothing, reported that he was in good health.

More than a month after arresting Imai, Russian authorities were yet to give an update about the progress of the investigation or a possible trial.

The Japan Newspaper Publishers and Editors Association called on their government to “continue making every possible diplomatic effort, including even closer international cooperation with other nations, to ensure the safety of the detained reporter and secure his immediate release”.

“The freedom of the press to conduct interviews and report the news as well as freedom of expression form the very foundation of a democratic society,” it said.

“It is unacceptable for these rights to be unjustly infringed upon or for journalistic activities to be obstructed.”

The Committee to Protect Journalists said it was “deeply chilling” a foreign journalist was arrested by Russian authorities.

“Images of a man being put into a cage, and the threat of a long period of pre-trial detention, are chillingly reminiscent of the ordeal US journalist Evan Gershkovich went through from 2023, when he was also held in Russia on sham charges of espionage,” said Fiona O’Brien, the group’s director for Europe and Central Asia. “Journalists must not be used as leverage in wider geopolitical disputes.”

The World Association of News Publishers said security laws must not be used to punish legitimate reporting, intimidate journalists or turn journalists into bargaining pawns in political disputes.

“The gathering of information by reporters is the entire purpose of responsible journalism and Imai is no different,” David Walmsley, president of the organisation’s World Editors Forum said. “It’s to the dismay of his colleagues across the industry that his responsibility has been perversely turned into accusations of wrongdoing. Imai should be immediately released.”

![Footage shows Russian officers arresting a Japanese journalist accused of espionage](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/14/06/2299481165..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Imai’s arrest came amid deteriorating relations between Moscow and Tokyo over Russia's invasion of Ukraine and a longstanding dispute over the Kuril Islands, which were seized by the Soviet Union at the end of Second World War but remain claimed by Japan.

It also came after a suspected member of the Russia-linked hacking group Qilin was detained in Japan and extradited to Germany to face charges in connection with a 2024 cyberattack on a logistics centre.

Japanese officials have protested Imai’s arrest, with chief cabinet secretary Minoru Kihara last week telling a press conference that the government was “strongly urging” Russia to immediately release the journalist.

He said that officials at the Japanese embassy in Moscow had their first consular visit with Imai on 26 October and they confirmed he was in good health.

Mr Kihara said Japan “will continue to seize every opportunity to strongly press Russia on the issue in accordance with our stance”.
