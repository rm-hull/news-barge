Andy Burnham’s hopes of winning the next election would be massively boosted if he pledges to take Britain back into the EU, new polling has revealed.

According to GB Omnibus, support for Labour jumps up by more than 7 per cent if they put rejoin in the manifesto.

It comes just 48 hours before Mr Burnham heads to Brussels to sign the latest Brexit reset deal as the government attempts to undo some of the harms of Brexit.

But while Mr Burnham will do a deal on joining the EU energy market, getting a youth mobility scheme and scrapping border checks on food and drink, he has made it clear he wants the summit on Friday to be a springboard for something much bigger.

![Prime Minister Andy Burnham will meet European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen on Friday for the Brexit reset summit (Stefan Rousseau/PA)](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/17/17/f690ad599165109537114109e4778e54Y29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzk1MDIwOTM2-2.86468843.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The PM is about to start a yearlong national conversation on options on Brexit including keeping the status quo, joining the customs union or single market or rejoining the block in its entirety as full members.

The conclusion will shape Labour’s manifesto pledge in the next election with Mr Burnham not hiding his dislike for Brexit and desire to rejoin.

The poll of 7,291 voters commissioned by Fortius Global (formerly the Messina Group) makes it clear that voters currently back the prime minister pushing for Rejoin.

According to the findings, Labour reached 31.5 per cent among the total electorate if they promise to rejoin compared with 24.3 per cent in the baseline voting-intention question.

This is a like-for-like comparison, including undecided voters and those unlikely to vote. In a separate scenario where Labour maintained its current approach to EU relations, its support was 25.9 per cent.

Other indicators also confirmed strong support for rejoining and a belief that Brexit has harmed the UK.

The poll found that 56.5 per cent support opening negotiations to rejoin, against 26.8 per cent opposed.

Meanwhile, 53 per cent say Brexit has been bad for immigration after the so-called “Boris wave” which saw a massive increase in the years after leaving the EU.

In another question, 43.3 per cent agreed that the immigration system was better when Britain was in the EU, against just 17.9 per cent who disagree.

The poll suggested that two thirds (65 per cent) of voters in a likely referendum favour rejoining against 35 per cent who would stay out.

Patience also appears to be wearing thin with 54 per cent wanting rejoin to happen immediately or within three years.

![Nigel Farage](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/11/14/2026-11-08T093703Z_1396979006_RC2JJOATWPYI_RTRMADP_3_BRITAIN-POLITICS.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Another question showed that 61 per cent believe the UK is on the “wrong track” outside the EU, backing up claims made by Mr Burnham in recent speeches.

Asked if Brexit has been a failure 55 per cent agree and only 13.8 per cent disagree.

However, Nigel Farage and Brexiteers will note that joining the Euro may be a bridge too far for UK voters with 85.5 per cent wanting to keep the pound.

The PM told his cabinet on Tuesday that, when it came to Europe, his government was showing leadership on big issues that “can build a more hopeful feeling across the country,” No 10 said.

But Downing Street also suggested Mr Burnham will not set out options for the UK’s future relationship with the European Union before the end of this year, and that he will discuss them with the bloc first.
