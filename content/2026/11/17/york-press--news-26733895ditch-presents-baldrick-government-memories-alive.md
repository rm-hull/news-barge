## Why I don’t want presents this Christmas

As regards fly-tipping: especially at this time of the year we are urged to spend on a lot of objects which we don’t need.

To my friends I have long requested that they don’t give me presents.

Much of what is bought for special occasions is produced at the cost of our natural resources and some by forced or low-paid work in other countries.

Then there is all the packaging to be disposed of, the air and road miles for transporting goods and the road miles for delivery.

Some of this is thrown out, and not always in the correct way.

Instead of flowers etc. I would rather have, for example, entry to the Art Gallery, a theatre ticket or a Hearing Dog adoption.

**Rose BerlDe Little Court,Vine Street, York**

## Baldrick’s planning another certain failure

We keep hearing of more cunning plans from Baldrick.

His latest is to create employment for the Neets as they are called.

This Government has created a job market where it is not feasible as an employer to take on these Neets.

The rules and regulations laid down by this Government have totally destroyed the job market.

With the rules as they are the employee has the upper hand in any employment dispute.

I know that people want to earn a decent wage, but companies cannot employ people that have no knowledge of the task required of them if the companies recruit them on a lower wage and give them wage rises when they are able to do the work required of them.

If there is a dispute between employer and employee and it goes to a tribunal you can almost guarantee that the outcome will go in favour of the employee.

These are just a few problems that face employers which make them very hesitant to take on new employees.

The Government sits in the Commons and pontificates as to what the employer must do yet very few of them have ever worked in industry, but for some unknown reason they know all the answers.

So come on Baldrick, try again with more of your failures and U-turns.

**T J Ryder,Acomb, YorkRecommended reads:**

## Why we must keep the memories of the fallen alive

D M Deamer makes a good point about remembrance “...fading into the pages of history..” in his letter of 13/11/26.

I volunteer for the Commonwealth War Graves Commission and one of the main aims of this organisation is to keep memories alive through the generations by making sure that all commemoration sites from both world wars, including individual graves, cemeteries and memorials, are visited and explored as a permanent part of local and national heritage.

This covers over 1.7 million British and Commonwealth service men and women who died in the two conflicts around the globe.

To this end the Commission provides educational opportunities for all ages from group talks on various aspects of the work to cemetery tours in order that people recognise the sacrifices made by previous generations.

These can be booked via the cwgc website (www.cwgc.org).

People can also record their own memories and those of others in a section of the website called “For Evermore: Stories of the Fallen”.

This has been designed to capture stories about the war dead whether a relative, loved one or someone you have researched and who is commemorated today at one of the CWGC cemeteries or memorials.
