Overall life satisfaction among people living in Germany declined this year compared to the year before, with economic concerns weighing heavily on morale, especially among women, young people and the unemployed.

This is the headline result of the SKL 'Happiness Atlas' (*Glücksatlas*) 2026, an annual report based on a series of 12 surveys which included a total of 12,461 people in Germany aged 16 or above.

Notably, self-reported life satisfaction scores for Germans and German residents fell overall.

Additionally the proportion of 'highly satisfied' individuals dropped from 48 to 44 percent, with a greater proportion of people instead stating that they felt 'moderately satisfied' with their lives.

**The numbers**

Each year the SKL survey presents a sort-of happiness score for people in Germany – this is based on the average score of thousands of participants who are asked how satisfied they are with their life on a scale of zero (not at all satisfied) to 10 (completely satisfied).

The 2026 reports that life satisfaction fell by 0.09 points to 7.00.

In 2024 and 2025, the average score had risen above 7.00. Excepting the covid pandemic years of 2020 to 2023, that score had stayed above seven since 2009.

According to the report, the proportion of "highly satisfied" people in Germany – who rate their lives 8, 9, or 10 – has fallen from 48 to 44 percent within a year.

Meanwhile, those who would describe themselves as "moderately satisfied" – with numerical scores from 5 to 7 – has grown from 44 percent to 47 percent.

The proportion of "dissatisfied" people, at the bottom end of the scale has risen only slightly from eight to nine percent.

Interestingly, satisfaction scores dipped more sharply within certain groups, for example; women, young adults between 18 and 39 years old, university students, the unemployed, large families and single parents.

Couples, on the other hand, were virtually unaffected compared to the previous year.

**Dissatisfactory incomes**

One aspect of life where satisfaction is markedly down was with income.

Satisfaction with people's own financial situation has fallen for the second time in a row, according to the Happiness Atlas report.

The youngest age group was hit hardest here, with those aged 18 to 24 losing 0.22 points in this category.

Overall, the gap in life satisfaction between rich and poor is widening in Germany.

The report doesn't get into recent policy updates, but beyond income, a series of reforms that were long-debated this year – such as reducing entitlements to sick leave or care benefits and pension tweaks including raising the retirement age to 67 – could be on the minds of many survey participants, potentially bringing down their reported satisfaction.

But the more surprising shift is a sudden dip in reported happiness from residents of the Germany's north, such as in Hamburg and Schleswig-Holstein.

Both areas had topped the regional happiness ranking for over a decade, but have now fallen to the middle of the pack.

Ranked by state, Bavaria was home to the most satisfied people in Germany, followed closely by North Rhine-Westphalia.

The states of Rhineland-Palatinate, Baden-Württemberg, Saxony-Anhalt and Hesse all scored higher than Germany's national average. Whereas satisfaction in Hamburg and Schleswig-Holstein was exactly matched with Germany's overall score of 7.00.

Satisfaction in Berlin was markedly low, according to the Happiness Atlas 2026. In the capital city residents' average satisfaction came to 6.79, just below that reported in Bremen and Saxony, and only beating Saarland and Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania.

**Keys to happiness**

Where you live can certainly have a big impact on your happiness, but so do many other factors. Alongside average scores and state or district rankings, the full Happiness Report 2026 includes sections unpacking other factors that play a large role in people's satisfaction.

Not all of these factors are within your own control. For example, your childhood can have a large impact on how satisfied you are throughout your adult life.

Among the things you can influence, however, would be making more time for leisure activities and fitness, investing time and effort toward friendships and relationships and making sure to take a nice vacation every now and then.
