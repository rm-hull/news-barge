## Europe wants to triple its data centre capacity, but residents and environmental campaigners are taking to the streets to demand a pause on new data centre projects. Why? And what’s at stake?

The European Union has set an ambition to triple its data centre capacity within five to seven years, as part of a push to reduce reliance on US Big Tech and develop Europe's own AI capabilities.

However, residents and environmental campaigners in Ireland, Poland, Denmark, Finland, Spain, the Netherlands, France and the United Kingdom took to the streets to call for a freeze on the development of these new projects.

In today's episode of Euronews Verify, **Evi Kiorri** gets the full picture with**Owen Espley**, coordinator of Stop Dirty Data Centres.eu, and** Michael Winterson**, secretary general at the European Data Centre Association.

"They're really concerned about the impact on land, on water, the huge energy use um that data centres demand, as well as the impact that that energy use could take away from housing," Owen Espley, coordinator of Stop Dirty Data Centres.eu, told Euronews Verify.

## Europe wants to be the world's first climate-neutral continent — but will it happen?

COP31 is also in focus in today's episode.

The EU has promised to be the world's first climate-neutral continent by 2050.

EU ministers insist they are committed to the green transition despite soaring energy prices and supply concerns.

However, voters want cheaper energy now, while many of the measures designed to deliver it will take much longer to pay off.

76% of people in the Netherlands, 72% in France and 68% in Germany believe their country is unlikely to make meaningful progress towards net zero. In the Netherlands, 54% say environmental policies have left ordinary people worse off.

Euronews Verify's **Inês Pereira** breaks down the challenges of this awkward political balance with**Sven Haremling**, head of climate at CAN Europe.

"People also appreciate it if they're told the truth and what needs to change, as long as there's someone who works with them for solutions and there are changes in government regulations," Haremling said.
