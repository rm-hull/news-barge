Residents of Kurfürstenstraße have called on Interior Senator Iris Spranger (SPD) to reassess whether the Kurfürstenkiez neighbourhood should be designated a high-crime area and to present concrete measures and a timetable for improving the situation. They have also invited Spranger to visit the area in person. This is according to a letter to the Interior Senator obtained by the Berliner Morgenpost, in which the authors describe the situation as “intolerable”.

“In our view, the area around Kurfürstenstraße has effectively developed into a lawless zone,” the letter states. The authors describe drug-related crime, including offences committed to finance drug use, as well as public drug use and risks to children and young people arising from street prostitution and pimping.

### Kurfürstenstraße Residents: No Discernible Lasting Improvement

The document is signed by the management of Strohauer Wohnungsunternehmen GbR, a property company based on Kurfürstenstraße. For many years, the company leased premises to two local social organisations, the Neustart association and the Olga women’s centre. Their leases were recently not renewed. However, new premises have been found for Neustart.

According to information obtained by the Berliner Morgenpost, other residents of Kurfürstenstraße also contributed to the letter and support its demands. However, they do not want their names made public because they fear for their safety.

The authors describe confrontations in building entrances, stairwells, courtyards and basements with drug users, dealers, sex workers, their clients and homeless people. Residents are reportedly insulted and threatened.

“Left behind are filth, used syringes, condoms and faeces – and with them, pathogens in the very places where people live,” the letter states.

At the same time, the authors explicitly highlight the “constructive” cooperation with the security authorities. They describe regular communication with the police and the State Criminal Police Office (LKA), as well as the commitment of the officers involved.

“It is therefore all the more disheartening that, despite these efforts, there has so far been no discernible, lasting improvement in conditions through political action,” the letter continues.

“The Senate Department for the Interior and Sport has received the residents’ letter and is reviewing it,” a spokesperson for the department said.

### Police Recorded a Total of 5,220 Crimes Around Kurfürstenstraße in 2025

The residents’ appeal for help comes against the backdrop of a high number of criminal offences, comparable to figures recorded in officially designated high-crime areas.

In 2025, police recorded a total of 5,220 offences in the area around Kurfürstenstraße. At Alexanderplatz, which is classified as a high-crime area, the figure was 5,329, while 4,046 offences were recorded around Görlitzer Park and the Wrangelkiez neighbourhood.

In response to a parliamentary enquiry from CDU politician Katharina Senge, the Berlin Senate currently considers the designation of additional high-crime areas, including Kurfürstenkiez, as “not necessary at present”.

Such a designation allows police to stop and check people without specific grounds for suspicion.

Asked for comment, a Berlin Police spokesperson said: “Overall, a downward trend in street crime can be observed; there is no indication of a sustained deterioration in the general security situation.”

Although there had been isolated fluctuations, including in March 2026, these did not indicate a lasting trend, the spokesperson added.
