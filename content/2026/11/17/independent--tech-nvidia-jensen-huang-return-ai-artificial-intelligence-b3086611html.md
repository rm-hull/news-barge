Former Nvidia boss Jensen Huang has said he would be “honoured” to return to the company after he was ousted in the wake of a scandal.

Mr Huang resigned from the chipmaker last week, amid a wave of scandal after he admitted to an affair with a woman accused of being a Chinese spy as well as a subordinate at Nvidia. Since then, petitions and supporters have called for him to return – but the company has said that it is not interested in inviting him back.

Nonetheless, Mr Huang signalled he would be willing to return and that he would work with an investigation into the incident. But he suggested that the decision was up to the board and whether it thought his return would be “in the best interest of our shareholders, our employees, the company, and America”.

“Since my departure, I’ve seen an outpouring of calls from Nvidia’s employees, investors, business partners, and customers for me to return as CEO,” Huang said in his first public statement since he resigned on 12 November over revelations of extramarital affairs that intensified scrutiny of the chip firm's China business. Mr Huang had not been quoted in Nvidia’s original statement about his departure, though it thanked him for his “profound” contributions to the company, and he has been silent since.

“I created problems for the Board that I deeply regret. I own my mistakes completely, and I am committed to cooperating with the Board’s independent investigation,” he said in the new statement.

Nvidia did not immediately respond to a request for comment on Mr Huang's statement, but multiple reports have suggested that its board is not planning to reappoint him. Nvidia shares ⁠pared some declines and were last down 1 per cent.

More than 180,000 people have signed a petition demanding Huang’s return, Elon Musk has thrown his support behind the effort, ⁠and US President Donald Trump said he would not mind seeing Huang reinstated as CEO. Thousands of people identifying themselves as Nvidia employees have joined the call, even as governance experts and some investors warn that bringing him back before investigations are complete could expose the company to fresh risks.

The groundswell of support for Huang has turned a corporate succession story into a test of whether Nvidia can separate itself from a founder whose influence stretches across its employees — some fiercely loyal to the former CEO — and shareholders, Silicon Valley, the Taiwanese supply chain and Washington.

Taiwan's tallest skyscraper, Taipei 101, was illuminated on Monday with messages including "Taiwan Loves Jensen." Some prominent shareholders such as Atreides ⁠Management's Gavin Baker and Altimeter Capital Management CEO Brad Gerstner have urged Nvidia not to rush to replace him ​before ⁠all the facts are known.

Earlier on Tuesday, before Mr Huang’s statement, Nvidia created a board-level ​committee ⁠to oversee national security and export controls, its first major governance move since he left.

Mr Huang's relationships have raised questions about whether Nvidia's oversight kept pace with its rapid growth and increasing importance to US national security. The crisis had erased more ⁠than $400 billion from the company's market value by Monday's close.

The National Security and Export Control Oversight Committee will be chaired by director Suzanne Nora Johnson, ​a former Goldman Sachs ⁠vice chair, Nvidia said. Directors Stephen Neal, former CEO of ‌law firm Cooley, and John Dabiri, a Caltech professor, will also serve on the committee.

Donald Trump welcomed the move, calling the launch of a national security initiative a good idea.

In the full statement, posted on X, Mr Huang thanked the public for its support and admitted to having made “serious mistakes”. He also included a message to his family, in which he suggested they were offering him “support”.

“Over the past week, I’ve been overwhelmed by the love and support of friends, industry colleagues, and people I've never met. Thank you to everyone who has shown kindness to my family and me. It means the world to me,” he wrote.

“I came to the United States 53 years ago, and becoming an American citizen is one of the greatest gifts and proudest moments of my life. Nvidia has been my life’s work. Building it into the company it is today alongside my colleagues has been a privilege and an honor. Nowhere but in America could a company like Nvidia be possible. I love our country, and I love Nvidia.

“I did not live up to the standards expected of everyone at Nvidia, and I have made serious mistakes in my personal life. I created problems for the Board that I deeply regret. I own my mistakes completely, and I am committed to cooperating with the Board’s independent investigation.

“Since my departure, I’ve seen an outpouring of calls from Nvidia’s employees, investors, business partners, and customers for me to return as CEO. Many continue to reach out to me directly as the company navigates complex technology, market, competitive, and geopolitical dynamics. NVIDIA is a once-in-a-generation company. If the Board of Directors decides that my return is in the best interest of our shareholders, our employees, the company, and America, I would be honoured to answer these calls and return as CEO.

“Thank you again to everyone who has shared their good wishes. I am profoundly humbled by your support. To my family: I love you, and I will spend the rest of my life being worthy of yours.”
