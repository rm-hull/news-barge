## Kuala Lumpur is making an exception to its ban on exporting raw minerals for Paris, as the two countries raise relations to a strategic partnership.

France and Malaysia have agreed to deepen cooperation on rare earth minerals and defence, with a joint mining and processing venture due to start operating in early 2027.

French President Emmanuel Macron announced the timeline on Tuesday during a two-day visit to Kuala Lumpur, Malaysia. He and Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim also agreed to raise bilateral relations to a strategic partnership.

The venture is between French company Carester and Malaysian firm Malaco Mining. In July, the two announced plans to build a rare earths separation plant in Perak state, in northern Malaysia, under a 10-year joint venture. The project is expected to add 13,000 tonnes of processed minerals, including those needed for high-performance magnets.

"Operations are due to begin in early 2027," Macron told a joint news conference. "This will allow us to proceed ... involving not only extraction, but also processing here, with reserved volumes and capacity, including the possibility of refining in France, which is crucial for our entire value chain."

Malaysia already has the largest rare earths processing plant outside China, which dominates global supply of the critical minerals used in smartphones, fighter jets, electric cars and wind turbines. That has made Malaysia a sought-after partner for countries trying to diversify their supply chains.

Anwar said Malaysia had made an exception to its policy against exporting raw rare earths in return for French help in developing its own industry, calling the arrangement "unique". He did not give further details.

"We do not export raw materials, but with France we have done so, because you are committed to that processing and working with the Malaysians as a team," Anwar said.

He added that the deal showed how resource-rich countries could work with foreign partners while securing technology transfer, training and industrial expertise.

Macron said the partnership reflected a shared commitment to national sovereignty and greater independence in a world increasingly shaped by competing major powers.

## Helicopters, chips and a "new milestone"

The two governments also signed agreements on defence, energy supply and digital cooperation. On defence, they agreed a new industrial road map and French support for a regional centre to counter hybrid threats, and Malaysia will buy Airbus-built combat search-and-rescue helicopters for its armed forces.

On semiconductors, Malaysian company SkyeChip and French firm SiPearl will work together on a high-performance computing platform.

"s we approach the 70th anniversary of our diplomatic relations, our two countries are reaching a new milestone with this visit," Macron said.

Trade between France and Malaysia was worth more than €4.9 billion last year. Macron said more than 300 French companies operate in Malaysia, making France its second-largest European investor.

His trip is only the third by a French president since the two countries established diplomatic relations in 1957. He arrived in Malaysia late on Monday from Cambodia, where he had attended a summit of French-speaking nations.
