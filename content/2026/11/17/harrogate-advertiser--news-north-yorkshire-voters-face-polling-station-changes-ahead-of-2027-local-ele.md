Voters across North Yorkshire could be asked to cast their ballots at different polling stations next year under changes proposed ahead of the 2027 council elections.

North Yorkshire Council is preparing to approve changes to polling districts and polling places following a review of electoral boundaries and the availability of voting venues.

The proposals affect communities across the county, including areas around Thirsk, Ripon, Scarborough and Whitby.

A report to the council’s standards and governance committee, which meets on Tuesday next week, says the changes are needed to ensure suitable polling arrangements are in place for next May’s local elections.

The review follows changes to electoral boundaries and parish arrangements, as well as the loss of some previously designated polling venues.

Among the proposed changes, voters in Asenby, Catton and Skipton-on-Swale would be directed to Topcliffe and Asenby Village Hall.

The current polling places for these communities include Rainton Village Hall and Sandhutton Village Hall.

In Burneston and Theakston, the polling place would move from the Woodman Inn in Burneston to St Lambert’s Church.

Voters in Broughton and Swinton, near Malton, would see their polling place moved from the Smithy Arms to Broughton, Swinton and Amotherby Sports Centre.

Other changes include moving the polling place for Pockley from Helmsley Methodist Church School Rooms to Pockley Village Hall.

In Scarborough, voters in parts of Castle ward would use Scarborough Railway Club, while South Cliff Golf Club would replace St Michael and All Angels Church Hall as a polling place in the Weaponness and Ramshill area.

The review has also prompted a change of direction in Whitby following objections from residents.

The council originally proposed merging two polling districts and requiring voters to use Ruswarp Village Hall.

However, the proposal attracted the largest number of objections during the consultation, with residents raising concerns about the additional distance they would have to travel and limited parking.

Officers have now recommended keeping the districts separate and using the academy building at Whitby School’s Airy Hill site as an alternative polling place for one of them.

A total of 102 responses were received during the consultation, which ran from August 3 to October 12.

According to the report, most respondents were broadly supportive of the original proposals, although several suggested alternative venues which were subsequently considered by council officers.

The changes also follow a review of electoral arrangements which has resulted in new ward boundaries for 14 parishes, including Richmond, Northallerton, Ripon, Harrogate and Knaresborough.

These boundary changes are due to take effect on April 1, 2027.

North Yorkshire Council currently has 854 polling districts and 560 designated polling places.

Officers say the proposed changes will be broadly cost-neutral because the overall number of polling places will remain almost unchanged.

Councillors are being asked to approve the final proposals before a revised electoral register is published on December 1.
