Sir David Evennett, who spent more than 30 years as a Conservative MP in south-east London, has passed away following a short illness.

First elected as MP for Erith and Crayford in 1983, he lost the seat in 1997, but returned in 2005 as MP for Bexleyheath and Crayford, serving continuously in Parliament until his decision not to contest the 2024 general election.

He acted as a Tory whip in government and opposition, while also serving as vice chairman of the party.

Sir David is survived by Marilyn, his wife, and two sons.

Conservative Party chairman Kevin Hollinrake said: “Sir David was a diligent local Member of Parliament and a true champion of Conservatism.

“Even once he left the House of Commons, he was still a regular attendee at Kemi’s (Kemi Badenoch) speeches and an assiduous local campaigner. May he rest in peace.”

He started his career as a teacher, and then became a councillor for Redbridge borough in London.

He also worked as a marine insurance broker at Lloyds and a lecturer in management for Bexley College.
