Britons are being urged to put together an emergency kit of essential supplies to prepare for extreme weather events as the government warns of an increasing risk of disruption.

The Red Cross has published its own list of items “every household” should consider stocking following the government’s Be Ready campaign, which urges people to keep enough supplies to last three days in the case of emergency.

It comes aftter the Met Office issued a yellow weather warning for snow covering parts of the UK. The forecaster has warned those living in parts of northern England and Scotland to expect disruption and plan journeys ahead of time as the snow moves northwards and melts later into Tuesday.

Met Office Chief Forecaster Dan Suri said: “A band of rain will move northwards across the UK during Monday night and Tuesday morning and, as it encounters colder air, it will turn to snow for a time.

![A yellow weather warning for snow is in place on Tuesday morning](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/17/8/21/Screenshot-2026-11-17-at-08-33-20.png?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“The greatest chance of settling snow will be over ground above 200 metres, with 1-5 cm possible in places and potentially 5-15 cm above 300-400 metres. Combined with strong easterly winds, this could bring some travel disruption to higher-level routes.

He said the snow will increasingly turn back to rain throughout Tuesday, but warned it will continue to feel cold in the north. The UK Health Security Agency has issued a yellow cold-health alert for the North West, North East and Yorkshire and the Humber, which is in place until Thursday morning.

![Britons have been urged to put together an emergency supply kit](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/27/12/35/iStock-2209167716-0b245168.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Here is everything the Red Cross says you should prepare in an emergency kit:

* Paper copy of important contact numbers, to communicate with others even without mobile devices
* Mobile phone charger and portable power bank, so you can keep in communication
* Cash for essentials such as food or fuel, in case of a cyber attack or power cut stopping card payments
* Torch and spare batteries, in case of a power cut
* Battery-powered or wind-up radio, so you can access information during a power cut
* Essential medication and copies of prescriptions if needed
* Basic first aid kit
* Hand sanitiser and wet wipes, to maintain basic hygiene
* Three days' supply of drinking water, in case a power cut, drought or flood disrupts the water supply
* Three days' supply of long-life or ready-to-eat food
* Tin opener
* Copies of important documents stored in a waterproof bag, in case of electrical devices not being available or originals destroyed in a flood or fire
* Pen and paper, in case anything needs to be recorded
* Spare home and car keys, in case these are lost in the emergency

The charity also says the following items are not essential, but useful to have in an emergency:

* Blankets or sleeping bags
* Warm spare clothing
* Toiletries and hygiene products
* Waterproof bags
* Whistle
* Spare glasses or contact lenses
* Duct tape

It comes after defence secretary Wes Streeting called for a "whole society approach" to strengthen national resilience, though he insisted people should remain "alert not alarmed" regarding potential risks.

“Having a few everyday essentials at home, from tinned food to torches, helps households prepare for disruption, whether that's flooding, power cuts or burst pipes,” he said. “We can all play our part in making Britain more resilient."
