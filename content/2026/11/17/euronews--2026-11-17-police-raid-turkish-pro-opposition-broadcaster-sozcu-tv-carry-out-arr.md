## It was the latest move against pro-opposition media, coming a month after police raided Turkey's most-watched news channel, Halk TV, where they shut down broadcasts and installed an administrator.

Turkish police raided the Istanbul headquarters of the pro-opposition Sözcü TV and its media group early on Tuesday, conducting arrests and installing a government-appointed administrator, the group said on X.

It was the latest move against pro-opposition media, coming a month after police raided Turkey's most-watched news channel, Halk TV, where they shut down broadcasts and installed an administrator.

"Police are searching the Sözcü building under a court order. Police have cordoned off the building," its official X account said in a post just before 5:00 am local time (0300 CET).

"A trustee has been appointed to Sözcü! Police are searching the building...There have been detentions," it said, with one of its journalists saying staff were not being allowed inside.

"Outside Sözcü TV as of 06:00. We are not being allowed into the newsroom," Sezin Ceren Övüç wrote on X.

Among those detained were Funda Tuncer Sıdalı, who chairs the board, and Sözcü TV's former general manager Asım Akgül, Turkish media reports said.

According to its news website, police had raided both Sözcü TV and the offices of its group's online operations, appointing an administrator for both.

"In the early hours of the morning, the police carried out a raid on SÖZCÜ TV and Sözcü.com.tr. A search was launched in the SÖZCÜ TV building by counterterrorism teams. While some executives were detained, trustees were appointed to SÖZCÜ TV and Sözcü.com.tr," its website said.

"No trustees were appointed to SÖZCÜ newspaper, Tavır or Korkusuz," it said, referring to two other newspapers owned by the group.

Ersan Barkın, lawyer for the Sözcü Media Group, said the raids were based on a case that had previously been thrown out by the Supreme Court after finding no evidence that a crime had been committed.

"We're faced with a trustee decision arising from an investigation based on a case in which the Supreme Court, the highest judicial authority in Turkey, found no intent and no crime," he told KRT TV, referring to a case dating back to 2016.

"This trustee decision stems from a file in which no offence was established," he explained.

## **'Blatant coup against democracy'**

Sözcü, which is close to Turkey's opposition, has faced repeated sanctions from Turkey's broadcasting regulator as President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan's government piles pressure on its opponents.

In July last year, it went dark for 10 days after being slapped with a ban by Turkey's RTUK broadcasting regulator over its coverage of the mass protests sparked by the jailing of Istanbul's powerful opposition mayor, Ekrem İmamoğlu on graft charges widely denounced as political.

The pre-dawn raids drew swift condemnation from Turkey's main opposition leader Özgür Özel, who heads the Yeni Parti.

"After Halk TV, now there are police and a government-appointed trustee at the doors of Sözcü TV as well! They no longer have any tolerance even for the slightest criticism," he wrote on X, referring to Erdoğan's government.

"They’re cornered; they’re mired in the swamp. That’s why they’re trying to choke out anything and anyone who isn’t one of them."

It also drew a sharp rebuke from the head of the nationalist opposition İYİ Party.

"The raid on SÖZCÜ TV and the attempt to effectively shut it down are a blatant coup against democracy, the rule of law, the sense of justice and the public’s freedom to access information, whatever justification is put forward," wrote Müsavat Dervişoğlu on X.
