Ikea has said it “abandoned” some UK investment plans due to the business environment and higher costs in the country.

The furniture and homeware group has still been putting money into Britain, according to UK boss David McCabe, who told the Press Association that recent budgets had pushed its costs up.

He said: “Companies like Ikea are looking at incredibly high costs and an increasingly complex operating environment.

“We believe the UK is definitely a market for investment and have recently invested in eight new units in the UK.

“But there are higher costs and complexities here, and it has meant that we have had investment projects in the UK that we have had to abandon, and that is a shame.”

The company also said it came under pressure from a “challenging” environment and “weakened” consumer confidence over the past year.

Ikea said that “structural cost pressures” in the region would weigh on investment opportunities and competitiveness in the wide retail sector.

Mr McCabe added: “There’s no doubt it continues to be a challenging period for people and a challenging retail environment.

“But we are optimistic – we are seeing people coming into our stores and enthusiastic for the festive period.”

The boss stressed that Andy Burnham’s Government “missed an opportunity to back retailers” at the latest autumn Budget last month.

The comments came as Ikea reported that UK retail edged 0.5 per cent higher to £2.26 billion for the year to August 2026.

Revenues had risen 4.7 per cent over the first four months of the financial year, but sales dipped in the second half of the year.

![Decathlon opened a new store within Ikea in Croydon this year](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/17/09/2026-11-17T081756Z_979139136_RC2LVKA5YDEG_RTRMADP_3_IKEA-RESULTS.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The company highlighted that “sustained pressure on household budgets” led to weak sentiment among consumers, knocking demand for big ticket items and more expensive product lines.

In August, the company invested £54 million to help reduce prices, focusing on affordability to help support sales in the face of the tough consumer backdrop.

Its continued expansion across the UK has also benefited the business, with sites opening in London Oxford Street, Brighton, Norwich and York last year, followed by a small-format store in Chester.

A plan and order site opened in Dundee, as well as one in Aberdeen last month.
