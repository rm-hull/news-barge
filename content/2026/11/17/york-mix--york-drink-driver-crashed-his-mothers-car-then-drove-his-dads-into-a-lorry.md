A York drink-driver took his mother’s car and wrote it off in a collision – then went back for his father’s vehicle and wrote that off too after crashing into a lorry.

Samuel Dale’s trail of destruction caused irreparable damage to both vehicles and also damaged the HGV, but he appeared to have escaped relatively unscathed, York magistrates heard.

The crashes, which occurred on the same day, came three months after 22-year-old Dale threatened to petrol bomb his father’s car and slit his throat, said prosecutor Alexander Steadward.

He said that Dale first drove off in his mother’s Nissan Juke on 19 June which was parked in Knapton.

He crashed the £8,200 SUV, causing so much damage it had to be written off by the insurers, said Mr Steadward.

It was found some time later “not in a driveable state” on the A1237 in York. The vehicle’s bumper was badly damaged and one of its tyres had “popped”.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/police-drink-drive-breathliser-ny.webp)

Dale then returned to his parents’ home in Barkston Avenue, Chapelfields, and took his father’s Hyundai Santa Fe – which he crashed into a HGV.

“That resulted in his father’s car being written off and there was about £600 damage to the HGV,” said Mr Steadward.

Dale was over the legal alcohol limit and was given a 46-month driving ban and fined £200 for that offence at a previous hearing.

## Shouted threats

Three months earlier, on 21 March, Dale – who had been living in a homeless centre in York after being turfed out of the parental home due to his alcohol abuse – turned up at their address in Knapton and started banging on their door and shouting verbal abuse from outside the house.

He shouted to his father that he would “come back to the house and petrol-bomb the car”.

“He said he would slit his father’s throat and stab him,” added Mr Steadward.

Police were called to the property where Dale resisted arrest. He was charged with two counts of aggravated vehicle taking, threatening behaviour towards his father and resisting a police officer.

He admitted the offences and appeared for sentence at York Magistrates’ Court yesterday (Monday).

Mr Steadward said that Dale, who lives in a homeless shelter in Fishergate, had a previous conviction for aggravated vehicle-taking from February last year when he received a community order.

## ‘He requires help’

Dale’s solicitor James Dixon said that the York man, who was “intoxicated” in both sets of offences, had relapsed into alcohol abuse at the time which had resulted in him being kicked out of his parents’ home and sleeping rough.

He had been living at the homeless shelter for the past eight months.

Dale did have a job but lost this due to his alcohol problem and “things have spiralled since then”.

“He is somebody who requires help,” added Mr Dixon.

Magistrates said the offences were so serious they merited a prison sentence, but that it could be suspended due to Dale’s age, his guilty pleas and the fact that the Probation Service averred that he could be rehabilitated.

The six-month jail sentence was suspended for a year. During that period, Dale will be supervised by the Probation Service and will have to undergo a six-month alcohol-treatment programme which will be electronically monitored.

He was ordered to pay his parents £150 each for the excess they had to pay for the damage to their vehicles.
