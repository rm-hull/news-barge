Donald Trump has claimed the US is “looking into” whether Iran is developing plans to detonate a so-called “dirty bomb” in Washington, even as the White House said there were “no credible threats” at this time.

Trump said his administration takes the idea “very seriously”

After a CNN report, citing seven unnamed US officials, that new US intelligence suggested Iran was working on plans that would use a dirty bomb, which combines conventional explosives with radioactive material, or a chemical weapon against senior Trump administration officials.

The report noted, however, that US officials “continue to debate whether Iran can or will attempt to execute such a plot”.

The US president’s war on Iran is deeply unpopular among Americans. His Republican party was widely rejected by voters in the midterm elections, in which the GOP lost control of the Senate and House of Representatives.

Asked in the Philippines on Tuesday if there was reason to believe that such an Iranian attack was imminent, Trump said: “If there is, they will pay a price the likes of which no country has ever paid before. I hope it’s not, but we take it very seriously because they happen to ​be crazy.”

Lauren Bis, a White House spokesperson, said in a statement: “We remain vigilant monitoring any and all potential threats, but at this time there are no credible threats to the homeland.”

CNN cited the unnamed officials as saying that Tehran’s planning was an effort by Iran to “give itself options rather than an indicator of an imminent attack”, but the plans include specific locations and targets.

While the plans would signify that Iran is willing to escalate its tactics against the US, there is no evidence to suggest they will go ahead with them.

The US and Israel went to war with Iran starting in February, with early airstrikes killing Iranian officials, including the supreme leader, Ali Khamenei. Trump has repeatedly claimed the war will be over soon, but it has continued.

The US president has argued that US involvement in the war is necessary so that the country does not build nuclear weapons. Iran has blocked trade through the strait of Hormuz, creating rising fuel costs – and rattling the global economy.
