## 'It's never too late': 85-year-old becomes York St John's oldest graduate

Robin Gallagher has made York St John University by becoming the universities oldest-ever graduate <i>(Image: PA Media)

AN 85-year-old student is set to make history this week when he becomes the a York university's oldest-ever graduate.

Robin Gallagher will receive a Doctor of Philosophy degree during the university's graduation ceremonies at York Minster, capping a remarkable academic journey that began long after retirement.

His achievement comes as more than 3,200 students graduate over five days of ceremonies, representing the largest graduating cohort in York St John University's history.




Robin left school at 16 to train as a chartered accountant and went on to enjoy a successful career in finance.

But in his seventies he embarked on a new challenge, enrolling on a Creative Writing course at Kirklees College aged 73.

He progressed to a master's degree at York St John University in 2017 before beginning a part-time PhD at the age of 79.

![More than 3,200 students graduate over five days of ceremonies, representing the largest graduating cohort in York St John University's history](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21729433.jpg?type=mds-article-620)More than 3,200 students graduate over five days of ceremonies, representing the largest graduating cohort in York St John University's history *(Image: York St John University)*

His doctoral research explored how the inheritance of family wealth and property can shape family relationships, drawing on his professional experience, personal interests and childhood memories of North Wales.

The work later inspired a Gothic family drama set in the Conwy Valley.

Reflecting on his achievement, Robin said: "My journey has shown me that it is never too late to pursue an ambition or take on a new challenge.

"I would encourage anyone considering further study to have confidence in themselves, make connections with other students and make the most of the support and guidance available. Above all, embrace lifelong learning and never stop believing in what you can achieve."

Professor Karen Bryan OBE, Vice Chancellor and Chief Executive of York St John University, said: "Graduation is a wonderful opportunity to celebrate our students’ achievements, recognise their hard work and reflect on the friendships and experiences that have shaped their time at York St John University.

"As they embark on their next chapter, I hope they take with them the knowledge, skills and confidence to embrace the exciting opportunities ahead, knowing they will always be valued members of our university community."

![The graduation ceremonies will take place at York Minster from November 16 to November 20 ](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21729434.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The graduation ceremonies will take place at York Minster from November 16 to November 20 *(Image: York St John University)*

Another graduate set to receive special recognition is assistance dog McGee, who will be presented with a special "Dogtorate" alongside owner Jan Hanson as she graduates with a Doctorate in Education.

The black lurcher has accompanied Jan throughout her studies, attending supervision meetings, conferences and writing retreats, while helping her manage the challenges posed by a hearing impairment.

Jan, who has dyslexia, dyspraxia, autism and ADHD, has spent more than nine years supporting neurodivergent students and employees, with her research exploring how universities and employers can better support neurodivergent graduates as they transition into employment.

She said: “McGee is my colleague and my friend, and his practical and emotional support has been invaluable throughout my doctoral journey.

"Alongside the support of my supervisors and York St John’s inclusive approach, his unwavering companionship has helped make this achievement possible.

"I’m delighted that the University has recognised his contribution by creating a special Dogtor of Education certificate for him.

"It’s a wonderful gesture that celebrates the important role assistance dogs play in creating inclusive higher education environments.”

Graduation ceremonies are taking place at York Minster from November 16 to November 20 and are being presided over by university chancellor and BBC presenter Reeta Chakrabarti.
