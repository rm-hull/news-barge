Federal Reserve governor Lisa Cook has refused, through her attorneys, to testify next month before a Trump administration panel evaluating her dismissal, calling the process unfair and predetermined.

This refusal prolongs the conflict between the White House and Cook's lawyers over her attendance before the committee, which officials originally demanded for Nov. 6.

Administration officials insist she address allegations of mortgage fraud in 2021 before joining the Fed's board, charges she denies.

President Donald Trump has tried to fire Cook since August 2025, with Cook arguing the president's actions threaten the central bank's independence and reflect his desire to force lower borrowing costs.

"Governor Cook has no legal obligation to participate in an unfair hearing — and indeed, has a duty to the Federal Reserve to decline to participate in any proceeding that would undermine the institution’s unique status and independence," Abbe David Lowell, Cook's lawyer, wrote in a letter released Tuesday to White House counsel William Scharf.

The three-person panel set up by the White House to consider the allegations "appears to be a façade designed to obscure a predetermined decision to fire Governor Cook."

![](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/17/15/2262841912..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

In June, the Supreme Court stopped Trump from ousting Cook as litigation regarding the dismissal continues, ruling that "the fact of independence but also the appearance of independence is key to the Federal Reserve’s design."

Trump officials have threatened to hold the hearing without Cook, stating in a Nov. 3 letter that the three-member committee "reserves the right to draw adverse inferences against you" for not appearing.

The unusual "committee of inquiry" formed by the White House features Kevin Hassett, Trump’s chief economic adviser; Andrea Lucas, appointed to lead the Equal Employment Opportunity Commission; and Keith Sonderling, Trump’s nominee for Labor Secretary.

The Supreme Court noted in a June ruling footnote that Trump could try again to dismiss her if provided adequate notice and an opportunity to respond. The committee represents an attempt to meet those terms.

Under law governing the Fed, the president can only fire a governor "for cause," though the Supreme Court did not decide if Cook’s actions met that benchmark. The committee will recommend whether cause exists to dismiss her, the White House said.

When asked at an Oct. 28 press conference if he or the Fed would join the proceeding, Fed Chairman Kevin Warsh said, "not to my knowledge."
