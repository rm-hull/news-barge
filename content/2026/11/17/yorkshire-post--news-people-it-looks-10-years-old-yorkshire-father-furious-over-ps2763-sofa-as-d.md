# ‘It looks 10 years old’: Yorkshire father furious over £2,763 sofa as dispute with ScS drags on

## A house-proud father has waged war with a national home furnishing firm - after he splashed out nearly £3,000 on a brand new settee set which looked "10 years old" upon delivery.

Over the last 11 months, Stephen Partridge, 58, and his wife Cheryl, 52, have been in battle with company ScS after they sent one settee from the set he purchased in the "wrong colour and damaged".

The couple spent £2,763 on a dark grey three-seat and two-seat settee set with built-in power recliner systems and a matching footstool, part of the Bagnolo collection, from a branch in Parkgate, in Rotherham in December 2025.

After they replaced the furniture, Stephen, from Mexborough, in Doncaster, South Yorkshire., says the new pieces came "damaged" and was later told he can't return it.

The father spent over £200 on an independent investigator to check out its quality which allegedly found several faults - but ScS dispute the report and has said they will not replace the settee.

Stephen, who is a full-time carer for his wife, said: "Cheryl suffers from multiple sclerosis, and the old settee we had just wasn't cutting it for her. It wasn't comfortable anymore.

"The whole ordeal was supposed to allow Cheryl and me to live comfortably in our home.

"But what was supposed to be a positive experience has turned into a nightmare.

"I'm bothered becuase I spent almost £3000, which is a big investment, and it needs to be right for my wife."

The couple bought the settee set on December 30, and it was delivered in April 2026.

![Stephen Partridge, who is locked in a dispute with SCS sofas after finding a number of issues with his brand-new suite delivery](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/jpim-static/image/2026/11/16/17/41/dad-wages-war-w-1633082.jpeg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


They returned to SCS to replace their 20-year-old leather sofa they previously bought from the company.

Stephen said: "The staff were great with us. We were sat on the settee for 45 minutes while we signed all of the forms when purchasing it.

"It offered everything Cheryl needed. It had comfort, good neck and back support. It was stylish and we thought it fit our living room nicely."

But a couple of days after receiving it, Stephen said he noticed the two-seater was a lighter grey colour.

He also says two large indentations on the armrest of the three-seater settee were present upon delivery.

Their daughter Stephanie, 30, visited and also noticed the colours didn't match.

Stephen said: "The first thing she said was 'you do know these colours aren't the same'."

The father then complained to ScS, who sent out an investigator from independent furniture contractor, Emmiera Group, who agreed with the issues the father raised.

But after returning the wrong colour settee, the couple claim ScS replaced it with a "withered" set which looks a "decade old" in August.

They also said it was missing padding in both the back right seat cushion and headrest.

Stephen said ScS sent out another contractor from Emmiera Group who contested his claims.

So in September he commissioned a £264 independent report which found multiple faults with the sofa.

The report read: “"The right-hand facing headrest lacks sufficient filling material, resulting in a visibly uneven profile and pronounced fabric creasing across its surface.

"On the right-hand facing seat cushion, a distinct dark discolouration or large black mark is present within the fabric weave; this is not a surface-only stain but appears embedded within the material.

"A clear linear indentation, measuring 10.5 cm in length, is also visible on the seating surface of the right-hand facing

seat.

"Additionally, this same seat cushion displays excess material, leading to bunching, wrinkling, and an uneven finish."

It is also found in both reclining elements the material has not been "cut, sized, or tensioned appropriately during manufacture to accommodate movement of the mechanism".

Stating this as a "shortcoming that is inherent to the product’s construction and not the result of use, wear, or mishandling".

The report concluded that the “repair is neither proportionate nor reliable; replacement or full refund represents the only satisfactory and permanent remedy.”

It added: “Upon professional assessment, it is my considered opinion that the sofa suite is not reasonably repairable cost-effectively or satisfactorily.”

Stephen said: "I just felt like we weren't being treated like customers, but instead being treated unfairly for complaining.

"I can tell you for a fact any of the people I have spoken to over email or on the phone wouldn't accept what they have given me.

"I just don't understand why they are being so difficult in replacing it."

ScS is owned by the Italian upholstered furniture retailer Poltronesofà SpA.

A Poltronesofa spokesperson said the company is "sorry" that Stephen and Cheryl are " unhappy" with their sofa.

They added: "Since receiving their complaint, we have been in frequent correspondence with the customer.

"We appointed an independent third party to investigate and the technician they sent to inspect the product found no manufacturing faults or defects.

"As with all aspects of any soft upholstered furniture regular light dressing and plumping will alleviate the complaints raised, which the technician demonstrated on their visit.

"While we appreciate that the customer may have hoped for a different outcome, our assessment remains that the issues raised are consistent with the expected behaviour of soft upholstery, rather than a manufacturing fault.

“We understand that the customer may wish to seek a further independent review and so we have suggested they refer the complaint to the Furniture and Home Improvement Ombudsman if they remain unhappy with our assessment and response.

"We follow the Ombudsman’s Code of Practice, and its decisions are binding on us.”
