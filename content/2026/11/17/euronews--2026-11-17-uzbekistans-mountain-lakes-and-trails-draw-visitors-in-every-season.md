## Emerald lakes, mountain trails and boat trips offer different ways to experience the Tashkent region, where seasonal landscapes and growing tourism are bringing more visitors into the mountains.

In the mountains of the Tashkent region, the landscape changes with every turn. At Urungach, the lower lake takes on an emerald hue as water gathers in spring and recedes by summer. Further along, Chimgan draws hikers and skiers, while Charvak Reservoir offers boat trips beneath the surrounding ridgelines.

Together, these places offer different ways to explore the region’s mountains, from walking remote trails to spending time by the water.

## Urungach’s changing emerald lake

Known for its jade-coloured water, Urungach attracts visitors looking for a distinctive mountain landscape. The smaller lake, also called Jade Lake, sits among rugged rock formations and steep terrain, with a larger lake above it.

According to Efrat Turaboyev, a representative of the tourism department, the smaller lake is around 180 metres long and reaches a depth of 16.5 metres at its deepest point. Water flows into it from the upper lake through underground channels, as well as from nearby mountain streams.

The water level changes throughout the year. It generally rises in April, reaching its highest point during spring, before receding around the middle of June. The cycle repeats the following year, giving the lake a different appearance depending on when visitors arrive.

The main visitor season runs from mid-April to June. Daily numbers can range from around 50 to 500 people, with weekends generally busier than weekdays.

For visitors from Germany, the scenery remains appealing even when the weather is cloudy or rainy.

Frank Karis says the changing weather gives the landscape a dramatic quality, while Simone Rode describes the mountains and natural surroundings as peaceful and relaxing.

## Tourism and village life

Urungach’s growing popularity is creating opportunities for nearby communities, where livestock farming, agriculture and beekeeping remain important sources of income.

Residents also sell honey and other local products to visitors. Guesthouses are beginning to open in nearby villages, offering travellers a place to stay and meals prepared with local produce.

Most visitors currently travel by private car, often using four-wheel-drive UAZ or Niva vehicles suited to the mountain roads. Access is improving as roads are developed around the villages, although the journey to the lake still requires planning.

Tourism representatives are also considering camping areas and tent accommodation in lower parts of the mountains. Plans for 2027 include creating places where visitors can stay overnight before making the trip up to the lake.

For local communities, the lake has become a source of pride as well as a growing attraction. Its distinctive colour is the main reason many people make the journey.

## **Chimgan throughout the year**

A journey from Urungach to Chimgan brings a different mountain experience. The area is known for its trails, broad views and access to outdoor activities in both summer and winter.

Shahzod Abduvaliyev of the Tashkent region tourism department says Chimgan has a long history as a recreational destination, dating back to the late 19th century. Its accommodation options now include hotels, hostels and family-run guesthouses.

During winter, visitors come for skiing and other snow-related activities. In warmer months, horseback riding, trekking and time by natural lakes and swimming pools are among the options.

The area also attracts travellers interested in village life. Some communities have retained traditional ways of living, and visitors can find local bread, honey and other regional products.

Tourism has become an important source of income for residents who provide accommodation, food and other services. According to Abduvaliyev, transport and visitor facilities have also improved, with connections from Tashkent and accommodation options ranging up to four-star hotels in the Bostanlyk district.

Safety measures are part of the area's tourism infrastructure. Warning signs identify places where swimming is prohibited and alert visitors to potential wildlife risks in forested areas.

## A boat trip on Charvak Reservoir

Near Chimgan, Charvak Reservoir offers another way to experience the mountain scenery. Visitors can take boat trips across the blue water, with steep rocky slopes rising along the shoreline.

For tourist Ekaterina Sidorchuk, the colour of the reservoir and the surrounding rocks made a strong impression. She describes the boat ride, the movement of the waves and the traditional music on board as part of the experience.

She says she would like to return with her children to share the landscape with them.

Andrey Sidorchuk, who first visited the area 18 years ago, has returned with his wife to explore places she had not seen before. He says the trip has left them with many impressions and that they plan to continue travelling through Uzbekistan.

## Different ways to experience the mountains

Urungach, Chimgan and Charvak each offer a different way to experience the Tashkent region. At Urungach, the lake’s emerald colour and changing water levels give the landscape a seasonal character. Chimgan draws visitors throughout the year for hiking and winter sports, while Charvak’s blue waters are best explored by boat, with mountain slopes rising along the shore.

Tourism is also creating new opportunities for people living in the surrounding villages, from guesthouses and local food to guiding and other visitor services.

As access improves and more travellers discover the area, the region’s mountain landscapes are becoming an increasingly important part of Uzbekistan’s outdoor tourism offer. The lakes, trails and villages each provide a different perspective on the same mountainous region.
