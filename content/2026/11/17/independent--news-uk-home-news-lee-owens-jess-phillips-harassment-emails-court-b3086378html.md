A man has admitted harassing former Labour government minister Jess Phillips by sending her aggressive and threatening emails.

Lee Owens, 47, appeared before Gloucester Crown Court on Tuesday to admit two charges under section 2 of the Protection from Harassment Act 1997.

The charges state that Owens, of Matson, Gloucester, sent an “excessive volume of aggressive and threatening emails” to Ms Phillips between 22 and 25, and 10 and 14 July last year.

Judge Rupert Lowe adjourned the case for sentencing and warned Owens that all sentencing options were open, particularly given the defendant has a previous conviction for harassment.

“You have pleaded guilty to harassment of Ms Phillips and you will be sentenced by me on January 7 of next year, which is the earliest date we can fix the matter for sentence,” he told Owens.

“I am ordering a pre-sentence report so that I have full information about you.

“The fact that I am ordering a pre-sentence report does not constitute any promise about what sort of sentence will be imposed, particularly given your conviction for similar types of offending.

“You are on bail on the same conditions as before – not to contact directly or indirectly Jess Phillips or any member of staff working on her behalf.

“There is an application for a restraining order, which seems to me to be likely to be granted, which will have similar conditions.”

Owens previously pleaded not guilty to a charge of stalking Ms Phillips, which was withdrawn by prosecutors on Tuesday.

The court heard Owens has a previous conviction for harassing a member of staff at a former workplace.

A psychiatric report has been prepared in advance of the sentencing hearing and the judge will also receive a victim personal statement from Ms Phillips, describing the impact of Owens’ offending.

In a statement issued after the hearing, Ms Phillips said: “While I already have a tiring and difficult job trying to help victims of harassment and abuse get justice and care, it’s made even harder when you are suffering as a victim yourself.

“I’m grateful to have been spared a trial by the guilty pleas, but we should not underestimate the assault on our democracy when people abuse MPs.”

Ms Phillips served as minister for safeguarding and violence against women and girls until May this year.

A Labour MP since 2015, before she entered Parliament Ms Phillips worked for Women’s Aid in the West Midlands, developing services for victims of domestic abuse, sexual violence, human trafficking and exploitation.
