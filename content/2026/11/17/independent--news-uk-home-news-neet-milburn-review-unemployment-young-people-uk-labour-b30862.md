Alan Milburn is set to release the recommendations of his government-commissioned report into young people and work, which calls for steps to overhaul schools, the health service, welfare system and jobs market.

It comes as the number of young people not in employment, education or training (Neet) continues to hover around the one million mark, recording a slight fall between April and June.

There were 981,000 young people with Neet status over this period, the latest official data shows, down 30,000 from the 1.01 million recorded for January to March. This new figure is 30,000 higher than at the same point last year.

Mr Milburn has warned that Britain could see 1.25 million Neets by the early 2030s without urgent government intervention.

Speaking on Tuesday, the former healthy secretary is expected to say: “A lost generation is not a fact of life. It is a choice. And I believe it is the wrong one.

“Not just because it is a fiscal crisis or an economic crisis. But because it is a moral one.

“It is time for a new settlement that makes work not welfare the future for the next generation.”

The issue is far from a new one, with the percentage of people aged 18-24 with Neet status peaking at nearly 20 per cent in late 2010, almost 5 per cent higher than current levels.

But this rate has continued to rise steadily following the Covid pandemic, with experts warning that steps must be taken now to reverse the current trajectory.

## What does Neet mean?

A young person is considered Neet if they are:

* unemployed, looking for work; or
* economically inactive, not actively looking for work, and not waiting to start a job or caring for family

Those in formal education, undertaking an apprenticeship or in part-time work are not considered to be Neet.

## How many Neets are there?

There are now 981,000 young people aged 16-24 with Neet status in the UK, according to data from the Office for National Statistics published in August.

This is equivalent to 13 per cent of all people aged 16 to 24, a figure Mr Milburn has said he wants to lower to 5 per cent.

Most of these fall into the economically inactive bracket, at 588,000.

The latest figure saw a decline from the 1.01 million recorded for January to March 2026, which had marked a 12 year high.

The largest quarterly Neets total was 1.24 million, recorded in July to September 2011, after the 2008 financial crisis – representing 16.9 per cent of all young people at the time.

## What will Alan Milburn say in his report?

The former Labour minister is expected to recommend sweeping changes to the benefit system and improved employment support, asking ministers to set this into action during this parliament.

He has said that the UK’s youth unemployment crisis is costing the country around £125bn a year, more than the country spends on education and almost double the defence budget. This sum takes into account lost tax income, alongside higher health and welfare spending.

On welfare, Mr Milburn has recommended a wholesale replacement of the system for those aged under 25. This would be accompanied by stronger and better-funded employment support.

Central to his report is also the creation of a new Youth Opportunity Service that would bring together employment, career and health support for young people.

Speaking to BBC Radio 4 on Tuesday, Mr Milburn said that a “fundamental reset” is needed to tackle the rising number of Neets.

He said: “I think this is a crisis. I believe in young people, I don’t think the story told about them being a snowflake or a soft generation is right.

“They want a job they want to get on in their lives.

“It’s a moment of choice for the country. I don’t think half measures will do, it’s a fundamental reset for the country.”
