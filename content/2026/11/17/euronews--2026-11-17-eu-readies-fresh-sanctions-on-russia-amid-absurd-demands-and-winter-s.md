## A new round of EU sanctions against Russia is already in the works. Banks, crypto platforms and the "shadow fleet" are the likeliest targets.

The European Union is preparing a new round of economic sanctions against Russia as Moscow intensifies its airstrikes, seeking to make Ukraine's winter unbearable.

The Kremlin's demands to redesign Europe's security architecture, which High Representative Kaja Kallas slammed as "absurd", add further urgency to the matter.

The European Commission began consulting member states on Tuesday. The process, known as "confessionals", is meant to test the waters and avoid surprises.

The sanctions package is expected to target Russia's banking sector, the cryptocurrency channels it uses to skirt mainstream payment systems, and the decrepit "shadow fleet" it has deployed to bypass the oil price cap.

The Commission has set up a dedicated sanctions office to improve enforcement inside the bloc and crack down on countries that help Russia evade restrictions.

The fight against circumvention will feature prominently in the proposal.

Brussels is keen to prevent a repeat of the previous sanctions package, which saw various member states **slap vetoes** to water down, postpone or outright eliminate elements they resented. The chaotic negotiations left a bitter aftertaste among officials and diplomats and prompted a reckoning on working methods.

The bloc has recently turned to listings – individual sanctions on people, companies and organisations – that are presumably easier to agree. The thinking was upended in September after France **shocked its peers** by demanding the renewal of a high-profile oligarch to secure the release of two prisoners in Azerbaijan.

The focus now returns to the big-tent packages that have become a staple in the European strategy against Russia's war of aggression.

The proposal in the works will be the 22nd since February 2022 and will be formally presented before the end of the month.

No major or headline-grabbing measures are expected to be included to ensure the negotiations proceed smoothly and conclude in December.

The discussions will overlap with efforts to advance the peace negotiations into a multilateral format, with European and Russian representatives sitting at the negotiating table simultaneously. Despite US pressure, Moscow continues to resist this format, claiming that Europe should first present a "constructive, substantial contribution".

Russian officials could use the announcement of the sanctions as a pretext to continue refusing direct talks with the European delegation.

The prevailing opinion in Europe is that Russia does not take the peace talks seriously, as shown by the relentless attacks it is launching against Ukraine to impair the energy system and trigger widespread blackouts at sub-zero temperatures.

Kyiv has already asked Brussels to increase humanitarian aid to keep the lights on and the economy running during the war's harshest winter yet.

"I don't think the appetite for more sanctions is diminishing among member states," a senior diplomat said, dismissing the risk that the sanctions might derail diplomacy.

"With negotiations ongoing, it's important to show that European partners are steadfast in their support for Ukraine and their pressure on Russia."

On Wednesday, the Commission will present its proposal for a €45 billion support loan to cover Ukraine's 2027 financial shortfall.
