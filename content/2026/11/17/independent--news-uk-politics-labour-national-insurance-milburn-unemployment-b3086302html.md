Policies introduced by the Labour government have affected the jobs market and “made it harder” for companies to employ young people, Alan Milburn has admitted.

Hours before the former Labour cabinet minister unveiled his blueprint to solve the UK’s youth joblessness crisis, he even warned a “course correction” was needed after his party’s National Insurance hikes.

His major report on the one million under-25s across the UK not in education, employment or training – known as Neets – proposes a benefits shake-up, new apprenticeships and a £3,000 incentive for employers.

But his comments on the impact of Labour’s own policies will come as an embarrassment for ministers.

Asked by Sky News whether former Labour chancellor Rachel Reeves’ National Insurance hikes had put companies off giving jobs to young people, he said: “Employers have come to me and said that has made it harder.”

He also told LBC: “I understand why they did it because of the fiscal position that we're in. But there's absolutely no doubt that it's had an impact on the youth labour market... We need to course correct.”

Mr Milburn was asked to help solve the Neets crisis by former prime minister Sir Keir Starmer, before he was ousted from office.

His recommendations will pose a challenge to Andy Burnham who could face backlash from some Labour MPs who will not want to cut welfare. A rebellion on the issue was one of the things that ultimately led to Sir Keir’s downfall.

![Rachel Reeves raised NI (PA)](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/01/09/5cafad97acaac2eeb0ecbe02fd58a249Y29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzkzNjEyNDI4-2.85305072.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Mr Milburn has already acknowledged his radical proposals would not be "pain-free", giving a candid warning that they could lead to some people getting less financial support.

His report includes recommendations for an overhaul of the benefits system, including a proposal for a new "youth allowance" for under-25s, which would stop young people receiving the disability benefit personal independence payment (Pip) and the health element of universal credit (UC).

Unveiling the report, Mr Milburn said: “Britain has had a youth Neet problem for 25 years and never a plan to end it. We are at a fork in the road and half measures will not do. A complete system reset is needed."

At a press conference on Tuesday, he said that the current youth unemployment rate is not just an economic crisis but a moral one too and that "youth participation has to run through public policy like a stick of rock".

He said: "A lost generation is not a fact of life. It is a choice. And I believe it is the wrong one. Not just because it is a fiscal crisis or an economic crisis. But because it is a moral one.

"We are failing as a nation to gift the next generation the opportunities they need to succeed. It is time for a new settlement that makes work not welfare the future for the next generation.

"Demographic change makes getting young people into work a necessity. Britain's future relies on us backing young people."
