Britain is providing Ukraine with more than 1,000 new short-range air defence missiles and launchers to help protect the nation against "barbaric Russian attacks" this winter.

Defence secretary Wes Streeting confirmed the extra £240 million equipment boost at a Brussels meeting of the Ukraine Defence Contact Group, an alliance of over 50 nations coordinating military assistance for Kyiv.

According to the Ministry of Defence, deliveries will commence in mid-December. The missile systems have previously proven effective at intercepting Iranian-designed Shahed drones used by Russian forces to target Ukraine.

The defensive package also features more than a dozen air defence radars to spot incoming strikes, following an earlier winter supply of 400 surface-to-air missiles.

Funding forms part of the overall £500 million military package announced in the Budget, with prime minister Andy Burnham set to outline remaining support details at a Coalition of the Willing meeting on Thursday.

Mr Streeting said: “In Ukraine, President Putin uses winter as a weapon. That’s why we must continue to convert our steadfast commitment for Ukraine into capability that will help defend against barbaric Russian attacks.

“We are using funding the Chancellor pledged at the Budget to support Ukraine this winter to deliver over 1,000 air defence missiles, as part of our ongoing commitment to mobilise allies and strengthen Ukraine’s defences.

“Ukraine’s frontline is the frontline for freedom and democracy, and we will stand with them for as long as it takes.”

It comes as Ukraine struck the Kerch Bridge linking Russia to occupied Crimea, while Russia said it downed a record number of Ukrainian drones on Tuesday.

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky said the bridge was struck using a combination of drones and missiles, forming part of a complex operation that had been in the works for weeks. In doing so, Kyiv was "reminding Russia that war will always return to where it began," he said.

The attacks on Monday and Tuesday were the culmination of weeks of preparatory strikes in several stages, Zelensky said on social media.

Ukrainian forces knocked out defense systems, radars, electronic warfare equipment and other designated targets before launching Monday night’s final attack, he said.
