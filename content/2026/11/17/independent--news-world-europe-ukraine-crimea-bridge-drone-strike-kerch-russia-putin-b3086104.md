Traffic across the Crimean Bridge – which links occupied territory with mainland Russia – ground to a halt after Ukrainian forces reportedly struck the strategic crossing on Monday.

The pro-Ukrainian monitoring channel Crimean Wind reported that a strike hit one of the arches along the bridge at around 5:38am local time.

Separately, Russian Telegram channel Mash reported that a drone warhead struck an arch on the Crimean side, leaving an estimated 2,000 vehicles stranded in queues.

Rail services also faced disruption, with Russian news agency Interfax reporting passenger trains from Moscow, St Petersburg and Adler were delayed by up to four-and-a-half hours.

![Ukrainian forces have targeted the Crimean Bridge, Russia has claimed](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/06/22/08/1243954958..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The scale of any structural damage could not be independently verified, and officials in Kyiv have not yet publicly commented on the reported attack. The Kremlin later downplayed any damage as “minor”.

The incident comes as Russia’s Defence Ministry claimed to have intercepted 1,336 Ukrainian drones overnight – its highest single-night figure of the four-year war so far.

And it comes just days before Vladimir Putin, Donald Trump and Xi Jinping are due to hold trilateral talks on the sidelines of China’s Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (Apec) summit.

Stretching 19km (12 miles) across the Kerch Strait, the £2.7bn crossing is the longest bridge in Europe and serves as Moscow’s only direct road and rail link between mainland Russia and the Crimean Peninsula, which the Kremlin illegally annexed in 2014.

In a post on Telegram Monday morning, peninsula governor Sergey Aksyonov said the bridge had been hit and that vehicle restrictions would remain in place until roughly midday while emergency teams responded.

An emergency response headquarters was established in Kerch, he added, urging drivers to use ferry crossings between Crimea and Russia’s Port Kavkaz. No details regarding any casualties have been released.

A flagship project for President Putin, the bridge was built between 2016 and 2019 by the Kremlin’s childhood judo partner – billionaire oligarch Arkady Rotenberg – and cemented Russian access to Crimea.

While Russia has increasingly relied on an overland rail and highway corridor running through occupied southern Ukraine along the Sea of Azov to move military supplies, the Kerch crossing remains a vital logistical artery – particularly after Ukrainian drone strikes in July wiped out roughly 75 per cent of Russia's Kerch Strait ferry capacity.

Kyiv has repeatedly targeted the bridge since Russia launched its full-scale invasion in February 2022, viewing it both as a military supply route and enduring symbol of Putin's occupation.

![The aftermath of a lorry bombing on the bridge in 2022](https://static.independent.co.uk/2023/02/23/17/AFP_338X9LQ.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

In October 2022, a massive lorry bombing collapsed two sections of the road deck, killing four people and halting heavy transit for months.

A second major blow followed in July 2023, when Ukrainian experimental naval drones packed with explosives struck the road supports, killing two civilians.

In June 2025, Ukraine’s security service (SBU) carried out a third high-profile operation, detonating underwater charges planted against the bridge's submerged pillars.

Monday’s incident coincides with a wider escalation in long-range strikes by both sides in the run-up to winter, where Russian monitoring channels reported major fires at four different industrial facilities in Azov overnight.

Both Kyiv and Moscow’s intensifying aerial campaigns underscore the fragility of recent diplomatic efforts.

In the fortnight since the brief humanitarian truce around Oleshky expired at the start of November, Russian forces have launched more than 5,300 attack drones and 130 missiles into Ukrainian territory, according to figures released by president Volodymyr Zelensky.

While Putin is expected at Apec on Thursday, US officials have not confirmed whether Ukraine will feature on the trilateral agenda – leaving little hope for an immediate diplomatic off-ramp.
