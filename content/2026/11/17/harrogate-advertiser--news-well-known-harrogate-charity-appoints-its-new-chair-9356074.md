A well-known Harrogate-based charity which began as a small community effort more than 30 years ago has revealed a new face at the helm as it bids a a fond farewell to its Chair.

From an eight-bed hostel in its very early days, Harrogate Homeless Project has grown to provide 43 bed spaces across the town and a day centre with around 10,000 visits every year.

Outgoing Chair, David Thomas said it has been a “privilege” to lead this impactful charity for the last five fulfilling years.

“Leading an organisation that helps the most vulnerable in our community is a privilege and one that I am sad to leave behind,” David said.

“The team are focussed on providing all-round support to people who have reached the lowest point of their lives can have.

“The impact they have is considerable.

“We are fortunate that our area has this expertise available.”

Mr Thomas is handing over to Lynne Atkin, a Harrogate resident who has a background in human resources and charitable work.

Most recently, Lynne has been a trustee at Action for Children and served a term as Vice chair of the Women’s Business Council.

“It’s great to be a part of the team particularly as we celebrate our 35th anniversary but it’s tough for charities right now with rising demand and rising costs,” Lynne said.

“I have been impressed with how Harrogate Homeless Project makes every pound donated count in their mission to change people’s lives.

“I am looking forward to shaping the future of this local charity which reflects and supports the compassion that is the characteristic of our area.”

Harrogate Homeless Project began in 1991 in response to a rise in rough sleeping in the Pinewoods.

From an eight-bed hostel in those early days, the charity has grown to provide 43 bedspaces across the town and a day centre with approximately 10,000 visits every year.
