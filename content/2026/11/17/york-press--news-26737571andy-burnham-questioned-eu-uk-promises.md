Andy Burnham has appeared to delay plans to outline the UK’s future relationship with the European Union.

The Prime Minister had previously promised in his Labour conference speech that he would present different options to the public around the time of the UK-EU summit, but has now said he will first discuss these with EU partners before making them public.

Speaking at the Lord Mayor of London’s Banquet on Monday, Mr Burnham said: "This will take time and we will present those options and our assessment of them to inform decisions in the next Parliament."

He explained he would begin talks with EU leaders after the summit, which takes place in Brussels this Friday.

Downing Street has since clarified that Mr Burnham will set out "next steps for this process" around the time of the summit, but not the full range of options.

A spokesperson said: "I don’t think I accept that characterisation.

"The PM has made it clear last last month that he will set out next steps for this process around the summit.

"That still stands and we’ll set out more detail shortly."

The spokesperson said that the immediate focus remains on the summit itself and the key negotiations underway.

They said: "The priority for this week is the summit and the deals the UK is negotiating on food, emissions trading and youth mobility.

"And beyond that, as the Prime Minister has been consistently clear, there is a need to settle our long-term relationship with Europe, and that will begin after the summit."

It is understood that no formal options will be presented to the public before Christmas.

Mr Burnham’s recent comments have led to speculation about whether the UK could seek to rejoin the EU or restore freedom of movement, but he has repeatedly emphasised that the public will have the final say on any major changes.

He has also reaffirmed his commitment not to cross key "red lines"—including rejoining the customs union, single market, or allowing the return of free movement—before any future general election.

Mr Burnham said that legislation enabling the UK to align with certain EU rules, such as those on food standards, would be introduced next week.

Reports suggest that reopening discussions about Brexit has caused friction within the Cabinet, but Mr Burnham told ministers on Tuesday that the Government’s approach could help foster a "more hopeful feeling" across the country.

A readout from the Cabinet meeting stated: "Turning to Europe, the Prime Minister said this was a Government showing leadership on big issues that can build a more hopeful feeling across the country.

"Echoing his Lord Mayor’s Banquet speech, the Prime Minister said the UK was in a strong place to get a good deal in our national interest.

"He added a deal would build a constructive, positive relationship with our neighbours, which was a win-win for the country, pointing to the boss of M&S Food who had said a deal meant lifting the pressure on the price of the weekly shop.

"He then asked the Minister for European Relations to update Cabinet on negotiations."

Asked to clarify what the Prime Minister meant by creating a "hopeful feeling," a spokesperson said: "What the Prime Minister is alluding to is that we hope to agree deals worth billions of pounds a year to the British economy.

"That’s good for Britain.

"That’s good for British people.

"It’s good for British business because it cuts the costs for business and consumers, and it restores opportunity to young people, and that is his view, and that’s the view he conveyed to Cabinet this morning."
