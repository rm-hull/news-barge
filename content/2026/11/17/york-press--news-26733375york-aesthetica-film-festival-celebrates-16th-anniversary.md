## York film festival celebrates anniversary with 300 screenings and awards

Still from This is Not a Memoir dir. Josh Earle, Best Animation Winner at ASFF 2026 *(Image: Aesthetica Film Festival)*

A film festival has celebrated its 16th anniversary with a packed programme of screenings, industry talks, and emerging talent.

The BAFTA-qualifying Aesthetica Film Festival, held in York from November 4 to 8, welcomed filmmakers, delegates, and industry professionals from more than 60 countries, cementing its reputation as a global meeting point for new voices in screen and media.

More than 300 films were screened across York, covering genres from drama and documentary to experimental and immersive VR.

The festival also featured a masterclass series with industry leaders including Mark Jenkin (Bait, Enys Men & Rose of Nevada), Brian J. Falconer (Derry Girls), and BBC Radio 1’s Jess Iszatt.

Cherie Federico, festival director, said: "Aesthetica is about discovery, ambition, and possibility.

"Over five days, York becomes a place where the next generation of talent is seen first, where ideas collide, and where creativity thrives across every discipline – from film and music to VR, games, and podcasts.

"This festival is the beating heartbeat of the UK’s creative sector, a space where innovation, culture, and storytelling converge, shaping the future of our industry."

Major organisations such as BBC, Film4, Framestore, ITV, Neal Street Productions, Ridley Scott Associates, and Ubisoft offered insights into directing, screenwriting, post-production, and emerging technologies.

Aardman Animations marked its 50th anniversary with guest screenings and a masterclass led by director Will Becher.

The festival’s awards highlighted standout talent across 15 categories.

Best Drama went to Mikolaj Piszczan for Slap!!, and Elham Ehsas won Best Documentary for Forgotten Spaceman.

The Best of Fest and Best Artist’s Film awards were both won by Shams Uz Zuha Mansur for Don’t Touch the Artwork.

Other winners included Josh Earle (Best Animation for This is Not a Memoir), Leïla Macaire and Romane Piffaut (Best Dance for A Sad Woman), and Eva Blackwell-Rogelj (Best Experimental for Rusalki).

Aesthetica also introduced a dedicated music programme this year, with the New Music Stage at York Theatre Royal.

Ten emerging artists performed live, and Dublin-based band WineMom was named the stage winner, impressing the jury and audience with their originality and stage presence.

Interactive storytelling was showcased through the Radical Listening Immersive Media Arts Trail, created in partnership with Audible, which brought public art installations by Squidsoup, Steve Messam, and Being Now Here to York’s streets.

The festival also hosted the UK Film Production Summit at The Grand, York, featuring panels and keynotes with speakers from Film4, Ridley Scott Associates, Sky, and Warner Bros.

The 17th edition of the Aesthetica Film Festival will take place from November 3 to 7, 2027.
