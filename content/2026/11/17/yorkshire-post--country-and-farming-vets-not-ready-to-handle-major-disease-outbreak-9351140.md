# ​Vets ‘not ready’ to handle major disease outbreak

## Fewer than three in ten vets believe the profession is prepared to handle such a major animal disease emergency, a study has concluded.

The finding of the British Veterinary Association’s Voice of the Veterinary Profession survey comes amid concerns over diseases including African swine fever, bluetongue and bird flu.

Last week, Defra announced some 14,000 hens would be culled on an East Yorkshire free range farm following the region’s first confirmed bird flu outbreak of the winter season.

Defra confirmed highly pathogenic avian influenza (HPAI) H5N1 at the premises near Melbourne, triggering fresh disease control restrictions.

Other outbreaks have been confirmed at a premises in North Lincolnshire involving 64,000 ducks and also near Garstang, in Wyre, Lancashire, at a mixed commercial poultry premises containing seasonal turkeys and geese.

The veterinarians’ study concluded eight in ten members who responded would be willing to support a national response during a time of crisis, for example through Temporary Veterinary Inspector work.

However, only 28 per cent felt confident the profession is prepared for a large scale outbreak, and only a similar proportion were confident in their personal preparedness for such a situation.

Lack of training emerged as the most commonly reported barrier.
