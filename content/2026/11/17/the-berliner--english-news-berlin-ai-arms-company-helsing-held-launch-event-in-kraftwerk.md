On November 11, Helsing, an AI arms company based in Munich, held an event in Kraftwerk Berlin. The self-described defence technology company launched Clarity, an ‘agentic intelligence platform’, at the event. According to Helsing, Clarity is a program that “processes vast volumes of data in parallel and identifies adversary assets at the pace and scale modern warfare demands”.

The event was heavily guarded and kept under wraps. Security guards in bulletproof vests, bomb-sniffing dogs and military vehicles could be seen outside the world-renowned venue on Köpenicker Straße.

Kraftwerk was turned into a mock-military bunker, where combat scenarios were played out, and military personnel manned the command posts. There were also several talks, with Helsing co-CEO / co-founder Gundbert Scherf, Chief Technology Officer Rob Fink and Programs Director Simon Pfeiffer headlining the invite-only event.

According to Helsing, its objective is to “help protect our democratic values and open societies”, through the development of AI-powered drones, which it markets to potential buyers with categories like ‘air combat’, ‘precision strike ’, ‘air defence’ and ‘maritime autonomy’.

Readers may be familiar with Helsing, as it’s the company that co-founder and former CEO of Spotify, Daniel Ek, pumped €600 million into last year. Ek now serves as Helsing’s chairman.

With the new AI platform Clarity, “We want to bring clarity to the flood of data,” says co-founder Gundbert Scherf. “Clarity allows decisions to flow,”. The types of decisions Clarity is assisting with determine which targets are attacked and which are not. “In short, our AI agents condense data into what contributes to deterrence: targets,” says Fink. Ultimately, however, the decision about what to target is still made by a human.
