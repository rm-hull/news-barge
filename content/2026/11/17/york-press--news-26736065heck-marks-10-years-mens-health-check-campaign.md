## HECK! marks 10 years of CHECK! campaign after co-founder’s cancer diagnosis

HECK!’s Jamie Keeble who was diagnosed with testicular cancer in 2024 (image: HECK!) *(Image: HECK!)*

HECK! is marking 10 years of its CHECK! health campaign.

The North Yorkshire food brand launched the campaign after co-founder Jamie Keeble was diagnosed with testicular cancer at 24.

As part of its 10th anniversary, HECK! extended its programme of onsite health screenings to six days during Men’s Health Month in November.

Jamie Keeble, co-founder at HECK!, said: "Ten years ago, I received a diagnosis that changed my life.




"Thankfully, because it was caught early, I was able to get treatment and make a full recovery.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21727498.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Yorkshire Vet star Peter Wright, who has backed the CHECK! campaign – seen here getting a blood pressure check (image: HECK!) *(Image: HECK!)*

"The experience made me realise how important it is to know your body, get checked and seek help when something doesn't feel right."

HECK! has partnered with men’s health charity Cahonas to deliver the campaign, which promotes preventative healthcare and early diagnosis.

Since its launch in 2016, the CHECK! campaign has raised more than £100,000 for men’s health charities.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21727496.jpg?type=mds-article-620)HECK! has rebranded to CHECK! for the month of November (image: HECK!) *(Image: Pic supplied)*

The funds have supported awareness, education and support services across the UK.

This year’s campaign began with six days of health checks at HECK! HQ, offering employees screenings to help identify potential health issues.

HECK! also temporarily rebranded its chicken sausage packs from HECK! to CHECK!

The packs feature the message "Don’t forget to check" and a QR code linking to information on the Cahonas website.

A portion of sales from each pack sold in November is being donated to Cahonas.

Mr Keeble said: "The CHECK! campaign was born from that personal experience and a desire to encourage more people to have potentially life-saving conversations about their health.

"Reaching our tenth year is a proud milestone, but there is still a long way to go in breaking down the stigma that prevents people, particularly men, from seeking medical advice."

HECK! and Cahonas have worked together for the past three years, delivering workplace talks, community outreach, and educational events to promote conversations around testicular cancer and mental health.

The company also operates the "Ball Buster" bus, which tours the country to raise awareness.

This year, the campaign will include city centre awareness in Leeds.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21727441.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The Cahonas team, who popped into HECK! HQ for an important talk on testicular cancer *(Image: Pic supplied)*

Ritchie Marshall from Cahonas said: "Workplaces have a huge opportunity to make a real difference when it comes to men's health.

"Many people spend most of their waking hours at work, making it the ideal environment to raise awareness, share potentially life-saving information and encourage people to check themselves regularly."

HECK! is now calling on employers to make men’s health education a routine part of workplace wellbeing programmes.

By offering talks, information, and health screenings, businesses can help employees recognise symptoms, seek support earlier, and feel more comfortable discussing health concerns.

Mr Keeble said: "Raising over £100,000 for men's health causes is a fantastic achievement, but the real success lies in the number of conversations we've started and lives we've potentially helped change through early intervention."
