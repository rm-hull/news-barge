Hundreds of demonstrators gathered outside an Oxford University college on Tuesday to oppose a talk by the academic who accused the late Professor Jason Arday of plagiarism.

The rally at Oriel College, organised by Stand Up To Racism, saw protesters attempt to block entry through the main gates. Some wore face masks, while dozens held placards reading "Nathan Cofnas not welcome" and "Remember Professor Jason Arday". Crowds were heard chanting "Nathan Cofnas shut him down".

Mr Cofnas, who promotes "race realism", targeted Professor Arday in a blog post alleging plagiarism in his doctoral thesis.

The former Cambridge University academic was found dead at his home in south London earlier this year after facing heavy scrutiny over claims regarding his work and life story. An inquest into the death of the 41-year-old in August is under way.

![Signs called for Nathan Cofnas to be 'shut down'](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/17/18/01M7NBCQZJS4MZCC977MCE1CFK.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Organised by Oxford Reform UK, the free event was titled *An Evening With Nathan Cofnas: Is DEI Undermining Higher Education?* An Eventbrite description stated that Mr Cofnas "faced recent controversy after exposing the plagiarism of Jason Arday".

Mr Cofnas previously claimed black people have lower intelligence than other groups and would "disappear from almost all high-profile positions outside of sports and entertainment" under meritocracy.

He was suspended from Belgium's Ghent University in August before being reinstated following pressure from US ambassador Bill White.

When university senior leadership was mentioned by a speaker, the crowd responded with shouts of "shame".

Following Professor Arday’s death in August, Sir Patrick Vernon, who played a leading role in the successful campaign to establish a national Windrush Day, set up a GoFundMe crowdfunding page to help support the academic’s family.

![Jason Arday was found dead in south London earlier this year (Joe Giddens/PA)](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/08/26/02/117bd8a405c96c4d9abdfee417436ceeY29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzg3NzU0NDEw-2.85498237.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

In a message on the page at the time, Professor Arday’s family said: “We are deeply moved by the extraordinary support we have received. The response to this appeal reflects the number of people whose lives were touched by our beloved Jason and the affection, respect and admiration in which he was held.

“Jason devoted his life and career to advancing education, widening participation, tackling race and inequality, and championing autism and neurodiversity. It means a great deal to us that so many people wish to celebrate his life and remember the difference he made to others.

“We are still grieving the loss of our beloved son, partner, father, brother, uncle and friend. We hope you will understand our need for time and space as we come to terms with this devastating loss and consider how best to move forward.”
