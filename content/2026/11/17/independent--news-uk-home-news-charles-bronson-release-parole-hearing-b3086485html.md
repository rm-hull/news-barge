One of the UK’s longest-serving inmates, Charles Bronson, has again been refused release after his ninth parole hearing.

Bronson has spent most of the past five decades in jail – bar two brief periods during which he reoffended – for a string of thefts, and firearms and violent crimes.

This has included 11 hostage-taking incidents in nine different sieges, with victims including prison governors, doctors, staff and, on one occasion, his own solicitor.

The Parole Board has considered him for release nine times; his eighth hearing in 2023 was the second ever to be held in public in the UK.

Bronson – whose real name is Michael Peterson – was first convicted in 1974 when he was 21 and was jailed for seven years for robbery, aggravated burglary, assault with intent to rob and possession of a firearm.

He has spent most of his life in prison since for a string of different offences, and was handed a discretionary life sentence with a minimum term of four years in 2000 for taking a prison teacher at HMP Hull hostage for 44 hours.

The long-serving prisoner, who has been diagnosed with anti-social personality disorder, has been repeatedly turned down for release.

Earlier this year he applied for his latest parole hearing to be held in public, but was turned down on the grounds that there were not the resources to do so.

Parole Board member Jeremy Roberts KC said he was prepared to believe that Bronson’s intentions in applying for another public hearing were “entirely genuine even if (as is likely) there is an element of attention-seeking in his application”.

Bronson, who changed his surname to Salvador in 2014, gave evidence at length at his previous parole hearing, telling the panel that he dreamed of walking barefoot on grass, and wanted his elderly mother to see him released before she died.

He said: “It’s no secret I have had more porridge than Goldilocks and the Three Bears, and I’m sick of it. I’ve had enough of it, I want to go home.”
