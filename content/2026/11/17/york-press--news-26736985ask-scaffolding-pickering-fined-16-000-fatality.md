## North Yorkshire scaffolding firm fined £16,000 after trainee dies in fall

A North Yorkshire scaffolding firm has been fined after a trainee worker died in a fall. (Stock image) *(Image: PA)*

A North Yorkshire scaffolding company has been fined £16,000 after a trainee worker died in a fall.

Michael Swales, who worked for Ask Scaffolding Ltd, died on July 5, 2023, after falling five metres from a partially erected scaffold at a housing development near Ripon in North Yorkshire.

He had been standing on an unsecured scaffolding board when he fell.

An investigation by the Health and Safety Executive (HSE) found that the company had failed to ensure work at height was carried out safely and in line with industry guidance.

David Beaton, HSE inspector, said: "This tragic accident underlines the importance of those in control of scaffolding work making sure that it is being done safely and in accordance with industry guidance, and the potential consequences where this is not done.

"Our thoughts remain with Michael’s family and friends."

Ask Scaffolding Ltd, based at Thornton Road Industrial Estate in Pickering, pleaded guilty to breaching Regulation 4(1)(c) of the Work at Height Regulations 2005.

![A Pickering company has been fined £16,000 following a fatality at a site in Ripon.](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21728795.jpg?type=mds-article-620)A Pickering company has been fined £16,000 following a fatality at a site in Ripon. *(Image: PA)*

The company was fined £16,000 and ordered to pay £8,000 in costs and a £2,000 victim surcharge at Leeds Magistrates’ Court.

Mr Swales had been erecting scaffolding at the front of a two-storey house under construction when the incident happened.

Emergency services attended, but he died at the scene.

The HSE investigation found that scaffolding work on the day of the accident was unsafe and did not comply with industry guidance.

No safe zones had been established, and neither Mr Swales nor his colleague was using a harness or lanyard.

The investigation also revealed that previous scaffolding work had not followed proper guidance.

A post-accident inspection found that guardrails were missing in areas where they should have been installed.

Witnesses told investigators that scaffolding work was regularly carried out without following the guidance.

Industry guidance requires the use of collective protection measures to create a "Scaffolder’s Safe Zone" as the primary way to prevent falls.

When a safe zone is not in place, personal protection such as a harness and lanyard should be used.

The HSE reminded employers and scaffolding contractors to ensure all scaffolding is erected, dismantled, and altered safely in line with regulations.

The prosecution was brought by HSE enforcement lawyer Iain Jordan and paralegal officer Zara Salman.
