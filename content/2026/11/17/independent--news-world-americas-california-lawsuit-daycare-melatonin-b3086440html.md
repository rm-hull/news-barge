Two California mothers are suing their children’s daycare after their kids were allegedly given “sleeping gummies” to make them sleep during nap time without their permission.

The lawsuit, filed November 4 in Orange County Superior Court, accuses Kiddie Academy Corporate Centers, KA Preschool of Orange and three employees of negligence, battery, emotional distress and negligent hiring, supervision and training, according to KTLA.

Cassandra Sandoval and Priscilla Ayala claim their children were given sleep-inducing gummies without their consent between May and September 2026 at Kiddie Academy of Orange.

Sandoval said she chose to enroll her 4-year-old and 2-year-old at the daycare because the facility offered livestreams that allowed parents to see what their children were doing throughout the day.

“It helped me know, you know, if my child was eating, or if they had a tough time for the day, or if they were making friends, hitting milestones,” Sandoval told KABC-TV.

![Two California mothers claim their children were given sleep-inducing gummies at daycare during their nap time. They have filed a lawsuit over the allegations](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/17/13/46/California-preschool-melatonin.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

However, in August, Sandoval began noticing changes in her 4-year-old son’s sleeping patterns. In September, her son asked her for the “sleeping gummies” he said he received during nap time at the daycare, according to the lawsuit.

Sandoval then filed a complaint with the California Department of Social Services, which oversees licensed child care centers, the lawsuit said.

She also began to monitor the daycare’s livestream more closely — and witnessed a teacher place an unidentified substance in her son’s mouth while he was underneath a blanket.

“I was so angry. I was angry for myself. Why’d I send him back?” Sandoval told KABC. “I left work immediately, and I got there, and I just looked straight into their faces — director, assistant director — ran into the daycare, grabbed one child and he was knocked out. That was my child who was given the sleeping gummy. And he was slobbering, and he was knocked out. It was really sad.”

Sandoval said she then removed both of her children from the daycare and never returned. She later took her son to the hospital and reported the incident to police.

Another mother, Priscilla Ayala, also detailed similar concerns involving her young daughter in the lawsuit. In the filing, she alleges that staff at the daycare asked for permission to give her daughter melatonin during nap time, which she declined. However, her daughter eventually told her mother she had received the gummies from a teacher.

![One mother said she declined to give the school permission to give her daughter melatonin, but that it was given to her anyway](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/17/13/21/California-preschool-melatonin.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

In the lawsuit, Ayala alleges that her daughter began experiencing nightmares and behavior changes while attending the preschool.

“As soon as she removed her daughter from Kiddie Academy and the child seemingly no longer had the melatonin in her system, all of her nightmares stopped,” Kimberly Degonia, an attorney representing both families, told KABC.

The lawsuit alleges negligence, battery, emotional distress and negligent hiring, supervision and training. It also cites California child care laws that require written parental authorization and documentation when nonprescription medicines are administered.

In a statement, Kiddie Academy of Orange said, “As an independently owned and operated Kiddie Academy franchise, we care deeply about the children, families and community we serve. Because this matter involves children and an ongoing review, we’re limited in what we can share publicly.”

“At this time, we’re cooperating fully with the appropriate licensing entities, and the staff member involved has been placed on administrative leave pending the results of the licensing investigation. The safety and well-being of the children in our care remain our highest priority.”

The families are seeking unspecified damages, including punitive damages, and have requested a jury trial.
