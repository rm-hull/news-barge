Sir Julian Smith told MPs the visitor levy should be paused, saying North Yorkshire hotels and campsites are only just surviving. More than 6,000 jobs at stake.

Sir Julian Smith, the Conservative MP for Skipton and Ripon, has asked the Government to put the Visitor Levy (England) Bill on hold, telling the Commons that hospitality businesses in North Yorkshire are “really frightened” by a new tax at a time when many are only just surviving.

He spoke on Monday 16 November in the Bill’s second reading debate. The Bill is before Parliament and would give strategic authorities the power to introduce a levy on overnight accommodation. His seat takes in Ripon and Nidderdale.

## Overview

* **What:** the Visitor Levy (England) Bill, which would let strategic authorities charge a levy on overnight stays.
* **When:** Sir Julian spoke in its second reading debate on 16 November.
* **His ask:** pause the Bill and talk further with hospitality businesses.
* **His figure:** more than 6,000 people in Skipton and Ripon work in accommodation and food services.

## Why rural businesses, he said, are different

His office says he argued that rural hospitality faces higher transport costs, staffing pressures, higher energy costs, supply chain difficulties and the limits of operating in remote places.

In the chamber, according to the Hansard record, he said: “It is challenging at the moment for hoteliers or campsite owners anywhere in the UK, but in North Yorkshire and in particular in my constituency which is very rural, those challenges are especially acute.”

He went on: “Let us take a hotel or a campsite deep in the heart of the Yorkshire Dales. Getting staff and the cost of getting staff is a major challenge. Some hotels have to offer accommodation or bus services. Transport, food and drink supply costs are higher in remote locations. Energy supply, already tough for all providers of accommodation, is more expensive off-grid.”

He also said a rural hotel “might be further away than is reasonable from a hospital, so more healthcare needs to be provided onsite”, and that planning an expansion in areas such as North Yorkshire takes longer.

## ‘One more hammer blow’

Sir Julian said North Yorkshire hospitality was “only just recovering from covid” and was now “being battered by high energy costs, high wage costs, high rates costs, inflation pressure impacting spending, and a welter of employment legislation that is about to come down the track.”

“The UK hospitality sector is worried about the Government’s plans, but those in North Yorkshire hospitality are really frightened by the additional layer of tax and bureaucracy at a time when they and their businesses are only just surviving,” he said.

“Almost all the hospitality businesses I work with are not making a profit. They are losing money, obviously, but one more hammer blow could tip them over, and that means less revenue and fewer jobs.”

## Jobs and neighbouring counties

He told MPs that more than 6,000 people in Skipton and Ripon are employed in accommodation and food services, and that suppliers depend on the trade too: “Local food producers, taxi drivers, electricians, technicians and others who supply the businesses, and the pubs, restaurants, tourist sites and venues all need the footfall.”

His office says he warned that an extra tax could make it harder for hotels, campsites, pubs, restaurants and attractions to compete when neighbouring areas may not bring in a levy. In the debate he said the Mayor of York and North Yorkshire had already spoken of a 5% levy, “which means that we will be competing against Lancashire, Northumberland and other attractive counties that may not apply a tax at all.”

He also raised the paperwork: “We want them to invest in their businesses, not in HMRC services.”

## An invitation to the Secretary of State

His office says he called on the Secretary of State for Housing, Communities and Local Government, Angela Rayner, to pause the legislation and engage further with hospitality businesses, arguing that the priority should be encouraging investment, growth and visitor spending in rural communities rather than adding costs.

“Given the context of the challenges facing the sector, the only responsible thing is to put the Bill on hold,” he said. “I want the Secretary of State to come to North Yorkshire. I will pay for a night in a hotel, and then she can see how hard those people are working.”

At the start of his speech he referred the House to his entry in the Register of Members’ Financial Interests.
