I've already had to write the word 'committee' a lot in recent weeks, due to the scandal developing over at Nvidia leading to CEO Jensen Huang's resignation. He's off to see a couple of committees in Congress in coming weeks. Though today it's Nvidia's turn to establish a committee: the National Security and Export Control Oversight Committee, or NSECOC as I will hereafter call it.

The NSECOC will answer direct to the Nvidia board. It's also led by a board member, Suzanne Nora Johnson, former vice chairman of Goldman Sachs Group. Though the company says it will keep independent experts around for advice and expertise. It will work with the existing National Security Team and Global Trade Compliance Team at Nvidia, which will now report to the committee and interim CEO, Colette Kress.

It looks to me like the company's board want to know who Nvidia is sending its GPUs to at all times; something that will be of utmost import in coming months and years as the company's customers have come under recent scrutiny by US lawmakers and agencies. An investigation is being carried out by the US Department of Justice into one of its customers, Megaspeed, the agency confirmed yesterday.

It also means the board has a say in the question of who gets Nvidia's GPUs while the company looks for a new CEO. As it stands, the company's CFO Kress is in the big chair at Nvidia HQ, but the company is looking for a replacement. Recently, Jensen Huang and his allies are said to be floating the idea of him making a return, and his pal president Trump doesn't mind the idea; however, the board isn't considering it according to a source speaking to CNBC.

Nvidia's newly-appointed chairman said of the new committee:

"Nvidia recognizes that advanced silicon technologies and the emergence of artificial and superintelligence present unprecedented challenges for national security. As the world’s leading advanced computing company, Nvidia has both a responsibility and an opportunity to lead the industry in addressing these challenges. We are committed to working constructively with government and industry partners to help establish and advance a robust framework that uses every tool at our disposal to safeguard national security while supporting continued technological innovation."

It signs off with a promise to "enhance its engagement with relevant government stakeholders." Basically, cosy up to the government to keep its business going with minimal disruption or share price drops.
