If the thought of this year's Christmas bill makes you wince, you're not alone.

According to new research from Nationwide, Brits expect to spend an average of £103 on each of their closest loved ones this Christmas.

Multiply that across partners, children, parents and grandchildren and it soon adds up.

Four out of five people asked said they were worried about how they'll afford a decent Christmas.

**The jewellery drawer everyone has**

Almost every home has one: a drawer, a trinket box or an old biscuit tin full of jewellery nobody wears.

A single earring whose partner went missing years ago. A chain that snapped and never got fixed.

A ring from a past relationship. A charm bracelet you loved at 14. Pieces handed down from a parent or grandparent that are lovely, but just not your style.

**So what could your jewellery be worth?**

Gold jewellery is valued mainly on two things: how much it weighs and how pure the gold is.

Pure gold is 24 carat, but most jewellery is mixed with other metals to make it harder-wearing.

A 9ct piece is 37.5% gold, and an 18ct piece is 75% gold.

**How to tell what you've got**

You don't need to be an expert. Grab a magnifying glass, or the zoom on your phone camera, and look for a tiny stamped number, usually on the inside of a ring, near the clasp of a chain or on the back of an earring:

* 375 means 9ct gold
* 585 means 14ct gold
* 750 means 18ct gold
* 916 means 22ct gold
* 925 means sterling silver

No stamp? Don't throw it out. Older and handmade pieces aren't always marked, and a reputable buyer can test it for you.

**Before you sell: what to keep**

Not everything should be sold for its gold. Hang on to it, or at least get proper advice, if it:

* means something to you. If a piece reminds you of someone you love, that's worth more than any price per gram. Some people keep one special item and sell the rest.
* has good-quality stones, such as diamonds or sapphires, which may be worth more than the metal.
* is antique, vintage or by a known designer. These can be worth more as jewellery than as scrap.
* could be remade. An old ring could be reset into something you'd actually wear.

**Getting a fair deal**

A few simple tips will help you get the best price:

* Sort your pieces into gold and silver, and by carat if you can.
* Ask for the price per gram for each carat before you agree to anything.
* If you choose a home visit, ask to see your items weighed and tested in front of you, at your own kitchen table.
* If you're posting, photograph and list everything first, and use an insured, tracked service. Ask what happens if you decide not to accept the offer. You should be able to get your items back.
* Have photo ID ready. Reputable buyers are required to ask for it.
* Don't feel rushed. A good buyer will happily give you a quote and let you think it over.
