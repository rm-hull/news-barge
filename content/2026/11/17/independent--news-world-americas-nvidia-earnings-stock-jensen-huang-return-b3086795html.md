Nvidia’s board has made clear that it will not bring Jensen Huang back as CEO after shareholders and his fellow tech bros called for him to be reinstated.

Huang resigned as head of the AI chipmaking company after he admitted to an affair with a Chinese executive suspected of being a spy for Beijing.

“Jensen Huang, voluntarily resigned his positions as President, CEO, and board member. The NVIDIA Board unanimously accepted his resignation and does not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO,” Harvey Jones, chairman of Nvidia’s board, said during an earnings call Tuesday. Executives refused to take questions from investors about the scandal.

Nvidia’s better-than-expected third-quarter earnings report caused its stock to pop in after-hours trading. Nvidia’s stock sat at $155.46 at the closing bell, and just thirty minutes later, it spiked by about 4 percent to $161.69.

The company reported revenues of $115.8 billion, beating Wall Street’s estimates of around $109 billion.

![Nvidia’s board has made clear that it will not bring Jensen Huang back as CEO after shareholders and his fellow tech bros called for him to be reinstated](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/17/22/59/GettyImages-2301328054.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

On November 8, the day Huang admitted the affair to employees, Nvidia said it would be conducting an investigation following reporting that Huang “has been the target of a foreign intelligence effort.”

Huang stepped down last Thursday and was replaced by Colette Kress, Nvidia’s executive vice president and chief financial officer, while the company looks for a permanent CEO.

Over the weekend, an online petition to reinstate Huang as Nvidia’s CEO garnered more than 100,000 signatures, including around 60,000 from shareholders, 6,000 from current Nvidia employees and 1,000 from former employees, according to reports.

Tesla and SpaceX CEO Elon Musk, Replit CEO Amjad Masad and Epic Games CEO Tim Sweeney publicly backed the effort, and there was a report that Huang had discussions with allies about possibly returning to the helm.

!['The NVIDIA Board unanimously accepted [Huang's] resignation and does not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO,' Harvey Jones, chairman of Nvidia’s board, said during an earnings call Tuesday](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/17/22/29/GettyImages-2304259055.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

One ally told the *Financial Times* that they were convening a “war room” of tech executives to reinstate Huang and would try to get support from the White House.

President Donald Trump, who has close ties to Huang and called him a “very high quality person” after the scandal broke, insisted he had no idea that U.S. intelligence found Huang’s mistress, Alice Huang, was a Chinese spy. But the *New York Times* reported in late October that Trump was briefed on the findings about a year ago and chose not to take action.

Earlier Tuesday, Huang released his first public statement since leaving Nvidia, admitting that he “did not live up to the standards expected of everyone at NVIDIA, and I have made serious mistakes in my personal life.

“I created problems for the Board that I deeply regret. I own my mistakes completely, and I am committed to cooperating with the Board’s independent investigation,” he wrote on X.

![Nvidia’s better-than-expected third-quarter earnings report caused its stock to pop in after-hours trading. Nvidia’s stock sat at $155.46 at the closing bell, and just thirty minutes later, it spiked by about 4 percent to $161.69](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/17/22/07/GettyImages-2305377860.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Huang acknowledged the push from supporters to reinstate him as Nvidia’s CEO and said he’d be “honored to answer these calls.”

“Since my departure, I’ve seen an outpouring of calls from NVIDIA’s employees, investors, business partners, and customers for me to return as CEO. Many continue to reach out to me directly as the company navigates complex technology, market, competitive, and geopolitical dynamics.

“NVIDIA is a once-in-a-generation company. If the Board of Directors decides that my return is in the best interest of our shareholders, our employees, the company, and America, I would be honored to answer these calls and return as CEO,” he wrote.

But, as Nvidia’s board said during the earnings call later Tuesday, that won’t be in the cards — for now.
