A Norwegian artist has talked about the horrific crash that inspired his latest stunning exhibition at the launch event in Harrogate.

Snowboarder turned painter Danny Larson has become internationally renowned for his starkly atmospheric black and white ‘pointillist’ paintings of the fairytale forested landscapes of his home country.

Talking to the Harrogate Advertiser at the opening of Silver Linings XVI at RedHouse gallery in Harrogate, the charismatic artist said breaking several ribs in a mountain biking accident this year had led to a change of direction away from his trademark monochrome into more colourful watercolours and the beauty of individual flowers.

“It was incredibly painful after the crash and I not only couldn’t paint, I could hardly lie down for months,” said Danny, who was born in 1981 in a small rural community outside Oslo.

“I had to keep moving or there was also the risk of getting pneumonia.

“I usually rush by the small things in life looking for grander stuff.

“But my ribs were so badly damaged that I had to move slowly the whole time and I started to see things I hadn’t seen before.

“I was forced to notice flowers and their colours.

“The accident unlocked a new part of me.”

Although as brilliantly detailed and painstakingly precise in technique as ever, the new collection of lone flowers comes with few hints of the dark behind the light in previous exhibitions by an artist whose daring-do has always been in an unresolved tussle with depression.

“I knew that doing paintings of flowers was beyond a cliché in the art world,” said Danny was internationally successful as a professional snowboarder in his 20s.

“It’s something you’re not supposed to do but, after the mountain bike crash, I felt it was time to show a different side to my personality.

“I’ve struggled with depression my whole life but I thought it was possible to do flowers in a new way.”

Running at RedHouse gallery at 15 Cheltenham Mount in Harrogate from now until December 12, Silver Linings may be a departure in some ways for the multi-faceted Danny Larson, though they are far from shallow or 'pretty-pretty'.

As always, nature remains at the heart of his work, perhaps, more so now.

Missing, however, is that lurking shadow of dark psychology.

“Wherever I am, whether that’s Norway or Japan or Harrogate, even, as long as I am out in nature I feel at home,” said Danny.

“Nature is a big part of humanity but art is, too.

“I can see it in my kids.

“When it comes to art, a light seems to switch on in them.

“I think art is more important in today’s world than ever.”
