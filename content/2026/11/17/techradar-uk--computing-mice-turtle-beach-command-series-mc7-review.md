## Turtle Beach Command Series MC7 review

The Turtle Beach Command Series MC7 gaming mouse left me with one question after testing it: just because you *can*do something, does that mean you* should*?

See, the Turtle Beach Command Series MC7 comes equipped with a touchscreen — just like the brand's Command Series keyboards. I can understand having a few useful shortcuts and commands available at the tap of a screen on your keyboard — especially if you’re a streamer or competitive gamer. But it just feels straight up unnecessary on a mouse.

First of all, the touchscreen is pretty small and didn’t feel particularly natural to use with my thumb. What’s more, a lot of the features available — like adjusting DPI or polling rate — can just be mapped to the customizable ‘M’ buttons scattered across the mouse. Sure, the touchscreen enables you to launch a gaming overlay or media controls, but again, it feels like these could be tied to button controls or keyboard shortcuts.

Don’t get me wrong, the touchscreen is nice and bright, it’s responsive, and the software is easy to get started with after downloading the Swarm II app, but overall, the interface feels superfluous.

Anyway, let’s look past the touchscreen for now, and assess how the MC7 fares against the best gaming mice in terms of performance. And luckily, it does pretty well in this department.

From the specs alone, you can tell that the MC7 is oriented towards competitive gamers. You’re getting true 8K polling with 0.125ms latency, a 30K DPI ‘Owl-Eye’ optical sensor, and Titan Optical Switches with adjustable debounce and, according to Turtle Beach, “a 150 million-click lifespan”.

But how does that translate into actual use? Well, I tried playing *Counter-Strike 2*, and had a very good experience overall. While you can get higher sensitivity from mice like the Steelseries Sensei Pro, I found the sensor to be excellent, with impressive speed and tracking.

The 8K polling is another great feature, and helped me aim with great precision, with ultra-smooth movement. The Titan Optical Switches also felt highly satisfying and snappy, with a pleasant mechanical sound to them. The 4D scroll wheel is pretty cool too, and can dynamically snap from small steps to free-spin scrolling — plus you can click it left or right for seamless navigation.

![Battery next to the Turtle Beach Command Series MC7](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/QwysqYKzuJVoeoLMCLA8ZD-1200-80.jpg)

What I would say, though, is that some may find the MC7 to be a little unwieldy. While its sloped design felt pretty comfortable to me, I will say that it's a pretty large mouse — and quite heavy at that. Those with smaller hands may want to steer clear, and if you prefer a lightweight feel, this mouse will likely feel too hefty for you.

On the topic of design, I have to say that the MC7 is one of the less attractive mice I’ve tested in my time at TechRadar. The bronze details feel a little garish alongside the purple touchscreen, black body and LED lighting — it’s all just a bit messy. The mouse also uses a strange combination of boldly angular and rounded segments, giving it an awkward form. That is to say that it feels like the MC7 is having a bit of an identity crisis, and I wish it was at least a bit easier on the eye.

I feel like I’ve bashed this mouse quite a lot, so I want to return to the positives for a moment. The Turtle Beach Command Series MC7 has an absolutely brilliant hot-swappable battery system, which essentially gives you infinite playtime. The wireless transmitter houses a battery that can charge while you use your mouse, and when your battery eventually dies, you can swap the transmitter battery right in. Other than the few seconds you’ll take to switch the batteries, you’ll get unbroken play.

Something else that impressed me was the sheer number of features on offer. You can pick between a 2.4GHz wireless, Bluetooth, or wired connection, providing exceptional versatility. The Swarm II companion software also opens up a lot of options, like button mapping, touchscreen customization, and lighting adjustments. What’s more, it allows you to drill into angle-tuning, debounce time alterations, motion sync options, and even haptics adjustments.

All in all, the Turtle Beach Command Series MC7 is a highly customizable, feature-rich gaming mouse. And its infinite battery system, solid performance, and detailed companion software all impressed me overall.

However, the cons are plain to see. The mouse is pretty hefty, it's also quite unattractive, and the touchscreen — which is the MC7’s USP — feels unnecessary.

Add in the fact that this mouse has a steep list price of $159.99 / £139.99 / AU$249.95, and it’s not possible for me to recommend this. If you feel like you’d benefit from the touchscreen, and you don’t mind a heavier feel, then it could be worth grabbing this one on sale, but otherwise, I’d be much more tempted by top-tier rivals like the Steelseries Rival Pro and Logitech G Pro X2 Superstrike — both of which are expensive too, but do more to justify that fact.

## Buy it if…

**You want excellent battery life** The MC7 uses a brilliant hot-swappable battery system that essentially gives you unlimited playtime. One battery will slot onto the transmitter and charge up while you’re gaming, and when your mouse eventually dies, you can substitute it right in. Pretty convenient, right?**You’re all in on the touchscreen**  
For me, the touchscreen on the MC7 is unnecessary — most of the functions it performs could be mapped to a keyboard or physical buttons. But if you disagree, and you think the screen will benefit you greatly, then it might just be worth snapping the MC7 up, though I’d still advise you to hold out for a sale.

## Don’t buy it if…

**You want something lightweight** At 4.8oz / 135g, the MC7 is a pretty hefty lad, and it's fairly large too. While it feels relatively comfortable to grip, I much prefer the lighter feel of rivals such as the Steelseries Rival Pro, which you can read more about in the ‘also consider’ section.**You want the best value for money**  
The MC7 is expensive, but as I’ve illustrated throughout this review, it doesn’t do enough to live up to its price. I feel that the two options below — despite being a touch pricier — actually offer you far better bang for your buck, with awesome specs, but also great designs.
