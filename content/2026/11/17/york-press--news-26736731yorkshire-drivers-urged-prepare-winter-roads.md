## Emergency services issue winter road safety warning

The North East Ambulance Service (NEAS) has teamed up with police, fire, and highways partners to share practical advice during Road Safety Week *(Image: North East Ambulance Service)*

Emergency services are urging road users across Yorkshire to prepare for winter journeys as part of a seasonal safety campaign.

The North East Ambulance Service (NEAS) has teamed up with police, fire, and highways partners to share practical advice during Road Safety Week, which runs from November 15 to 21, to help people stay safe during darker nights and winter weather.

The campaign, part of NEAS’s wider ‘NEAS: Be winter ready’ campaign, aims to keep communities well and prepared for the challenges of the colder months.

Paul Purvis, clinical team leader at NEAS, said: "Being winter ready means planning ahead and making sure you and your vehicle are prepared before you travel.

"Check the weather and road conditions, make sure your tyres and lights are in good condition, and give yourself extra time.

"If severe weather is forecast, consider whether your journey is necessary."

Road safety is a core focus of the campaign after NEAS attended more than 1,300 road traffic incidents between October 2025 and February 2026.

People are being reminded to avoid travelling while distracted, under the influence of alcohol or drugs, or excessively tired.

Mr Purvis said: "Darkness and poor weather can make hazards harder to see, so everyone using the roads should take extra care.

"Cyclists, pedestrians, and runners should also make themselves as visible as possible by using appropriate lights and wearing bright or reflective clothing."

According to figures from road safety charity Brake, nearly 30,000 people were killed or seriously injured on Britain’s roads in 2025, with pedestrians and cyclists making up one in four fatalities.

NEAS covers 3,230 square miles and 2.7 million people across the North East and works closely with police, fire services, National Highways, and other partners to deliver safety advice and respond to incidents.

National Highways, which maintains and manages 670 miles of motorways and A roads across the North East and Yorkshire, is supporting the campaign through its T.R.I.P. messaging, which encourages drivers to check tyres, fluids, and travel plans before setting off.

Colin Stevenson, road safety information lead at National Highways, said: "Winter weather can arrive suddenly, bringing heavy rain, strong winds, snow, ice and poor visibility.

"By following our simple T.R.I.P. advice, including checking tyres, topping up essential fluids and planning ahead, motorists can help reduce the risk of a breakdown and keep themselves and others moving safely this winter."

Inspector Phil Patterson of Northumbria Police said drivers should adjust their behaviour for winter hazards.

He said: "Winter brings darker days, slippery roads and unpredictable conditions.

"Drivers should reduce their speed where appropriate, allow more time for their journey and remain alert for hazards such as black ice.

"Before setting off, fully clear snow and ice from your vehicle and make sure your windscreen is properly defrosted.

"Keep warm clothing and essential supplies in your car in case you break down."

Paul Russell, community safety area manager at Tyne and Wear Fire Rescue Service, urged drivers to ensure their eyesight is up to standard.

He said: "Road users should ensure they have regular eye tests and that their eyesight complies with DVSA standards to help keep themselves and others safe on our roads."

Further winter travel advice is available on the NEAS winter safety advice webpage.
