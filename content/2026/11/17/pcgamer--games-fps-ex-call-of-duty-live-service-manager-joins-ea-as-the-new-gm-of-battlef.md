A few things come to mind when I think about what the greatest fear of a Battlefield player is. It could be a glint atop the mountains on Liberation Peak, reliving the launch of Battlefield 2042, or maybe it's the randomised recoil from the first Battlefield. All of these are good bets, but I think the real shared nightmare of most Battlefield players is that someday it'll morph into Call of Duty.

To be fair, this fear isn't entirely unfounded. Bryan Beede was previously appointed as Battlefield's general manager after spending 19 years working on Call of Duty and has now been promoted to group general manager at EA. Taking his place is another Call of Duty veteran, Sam Cooper, who worked as Call of Duty's global product management and head of live service.

![battlefield 6](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/5bEYmhQutSmN4J2CprVJtf-1200-80.jpg)

"I'm excited to share that I've joined Electronic Arts as general manager of Battlefield," Cooper says in a LinkedIn post. "I started my games career at EA on the FIFA team, so coming back to where it all began feels extra special.

"Battlefield is an iconic franchise that has something no other shooter has: all-out warfare, destruction at massive scale, squads play, and a passionate community that has stuck with this franchise for more than two decades."

Now this in and of itself isn't enough to sound any alarm bells for players concerned whether or not Battlefield can stay true to form, especially since Beede was appointed as Battlefield GM back in 2021 prior to Battlefield 6's release. But this alongside some questionable new skins has some Battlefield players worried that Battlefield is becoming more "en-cod-ified".

A big talking point during Battlefield 6's launch was players begging the devs to ignore COD's trend of gimmicky skins and instead stay true to its in-game realism for cosmetics. This has largely been the case—although the Dead Space collab was a near miss. But now some believe the cracks are starting to show with the new Fox Hunter skin pack.

"There's absolutely no way you can defend this," one player says in response to the skin pack. "There's a pseudo furry skin in Battlefield now. The defense I've heard is that 'it's plausible', while these are plausible, the masks are just, no? What tactical purpose does wearing a Japanese baby mask [or] a furry mask serve?" I'd argue, however, that it could stun an enemy Battlefield player long enough for you to secure a kill, and therefore there's at least some tactical purpose to it.
