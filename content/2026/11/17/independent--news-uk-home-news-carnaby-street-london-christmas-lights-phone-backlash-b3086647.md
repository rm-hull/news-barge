The internet has reacted with outrage after footage of the Carnaby Street Christmas lights switch on showed a sea of mobile phones for the occasion.

Each year, hundreds of people gather on London’s busiest shopping streets - Oxford Circus, Regent Street and Carnaby Street - to watch the Christmas lights get switched on. But this year, video shared on social media attracted attention not because of the decorations but because of the number of people staring at the display through their mobile phones.

Video shared on social media showed dozens of people crowded on Carnaby Street with their phones lit up to record the moment the lights were switched on, with fireworks on display, earlier this month.

People took to the comments to criticise those present for being on their phones rather than taking in the light display with their eyes.

"Remember when we used to look at stuff directly with our eyes? Those were the good old days," said one comment. Another described the scene as “such a sad sad sight.”

![Footage of people filming the lights has sparked criticism](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/16/10/24/carnaby-street-lights-b5a8b2d1.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Former EastEnders actress Natalie Cassidy commented: "Why is this piece not mentioning the fact that no-one is looking through their eyes at this beautiful display? It's really scary and depressing."

Another added: “Christ that's depressing seeing all those automatons watching it through their phones”.

As smartphones have become more available, it’s become an increasingly common phenomenon that people watch events through their phone cameras.

So much so that studies have suggested that humans subconsciously rely on the camera to do the work of remembering instead of their brain, which prevents us from memorising events accurately.

Dr Kaitlyn Regehr, associate professor in the Digital Humanities from University College London and the University of Toronto, told the BBC that while the phenomenon of people watching events through their phones was not new, the outrage was.

“[What's] significant is that this year there's a lot of critical public discourse happening. And that, I think, is really, really positive," she said, adding that the comments "say a lot about where we are as a society at the moment.”

![Carnaby Street is known for its Christmas lights display](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/06/18/13/2250440432..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Specifically, she told the broadcaster it showed "our discomfort with the amount of influence that big tech is playing on our lives".

The controversy comes alongside a change in attitude to technology, with a social media ban coming into place for under 16-year-olds in the UK from March 2027.

On Monday, the Tories pledged to halt “screen-based” exams and cut coursework in a bid to limit the use of AI by schoolchildren if the party returned to power.

Shadow education secretary Laura Trott said:“We have sleepwalked into a situation where nearly a million children spend almost every lesson on a screen, while medical professionals warn of the damage excessive screen time can do to children’s health, development and learning.

“I want to slash screen use in classrooms and set a new norm where screens are no longer the default,” she added. “Everything in schools must be safe for children and improve standards.

“We’ve forced the government to get under-16s off social media and ban smartphones from the classroom, now we need to reduce screen time.”
