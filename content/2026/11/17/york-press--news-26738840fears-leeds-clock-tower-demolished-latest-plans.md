The future of a historic newspaper clock tower remains uncertain after the city council confirmed it could be knocked down.

The iconic clock and temperature gauge structure is still standing on Wellington Street after the former home of Yorkshire Post Newspapers was demolished.

Urbanite Leeds Ltd secured planning permission in 2023 to turn the site into student flats.

Office buildings of 15 and 17 storeys are now planned for the site after the original scheme ran into complications.

Leeds Civic Trust was opposed to demolition of the tower, which fell into disrepair after the land was vacated by the Yorkshire Post and Evening Post in 2012.

Civic trust director Martin Hamilton said: “Our feeling is, if at all possible, it should remain on site.”

Mr Hamilton said options could include moving it elsewhere on site or the tower being integrated into one of the planned buildings.

He said: “I think to lose it completely would be a shame.”

In its design documents, Urbanite said a new interpretation of the clock tower could see digital displays show the time and temperature on the façade of the office buildings.

Labour MP Jon Trickett, a former Leeds City Council leader, also called for the tower to be preserved.

He said: “Thousands of people pass it every single day. It was always there. It felt familiar.

“You can’t stop progress but you have got to try to preserve things like that, which seems to me a symbol of that part of Leeds.”

Leeds City Council said Urbanite’s latest planning application for the Wellington Street site was still under consideration.

A spokesperson said: “As with the previously approved scheme, the application proposes the removal of the existing clock tower, which is understood to be in poor condition, while retaining the clock and temperature gauge as part of the redevelopment.

“We recognise the clock tower’s heritage significance and its importance to many people in the city, while also acknowledging its current poor condition.

“We also understand the applicant is considering options to improve the appearance of the tower in the meantime.”
