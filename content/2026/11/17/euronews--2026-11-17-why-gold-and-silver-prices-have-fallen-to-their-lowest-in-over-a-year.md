## Gold and silver prices have slumped to their lowest levels in more than a year as a strong US dollar, high bond yields and bets on another US interest rate hike sap demand. The gold price is on track for its biggest annual fall since 2015.

Gold and silver prices sank to their lowest levels in more than a year on Monday, as a strong US dollar, rising bond yields and bets on another US rate hike in December eroded the appeal of precious metals.

The gold price is now heading for its biggest annual drop since 2015.

Spot gold fell as low as $3,832 an ounce on Monday, its weakest level since October 2025, while silver dropped to $46.50 an ounce.

On Tuesday, both metals’ prices rebounded from Monday’s lows. Gold rose about 0.75% to $3,861 an ounce, while silver climbed just over 2% to $47.50 an ounce.

## A rates trade, not a safe haven

Gold pays no interest, so higher rates make it less attractive, as investors can earn more on bonds and savings.

The US Federal Reserve raised rates in September for the first time since 2023, and markets now fully expect another hike in December, according to CME Group's FedWatch tool, which tracks traders' bets.

The yield on 10-year US government bonds has climbed to around 5%, and on Tuesday the US dollar reached its strongest level since April 2025, making metals priced in US dollars costlier for buyers holding other currencies.

The Iran war has not helped either.

Rather than acting as a safe haven, gold has suffered as the conflict drove oil prices and inflation higher, which in turn fuelled bets on higher interest rates.

It is a sharp reversal from January, when gold hit a record of about $5,600 an ounce, and silver peaked at around $121.65 an ounce, before both crashed at a time of escalating geopolitical tensions in Venezuela and Iran, and after US President Donald Trump picked Kevin Warsh to lead the Fed.

Gold has since lost more than 30% from its peak and is down over 10% since the start of the year.

Silver, which tends to swing even more sharply than gold, has dropped about 60% from its peak and is down about 34% since the start of the year.

## What comes next

Gold edged higher in early trading on Tuesday as the US dollar and bond yields eased slightly, but expectations of a December hike capped the rebound.

Investors now await Wednesday's release of the minutes from the Fed's October meeting for clues on how high rates could go.

Warsh did not address the December decision when he spoke at an event on AI at the San Francisco Fed on Monday.

US investment bank Goldman Sachs has turned more cautious, cutting its forecasts earlier this month. It now sees gold at $4,150 an ounce at the end of the year, according to a note, which is still above current prices.

That would require gold to climb roughly 7.5% from the current level until the end of 2026.

Central banks also remain a source of support for demand. Central banks and other official institutions bought a net 675 tonnes of gold between January and September, 6% more than a year earlier, according to the World Gold Council, an industry body.
