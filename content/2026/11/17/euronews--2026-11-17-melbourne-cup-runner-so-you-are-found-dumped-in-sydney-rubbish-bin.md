## The gelding, who had won over €800,000 in prize money, was put down after a severe bout of colic, and his trainer wants a full explanation.

The body of a racehorse that ran in the Melbourne Cup this month has been found in an industrial waste bin near Sydney, Australia, and the equine hospital responsible for it has apologised.

So You Are (seen on the right in the cover photograph), a five-year-old gelding who finished eighth in the race on 3 November, was put down earlier this week after a severe bout of colic, local media reported.

A member of the public came across the carcass about two weeks after the horse ran in Australia’s best-known race.

Sired by So You Think, the gelding was bred and raced by Stuart Ramsey’s Ramsey Pastoral. He won eight of his 29 starts and earned A$1.4 million (€860,000) in prize money.

Trainer Bjorn Baker said on Tuesday that he was “deeply distressed” by the discovery.

“Like everyone who has seen the footage I am deeply distressed by the handling of So You Are’s remains,” Baker said in a statement posted on social media.

“He was a much-loved horse and he deserves to be treated with respect and dignity after his death.

“I understand the specialist hospital facility REC Equine Specialists are investigating the circumstances and I expect a full explanation of what occurred.”

REC Equine Specialists, the veterinary practice that treated the horse, apologised and said So You Are should have been given a respectful burial, according to national broadcaster ABC.

“After his death So You Are was collected by our contracted service provider and taken to a licensed landfill. He should have been promptly and respectfully buried,” the company said.

“What happened was unacceptable and disrespectful to So You Are and to everyone else who cared for him.

“We are deeply sorry.”
