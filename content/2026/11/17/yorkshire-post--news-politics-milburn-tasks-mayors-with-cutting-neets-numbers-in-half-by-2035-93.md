# Alan Milburn tasks mayors with cutting Neets numbers in half by 2035

## Alan Milburn will publish his widely trailed report today to tackle the crisis which has seen almost a million young people not in education, employment or training.

Yorkshire’s mayors will be tasked with getting young people into work, education or training under major reforms aimed at making “work not welfare the future for the next generation”.

The number of young people across the UK not in education, employment or training – known as Neets – topped a million earlier this year for the first time in more than a decade.

This is the highest rate in Europe after Romania, making Britain a huge outlier in youth disengagement.

One-time Labour health secretary Alan Milburn has previously warned the figure could rise from one in eight to one in six by 2031 without action, representing 1.25m young people.

It estimated the crisis is costing the UK around £125bn a year – taking in factors including losses in taxes alongside higher health and welfare spending.

In Bradford, Doncaster and Barnsley, almost one in five 16 to 24-year-olds are classed as Neet, according to the Centre for Cities.

Mr Milburn will publish his widely trailed report today and is expected to insist radical change is needed, arguing “youth participation has to run through public policy like a stick of rock”.

He has already acknowledged his proposals would not be “pain-free”, giving a candid warning that they could lead to some people getting less financial support.

He will say: “A lost generation is not a fact of life. It is a choice. And I believe it is the wrong one.

“Not just because it is a fiscal crisis or an economic crisis. But because it is a moral one.

![Alan Milburn, Prime Minister Andy Burnham and Education Secretary Lucy Powell during a visit to Alstom Transport in Derby in July (PA)](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/jpim-static/image/2026/11/16/00/v2-image-production-9d28fe439a2bad26e27f5446c4381cf6Y29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzk0ODcxMzg2-2.85437364.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


“We are failing as a nation to gift the next generation the opportunities they need to succeed.

“It is time for a new settlement that makes work not welfare the future for the next generation.”

His report will set out a national ambition to reduce the Neet rate by more than half from 13 per cent to around 5 per cent by 2036, which would make the UK one of Europe’s best performers on youth participation.

Other recommendations include gradually cutting national insurance paid by businesses on workers aged under 25, starting in retail and hospitality, as well as a “legal guarantee” of work, training or employment support for young people, with those who refuse to take up the offer potentially losing benefits.

A new so-called youth opportunity service – across all of England by 2029 – will aim to ensure employment, careers, health and practical support are available to a young person, with a personalised participation plan and a named adviser.

An opportunity guarantee would offer a route into education, training or work, while a support guarantee would provide any health, employment and practical help needed alongside it.

This will be led by mayors, who will be responsible for delivering multi-year participation agreements.

She has called for Prime Minister Andy Burnham to devolve education for people aged 16 to 19 and responsibilities for employment.

“We will not write this generation off. Give mayors responsibility for their futures, and we’ll make sure every young person gets a fair chance to flourish,” Ms Brabin says.
