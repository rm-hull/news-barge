Prince William warned that the effects of climate change are becoming clear to see, but also delivered the upbeat message that positive action on climate change would become "irreversible" as he announced the winners of his annual environmental awards on Tuesday.

At a glittering awards ceremony in Mumbai for the Earthshot Prize, the heir to the British throne said repeated heatwaves and devastating floods were evidence that the impacts of climate change were clear, calling it a pivotal moment for the planet.

"We cannot look into the eyes of the next generation and tell them that we knew what was happening, we knew what was at stake, and yet we did not do everything in our power to change it," he said.

"They will not judge us by the promises we made, but by the world we leave behind. And they deserve a generation prepared to act with urgency, determination and optimism."

## Prize held during Cop31 talks

William, 44, set up Earthshot six years ago to find practical solutions to climate change.

Five winners receive £1 million each to scale up their projects.

The latest ceremony coincided with the UN's COP31 climate talks, which have highlighted the difficulty in finding agreement between countries over fossil fuels and climate financing.

![Prince William, Prince of Wales and Catherine, Princess of Wales pose with Gary Barlow, Mark Owen and Howard Donald of the band Take That and guests during the Earthshot Prize awards night](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/17/17/2305071799..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

A UN report last month said the world is already on course to exceed the 1.5C (2.7 degrees Fahrenheit) warming threshold, a level scientists say should limit the worst effects of climate change.

Despite the gloomy background, William said he believed people were rising to meet the challenge.

He heralded the innovation of the latest Earthshot Prize winners as a testament to the ability to produce answers to the growing challenges.

The winners included Mumbai-based electric vehicle maker Mahindra Last Mile Mobility.

![Catherine, Princess of Wales talks with guests during the Earthshot Prize awards night](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/17/17/2305072018..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The Kingdom of Bhutan also won for requiring 60 per cent of its land to stay under forest cover.

"I choose to believe that this will be the decade where positive change becomes irreversible," William said. "To our finalists, and to everyone choosing action over apathy, thank you for proving that change is possible."

Bollywood filmmaker Karan Johar hosted the ceremony.

The prince was joined there by Indian cricketing great Sachin Tendulkar, actors Alia Bhatt and Rami Malek, and British pop group Take ‌That, ⁠who gave their first-ever performance in India.

## Kate joins William

William's wife Kate also presented one of the awards.

The visit is her most high-profile overseas trip since receiving treatment for cancer in 2024.

"The people we’re celebrating tonight are a huge part of that story," said the princess, 44, often referred to by her maiden name, Kate Middleton.

![Prince William drives an electric rickshaw with Catherine, Princess of Wales, seated behind him as they arrive for a group photograph with finalists of the Earthshot Prize](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/17/17/download..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

"Their work will inspire and create seismic change that will improve lives for generations to come."

Earlier, William drove Kate in a Mahindra electric auto-rickshaw through Mumbai, India's financial capital.

The drive came before a meeting with the Earthshot finalists.

Their three-day visit concludes on Wednesday, when they will meet Prime Minister Narendra Modi in New Delhi.
