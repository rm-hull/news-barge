Andy Burnham has vowed to negotiate to “the last minute” after EU sources revealed a crucial deal on youth mobility is “going down to the wire” ahead of the crunch summit in Brussels.

With the prime minister set to arrive in the EU capital on Friday for an already delayed summit on a Brexit reset, it is understood talks have hit crisis point over setting up a visa scheme for under-30s to travel and temporarily live and work in the EU and UK.

The block appears to be agreeing a total cap with the Commission – with an added complication that EU member states technically have individual control over how many can come to each country.

The UK has also delayed finalising an agreement on tuition fees for EU students studying in the UK in a bid to create leverage for a later agreement on the Made in Europe manufacturing legislation.

It comes as exclusive polling seen by The Independent has revealed that Labour’s hopes of winning the next election will be hugely boosted by a promise to rejoin the EU.

The GB Omnibus poll of 7,291 adults for Fortius Global (formerly the Messina Group), shows Labour’s support rose by 7.2 percentage points when voters were presented with a scenario in which it backed rejoining the EU.

Labour reached 31.5 per cent among the total electorate in the rejoin scenario, compared with 24.3 per cent in the baseline voting-intention question.

Overall, the poll showed that 57 per cent support opening EU membership negotiations compared to 26.8 per cent who are opposed.

On a key issue for Reform UK and Nigel Farage, 53 per cent say Brexit has been bad for immigration, while 43.3 per cent agree that the immigration system was better when Britain was in the EU compared to less than 20 per cent who disagree.

The attempts to get a youth mobility scheme over the line have bedevilled both the Keir Starmer and Andy Burnham Labour governments.

Similar schemes already operate with other jurisdictions where an agreed number of under-30s can live and work for two years and then extend their stay for a further year.

A youth mobility scheme has been seen as essential to right one of the wrongs of Brexit which has seen opportunities for young people in the UK and EU restricted by no longer being able to travel and work in each jurisdiction.

Asked about the failure to agree a scheme with just three days to go until the summit, a Downing Street spokesperson said: “We will keep working to the last minute.”

A senior EU source explained that part of the issue was that, while the UK and EU are attempting to agree on global figures, the jurisdiction on the issue resides in member states.

The source said: “It is coming up to the wire on youth mobility because I think from the UK perspective, understandably, they are nervous about signing a deal with the Commission that is in relation to a member state competences.”

They said that the EU “would be very surprised if it's not agreed [on Friday]” but reiterated: “Negotiations are going to the wire.”

Mike Galsworthy, chair of the European Movement UK, urged Mr Burnham and EU to be willing to compromise on the issue.

He said: "One thing that you cannot deny is that Andy Burnham is really going for it. That is absolutely the right approach with the Commission and member states, because they can see, and will appreciate, that he is sticking his neck right out on this one.

“I really do hope that they can respond in kind, because landing a deal here on youth mobility, whatever the cap, very clearly sets a foundation for future work. Being bold and energetic now with pay dividends for all future engagements."

The deal will see agreements on food and drink imports with border checks removed in exchange for the UK aligning with EU regulations with a sanitary and phytosanitary (SPS) rules deal.

The source said discussions were still ongoing over what access the UK would have to discussions on new regulations, given it is not a member state.

But, while the EU has been pushing for an agreement on tuition fees to end EU students having to pay a premium rate, the source noted it was unlikely to be agreed by Friday.

“There is still back and forth on it,” the source said.

“The UK was very keen that tuition fees as a decision was separated out and de-linked from the overall SPS deal. I think that's pretty much almost certainly going to happen.

“The concept of separating out tuition fees from the SPS agreement is what happens with Made in Europe and assurances the UK gets on that as a trusted partner.”

They noted this is linked to the Industrial Accelerator Act currently in the European Parliament which has 5,000 amendments laid down already.

Meanwhile, the EU has parked the idea of the UK using the summit on Friday as a launchpad for a new relationship taking whatever deal is signed as an entity in itself.

They are looking at Mr Burnham to have a “national conversation” over four future options including the current Brexit status quo, customs union membership, joining the single market or rejoining the bloc altogether.

As a result they are not expecting Mr Burnham to call an early election to give himself time to work out the options but they believe that the UK will only be able to look at changing its status after an election.

“The [Starmer] red lines [on not rejoining the EU, customs union or single market] still exist and a change would need to be put to voters in an election manifesto,” the source.

However, it is understood Mr Burnham has lost patience with the status quo and plans to push forward with legislation to align with the EU and bring the latest deal into law as soon as next week.

Pro-EU campaigners believe Mr Burnham’s announcement that legislation to bring in the deal agreed in Brussels will go ahead next week reveals the prime minister’s impatience to go further and faster on improving our relationship with Europe.

The PM told his cabinet on Tuesday that, when it came to Europe, his government was showing leadership on big issues that “can build a more hopeful feeling across the country,” No 10 said.

But Downing Street also suggested Andy Burnham will not set out options for the UK’s future relationship with the European Union before the end of this year, and that he will discuss them with the bloc first.

EU leaders have taken notice of Mr Burnham’s Mansion House on Monday evening where he again blasted Brexit and the isolation it has created.

Mr Burnham vowed his government would take a new and more collaborative approach to foreign policy, stating that Britain is “back in the game in a big way”.

“Looking back, Brexit was the first domino to fall in a global move towards more isolationism and protectionism, vested interests rather than values, driving international affairs,” he said.

“And the saddest thing of all is this: it is exactly what our enemies wanted,” he added. “Russia wanted disunity within NATO and a weaker Europe, less able to counter its plans for Ukraine.”
