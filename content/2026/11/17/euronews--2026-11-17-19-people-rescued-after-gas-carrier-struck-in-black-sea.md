## It marks the latest in a string of similar incidents in the Black Sea believed to be linked to Russia's invasion of Ukraine.

All 19 crew members were rescued after a gas carrier was "struck" in the Black Sea off Bulgaria on Tuesday, authorities and the ship's management company said.

Stealth Maritime, the Greek management company, confirmed in a statement that the Gas Husky had been "struck" early on Tuesday morning as it was sailing within Bulgaria's exclusive economic zone.

It did not clarify what had hit the Liberian-flagged carrier.

"The vessel was carrying a cargo of LPG loaded in Alexandria, Egypt, and was heading to Midia, Romania, for discharge," it said, adding that the ship's condition was currently unknown.

According to Bulgaria's ministry of transport, the vessel was carrying 3,400 tonnes of liquefied petroleum gas (LPG) before it disappeared from radar shortly after midnight. It then sent a distress signal at around 8:02 am, around 50 nautical miles east of Byala.

"Upon receiving the distress signal, assistance was requested from the air force to conduct an aerial survey of the area. During the survey, an overturned vessel was identified, believed to be the Gas Husky vessel," the ministry said in a statement. "About 300 metres away, a life raft was spotted, from which a distress flare was launched."

The crew were rescued from the life raft and flown by helicopter to the Chaika air base in Varna, it added.

It marks the latest in a string of similar incidents in the Black Sea believed to be linked to Russia's invasion of Ukraine.

On Monday, 12 people were rescued after the Somali-flagged Betul 8 cargo ship capsized off the coast of Bulgaria following a suspected drone strike.

The Betul 8 had been travelling from Tulcea in Romania to Egypt and was carrying almost 3,000 tonnes of bulk wheat, authorities said.

The European Union has pledged €50 million for a Black Sea Maritime Security Hub hosted by Bulgaria and Romania to help combat security threats in the region.

The bloc has also committed a further €10 million in funds to help bolster Bulgaria's search and rescue operations.
