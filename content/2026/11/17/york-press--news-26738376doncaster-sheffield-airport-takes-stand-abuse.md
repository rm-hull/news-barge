A new safety charter is being introduced to help protect aviation workers from abuse at a Yorkshire airport.

Doncaster Sheffield Airport (DSA) is the first in the UK to fully adopt Unite’s Aviation Worker Safety Charter, which aims to tackle abuse from passengers and improve workplace safety for aviation staff.

DSA, which is expected to reopen in spring 2028, has committed to a zero-tolerance approach to worker abuse once it is operational.

The charter calls for abuse to be recognised as a serious safety issue rather than dismissed as customer frustration or simply "part of the job."

It also calls for clear reporting systems and proper employer support for affected workers.

Sharon Graham, general secretary of Unite, said: "This is a significant moment in the campaign to stamp out abuse of aviation workers once and for all.

"Abuse is not simply 'part of the job', it puts workers and passengers at risk."

The charter will be signed at a ceremony on Wednesday, November 18, at the airport, with Unite officials in attendance.

The move supports Unite’s Abuse Doesn’t Fly campaign, which found that most airport staff experience abuse at work.

A recent survey by the union found 72 per cent of ground staff and 71 per cent of cabin crew report verbal abuse at least once a month.

Physical assaults were reported by 31 per cent of ground staff and 42 per cent of cabin crew.

Alcohol was identified as the leading cause of passenger abuse, cited by 95 per cent of cabin crew and 55 per cent of ground staff.

Other triggers included flight delays, cancellations, and baggage problems.

Jon Moorcroft, organiser at Unite, said: "Unite is very pleased to announce that Doncaster Sheffield Airport is leading the way by signing our Charter.

"This sets an important precedent."

The Aviation Bill currently progressing through Parliament would allow the Civil Aviation Authority to ban disruptive passengers across multiple airlines.

However, this power would only apply to incidents that occur on board aircraft.

Unite is calling for these powers to be extended to cover abuse that occurs in airports, allowing staff to intervene before passengers board their flights.

The union also wants new laws to ban violent and abusive passengers from airports entirely.
