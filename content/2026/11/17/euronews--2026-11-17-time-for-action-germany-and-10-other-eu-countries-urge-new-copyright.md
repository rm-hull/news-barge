## Germany and 10 other EU countries urge the Commission to propose new legislation protecting copyright in the age of AI, ahead of a Culture Ministers’ meeting on Thursday.

Germany and 10 other member states are urging the European Commission to table new legislation to safeguard copyrighted works in the age of artificial intelligence.

Germany, Spain, Poland, Austria, Cyprus, Croatia, Denmark, Estonia, Portugal, Slovakia and Slovenia have thrown their weight behind stronger protection for the cultural and creative sector as AI technologies advance.

“We call on the European Commission to present a corresponding legislative proposal as soon as possible,” reads a note the 11 countries submitted for the agenda of the Culture Ministers’ meeting on Thursday and Friday.

Advanced AI systems such as large language models are trained on trillions of data points, scraped from the open internet among other sources, often with little regard for whether the material is protected by copyright.

For the tech sector, restricting access to copyrighted material would leave Europe further behind the US and China in the AI race. For the creative and cultural industries, current practices amount to mass theft and undermine their livelihoods.

“Since AI has moved from pure research into broad practical application, it has led to profound upheavals in human creative processes and the perception and experience of art and culture. Its extent is becoming increasingly clear, and the need for action more urgent,” the document continues.

Balancing technological innovation with the protection of human-created content also divides national governments, where economy ministries tend to side with start-ups and culture ministries with creators.

The initiative is the latest push by the supporters’ culture ministries for a new legislative tool to shield copyright holders from the data scraping of AI developers. It follows a similar call in May by eight countries, showing the initiative is gaining support.

“The aim is to protect human creative work while enabling AI innovation in Europe. European innovation and AI development require fair competition and a level playing field with non-European providers, irrespective of where the AI was developed and, in particular, where it was trained,” the document states.

The Commission is due to present a legislative package on digital transformation, simplification and copyright in the second quarter of 2027, according to its work programme.

Earlier this month, the EU executive closed a public consultation on possible measures to strengthen copyright protection, including the use of protected content in AI.

The EU already has a Copyright Directive, which allows creators to opt out of having their work used to develop AI. In the note, the countries call for swift action to make that opt-out easier to enforce.
