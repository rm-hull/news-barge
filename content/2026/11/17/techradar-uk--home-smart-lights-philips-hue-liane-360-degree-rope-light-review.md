### Philips Hue Liane 360º Rope Light: two minute review

The Philips Hue Liane 360º Rope Light is the most stunning Hue light to date, taking Philips' impressive, seamless OmniGlow technology and turning it into a very bright rope light that emits white and colored light in all directions. It's architectural lighting rather than functional or task lighting: this is a light made to be looked at, whether that's in a huge contemporary kitchen or on the wall of a hip and cosy café.

The Liane Rope Light delivers everything that's great about Hue's OmniGlow Lightstrip, which uses miniLEDs to deliver a completely seamless light without visible LEDs spoiling the illusion, and it extends that light in all directions so it can be located anywhere. One Liane Rope Light is striking; multiple ones in different colors are stunning.

Once connected to a Hue hub you can use the Hue app to create light recipes and automation either for the Liane on its own or as part of a wider Hue setup. Hue's technology is well established and its app is highly polished, so setting up your Liane and getting it just-so is very straightforward.

The Liane Rope Light is the most beautiful Hue light yet. But the Hue OmniGlow is likely to be a better buy for most due to its much lower price.

* **3m/10ft: $399.99 / £379.99 (about AU$740)**
* **5m/16ft: $529.99 / £469.99 (about AU$920)**
* **Launched September 2026**

![Hue Liane 360&amp;ordm; Rope Light being tested draped across wall mounted guitars](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/ULMh5Ld3YQ4VLgMdGJfk39-1200-80.jpg)

The Philips Hue Liane 360º Rope Light was unveiled in September 2026 and is available in two lengths, 3m (10ft) and 5m (16ft). The 3m has an list price of $399.99 / £379.99 (about AU$740), and the 5m is $529.99 / £469.99 (about AU$920).

That's roughly three times the price of the equivalent OmniGlow lightstrip, but the OmniGlow lightstrip is single-sided. The Liane 360º emits light in all directions at once: it's effectively two OmniGlow lightstrips back to back, which is why it costs so much.

Although the Liane Rope Light is expensive for a consumer lighting product I suspect it'll find a home in commercial premises such as shops, bars, hotels, cafés and so on thanks to its neon-esque brightness, its controllability and its flexibility: being able to do colors and gradients is a big plus compared to other rope lighting. Commercial lighting is much less price-sensitive than lights for your living room.

### Philips Hue Liane 360º Rope Light review: design

* **Featureless white silicone**
* **Seamless colors and smooth gradients**
* **Complete with mounting accessories**

![Hue Liane 360&amp;ordm; Rope Light being tested draped across wall mounted guitars](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/4XrjV7czxRebSDkoBJkf39-1200-80.jpg)

If you've ever seen one of those 'painting with light'photographs, the Liane Rope Light looks like that: it resembles a really thick halogen filament or a neon light floating in space. Using this as a traditional lightstrip feels like a waste of its potential: I'd say it's best thought of as architectural lighting, a talking point rather than task or ambient lighting.

The Liane is designed to be seen, and to look good both on and off. Switched off it's a completely featureless, flexible white silicone tube with a diameter of 22mm and a cable to the power supply on one end. Philips also includes discreet transparent mounts so you can hang the Liane from the ceiling or mount it across a wall without the mounts spoiling the Liane's looks. The only downside to the white silicone is that it's a material that attracts dust, dirt and dog hair very easily, and that dust is quite visible when it's powered off or brightly lit.

This is an OmniGlow light, and that means unlike cheaper lightstrips there aren't any visible, distinct LEDs: it uses microLEDs to deliver unbroken illumination. Installation is simple, and the Hue app also tells you how to put the parts of your light together and how to hang it from the ceiling. The Liane we tested came with a ceiling hook for easy installation, a weight for the other end, and a bag of transparent clips for hanging it from walls and other surfaces.

Like other OmniGlow lightstrips, the Liane can be cut to reduce its size but it can't be extended. It's rated IP20, which means it's designed for dry indoor environments only.

**Design score: 5/5**

### Philips Hue Liane 360º Rope Light: performance

* **Stunning colors and gradients**
* **Up to 3,750 lumens (5m)**
* **Wide range of colors and temperatures**

![Hue Liane 360&amp;ordm; Rope Light being tested draped across wall mounted guitars](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/2Lwwzqn3XwgmUPv3Jb4o39-1200-80.jpg)

Length: 3m or 5m

Mounting options: Ceiling, wall

Bulb type: Dimmable gradient coloured and white light (RGB1CW)

Colour temperature: 1,000-20,000K

Output (4000K): 2,250 lumens (3m) / 3,750 lumens (5m)

Output (2700K): 1,950 lumens (3m) / 3,250 lumens (5m)

Wattage: 60W

Zigbee repeater: Yes

The Hue Liane 360º Rope Light is capable of very high brightness: the 5m version can put out up to 3,750 lumens of brightness at a color temperature of 4,000K, which is the sweet spot between warm white and cool white if you like a halogen-style warmth. It can deliver color temperatures between 1,000K and 20,000K, and it's capable of subtle effects such as emulating a flickering campfire or a running a subtle color-changing loop.

When I first tried the Liane Rope Light there were some issues with the motion effects and color transitions, with visible banding rather than the smooth movement I've already enjoyed in the OmniGlow lightstrip. The culprit appears to have been a software or firmware bug, as updating the Liane's software made the banding go away. Once updated the lighting was completely seamless, with colors blending happily and motion effects coming and going smoothly.

The Liane Rope Light responds quickly to commands from the app, and you can group it with other Hue lights and bulbs for multi-light scenes and for controlling via your voice assistant; I used Apple's Siri but Hue also works with Alexa, Google Assistant and Sonos Voice Control.

**Performance score: 5/5**

## Buy it if...

**You've got great style** The Liane Rope Light is more of a statement than a smart light: it's made to be seen, and to attract attention.**You're so modern** Smart rope lighting is always going to be a bit Blade Runner, and that might be incongruous if installed in a non-contemporary space.**You've got cash to splash**

There's no getting away from it: this is really expensive compared to an ordinary smart lightstrip.

## Don't buy it if...

**You're on a budget** Normal OmniGlow lightstrips are a third of the price, and basic Hue lightstrips are even cheaper.**You've got a small space** 3m of rope lighting might not sound big, but it's taller than most ceilings.**You're a hermit**

The Liane Rope Light is a feature, a talking point rather than an ordinary light. Using it for ordinary illumination that others won't see is a waste of its talents.
