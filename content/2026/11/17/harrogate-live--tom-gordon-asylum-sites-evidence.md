Tom Gordon MP wants ministers to publish suitability assessments and costs for asylum sites such as Linton-on-Ouse. The amendments return on 17 November.

Harrogate and Knaresborough MP Tom Gordon has urged the government to back amendments that would force ministers to publish site suitability assessments, cost estimates and a financial breakdown for asylum accommodation sites, including the former RAF Linton-on-Ouse in North Yorkshire.

Mr Gordon, the Liberal Democrat MP, made the call in the House of Commons on Monday 16 November, during an Urgent Question on plans for MOD Bicester secured by fellow Liberal Democrat MP Calum Miller. Mr Miller’s amendments come back at Report Stage of the Immigration and Asylum Bill on Tuesday 17 November.

## Overview

* **What the amendments would do:** make ministers publish site suitability assessments, cost estimates and a financial breakdown for asylum accommodation sites
* **The local site:** the former RAF Linton-on-Ouse, where Mr Gordon says the same questions apply as at Bicester
* **When:** the amendments return at Report Stage of the Immigration and Asylum Bill on Tuesday 17 November
* **The government’s position:** the minister said the amendments should be debated fully, but that it “may not be able to publish everything at every stage”

## What was said in the Chamber

Speaking in the Chamber, Mr Gordon said: “This is a really emotive issue, and one that’s becoming a political football. The way to cut through that would be to take up those amendments when they come back at report stage tomorrow.”

Replying, the minister, Anna Turley, said the amendments should be debated fully. She warned that the government “may not be able to publish everything at every stage” because of commercial sensitivities.

The full exchange can be watched on Parliament Live, from 16:09:53.

## “They deserve answers”

In a statement, Mr Gordon said: “Whether it’s Bicester or Linton, communities are asking the same basic questions. Is the site suitable? What will it cost? Who pays? They deserve answers. Tomorrow the Government can back these amendments and put transparency into law.”
