# DWP urged to take 'new approach' towards PIP claimants with 4 conditions

## The Department for Work and Pensions could take a 'new approach' to young people with anxiety, depression, ADHD and autism under recommendations from the Milburn Review

The Department for Work and Pensions is set to adopt a 'new approach' for individuals with four specific health conditions following the Milburn Review, according to expectations. Alan Milburn submitted his final report and recommendations for DWP reforms on Tuesday, aimed at addressing the NEET crisis and reducing expenditure on benefits.

His proposals include introducing a single Youth Allowance for young people, calculated at the standard Universal Credit rate for under-25s, with an enhanced amount available for those living independently.

He further recommends establishing a local flexible Health and Disability Fund, administered by the local YOS, to help cover additional expenses while a disabled young person receives support towards participation, or during serious illness, injury or intensive treatment, or when longer-term requirements remain uncertain, reports Birmingham Live.

The review suggests creating an Additional Cost Allowance to supersede PIP, designated for young people whose disability-related extra costs are substantial, established and continuing.

Additionally, he recommends introducing Severe and Complex Needs Insurance, a more substantial long-term payment without a qualifying period, targeting the small proportion of young people with the most severe and enduring disabilities, where it's already evident they will probably never be capable of working.

The Labour Party grandee said: "We expect those with conditions such as anxiety, depression, ADHD and autism, who currently form the majority of new PIP claimants, will be particular beneficiaries of the new approach.

"In the current system young people with these conditions routinely end up trapped in economic inactivity, when under the new system they would be both expected and enabled to be in work." He went on: "The design of this new youth benefits system would mean that instead of young people being set on an early path towards long-term benefit dependency, they will be actively supported into participation.

"Rather than an early judgement being set in stone they will be given time and help to overcome the barriers that prevent them participating. Instead of practical help with illness and disability being delayed for months on end it will be available within days or weeks.

"Instead of people with lower-level and changing needs being stuck on PIP, they will get the individual support, flexible opportunities and financial help to have greater independence and move into participation.

"Instead of those with significant and ongoing extra cost needs having to go through repeat PIP assessment processes, they will get access to more secure financial help. And for those with the most severe disabilities instead of months long wait times, repeated reassessments and ongoing insecurity, they will be fast-tracked to long-term financial security."
