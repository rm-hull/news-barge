According to the Senate Department for the Environment, interested individuals can now register for free online courses to obtain a license.

Berliners with wood-burning stoves can once again earn their "Berlin Stove License" this heating season and get tips on how to heat their homes properly.

According to the information provided, the courses - which last about 60 minutes - offer practical tips on selecting and storing firewood, lighting a fire properly and ensuring optimal airflow. Proper operation and the use of dry wood could significantly reduce wood consumption and pollutant emissions, officials said.

The campaign is part of Berlin's Clean Air Plan and will continue through the 2028–2029 heating season. Since its launch in the fall of 2023, approximately 5,400 Berliners have registered for the course. About 2,600 participants have successfully completed it and received a personalized digital certificate. According to the Senate, there are approximately 115,000 wood-burning stoves in Berlin. Burning wood produces particulate matter and other pollutants. The level of emissions depends not only on the stove's design but also, to a large extent, on the fuel used and how the stove is operated. Heating properly can therefore help improve air quality.
