**Harrogate and Knaresborough MP Tom Gordon has called for any money raised through a visitor levy to be ring-fenced for the local area, warning that Harrogate must not become a “cash cow” for projects elsewhere in York and North Yorkshire.**

Mr Gordon raised the issue during the Second Reading of the Government’s Visitor Levy (England) Bill in the House of Commons on 16 November 2026.

The proposed legislation would give mayors the power to introduce a levy on overnight accommodation, with Mayor of York and North Yorkshire David Skaith able to decide whether to introduce one across the combined authority area.

Although local council leaders would be able to object to a proposed levy, Mr Gordon said the final decision would rest with the Mayor.

He said local businesses had indicated support for a visitor levy in principle, but wanted assurances that money raised in Harrogate and Knaresborough would be spent locally.

> **Tom Gordon said:** Local businesses tell me they support a visitor levy in principle. What they can’t accept is a far-off mayor in York funnelling the money away. Harrogate cannot be used as a cash cow to pump money into other areas and a mayor’s pet projects.

His comments come as Mr Skaith launches a consultation on the 2027/28 police and fire precepts, which for the first time includes a transport element to help fund bus services. The proposal could raise up to £2 million a year.

Mr Gordon questioned why another charge was being considered alongside the proposed visitor levy.

> **Tom Gordon said:** Now he wants a bus tax on top. If buses are being paid for by a transport levy, where will the visitor levy really go? It’s yet another tax on working people and the visitors who keep our local economies alive, and that simply won’t do.

Mr Gordon said any revenue raised in Harrogate and Knaresborough should be retained and invested in local infrastructure and community facilities.

He identified improvements to the Knaresborough to York railway line, road repairs and support for town and parish councils as potential priorities.

> **Tom Gordon said:** If ministers want this levy to work, every penny raised in Harrogate and Knaresborough must be ring-fenced for Harrogate and Knaresborough. That means dualling the Knaresborough to York line, fixing our pothole-riddled roads, and backing the town and parish councils that run festivals and events and look after our toilets and flower beds.

He also questioned the level of local control under the proposed arrangements.

> **Tom Gordon said:** Local leaders can object, but the Mayor has the final word. That isn’t devolution. Quite frankly, it’s a con.

Mr Gordon also criticised the Government for publishing no impact assessment alongside the Bill, despite requiring mayors proposing a visitor levy to produce one.

He raised concerns about the penalties contained in the legislation, including the possibility of up to 14 years in prison for fraudulent evasion of the levy. He called for the Government’s justice impact test to be published to establish the potential consequences for courts and prisons.

Comparing the proposed penalties with arrangements in other European countries, Mr Gordon said the approach should be proportionate.

> **Tom Gordon said:** In France, a hotel that fails to collect the tourist tax gets a fine. In Spain, you only face jail if the fraud tops €120,000. Italy used to lock people up and switched to fines. Nobody should get off scot-free, but this is about proportionality.
>
> Ministers demand impact assessments from every mayor but won’t do the sums themselves. Our hospitality sector needs a VAT cut, not a new tax.

There is also a significant question over enforcement. Under the proposed legislation, accommodation providers who fail to collect the levy could face serious penalties, including up to 14 years in prison for fraudulent evasion.

Tom Gordon argues that this is disproportionate, pointing to France, where hotels failing to collect tourist tax face fines, and Spain, where imprisonment is reserved for cases involving fraud exceeding €120,000.

It’s a particularly important issue for the hospitality sector, which could be left carrying significant responsibilities for collecting the charge.

**Full Reading in the House**

The Visitor Levy (England) Bill had its Second Reading in the House of Commons on 16 November 2026.
