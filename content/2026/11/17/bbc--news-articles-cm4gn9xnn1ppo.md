**After 13 years of waiting, some GTA 6 fans are taking extra steps to play the game - from booking time off work, buying a console to play it on or even pretending they're halfway around the world to get early access.**

Market intelligence firm Sensor Tower estimates, external GTA 6 has already generated $1.18bn (£894m) from 12 million pre-orders - although Rockstar has not confirmed this.

Despite the option to preload the 212GB game, Jennifer Holmes, chief executive of the London Internet Exchange, said several UK internet providers including BT and Virgin Media had "increased capacity in anticipation of higher demand" ahead of launch.

But for some fans, simply having the game ready to play is not enough.

Gamers have long been known to book time off work for major releases.

When Red Dead Redemption 2 launched in October 2018, social media users were sharing plans to take the day off, with one analytics firm reportedly finding, external more people talking about calling in sick for it than any other game that year.

Similar conversations took place ahead of Cyberpunk 2077's release in December 2020, with fans discussing , externaltaking several days off to play.

GTA 6 is no exception, but it appears to characteristically be taking things to a whole new level.

For the game's launch day, HR software company BrightHR, external looked at absence data from more than 1.2 million employees from 70,000 businesses across the UK.

It found GTA was mentioned in 4.6% of time-off requests by employees. Throughout November and December, it said an estimated 83,000 days had been booked off for the game.

According to the HR firm, one person put "new GTA is coming out! I will be spending a week on the sofa in my pants!" as the reason for their leave request, while another said: "play the new GTA - I've waited a billion years".

A third said in their request: "GTA Release - I'll put my notice in if this is denied."

The company added it had no way of tracking if the requests had actually been approved by employers.

Some places have even decided to lean into the trend by offering incentives around the game – including the US Army's 3rd Infantry Division based at Fort Stewart, Georgia.

It gave some active-duty soldiers a four-day pass to play the game when it is released in November, as long as they sign a reenlistment contract for a minimum of two years between 1 August and 12 November.

"The idea was to have a unique incentives programme that connects to what soldiers are interested in," public affairs officer for the division, Lieutenant Colonel Angel Tomko confirmed to the BBC in August.

"There are 130 soldiers eligible within the battalion, and 20 have selected this incentive as part of their reenlistment," she said at the time.

## Moving to New Zealand

Aside from taking time off, some gamers have also been discussing a technical loophole which could give them valuable extra hours of play before it officially unlocks in their country.

Because GTA 6 will release at midnight local time in all regions, New Zealand will become one of the first places where it is available, due to it reaching midnight earliest because of its position near the international date line.

This has led to some investigating ways to "trick" their consoles into thinking they're based in the island country when they are in fact thousands of miles away.

According to Google Trends, searches for the "GTA 6 New Zealand trick" have surged by more than 5,000% in the UK over the past month.

Games journalist Vic Hood said many gamers would be looking to play earlier to "get in on the action as soon as possible", as well as to prevent spoilers.

"As New Zealand is 13 hours ahead of the UK, that's 13 hours of gameplay that has time to spread across the internet and potentially spoil some key story beats and in-game surprises," she said.

"Took an extra day of paid time off when I found this out," one fan posted on Reddit on the trick.

It also means gamers would be playing the game at the much nicer time of 11:00 GMT, rather than having to stay up all night.

But another Xbox user shared caution on Reddit, noting that while the loophole had been exploited for many years for other game releases, GTA 6 "might be the game where Microsoft finally does something about this 'exploit'" - which could include banning players.

GTA 6's developers Rockstar and publishers Take Two Interactive have not commented on the loophole, and neither has Sony nor Microsoft.

Louise Wooldridge from analyst firm Ampere Analysis said both Microsoft and Sony had "rules governing regional accounts and purchases" for the Xbox and PlayStation, adding that Sony's were "slightly more restrictive".

Players who choose to use the workaround could therefore risk breaching those terms and conditions and potentially face restrictions on their accounts or lose access to their digital games.

Some gamers will have to wait even longer than their console-playing counterparts to get their hands on GTA 6, as the game will not be available on PC at launch, similar to previous GTA releases.

"Spare a thought for the PC users who will have to live vicariously through others' YouTube videos and Twitch streams," gamer Stephen Finn told the BBC.

A PS5 now costs from £520, while the more powerful PS5 Pro is nearly £790.

Despite the hefty price tag, for some the temptation to join the community in playing what many have called the biggest game launch of the year, if not decade, has been too great.

Reddit posts and videos, such as one by US YouTuber isoish, external, show fans buying consoles specifically to play the game.

"So, the only game I'm going to have on here is GTA," the content creator said in the video.

"The whole PS5 is for GTA 6."

After more than a decade of waiting, it seems some fans are determined not to waste a single minute of GTA 6's release - no matter what the cost or extra effort.
