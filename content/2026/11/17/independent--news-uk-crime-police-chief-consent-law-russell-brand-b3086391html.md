A police chief has urged ministers to consider bringing in a staggered age of consent in the wake of the conviction of Russell Brand.

The government is said to be reviewing the idea of an overhaul of consent laws to stop adults above a certain age from having sex with children aged 16 and 17.

Asked about the proposals on Tuesday, Gavin Stephens, the chair of the National Police Chiefs’ Council, said they would need to “look carefully” at how any changes would work, but added: “If there is a better way to avoid, in particular, powerful men in positions of influence being able to exploit vulnerable people and to strengthen the legislation, then we should consider it.”

He said policing chiefs should “actively” join the debate over consent laws in the wake of Brand’s trial, adding: “We should have a voice in what comes next from our professional experience, and we would need to look carefully about how that works.”

The comedian and Hollywood actor, 51, was this month jailed for 10 years and six months for rape and three sexual assaults during the height of his career from 2004 to 2009.

Mia Latter, 37, who was not part of the trial but had a relationship with the celebrity when she was a 16-year-old girl and he was 30, has led calls for an overhaul of consent laws in the wake of his conviction.

Mr Stephens said it was a “shame” that it had taken such a high-profile case to shine a light on long-term issues facing rape victims in the criminal justice system, including how they are cross-examined.

Brand’s lawyer, Ian Winter KC, faced criticism for perpetuating rape stereotypes in his closing speech as he repeatedly questioned why the women did not scream, kick out or to try to leave during their assaults.

“Whenever you get a case like this where it's somebody of significant profile, we ought to remember that there are there are victims giving evidence in court every week in relation to similar really horrible offending, and they don't get the attention and profile and interest taken in how they're being cross-examined in court by barristers about whether the judges are giving the right directions or not to prevent them from being re-traumatised,” the police chief said.

“So in some ways, it's a shame that it's taken such a high-profile case to draw attention to the issue that has been a problem in the system for some time. But we ought not to waste the opportunity. This is now at the very forefront of public attention, and we should use that to find ways to better support victims through such investigations.”

Mr Stephens said he was “not aware” of the major case triggering a widespread probe into misconduct in the media and television industry at this stage, but said more complainants are stepping forward each day.

Last week, David Walliams was unmasked as the celebrity who failed to gag reports accusing him of grooming teenagers as young as 15.

Multiple women accused the Little Britain star of sexual misconduct in an investigation published by The Guardian and ITV News. Mr Walliams, 55, denies any wrongdoing and his lawyers took a case to the High Court to try to stop the accusations being published.

The Metropolitan Police launched an investigation into allegations of serious sexual offending against the *Little Britain* star on Friday.
