## Iran denied media reports based on US intelligence that it may be planning a dirty bomb attack in the US capital, with questions swirling about its actual intentions or real capabilities for such an act, but Trump issued a strong warning against Tehran "because they happen to be crazy."

US president Donald Trump issued a stern warning to Iran on Tuesday about the possibility that Tehran could be planning an attack involving a so-called dirty bomb or a chemical weapon in Washington DC, as reported by US broadcaster CNN citing what it called "sources familiar with the latest intelligence."

When asked whether the threat was imminent, Trump told reporters in Manila that "well, if there is, they will pay a price the likes of which no ​country has ever paid before."

Asked again whether there ​was reason to believe an attack was imminent, the US president replied that "I hope ‌it's ⁠not, but we take it very seriously because they happen to be crazy," adding that "we are looking into it."

While Iran continues its fiery rethoric threatening with devastating consequences if attacked again, CNN reported that new US intelligence indicated that Tehran was developing plans to potentially detonate a dirty bomb or a chemical weapon in the US capital. However, according to the report, US officials were still divided over whether Iran has the capability to carry out such a plan or would actually move to execute it, and that officials were divided over the credibility of the intelligence.

The CNN report said that, according to its sources, the plans the United States has learned about were very specific and included locations and targets, prompting contingency planning, but CNN quoted one source as saying that "there is disagreement over whether they actually have the personnel in place to execute the plan." According to CNN's sources, Iran's capability to launch this kind of an attack has not grown, but what has changed is the Iranian incentive and that there was no evidence that Iran decided to move forward with its plans.

A dirty bomb is a weapon made of conventional explosives packaged with radioactive material that contaminates the environment through a non-nuclear explosion, affecting several city blocks or several kilometres.

White House spokesperson Lauren Bis downplayed the concerns by issuing a statement saying that the United States is "monitoring any and all potential threats, but at this time there are no credible threats to the homeland,” while US Secretary of Homeland Security Markwayne Mullin told the US media that the dirty bomb threat "is not new".

The report was published the day after Israel's Mossad chief Roman Gofman returned from Washington where he reportedly met senior US officials, including CIA Director John Ratcliffe, to share Israel's latest intelligence on Iran. Israel's prime minister Benjamin Netanyahu has been insisting that the US and Israel must continue the military campaign against the Iranian regime.

According to multiple US media reports, senior members of the US administration have reportedly been moved over time to military compounds or protected government residences due to potential security threats.

Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi dismissed the allegations as "rubbish", stating in a post on X to “spare us the Iraq War-style propaganda, especially with Israeli fingerprints all over it, nobody is buying it.”

Iran's Foreign Ministry spokesperson Esmaeil Baghaei called the allegations "baseless".

In a statement on X, Baghaei said that the dirty bomb plans allegations stem from a media narrative "designed to justify attacks on Iran's vital infrastructure."

The Iranian diplomat said that Tehran "considers the use of chemical weapons under any circumstances to be completely illegitimate and firmly rejects and explicitly denies the false and fabricated allegations made in this regard."

He warned that "Iran knows very well how to respond to any act of aggression and assures that this response will not be forgotten."

Tehran has been threatening the United States and Israel as well as any country supporting new military actions against Iran with a fiery response, including on their home territories.

These mounting threats come as the United States and Iran remain locked in an ongoing confrontation in the Strait of Hormuz with both sides increasing pressure to force concessions for most favourable terms in case of a peace deal.
