## Mercosur meat imports remain a contentious issue among EU countries, amid divisions over a deal liberalising trade between the two blocs.

The 27 EU member states postponed a vote on Tuesday to lift the ban on Brazilian chicken imports after several countries asked for further clarification from the European Commission regarding Brazil’s compliance with EU antimicrobial regulations.

The vote was scheduled after the EU **banned** imports of Brazilian meat in September over Brazil’s use of antimicrobials to stimulate animal growth.

The ban angered Brasilia, as it came only a few months after the contentious Mercosur trade deal with Latin American countries provisionally entered into force, opening up trade in agricultural products, including Brazilian meat.

“A number of Member States requested further clarifications on the system put in place by Brazil,” Commission spokesperson Eva Hrncirova said. She added that the Commission would now “take the time to address these comments and questions” before a new meeting takes place in early December.

The ban, which followed a vote by national experts in May, covers beef, poultry, eggs and honey. However, an EU audit found in early September that Brazil’s poultry control system was operating effectively.

## **Heated topic between EU countries** Imports of Brazilian meat remain a divisive issue among EU countries, with several countries, led by France, strongly opposed to the Mercosur agreement**signed** in January this year.

EU farmers fear being exposed to dumping due to lower production standards in the countries that signed the deal — Argentina, Brazil, Paraguay and Uruguay — particularly regarding health and phytosanitary rules.

To address farmers’ concerns, the deal includes quotas capping tariff-free access for sensitive agricultural products. Annual beef imports are limited to 99,000 tonnes at a reduced tariff of 7.5%, equivalent to 1.5% of EU production, while poultry imports are capped at 180,000 tonnes a year, or 1.3% of EU output.

However, ratification of the deal has been delayed since the European Parliament referred the agreement to the Court of Justice of the European Union, questioning its legality.

Facing pressure from Mercosur countries and EU member states backing the deal, notably Spain and Germany, the European Commission eventually decided in May to provisionally implement the agreement pending a ruling by EU judges on its legality.
