**Parts of northern England and southern Scotland are set to get an early taste of winter as hill snow moves in on Tuesday.**

A Met Office yellow warning for snow is valid until midday Tuesday with 5-15cm expected at some locations above 300-400m.

Major routes like the A74(M), A66 and M62 could all have problems with transport disruption.

Some railways are also likely to be affected with longer journey times for train services.

Cold health alerts are also in place, external for northern England issued by the UK Health Security Agency (UKHSA) until 09:00 GMT on Thursday.

The Met Office has issued a yellow snow warning for Tuesday

## What's causing the snow?

A weather front is pushing north and east across the UK bringing a spell of rain to many areas. As this rain reaches northern England it will encounter a reservoir of colder and drier air.

Some of the rain will evaporate in this drier air, which will cool the atmosphere further by a process known as evaporative cooling - the same process involved in cooling your body down when you sweat.

Eventually rain will start to turn to snow, initially over higher hills in the Pennines, moving to lower hills with time. As the precipitation moves northwards and eastwards, the snow will spread to the Cumbrian hills and into Scotland's Southern Uplands.

The snow will come down in big, wet, chunky flakes which could stick to powerlines and tree branches.

The extra weight of the snow combined with wind gusts of 40-50mph over high ground could bring down tree branches and powerlines, bringing an additional risk of some localised power cuts.

If you are planning a journey early on Tuesday, check on road conditions before setting out

## How long will the snow last?

Some snow may accumulate down to 100m elevation or so, with around 1-5cm possible.

Any snow will probably be quite short-lived, turning back to rain by 09:00 in most parts.

Cold outbreaks of rain will be left across northern areas of the UK through Tuesday afternoon with some hill fog and a chilly wind.

Meanwhile the south will have sunshine and blustery showers but it will also turn milder with temperatures up to 14C.

Rain and showers will continue to impact Northern Ireland, England and Wales for the next few days.

There will still be some rain in Scotland on Wednesday but then high pressure will take over with sunnier weather returning.

This drier, sunnier weather will then push southwards across most of the UK by the end of the week with cold air returning, along with some overnight frosts and a few coastal showers.
