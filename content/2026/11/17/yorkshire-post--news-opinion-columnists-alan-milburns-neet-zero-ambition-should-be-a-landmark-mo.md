# Alan Milburn's 'NEET Zero' ambition should be a landmark moment: The Yorkshire Post says

## At the start of Alan Milburn’s 278-page report setting out his proposals for how to tackle the nation’s youth unemployment crisis and move ‘from a welfare state to a working state’, he reflects that he does not like the commonly-used term ‘NEETs’.

Mr Milburn says one young woman told him the acronym – which stands for ‘not in employment, education and training’ – would be better replaced by a different description – ‘desperate for education, employment and training’.

That is a wise distinction and the guiding force behind the policy suggestions he proposes as a way forward. As Mr Milburn rightly says, no report has all the answers and many of his suggestions will meet opposition.

But the central argument is one that should be indisputable; “A young person’s future should depend on their aptitudes and aspirations, not their background or their birth”.

There is no dispute that change is required; only Romania has a higher ‘NEETs’ rate than the UK in Europe. Mr Milburn says the ambition within the next 10 years should be to go from one of Europe’s worst performers on this measure to its best – an effective ‘NEET Zero’ ambition as he memorably puts it.

To do so will require delivering multiple measures – from the creation of more job opportunities for young people through measures such as financial incentives and lower National Insurance rates for hiring companies to changing the welfare system so it is actively designed around participation in the economy rather than ‘simply writing them a cheque’.

While achieving ‘net zero' has become a bitter political battlefield, ‘NEET Zero’ should be the launchpad for turning the future of our young people into a key political priority. A mindset shift of treating ‘NEETs’ as ‘DEETs’ instead is just the beginning.
