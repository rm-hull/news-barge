At around three o’clock in the morning last Thursday, a noise could be heard coming from the hallway. The lift in the multi-storey building stopped on the floor where Idris Sayyid\* and his family live. Its doors opened. Unusual at that time of night. The cat in the living room woke up and meowed. This is how the 16-year-old later described the incident to the Berliner Morgenpost.

When his eldest brother opened the flat door a few hours later to leave for work, he almost tripped over a large pinkish lump of flesh on the doormat. On closer inspection, he could make out something resembling a nose – a pig’s snout. An ear was visible too.

The animal’s severed head had been placed next to a pair of white trainers belonging to one of Idris’s siblings. It was a shock for the family from Afghanistan. Pigs are considered “haram” in Islam. Practising Muslims are forbidden from eating pork.

Was this an attempt to intimidate the family? Police are investigating that possibility after Idris called them upon discovering the pig’s head. The police’s state security division is handling the case, a police spokesperson confirmed in response to an enquiry.

The family of seven lives in Lankwitz. Police regularly patrol the area near their home. However, this is not because of the incident outside their door, but to “prevent potential threats to public safety”, according to a police spokesperson. In early November, shots were fired at an apartment building not far away.

Idris says he already feels unsafe in the neighbourhood. The incident has made things even worse. “When I listen to music, I always look around. Especially when it’s dark.”

The family’s surname stands out among the names on the apartment building’s doorbell panel. “They know we are Muslims,” Idris says, referring to the possible perpetrators. He is certain: “They want to frighten us.”

He shows photographs of the discovery on his smartphone. “The meat had also been smeared with ketchup, probably to make the whole thing look more dramatic.”

### Afghan Refugee Draws Attention Through His Work With Homeless People

Was the Sayyid family targeted in a racist attack simply because the perpetrator or perpetrators disliked their surname?

> No matter which country you come from or what colour your skin is, you have to see people as human beings.
>
> Mother of the Sayyid family

There is another possible explanation: Idris has been volunteering to help homeless people for four years – since he was 12. His commitment has attracted considerable attention, and he has even received an award.

A few years ago, he and his family spent two days living on the streets themselves. In the middle of winter. Before that, the Sayyids had lived in a homeless shelter.

“We wanted to leave because there were drug dealers there and people were aggressive,” Idris recalls.

The experience left a lasting impression on him. “I know what it’s like to live on the streets.”

“THIS is integration,” reads one comment beneath a video highlighting Idris’s work helping homeless people.

The reactions are overwhelmingly positive – but not exclusively. Others are annoyed that his commitment receives so much attention, describe him as an “absolute exception” or even accuse him of being a criminal.

### Family Fled the Taliban in Afghanistan

Idris is not the only family member involved in voluntary work. His mother also volunteers in the local community, she says.

In Afghanistan, she worked as a journalist for a radio station funded by the German armed forces, the Bundeswehr.

“The Taliban asked me why I was working with non-Muslims,” she recalls.

Eventually, the situation in her home city of Mazar-i-Sharif became too dangerous. The family arrived in Germany in February 2016.

Despite their community involvement and efforts to integrate, the Afghan family has now become the target of a suspected racist attack.

Mrs Sayyid sees parallels between the Islamist Taliban and whoever left the pig’s head outside their door: both deny the humanity of people who do not fit their worldview.

“No matter which country you come from or what colour your skin is,” the mother says, “you have to see people as human beings, always treat them with respect and always help them.”

*The family’s name has been changed at their request. The Berliner Morgenpost knows their real names.*
