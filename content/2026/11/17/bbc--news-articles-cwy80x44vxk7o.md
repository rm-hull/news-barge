King Charles speaks at the opening of General Synod at Church House

**King Charles has said the Church of England does not just protect the Christian faith, but also the space "for every belief" within British society.**

He was speaking in his role as Supreme Governor of the Church at the inauguration of a new term of its national assembly, General Synod.

While such comments have caused fierce theological debate in the past, they are consistent with the King's long-expressed beliefs.

Similar statements about the Church were also made by Queen Elizabeth II in a landmark speech given during her Diamond Jubilee.

The late Queen attended 10 Synod inauguration ceremonies since the assembly was established in 1970, though the then Earl of Wessex attended on her behalf at the last inauguration in 2021.

The day's ceremony started with a worship service at Westminster Abbey. The King and members of Synod then moved the short distance to Church House, the Church's administrative headquarters, for the Sovereign's address.

Speaking to the nearly 500 members of the assembly - including all the Church's bishops as well as clergy and lay people, many of whom were newly elected for a 5-year term - the King talked of his own deep faith and the way it guided him.

He then talked of a clear vision of the Church's public role.

"At a time when our society can feel divided, it is all the more important that the Church of England continues to extend its ministry among all the people of this country," the King said.

"The Church protects not just the Christian faith of all of us gathered in this room, but the very space for faith itself – of every belief – within our whole society and national discourse," he continued.

In an interview in 1994, the then Prince of Wales caused controversy by talking of a desire to be seen as "Defender of Faith" rather than "Defender of the Faith", the term by which sovereign leaders of the Church of England are referred.

He later clarified that his comments had been misinterpreted, saying he never intended to abandon his Church of England role, but could also act as a "protector of faiths" having long talked and written passionately about interfaith harmony and protecting everyone's right to worship.

In 2023 the King maintained his traditional coronation oaths, including a vow to be "Defender of the Faith", while also prominently incorporating multi-faith elements into the coronation ceremony.

But the King's comments today are reminiscent of a speech given by Queen Elizabeth II to an interfaith audience at Lambeth Palace in 2012.

She spoke of the role of the Church of England often being misunderstood, saying its duty was not to exclusively defend Anglicanism, or to exclude other religions, but to protect the free practice of all faiths in the UK.

Speaking today, the King did not speak directly of other contentious issues facing the Church such as safeguarding, or Christian nationalism, slavery reparations or the financial pressures on parishes

But it is clear that while this was something of a celebratory day for the Church, and particularly for the new members of Synod, there will be solemn and serious work to be done in the coming years.
