The restored frontage of Ripon Town Hall is back in view on the city's Market Place.

The scaffolding came down in time for Ripon’s Remembrance commemorations.

Thousands of people then gathered in front of the building on Saturday 14 November for the city’s Christmas light switch-on.

Ripon City Council said the first phase of restoration was completed on time and within budget.

The Grade II\* listed Town Hall was designed by James Wyatt and built in 1799.

![](https://mmo.aiircdn.com/409/6afc73e51f546d7d464.png)

York-based Pinnacle Conservation carried out lime repairs and gilding work and repainted the frontage with breathable mineral paint.

The lighter, more matt finish was chosen after specialists examined historic paint layers under a microscope.

North Yorkshire Council contributed £10,000 towards the work after contractors found more extensive repairs were needed.

It also improved the Town Hall clock’s timekeeping following a request from the city council.

The Mayor of Ripon, Councillor Andrew Williams, said:

> “There's something particularly special about seeing the scaffolding come down and the building looking its best in time for Remembrance, and then providing such a wonderful backdrop to our Christmas Light Switch-On.
>
> "The Town Hall is such an important part of the Market Place, and I think people will take real pride in seeing it restored.”

The two councils have agreed in principle to a 99-year lease that would put the Town Hall in Ripon City Council’s care.

They are still working through the legal details, including leases and land charges accumulated over more than 50 years.

The city council is working with conservation architects and plans to seek residents’ views on how the building could be used.

It hopes to have options ready for consideration by June 2027.
