Hundreds protested outside a Rutgers University campus building on Monday night, jeering former border patrol commander Greg Bovino, who was inside addressing a crowd of about 25 people.

The university’s Republicans club had invited Bovino to speak, calling him the “Great Commander” and the “GOAT” in social posts promoting the event at the New Brunswick, New Jersey, campus. During the two-hour talk, the room never quite filled up, occupied mostly by journalists, club members and other students criticizing the former border patrol chief.

“Show of hands, can we remove 100 million illegal aliens by 2028? Do you think we can?” Bovino asked the crowd. Two or three hands went into the air. “Well, I’m telling you what, my hand’s raised! American exceptionalism! We can do it before then!”

Bovino was the face of the Trump administration’s immigration crackdown, overseeing massive Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) surges in Chicago, Minneapolis and other US cities. Scrutiny and public outrage over agents’ brutal handling of immigrant arrests, pulling children off the streets and taking people from cars, only intensified after agents killed Minneapolis residents Alex Pretti and Renee Good in January. Bovino, who argued Pretti was a “suspect” rather than a victim, was taken out of the field by the end of the month and retired in March, reportedly while under internal investigation.

A few minutes into his talk on Monday, about a dozen protesters drowned out his remarks. “What’s so crazy is that you all allowed a Nazi to be here,” one protester shouted over him, standing from her seat.

“Your fascist death cult is failing!” yelled another. Several police officers gathered in front of a grinning Bovino on stage, who seemed to be relishing the attention.

Helping lead the disruption was immigration advocate Gabriela Soto, whose husband, Martín, helped lead hunger strikes over conditions and deaths at the Delaney Hall immigration detention facility in New Jersey earlier this year. When Martín was transferred to another facility in May, intense protests ensued for weeks.

Gabriela was pregnant when Martín was taken into ICE custody; while he was held in solitary confinement, she miscarried.

“You’re taking children!” she screamed, standing on a chair at the Rutgers event. “It’s not OK!”

![a person shouts in front of a police officer](https://i.guim.co.uk/img/media/14e5a59a6ecb7e445d2adb6211f660a0ba102256/0_0_4000_2667/master/4000.jpg?width=445&dpr=1&s=none&crop=none)

Bovino eventually left the stage for a few minutes, while police ordered protesters to either stop the disruption or disperse; they largely complied. He then returned to give his talk, in which he repeatedly mentioned a small Michigan city that banned LGBTQ+ pride flags in 2023, made a seemingly homophobic pun out of Pete Buttigieg’s last name and ridiculed New Jersey’s governor, Mikie Sherrill, who earlier on Monday issued a statement condemning the event without ordering its cancellation.

Even among the smaller crowd, multiple people expressed their disagreement with Bovino during a question-and-answer session. When retired teacher Alan Cummings asked about his goal of 100m deportations, even though the estimated number of undocumented immigrants in the US is a fraction of that, he argued Cummings wasn’t proud to be American.

After Rutgers’s College Republicans announced the event last month, it was immediately met with backlash. The ICE Out of New Jersey coalition, comprising several advocacy organizations, and student organizers called on the university to cancel it. The students’ petition garnered more than 2,000 signatures.

Dory Devlin, assistant vice-president of news and media relations at Rutgers, said the university is committed to freedom of expression, but that “providing space for a student-organized event does not constitute university endorsement of the speaker or the views expressed”.

“We are all about free speech,” said Brea Yumiguano, a Rutgers senior and student organizer with Climate Revolution Action Network. “The issue comes when it’s Greg Bovino, someone who has been violent in the past, who has eroded our constitutional rights, who’s pepper-sprayed protesters in Minneapolis – that’s not what we want in New Brunswick.”

![a woman holds a photo of Renee Good](https://i.guim.co.uk/img/media/bc33b813a0853887b7167d5c6147a90c7fab747b/0_0_3000_2000/master/3000.jpg?width=445&dpr=1&s=none&crop=none)

Yumiguano and other students at the protest talked about the lack of safety facing immigrant students, students with immigrant family members and international students who fear being targeted by ICE. Earlier that Monday, it was reported that US international student enrollment had dropped for the second consecutive year amid the Trump administration’s ICE campaign.

A Rutgers sophomore named Ninnie broke down in tears while telling the crowd she doesn’t feel safe bringing her family to campus, describing reports of ICE agents targeting local public transportation. “There are plenty of us students here who are people of color who are afraid to have our family members come visit us at Rutgers,” she told the crowd.

After more than an hour of chanting to drums and bagpipes, most of the protesters left. Eventually, those who remained at Bovino’s talk trickled out through the security perimeter established by police. A “prosecute Bovino” sign was left by the exit door.

“This is an example of what New Jersey is about: New Jersey is an immigrants’ rights state. We welcome immigrants,” said Jorge Torres of the National Day Laborer Organizing Network, a member of the ICE Out coalition. Torres was among those who interrupted Bovino’s speech. “We don’t want no neo-Nazis in our state.”
