## Developed by the Soviet Union's Mikoyan-Gurevich Design Bureau in the early 1970s, the MiG-29 entered service with the Soviet military in 1983.

A Serbian armed forces MiG-29 fighter jet crashed near Belgrade on Tuesday morning, killing the pilot, according to the country's defence ministry.

The incident occurred at around 8:45 am local time in the vicinity of Stara Pazova, northwest of the capital.

The pilot has been identified as Major Goran Bulajić, 53. Bulajić had served in the Serbian military since 1996 and is survived by two children, the defence ministry said.

Military police are at the scene.

"A commission has been established to investigate the accident, with a mandate to establish the circumstances surrounding the incident and determine whether there was any responsibility on the part of the military organisation," it added in a statement. "An expert commission from the air force has also been appointed to establish the cause of the aircraft crash."

Video footage circulating on social media appears to show the aircraft on fire in a field.

Developed by the Soviet Union's Mikoyan-Gurevich Design Bureau in the early 1970s, the MiG-29 entered service with the Soviet military in 1983.

The fighter posed a "formidable threat" to Western air forces, according to the National Museum of the United States Air Force. It is equipped with one 30 mm cannon and can carry six air-to-air missiles as well as bombs and rockets.
