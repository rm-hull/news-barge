# Almost half of Yorkshire and North East maternity units need major repairs

## The Lib Dems said: “Leaking roofs, broken equipment and closed wards put mothers, babies and hard-working staff at risk every single day.”

The Government has admitted it has no idea how much it will cost to repair decrepit maternity units, with almost half in Yorkshire and the North East in need of major work within three years.

The Liberal Democrats, who quizzed the Department for Health and Social Care, accused Labour of “gambling with the safety of mothers and babies” over the response.

An NHS report last year found that 47 per cent of maternity and neonatal units in Yorkshire and the North East either need “major repairs or replacement” within three years, or worse are at “serious risk of imminent breakdown”.

It explained that “the condition of physical infrastructure affects the quality of care provided, staff and patient experiences, and overall service efficiency”.

In answer to a written parliamentary question from Liberal Democrat health spokesperson Helen Morgan MP, Health Minister Karin Smyth confirmed that no estimate has been made of the cost of repairing these units.

She said: “We have announced the investment of an initial £271m through the Estates Safety Fund to be used for resolving urgent safety issues in the maternity and neonatal estate.”

Ms Morgan responded by saying: “Every expectant parent deserves to know the place they will give birth is safe.

“Yet this Government has been told that dozens of maternity units are falling apart, and it has no idea what it would cost to fix them.

“You cannot fix a problem you refuse to measure. Leaking roofs, broken equipment and closed wards put mothers, babies and hard-working staff at risk every single day, while the NHS pays out more than a billion pounds a year when things go wrong.”

![A new children’s hospital, an adult hospital and maternity centre are planned for Leeds General Infirmary.](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/jpim-static/image/2024/10/09/7/06/BtLW-new-hosp-image_small.jpg?trim=0,229,0,229&width=640&quality=65)


This comes with senior midwife Donna Ockenden embarking on an independent review into failures at Leeds Teaching Hospitals NHS Trust’s maternity and neonatal services.

A BBC investigation revealed the deaths of at least 56 babies and two mothers over the past six years may have been prevented, while legal firm Irwin Mitchell is now representing more than 50 families in action against the trust.

The trust has been waiting for a new maternity centre to be built at Leeds General Infirmary, which Boris Johnson had promised would be completed before 2030, however this is now delayed by a decade.

“This government inherited struggling maternity services in buildings left to crumble,” a spokesperson said.

“That is why we’ve invested £271m since April 2025 into schemes related to critical safety risks in the maternity and neonatal estate and recently announced more than £1.5bn to improve NHS buildings and facilities.

“We know there is a long way to go, but we have backed the NHS with a record capital budget for health, rising to £15bn in 2029-30.

“Our 10-year capital plan will also put investment on a long-term footing and rebuild the health service for mothers, babies and all the other patients who rely on it.”
