The Mayor of York and North Yorkshire is considering introducing a new council tax charge to fund local bus services, despite pledging before his election that he had no plans to introduce additional mayoral taxes.

Conservative councillor Keane Duncan, who stood against Labour’s David Skaith in the 2024 mayoral election, has accused the mayor of preparing to break his promise to voters by proposing a transport levy that could raise up to £2m a year.

The criticism comes after Mr Skaith launched a public consultation on Monday (November 16) on funding for policing, fire and rescue services and, for the first time, improvements to local buses.

During the 2024 election campaign, Mr Skaith said he had no plans to make any additional taxes for mayoral responsibilities.

Councillor Duncan says the latest proposal is at odds with that commitment.

He said: “In 2024, David Skaith told voters he had ‘absolutely no plans’ to introduce additional mayoral taxes.

“Today, the Labour Mayor is proposing precisely that.

“He is pushing to abandon his election pledge with a proposed tax raid on local people of up to £2m.

“David Skaith has a simple choice - will he scrap his tax proposal and keep his word, or press ahead and break the promise he made to voters?”

A mayoral precept is an additional charge collected through council tax bills, with the money used to fund services across York and North Yorkshire.

The mayor already oversees precepts for policing and fire and rescue services, but the latest proposals would introduce a transport element to provide dedicated funding for bus improvements.

Mr Skaith says the additional money could help make bus travel more accessible and affordable, particularly for young people.

He said: “We want to keep precepts as low as possible, while making sure that our public services have the funding to deliver for you and to keep our county safe.

“For the first time, we’re looking at adding a small amount to invest in our bus services, focusing in particular on making it easier for young people to travel across our county.

“Before I make any decision, I want to hear from you.

“Please share your views on what this means for our police, fire, and transport services.”

In response to the criticism from Councillor Duncan, a spokesperson for the mayor said: “Before he was removed from his role, Councillor Duncan presided over the managed decline of bus services across North Yorkshire.

“It speaks to the lack of ambition that Conservatives have always had for our county.

“He can’t claim to have economic credibility when the cornerstone of his campaign was buying a hotel that wasn’t for sale.

“The mayor will never shy away from making difficult decisions that are in the best interest of all of York and North Yorkshire.”

The mayor’s office says rising costs and increasing demand mean additional funding is needed simply to maintain existing police and fire services.

The consultation offers options ranging from increases designed to maintain current services to higher levels of funding that would allow further investment.

The mayor will consider the consultation responses before presenting his proposals to the North Yorkshire Police, Fire and Crime Panel and the York and North Yorkshire Combined Authority early next year.

Residents have until 10am on Monday, January 4, 2027, to respond.
