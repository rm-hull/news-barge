A family-owned business in North Yorkshire is no longer at the mercy of diesel prices thanks to its new van.  
Roost Coffee Roastery, in Malton Enterprise Park, bought a second-hand electric van to replace their diesel delivery van this summer.  
Co-owner of Roost, Ruth Elkington, said the new van had calmed concerns she had when diesel prices reached £2 per litre this year:  
“Having the electric van has taken away the concern for the future that I had when diesel prices hit £2 per litre.

“I can’t see prices coming down soon – so this has been really good for the business.”  
Ruth’s husband David, Head Coffee Roaster, added that they’re already seeing financial benefits from the electric van:  
“We used to pay £25 per week on diesel, and now we pay about £4 to charge the van – and we only need to charge it once a week.”  
For Ruth and David, switching to an electric vehicle was part of keeping their business going through fuel and cost of living crises:  
David said: “The environmental benefits are great but for us it was also about being a long-term business."  
The typical route for Roost’s electric van is local deliveries, plus weekly trips to Ryedale and York.  
David said that despite their initial concern about the range of their van, it can easily make trips across the county:  
“Our diesel van could only do about 115 miles without re-fuelling, and now we can do up to 180 miles on one charge.  
“The van should cover about 200,000 miles in its lifetime, which means we can use it for another 15 years.”  
On top of the financial benefits, Ruth said she’d received good feedback from delivery staff about the electric van:  
“Staff said they’ve really enjoyed the trips to York, and that the new van is a pleasure to drive.”  
Autotrader research revealed that 70% of lower-income households feel ‘priced out’ of the world of electric vehicles (EVs) by their upfront cost.  
As a small business, buying a new electric car wasn’t possible, so Ruth and David looked to the second-hand market.  
David said they got their van for half the price of a new car, and would encourage drivers to investigate the second-hand market:  
“I initially looked into new and leased electric vans – but we didn’t have £30,000 to spend on a brand-new car.  
“The used market saved us about 50% of the price of a new model, even for a 2-year-old van with less than 10,000 miles on it.  
“I’d definitely encourage people to use the second-hand market, and there should be more conversation around it.”  
Roost has a grant-funded on-site EV charger, which uses energy from the business’s solar panels to charge for a low price, but wants to see better charging infrastructure for people whose homes don’t have a driveway:  
Ruth added: “The more that local authorities can put in public charging points for good prices, the easier it will get for people to make the switch."
