A new Europe-wide study has put a price on romance, and the news for Germany is not so bad actually.

The Cheap Date Index, part of Deutsche Bank Research Institute's *Mapping the World's Prices 2026* report, compares 28 European cities. All three German cities on the list come in below the European average of €270.

And if you've got a steady job, take heart. Researchers cited by Euronews link financial security with relationship readiness, so a steady income may help your love life too.

**Total costs for a 'cheap date'**

If €250 sounds like quite an expensive date to you, note that we're not talking a bout a modest coffee date here. The index price is set based on the cost of; a bottle of wine, a mid-range dinner for two, two coffees, two cinema tickets, two single public transport tickets and a five kilometre taxi ride.

It also includes a pair of jeans and a dress, so that each ‘date’ comes with at least part of a new outfit.

Munich is the priciest of the three German cities, with the above costs adding up to €260. Berlin and Frankfurt tied behind on €254, according to the study.

By comparison, Geneva and Zurich come in at €407 and €400 respectively. That means that you could have roughly one and a half Berlin dates – with change to spare – for the price of one Swiss evening.

At the other end of the scale, Istanbul was revealed as the cheapest date on the list at €168.

But the picture changes once you adjust for relative salaries. According to these calculations, a romantic night out in Frankfurt accounts for 7.2 percent of residents’ estimated take-home pay.

Munich is close behind at 7.3 percent and Berlin trails in on 8.8 percent. So Berlin's dates might cost the same as Frankfurt's, but the same evening hurts a little more because local wages are lower.

**The Swiss pay the most and feel it the least**

Considering dates across Europe adjusted for regional wages, Athens tops the list at 19.6 percent of net monthly pay for a single date while those in Zurich actually pay just 5.6 percent of average monthly wage on their night out.

Deutsche Bank Research Institute took the prices and salary figures from Numbeo, a website where users submit their own prices. Numbeo is not an official statistics source, and the salaries are city-level estimates.
