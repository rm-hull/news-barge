A knifewoman threatened to kill her boyfriend, smash up his home and damage his family’s business after he ended their relationship, York Crown Court heard.

After Effie Gray went to the family home with a knife and jabbed at the man's leg, his mother told police Gray was “capable of anything” that night.

“I feel betrayed and upset,” she said.

“I welcomed her into my home and family. She was practically living with us at the end.”

Ms Clarke said that the son had decided to end their relationship after Gray accused him of cheating on her.

He told police she had mental health issues.

After he told her his decision, she made threats by messages and calls including to kill him, smash up his house and harm the family’s business, said Ms Clarke.

On February 27, he went to the family home to find Gray, 19, at the door talking to his mother and refusing to leave.

He tried to get her to go and felt a sharp object hit his thigh.

Until then he hadn’t realised she was armed.

He managed to wrestle her to the ground and get the knife off her.

York Crown Court heard that the son suffered no injury because the knife was blunt.

“I did love Effie and she was my girlfriend,” he said in a personal statement.

“She was the Effie I loved but also the Effie who was defensive and aggressive.”

Gray could be normal and then “flip” when she would be “dangerous”.

“I fear she could cause significant injury to my family and home,” he said.

“I feel upset and annoyed about what she did to me and this will affect me for the rest of my life.”

Gray, of Church Lane, Selby, pleaded guilty to affray.

Recorder Richard Wright KC told Gray: “Just taking the weapon to the scene and using it in the way you did to cause distress makes this offence so serious only a custodial sentence can be justified.”

He gave her a 12-month sentence and suspended it for 18 months under the law that sentences up to 12 months long must be suspended unless certain conditions are met.

He ordered that probation officers supervise her for the 18 months and made a five-year restraining order banning him from contacting her former boyfriend and any of his family.

Defence barrister Andrew Semple said Gray had a “dreadful background” and no previous convictions.
