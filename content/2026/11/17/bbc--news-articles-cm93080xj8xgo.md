Vickrum Digwa, who fatally stabbed 18-year-old Henry Nowak in Southampton in 2025, is also seeking a sentence review

**The man jailed for murdering Henry Nowak has applied to appeal against his conviction.**

Vickrum Digwa, who stabbed the 18-year-old student with a large dagger in Belmont Road, Southampton, on 3 December 2025, has further asked the Supreme Court to review his sentence, his lawyers have confirmed.

Earlier this month the Court of Appeal raised his minimum term from 21 to 25 years, after the Attorney General argued it was unduly lenient.

The BBC understands Digwa wants to argue that his conviction for murder is unsafe on four grounds. The applications do not mean Digwa will automatically get any further hearings or reviews.

Imran Khan and Partners, Digwa's lawyers, said: "An application has been lodged seeking to appeal against Vickrum Singh Digwa's conviction.

"That application concerns the safety of the conviction and is separate from the proceedings concerning sentence."

Henry Nowak, 18, was stabbed to death in Southampton in December 2025

Nowak, from Chafford Hundred in Essex, was stabbed to death with a 21cm (8in) blade that Digwa said he carried as part of his Sikh faith.

Digwa, then 22, told police that Henry had attacked and racially abused him, which led to Henry being handcuffed as he lay dying. The trial judge later found Digwa's claims of racism to be a lie.

Digwa was jailed for life at Southampton Crown Court in June with a minimum term of 21 years.

One of the four grounds on which Digwa plans to challenge his murder conviction is that the trial judge wrongly excluded from evidence WhatsApp messages Nowak sent when he was about 13, which Digwa suggested displayed a history of racist language.

The messages were found by police during their review of Nowak's phone but the judge ruled them irrelevant and inadmissible.

The court heard there was no evidence Nowak was racist and Digwa's attempt to present the student as racist was found to have been a lie.

Digwa is also seeking to appeal his conviction on the basis the jury should not have been told that police had secretly recorded him confessing to the stabbing. The recording, he argues, breaks evidence rules relating to such material.

Lady Chief Justice Baroness Carr increased Digwa's minimum term to 25 years

On 5 November, the Court of Appeal ruled that the trial judge had been right to calculate Digwa's minimum term from a starting point of 15 years as there had been no evidence he had planned to use the knife as a weapon.

Had that been the case, the starting point under sentencing law for murder would have been 25 years.

But the Lady Chief Justice Baroness Carr said the final figure of 21 years had been too low because it understated Digwa's repeated use of the knife and the "deliberate cruelty, additional suffering and obstruction" that followed.

She and two other senior judges ordered a "a very significant uplift ... to reflect the use of the large kirpan alone" and extended the minimum term to 25 years.

Digwa and his lawyers are seeking a review into the way the judges arrived at this figure.

"The Court of Appeal upheld the sentencing judge's finding that Vickrum Singh Digwa had not taken the kirpan to the scene intending to commit an offence or intending to have it available for use as a weapon," his lawyers said in a statement.

"The court nevertheless concluded that the circumstances surrounding the weapon justified a very significant uplift and ultimately substituted a 25-year minimum term.

"The application therefore raises an issue of wider importance."

The Supreme Court reviews relatively few criminal cases and only reconsiders convictions where a point of law is disputed and it has wider implications for other cases.
