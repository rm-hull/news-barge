## Council in court over closed road at historic race track

*(Image: P.G.FARTHING.)*

North Yorkshire Council is facing legal action over the condition of a closed Scarborough road while warning that permanent repair works may not begin until 2028.

The case centres on Jacksons Lane, which has been closed on safety grounds because of land instability and has become the subject of proceedings under Section 56 of the Highways Act 1980.

The road is used by Oliver’s Mount race circuit and serves as the “sole route” for public vehicular access when events are running.

Operators of England’s only natural “road” race track have warned that “events will not be financially viable should Jackson’s Lane be inaccessible over an event weekend”.

Court documents state that liability has been admitted, with the council accepting both that Jacksons Lane is a highway maintained at public expense and that it is responsible for maintaining it.

In a statement issued after a hearing at Scarborough Magistrates’ Court last week, the authority said: “As the local highway authority, North Yorkshire Council is responsible for the maintenance of Jacksons Lane, which is temporarily closed due to concerns about the stability of the land.”

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21728147.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Jackson's Lane, Scarborough. Google Maps *(Image: Google Maps)*

The council also revealed that it has allocated almost £800,000 towards the design and investigation phase of a scheme to address the instability.

It said: “A total of £780,000 has been allocated for the design stage of the project to address the land instability.”

According to the authority, engineers are currently carrying out site investigations and assessments, and are developing design options before a preferred solution can be agreed.

The council added: “It is hoped that construction can begin in late 2027 or early 2028.”

The lane’s closure has become a major issue for the operators of Oliver’s Mount, who say uncertainty over access threatens the long-term future of racing at the circuit.

At a council scrutiny meeting in October 2024, Andy Hayes, a director of Oliver’s Mount Racing Ltd, warned councillors that the route was critical to the financial viability of race events.

He told the committee that Jacksons Lane had been closed since 2019 and said organisers could not continue planning future events without certainty over access arrangements.

Responding at the time, the council’s assistant director for highways, Barrie Mason, acknowledged the importance of the circuit to the local economy and confirmed that £780,000 had been allocated to procure a design-and-build contractor to devise a permanent engineering solution.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21728146.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Jackson's Lane, Scarborough. Google Maps *(Image: Google Maps)*

However, officers said the final design had not yet been determined, and therefore a delivery timetable could not be provided.

More recently, the operators of Oliver’s Mount served a formal Section 56 notice on the council, arguing that years of delays had left them with little confidence that a permanent solution would be delivered.

In a statement published in May, the circuit said: “After seven years of closure and previous commitments that have continually slipped, we need a legal framework in place that gives us recourse if timelines slip again.”

The operators added: “After seven years, it seems likely that a court order is the only way to ensure the expensive and difficult repair of Jackson’s Lane actually happens.”

A council spokesperson told the Local Democracy Reporting Service (LDRS): “Proceedings brought under section 56 of the Highways Act 1980 are ongoing.”
