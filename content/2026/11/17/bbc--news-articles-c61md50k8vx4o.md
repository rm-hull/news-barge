**Hundreds of people have gathered to protest against a talk at an Oxford University college by the academic who accused the late Professor Jason Arday of plagiarism.**

Stand Up To Racism organised a demonstration against Nathan Cofnas's talk at Oriel College, organised by Oxford Reform UK, on Tuesday evening.

Protesters, some with face masks on, appeared to try to block the entrance gates to stop people getting into the college before the talk.

Dozens of protesters held signs saying "Nathan Cofnas not welcome" and "Remember Professor Jason Arday".

Chants of "Nathan Cofnas shut him down" could be heard.

Cofnas, who has advocated for "race realism", wrote a blog accusing Prof Arday of plagiarism in his doctoral thesis.

Confas's talk was entitled "Is DEI (diversity, equality and inclusion) Undermining Higher Education?"

It prompted a weeks-long plagiarism row that saw 41-year-old Arday face intense public scrutiny about his work and other achievements.

Although he denied the allegations made against him, Arday resigned his post as a Cambridge professor of sociology of education. He was found dead at his home in south London on 14 August.

Theo Biggs, 20, who is a student at nearby Oxford Brookes University, said: "Oxford University is a prestigious university, and what is talked about here is talked about around the world.

"Giving this sort of conversation a platform is legitimising it essentially."

A course administrator at the university, who did not want to be named, said: "I've seen a lot of students of colour are feeling unwelcome, uncomfortable, frightened by the fact that Oriel and the wider university has not shut this down."

Another student added: "I've worked really hard to get here. I pay a lot of money to be here, and then me being a minority is like the biggest sin in the world. It's not something I want, it's not something I'm standing for at all."

Arday resigned as a Cambridge professor after the allegations of plagiarism that he denied

The talk's event page stated Cofnas "faced recent controversy after exposing the plagiarism of Jason Arday".

Cofnas has previously written that black people are less intelligent than other groups and would "disappear from almost all high-profile positions outside of sports and entertainment" under a meritocratic system.

He was suspended from Ghent University in Belgium in August before being reinstated after pressure by US ambassador Bill White.

On Thursday, protesters against the talk placed a Ku Klux Klan hood on a controversial statue of the 19th Century colonialist Cecil Rhodes at Oriel College.

In response to the protest, the college said it was "committed to upholding freedom of speech within the law and has a statutory duty to do so".

"We are also committed to promoting inclusion and maintaining an environment that is free from all forms of discrimination and harassment, including racial discrimination," it said.

It added that permitting the event "does not imply endorsement by the college of the views of its speakers".
