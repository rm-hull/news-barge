A Donald Trump appointee arranged for a Russian flag to be draped from a Georgia landmark in tribute to President Vladimir Putin’s late ambassador to the United States.

The Russian tricolor hung from the Millennium Gate Museum, an 82-foot classical arch in Atlanta, on Monday, according to a social media post by the Russian Embassy.

The embassy said the flag was displayed “at the initiative” of Rodney Mims Cook Jr., chairman of the U.S. Commission of Fine Arts, to honor Alexander Darchiev, who died last week.

The pair “met numerous times, paying special attention to the restoration of cultural and humanitarian ties, as well as popularization of the common pages of the history of Russia and the U.S.,” the embassy wrote. “We are deeply grateful to our American colleague for his compassion and friendly support.”

Putin appointed Darchiev as Russia’s ambassador to Washington in 2025. During Russia’s war in Ukraine, the diplomat was a fierce critic of the U.S. and the West.

![A large Russian flag was draped from the Millennium Gate Museum, an 82-foot classical arch in Atlanta, this week in honor of the late Russian ambassador](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/17/13/23/HXSzp5YW4AAI9LY.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Last December, Darchiev declined to testify before a Senate committee examining Russia’s abduction of Ukrainian children.

Darchiev, who previously served as Russia’s ambassador to Canada, died suddenly in Moscow on November 11 of a “chronic illness,” Russian officials said.

Cook’s connection to the Atlanta landmark runs deep. He founded the National Monuments Foundation, which led the design and construction of the Millennium Gate Museum. A longtime champion of classical architecture, he also helped develop the World Athletes Monument for the city’s 1996 Olympic Games.

Trump first appointed Cook to the Commission of Fine Arts in January 2021 and tapped him again in January 2026, putting him at the helm of the federal panel that reviews designs for prominent buildings, monuments and public spaces in Washington, D.C.

![‘Ambassador Darchiev and Rodney Mims Cook-Jr. met numerous times, paying special attention to the restoration of cultural and humanitarian ties,’ the Russian embassy wrote](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/17/13/42/HXSzq4aXYAAVg0S.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

In that role, Cook has helped advance Trump’s efforts to reshape the capital, including the president’s White House ballroom project. He has also championed Trump’s proposed 250-foot triumphal arch on the Potomac River, arguing that the capitol actually needs three arches.

Cook has also cultivated ties to Russia beyond his meetings with Darchiev.

Earlier this year, he traveled to the country to meet with Putin and actor Steven Seagal, according to a profile in *The New Yorker*, which described Cook as an “unapologetic Russophile.”

Cook’s push to honor the late Russian ambassador comes more than four years into Russia’s invasion of Ukraine, a war that has killed hundreds of thousands of people. A February YouGov poll found that 61 percent of Americans sympathize more with Ukraine, compared with just 3 percent who sympathize more with Russia.
