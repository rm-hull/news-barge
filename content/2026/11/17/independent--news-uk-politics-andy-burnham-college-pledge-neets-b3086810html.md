Andy Burnham is promising to guarantee a college place for every young person, following calls to rapidly expand access and stop an entire generation from "doomscrolling in their bedrooms".

Former Labour health secretary Alan Milburn urged an extra 150,000 18 to 24-year-olds to take up further education (FE) courses each year in his review examining the high numbers of young people who are out of work and education.

This has generated backing for colleges to offer a higher number of places than originally allocated, rather than facing financial risk by admitting more students than in the previous year.

Funding rates are going to be supplied to colleges "well ahead of the academic year" so they can plan more effectively, according to the Department for Education.

The funds needed to provide a guaranteed college place for everyone will initially be introduced for the 2027/28 academic year.

The Prime Minister said: “It is an economic catastrophe and a moral failure that we have a million young people who are not in education, work or training.

![Alan Milburn speaking at Google’s offices in central London about his final report into young people not in employment, education or training (Neets)](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/17/17/768417a4cab360cb69d8fbb52f5c3f0eY29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzk1MDIwNzQ3-2.86519499.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“We cannot write off a generation of young people, and I am determined to put that right.

“If you are a young person and want to go to college, we’ll make sure you can. We are backing colleges to offer more places and giving them more freedom to engage young people and help them into work.

“And this is just the start of our new settlement for young people, making sure we step in earlier and give every young person the chance to get on in life.”

Education Secretary Lucy Powell said: “Every young person should know they have a place at college and a home in the education system if they want it. An opportunity for a fresh start and a route into work.”

The review also called for work experience to be made “a statutory duty” on schools to ensure there is “universal access to high-quality experience of working life”.

The Government is seeking to tackle barriers to work experience, with new guidance issued for employers that they are not generally required to get a DBS check for those supervising under-16s they host.

![Alan Milburn (left) with Burnham and Powell](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/14/22/e61597deccfe6a48585d5b795e764117Y29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzk0NzU2Njkw-2.85437364.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

But they must not allow anyone to supervise who they know or have reason to believe is barred from working with children.

Speaking to journalists on Tuesday, Mr Milburn said: “We should increase the number of college places, that’s definitely what we should do, and we should increase it as fast as we can.”

He said: “What we can’t keep doing is what we’re doing at the moment, which is leave a whole generation of young people, sometimes doomscrolling in their bedrooms, out of society, out of participation, with no prospects for the future.”

Nigel Thomas, of The Gatsby Charitable Foundation, said guaranteeing a funded college place for every young person is an “important step” towards reducing the number of Neets.

“The next priority is to ensure those places offer high-quality education and training that gives young people the knowledge and skills employers value,” he said.

Lisa O’Loughlin, chief executive at East Lancashire Learning Group, said: “The funding commitment is particularly welcome, giving colleges greater certainty to plan effectively and develop the tailored support and pathways needed to ensure every young person has the opportunity to study or train for a good job and a better future.”
