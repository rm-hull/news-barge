An explosion at a California landfill sent a thick black geyser of decomposing trash and methane 500 feet into the air, sparking health concerns for residents in the region.

The Los Angeles Department of Public Health issued a directive ordering operators of Chiquita Canyon Landfill in Castaic to offer hotel accommodations and other emergency assistance to nearby residents after an eruption sent a noxious cloud into the air Monday afternoon.

The explosion sent odorous landfill gas and a large plume of dark black smoke into the air, impacting residents of Val Verde, Hasley Hills, Industry, Cambridge and Hillcrest, officials said.

"No one is at risk. We wouldn't be standing here without respiratory devices if there was any sort of risk," Los Angeles County Fire Department Capt. Aaron Katon told reporters, according to ABC 7.

No hazardous findings have been discovered by LA County fire officials, but the incident led to strong odors that may cause residents to experience headaches, nausea, dizziness or irritation of the eyes, nose or throat, according to the health department.

![A crew working at a landfill in California hit a pocket of methane below the ground that caused gas to erupt sending trash into the sky, officials said](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/17/07/California_Landfill_Gas_Release_719_.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Fire officials said a crew conducting routine drilling at the landfill encountered a pressurized pocket of methane about 150 feet below ground, causing the gas to erupt from the opening.

“There was no fire at any point,” Katon said in an Instagram video, noting that the eruption was pressurized gas escaping from underneath the surface.

Hazardous materials crews decontaminated six workers, and four went to hospitals as a precaution.

The incident prompted evacuation warnings for several nearby communities, as officials urged residents to remain indoors and keep windows and doors closed. However, by Monday night, all evacuation advisories were lifted. It was not immediately clear how many residents were impacted, and if any evacuated as a result of the odor.

Katon, the fire chief, said the fire department’s air quality monitoring had found no danger to surrounding residents.

“All of our tools, and we have the best tools, they say there is no danger,” Katon said.

Landfill operators said in a statement that the incident unfolded during a “routine drilling operation to install a new well.”

“Crews encountered a pressurized pocket within the landfill, resulting in an above-ground release of gases and particulate material at the drilling location,” the statement continued.

“No liquids were released. Work was stopped immediately and the area has been secured,” officials added.

The landfill operator said no injuries had been reported, including among its employees.
