Andy Burnham said he would not “rush in” with plans to change the age of consent amid calls for it to be staggered to prevent adults preying on 16 and 17-year-olds.

The prime minister was asked about the topic after Mia Latter waived her anonymity to publicly accuse comedian Russell Brand of raping her when she was a 16-year-old schoolgirl, as she called for a change to the law.

TV personality David Walliams is also facing allegations of misconduct, which has ignited debate around the matter.

Under current UK law, adults of any age are legally able to have sex with 16 and 17-year-olds.

Burnham told Sky News on Tuesday: “I’m not going to rush in and reopen issues around the age of consent.

![Prime minister Andy Burnham (Annabel Lee-Ellis/PA)](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/17/00/79fd0b288e6af13dd3a6c04b4e95156eY29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzk0OTI3Nzkx-2.86485800.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“I’ll give careful consideration to what people are saying because I think everything has to be discussed. Everything has to be on the table.”

He said the media industry must seriously take on the issue of “power imbalances”.

“But also more broadly in all workplaces,” he added.

“I’m not going to rule things out at this stage, but I think we need to have a careful, considered, discussion about all of these things and keep working relentlessly to improve the safety of women and girls in the workplace and wider society.”

Mr Burnham’s de facto deputy Louise Haigh had already said it is important to consider Ms Latter’s call for change, while Work and Pensions Secretary Pat McFadden said they will be discussed.

Brand, 51, is serving a 10-and-a-half-year prison term after he was convicted of one count of rape and three sexual assaults at Southwark Crown Court on 5 November.

Allegations from Ms Latter, who alleges Brand raped her when she was 16, were not part of his trial.

She has said she believes a staggered age of consent could have stopped her relationship with Brand “dead in its tracks”.

![Mia Latter has called for the law to be changed](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/16/12/39/mia-latter.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Ms Latter told the BBC her parents had tried to prevent her from seeing him and took her phone away, but that she still had to go to school, where he sent cars to pick her up.

“But if it had been a criminal offence for him to be having a relationship with me, none of the subsequent abuse would have ever happened because they would have been able to stop the relationship dead in its tracks,” she said.

“And when my mum met Brand face to face, and she wanted to look him in the eye and plead with him, adult to adult, and he just shrugged at her and said, ‘What?’ because he knew there was nothing she could do. She had nowhere to go legally.”

Multiple women have accused Walliams of grooming them when they were teenagers, with one describing her alleged encounter with him when she was 15 as a “sexual assault”.
