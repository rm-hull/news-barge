A Liberian-flagged gas tanker sank in Bulgaria’s exclusive economic zone in the Black Sea on Tuesday, according to the Defense Ministry, the latest in an unprecedented series of incidents affecting shipping there.

The gas carrier GAS HUSKY, en route from Alexandria, Egypt, to the Romanian port of Midia, is the second merchant ship to sink off Bulgaria’s coast in two days.

The 19 crew members were rescued and flown to the port city of Varna after the vessel sank about 50 nautical miles (nearly 60 miles or 100 kilometers) off the coast, the ministry said in a statement.

The sailors, all Filipino nationals, underwent medical checks and were found to be in good health, it said.

The tanker had disappeared from radar shortly after midnight local time, and at 8:02 a.m. the emergency buoy was activated, emitting a signal for a sunken ship.

A shipping news website, maritime.bg, quoted the rescued captain as saying the GAS HUSKY had been hit by four drones. There is no official confirmation so far on the cause of the incident, and an investigation is underway.

On Monday, a Somali-flagged cargo ship sank in Bulgaria’s exclusive economic zone not far away from the site of Tuesday’s incident. All 12 crew members on board were saved in a rescue operation.

The GAS HUSKY incident follows an unprecedented series of attacks on commercial shipping in the western Black Sea over the past six weeks as Russia’s war in Ukraine brings growing danger off the coasts of NATO members Bulgaria and Romania.

Over 24 days in October, nine cargo ships and tankers were struck by aerial or naval drones near the Bulgarian and Romanian coastlines. At least 30 sailors on commercial ships were confirmed dead or reported missing.
