Former health secretary Alan Milburn has urged the government to take on a “complete system reset” in order to help nearly half a million young people into jobs over the next decade.

A major report on one million under-25s trapped across the UK not in education, employment or training – known as Neets – will include a raft of measures aimed at making “work, not welfare, the future for the next generation”.

A benefits shake-up, new apprenticeships and a £3,000 incentive for employers to take on people who have been ‘Neet’ for at least six months.

Campaigners have described the report as “a once in a generation opportunity” to resolve the issue which is costing British taxpayers around £125 billion a year.

Prime minister Andy Burnham is set to bring forward new legislation to overhaul the welfare system for young people, which includes moving them off the two main sickness and disability benefits.

Meanwhile, he has declared the UK’s international isolation as a result of Brexit is “what our enemies wanted”,in a major speech.

Mr Burnham vowed his government would take a new and more collaborative approach to foreign policy, adding: “Looking back, Brexit was the first domino to fall in a global move towards more isolationism and protectionism, vested interests rather than values, driving international affairs.”

## Unemployment falls to 4.6%

The rate of UK unemployment fell to 4.6 per cent in the three months to September, the Office for National Statistics said.

UK average regular earnings growth fell to 3.5 per cent in the three months to September and was 0.4 per cent higher after taking Consumer Prices Index inflation into account.

## Andy Burnham to push for welfare overhaul before next election

Andy Burnham is set to bring forward new legislation to change the welfare system for young people ahead of the next election.

The prime minister is hoping to win over Labour MPs who are concerned about the cuts, which is likely to include moving young people off the two main sickness and disability benefits on universal credit and Pip.

Mr Burnham is expected to back former Labour health secretary Alan Milburn’s recommendations on these two areas, having previously agreed that the “status quo clearly isn’t working”.

He is expected to publish his plans for welfare reform early next year before pressing ahead with legislation in mid 2027.

## What has Milburn proposed in his report?

Here are some examples of the proposals laid out by Alan Milburn in his report:

* A new benefits system for under-25s, with the Universal Credit health element replaced
* A doubling of the number of young people achieving an apprenticeship over the next decade
* Instant access to needs-based mental health support without waiting for a diagnosis, including fast-track help for young people who are NEET
* More support for parents to ensure 90 per cent of children are ‘school-ready’ by age five
* A payment of £3,000 to an employer who hires young people who have been NEET for six months, with higher payments for small businesses

Campaigners have described the report as “a once in a generation opportunity” to resolve the issue which is costing British taxpayers around £125 billion a year.

However, the recommendations will represent a challenge to Andy Burnham who could face backlash from some Labour MPs who will not want to cut welfare – a rebellion which ultimately saw the downfall of his predecessor Sir Keir Starmer.
