Temu faces another hefty fine for not doing enough to stop the sale of illegal products on its platform, despite its online content rules.

The Chinese e-commerce giant, known for its super-low prices, was charged by the European Commission, which acts as the tech regulatory for the bloc, on Tuesday following a preliminary investigation.

It found that traders can commit a large number of serious infringements, including selling products that fail safety checks, before Temu suspends any of their stores.

![A Temu spokesperson says it’s confident it can reach an ‘amicable resolution’](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/17/13/2292476444..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“Shopping online should not mean risking the purchase of illegal and unsafe products,” said Henna Virkkunen, the Commission’s Executive Vice-President for Tech Sovereignty, Security and Democracy.

“The preliminary findings against Temu mark another step towards making the online world safer.”

A spokesperson for Temu, which is owned by PDD Holdings, said the company disagreed with the Commission’s preliminary findings and that the company makes significant efforts to mitigate illegal product risk.

“We’re confident that we will reach an amicable resolution of the matter that fully addresses the Commission’s remaining concerns,” the spokesperson said.

According to the Commission, Temu has the possibility to exercise its rights to defence and reply to the preliminary findings.

However, if they are confirmed, Temu could be fined up to six per cent of its annual worldwide turnover.

Temu’s worldwide gross merchandise volume (GMW) reached $9.25 billion (approximately £7.2 billion) in 2025, an increase of about 71 per cent compared to the year before.

![Temu and Shein, along with other platforms, have also been hit by a new EU fee](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/08/26/8/53/GettyImages-2274818561.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

This would be the second fine imposed on Temu, following a €200 million (approximately £166 million) penalty back in May in the first part of an investigation opened two years ago under the Digital Services Act that requires large online companies to do more to tackle illegal and harmful content on their platforms.

The earlier fine was for not assessing the risks of sales of illegal products, while Tuesday's charges were for not taking adequate measures to stop such sales.

In October, the Commission said it had accepted ⁠Temu's action plan to remedy the breach that had triggered the ⁠existing fine.

Temu, along with Shein and other e-commerce platforms, has also been hit by the EU imposing fees on low-value e-commerce packages since the start of July.
