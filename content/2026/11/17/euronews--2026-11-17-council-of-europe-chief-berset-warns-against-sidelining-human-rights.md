## Speaking to Euronews, Council of Europe Secretary General Alain Berset defended governments’ right to tighten migration policies but insisted they must honour their human rights commitments, as five EU countries unveiled plans for a return hub in Rwanda.

European governments have the right to tighten their migration policies but must not undermine their commitments to human rights, Council of Europe Secretary General Alain Berset has told Euronews.

Berset rejected suggestions that Europe was facing a migration crisis or that the right to asylum was becoming politically unsustainable, insisting that governments could reconcile tougher immigration controls with their international obligations.

“Human rights are human rights for everybody or for no one,” he said.

Berset stressed that European human rights legislation did not prevent governments from deporting migrants who had no legitimate right to remain, pointing out that the European Court of Human Rights had upheld some expulsions while ruling against others.

He also insisted that plans to establish so-called return hubs outside the European Union had to meet European human rights standards. "Can they be compatible with European human rights standards? We need to prepare this well and have a proper discussion about it," he said.

His remarks come as five EU countries announced a partnership with Rwanda on Monday to establish a return hub for rejected asylum seekers.

Berset expressed regret that preparations appeared to have taken place without continuing earlier discussions initiated at the Council of Europe.

He warned that respect for the rules-based order, which he described as essential to ensuring stability, peace and predictability, must also underpin Europe’s approach to migration.
