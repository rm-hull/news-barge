A senior member of the Royal Family once advised a Heathrow Airport employee to boil an egg inside a kettle during a period when catering options were restricted, a new book has claimed.

*Heathrow: Gateway to the World* commemorates the aviation hub’s 80th anniversary and documents the personnel, passengers and key events shaping its history.

Written by Rosalyn Wikeley, the work includes stories involving high-profile travellers, spanning global leaders, royals and celebrities, who have passed through the west London airport.

In the text, Sonia Dixon, who served as VIP liaison officer at the airport’s exclusive The Windsor suite for over three decades, recalls her interaction with the senior royal during restricted catering conditions.

“Prior to The Windsor suite opening, all VIP suites were airside within terminal buildings, and were only used by diplomats or royals,” Ms Dixon said.

“Only tea, coffee and biscuits were provided.

![Queen Elizabeth II pictured arriving at Heathrow Airport after a visit to the United States in 2007](https://static.independent.co.uk/2025/01/30/13/10d8bbdf96a8a6d5a614f73f71e542fbY29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzM4MzI4MzU0-2.4637797.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“One senior royal requested a boiled egg.

“I explained that we lacked the facilities to do a boiled egg.

“She said, ‘Pop it in the kettle, my dear, I do’.

“My response was ‘Ma’am, I would, if I had an egg to boil’.”

Heathrow declined to reveal which royal had requested the boiled egg.

The Windsor suite, reserved for the most important of VIPS, comes with a retinue of 96 staff to take care of guests’ every need.

Back in 2021, The Duke and Duchess of Cambridge were said to have used the suite, which cost around £3,300 at the time, ahead of a half-term holiday with their children.

Ms Wikeley also wrote about the late Queen Elizabeth II’s arrival at Heathrow where she took her first steps on British soil as sovereign after the sudden death of her father King George VI in 1952.

Following the news of his death, Elizabeth returned to London from Kenya, where she was undertaking a Commonwealth tour, meaning a mourning dress had to be “smuggled on board for a quick change” before she descended the steps of her aircraft, Ms Wikeley writes.

“Given the unforeseen circumstances, the 25-year-old sovereign returned to a fairly quiet reception, greeted by dignitaries and senior politicians, including then prime minister Sir Winston Churchill, at the foot of the aircraft steps,” the book reads.

![King Charles pictured at Heathrow in 2023](https://static.independent.co.uk/2023/04/26/10/7d111ce87b375279cb19797e433f80e3Y29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNjgyNTg2MzE4-2.35501335.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“Mere minutes before the now iconic photographs were taken of the Queen descending the aircraft steps, the appropriate mourning dress had to be smuggled on board for a quick change.

“No mourning dress had been packed for the tour.”

The royal family’s relationship with the airport spans decades, with Elizabeth II inaugurating the Europa Building, which later became Terminal 2, in 1955.

She went on to open Terminal 1 in 1969 and then later unveiled the London Underground Piccadilly extension to the airport in 1977.

The King, then the Prince of Wales, and Diana, Princess of Wales, opened Terminal 4 in 1986 and Elizabeth returned in 2008 to officially inaugurate Terminal 5.

During this visit, the late Queen described the new building as “a 21st century gateway to Britain”.

Following an extensive renovation, Terminal 2 was renamed the Queen’s Terminal in 2014.

![Diana Princess of Wales leaving Heathrow Airport in 1981](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/04/12/04100102-4cdb5936-50fe-4304-a594-54e86cf827b5.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The book also features photographs of famous passengers at the airport musician Sir Mick Jagger, the Spice Girls, Dame Joan Collins and Marilyn Monroe.

According to the airport, passengers have travelled an estimated 40 billion miles through Heathrow since its first flight in 1946.

New images of the Heathrow’s proposed third runway and £33 billion expansion plans are also included as the book nods to the future of the airport.

Heathrow chief executive Thomas Woldbye said: “Heathrow’s 80th year has been about more than celebrating eight decades of travel.

“It’s been an opportunity to recognise the extraordinary role Heathrow has played in shaping British culture and aviation history.

“But while we’re immensely proud of our past, our ambitions are firmly focused on the future.

“As we mark this milestone, we’re looking ahead to the next chapter of Heathrow’s story, and the role we’ll continue to play in connecting people and places for generations to come.”

*Heathrow: Gateway to the World*, will be available to purchase online through Assouline from November 19 before being sold at Harrods stores across all Heathrow terminals and in Assouline boutiques from November 20.
