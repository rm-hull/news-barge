Ikea has ditched some planned store openings in the UK blaming the cost of business taxes, which it said were the highest in Europe.

As its annual UK sales little more than flatlined at the Swedish flatpack furniture retailer, its UK boss, David McCabe, said: “There are locations where we were planning to open a new unit, and those plans have been abandoned due to the cost that we experience and the complexity within the market.

“I would hope that we’re able to bring those projects back to life,” he added. “But it’s a very real impact here in the UK, when those investments will go to other countries in Europe.”

The planned investments were smaller UK stores, but the retailer declined to comment on exact locations. Ikea has been pushing further into smaller high street shops, rather than its traditional out-of-town outlets.

Ikea’s UK arm pays the most business taxes of its operations in Europe, and the third highest in the world, after the US and China, he said.

The retailer’s UK sales edged 0.5% higher to £2.26bn in the year to the end of August. Sales rose 4.7% in the first four months of the year but fell in the second half as weak confidence and “sustained pressure” on household budgets led consumers to pull back on big purchases.

McCabe said he had talked to customers in stores in recent weeks who were planning to replace an entire bedroom, but then decided to just buy one or two smaller pieces of furniture and a refresh with new textiles to save money.

“We are seeing a change in the way that customers shop and we do anticipate a difficult period going forward,” he said. “We don’t think that there’s going to be a sudden, miraculous turnaround in confidence.”

McCabe described John Healey’s recent budget as a “missed opportunity to back retailers under real pressure”, but said Ikea continued to talk to the government.

The company has spent £54m on reducing prices by an average of 20% on 425 products, including its popular Billy bookcase, Hemnes daybed and the cantilever Poäng armchair.

Ikea is using seasonal offers to pull in customers, for example discounts on extendable dining tables, folding chairs and soft toys in the run-up to Christmas.

McCabe said smaller stores, between 1,000 and 2,000 sq metres, had been popular with customers, in sites such as Oxford Street in London and retail parks.

As well as the Oxford Street shop which opened within the former Topshop flagship site, Ikea opened stores in Brighton, Norwich and York last year, followed by a small store in Chester. It also opened a “plan and order” site – dedicated to kitchen, bedroom, and living room planning – in Dundee, and one in Aberdeen last month.
