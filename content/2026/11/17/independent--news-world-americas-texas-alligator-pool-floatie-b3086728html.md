Texas authorities rushed to the report of an alligator on the loose, but to their surprise — and relief — it turned out to be a pretty realistic pool floatie.

Animal Services in Sugar Land, Texas, recently received a call about an alligator that appeared to be sick and they rushed to check out the situation.

When the wildlife officer went to investigate, “she was in for a big surprise,” the agency said in a recent Facebook post.

“What looked very real from a distance turned out to be a runaway pool floatie!”

The floatie included inflatable legs, a tail and a mouth that opened to reveal fake spiky teeth.

![Animal Services in Sugar Land, Texas, recently received a call about an alligator that appeared to be sick. But it turned out to be a pretty realistic pool floatie.](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/17/20/29/903684451_1910821663236257_6146093142515893842_n.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“While this call definitely gave our team a good laugh, we always take every report seriously,” Animal Services said.

After the officer knew she was clear of danger, she made sure to snap some photos of the alligator floatie lying in a patch of green grass.

Animal Services took this opportunity to remind the public, “If you ever spot wildlife that looks sick or injured, please keep a safe distance and call the experts to handle it. You never know if it’s the real deal!”

![The floatie included inflatable legs, a tail and a mouth that opened to reveal fake spiky teeth](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/17/20/18/904702355_2017697322236004_8584603489335616386_n.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Facebook users jumped to the comment section to crack jokes, with one writing, “Well, that was a rather inflated story, then!”

“That’s hilarious! Good job on getting that gator under control,” another wrote.

A third channeled his inner “Crocodile Hunter” Steve Irwin, writing, “Crikey!”

“She’s very brave!” another commented about the Animal Services officer, thanking her “for all you do.”

Others suggested it may be a good time to close pools for the cooler months — or at the very least, take out any floaties that can be mistaken for dangerous animals.
