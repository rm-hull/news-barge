Working to a deadline is hard enough, but working towards one that can be moved if you really need it is worse. Grand Theft Auto 6 has been in the works for around a decade, including a couple of delays so devs could polish up some rough edges, and after all this time it must be really hard to step back and say enough is enough.

"They would never stop working on this game if they had the choice," Jennifer Kolbe, head of publishing, says in an interview with The Guardian.

![Grand Theft Auto 6 launch gallery: Armed men aboard a swamp boat shine lights at an alligator.](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/qQkiWYKyDfn2zRKo8HKJqE-1200-80.jpg)

We now know that part of the reason why GTA 6 was delayed was because of the sheer amount of NPCs that needed kitting out with dialogue and audio. Talking to Bloomberg co-head of Rockstar North, Rob Nelson, explained how the delay wasn't strictly necessary but not delaying would have meant GTA 6 "won't be the game we want to make."

Nelson expands on this with The Guardian: "It’s really hard to close it down. If you step away and get a little bit of space from it, you can look at it objectively and go: 'I'm proud of what we’ve done'. But ultimately, it is being taken away from you, and you do the best you can to finish it in the time that you’re told."

“They could have made GTA 5.5, and done it faster,” Kolbe adds. “Ultimately, that’s not what they wanted.” No, instead the devs were hellbent on creating something new, expansive, and an aviary for a few dozen bird species.

"What we have grappled with is the transition from being limited by technical constraints to being limited by human resource constraints," Nelson says. "Like, how much can we physically build? You can keep going forever … People say you can throw money at the problem, and you can to an extent, but it’s no guarantee of making something good."

"You have to take big swings. It can’t be just more of the same."
