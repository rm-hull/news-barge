A further £168 million in Government funding has been secured as part of Mayor of London Lord Sadiq Khan's initiative to tackle and ultimately eliminate rough sleeping in the capital.

The three-year financial package will fund shelter and dedicated services to help individuals transition off the streets and into settled housing. A total of £41m is designated for immediate winter measures, forming a key component of Lord Khan’s pledge to eradicate rough sleeping by 2030.

Emphasising the priority of the initiative, Prime Minister Andy Burnham stated: "Ending rough sleeping was my first pledge as Prime Minister because I believe everyone deserves the dignity of a safe place to call home. This has to be a national effort, with everyone playing their part.

"Mayors and local leaders know their communities best, and with the backing of government they can bring together the people and services needed to get people off the streets and help them rebuild their lives.

"The mayor of London has made consistent efforts to reduce rough sleeping across the capital. We want to build on that work, keep up the momentum and make sure nobody is left behind."

Addressing the new support, Lord Khan noted that the funding would "help us break the vicious cycle of street homelessness".

![Andy Burnham has pledged to end rough sleeping (Leon Neal/PA)](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/16/22/acbb52edb1b9381c4ddb7ea5ac038707Y29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzk0OTE5NDg3-2.86434034.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

"No one should have to sleep rough. Everyone deserves somewhere safe to stay and the support they need to build a life off the streets," he added.

"It will give boroughs and charities the resources to help people off the streets and into secure homes, with the support they need to stay there.

"The urgency the Prime Minister is bringing to this issue is welcome, and we’ll work closely with the Government, boroughs and charities to help people off the streets this winter and deliver our ambition to end rough sleeping in London by 2030."

The mayor also encouraged members of the public who spot individuals in need of assistance to notify Streetlink.

More than 90 per cent of the overall settlement will be allocated directly to London boroughs, alongside key charities including St Mungo’s, Depaul UK, Housing Justice and Crisis.

Under the provisions, Crisis will receive £300,000 for hotel spaces over Christmas and more than £160,000 for a dedicated hotel for women. Housing Justice is set to receive nearly £1.3m for specialist accommodation designed to assist people moving into permanent housing, while £400,000 is earmarked for a London Youth Hub catering to 18 to 24-year-olds facing street homelessness.

In January, Lord Khan is scheduled to introduce his Homes off the Street scheme, aimed at supplying 500 extra permanent properties for vulnerable individuals requiring intensive support.

Bonnie Williams, chief executive of Housing Justice, praised the announcement: "This is a significant and very welcome investment in ending rough sleeping in London.

![St Mungo's CEO Emma Haddad greeting the Queen earlier this year](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/16/22/2280231667..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

"It’s encouraging to see the Government and mayor working together with the voluntary sector to turn that ambition into action."

She added: "Getting someone off the streets is the first step, but we need to make sure they have the support to stay off them.

"We know how important relationships, practical support and a sense of belonging are in helping people sustain a home.

"This investment gives us a real opportunity to do more of that work, alongside our members and partners, and help end rough sleeping for good."

Emma Haddad, chief executive of St Mungo’s, also highlighted the importance of the "significant investment in ending rough sleeping in London".

"Every day that goes by without somewhere safe and warm to stay causes people’s physical and mental health to decline rapidly, so any response to homelessness needs the right types of accommodation as well as the right support for people’s individual needs", she said.

"This funding recognises exactly that, as well as the unique circumstances of particular groups of people, with tailored support for women, young people and migrants."
