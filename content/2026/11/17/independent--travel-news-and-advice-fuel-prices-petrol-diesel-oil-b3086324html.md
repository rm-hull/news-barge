Motorists ought to be seeing a "far greater benefit at the pumps" following a decline in crude costs, the RAC has stated, demanding a swift reduction in retail fuel prices.

The motoring organisation suggested unleaded petrol could fall to 150p per litre or lower in the coming weeks, following a dip in oil costs as an easing of tensions between the United States and Iran helps depress prices.

Crude is currently trading at under 100 dollars per barrel on a consistent basis for the first time since August, triggering a sharp drop in wholesale fuel costs, according to the motoring group.

This comes after unprecedented highs for diesel, which reached a record 199.18p a litre in September and breached more than £2 per litre the following month, passing the former record set in the wake of Russia’s 2022 invasion of Ukraine.

On Monday, average UK forecourt figures were recorded at 164.68p per litre for unleaded petrol, while a litre of diesel stood at 194.90p, according to figures released by the RAC.

![Diesel prices on average hit £2 a litre for the first time earlier this year](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/02/08/2297622048..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

RAC head of policy Simon Williams said: “The onset of the US and Iran conflict sent pump prices spiralling upwards, something that drivers have continued to pay the price for every time they fill up.

“Granted, prices have fallen a little in recent weeks but nowhere near as much as drivers would reasonably have expected them to, based on wholesale prices being down considerably.

“They should really be seeing far greater benefit at the pumps.”

If wholesale price reductions were passed on in full, petrol could fall to less than 150p per litre and diesel to 190p per litre in the next few weeks, Mr Williams said.

“The Competition and Markets Authority noted in the summer that falls in wholesale prices earlier in the year weren’t quickly passed on at the pumps,” he continued.

“It said it expected any reductions to be ‘rapidly and fully passed on’ to drivers. We fear history might be repeating itself.

“As we’ve said in the past, the stark difference with fuel retailing in Northern Ireland deserves investigation.

“Average pump prices there are, incredibly, more than 5p lower than in the rest of the UK with petrol at 159.2p and diesel at 189.2p – the equivalent of around £4 a tank.

“If retailers on one side of the Irish Sea can charge that for fuel, drivers may rightly wonder why those elsewhere can’t do the same.”

In August, the Competition and Markets Authority published a report which found some petrol stations were not passing on changes in wholesale prices quickly enough between May and June.
