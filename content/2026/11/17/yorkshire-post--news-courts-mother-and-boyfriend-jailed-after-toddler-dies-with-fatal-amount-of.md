# Mother and boyfriend jailed after toddler dies with fatal amount of cocaine in milk bottle

## A mother and her boyfriend have been jailed for child cruelty following the death of a two-year-old girl who was found to have a fatal quantity of cocaine in her milk bottle.

On Wednesday (Apr 10), 2024, emergency services were called to an address in Billingham following reports that two-year-old Laylah Hatch was not breathing.

Despite the best efforts of medical staff, Laylah died at North Tees Hospital.

Her mother, Jodie Hatch, 22, and her boyfriend, Dale Mathie, 30, were subsequently arrested and questioned by officers from Cleveland Police's Homicide and Major Enquiry Team.

During a search of the property, officers discovered a substantial amount of cannabis and cocaine within reach of a toddler. Further illegal drugs were also found during a search of Mathie's address.

Toxicology tests carried out as part of the investigation revealed a fatal quantity of cocaine in Laylah's milk bottle.

Cocaine was also detected in the little girl's blood, stomach contents and hair strands.

![30-year-old Dale Mathie and 22-year-old Jodie Hatch](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/jpim-static/image/2026/11/17/15/27/MixCollage-17-Nov-2026-03-27-PM-6974.jpeg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


Hatch and Mathie initially denied two offences of child cruelty relating to Laylah and a second child, but changed their pleas to guilty on the third day of their trial.

Both also pleaded guilty to possession with intent to supply a Class A drug, while Hatch admitted being concerned in the supply of a Class B drug.

The pair appeared at Teesside Crown Court on Monday (Nov 16), where Hatch was sentenced to five years and four months in prison and Mathie was jailed for 13 years.

Mathie was also sentenced for a separate charge of violent disorder following an assault on Surrey Terrace in Billingham on September 15, 2025.

Senior Investigating Officer Detective Chief Inspector Chris Pringle said: “This was an extremely distressing and emotional investigation involving a two-year-old little girl who should have had her whole life ahead of her. Every child should be safe in their own home.

“The levels of cocaine found in Laylah’s system could have been fatal to an adult, let alone a young child. There was a significant amount of illicit drugs found in the home which were easily accessible to toddlers making it an extremely dangerous environment.

“Whilst no outcome could ever bring Laylah back, I hope today’s sentencing brings some closure to her family. Our thoughts continue to be with them as they continue to come to terms with their devastating loss and I’d like to commend them on the courage they have shown throughout this process.”
