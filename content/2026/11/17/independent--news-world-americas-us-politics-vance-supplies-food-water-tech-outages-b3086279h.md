Vice President JD Vance has insisted that there is no need for Americans to stock up on food and water or “build a bunker”.

​Vance’s remark came days after a series of unrelated, major technological outages, which saw Microsoft services being disrupted and a nationwide ground stop on flights.

​Amid rampant speculation about the outages and ahead of the testing of the Emergency Alert System on Tuesday, Vance appeared on Fox News’ *The Ingraham Angle* and was asked directly if Americans need to stock up on supplies.

Host ​Laura Ingraham pointed to efforts in European countries, such as the United Kingdom and France, to get citizens to prepare for an emergency.

​“A lot of my friends have reached out asking if they should prepare to build a bunker or stock up on food and water,” Vance said on Monday. “My answer to that is no.

![Vice President JD Vance has said that Americans do not need to stockpile supplies in case of an emergency](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/17/11/29/Screenshot-2026-11-17-at-10-58-54.png?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&trim=23%2C0%2C152%2C2&auto=webp)

​“We don’t see a crisis coming down, but you have to be prepared for everything,” he continued. “We want to make sure we prevent the worst outcomes, but we’re also prepared for the worst outcomes.”

​The U.K. rolled out its “Be Ready” campaign earlier this year, urging its citizens to have 3 days’ worth of supplies in the event of an emergency. France has warned its own residents to have 72 hours’ worth of food, while Germany has suggested that citizens should have as much as 10 days’ worth of supplies ready.

​Each country says that the campaigns are to help citizens manage in the event of extreme weather and cyberattacks, among other emergencies.

Former FBI Deputy Director Dan Bongino suggested on his podcast, *The Dan Bongino Show*, last month that Americans should start preparing too.

​“There have been some warnings put out by the U.K. government and elsewhere about 30 days of preparedness and having food ready and power,” he said, although the U.K. has recommended preparation for 3 days. “You know, I am a firm, committed believer in the ‘don’t hit the bed theory’ - ‘hit’ with an ‘s’ in front of it.”

​“However, the way to reduce anxiety and panic in tumultuous times is to be prepared,” he added. “Have yourself an emergency radio, have yourself some batteries, get yourself some emergency food, get yourself rain collection and water filtration, some filtration tablets.”

![Some European countries have urged their citizens to stock up on nonperishable food items](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/17/11/2026-11-10T050013Z_502539389_RC2I6NAESSNE_RTRMADP_3_USA-ECONOMY.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

​Everyday life in the U.S. has been disrupted at least three times in recent days, with a Microsoft outage caused by a “technical issue” disrupting Copilot, Teams and email services on Thursday. A day later, a rare nationwide ground stop on flights was issued by the Federal Aviation Administration due to a “technical problem.”

​On Monday, JPMorgan Chase experienced a “software bug” that left thousands of customers struggling to access banking services.

​The three organizations have all stressed that cyberattacks were not responsible for the outages.

​Vance, though, told Ingraham that the Trump administration has made the U.S. better prepared in the event of a crisis.

​“A lot of what is being reported on is just basic good government, making sure that our military is prepared, God forbid, if there was a crisis,” he said. “But we don’t see a crisis coming down the pipe.

​“In part, what the president has been so successful at is ensuring we are reinvesting in the United States of America and we are better prepared for crises,” the vice president continued. “That doesn’t mean crisis is around the corner; that just means you’ve got a government who is actually preparing for it.”
