David Skaith is considering introducing a "transport precept" to invest in bus services

**A new council tax charge could be introduced in York and North Yorkshire to help fund local bus services.**

Labour Mayor David Skaith has suggested introducing a "transport precept" of between £2 and £6 per year.

According to the mayor's office the levy would raise between £650,000 and £2m, which could be used to pay for reduced bus fares for under 19s, free bus travel for care leavers and additional services.

However, Conservative councillor Keane Duncan has accused the mayor of preparing to break his promise to voters not to put forward any additional taxes for mayoral responsibilities.

According to the Local Democracy Reporting Service, Duncan said: "In 2024, David Skaith told voters he had 'absolutely no plans' to introduce additional mayoral taxes.

"Today, the Labour mayor is proposing precisely that. He is pushing to abandon his election pledge with a proposed tax raid on local people of up to £2m.

"David Skaith has a simple choice: will he scrap his tax proposal and keep his word, or press ahead and break the promise he made to voters?"

A mayoral precept is an additional charge collected through council tax bills, with the money used to fund services across York and North Yorkshire.

The mayor already oversees precepts for policing and fire and rescue services, but the latest proposals would introduce a transport element to provide dedicated funding for bus improvements.

## 'Difficult decisions'

Skaith said: "We want to keep precepts as low as possible, while making sure that our public services have the funding to deliver for you and to keep our county safe.

"For the first time, we're looking at adding a small amount to invest in our bus services, focusing in particular on making it easier for young people to travel across our county."

A public consultation has been launched on the proposed increases to the police and fire precepts, and the introduction of a transport charge.

According to the mayor, a £3 precept would pay for £1 bus fares for younger people and allow additional fare reductions for people leaving the care system, as well as improving services in some rural areas.

With greater levels of funding, additional evening and Sunday journeys could be added on key routes.

In response to the criticism from Duncan, a spokesperson for the mayor said: "Before he was removed from his role, councillor Duncan presided over the managed decline of bus services across North Yorkshire.

"It speaks to the lack of ambition that Conservatives have always had for our county.

"The mayor will never shy away from making difficult decisions that are in the best interest of all of York and North Yorkshire."

Residents have until 10:00 GMT on 4 January to respond to the consultation.
