Andy Burnham has paved the way for a completely new welfare system in the wake of a long-awaited blueprint designed to end Britain’s youth unemployment crisis.

No 10 said the prime minister wanted the wide-ranging report by former Labour cabinet minister Alan Milburn – which recommended a system with more employment support but significantly lower benefit payments – to be a “watershed moment in how we think about welfare” as ministers promised “a new settlement for young people”.

The government endorsed Mr Milburn’s 278-page report shortly after it was published on Tuesday – including by giving the author a new government role – but Mr Burnham will now come under pressure to commit to the sweeping proposals, which includes £3.6bn in savings a year in social security cuts by 2036.

Campaigners and charities have welcomed the report as an attempt to solve the problem of a “lost generation” of under-25s across the UK not in education, employment or training – known as Neets.

![Andy Burnham has already said his government is ‘ready to grasp the nettle here and make a better system for young people’](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/16/22/acbb52edb1b9381c4ddb7ea5ac038707Y29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzk0OTE5NDg3-2.86434034.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

But some pointed to the fact the projected savings would only amount to £200m a year after a decade, while the IFS, the leading economic think tank, said it was hard to judge “the plausibility” of some of the projected effects, including Mr Milburn’s aim to get 440,000 young people into work by 2036.

The major report called for:

* An “entirely new” benefits system for young people that would see the health element of Universal Credit and personal independence payments (Pip) replaced with a “youth allowance”
* Devolved Youth Opportunity Services to bring employment, health and career support together under one roof, guaranteeing young people access to a named adviser after 14 days
* The creation of incentives for employers to hire young people, including payments of between £3,000 and £7,000 for hiring those who have been Neet for six months
* The lowering of the rate of national insurance contributions for employees between 21 and 24 over a five-year period, with retail and hospitality prioritised for the tax cut
* A boost in the number of children ready for school by age five from 63 per cent to 90, as well as introducing a more vocational curriculum to make pupils ready for the working world

After the report was published, work and pensions secretary Pat McFadden told MPs: “We need a new settlement for young people.

“This new settlement must give people the best chance of higher living standards for the whole of their lives. It must change the equation that sees us spend £25 on the consequences of inactivity for every pound to be spent on opportunity.”

![Alan Milburn warned of the risk of a ‘lost generation’](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/17/15/01M7MZBG31VDJKTN2V2SY4JHP9.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The proposals would see ministers spend £5.8 bn by 2036-37, funding the new youth opportunity service and support, including tax breaks and bonuses for firms employing young people.

In return, they would save £3.6bn from the welfare bill and £2.4bn in extra tax revenue, saving £200m that year.

Speaking to *The Times* on Tuesday night, Mr Burnham suggested the reforms could also be applied to over-25s.

“I think the same philosophy could apply right through the age range. What we are doing is creating an approach that is preventative, that supports people where they can prosper. The same applies up the age range.”

Mr Burnham earlier said he was “confident” that Labour backbenchers would get behind the sweeping reforms to tackle youth unemployment, after they hampered previous Government efforts to cut the welfare bill.

Launching his report, Mr Milburn said: “Britain is at risk of losing a generation of young people, not because they lack talent, not because they lack ambition, not because they do not want to work. This is not a crisis of aspiration; it is a crisis of opportunity.”

He also warned ministers that “half measures will not do” because “the scale of the problem is just too big for that”. Advising against cherry-picking his recommendations, he added: “It sort of does fit together as one package.”

Mr Milburn also demanded a “downpayment” of £500 million in the next financial year, arguing his proposed package must be a top priority in next year’s spending review.

He also warned Mr Burnham he did not have much time. “Prime Minister Burnham is a half-term prime minister and so he needs to get on with it. And I know that he wants to. So I’m very hopeful indeed,” he said.

![Work and pensions secretary Pat McFadden made a statement to MPs following the publication of the Alan Milburn's review into youth unemployment](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/17/16/01M7N0AFGA3PTB64Y7HXD6YS02.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

If legislation was passed quickly, he said, then welfare savings could begin to be delivered by the end of the decade.

Mr Milburn also told Labour MPs, who derailed attempts by Keir Starmer to cut he benefits bill, that his scheme aimed to get people into work, which was “a very different argument” and was a change from a “fiscal grab”.

In response, the government said it would take a number of steps, including creating a new youth opportunities unit reporting directly to the PM, but only respond fully to the report later this year.

Labour MP Rachael Maskell, previously a rebel on welfare issues, said she welcomed the “ambition” though the “details are yet to be spelt out and therefore I will work with government to ensure they land in the right place.”

Earlier Mr Milburn admitted that policies introduced by the Labour government have affected the jobs market and “made it harder” for companies to employ young people.

Hours before he unveiled his report, he even warned a “course correction” was needed after his party’s National Insurance hikes.

Meanwhile, the Institute for Fiscal Studies said the proposals would mean “many of those with less severe disabilities would be likely to see large income losses initially”, although this could be offset if they enter work.

The forecast £3.6 billion a year savings would be equivalent to 41 per cent of projected spending on health-related benefits for young people in 2036–37, the think tank said.

The report’s own modelling anticipates its proposals would bring an extra 440,000 into work, bringing the Neet rate to 7% by 2036, slightly short of their target.

Conservative leader Kemi Badenoch said: “We don’t need costly government schemes to fix Labour’s own mistakes.

“We need to stop making it so expensive and risky to hire young people.”
