## The review’s author says only Romania fares worse in Europe on youth inactivity, even though young Britons are less likely to be unemployed than their peers in France, Spain or Italy.

A government-commissioned review has called for a separate welfare system for young people in the United Kingdom, cutting back some cash payments in favour of work placements and paying employers to hire those left out of the labour market.

The 267-page report, led by former Labour minister Alan Milburn, demands a “complete system reset” in how the state treats 16- to 24-year-olds who are Not in Employment, Education or Training, known as NEETs.

Their number passed one million earlier this year. The review warns it could reach 1.27 million by 2031 without action.

"A lost generation is not a fact of life... it’s a moral crisis," Milburn told reporters at the launch on Tuesday.

UK Prime Minister Andy Burnham’s government will publish its full response next year, followed by draft legislation. Work and Pensions Secretary Pat McFadden called the report "a radical blueprint for a new settlement with young people". He said ministers had already agreed to some measures, including more tailored help for young jobseekers.

## **Out of work, or out of reach?**

The review aims to cut the NEET rate from 13% to about 5% by 2036, close to the level in the Netherlands.

“Across Europe, only Romania had a higher NEET rate than the UK in 2025,” Milburn wrote.

The headline jobless figures tell a different story. Office for National Statistics data published on Tuesday showed UK youth unemployment fell to 15.1% in the three months to September, though that remains more than three times the overall rate of 4.6%.

That puts the UK slightly below the EU average of 15.5% for under-25s in September, according to Eurostat. Young people fare far worse in Spain (22.8%), France (21.9%) and Italy (19.9%). Germany (7.5%) and the Netherlands (8.8%) are well ahead.

The gap between the two measures points to the problem Milburn’s review is trying to tackle. Many young Britons are not counted as unemployed because they have stopped looking for work altogether.

Across the EU, Eurostat puts the NEET rate for 15- to 29-year-olds at 11% in 2025, down from 15.2% a decade earlier. Italy has made the biggest gains, cutting its rate from 25.7% to 13.3%.

## **Less cash, more support**

The report’s main proposal is a dedicated welfare system for the young that puts placements and support ahead of payments. It admits this could mean that “some people get less financial support at the beginning of their benefit claim than under the current system”.

Milburn argued that the present system can be “detrimental to their longer term life chances” by allowing young people to stay out of work.

Young people would face a “duty to engage”, meaning those who turn down offers of work could lose some benefits. Each would be given a dedicated adviser and a personalised route into employment. A national network of local services combining careers advice, job support, health care and practical help would be in place by 2029.

The proposals are likely to be politically sensitive. Former prime minister Keir Starmer was forced to water down his own welfare reforms after a revolt within the Labour Party.

To encourage hiring, the review suggests raising the earnings threshold at which employers start paying tax on wages for workers aged 21 to 24. It also proposes a £3,000 (€3,400) payment to firms that take on someone who has been NEET for six months, with extra help for small businesses and for hiring young people with disabilities.

“Rather than simply writing young people a short-term check, we have to build them a long-term future,” Milburn said.

## **‘Easy to get burnout’**

Milburn described the UK jobs market as “very fragile”, and young jobseekers say the competition is fierce.

Abigail Gomes, 21, has been looking for work for seven months since graduating from university. “There are just too many young people applying for one job,” she told AFP.

She said the constant cycle of applications and rejections “can create anxiety and depression among young people”, and that “it’s easy to get burnout”. Gomes has recently been offered a temporary retail job over Christmas.
