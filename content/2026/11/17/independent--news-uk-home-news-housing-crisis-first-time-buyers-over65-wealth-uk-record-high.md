Property owners aged 65 and over are holding a record £2.25 trillion in their homes, new research has found, with high levels of under-occupation while younger generations struggle to get on the ladder.

Up from £232 billion in 1995, older homeowners now hold 43 per cent of the £5.29 trillion of equity across the UK, according to the analysis from estate agent Savills. The generation accounts for less than a quarter of the total population.

The increase has been driven by long-term house price growth and a rising number of older homeowners, researchers say, with around 79 per cent owning their own home. In 2025, over 6 million UK households were owned by someone aged over 65, compared to 3.3 million in 1995.

At the same time, the average equity has risen 421 per cent, from £67,816 to £353,539.

Lucian Cook, head of residential research at Savills, said: “The extraordinary increase in housing wealth held by older homeowners reflects three decades of house price growth and a significant demographic shift.

“An ageing population means their numbers continue to grow, with many reaching retirement with little or no mortgage debt”.

A majority of older people are under-occupying their properties, official data has shown. Around 58 per cent of those aged 65 or over were doing so in 2024/25, according to the most recent English Housing Survey, representing 4.3 million households.

At the same time, rising house prices have made it harder for younger generations to buy their own homes. The median house price was 7.3 times average gross annual earnings in 2025, official data shows, rising from 3.9 in 1999.

This is down slightly from a Covid-era high of 9.1 per cent, but showcases the trend of rising low affordability for first-time buyers.

Meanwhile, Savills says that there is a shortage of older owner-occupiers downsizing, impeding the potential for housing wealth to be spread more equally.

Around 131,200 households downsized in the last financial year, researchers found, selling £60.7bn of property, and reinvesting £39.9bn, to unlock £20.8bn of equity.

Mr Cook said: “How efficiently that wealth moves between generations will be increasingly important. It could support retirement incomes, help younger family members onto or up the housing ladder and reduce mortgage debt.

“But a shortage of suitable homes, combined with the financial and practical costs of moving, means many older homeowners have little incentive to downsize.”

Looking at ownership across UK regions, researchers found that the largest concentration of housing wealth is held by those in the South East. Around 942,000 households hold a combined £468 billion of equity in the region.

Meanwhile, older homeowners in London hold the highest average amount of equity at an average £712,433.

The lowest average equity per homeowner is in the North East, where 264,000 households hold an average £187,406.
