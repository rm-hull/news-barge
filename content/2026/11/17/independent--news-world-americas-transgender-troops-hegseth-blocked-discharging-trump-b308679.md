A federal appeals court on Tuesday blocked the Pentagon from discharging more than two dozen transgender service members, months after determining that President Donald Trump’s administration unlawfully imposed the ban.

The decision implements a prior ruling by the same judicial panel and could set up a return to the Supreme Court, which previously allowed the policy to go forward in a separate case.

Trump's administration asked the U.S. Court of Appeals for the District of Columbia Circuit to delay implementing its June 1 ruling while appealing to the Supreme Court.

A three-judge panel voted 2-1 on Tuesday to reject the request for a stay.

The Supreme Court previously ruled in favor of the administration last year, permitting the Pentagon to enforce the ban while litigation continued.

Circuit Judge Robert Wilkins, partially joined by Judge Judith Rogers, ruled in June that the Pentagon policy was designed to exclude troops based on gender identity.

Their majority decision prevents the military from discharging current personnel in the suit, but does not allow new transgender recruits to join.

Attorneys representing the plaintiffs say 27 service members are covered by Tuesday’s order.

Shannon Minter, legal director of the National Center for LGBTQ Rights, called the plaintiffs "decorated troops who have met every standard the military sets."

"The Trump administration asked the court to let it keep pushing these service members toward the door while it tries to get a different answer from the Supreme Court. Today, the D.C. Circuit said no," Minter said in a statement.

On Tuesday, Wilkins stated the administration failed to show how keeping the plaintiffs in the military causes irreparable harm to the government or threatens national security. He noted the June 1 opinion was narrower than the injunction stayed by the Supreme Court last year.

"To the extent the government suggests that any injunction against the Executive Branch imposes irreparable harm, I am also unpersuaded," Wilkins wrote.

The panel's June 1 opinion partially upheld a March 2025 ruling by U.S. District Judge Ana Reyes, who concluded Trump’s executive order excluding transgender troops likely violates their constitutional rights.

In January 2025, Trump signed an executive order asserting that the sexual identity of transgender troops "conflicts with a soldier’s commitment to an honorable, truthful, and disciplined lifestyle, even in one’s personal life" and damages military readiness.

Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth issued a policy that presumptively disqualifies individuals with gender dysphoria from military service. Gender dysphoria is the distress experienced when a person's assigned gender and gender identity do not match, a condition linked to depression and suicidal thoughts.

Wilkins was nominated to the court by Democratic President Barack Obama, while Rogers was nominated by President Bill Clinton, also a Democrat.

Circuit Judge Justin Walker, nominated by Trump, a Republican, wrote a dissenting opinion in June stating judges lack authority to second-guess decisions excluding transgender troops. Walker voted to grant the government's request to pause the decision.
