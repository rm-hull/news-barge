## Is snow expected in York and North Yorkshire?

A person removing snow off their car in Tow Law, County Durham this morning. *(Image: PA)*

A yellow cold-health alert has come into force for York and North Yorkshire, which could particularly impact vulnerable people.

The UK Health Security Agency (UKHSA) warning also stretches across the North West and North East of England, as well as Yorkshire and the Humber and runs until 9am on Thursday (November 19).

The agency said low temperatures can result in increased requirements on health services and an increased risk to health for those over 65, and people with pre-existing health conditions, as well as other vulnerable groups including rough-sleepers.




Paul Coleman, Consultant in Public Health at UKHSA, said: "This is the latest cold-health alert of the season, and we can expect more as we move further into winter.

"Cold weather can have a serious impact on the health of some people, including those aged 65 and over and those with pre-existing health conditions, so it is vital to check in on friends, family and neighbours who are most vulnerable.

"We’re also reminding people to take simple steps to stay warm and look after their health. If you can, try to heat the rooms you spend most of your time in, such as your living room or bedroom, and dress warmly to help you stay safe and well during colder weather."

If follows news of a Met Office yellow snow alert for parts of North Yorkshire, as reported by The Press yesterday.

The warning, which stretches from Staffordshire in the West Midlands to south-west Scotland, has been in force from 12am until 11am today.

The Met Office has forecast heavy rain throughout the day in York but no snow is expected to fall in the city.

Snow has fallen in the North East this morning in County Durham.
