Ripon councillors have raised new fears over the impact of controversial plans for 190 new houses on a cherished Ripon nature reserve held in trust for the benefit of the city since World War Two.

Concern has been growing as Keepmoat Homes prepares to submit a formal planning application for the major housing development off the A61 on the southern edge of Ripon, near the tranquil beauty of much-loved Quarry Moor.

An extraordinary meeting this week saw members of Ripon City Council ask the developer to rethink the location of their proposals, which would see around eight hectares of neighbouring fields developed for housing and a new vehicle access point created off Harrogate Road.

Independent city councillor, Andrew Williams, Mayor of Ripon, said: "The site access simply can’t be allowed to be on the Harrogate Road, removing mature trees, making an already busy stretch of road even more dangerous.

"The developer needs to look again push their development further back to create a green buffer between the Quarry Moor Nature Reserve and any new housing.

"The site must be accessed from the bypass and not the Harrogate Road."

A Site of Special Scientific Interest, Quarry Moor was first donated to the people of Ripon by by Alderman Thomas F Spence in 1945 and has been run by the trustees of the Alderman T F Spence Charity under the stewardship of Ripon City Council.

Keepmoat Homes say the plans include 40 per cent ‘affordable’ housing, as well as a public open space, a children's play area and pedestrian walking routes.

But opponents of the plans now say a full Environmental Impact Assessment is required before a planning application is submitted to North Yorkshire Council.

In documents submitted to North Yorkshire Council, developer consultants Johnson Mowat acknowledge the development would permanently remove agricultural land and significantly change the appearance of the site.

But, Johnson Mowat argue, the scheme for 190 new homes would not have significant environmental effects requiring a full assessment.

North Yorkshire Council officers previously raised concerns about the potential impact on the protected site when an earlier proposal for 150 homes was considered.

Coun Williams added he was grateful to Councillor Sherrie Hunt for asking him to call an extraordinary council meeting to discuss the concerns related to the proposed development.
