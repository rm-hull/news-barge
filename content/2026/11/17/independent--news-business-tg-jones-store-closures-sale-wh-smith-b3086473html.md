TG Jones, the high street retail chain previously operating as WH Smith, has been placed on the market just 18 months after changing hands.

Private equity business Modella Capital purchased the 233-year-old brand last year in a deal worth approximately £42 million.

A subsequent major restructuring programme has already seen the closure of more than 100 outlets, leaving the group with 346 active locations.

The business, which currently maintains a workforce of around 3,500 employees, is expected to shutter dozens more stores to bring overall closures to roughly 150.

Modella is understood to have appointed advisors from Teneo to oversee a prospective sale process for TG Jones after receiving unsolicited approaches for the firm.

Any final transaction is unlikely to take place before the first quarter of 2027 at the earliest, according to *Sky News,* which originally reported the sale.

Alex Willson, chief executive of TG Jones, said: “As ever, our focus is on providing a great experience for customers, especially with the critical Christmas period coming up.

“We continue to execute our turnaround plan, which is improving our performance month by month.”

Joe Price, managing director of Modella Capital, said: “When TG Jones was acquired in 2025, it was a loss-making business with an uncertain future.

“Turning it around meant taking some hard decisions, including closing stores through a court-approved restructuring plan.

“We are extremely grateful to our colleagues from across the business for their continued support.

“The turnaround is starting to deliver, and we are seeing improved trading results.”

The potential sale process could pave the way for another major high street name to change hands as discount retailer Poundland seeks new owners.

Modella was among potential suitors for Poundland, although it is understood the group pulled back from the process.

Last week, Poundland filed a new notice of intention to appoint administrators to give it more breathing space to secure a possible rescue deal.
