# FirstGroup sees profits lift as it sells off Yorkshire bus depots in franchising drive

## Transport operator FirstGroup has seen its half-year profits lift in a period which saw the company sell off a string of its bus depots in Yorkshire.

The firm, which operates a large number of bus routes in Yorkshire, posted adjusted group pre-tax profit of £89.2m, up from £76.1m in the same period a year earlier. This came alongside adjusted group revenue of £1bn, up 21 per cent on a year prior.

Speaking on the results, FirstGroup chief executive officer, Graham Sutherland, said: “We have delivered on our commitments in H1 2027 and remain on course to maintain full year earnings per share, from a high-quality, cash generative earnings base. Alongside this, we have continued to secure strategically important contracts and unlock value from our expertise and well-capitalised asset base.”

Earlier this month, the company also announced an agreement with the West Yorkshire Combined Authority (WYCA) regarding the sale of five depots and 330 buses for £109m.

The sales come as South and West Yorkshire shift how their bus networks are run, with the regions eventually set to take the majority of bus routes under public control.

FirstGroup said that following the transition to franchising in West Yorkshire, South Yorkshire and Wales, its owned fleet of regional buses is expected to reduce to around 2,900 vehicles from around 4,500 currently.

It added that an estimated 300 vehicles that will not be used in franchising will be available to be cascaded to other depots, resulting in a roughly £10m further deferral of medium-term capital expenditure.

FirstGroup previously said it anticipates a post-tax disposal gain of around £30m in relation to the completed depot sales.

FirstGroup was also awarded two bus franchise contracts in Bramley and Huddersfield, where the group currently operates, as part of the first of three tranches of WYCA's franchise programme.

The contracts are due to commence in May 2027, with a core term of five years and an option to extend for a further two years.

FirstGroup said it anticipates combined annual revenues of around £40m from these contracts.

The move comes as part of West Yorkshire’s transition to a bus franchising system, led by West Yorkshire Mayor Tracy Brabin and the Combined Authority, which will see the bus network for the majority of services come under public control.

Through the scheme, the Combined Authority will manage the routes, frequencies and fares for the buses, with private firms given contracts to operate the routes. WYCA will own the depots and will purchase any electric buses, with operators required to lease them at a peppercorn lease.
