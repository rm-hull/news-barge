A Toll truck driver was fired from work in Victoria after using smartglasses to snap photos at an LPG terminal, in what was described as an “intrinsically unsafe” incident.

It is the first reported case in Australia of a worker dismissed for using smartglasses in a workplace.

The employee, Ricardo Martins Silva, was fired from Toll Transport in July 2025, after eight months with the company as an LPG (liquefied petroleum gas) tanker driver, the federal circuit court heard. His role was to drive a loaded LPG tanker for Origin Energy from the Long Island Point terminal in Hastings, Victoria, for onward delivery.

A federal circuit court judgment last week stated that on 17 June 2025, Martins Silva raised concerns about a truck allocated to him, telling superiors the truck had too much vibration and was jumping excessively.

The judgment stated he was advised to take a different truck, and when he went to the terminal, he took photographs using his smartglasses, and sent them to a superior with a message stating: “I’m going to take the truck back to Dandenong and I’m leaving! There’s no more time for deliveries today!”

The Esso worker at the terminal told Toll that any use of electrical equipment on-site is “intrinsically unsafe” due to the risk of an explosion caused by electrical spark.

Martins Silva was prevented from taking on additional work until an investigation into the incident had been completed, and Origin Energy later asked the employee be removed from work as soon as possible because “his attitude is poor” and “not doing planned deliveries because he is ‘tired and stressed’ is not acceptable”, the judgment read.

Martins Silva sent emails between 17 and 19 June raising concerns about his role, truck allocations and safety issues. The worker on 20 June submitted a complaint about his employment, including safety, and impact of working conditions on his health, among other things.

On 22 June, the employee was advised he was stood down, the judgment stated.

At a meeting on 24 June, what Toll termed as “the glasses incident” was raised with the employee. He was told it was regarded as a “critical safety breach” and that Toll had commenced an investigation process due to its seriousness.

By 30 June, the company decided Martins Silva should be suspended from employment citing, among other reasons, the seriousness of the glasses incident. On 9 July, the employee was informed that taking a photograph with the smartglasses was in breach of Toll’s code of practice, and his contract of employment. His employment was terminated on 18 July, the judgment stated.

Martins Silva claimed the action was in breach of the Fair Work Act, arguing Toll’s actions were as a result of complaints he had made.

The federal circuit court found in the ruling last week that the glasses incident was “on any objective view a serious incident”.

“On the day he wore the smart glasses, Mr Martins Silva was plainly aware of the risks of what he was doing – he went so far as to seek to mitigate the risk by covering his smartglasses with safety glasses,” the ruling from federal circuit justice Karl Blake stated.

“The glasses incident was not concocted or made to look more serious than what it was. It was objectively very serious.”

Martins Silva had conceded in cross examination that he knew at the time he had done the wrong thing, Blake’s judgment stated, but had written to his employer at the time that his judgment had been affected by the unresolved trauma of a truck fire in which he had been injured in March 2025.

Blake found Martins Silva was not dismissed because he made complaints but “because he wore smartglasses” at the terminal and his employer was of the view that it amounted to a serious safety incident. The application was dismissed.

While the case had focused on safety concerns, since the arrival of Meta’s Ray-Bans and cheaper alternatives such as the Kmart Anko brand glasses, governments have faced pressure to ban the devices over privacy concerns.

Councils across Australia have banned the glasses from some public spaces including gyms and pools, while the federal government is considering bans in government offices.
