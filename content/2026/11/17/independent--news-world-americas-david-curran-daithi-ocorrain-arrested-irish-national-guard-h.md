An Irish national was indicted for breaking into and destroying a $60 million U.S. Air National Guard C-130 Hercules aircraft parked on a taxiway at Shannon Airport in County Clare, Ireland, the DOJ announced on Tuesday.

Daithi O’Corrain, also known as David Curran, is accused of carrying out a planned attack “against a U.S. military aircraft, causing tens of millions of dollars in damage and jeopardizing critical military operations,” the FBI said in a statement.

On April 11, O’Corrain “breached the security perimeter” of the airport and gained entry to the aircraft by breaking the lock on a crew door and barricaded himself inside while inflicting “severe damage to the interior,” according to the indictment.

The 48-year-old was charged with destruction of government property, which carries a maximum sentence of 10 years in prison.

O’Corrain is accused of cutting wire bundles and hydraulic lines, destroying every gauge in the Aircraft’s cockpit, and puncturing the Aircraft’s air ducts.

![O’Corrain is accused of destroying every gauge in the Aircraft’s cockpit and puncturing the Aircraft’s air ducts](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/17/20/48/Screenshot-2026-11-17-at-3-23-52-PM.png?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The government also alleges O’Corrain brought multiple capsules of gallium, a highly corrosive metal that is a liquid at room temperature, and eats through aluminum in minutes and causes long-term corrosion to steel.

Hours after he entered the airport, the flight crew returned to the aircraft to depart and spotted O’Corrain through a cockpit window and notified Shannon Airport security.

![O’Corrain allegedly escaped through the aircraft roof while holding an axe](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/17/20/42/Screenshot-2026-11-17-at-3-23-45-PM.png?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Crew members then watched him escape out of a hatch in the aircraft’s roof, allegedly striking with an axe.

O’Corrain was then arrested by Irish law enforcement.

The Department of Justice alleges O’Corrain’s attack effectively destroyed the aircraft.

“The damage inflicted rendered the Aircraft inoperable, and the Aircraft—worth approximately $60 million—will not return to active flight status,” the indictment said.

“This was not an act of spontaneous vandalism. The defendant allegedly spent months researching the aircraft, acquiring tools and materials, and preparing to maximize the destruction he could inflict.”
