# DWP confirms cash boost available for some state pensioners retired before 2016

## The Department for Work and Pensions (DWP) has confirmed that some older state pensioners may be entitled to additional state pension payments

The Department for Work and Pensions (DWP) has supplementary funds available for certain older state pensioners in particular circumstances.

Eligible older state pensioners can claim the additional state pension (AP) on top of their standard state pension - but only if they satisfy specific birth-date requirements (men born before 6 April 1951, women before 6 April 1953) and weren't contracted out of the scheme.

The basic pre-2016 state pension increases by 3.9% to an estimated £192.10 weekly (£374 additional annually) under the triple lock mechanism, while AP and selected other benefits rise by 3.5% from April. AP doesn't have a set figure; payments vary based on years of National Insurance contributions, earnings history, contracting out status, and any basic pension enhancement (which was only available between 12 October 2015 and 5 April 2017), reports Birmingham Live.

The DWP clarifies in its official guidance: "The additional state pension is an extra amount of money you could get on top of your basic State Pension if you're: a man born before 6 April 1951; a woman born before 6 April 1953."

It continues: "You get the additional state pension automatically if you're eligible for it, unless you've contracted out of it. The additional state pension is paid with your basic state pension. There is no fixed amount for the additional state pension. How much you get depends on: how many years you paid National Insurance for your earnings; whether you've contracted out of the scheme; whether you topped up your basic state pension (this was only possible between 12 October 2015 and 5 April 2017)."

The UK's Parliament website has confirmed: "Other state pension and benefit rates covered by my statutory review will be increased by 3.5%, in line with the increase in the consumer prices index in the year to September 2026.

"This includes most working-age benefits and other benefits for people below state pension age; benefits to help with additional needs arising from disability; statutory payments including statutory sick pay and statutory maternity pay; and additional state pension. The pension credit savings credit maximum amount will also increase by 3.5%."

Consumer magazine Which? explains: "Before 2002, you could only contribute to the additional state pension (then known as the State Earnings-Related Pension Scheme, or SERPS) if you were employed.

"However, under the state second pension scheme, which ran from 2002 to 2016, you could contribute through your National Insurance contributions if you were: an employee earning at least £113 a week; caring for one or more children under 12 and claiming child benefit; claiming carer's credit; claiming certain disability-related benefits.

"There is no fixed amount for the additional state pension. The amount of additional state pension you'll get depends on how many years you paid National Insurance for, how much you earned and whether you contracted out of the scheme."
