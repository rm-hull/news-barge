Upmarket retailer David Jones collapsed into administration earlier this month owing well over $400m, after accelerating financial losses and reputational damage threatened its ability to stock shelves for Christmas.

About 500 creditors attended its first meeting on Tuesday, where administrators McGrathNicol broke down the 188-year-old chain’s massive debt pile, including an estimated $180m owed to suppliers and $11m to employees.

There is also about $33m in unredeemed gift cards held by shoppers, which the retailer is still accepting, and significant sums owed to secured creditors including the retailer’s main financier, Hilco.

David Jones collapsed into voluntary administration in early November after years of steep losses left the heritage retailer falling behind on rent and supplier payments.

The retailer is set to shutter half of its 38 department stores by the end of the year, triggering mass job losses under a sweeping restructure.

Under a dual appointment, FTI Consulting has day-to-day control of operations, while administrators McGrathNicol seek a buyer or fresh injection of capital.

David Jones recorded a net loss of $228m last financial year, according to updated figures disclosed to creditors. It lost a further $69m in the first four months of the new financial year leading up to the administration.

Creditors were told that the funding pressures it was facing, coupled with reputational damage, was affecting its ability to get stock for the Christmas trading period.

Guardian Australia has reported that some suppliers are owed money from stock sent as early as April.
