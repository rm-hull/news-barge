A JD Wetherspoon pub in a historic city centre building will get upgraded heating and air conditioning after plans were approved.

Leeds City Council granted listed building consent for internal works at the Beckett’s Bank on Park Row.

The building, a former bank chambers which dates back to the 1920s, will be fitted with new heating, ventilation and air conditioning (HVAC) ductwork.

A planning report said the modernised equipment would improve an existing downstairs kitchen and first floor toilets at the site, which sits among historic city centre buildings.

It said: “The proposed works would preserve the features and special architectural or historic interest of the listed building.

“The proposed works will not affect the external appearance or fabric of the listed building.

“As such the proposal would not impact upon the character, appearance or setting of the Leeds City Centre Conservation Area.”

The report said the four-storey former Barclay’s branch was built around 1925 and converted into a pub in the early 2000s.

Upper floors of the building at 28-30 Park Row were converted for residential use in 2004.

The report said: “The main bar and customer area retains original interior detail, including ceiling cornices and decorated columns.

“Various modern alteration to the rear of the ground and first floors, including modern ducting, has been undertaken, these changes being made when the building was converted to a public house.”

Wetherspoon said the building alterations were need to help the Beckett’s Bank continue trading efficiently as a pub.

A design report by the company said: “The property is a Grade II-listed asset and therefore there is an obligation on the owners for the upkeep of the building.

“The proposed intention of the works is to ensure the future of the building and its attractiveness. The proposal will help to maintain the future of this heritage asset.”
