More than 2,000 children have been lifted out of poverty following a major change to benefit rules.

In York and North Yorkshire, 2,200 children from families on Universal Credit are no longer in poverty after the Government scrapped the two-child benefit limit, according to new statistics published on November 17.

The policy change was announced in the Autumn Budget in November 2025 and took effect in April 2026.

Pat McFadden, Secretary of State for Work and Pensions, said: "This is about changing the story of children's lives.

"Children who grow up in poverty are more likely to have mental health difficulties, to be NEET and to earn less as adults.

"We want to give every child a genuine shot at life – to do well at school, stay healthy, succeed at work – ultimately contributing to their community as adults.

"Removing the two-child limit is the most cost-effective way to drive down child poverty rates and is an investment worth making – for those children, for their families, and for the whole country."

The data shows that in May 2025, 21,500 children in the York and North Yorkshire Combined Authority area were living in poverty, compared to 18,300 by May 2026.

This represents a 15 per cent reduction in child poverty among Universal Credit families in the region.

Lucy Powell, Education Secretary, said: "No child's future should be decided by the circumstances they are born into.

"Yet for too long, growing up in poverty has held children back, at school and in the years that follow.

"These first figures show us what is possible.

"Scrapping the two-child limit has already lifted 300,000 children out of poverty and given them a springboard for the future.

"But we are not stopping there.

"Free school meals, free breakfast clubs and more childcare are easing the pressure on families and will help tackle one of the root causes of the NEETs crisis to get young people earning and learning."

Across Great Britain, 300,000 children have been lifted out of poverty so far, with projections rising to 450,000 by the end of the current parliament.

When including the full set of measures in the Government’s Child Poverty Strategy, the total number of children expected to be lifted out of poverty reaches 550,000.

The statistics are broken down by constituency, covering Harrogate and Knaresborough, Richmond and Northallerton, Scarborough and Whitby, Selby, Skipton and Ripon, Thirsk and Malton, Wetherby and Easingwold, York Central, and York Outer.

The figures were released on the same day as Alan Milburn’s final report on ending Britain’s NEET crisis, which looks at how to help more young people into work and learning.
