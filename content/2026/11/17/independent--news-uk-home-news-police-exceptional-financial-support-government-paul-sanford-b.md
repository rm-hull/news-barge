Growing financial pressures facing English police forces have driven officers to "retreat from the streets", a chief constable has warned.

Paul Sanford, chief constable of Norfolk police and finance lead at the National Police Chiefs’ Council (NPCC), told *The Guardian* that nine forces are currently requesting exceptional financial support (EFS) from the central government.

The EFS scheme offers temporary state financial help in situations where local bodies face severe financial pressures. He stated forces across Greater Manchester, Durham, Cleveland, Bedfordshire, South Yorkshire, Norfolk, Gloucestershire, Wiltshire and Lincolnshire will struggle without EFS.

"Demand has gone up, population has increased. There is a growth in complex cases, sexual offending and historic inquiries, and civil orders. All of those are the right thing to do, but they’ve not come with the funding required," said Chief Constable Sanford.

"That is why you see the retreat from the streets. Policing is in worse financial shape now than it has been for decades." He added that those forces which applied for EFS believe they are "close to the tipping point where the ability to deliver core services can no longer be sustained".

A Home Office spokesperson said police force funding was at a “historic high of £18.5bn”.

“We have established the Financial Resilience Framework to ensure a consistent approach to assessing financial resilience in policing,” they said.

“We are also reviewing the police funding formula to ensure the system is fair and continues to prioritise public safety. The new approach to funding will be brought in as part of our reforms to police structures.”

One of the biggest impacts on forces’ budgets will be the government’s early release scheme for prisoners, which will see 5,000 detainees released ahead of schedule in the next year in an attempt to ease overcrowding in jails.

The NPCC estimated crime would rise by up to 6 per cent from the scheme and place further strain on dispatching police officers.

It also projected the funding gap for forces by 2030 would rise from £3bn to £4.3bn.

Pressure on police forces was also flagged earlier this month by Metropolitan Police Commissioner Sir Mark Rowley during a speech in Manchester.

![Pressure on police forces was also flagged earlier this month by Metropolitan Police Commissioner Sir Mark Rowley during a speech in Manchester](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/21/15/37/GettyImages-1716219521-f0292dbe.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Sir Mark warned that the country faces a choice between policing reform and a decline in services.

He also addressed a decrease in police funding – from around 0.9 per cent of GDP to around 0.7 per cent across England and Wales since 2010.

The commissioner argued that eight to 10 regional police forces across England and Wales should be supported by hundreds of local policing commands and a new national police service.

“I believe this is a choice between the third greatest revolution in British policing or the inevitable decline and fewer bobbies on the beat,” he said.

Chief Constable Sanford’s comments come ahead of this week’s NPCC and Association of Police and Crime Commissioners (APCC) conference in Westminster.

Home secretary Shabana Mahmood will address the conference.

At the same event last year, she hinted at introducing major reforms to policing in England and Wales.

She told police leaders that “the structure of our police forces is, if we are honest, irrational,” and there were “disparities in performance” between forces.
