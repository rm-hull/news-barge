# Hull taxi driver dies in horror crash after altercation as man, 62, arrested

## A 62-year-old man has been arrested after a taxi driver died in a crash in Hull following an alleged altercation shortly before the incident.

At around 9.50pm on Saturday (Nov 14), emergency services were called to Hanley Road in Hull following reports that a blue Ford Tourneo Connect taxi had crashed into a garden fence.

The driver, a 55-year-old man, was pronounced dead at the scene.

Detectives investigating the circumstances surrounding his death have since established that the taxi driver was involved in an altercation with another man shortly before the crash.

A 62-year-old man has been arrested in connection with the investigation and remains in police custody.

![Hanley Road, Hull](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/jpim-static/image/2026/11/17/11/45/Hanley-Road-Hull.png?trim=0,3,0,0&crop=&width=640&quality=65)


Detective Chief Inspector Leanne Murphy, who is leading the investigation, said: “We remain in the very early stages of our investigation, and my officers continue their work to ascertain the full circumstances of this incident.

“At this time, it is believed that the 55-year-old taxi driver and another 62-year-old man were involved in an altercation on Hanley Road before the driver left the area in the Ford.

“Shortly after the taxi driver pulled away, it is believed the car left the carriageway and collided with a garden fence before he was found to have died at the scene.

“His family have been informed and are being supported by officers at this difficult time.

“As part of our investigation, the 62-year-old man has since been arrested. He remains in police custody at this time assisting with our enquiries.

“I would appeal to anyone who was in the area of the collision or witnessed the altercation to come forward with information. We are particularly interested in speaking to anyone who witnessed the Ford prior to the collision on Hanley Road.

“Anyone with information that may be useful to our investigation is encouraged to call us on our non-emergency number 101 quoting log 477 of 14 November 2026.”
