# Closer ties with EU would give us worst of both worlds: Yorkshire Post Letters

## MP Laycock, Lund Lane, Killinghall, Harrogate.

Andy Brown, (The Yorkshire Post, October 26), is right to point out that, “the soft alignment with the EU (of the current Government) risks giving us the worst of both worlds. Getting access to the single market while outside the EU means following the same standards as everyone else without any say.”

He is also right to say that “asking nicely if we could be allowed to change our minds and come back in on much worse terms than we left”, “does not seem like a very sensible way forward”.

He also asks that the EU recognise “that many of its members don't actually want to be part of a European superstate…”.

![A union and EU flag flying outside the Palace of Westminster, London in 2019.](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOmEyMTgzMDU2LTZmZGQtNGYxZC05MTk2LTc3YjI5NDM5ZDhkYjo2NWQzMzkzYS1mNTRiLTRlMDAtYjVlOS00NTM1OTRiZmEwZjk=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


Unfortunately for those members, a “European superstate” is precisely what the EU project is about.

A “Common Market” and economic co-operation have been a cover for achieving this.

It would be good for us to have “a sensible degree of collaboration” with our EU neighbours but, so long as they remain determined to punish us for leaving, prospects for achieving this are not good.

**Gareth Robson, Kent House Road, Beckenham.**

Another letter from TW Jefferson (The Yorkshire Post, November 6) suggesting that nations cannot keep their identity if they are part of larger groupings such as the EU, and accusing me of wanting to see the UK (or England and Scotland) "subsumed" into the EU.

What a contrast with the words of Sir Patrick Stewart and John le Carré elegantly quoted by James Bovington in his letter published alongside TWJ's – calling for international co-operation rather than the psycho-drama of national delusion which lured so many to vote for the false promise of sovereignty.

James reminds us of the pithy quote from Dean Acheson about Britain being unable to understand the reality that after a brief era of dominance it has returned to the second tier of nations.

By 2016 roughly half of us still needed to grasp that; I wonder how many more have been enlightened by the realities the country has faced since it struck out alone. Individuals are not subsumed when we marry or form partnerships; yes, there have to be compromises and we have to accommodate each other – but we are the better for it.

The EU is made by European nations. It's not our enemy. It's not a foreign power.

It is our own central council and it cannot move very far beyond what all of us collectively accept – bitter medicine at times but we know it has to be swallowed. If over recent decades we had all shown more co-operation and less strutting national ego then our Europe might be better placed to resist the pressures from better-integrated power blocs.

Plenty in the USA indulge in fantasies of secession from their union - but they are too vastly outnumbered by their more mature peers who understand trade-off and compromise.

The Chinese chafe against draconian central power but none of them think their individual provinces would be better-off on their own.

Britain tried its own union of very unequal nations over recent centuries but it's too small for today's world – if those two referenda had gone the other way we would by now be independent countries prospering and co-operating within a larger and more equitable union. If only. Perhaps it's not too late.

I don't fear being subsumed; I fear being left out in the cold (with another nod to Le Carré).
