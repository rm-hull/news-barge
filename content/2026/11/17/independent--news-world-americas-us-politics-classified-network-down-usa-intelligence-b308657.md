A “highly classified network” used by the National Security Agency for internal communications has gone down, multiple sources familiar with the matter told CNN on Tuesday.

NSA employees use the network, known as NSA Net, to share classified materials internally. CNN reported that there was no indication of a cyberattack. As a precaution, the Maryland-based intelligence agency has “enacted staggered arrivals for personnel.”

The NSA collects and analyzes foreign electronic communications and protects sensitive government communications. Its exact workforce is classified, but it is estimated to have tens of thousands of employees.

The outage is the latest in a string of high-profile technology failures, several of which have disrupted daily life across the U.S.

On Thursday, Microsoft Outlook, Teams and AI Copilot were hit by a massive outage that threw workplaces around the world into disarray and stretched into Friday. Microsoft attributed the disruption to a software bug compounded by heavier traffic and reduced server capacity.

![A 'highly classified network' used by the National Security Agency has gone down, multiple sources familiar with the matter told CNN on Tuesday. Pictured: the NSA headquarters at Fort Meade, Maryland](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/17/16/1227141714..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

On Friday, the FAA ordered a rare nationwide groundstop — the longest since 9/11 — that led to thousands of flight cancellations and left passengers stranded across the country. Federal officials blamed the disruption on a routine router upgrade in Arkansas.

Then on Monday, a technical issue at JPMorgan Chase rippled through the U.S. financial system, preventing thousands of customers from accessing their online accounts. A spokesperson said the bank had sustained “a temporary issue related to a software bug in an on-premise hardware system.”

None of these outages have been attributed to cyberattacks or malicious behavior.

*The Independent* has contacted the agency for comment.

The spate of outages prompted tech experts to warn of weaknesses in the infrastructure underpinning the modern economy.

“The real lesson is how many separate services quietly depend on the same few providers,” Danielle Lemons, vice president of operations at Nashville-based IT firm DirecTech Connect, told *The Independent. “*When one of them goes down, email, file sharing, sign-ins and even phone systems can fail together, because they were all built on the same foundation.”

”The real cause for concern isn't a single master threat—it is the underlying reality that our digital economy has become so hyper-connected that isolated software bugs, database corruptions, or routing errors now trigger immediate, high-visibility disruption across millions of lives,” added Fran Villalba Segarra, founder and CEO of INTERNXT, a cybersecurity company based in Spain.
