As U.S. gas prices begin to come down after months of highs caused by the war with Iran, a top industry analyst has argued the relief could have come “far earlier” had President Donald Trump done more to help.

Tehran responded to the Operation Epic Fury airstrikes launched by the U.S. and Israel on February 28 by closing off the Strait of Hormuz, through which approximately one-fifth of the world’s oil is shipped, creating a shortage designed to drive up fuel prices and place economic pressure on the Trump administration.

For many months, the strategy worked, with U.S. gas prices spending at least 158 days above the $4 per gallon mark this year – sparking widespread consumer backlash and disaffection with Trump.

Fuel costs are now finally beginning to come down as the conflict languishes in stalemate and the president promises an end is in sight, with the AAA recording the national average at $3.78 per gallon Tuesday morning and GasBuddy at $3.77.

Patrick De Haan, head of petroleum analysis at GasBuddy, wrote on X in response to the gradual easing: “Had Trump chosen to make a deal with Iran or improve the geopolitical tensions earlier, gas prices would’ve declined earlier.”

![Gas prices have begun to fall in the U.S. after months of highs caused by the war in Iran](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/05/20/31/GettyImages-2296974309.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&auto=webp)

In another post, he was more specific: “If the Iran situation had been resolved months and months ago prices would have fallen far earlier. By extending the uncertainty over Iran/Strait, high gas prices were extended. Now they’re easing as Trump signals lack of new escalation.”

The president paid a price for the war at this month’s midterms, when the American electorate delivered a stunning rebuke by sweeping opposition Democrats back to control of the House of Representatives and Senate as they won memorable victories in deep-red states from Texas to Mississippi.

Vice President JD Vance was quick to blame the Republican shellacking on the war in a lengthy post on X, with which the president said he broadly agreed while insisting the conflict had been necessary to prevent Iran from acquiring a nuclear weapon and saying he would not act otherwise if he had his time again.

Speaking to Stephen A Smith at the White House last week for the commentator’s *Straight Shooter* podcast, Trump said: “Maybe the war was not unpopular as a war but unpopular because of the fact that the gasoline prices were higher, oil prices, and I actually thought that was something that people would be able to handle pretty well.”

He went on to say voters needed to understand that paying more for gas in the short-term was worth it to ensure the threat posed by Iran was neutralized, blaming the GOP’s candidates for failing to sell that message.

![President Donald Trump has acknowledged in post-midterms interviews that the war and its impact on fuel prices led to the downfall of Republicans at this month’s elections but refused to accept any blame personally](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/13/16/32/Trump-Smith.png?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&auto=webp)

Trump did not acknowledge any personal blame for the disaster, nor the possibility that his supporters might also have felt betrayed by his reneging on his 2024 election pledge not to engage in any further “forever wars” in the Middle East.

The fact that gas prices reached a recent high on Election Day – November 3 – but have since fallen has already been the subject of baseless MAGA conspiracy theorizing.

Colorado Republican Rep. Lauren Boebert, who just lost her seat to Democrat Eileen Laubacher, said in an interview with NewsNation last week before her fate was officially sealed that her party had been “played by the markets.”

Asked to clarify what she meant by anchor Blake Burman, the congresswoman answered: “I have gas that is under $3 a gallon in my district now, when everything was closer to $6 a gallon just over a week ago. There needs to be some accountability there.”

Boebert appeared to be implying that gas prices had been fixed to make Trump look as bad as possible when voters went to the polls and had since been allowed to return to normal, without offering any evidence to support her claim.
