World of Warcraft: Forever has a unique problem—it's intended to be a "forever" version of baseline Azeroth, with no planned level-increasing expansions and an emphasis on horizontal progression with only minor vertical item level increases.

But expansion packs are more than just a stack of a few levels, they're also an opportunity to adjust to the MMO economy. Which so often suffers because, well, you're essentially printing money *all the time.* Every enemy killed, every resource node farmed, every trash item sold, effectively adds more money into the world.

There are ways to remove this money, sure. Repair costs, flight paths, goldsinks like a mount grind, dual spec, or respec fees. But if someone's dedicated to grinding, they can put together a hundred gold in a few hours. Or, in this case, thousands.

As shared by WoWHead, a gold farm was discovered in Blackmaw Hold, one of its new dungeons, and thoroughly rinsed over the past couple of days: "In Blackmaw Hold, there is a room filled with around 30 Blackmaw Devotees. These creatures are non-elites that only auto-attack, but can drop a lot of loot."

All of that loot adds up. Silver drops and vendor items, motes, quest items—and hey, sometimes there's just a good grinding spot in an MMO. I personally made my mount money by rinsing a lucrative little ogre spot with a lot of iron ore. But in that case, I was able to scrape together a humble 15 gold an hour and a half, an honest wage for an honest day's work, whereas these folks have been raking in thousands.

Here's a clip of one such streamer leisurely jogging about with 4,000+ gold in his bags: "I slept for four hours, man, if I didn't sleep, I could've hit 5k."

This is, uh, bad—streamers are in a sense the very tip of the iceberg. If their farming spot has breached containment, then you can bet that ambitious gold sellers and farmers have already been on that grindset for longer. Exactly how much longer is up for debate, mind, since Blackmaw Hold is a high-level dungeon; but these guys are efficient. Even my leisurely pace, taking in the sights, has seen me hit level 51 since launch.

Unsurprisingly, parts of the community are baying for blood: "All of the gold that entered into the economy needs to be removed and everyone involved needs to be banned. This single exploit left unaddressed will affect WoW Forever for its entire lifespan. This gold was created by vendoring items in a raw gold farm. It's gold that was never meant to, designed to, or expected to enter into circulation. Inflation is about to ripple everywhere," writes one player on the WoW subreddit.

Making things slightly more awkward for Blizzard is the fact that the aforementioned streamer I linked above, Ziqoftw, has commentated on PvP tournaments in an official capacity before. He's made the argument on his X account that this wasn't an exploit, and that while the farm was absolutely very *lucrative,*a hotfix doesn't necessarily mean an* exploit* happened.

"Hotfixing something because it's too strong or because they don't want players to do it doesn't mean it's bugged, it means that it's too strong," Ziqo writes.

The game's terms of service define "exploits of any in-game bugs, and thereby granting you and/or any other user an advantage over other players not using such methods" as grounds for a ban, so I suppose the question is whether this was considered a bug or not.

On the one hand, it's definitely unintended, otherwise it would've been hotfixed. On the other, MMO players are like water—they'll seep into any weakness your game design has. Should Blizzard have, as Ziqo points out, given this massive group of enemies in a dungeon the exact same loot table as a normal, grindable mob out in the open world? I could probably tell you that's a bad idea.

The question right now is: What the hell does Blizzard do about it? I've reached out to the developer for comment, and I'll update this article if I receive a response.
