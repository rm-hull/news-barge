# Alan Milburn calls for ‘radical reprioritisation’ of Whitehall spending to tackle youth unemployment crisis

## Publishing his long-awaited report, the former Cabinet minister said “this is not a crisis of aspiration; it is a crisis of opportunity”.

Alan Milburn urged Andy Burnham to cut Whitehall budgets to fund his sweeping changes to tackle youth unemployment or risk a “lost generation” of jobless youngsters.

The former cabinet minister called on backbench Labour MPs to back his plan, even though welfare cuts could mean some youngsters having their benefits reduced.

The Government-commissioned report calls for billions of taxpayers’ money to be spent creating opportunities for those aged under 25 to get off benefits and into work, partly offsetting the cost by squeezing welfare payments.

Launching his report, Mr Milburn said: “Britain is at risk of losing a generation of young people, not because they lack talent, not because they lack ambition, not because they do not want to work.

“This is not a crisis of aspiration; it is a crisis of opportunity.”

He said “half measures will not do” because “the scale of the problem is just too big for that”.

![Alan Milburn speaking at Google offices in London, about his final report into young people not in employment, education or training (Neets).](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/jpim-static/image/2026/11/17/12/23/01M7MQ86V5XGSZY100065AKMRF.jpeg?trim=0,0,0,0&width=640&quality=65)


Earlier this year, the number of 16 to 24-year-olds not in education, employment or training, so called neets, hit one million.

However, highlighting the lack of opportunity, Mr Milburn revealed that one young person he had spoken to had applied for 2,500 jobs, while six in 10 of the neets have never had a job.

The report calls for the Government to spend around £5.8bn a year by 2036-37, funding a new youth opportunity service and support, including tax breaks and bonuses for firms employing young people.

The cost would be offset by £3.6bn of annual welfare savings and £2.4bn of extra tax revenue from people in work.

Mr Milburn demanded rapid action from the Government, with a “downpayment” of £500m in the next financial year to fund the package he set out, arguing it must be a top priority in next year’s spending review.

“The majority could come from a radical reprioritisation of government spending,” Mr Milburn added.

Mr Milburn said there was a “clear political imperative” for Mr Burnham and Chancellor John Healey to press ahead with the measures.

![Alan Milburn, Prime Minister Andy Burnham and Education Secretary Lucy Powell during a visit to Alstom Transport in Derby in July (PA)](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/jpim-static/image/2026/11/16/00/v2-image-production-9d28fe439a2bad26e27f5446c4381cf6Y29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzk0ODcxMzg2-2.85437364.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


“Prime Minister Burnham is a half-term prime minister and so he needs to get on with it. And I know that he wants to. So I’m very hopeful indeed.”

The Government will create a new youth opportunities unit reporting directly to the Prime Minister in response to the Milburn report, Work and Pensions Secretary Pat McFadden told MPs.

However, he declined to commit to all recommendations, instead saying a full response would be set out in the new year.

Previous attempts to cut the welfare bill have been derailed by opposition within Labour but Mr Milburn said the party as a whole must get behind the reforms.

It already appears as if MPs who rebelled against previous welfare changes are prepared to get behind Mr Milburn’s plans.

Calder Valley MP Josh Fenton-Glynn, a vocal opponent of cuts to the budget proposed by Rachel Reeves, told The Yorkshire Post: “This is a very different proposition than what we were offered in summer 2025.

“It’s a serious attempt to support people into work. The devil is in the details but there is a lot to support.”

Dr Marie Tidball, the Labour MP for Penistone and Stocksbridge who previously rebelled over the changes to disability benefit, said: “I am pleased to hear there won’t be ‘crude cuts’ to benefits, and that reforms to fundamentally change our welfare system are instead focused on increasing the participation of young disabled people in society.”

York Central’s Rachael Maskell, who was temporarily suspended by Labour for consistently rebelling, described it as a “sea change” for the previous proposals.

West Yorkshire Mayor Tracy Brabin, who was on Mr Milburn’s expert panel, said she thought it was just the kind of challenge the Prime Minister would take on.

She told this paper: “I think this is a compelling document, it's a vision for change and I think Andy is in the market for rewiring the system for better outcomes.

“My understanding is that Alan got a standing ovation in the PLP (Parliamentary Labour Party meeting) which is probably unheard of and it does feel that people understand the imperative.

“I know Andy's in the market to be radical and having those difficult conversations, as you've seen about social care, about the European Union, I think he's going to run with this.”
