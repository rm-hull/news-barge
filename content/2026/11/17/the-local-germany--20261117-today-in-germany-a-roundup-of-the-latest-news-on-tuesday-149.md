**Tuesday's top story:Tesla to invest three billion euros in German 'Gigafactory'**

Tesla will invest three billion euros to expand its Grünheide "Gigafactory" outside Berlin, the company said on Monday. A new facility will eventually produce up to one million driverless Cybercabs a year, with construction due to begin in the first half of 2027 and production in 2029.

The move contrasts sharply with the rest of Germany's struggling auto sector. Volkswagen is cutting tens of thousands of jobs, and Mercedes said last week it would halt all investment in Germany and was weighing factory closures.

The Grünheide plant opened in 2022 after a tumultuous approval process. Tesla scaled back some plans after residents opposed them in a non-binding poll, and activists camped in treehouses to block expansion.

Tesla, which employs around 11,000 people at the site, says it is already seeking hundreds of new workers. IG Metall has previously accused it of poor working conditions and covert redundancies, but regional leader Jan Otto called the expansion "good news" that would create and safeguard jobs.

**Big electricity firms used market power to inflate prices during energy crisis: Ifo**

Germany's five biggest electricity companies used their market power to charge higher prices during the 2021-22 energy crisis, according to a study by the Ifo Institute. The price spikes "cannot be explained solely by higher generation costs", it said.

The institute estimates the largest producers made between 10.7 and 20.5 billion euros in extra revenue from September 2021 to the end of 2022. Researcher Till Fladung said record prices were "not solely a consequence of the gas price shock, but also of a structure that allows for the abuse of market power".

The team simulated prices under competitive conditions and compared them with real prices. The two tracked each other until September 2021. After that, market prices averaged about 45 euros per megawatt-hour above the simulation, and still around 20 euros under the most conservative assumptions.

RWE, LEAG, EnBW, Uniper and Vattenfall hold about 56 percent of conventional power plant capacity. Fladung and co-author Anna Saile of the University of Bayreuth urge more storage and flexibility, broader ownership of controllable plants and better regulation of price formation.

Ifo warns the trend is going the other way. The tender for new gas-fired plants set no cap on awards per bidder, and RWE and LEAG won 94 percent of the subsidised capacity in the first round.

**Right-wing extremists "well-established" in Thuringia**

Right-wing extremists are firmly established in Thuringia, according to the state's Office for the Protection of the Constitution. Interior Minister Georg Maier (SPD) and the office's president, Stephan Kramer, presented the latest report in Erfurt on Monday.

They said membership had surged last year, particularly in the non-partisan and largely unstructured far-right extremist spectrum. About 400 members of the scene are classed as violence-prone.

The far-right landscape is still dominated by the Alternative for Germany (AfD), which the state office classifies as definitively far-right. With about 2,500 members, it far outstrips the minor parties Die Heimat and Der III. Weg.

The report says the AfD continues to recruit and is institutionalising ideological training for future leaders, having founded a so-called Thuringia Political Academy in November.

The number of "Reich citizens" and "self-governing citizens" rose by about 10 percent to roughly 1,100. They deny the existence and sovereignty of the Federal Republic and reject its laws and representatives.

The office puts the number of violent left-wing extremists at around 220, a slight rise on the previous year, with Jena and its surroundings a regional hub for the autonomous scene.

**Wirtz scores twice as Germany thrash Netherlands 5-0 to reach nations league last eight**

Florian Wirtz scored twice and Brajan Gruda added a goal and two assists as Germany beat the Netherlands 5-0 in Berlin on Monday to reach the Nations League quarterfinals.

Derry Scherhant and Nico Schlotterbeck also scored in Germany's biggest win over their neighbours since a 7-0 victory in 1959.

**Seven in ten immigrants in Germany now in work, but pay and job gaps remain**

Seventy percent of immigrants aged 15 to 64 in Germany were employed in 2025, up 13 percentage points on 2005, according to the Federal Statistics Office (Destatis). That is 11 points below the 81 percent rate for people without an immigrant background.

The figures come from the Microcensus and feature in the 15th Integration Report. Of the 52.7 million people aged 15 to 64, 12.8 million are immigrants.

Employment among descendants of two immigrant parents rose faster, from 47 percent to 70 percent. Destatis says this largely reflects ageing – their average age rose from 12.8 to 22.2 years, so more have reached working age.

Full-time immigrant workers earned an average gross 3,494 euros, 16 percent less than the 4,165 euros of those without an immigrant background. Immigrants also worry more about their jobs – 55 percent have no concerns, against 71 percent.
