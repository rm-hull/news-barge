United ⁠Airlines said on ⁠Monday ⁠that it had resolved ⁠a widespread system ⁠outage, just three days after the Federal ⁠Aviation Administration issued a rare nationwide ground stop on all US flights.

“We have resolved a technology issue that delayed flight departures. While some flights were delayed, there was no impact on flights in progress, or on United Express flights,” Chicago-based United said in a statement. “There is no indication that this was a cybersecurity-related issue.”

According to an internal memo from ​United’s pilots’ ‌union seen ‌by Reuters, a company-wide ‌web-based outage had affected multiple applications used by pilots, including crew communications. It said United was unable to provide final ‌aircraft weights for departing flights and advised pilots to ​follow airport station guidance on whether to leave their gates.

Before every departure, pilots use performance software, often on a cockpit tablet, to check that the aircraft can safely take off under current weather and runway conditions. The preflight calculations include the aircraft’s weight along with information about the fuel, baggage, cargo and passengers the plane is carrying.

Sean Duffy, the US transportation secretary, said in an interview Monday night that he did not know what caused the one-and-a-half-hour outage. “This is not ‌an FAA [Federal Aviation Administration] system-wide problem,” Duffy said on Hannity on Fox News.

The United computer issue is just the latest high-profile technology meltdown to affect Americans in the last few days. On Friday, ageing equipment led to a temporary grounding of all commercial airplanes in the US for more than four hours, sparking travel chaos as thousands of flights were delayed or canceled.

Airlines have experienced similar disruptions before. ‌In August 2025, United temporarily grounded flights after a problem with its Unimatic system, which supplies flight information to systems used to calculate aircraft weight and balance. It was not immediately clear whether Monday’s outage ​involved United’s Unimatic system.

The Trump administration said the rare ground stop stemmed from a fiber upgrade during routine maintenance that led to data flooding, which crashed a critical communication system.

And earlier on Monday, customers reported service outages and difficulty accessing their online accounts at several major US banks and the money transfer service Zelle after a technical issue at JPMorgan Chase caused ripple effects throughout the financial system.

It also comes after a major outage by Microsoft that began on Thursday and lasted into the next day, which crippled a host of services including Outlook, Teams and AI assistant Copilot. The outage had serious repercussions for millions of people and organizations all over the world, including schools, hospitals, businesses, courts, banks, prisons, police forces and governments.

Microsoft said it had resolved the issue and restored service by Saturday, also issuing an apology. The office software giant did not attribute the blackout to a cybersecurity breach, identifying in an initial report on Monday that a combination of a months-old bug, malfunctioning internal connections, and server capacity sparked the outage.
