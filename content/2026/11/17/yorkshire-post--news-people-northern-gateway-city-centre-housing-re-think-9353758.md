# ‘Northern Gateway’: City centre housing re-think

## A new ‘Northern Gateway’ could be created as part of a shift in focus on providing new homes in Wakefield city centre.

Proposals are expected to be drawn up for a major development to include housing and public spaces based around Borough Road car park and surrounding land.

The scheme comes as Wakefield Council looks set to officially scrap plans to demolish The Ridings Shopping Centre in favour of repurposing it to become a leisure-led venue.

The authority previously planned to flatten the centre, along with four residential tower blocks, to enable the delivery of the Cathedral Quarter.

![Borough Road Car Park in Wakefield city centre. LDRS image](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOmI1NjdhYmMwLTA1ZTctNDRjMC04OWJmLWJmNjRiNzFkNWMzZjphNGNjM2U5Mi1kNWZjLTQzMDMtOTIyNy0yNGY0OGEyMTBkYWU=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


The project was part of a masterplan to create 1,000 city centre homes, public spaces and leisure facilities.

The Reform UK-led council looks set to sign off on a “significant departure” from the originally policy, which was put forward when the authority was under Labour control.

Instead, a new vision to provide city centre housing has been included in the Northern Gateway proposals.

A report to cabinet members said the scheme “represents a significant opportunity to bring forward a residential-led mixed-use development” at one of the main entrances to the city centre.

The document said: “The area has the potential to deliver high -quality new homes, introduce active ground floor uses, create attractive public spaces and strengthen the connection between surrounding neighbourhoods, the city centre core and its main transport hubs.”

As part of early development plans, senior councillors are also expected to overturn an agreement to transfer ownership of Lightwaves Leisure and Community Centre to a community trust.

In March this year, the Labour administration agreed to dispose of the former public swimming pool building for a nominal price of £1.

The report, to be considered on Tuesday (November 17), said the property was within the Northern Gateway area and “it would not be appropriate to complete the freehold transfer until further assessments and proposals are developed.”

The document explained: “The priority project area reflects the need to consider the Northern Gateway as a comprehensive regeneration opportunity rather than a collection of individual sites.”

Wakefield House office block, opposite the city’s bus station, has also been earmarked as one of the “strategically important assets” within the site boundary, according to the report.

If the proposals are approved, Muse, the council’s strategic regeneration partner, would develop a masterplan for the area alongside Homes England and West Yorkshire Combined Authority.

More detailed proposals would then be presented to cabinet next year.

The report also said: “A growing residential population is increasingly recognised as an essential component of a successful city centre economy.

“Additional residents support local businesses, increase footfall, improve perceptions of safety, support public transport and contribute towards a stronger day and evening economy.”
