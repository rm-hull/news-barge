A listed hotel can be returned to its original use as a grand, Victorian mansion – planning officers have decided.

An application to convert Oakwood Hall in Bingley back into a single home was submitted to Bradford Council earlier this year.

The application, by Vikesh Tailor, showed that the conversion would create a house that would have around 21 bedrooms.

The application has now been approved, despite the concern of one objector that it would leave the area with a shortage of hotel accommodation.

## Building's history

The property, off Lady Lane, was built in the 1860s as a home for the family of Thomas Garnett, a prominent local cloth merchant who helped Bingley prosper in this period.

It has since been converted into a hotel, and has been Grade II listed since 1973.

## What did the application say?

The planning application said: “The building will be occupied as one dwellinghouse, with its existing accommodation used for domestic purposes.

“The principal reception rooms, dining rooms and other communal areas will provide generous family living accommodation.

“Existing bedrooms and associated facilities will continue to provide residential accommodation appropriate to a substantial private dwelling.

“All existing historic architectural and decorative features will be retained, including the principal staircase, historic panelling, fireplaces, ornamental plasterwork and stained-glass windows.

"The proposed change of use will enable Oakwood Hall to continue functioning as an occupied building, with its maintenance and upkeep supported through private residential ownership.”

## 1 objection

There had been one objection to the plans, with the objector arguing the building was too large to be a single dwelling, and raising concern over the loss of a hotel in the area.

There had been three letters of support, saying the building’s use as a single home would be less disruptive to the area than a hotel

Planning officers said the building’s grand setting had been compromised over the years by nearby developments and extensions to the original building.

They said: “The house originally stood in its own grounds with glasshouses, outbuildings and a backdrop of woodland.

“The setting has been heavily compromised by the development of The Orchards a suburban housing development of the 1980s.

“The houses extend uncomfortably close to the mansion, leaving a disproportionately small curtilage and little outlook.

“The woodland to the east fortunately remains.

“The hotel carpark is featureless and adds nothing to the setting of the property. The extensions on the west facing garden front are disproportionate and incongruous, masking the ground floor of the villa and visually cutting it in half horizontally.”

Approving the scheme, they said: “The use of the site will be significantly reduced and will therefore have a positive impact on neighbouring occupants.”
