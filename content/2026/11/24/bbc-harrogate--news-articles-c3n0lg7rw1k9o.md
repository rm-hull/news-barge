**Richmond Library has opened the doors to its new home.**

The library has relocated from its long-standing premises on Queens Road to the council-owned Mercury House on Station Road, where it now shares space with other North Yorkshire Council services.

Concerns had previously been raised about the site's accessibility, but the council said the new premises had an accessible entrance, disabled parking and bus stops outside the building.

Councillor Simon Myers, executive member for culture and arts, said the authority was "delighted to welcome users" to the library's new home, which had been "months in the planning".

The Queens Road site was partly leased from a private landlord, with the council saying the relocation would reduce running costs by making use of a building it already owned.

The former library building had also suffered maintenance problems in recent years, resulting in some areas being closed to the public.

Staff and volunteers spent recent weeks transferring books and other resources to Mercury House, which also provides customer and registration services.

Myers said the library was now under the same roof as "other important council services", adding staff and volunteers hoped the new premises would encourage more people to visit the library and bring in more volunteers to help run it.

New facilities include an improved children's section, designed to accommodate more school groups, a dedicated IT area and ground-floor space for the local studies collection, according to the Local Democracy Reporting Service.

Since 2017, Richmond Library has been run in partnership with community organisation Catterick, Richmond and Colburn Community Libraries (CRACCL).

The organisation relies on volunteers to help deliver day-to-day services, supported by council staff.
