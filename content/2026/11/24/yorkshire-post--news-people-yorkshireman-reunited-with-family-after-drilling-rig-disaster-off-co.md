# Yorkshireman reunited with family after drilling rig disaster off coast of Africa

## A Yorkshireman who survived a drilling rig sinking off the coast of west Africa has been reunited with his family.

Richard Richardson, from Hilston, East Yorkshire, arrived back at Heathrow a month after the Prospector 5 went down off the coast of Mayumba, Gabon, with 115 people on board, during jacking operations.

Mr Richardson escaped with only the clothes he was wearing and a few euro as the disaster unfolded.

Four bodies have since been recovered, with a fifth still unaccounted for.

Initially the rig’s owner Borr Drilling thought it was a “punch-through” event, when one of its legs unexpectedly penetrated the seabed, leading to a “catastrophic loss of stability”.

However according to reports last week, Borr said review of ​additional information, including Remotely Operated Vehicle survey data ​and video footage, had not confirmed this as a cause and the cause of the accident has yet to be determined

Mr Richardson and other colleagues had been staying at a secure hotel complex in Port Gentil.

An independent investigation has been launched following the incident on October 24.

MP for Beverley and Holderness Graham Stuart said earlier: "I'm delighted that Richard is on a flight home after so many weeks of stress and worry both for him and his family.

"The whole family has worked tirelessly to promote his cause and I'm grateful to them, to the Foreign Office and to ministers for their help in getting him home."

In a statement on November 2, Bruno Morand, CEO of owner Borr Drilling, said: “This continues to be an incredibly difficult time for everyone involved, and above all, for the families and loved ones of the four individuals who have lost their lives and for the individual who remains missing.

"Our priority remains staying close to them and our employees, providing all the care and support we can.

“It is also critically important to establish a thorough and objective understanding of what occurred and all possible causes of a catastrophic loss of stability are in scope and will be considered.

"The investigation will fully consider all evidence and assess every potential cause before reaching a final determination.”
