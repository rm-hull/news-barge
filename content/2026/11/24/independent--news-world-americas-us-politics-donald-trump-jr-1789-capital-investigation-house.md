House Democrats investigating the business activities of Donald Trump Jr. have requested records from four firms linked to venture capital firm 1789 Capital, where the president's son serves as a partner.

The targeted entities include prediction market platform Polymarket, vaping brand Juul, online firearm seller GrabAGun, and manufacturing firm Hadrian Automation.

The requests were detailed in letters released on Tuesday by Rep. Jamie Raskin, a Maryland Democrat slated to take over leadership of the House Judiciary Committee next year.

Raskin requested comprehensive records of all communications exchanged between the specified companies, 1789 Capital, and its executive team.

"The probe examines whether the President’s son and his business partners helped steer federal contracts or regulatory decisions to companies in which they had a significant financial interest or used advance knowledge of those forthcoming decisions to make investments and profit," Raskin's office wrote in a press release.

Raskin initially launched the inquiry in August.

With Democrats set to assume control of Congress in January, lawmakers are preparing a broad series of probes targeting Republican President Donald Trump and his administration.

These inquiries will encompass government contracts, potential financial conflicts of interest, immigration policies, and the investigation concerning the late financier and convicted sex offender Jeffrey Epstein.

Speaking at an Axios event last week, 1789 Capital President Omeed Malik and Trump Jr. refuted any claims of wrongdoing.

"We responded to Congressman Raskin back in September pointing out in detail the many flaws in his understanding of how 1789 has operated and a number of basic factual errors,"

Bill Burck, an attorney representing 1789 Capital, said in a statement. "These are personal attacks untethered to actual evidence."

Juul stated that it plans "to cooperate with any Congressional effort to understand our company."

Meanwhile, a spokesperson for online gun retailer GrabAGun rejected the claims, asserting, "Congressman Raskin's letter is a political document, and its allegations are baseless."

Representatives for Trump Jr., Polymarket, and Hadrian Automation did not immediately respond to requests for comment.

In a move legal experts expect will face challenges, President Donald Trump issued an order on Friday attempting to restrict corporate cooperation with congressional investigators.

The directive instructs firms holding federal contracts to inform the White House prior to sharing material with lawmakers and to withhold confidential data.

Under the U.S. Constitution, Congress maintains broad oversight authority and subpoena powers to compel information, mechanisms the administration has sought to limit in scope.

Raskin’s expanded probe concentrates on whether the four businesses "benefited, or stand to benefit," from Trump administration contracts, regulatory decisions, or proposed rule adjustments, which Raskin said could indicate misconduct or conflicts of interest.

In his letter to Polymarket, Raskin pointed out that the firm was effectively barred from operating in the US and faced federal scrutiny before Trump returned to office.

In July 2025, regulators concluded that probe and approved Polymarket's acquisition of a US platform, paving the way for its market re-entry.

The following month, 1789 Capital disclosed a strategic investment that news outlets reported was worth tens of millions of dollars. The Commodity Futures Trading Commission has since reopened its inquiry into Polymarket.

Raskin also noted that in July 2025, months after 1789 Capital invested $5 million into Juul, authorities loosened sales restrictions on certain products, with further authorizations granted earlier this year.

GrabAGun went public in 2025 following a merger with a special-purpose acquisition company backed by Malik. In May 2026, the Trump administration proposed rules to simplify direct-to-consumer gun shipments.

The GrabAGun spokesperson noted these proposals were well known across the industry and that the business only provided input through public comment channels.

Raskin also contacted Hadrian Automation, noting that the manufacturing firm received nearly $1 billion in US government contracts shortly after 1789 Capital secured a stake in the business.

Raskin cited public remarks made by the president himself regarding his family’s commercial affairs, recalling his statement to CNBC in July: "I feel badly in a way for my kids ... Almost anything they do ... they have inside information."
