Discount supermarket Aldi is cutting its range of branded goods in a bid to win more customers looking for a bargain.

While Aldi remains a more popular overall shop in the UK than Lidl, it is the latter which is growing much quicker – something Aldi management wants to change, both here and abroad.

The supermarket says it regularly reviews and replaces products as part of a normal check on keeping prices down, but a *Financial Times*report suggests the variance of products on offer could be notably cut. Aldi did not confirm how many products or brands would be removed, when contacted by* The Independent*.

Earlier this month, consumer group Which? named Aldi the UK’s cheapest supermarket for a full year in a row, according to their monthly price ranking index of a standard basket of groceries.

Measured once a month to track price movements across branded and non-branded goods, the price gap between Aldi (cheapest) and Waitrose (most expensive) was most recently £61.53.

But it was Morrisons who picked up the Retailer of the Year award at the 2026 Quality Food Awards in mid-November.

In future, Aldi aim to open 1,500 stores across the UK, up from the present level of just over 1,100. Next year, 40 new stores are anticipated to be opened, with additional refurbishment to some existing ones.

An Aldi spokesperson added: “Aldi is the only major supermarket where average prices are lower than a year ago, according to Worldpanel. Our volume sales are also growing ahead of the market, and we’ve welcomed an additional 140,000 shoppers through our doors in the past 12 weeks as customers recognise that the simple everyday low prices we offer can help them get more value from their weekly shop.

“As a discounter, we’ve always kept things simple so that we can deliver what matters most to our customers – award-winning quality at everyday low prices. As we continue to open more stores, even more shoppers will be able to enjoy those same low prices and high-quality groceries.”

Lidl, meanwhile, is set to add signs across its UK stores this week to highlight “perfect to donate” items, encouraging shoppers around its middle aisle to purchase products like books or creative sets which can be set aside in-store for those in need ahead of Christmas.
