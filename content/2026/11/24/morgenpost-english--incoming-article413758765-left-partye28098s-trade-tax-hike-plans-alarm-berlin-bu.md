The potential red-green-red coalition of the Left Party, Greens and SPD has yet to be finalised in Berlin, but it is already coming under considerable pressure. Following the ongoing debate over bringing residential property companies into public ownership, Berlin’s business community is now sounding the alarm.

In a joint appeal, more than 150 companies of all sizes and from all sectors in the capital warn that higher business taxes would make Berlin less attractive to businesses. They are calling on the prospective coalition partners to abandon plans to raise the municipal business tax multiplier. “Any increase would damage Berlin’s competitiveness both nationally and internationally, putting growth and jobs at risk,” the letter states.

### Berlin Businesses Would Face an Additional €460 Million in Taxes

The Left Party, which emerged as the largest party in the House of Representatives election at the end of September, has floated the idea of raising the municipal business tax multiplier from its current level of 410% to 470%. According to the letter, this would impose an additional annual burden of around €460 million on Berlin businesses.

“Berlin would risk losing considerable appeal as a business location, both nationally and internationally. There are also fears that companies and jobs could relocate to neighbouring areas: Of Brandenburg’s 413 municipalities, 411 are seeking to attract businesses with tax multipliers that are, in some cases, significantly lower,” the signatories warn.

### Higher Tax Multipliers Do Not Guarantee Higher Revenue

Furthermore, “higher tax multipliers are no guarantee of permanently higher revenue”. What matters is economic development and companies’ taxable profits. According to research by the Ifo Institute for Economic Research, a one-percentage-point increase in business tax rate would reduce corporate investment by an average of three per cent. During a recession, the reduction would be as much as six per cent.

“Following the increase in the tax multiplier from 300 to 340 per cent in 1996, business tax revenue fell by €18 million, and after the increase from 390 to 410 per cent in 1999, it fell by €15 million,” the letter continues. Thanks to a growing economy, “Berlin’s business tax revenue has more than doubled in inflation-adjusted terms over the past 25 years – while the tax multiplier has remained stable. The coalition should therefore focus consistently on attracting new businesses and creating favourable conditions for growth.”

### Left Party Plans Would Undermine Federal Government Efforts to Reduce the Tax Burden

At the same time, the companies criticise the fact that the federal government’s efforts to provide tax relief would also be undermined. This was demonstrated by a model calculation based on Berlin’s business tax statistics and industry-standard benchmarks. According to the calculation, construction companies would face a net additional burden of €2,430 next year despite electricity tax relief. For larger retailers, the additional cost would amount to €2,250, and for a medium-sized industrial company, as much as €12,250.

“Even the first stage of the federal corporate income tax reduction from 2028 would, in many cases, not be sufficient to reduce overall costs. Ultimately, Berlin businesses would therefore face a greater burden than before,” the signatories of the appeal conclude.
