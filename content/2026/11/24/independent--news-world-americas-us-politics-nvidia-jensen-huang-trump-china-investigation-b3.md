Nvidia and its chief executive, Jensen Huang, are facing a series of state and federal investigations and reviews, even as President Donald Trump welcomed Huang's reinstatement and said the tech billionaire's return posed no national security risk.

The company's board has formed a committee to investigate whether foreign intelligence efforts targeted Huang. It is also starting a search to name a new chief compliance officer. Nvidia, a cornerstone of the tech economy and AI boom, has declined to comment on the investigations.

## Intelligence agencies probe extramarital affair with Chinese businesswoman

Trump said on November 9 that the FBI was "looking into" the issue, after reports that the Nvidia CEO had an extramarital affair with an unrelated Chinese businesswoman, Alice Huang.

U.S. intelligence agencies had probed whether she was a Chinese asset. Jensen Huang has acknowledged being unfaithful to his wife and apologized. He has not publicly addressed the situation further. China has denied the espionage allegations.

![President Donald Trump presents the National Medal of Science to Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang during the 'A New Golden Age' summit at the US Institute of Peace](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/24/04/2026-11-24T014215Z_1277962467_RC27ZNA9W5NV_RTRMADP_3_NVIDIA-CEO-RETURN.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

People familiar with the matter have told Reuters that U.S. intelligence agencies examined the extent of the Chinese businesswoman's ties to China's Ministry of State Security and what information she may have passed to Beijing.

Asked after the Nvidia CEO's reinstatement what the CIA had told Trump, the president told CNBC: "Let me just say, I have clearance. And if I didn't have clearance, I wouldn't have done the deal (with Nvidia's board for Jensen Huang's return)."

Trump said Attorney General Todd Blanche confirmed the federal government has no evidence that Huang engaged in unlawful conduct. *The New York Times* reported that Alice Huang kept in contact with an official from China's top spy agency for more than a year during the affair.

China's foreign ministry has previously said the spy accusations were "groundless." Reuters has been unable to determine Alice Huang's whereabouts to seek comment.

## Justice Department scrutinizes Hugging Face deal and chip sales practices

The Justice Department has also been reviewing Nvidia's $12.9 billion deal to buy open-source platform Hugging Face alongside a broader antitrust investigation into the company's acquisitions and practices, Reuters reported in October.

![The Justice Department has also been reviewing Nvidia's $12.9 billion deal to buy open-source platform Hugging Face](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/09/15/11/2026-09-14T080822Z_1561001948_RC2WINA2VF5M_RTRMADP_3_NVIDIA-HUGGING-FACE.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The department's recent questioning covers Nvidia's acquisitions, investments, and sales practices, and whether the chip designer has pressured its customers, people familiar with the matter said in October.

The DOJ has also scrutinized Nvidia's conduct following other transactions, including its 2025 deal to license technology from chip startup Groq and hire its engineers, Reuters has reported.

Officials have explored whether Nvidia unfairly offered larger allocations of AI chips if customers purchased its networking equipment and Groq processors. That has raised questions about whether Nvidia used its dominant product to push into a newer market.

## Watchdogs investigate accounting and circular AI financing deals

The U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission is investigating Nvidia's accounting practices, *Reuters*reported on November 20, citing two people briefed on the matter.* Reuters* could not immediately determine when the SEC opened its inquiry or exactly what potential accounting issues the watchdog examined.

Analysts and investors have grown increasingly concerned about circular AI financing deals. Under such deals, chipmakers including Nvidia have pursued complex financial arrangements to help customers fund chip purchases and the construction of costly AI infrastructure, such as data centers.

![Silhouettes of US President Donald Trump and Jensen Huang, the NVIDIA logo and a rising stock graph in this illustration](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/24/08/2026-11-23T170750Z_603820255_RC2TTOAGX1RZ_RTRMADP_3_NVIDIA-CEO-TRUMP.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Nvidia has backed AI companies and cloud providers and provided guarantees connected to data-center projects that use its equipment. Critics say such arrangements can make it harder for investors to assess the quality of chipmakers' revenue and their true financial exposure.

On its November 17 earnings call, Nvidia's then-interim CEO Colette Kress said the company was proud of its role in supporting the AI industry, but would be far more selective about future AI industry financing deals.

## California subpoena and congressional hearings loom for Huang

California Attorney General Rob Bonta said on November 19 that his office had served an investigative subpoena to Nvidia related to reports of Huang's affair with the Chinese businesswoman.

The attorney general's office said the investigation will help determine whether Nvidia's actions violated California law. Democrats, who will hold majorities in the House of Representatives and the Senate starting in January after the November midterm elections, have been very critical of Huang, Nvidia, and Huang's ties to the Republican president.

U.S. lawmakers from both parties sharply criticized Nvidia's decision to reinstate Huang as CEO.

![California Attorney General Rob Bonta said on November 19 that his office had served an investigative subpoena to Nvidia](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/11/15/2026-11-05T182335Z_342413098_RC2QCOASYQIR_RTRMADP_3_CALIFORNIA-SOCIAL-MEDIA-LAWSUITS.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

"Democrats will wield their full power to investigate any wrongdoing by the Trump Administration, Jensen Huang, other Nvidia executives, and anyone selling out our national security for financial gain," top Senate Democrat Chuck Schumer said after Huang was reinstated.

Top House Democrat Hakeem Jeffries said Congress will "not be deterred from conducting a swift, thorough and wide-ranging investigation into business malfeasance by Nvidia." Huang has voiced support for Trump's AI agenda and met the Republican leader many times.

Nvidia has sought to sell advanced chips to the Chinese market and lobbied for looser restrictions on Chinese access to its sophisticated AI chips. Democratic and Republican lawmakers have called to halt exports of advanced AI chips to China.

![Hakeem Jeffries said Congress will "not be deterred from conducting a swift, thorough and wide-ranging investigation into business malfeasance by Nvidia."](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/22/19/download.-3..jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Huang and the White House have argued that continuing U.S. sales to China would keep Chinese customers reliant on U.S. technology. Huang has agreed to testify before the U.S. Senate in a public hearing on December 3, the Senate Banking Committee said.

The chairs of the House committees on China, intelligence and foreign affairs separately said Huang and Nvidia General Counsel Tim Teter have confirmed they will appear for transcribed interviews on December 1 and December 2 as previously planned.

## Lawmakers question Nvidia influence over Megaspeed export probe

Democratic lawmakers have opened an investigation into how much influence Nvidia wielded over a U.S. Commerce Department probe into Singapore-based cloud-computing company Megaspeed for possible illegal diversion of chips to China, according to a November 19 letter Reuters reviewed.

The Chinese businesswoman with whom the Nvidia boss had an affair is a former executive at Megaspeed, an Nvidia partner that operates data centers. Megaspeed said it complies with all applicable laws, including U.S. export control requirements.

Nvidia said that as an American company, it consults with department officials to answer questions, provide information, and request guidance. The Commerce and Justice departments released information on November 16 about Washington's investigation into Megaspeed, Nvidia's Asia-Pacific partner.

The departments said the investigation began two years ago. They said Commerce investigators requested records from Nvidia in May 2025 about sales to the company. With Singaporean authorities, the investigators conducted an on-site inspection at Megaspeed in June 2025 and interviewed Alice Huang.

Washington added Megaspeed to its trade blacklist on November 23.
