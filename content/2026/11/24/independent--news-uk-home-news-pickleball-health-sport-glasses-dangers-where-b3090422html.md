Doctors have warned pickleball players about a rise in eye injuries linked to the sport as it grows in popularity across the world.

It comes after a stray ball struck a woman in the face, triggering a severe form of glaucoma that required surgery to save her sight.

The player, in her early 50s, was not wearing protective eyewear when she was hit directly in her left eye by a shot from her own teammate during a friendly match.

Eye specialists at UMass Chan Medical School in Massachusetts wrote in the *BMJ Case Reports* journal that the blow caused internal bleeding, dangerously high eye pressure and lasting damage to her optic nerve, requiring surgery four months later.

![Doctors have warned pickleball players about a rise in eye injuries linked to the sport as it grows in popularity across the world.](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/24/09/1745054241..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Although the operation successfully stabilised her eye pressure, doctors noted that her left pupil remains permanently dilated and she faces a heightened long-term risk of developing cataracts.

The medical team warn that the case could reflect a wider, emerging threat as the uptake of pickleball continues to grow.

The sport combines elements of tennis, badminton and table tennis, and has surged in popularity in recent years, particularly among middle-aged and older adults.

Researchers wrote: “Pickleball-related ocular injuries include reports of retinal tears requiring surgical repair, traumatic lens [dislocation], and a comprehensive series describing various patterns of ocular injury directly attributable to pickleball participation.”

According to Sport England, regular players in the country have nearly quadrupled from 20,000 in 2022/23 to an estimated 80,000 today.

The UMass Chan Medical School team say pickleball’s low-impact image could hide serious hazards on the court. Modern carbon-fibre paddles can fire the hard plastic balls at speeds exceeding 60mph, while players stand just feet apart across a compact court, leaving little time to react.

![According to Sport England, regular players in the country have nearly quadrupled from 20,000 in 2022/23 to an estimated 80,000 today.](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/19/12/1482098981..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

They write: “The unique characteristics of pickleball, including the proximity of players to the net, the high velocity of a struck ball, the speed of play, and the demographic profile of participants, may contribute to distinct injury patterns that warrant specific preventive measures.”

Unlike squash or racquetball, where protective goggles are widely accepted and often compulsory, eye protection remains optional in organised pickleball.

The specialists urged players to wear protective sports glasses and called for formal safety guidelines to be introduced to prevent more injuries.

The team added: “With the growing body of players and advancement of technology in the sport, such as modern paddles that can generate shots exceeding 60 miles per hour, these injuries will continue to occur unless proper safety measures are implemented.”
