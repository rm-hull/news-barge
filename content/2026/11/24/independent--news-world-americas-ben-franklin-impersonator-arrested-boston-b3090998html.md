A Founding Father found himself in a boatload of trouble.

A Benjamin Franklin impersonator was arrested Monday afternoon in Boston’s North End, just down the road from Paul Revere’s house and the Freedom Trail, as police ran what they called a “coordinated warrant sweep.”

Timothy Kingman, 66, was donning knee-length stockings, a brown frock coat, white hair and a tricorn hat at the time of his arrest, Boston.com reported.

He was arrested on two outstanding warrants for assault and battery and shoplifting, the Boston Police Department said.

“Officers on Hanover Street noticed a man dressed head to toe as Benjamin Franklin. On closer inspection, officers recognized him not as the Founding Father but as [Kingman],” the Boston Police Department said in a statement.

![Ben Franklin impersonator Timothy Kingman was arraigned in Boston this week on two outstanding warrants](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/24/18/07/benjamin-franklin-boston1-997ddf02.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Video of the arrest shared online shows two officers talking to Kingman and checking his pockets while he is in handcuffs.

Court records obtained by the outlet showed that Kingman was charged with shoplifting in March and assault and battery in May. He failed to appear in court in June, prompting the warrants for both cases.

Kingman spent the night in the Nashua Street Jail and was arraigned Tuesday morning in Boston Municipal Court, while still wearing his Ben Franklin garb.

His attorney, Nicholas Santiago, said the assault and battery charges stemmed from Kingman’s “alcohol issues,” but that he has since become sober.

“Since this event, Mr. Kingman has been sober, so he’s been sober for the last eight months,” Santiago told the court Tuesday as he entered a not guilty plea on Kingman’s behalf, according to CBS Boston.

The attorney argued for Kingman to be released on personal recognizance, noting he is self-employed and gives tours in Boston as Ben Franklin.

![Kingman pleaded not guilty at his arraignment on Tuesday](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/24/18/14/benjamin-franklin-boston-481f719f.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

"That has been his source of income for the last six years, and he's passionate about that," Santiago told the judge. "Pretty much every day for the last six years, he has been giving tours, doing research of Benjamin Franklin and he's had some success with that."

The judge released Kingman on the promise that he would return to court in January.

"It takes guts. He's committed. He wears the outfit," Santiago said of Kingman. "He deeply cares about Boston."
