Tom Gordon MP backs the PIP fast-track and the end of repeat reviews for lifelong conditions, but says young disabled people face months of uncertainty.

People with serious, well-evidenced conditions will get a fast-track route onto Personal Independence Payment (PIP), and people whose conditions are lifelong will no longer face repeated award reviews, after the Government accepted two recommendations of the Timms Review on Tuesday 24 November. Tom Gordon, the Liberal Democrat MP for Harrogate and Knaresborough, welcomed both, but warned that disabled people and carers across North Yorkshire now face months of uncertainty over the rest.

Disability Minister Sir Stephen Timms set out the final report of his review of PIP in the House of Commons. The review, co-produced with disabled people and their organisations, concluded that the current system is “no longer fit for purpose”.

## Overview

* **Agreed now:** a fast-track route, without an unnecessary full assessment, for people with serious and well-evidenced conditions.
* **Also agreed now:** an end to repeated award reviews for people whose conditions are lifelong, progressive or unlikely to improve.
* **Still to come:** the rest of the review will be considered alongside Alan Milburn’s review of young people and work, with a White Paper due early next year and legislation to follow.
* **Where it applies:** England and Wales. Disability benefits are devolved in Scotland and Northern Ireland.

## “The right thing to do”

“Ending repeated, stressful reassessments for people whose conditions will never improve, and fast-tracking those with the most serious illnesses, is the right thing to do,” Mr Gordon said. “Credit for that belongs to the disabled people who helped write this review.”

His office says the review heard from more than 40,000 people and organisations.

## Two reviews, two directions

The Liberal Democrats say the Timms and Milburn reviews pull in different directions for young disabled people. They say Mr Milburn’s proposals would take £3.6 billion from disability support for 18 to 24-year-olds, and point to the Joseph Rowntree Foundation’s warning that young people are already at disproportionately high risk of destitution.

“Disabled young people now face months of uncertainty, with Stephen Timms recommending one thing and Alan Milburn another,” Mr Gordon said. “The Government must not waste time in setting out its approach.

“Families in Harrogate and Knaresborough who rely on PIP, and the carers for whom PIP is the gateway to Carer’s Allowance, deserve to know where they stand. Last year the Government’s own figures showed its original PIP plans would have pushed 250,000 people, including 50,000 children, into poverty. We cannot let that happen again by the back door.”

PIP also opens the door to Motability and Blue Badges. About four million people claim it, at a cost of about £32 billion this financial year, projected to rise to £41.5 billion in 2030-31, according to figures given by Mr Gordon’s office. It also cites the Joseph Rowntree Foundation’s finding that 3.8 million people, including 1 million children, experienced destitution last year, and that 61% of them reported a disability or long-term health condition.

## What the Liberal Democrats want

The party is calling on the Government to:

* Publish a full poverty and destitution impact assessment of the Timms recommendations before acting on any of them, including the effect on Motability, Blue Badges and Carer’s Allowance.
* Make sure disabled people co-produce the new assessment process, and that Parliament scrutinises the White Paper in full before any legislation is brought forward.
* End the Carer’s Allowance cliff-edge by introducing an earnings taper, so carers are not punished for taking on more paid work.
* Build on Charlie Mayfield’s review into how government and business can support disabled and ill people into work, rather than relying on benefit cuts.

“We need to manage the welfare bill after it soared thanks to the Conservative Party’s economic chaos,” Mr Gordon said. “But the way to do that is to tackle the root causes: grow the economy to create jobs, bring down the cost of living, and fix the NHS and social care so health problems are treated early.

“That means guaranteeing everyone can see a GP within seven days, setting up mental health hubs for young people in every community, and giving disabled people who can work the support they need to do so.”

Sir Julian Smith, the Conservative MP for Skipton and Ripon, set out his party’s position on disability benefits earlier this month when he backed a plan to cut £36bn from welfare.
