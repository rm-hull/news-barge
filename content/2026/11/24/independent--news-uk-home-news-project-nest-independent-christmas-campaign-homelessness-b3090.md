Entrepreneur and philanthropist Cyrus Vandrevala has made a substantial donation to *The Independent’s* Christmas appeal, which will help give homeless young people a safe place to live.

The support coincides with a meeting between Prince William and Andy Burnham at No 10 Downing Street where the pair were due to talk about issues close to their hearts, including tackling homelessness.

Vandrevala, who has a foundation for mental health issues, has been involved also with building projects and he sees this as a vital area of concern for young people.

He said: “It is heartbreaking to think that there are young people facing Christmas without a safe place to sleep, without the comfort of knowing where they will be tomorrow, and without the security that so many of us take for granted.

"We have always believed that everyone deserves somewhere safe to call home and the opportunity to live with dignity, security and hope. Through our family’s longstanding commitment to developing homes and communities in India, we have seen first-hand how transformative having a place to call home can be.”

![Cyrus Vandrevala](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/24/16/59/Cyrus-Vandrevala.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

*The Independent*has joined forces with charity* EveryYouth* to launch Project Nest – a groundbreaking appeal which will give young homeless youngsters the best opportunity to succeed by giving them a secure place to call home.

The project will simultaneously put a roof over young people’s heads while breathing life into dilapidated buildings that are blighting Britain’s city centres.

Mr Vandrevala is the latest backer of this publication’s campaign after the prime minister urged readers to back the cause, saying it would “put hope in the hearts” of some of Britain’s most disadvantaged young people

Mr Vandrevala’s work thorough Cyrus and Priya Vandrevala Foundation has given him "understanding of the importance of mental health, emotional wellbeing and ensuring that people experiencing life’s most difficult challenges have somewhere to turn for support."

"This is why the work of EveryYouth particularly resonates with us. Bringing together a network of charities dedicated to helping young people experiencing homelessness, EveryYouth recognises that these young people need so much more than a roof over their heads. They need emotional support, help with their mental health, opportunities to build independent lives and, above all, the belief that they have a future.

“We are particularly moved by the commitment to tackling homelessness among young people, an issue that has also been championed by Prince William through his work with Centrepoint and his Homewards initiative.

“It is heartening to see such attention being brought to a challenge that can so profoundly affect the lives and futures of vulnerable young people.

"At Christmas, when home, family and belonging mean so much, we feel especially strongly that no young person should be left feeling forgotten, alone or without hope.

"We are proud to support this appeal and hope our donation will help give more young people the opportunity to find safety, rebuild their confidence and look forward to a brighter future."No young person should feel that they have nowhere to turn. Sometimes, giving someone a home is the beginning of giving them their life back.”

*EveryYouth* currently runs more than 60 bed spaces across 21 homelessness hubs around the UK, most of which are in houses rented from local councils at a peppercorn rate. If successful, Project Nest will allow the charity to purchase its first property.

With the help of generous readers and supporters, *The Independent* is aiming to raise £200,000 to fund the renovation of three properties in Sheffield, Fife, and Basingstoke. Every single penny raised during the appeal will go to fund these homes to change young people’s lives.

In Sheffield, the money will be used to buy and renovate a derelict three-bed house while in Fife and Basingstoke it will fund the renovation of empty, dilapidated one-bed properties.

The accommodation will specifically serve young people aged between 16 and 25 who are in work. With no savings or family to fall back on, moving into work can bring its own challenges: young people in supported housing can face changes to their support, while those earning a salary can still struggle to afford private rents.

Project Nest breaks that cycle, with ultra-affordable, no-deposit homes where young people can live for up to two years, keep a job, save and get support to move into independent living.

Crucially, young people in these homes can also access support offered by youth homelessness charities to address the childhood trauma that leads to homelessness. The young people *EveryYouth*supports have been failed long before they lose their home. Virtually all grew up in absolute poverty, and 90 per cent experienced traumatic childhoods. Approximately half were physically abused by a parent or guardian, and 70 per cent say they never felt loved.* The Independent*’s editor-in-chief Geordie Greig said: “This is campaign by* The Independent* is a page turner for hope and decency as Every young person deserves to feel seen, heard, supported and given the opportunity to have a roof over their head. Backing Every Youth charity with our Project Nest campaign.

“It means backing every young person’s basic needs. The support and donations we have had so far has been very moving and motivating.”
