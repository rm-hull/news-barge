Triple killer Anthony Russell has been given a second whole-life order for murdering Soham killer Ian Huntley at high-security jail HMP Frankland.

Russell went on trial at Newcastle Crown Court on Monday, accused of killing the 52-year-old by swinging a 4kg metal bar at least six times at his head, in what the prosecution said was a “cold-blooded murder” that he planned for weeks.

On Tuesday, Russell, 44, asked to change his plea and admitted the single charge of murder.

Already convicted of three murders, Russell launched the attack when the two men were working in the recycling workshop at HMP Frankland, near Durham, on 26 February.

Huntley died in hospital nine days later, having sustained a fractured skull and brain bleed and never having regained consciousness.

Huntley murdered schoolgirls Holly Wells and Jessica Chapman in the Cambridgeshire town of Soham in August 2002.

![Anthony Russell went on trial at Newcastle Crown Court on Monday, accused of killing the 52-year-old by swinging a 4kg metal bar at least six times at his head, in what the prosecution said was a ‘cold-blooded murder’ that he planned for weeks](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/23/16/01M83Q3MJ6XN95NJ35N79SXHEG.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

He was jailed for life and ordered to serve a minimum of 40 years.

The judge, Mr Justice Hilliard, handed Russell a second whole-life order on Tuesday afternoon. Sentencing Anthony Russell, Mr Justice Hilliard said there had been some tension between him and Huntley from about a year before the murder.

He said: “Tension between two such dangerous men is hardly surprising and I’m sure you were able to give at least as good as you got.”

Prosecutors told the jury earlier this week that Russell had told a governor he was “sick” of Huntley, adding: “He put it on me about six months ago and I walked away, but when he sees weakness in people he tries to bully them and he’s been getting in my face the last few weeks and my head just thought ‘f\*\*\* it, I ain’t putting up with it anymore’.

“I’m not getting out, I’m a natural lifer, so I thought ‘f\*\*\* it’.”

The phrase “natural lifer” referred to Russell being sentenced to a whole-life term, meaning he will never be released, jurors were told.

Russell later told police: “He’s been getting really arrogant, cocky, in my face, winding me up, just little smiles as if to say ‘yeah’ sort of thing, and I’d just had enough of it.”

![Ian Huntley murdered schoolgirls Holly Wells and Jessica Chapman in the Cambridgeshire town of Soham in August 2002.](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/23/13/b53b802c8cc985ac5c734e9adbbea450Y29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzk1NTI3MjI3-2.1612287.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Asked when he started to plan the attack, Russell said it had been over weeks, “because of the way he just talks down to you… his arrogance, it was just eating at me”.

Matthew Bean KC, prosecuting, told jurors: “The prosecution case is that this was a cold-blooded murder that had been planned weeks in advance of its execution.”

He said: “The prosecution say that this plan would seem to have been driven by nothing more than this defendant’s personal sense of grievance towards Ian Huntley.”

The two murderers were held on A Wing at the high-security prison and there had been “tension” between them in the months leading up to the killing, the court heard.

In March 2025, prison staff witnessed an argument between them in which they threatened one another and had to be separated.

Christopher Atkinson, from the Crown Prosecution Service (CPS), said: “This was a deliberate and planned attack in which Russell selected a weapon and concealed it, before waiting for the opportunity to strike.

“Russell claimed that he had ‘nothing to lose’ by carrying out the attack because he was already serving a whole-life term for murder and rape.

“We have worked closely with Durham Constabulary to build a robust case against Russell, which has secured his guilty plea today.”

![Russell was sentenced to a whole-life tariff in 2021 for the murders of Julie Williams, 58, and her son David Williams, 32, at separate flats in Coventry, and pregnant 31-year-old Nicole McGregor, who was found in woodland near Leamington Spa three days later](https://static.independent.co.uk/2020/10/30/12/POLICE%20Coventry%20%2016122204.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Huntley murdered Holly and Jessica after they left a family barbecue to buy sweets in Soham on 4 August 2002.

He dumped their bodies in a ditch 10 miles away.

They were not found for 13 days, sparking a search involving hundreds of police officers.

At the time, Huntley lived with Maxine Carr who was a teaching assistant at Holly and Jessica’s primary school.

He denied murdering the two 10-year-olds but was convicted after a trial at the Old Bailey in 2003.

Carr gave Huntley a false alibi and was jailed for 21 months for perverting the course of justice.

She is now living under a new identity.

Russell was sentenced to a whole-life tariff in 2021 for the murders of Julie Williams, 58, and her son David Williams, 32, at separate flats in Coventry, and pregnant 31-year-old Nicole McGregor, who was found in woodland near Leamington Spa three days later.

Russell also raped Ms McGregor.
