The CIA was hacked in the latest cyberattack targeting the United States’ intelligence agencies, according to multiple reports.

An unclassified CIA system was breached and the attack did not involve any highly sensitive information, a source told *The Associated Press.*The issue has since been resolved.* The New York Times* reported earlier Tuesday that the CIA and Office of the Director of National Intelligence recently discovered a cybersecurity breach that exposed some unclassified information. The intrusions affected a network that looks for intelligence insights in public, unclassified material.

While it was not immediately clear what unclassified material was accessed, information at other government agencies was also believed to have been exposed in the breach, per *the* * Times*.

It is also not yet clear who was behind the attacks targeting the CIA and ODNI.

Asked about the report, the ODNI said there had been "no disruptions to critical capabilities or classified systems." The CIA did not immediately respond to requests for comment.

The new breaches are the latest in a series of tech outages and cybersecurity incidents plaguing US agencies, including a breach at the US Department of Agriculture and a temporary outage affecting an internal classified communications network used by the National Security Agency.

## FBI hacked again

The FBI has been targeted in two separate breaches in recent months, while the Pentagon and the Department of Homeland Security have also been caught up in recent cyber incidents.

Trump acknowledged the second breach at the FBI on Saturday, telling *Fox News: “*We just heard about it, and I will say it’s not good. I don’t like that. But maybe we’ll learn things that we would have never learned otherwise.”

The breach targeted a nationwide database that allows federal, state and local law enforcement agencies to share criminal justice information.

The hack came two months after hacking group ShinyHunters claimed to have stolen personal information about FBI employees and job applicants from the bureau’s recruitment website.

The breaches at high-profile US agencies follow a string of technology disruptions that affected Microsoft, JPMorgan Chase, the FAA and airlines.

The failures have highlighted weaknesses within key infrastructure networks, financial institutions and everyday digital services relied upon by millions.

Officials in President Donald Trump's administration have insisted that the widespread disruptions do not appear to be from a coordinated cyber strike.

Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton tried to reassure Americans Sunday in TV interviews, saying that the breakdowns mostly result from urgent system updates meant to counter cybercriminals who are increasingly leveraging advanced artificial intelligence applications to launch attacks of unparalleled size and frequency.
