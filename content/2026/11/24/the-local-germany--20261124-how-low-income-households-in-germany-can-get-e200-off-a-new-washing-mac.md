Germany's Ministry for the Environment aims to expand its efforts help low-income households save electricity with free advice and subsidies worth €200 for buying energy efficient washing machines next year.

Until now, funding under similar initiatives has been focused on refrigerators.

The ministry announced that funding for the nationwide electricity saving check is secured for the coming years, with a total of €165 million from 2027 to 2029.

The goal is to support more than 100,000 low-income households with free advice on saving energy over the next three years.

Speaking in Potsdam this week, Environment Minister Carsten Schneider (SPD), said: "I know what it's like to have little money."

He added that low-income households tend to buy the cheapest appliances, and then end up paying more to run devices that consume lots of electricity.

More efficient devices are cheaper in the long run, but for low-income households the upfront cost being €200 to €400 more can be a deal breaker.

This is why the government created the Energy Saving Check (*Stromspar-Check*) programme.

Schneider said the programme is "good for the climate, good for the wallet, and also for electricity consumption and prices".

As the initiative expands in the coming year, 110 new locations are being added where people can seek out consultations.

The energy minister visited a newly opened location for the Energy Saving Check project in the Drewitz district of Potsdam.

Eligible households can receive a voucher worth €200 for the purchase of an energy-efficient appliance, such as refrigerators and washing machines.

There are also plans to equip eligible households with programmable thermostat valves, and to give repair vouchers – to promote repairing appliances instead of buying new ones.

Energy saving consultants may also provide water-saving showerheads or efficient lighting bulbs free of charge.

The Energy Saving Check is implemented by the German Caritas Association and the Federal Association of Energy and Climate Protection Agencies of Germany.

According to Caritas, since 2008 more than one million people have taken a free energy-saving check in Germany, and those who did saved on average around €300 in energy costs per year.

The energy-saving consultation involves a visit to the apartment where consultants measure the energy consumed by certain appliances and record the price of energy bills. They then provide tips on where energy can be saved.

People in Germany who receive basic social security benefits can apply for a free consultation. Households with low income or government transfer payments can also check online whether a free check is available to them.

The states of Berlin, Brandenburg, and Western Pomerania have 50 people working as energy-saving checkers, according to Ulrike Kostka, chairwoman of the Caritas Association in Berlin.
