Prince William and Kate’s country estate in Norfolk was targeted by masked intruders who broke into their home.

Norfolk Police are reportedly investigating the break-in at Anmer Hall on Monday night.

The 10-bedroom hall, a gift from Queen Elizabeth II, is used by the couple and their family as a country retreat.

Reports suggest the family were not in the home at the time of the alleged break-in.

Kate and William, and their children George, Charlotte and Louis, are more commonly based at their Forest Lodge mansion in Windsor.

The family spend the majority of their time at Forest Lodge, the grade II listed mansion in Windsor, which they have described as their “forever home”.

After Kate announced her cancer diagnosis two years ago, it was understood the family retreated to Anmer Hall to take time away from the public eye.

The secluded red brick Georgian mansion sits on the private Sandringham estate and is a short drive from Sandringham House.

The incident, first reported by *The Sun*, has yet to be confirmed by the force.

On Tuesday morning, William was pictured visiting No 10 Downing Street for the first time in the latest example of the evolution of the future king’s public life.

*The Independent* has approached Kensington Palace and Norfolk Police.

## Breaking: Two men arrested, say Norfolk Police

Norfolk Police have just issued this statement:

*Two men have been arrested on suspicion of burglary following an incident at a property on the Sandringham Estate last night (Monday 23 November).*

*Police were called just before 6pm following a report of suspicious activity at the address.Officers attended and specialist resources, including police dogs, drones and the National Police Air Service helicopter, were deployed to assist with searches in the area.*

*Two men were arrested on the estate on suspicion of burglary and trespass. They remain in police custody.The property was unoccupied at the time of the incident.Inquiries remain ongoing.*

## A famed fraudster to royaly: The past residents of Anmer Hall

Since its construction in 1802, Anmer Hall, a country house in the village of Anmer, has been lived in by several notable individuals.

It was first a seat for the Coldham family, before being bought by famed fraudster Ernest Terah Hooley in 1896, according to the History Hit.

The future King Edward VII bought it in 1898, and it became part of the Sandringham Estate.

Among those the home was leased to include John Loader Maffey, 1st Baron Rugby who served as Governor-General of Sudan, and the Duke and Duchess of Kent.

From 1990 it was rented out to Hugh van Cutsem, a friend of King Charles III, and then the family of James Everett, owner of Norfolk Oak kitchen timber company.

Finally, in 2011, it was presented to William and Kate as a wedding gift from Queen Elizabeth II.

## The last time an intruder was arrested on Sandringham Estate

Today’s reports come a year after an intruder was arrested for trespassing on the grounds of Windsor Castle.

Met Police detained the man who was “quickly challenged” by officers on the restricted site on 1 June last year.

He was arrested on suspicion of trespass on a secure site and possession of class A drugs.

Under the Serious Organised Crime and Police Act 2005 it is a criminal offence to trespass on a protected site, which includes crown land and land belonging to the King or his heir in their private capacity.

## Where is Anmer Hall

Anmer Hall, in the village of Anmer in Norfolk, was a wedding gift to William and Kate from Queen Elizabeth II.

The secluded red brick Georgian mansion sits on the monarch’s vast, private Sandringham estate and is a short drive from Sandringham House.

It was built in 1802 and purchased by Queen Victoria in 1862.

Previously, before being taken on by William and Kate, it had been leased to business owners, members of the royal family and civil servants.
