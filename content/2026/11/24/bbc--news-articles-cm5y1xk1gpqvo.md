Prince William meets Burnham in first visit to Downing Street

**The Prince of Wales walked up Downing Street for the first time, as he visited Prime Minister Andy Burnham.**

Behind the black door of No 10, Prince William is expected to talk to Burnham about issues including homelessness, with both the prime minister and the prince committed to tackling the problem.

The two men have met before, such as the Omani state visit last month, where they both attended the state banquets at Windsor Castle.

But this is the first visit that Prince William has made to meet a serving prime minister in Downing Street - and the first senior royal to visit No 10 since the late Queen Elizabeth II came here in 2012.

Prince William and Andy Burnham are expected to discuss homelessness

Burnham has a weekly meeting with the prince's father, the King, and they are said to have developed a friendly relationship.

By convention, the Prince of Wales is meant to meet the prime minister once a year, but not necessarily at Downing Street. He met Sir Keir Starmer last year at Kensington Palace.

Prince William's appearance on a chilly morning will be seen as another step in his apprenticeship as a future king and head of state.

And it's expected that next year the prince will be carrying out more diplomatic visits at the request of the government.

The two men have a shared interest in addressing homelessness, which is expected to be among the topics under discussion.

Prince William has his Homewards project, which is testing ideas to reduce homelessness in six areas of the UK.

Burnham has pledged to give rough sleepers in England support to get off the streets by Christmas.

The prince and Andy Burnham have been active supporters of the Big Issue magazine which supports the homeless.

Both of the men have a reputation for being relaxed communicators in public, with Prince William recently getting plaudits for his unpompous appearance on the Graham Norton Show.

It's a relatively rare sight to see a royal in the prime minister's domain. The late Queen Elizabeth II visited in 1985 to commemorate the 250th anniversary of Downing Street and was here in 2012, when David Cameron was prime minister.

But there were no royal visitors during the intervening years, when Theresa May, Boris Johnson, Liz Truss, Rishi Sunak and Keir Starmer were prime ministers.

The then Prince Charles and Princess Diana visited 10 Downing Street in November 1989, for a dinner hosted by then then prime minister, Margaret Thatcher.
