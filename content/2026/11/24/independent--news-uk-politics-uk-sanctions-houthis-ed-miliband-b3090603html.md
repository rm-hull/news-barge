The UK government is imposing sanctions on the Houthis and four of its senior figures over alleged torture and rape, in a move announced on Tuesday.

The action follows the Yemeni Islamist militant group's decision to resume conflict across the region and inside Yemen itself.

The designations are part of a stepped-up UK response to the crisis in Yemen.

“The Houthis' renewed aggression targeting our partners and innocent civilians is unacceptable and they must be held to account,” Foreign Secretary Ed Miliband said.

“This government has today taken steps to do just that.

“We will not stand by whilst groups like the Houthis take action that drives up the cost of living for the British people while deliberately attempting to undermine the stability of the Middle East.”

![Houthi forces take over from Dhubab to Bab el-Mandeb after Saudi-backed forces withdraw from the area, in Taiz Governorate, Yemen](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/24/12/2026-11-18T142816Z_327129660_RC2TOOABFNAQ_RTRMADP_3_IRAN-CRISIS-YEMEN.JPG?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&auto=webp)

The Houthis are subject to an asset freeze and director disqualification.

The government said that was because of “involvement in activity which threatens the peace, security and stability of Yemen, including escalation of the conflict within Yemen, attacks on international shipping in the Red Sea and Gulf of Aden and missile and drone attacks against regional partners”.

The four sanctioned individuals face an asset freeze, a travel ban, and director disqualification.

They are:

* **Abdulkarim Amir al-Din Hussein Al-Houthi**, who served as Houthi Minister of Interior of the Houthi authorities in Sana’a since 2019. The government said he has been responsible for the police, security forces, Criminal Investigation Department, women’s security unit and detention facilities of the Ministry of Interior, in whose custody detainees have been subjected to torture and other cruel, inhuman or degrading treatment, including electric shocks, beatings and sexual violence.
* **Abdul Hakim Hashem Ali Al-Khaiwani**, who was the Head of the Houthi Security and Intelligence Service and previously served as Houthi Deputy Minister of Interior.
* **Mohammed Moufleh**, a Houthi-appointed judge in Yemen who has allegedly been involved in a serious violation of an individual’s right to liberty and security of a person.
* **Sarim Al-Din Muffadal**, a senior Houthi prosecutor in Yemen. The government said that he is reasonably suspected of having directly perpetrated, or knowingly permitted or acquiesced in, sexual violence including rape, torture and other cruel, inhuman or degrading treatment of women detainees.

![Foreign secretary Ed Miliband has announced sanctions for four Houthi leaders](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/21/09/2305461673..?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&auto=webp)

In a statement, the Foreign, Commonwealth & Development Office said that the behaviour of the Houthis in the Bab el Mandeb Strait has continued to disrupt global supply chains.

That, in turn, has put added pressure on the cost of living for British families.

Backed by Iran, the Houthis have increased attacks against Saudi Arabia's civilian infrastructure, resulting in a number of fatalities, as well as renewing conflict inside Yemen, it said.

The UK has recently led international efforts to strengthen the UN Yemen sanctions regime, using its role as UN Security Council penholder on Yemen to increase pressure on the Houthis to cease their aggression.
