## Security is tight after a recent drone strike near Mukachevo, as Budapest names Russia as the aggressor but rules out sending weapons or troops.

Hungarian Prime Minister Péter Magyar is holding talks with Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy in western Ukraine, where he will also meet leaders of the region's ethnic Hungarian community.

Magyar announced on Facebook that Foreign Minister Anita Orbán would go with him to Zakarpattia, a Ukrainian region bordering Hungary that is home to a sizeable Hungarian minority.

According to preliminary information obtained by the Hungarian outlet Népszabadság (source in Hungarian), the programme may take in three cities and include cultural events alongside the political talks.

Security is a concern: in mid-November a drone attack hit the area around Mukachevo (Munkács in Hungarian), one of the region's main towns.

Citing the regional military administration (source in Ukrainian), the Hungarian-language news portal Kárpátalja.ma has warned residents to expect traffic restrictions in several places during the visit.

Magyar and Zelenskyy have already met at EU and NATO summits. However, Budapest had made a bilateral meeting conditional on Kyiv meeting its demands over the rights of Ukraine's Hungarian minority.

Magyar signalled that the meeting was imminent in a speech to the Hungarian parliament on Monday. In it, he accused the government of his predecessor, Viktor Orbán, of running a "hate-inciting" election campaign against Ukraine and its president. His government, he said, would stand up for the Hungarians of Zakarpattia and for the Ukrainian people under attack, even if Moscow objected.

Magyar said he would bring Hungary's message to the Hungarians of Zakarpattia. He said they had spent almost five years suffering, living in fear and freezing through the winters since Russia launched its full-scale invasion.

He stressed that Russia was the aggressor and that Ukraine and its people were the victims. He added that Hungary would continue to support Ukraine, though not with weapons or troops, but with humanitarian aid and diplomatic backing.
