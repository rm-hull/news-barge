The U.S. ambassador to Israel, Mike Huckabee, has questioned whether Washington will actually halt its $3.8 billion in annual military aid to Israel by the end of this year, as President Donald Trump promised on the day of this month's midterm elections.

Trump's announcement came as voters headed to the polls on November 3, and it surprised many. He said he had struck a deal with Prime MinisterBenjamin Netanyahu several months earlier to stop providing Israel with around $4 billion in annual U.S. funding.

Trump did not explain how the funding would be halted or offer any details. The U.S. has a longstanding agreement with Israel, known as a Memorandum of Understanding, under which it provides $3.8 billion a year until 2028 to fund purchases of American-made military equipment.

![US ambassador to Israel, Mike Huckabee, has called the move a 'discrimination against the Jewish people'](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/09/08/08/2026-09-06T145239Z_574314735_RC2WCNAY9CML_RTRMADP_3_ISRAEL-PALESTINIANS-SETTLEMENTS.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Huckabee, a Republican former governor of Arkansas, said Congress had allocated the funding and that he was unsure whether it could be stopped without congressional approval.

He was speaking in a pre-recorded interview shown on Tuesday at a conference on U.S. -Israel relations at the Institute for National Security Studies in Tel Aviv, where he said the MoU "is supposed to continue for two more years, so I'm not certain that ‌there ⁠will be a cessation on an abrupt basis by the end of this year."

Trump had said Israel no longer needed U.S. funds, and that the issue had been used as a "bad talking point" by his left-wing critics. The election that day subsequently saw Democrats win control of both houses of Congress from Trump's Republicans, including many who publicly oppose military funding for Israel.

Huckabee said U.S. and Israeli officials had been working on a new MoU "for months". He did not say whether those talks continued after Trump announced the military aid would end.

![Trump had said Israel no longer needed U.S. funds, and that the issue had been used as a "bad talking point" by his left-wing critics](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/09/12/2026-11-03T150942Z_1570666144_RC2KQIAIJNNP_RTRMADP_3_USA-TRUMP-ISRAEL.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Public support for Israel has fallen in the United States, particularly among Democrats but also among Republicans, over Israel's conduct in the war in Gaza and over the unpopular war Trump launched alongside Netanyahu this year against Iran.

Netanyahu, who lost his parliamentary majority in an October 27 election but is still serving as a caretaker, has publicly discussed phasing out U.S. military aid, a move analysts say was intended to preempt growing criticism in the U.S. of Israel.

The Democrats' resounding victory over the Republicans has been met with apprehension in Israel, where Netanyahu emphasized his close relationship with Trump in his own campaign.

Huckabee, an Evangelical Christian and staunch supporter of Israel, said that he hoped there would not be a "dramatic, seismic ​shift" in U.S. -Israel relations, arguing that the partnership benefited the citizens of both countries.
