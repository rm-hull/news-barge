The number of Britons who think the government is handling Brexit well has risen significantly over the past week, in a boost to Andy Burnham.

The findings come from a survey carried out after the prime minister secured a landmark agreement with the European Union – which will bring about a long-awaited youth mobility scheme and end food and drink checks at the border – in £17bn boost for the British economy.

Among those who voted Remain, 51 per cent now say the issue is being handled well, up from 37 per cent last week, YouGov found.

Leave voters were much more negative, with 74 per cent now thinking the issue is being handled badly, compared to 69 per cent last week.

![Prime minister Andy Burnham at a roundtable with business leaders at the official residence of the British ambassador to Belgium, in Brussels](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/24/13/f9c81b41376d819e9107efe121526eefY29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzk1NjE0MzUw-2.86547349.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Overall. 30 per cent of Britons now think the government is handling the issue well, up from 24 per cent – a six point rise in a week.

Matthew Smith, from YouGov, said: “The percentage of Britons saying the government is handling Brexit well has reached 30 per cent, its highest point since May 2022. This includes a six-point jump after a week of media speculation about the possibility of rejoining the EU.”

In Brussels on Friday, the PM said that both sides would "seek to conduct an informal brainstorming process" to look at the options for the UK's "long-term relationship" with the EU, which he has not ruled out could even see Britain rejoin the bloc.

Mr Burnham has previously suggested these options could include rejoining the customs union, the single market or a fresh referendum on full EU membership.

However, he has ruled out major changes to the UK-EU relationship before the next election, saying he will stick to Labour's 2024 manifesto pledge and so-called red lines not to return to the single market, customs union or freedom of movement.

![](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/20/09/download.-1..jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

On Monday he defended his new EU deal, arguing that pragmatic negotiations on food, emissions, and youth schemes will lower the cost of living and help British exporters.

A number of former prime ministers have warned against reopening the debate over rejoining the EU. Rishi Sunak told Sky News the issue was "divisive" and "ends up being a huge distraction for government".

Baroness Theresa May told the BBC she was "aghast" that Mr Burnham could be considering rejoining the EU.

And ex-Labour PM Sir Tony Blair warned Britain should only rejoin the bloc if it reforms, and allows more innovation on technology.
