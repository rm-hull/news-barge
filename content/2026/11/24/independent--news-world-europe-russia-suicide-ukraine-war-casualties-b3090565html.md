More than 1,000 Russian soldiers have taken their own lives since the invasion of Ukraine, according to a new report.

At least 1,087 military personnel ended their own life in Ukraine or in Russian border regions between 2022 and 2026, according to confidential data held by state statistics agency Rosstat and seen by *The Guardian*.

Leaked data from a corpus of 11 million death certificates shows that the trend has steadily increased since the full-scale invasion, with war and military-related deaths rising from more than 50 in 2022 to 365 last year.

The newspaper also identified a further 885 deaths in or near Ukraine that were classified as ‘firearm-related injuries’ of ‘undermined intent’, which experts said could include suicides.

![In September, president Vladimir Putin (pictured on Monday) authorised another expansion of the military - the fourth this year - allowing Moscow to stand 2,441,630 units, including 1,550,500 active duty military personnel.](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/24/05/2026-11-23T123200Z_573496948_RC2OTOAJAJU2_RTRMADP_3_RUSSIA-PUTIN.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Russia does not publish casualty figures, but Ukraine estimates that Moscow has lost as many as 1,641,489 troops in Ukraine since February 2022, including deaths and injuries - more than the the 1.5m active duty personnel currently at its disposal.

A separate analysis of leaked Rosstat records by the *Financial Times* concluded that at least 1,229 Russian soldiers died by suicide between 2022 and September of this year.

That made Russian army soldiers more than twice as likely to die by suicide than the population at large, according to their analysis.

The FT also found a steadily increasing trend over the duration of the war, rising from 235 in 2023 to around 300 in 2024 and 371 in 2025.

This year was expected to track more than 400 deaths by suicide, based on current trends, the newspaper reported.

More than 600 of the total deaths occurred in the frontline areas partly occupied by Russia, which have seen some of the conflict’s most bitter attritional fighting.

It emerged on Monday that as many as 700,000 Russian soldiers are estimated to have been killed in the war since 2022, based on confidential Rosstat data revealed in a hack earlier this month.

Analysis by exiled Russian outlets Meduza and Mediazona tallied 490,000 losses up to September 2026, rising past 700,000 when accounting for those missing in action.

Ukraine said in September that Russia had taken 300,000 casualties in 2026 alone - 18% of the casualties in 16% of the time since the invasion.

British intelligence also estimated in September that around 500,000 Russian soldiers had been killed since the start of the invasion.

![An aerial view of destroyed buildings in the frontline town of Kostyantynivka, Donetsk region late last year](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/15/12/2246236854..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

That toll is becoming unmanageable, according to Western officials, who in August estimated that the Kremlin was losing six thousand more troops per month than it was able to enlist.

That trend has only worsened with October 2026 becoming the deadliest month for Russian combat losses since 2022, according to Ukraine’s general staff.
