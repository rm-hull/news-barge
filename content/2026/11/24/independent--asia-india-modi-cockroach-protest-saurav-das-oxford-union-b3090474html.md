A leader of India's cockroach movement accused the government of harassment after he was barred from flying to the UK, where he was due to speak about the recent student protests at multiple universities.

Saurav Das, journalist-turned-spokesperson for the pressure group Cockroach Janta Party (CJP), was one of the most recognised faces of the youth-led movement that forced the resignation of education minister Dharmendra Pradhan earlier this year, in a major embarrassment for prime minister Narendra Modi.

In the wake of those protests in the summer, the CJP emerged as one of the biggest challenges to Mr Modi in his third term in office.

![Saurav Das speaks during a press conference in Delhi on 24 November 2026](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/24/12/2026-11-24T114641Z_146387629_RC2AUOAHJ80P_RTRMADP_3_INDIA-POLITICS.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Mr Das alleged on Tuesday that immigration officials at the Delhi airport told him his passport had been "flagged" and that he would not be allowed to board his flight to London "despite having a valid visa and a passport."

At the immigration office, he found that the foreign ministry had emailed him a show-cause notice over “discrepancies about his place of birth” just five hours before his flight was scheduled to take off at 1am local time. He was required to respond to the notice within seven days, Mr Das told *The Independent.*

He said the last line of the email stated: “You are advised not to use the passport until you submit a response.”

“It was all a pre-planned and targeted harassment tactic," he said. "Because they would have very well sent a notice before, but they chose to do it four, five hours before the flight so that I have no recourse to the courts.”

Mr Das said he had been invited by the Oxford Union to speak about the cockroach movement on 1 December. Over the course of 10 days in the UK, the 27-year-old was also scheduled to speak at events organised by Indian students at the University of Edinburgh, London School of Economics, Cambridge University, and Queen Mary University.

Mr Das said he would move court to determine the exact grounds for the show-cause notice because it only mentioned that the foreign ministry had "received inputs about discrepancies in my place of birth".

"I will ask them to furnish what are the inputs that they have gotten so that I can respond properly, you know, in my defence. I'll ask them to give me detailed grounds and I will respond to them," he said.

"I'll also be challenging this in the court."

The Indian government has not issued an official statement explaining why Mr Das was stopped from boarding his flight.

![Supporters of the Cockroach Janta Party take part in a protest march on 16 October 2026](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/17/09/2300535682..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Mr Das said his harassment was not "entirely unexpected".

"I would have been surprised if they would have let me board the flight in any case or go out of the country," he said.

"Because they are known to target people when they are scared or are anxious about a movement like this," he added, referring to the Modi government. "Since they do not have anything on us, because we are all very clean and don't come with any kind of political baggage, they’re resorting to such frivolous methods, which people are seeing and condemning.”

Mr Das, along with fellow cockroach movement leader, Ashutosh Ranka, spent seven days in prison last month for trespassing at Jantar Mantar, an 18th-century observatory which had newly been denotified as an official protest site.

This would have been Mr Das's first international trip since the arrest.

Hundreds of cockroach party supporters, students, politicians, and journalists were detained in October when they hit the streets to demand the resignation of the chief election commissioner, Gyanesh Kumar, over a controversial revision to voter rolls that led to the removal of 130 million names. Mr Kumar and the Modi government denied any wrongdoing.

The brutal crackdown, which saw the government deploy nearly 20,000 police and paramilitary personnel and turn the city into a fortress, took the wind out of the protests.

But Mr Das said "we will come back very soon with full force".

![Cockroach Janta Party spokespersons Saurav Das, centre, and Ashutosh Ranka, right, and Indian’s health minister JP Nadda attend a press conference after their meeting in Delhi on 25 July 2026](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/07/26/11/2286919755..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

"We are identifying deleted voters. We are talking to them and we want to give them a face," he added.

The UK chapter of the Students Federation of India, which was organising the discussion in Edinburgh, condemned the Modi government’s decision to "restrict" Mr Das's trip.

It is rare but not unprecedented for the Modi government to bar private citizens from leaving the country.

Activist Bhanu Tatak was stopped from boarding a flight to Dublin last September.

In 2022, Amnesty International India head Aakar Patel was stopped at the Bengaluru airport before he could board his flight to the US. The same year, journalist Rana Ayyub was stopped from taking a flight to London at the Mumbai airport.
