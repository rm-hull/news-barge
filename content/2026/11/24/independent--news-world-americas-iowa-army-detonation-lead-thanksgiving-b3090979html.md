Iowa families have had their Thanksgiving plans upended after the Army’s planned detonation of bomb-making chemicals was scheduled for Wednesday morning.

The controlled blast of nearly 70 barrels of lead azide near Middletown, Iowa, will send a plume of toxic lead dust into the air the day before millions of Americans sit down to enjoy a home-cooked meal together. New maps from the Army show the plume potentially drifting all the way toward northern Missouri.

In an alert to residents, federal emergency officials said some people within five miles southwest of the detonation site may be evacuated before the blast scheduled for 9 a.m. Central Time and that people living five to 20 miles away should stay indoors with the windows shut.

State health officials in neighboring Illinois warned residents to stay indoors and keep their windows closed for 24 to 48 hours after the detonation — ruining any plans families may have had the day of to travel to see family or have guests over.

Newsmax National Correspondent Joe Moeller reported Tuesday that federal officials were going door-to-door in Middletown, handing out flyers advising residents to evacuate to the Salvation Army in Burlington during the detonation.

![The controlled blast of nearly 70 barrels of lead azide near Middletown, Iowa, will send a plume of toxic lead dust into the air the day before millions of Americans sit down to enjoy a home-cooked meal together](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/24/20/32/GettyImages-2306414929.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

One local, Bob Kemp, said that he ditched his travel plans to watch over his and his sister’s homes.

“I’m going to stay here and see how things go,” Kemp told WQAD News 8. “If they would have blown it today, I probably would have went to Texas for Thanksgiving, but since that’s not happening, it’s going to happen on Wednesday, that changed all our plans.”

The detonation was initially planned for last Friday, but the Army changed plans three times over health and environmental concerns.

Environmental groups sued last week to block the blast, but a federal appeals court ruled Monday that the Environmental Protection Agency put in adequate protections in an emergency permit to minimize the spread of lead dust.

“Today, the 8th Circuit decided to let the Army proceed with its plan, and we respect that decision,” environmental lawyer Rachel Rintelmann said in a statement Monday. “But the Army created this emergency through neglect, and it owes the people of Iowa more information about the planned detonation and more safeguards for their health.”

![In an alert to residents, federal emergency officials said some people within five miles southwest of the detonation site may be evacuated before the blast scheduled for 9 a.m. Central Time and that people living five to 20 miles away should stay indoors with the windows shut](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/24/20/32/GettyImages-2306414925.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Another resident felt as if she and other locals were like test animals in the government’s detonation.

“I’m really angry about this. I feel like we’re guinea pigs,” Krys Plate, a retired second-grade teacher who lives with her husband and their two teenage daughters two miles south of the fence line of the Iowa Army Ammunition Plant where the detonation is occurring, told *The New York Times*.

“Everybody knows that the acceptable level of lead is zero,” Plate said.

The Army has said that the weather was most favorable for the detonation on Wednesday, but it’s unclear why it couldn’t wait till after the busy holiday week. *The Independent* has reached out to the Army for comment.

![One local, Bob Kemp, said that he ditched his travel plans to watch over his and his sister’s homes, telling local news, ‘I’m going to stay here and see how things go’](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/24/20/27/GettyImages-2306414912.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Instead of going to the store to pick up a turkey and pumpkin pie for Thanksgiving, Iowans are grabbing window insulation kits and N95 masks.

Keara, a mother who lives 60 miles from the detonation site, posted a vlog on TikTok Monday of her shopping for some last-minute supplies.

She picked up masks, disinfectant wipes, disposable gloves and other essentials she felt were necessary after the blast.

“They just expect us to wipe our cars off with a baby wipe & move on the day before Thanksgiving? WTF,” Keara wrote in the caption of her video.

Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin wrote in an X post Tuesday that federal emergency officials will “ensure every resident who needs to temporarily evacuate will receive the support they need, including transportation, lodging, meals and other essential assistance.

“The safety and security of the American people is our number one priority.”

![Keara, a mother who lives 60 miles from the detonation site, posted a vlog on TikTok on Monday of her shopping for some last-minute supplies, including masks, disinfectant wipes and disposable gloves](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/24/20/47/GettyImages-2306414936.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

In a Truth Social post before the appeals court’s decision Monday, President Donald Trump defended the detonation while also trying to make Democratic Governor-elect Rob Sand responsible if the blast did not occur.

“The Experts, in particular the United States Military, feel that this approach will be a far safer one than allowing the situation to fester, not knowing when they will detonate by themselves,” Trump wrote.

“The weather, right now, is optimal for this planned response to take place. I feel the proper course is to get rid of this longterm problem but, at the same time, have ordered the Military not to do so until such time as the Eighth Circuit agrees that we can go forward, and am further making this decision fully subject to the incoming Governor Elect, Rob Sand, agreeing to the operation.”

Trump then said if Sand did not agree, the Army would not proceed with the detonation and he would “be forced to hold the incoming Governor responsible for any and all death or destruction caused by this very unfortunate, and longterm, situation.”

Sand later wrote on X that the Trump administration “should make this decision based on the information they have from the people they brought in to do the job months ago and have been on the ground in Middletown for weeks.

“Everyday Iowans are entitled to know how Middletown came to this situation, and learn why the administration believes this is the best path forward.”
