## York girl to walk 10km for children spending Christmas in hospital

Layla-May Scott, 15, from Acomb, who could not walk last Christmas, is now raising money for the children's ward at York Hospital *(Image: Siobhan Scott)*

A teenager who could not walk last Christmas is now raising money for children in hospital.

Layla-May Scott, 15, from Acomb, York, is now planning to walk ten kilometres dressed as an elf on December 19th, exactly one year after undergoing surgery that left her unable to walk during the festive season.

Layla-May's operation, which took place on November 13th, 2025, involved three procedures in a single session to treat Haglund's deformity and coccydynia.

These conditions had caused her years of foot pain and were aggravated by a back injury sustained during two separate falls.

This year, Layla-May and her mother, Siobhan, are set to embark on the long journey through York, taking in historic sites such as Micklegate Bar, the Minster, Clifford's Tower, and Rowntree Park.

Their aim is to raise funds for toys and gifts for the children's ward at York Hospital.

The pair, who started their training on Sunday, November 15th, are determined to give this challenge their all and raise as much money as possible for the cause.

They plan to deliver the gifts to the hospital dressed as Christmas elves on December 21st.

So far, they have managed to raise £200 towards their £250 target.

In a heartfelt statement on their GoFundMe page, Siobhan and Layla-May shared their motivation behind the initiative.

They wrote: "This time last year, Layla-May was unable to walk due to surgery on her feet.

"Watching her go through the sadness of missing out on a lot of the Christmas joy and seeing how much her mobility was affected just made us realise how precious something as simple as being able to walk can be.

"This year we wanted to turn that experience into something positive.

"Now that Layla-May is back on her feet, we decided we want to do this walk for charity to challenge ourselves and, most importantly, to give something back to the children and families that have to spend Christmas in hospital this year.

"We have always been really passionate about helping others when in need, and knowing that we are making others happy in hard times makes us happy too.

"We have chosen York Hospital children’s ward because we want to help bring the Christmas spirit back a little bit for these children who don’t get to spend it in the way that they would wish.

"Hopefully, us doing this will give them some happiness and distraction in difficult times."

The pair have expressed their hope that every donation, no matter how big or small, will help them make a difference.

They invite supporters to contribute to their GoFundMe page at gofundme.com/f/christmas-10km-walk-for-york-hospital-childrens-ward.
