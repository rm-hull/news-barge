The US commerce department published an official notice on Monday adding the Singapore-based company Megaspeed, and its subsidiaries in Thailand and Malaysia, to a trade blacklist.

Megaspeed, an Nvidia partner which has been under investigation by US authorities since 2024 over whether it diverted AI chips to China, will now need approval from the commerce department to obtain US exports, which is likely to be denied.

The expected addition of the company follows revelations that the recently reinstated Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang had a ⁠relationship with Alice Huang, a Chinese businesswoman and Megaspeed’s controlling shareholder, who is reportedly alleged to be a ⁠spy for Beijing. China has said the spy accusations are “groundless”.

“The Department of Commerce still needs to explain to Congress why this took so long,” Senator Pete Ricketts, a Nebraska Republican, said in a social media post.

The New York Times reported on Monday that Alice Huang “kept in contact with an official of China’s top spy agency for more than a year while she was carrying out a secret affair with Jensen Huang, according to multiple people with knowledge of US intelligence reports that describe the encounters”.

The US justice department is investigating whether Megaspeed violated US export controls by shipping Nvidia chips to China.

Megaspeed did not immediately return a request for comment. A week ago, the company issued a statement saying it conducts legitimate and lawful business, including complying with US export controls.

It also said Alice Huang officially left Megaspeed in August 2025.

Nvidia has said it recently formed an export control committee to oversee trade compliance and national security risks.

As Semafor notes, the US commerce secretary, Howard Lutnick, has been under pressure to act since the news of Jensen Huang’s relationship with the former reproter for Chinese state television who became a director at Singapore-based Megaspeed in 2023 became public.

The US treasury secretary, Scott Bessent, said earlier this month that his agency along with the Pentagon, the state department and the US trade representative’s office had all wanted Megaspeed on the blacklist “for months” but that “Commerce holds the pen”.

After the news broke, treasury secretary Scott Bessent publicly complained that the commerce department had not yet placed the company on its entity list.

He also called out Nvidia. “The real story here should be: did Nvidia know their client and was there a robust effort at Nvidia to make sure that there was no leakage and that they were selling to what were obviously pass-through shell companies?” Bessent told CNBC’s Squawk Box program.

The US justice department and commerce department issued a joint statement last week saying they would “bring the full extent of the law against Megaspeed for any criminal conduct that may have occurred”.
