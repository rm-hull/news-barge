Families of young girls who survived the fatal Southport attack have accused local council authority figures of "shameful and outrageous" decisions regarding school support for their children.

A single school received more than £500,000 to help pupils cope with the trauma after Axel Rudakubana killed three children at a Taylor Swift-themed holiday dance class in July 2024.

However, other schools with affected youngsters received no financial support, as outlined in a report released on Tuesday by Sefton Council.

The local authority stated that schools needed to inform it of any required help and funding, which seven area schools have done.

Rudakubana, 20, is serving a life sentence for the murders of nine-year-old Alice da Silva Aguiar; six-year-old Bebe King; and seven-year-old Elsie Dot Stancombe; as well as the attempted murder of eight other children and two adults.

Sefton Council has since spent more than £1 million on a dedicated educational fund to address the fallout from the killings and the following riots across the country.

But Nicola Ryan-Donnelly, partner at Fletchers Solicitors – representing a number of child survivors and their families, said: “The disparity between funding given to schools attended by the Southport survivors is shameful and outrageous.

“Eye-watering sums have been handed out to single schools, while six schools attended by 13 surviving girls we represent received absolutely nothing from this pot.

![Sefton Council has since spent more than £1 million for a special education fund to deal with the consequences of the attack](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/07/08/11/2226890294..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“It is clear now why the council has taken so long to share these figures with parents – because it has completely failed in its due diligence to establish where money and support was most needed to help little girls who were returning to class utterly traumatised by what they had witnessed and endured.”

A mother whose daughter, identified only as C1, was stabbed 33 times said: “I’m horrified by the amount of money that has been given to some schools while we have had to fight for every penny to support our daughter, and none of it has come from this fund.

“To know that we have had such a private, anonymous, invisible battle to get our daughter the most basic support and care she needs at school, and that it has been provided by other local authorities, while Sefton Council was handing out over half a million pounds to a single school just makes me feel incredibly angry and sad.

“Sefton Council has often used the phrase that it has supported the ‘most affected’ children, but our daughter went back to school with visible injuries. Her classmates suffered trauma because they could see what had happened to her, yet they and her teachers received no funding for wellbeing support.

“We had no idea that this level of funding was available, and lots of the headteachers were in the classroom with our girls trying to help and support them. Funding was not at the forefront of their mind, they didn’t have the capacity.

“Sefton Council were wrong to put the responsibility on them to ask for money. The council should have come to every school with children who were actually present at the incident and given them fair and equal access to support.”

The mother of C6 and child T, whose daughters attended the class together, said: “I feel absolutely disgusted by the huge discrepancies in the money that has been given to schools. The council never spoke to us or asked us what we needed, meanwhile our daughters have been passed from pillar to post, moving schools to try and get the help they needed in class after everything they had been through.”

![Six-year-old Bebe King, seven-year-old Elsie Dot Stancombe and Alice Dasilva Aguiar, nine, died in the Southport attack](https://static.independent.co.uk/2024/08/06/09/02e469a0e6e148877942ed386fd7d0d8Y29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzIzMDE4MDA3-2.77002632.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The mother of child C3 added: “The schools with surviving girls should have been looked after as a priority with this education recovery funding.

“There are many children (our daughter included) whose school was not considered in this. Her school received nothing from the recovery fund with four surviving girls attending.

“I feel extremely frustrated and disappointed with Sefton Council and whoever made these decisions. Trauma training was received in generic pre-recorded videos for all staff. This is not substantive in supporting the trauma our girls endured.”

A spokesperson for Sefton Council said: “Immediately following the 29th and 30th of July 2024, Sefton Council had to make a series of decisions to provide urgent humanitarian assistance to the community of Southport.

“This included making support available across Southport schools with additional funding available for schools facing specific pressures arising from the events.

“Those pressures varied, as did the staffing, specialist support and other resources already available within each school who approached the council for help.

“Schools who came to the council with additional needs were asked to provide specific information about what they required.

“Allocations varied based on the type of help required by those schools. Funding supported counselling, pastoral and therapeutic provision, additional staffing and measures to help children remain safe, connected to trusted adults and engaged in education.

“Supporting school staff was also part of sustaining that care as in many cases staff were helping children and families through exceptionally difficult circumstances while managing the impact on themselves and their colleagues.

“Individual school grants were one part of a wider response.

“As we have already stated, recovery continues beyond this reported funding.

“Lancashire County Council and schools in Lancashire have led support arrangements for affected children in their authority, while both Lancashire and Sefton Councils have worked closely to ensure a consistent supportive approach was undertaken.”
