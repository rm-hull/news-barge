York BID is calling on the council to explore further parking incentives after the group found high charges are “putting local customers off visiting” businesses.

The business improvement district has urged council bosses to go further after they announced that York residents who hold a £15-a-year Minster Badge would receive a 50 per cent discount at council-run car parks outside the city centre.

Cllr Kate Ravilious, the council’s executive member for transport, said any further proposals on parking would be considered “carefully”.

York BID said it wants the council to “consider how similar incentives could support city centre trade”, after York’s hourly parking rate rose from £3.10 to £4.85 last year.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21757532.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Andrew Lowson, York BID’s executive director *(Image: Supplied)*

Andrew Lowson, York BID’s executive director, said it is “hearing from local traders that higher parking charges are putting local people off, especially when they only want to come in for an hour or two”.

“Those visits matter. They’re people popping into a shop, meeting a friend for coffee or staying for lunch.”

He said the BID understands the “financial pressures the council faces, and we support better sustainable transport”, but added: “We also need to look at what can be done now to encourage local people into the city centre.

“The changes at community car parks give us a starting point for that discussion.”

Michael Hjort, who is York BID’s chair, a founder of Melton’s restaurant in Scarcroft Road and the proprietor of Walmgate Ale House and the Chopping Block, added: “York has a huge amount to offer, and we want residents to be able to enjoy it regularly.

“For businesses, local customers who come back week after week make a real difference.

“We’d really like to work with the council to agree which of the proposed measures can be taken forward and when.

“Businesses need to see progress, and we’ll keep bringing their experiences to the table as those plans develop.”

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/19384696.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Cllr Kate Ravilious, City of York Council's executive member for transport *(Image: Dylan Connell)*

Cllr Ravilious said: “We appreciate the BID’s constructive approach to this issue and have listened to its concerns.

“There are different measures that could help, and we need to consider each carefully, including as part of current budget setting discussions, before making any decision.

“We share the BID’s commitment to wanting to get as many residents and visitors into York to spend their money and support local businesses as possible.”

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21751960.jpg?type=mds-article-620)A poster against the initially 500 per cent hike in fees at the Bishopthorpe Road car park last year *(Image: Dylan Connell)*

City of York Council’s changes to the Minster Badge scheme followed backlash from residents and traders across the city after hikes to parking charges.

More than 8,000 people signed a petition calling on the council to rethink parking charges at the Bishopthorpe Road car park, which initially saw parking fees rise by more than 500 per cent.

Cllr Ravilious said the changes to the Minster Badge scheme would help people “save money while making it easier to access local shops, services and community facilities”.
