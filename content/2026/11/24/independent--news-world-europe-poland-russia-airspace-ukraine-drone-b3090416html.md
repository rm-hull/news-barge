A Russian drone briefly entered Polish airspace during an attack on Ukraine, Poland’s military warned on Tuesday.

The alleged incursion comes after officials said that three Russian Kh-101 missiles landed in Poland on Friday, deepening concerns that the war could spill over into Nato territory.

“It’s hard to imagine this being a coincidence, so we assume it was an intentional act,” Deputy Defence Minister Pawel Zalewski told local news.

![Mr Zalewski assumes the move was an 'intentional act'](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/24/08/2251183492..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

While Poland’s military said authorisation was given to shoot the drone down, a decision to not open fire was taken after it changed course and headed east – taking into account the “potential threat” to civilians.

Mr Zalewski added that Nato will deploy an “AWACS early-warning system aircraft” to Poland as part of its response to the missile incident.

AN AWACS plane tends to operate at high altitude and can spot low-flying aircraft, ships and missiles beyond the line of sight of ground radar.

The warning comes after Russia’s Foreign Ministry accused Estonia on Monday of letting Ukraine use its airspace “on the quiet” – arguing that such a provocative move risked triggering an open war with the country.

The ministry also accused Estonia of attempting to obstruct the movement of Russian cargo ships in the Baltic Sea.

But Estonia denied the accusation in a swift response.

“Estonia has never allowed and will never allow its territory to be used for military operations by another country,” the foreign ministry said in a statement.

![Estonia will close its land borders with Russia on December 1](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/20/12/2026-11-17T154428Z_356970175_RC2MPOAGCV7N_RTRMADP_3_UKRAINE-CRISIS-ESTONIA-BORDER.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The spat comes after Estonia confirmed on Friday that it is closing in road border crossings with Russia for at least months after accusing Russian security services of staging an attempted arson attack on an Estonian defence company.

The Kremlin has denied any involvement in the weekend arson attack and Foreign Ministry spokeswoman Maria Zakharova said that Russia would respond with political and diplomatic measures if Estonia went ahead with the closure.

She described such a move as “tantamount to attempts to erect a new Iron Curtain under the false pretence of some sort of threat from the East”.

Estonia is expected to close its road borders with Russia on December 1. Rail freight will not be affected by may be subject to strict custom checks.
