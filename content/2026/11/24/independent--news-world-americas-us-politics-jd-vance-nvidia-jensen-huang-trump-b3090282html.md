Vice President JD Vance personally intervened over the weekend to help bring Jensen Huang back as chief executive of Nvidia, working behind the scenes with the company’s board and the ousted executive to secure his reinstatement, according to reports on Monday.

Nvidia announced early Monday morning that Huang was returning to lead the chipmaker, reversing his removal slightly more than a week earlier following revelations of multiple extramarital affairs, including allegedly one with a woman U.S. officials believe to be a Chinese intelligence asset.

While President Donald Trump publicly took credit for spearheading the effort, he asked Vance — who entered office with deep ties to Silicon Valley — to communicate privately with Huang and negotiate with Nvidia’s board, people familiar with the discussions told *The New York Times*.

A source familiar with the matter also told *The Hill* that Vance’s standing in the technology sector positioned him to effectively lobby the company’s directors.

Trump’s push to restore Huang intensified late last week after the president returned to Washington from a trip to China and watched the stock market decline on Friday, as reported by *The New York Times*.

![Vice President JD Vance reportedly worked directly with Nvidia’s board and ousted chief executive Jensen Huang over the weekend to help secure the tech leader’s return to the company](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/23/20/2306100795..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

To persuade Nvidia’s board to reverse its prior stance against rehiring Huang, Vance reportedly worked to deliver two specific assurances from the White House: confirmation that the administration did not view Huang as a national security threat, and a statement that he was not facing criminal charges. Only a small circle of senior government officials and Nvidia insiders were aware of the talks, which concluded late Sunday night.

Trump addressed his involvement during a Monday interview with CNBC, confirming he had reached out to the company’s leadership.

“I’ve been speaking to the board directly and indirectly, and I felt very strongly about it,” Trump told CNBC. “And I felt more strongly as I saw what was happening with the company, because that was to me a very important part of our country.”

Vance later posted a video clip on X of Trump’s CNBC remarks discussing the vice president's lobbying efforts. In a separate telephone interview with The New York Times after the deal became public, Trump expressed surprise at the immediate market surge following the announcement.

“I just thought he should be running the company, and I didn’t know it was going to have this kind of an impact,” Trump told *The New York Times*. “I’ve never seen anything quite like it, actually. The stock is up.”

![To secure the ousted chief executive’s return, the administration assured Nvidia’s board that Jensen Huang was neither a national security threat nor facing criminal charges](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/23/14/2026-11-23T142506Z_773353279_RC2YEMA3VO46_RTRMADP_3_NVIDIA-CEO.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

He also asked the outlet, “Did I do a good job last night?”

In a post on Truth Social on Monday morning, Trump provided the public assurances the board had sought.

“The United States government has no reason to believe that Jensen’s return to Nvidia poses any risk to national security,” Trump wrote. “The attorney general of the United States has confirmed that the U.S. government does not possess evidence that he has engaged in any unlawful conduct.”

A source told *The Hill* that both Trump and Vance were briefed on the national security aspects of the situation and saw no evidence that Huang acted illegally or harmed U.S. interests. The source added that the administration views Huang as a crucial figure in protecting open-source artificial intelligence models and preventing closed-source competitors like OpenAI and Anthropic from gaining too much market dominance.

![President Donald Trump intensified his push to restore Huang after watching the stock market slide on Friday, viewing the executive as essential to protecting open-source AI models](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/24/1/12/GettyImages-2297774940.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Vance also maintains close relationships with several of Huang’s prominent allies in the tech industry, including investor David Sacks, co-chairman of the President’s Council of Advisers on Science and Technology, as reported by *The New York Times*.

Despite the White House’s backing, the decision drew immediate pushback from lawmakers on Capitol Hill, where concerns linger over national security and potential export control violations.

*The New York Times* reported on Sunday that Alice Huang, the woman involved in the affair with the Nvidia chief, maintained contact with an official from China’s primary intelligence agency for over a year during their relationship. Additionally, her employer, the Singapore-based firm Megaspeed, is currently under Justice Department investigation for allegedly smuggling restricted Nvidia chips into China.

In its Monday announcement, Nvidia stated it would hire a chief compliance officer and that its internal investigation into the matter would continue with Huang’s cooperation.

![As part of its Monday announcement, Nvidia agreed to hire a chief compliance officer and said its internal investigation into the matter will continue with Huang’s cooperation](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/24/01/2026-11-23T170601Z_1582719674_RC2TTOAQZMB6_RTRMADP_3_NVIDIA-CEO.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer criticized the administration’s intervention in a post on X, accusing the president of prioritizing personal allies over national defense.

“Trump is putting his own financial interests and the needs of his billionaire buddies before our national security, emphasizing that he’s learned nothing from his resounding election defeat. Democrats will wield their full power to investigate any wrongdoing by the Trump Administration, Jensen Huang, other NVIDIA executives, and anyone selling out our national security for financial gain,” Schumer said.

Several Republican lawmakers also indicated that Huang’s reinstatement did not end congressional scrutiny.

“Nvidia’s decision today doesn’t excuse Jensen Huang from testifying before Congress,” Sen. Pete Ricketts of Nebraska said. “It also doesn’t absolve Nvidia of answering for national security concerns and unaccounted-for chips. I look forward to Mr. Huang coming to the Senate in December.”

Nvidia’s board has not publicly detailed what prompted its sudden reversal between last week and Sunday night.
