An 89-year-old pensioner drove at a man in Ripon, who was left clinging to the car bonnet as the vehicle sped towards a main road.

George Fisher ignored the victim’s pleas to stop and drove for “50-to-60 yards” with the man on the vehicle, York Crown Court heard.

When the car reached the junction of Wellington Street and King Street, Fisher drove straight over the junction onto the main road “without stopping”, said prosecutor Daniel Ingham.

He finally stopped the car when he reached a roundabout where the victim - whose ankles were said to be dragging on the road - managed to roll off the bonnet.

The victim escaped relatively unscathed and the only injury appeared to be bruising to his hip which was caused by his rolling off the bonnet onto the ground.

Fisher drove from the scene and headed up the A1 in a northbound direction towards his home in Blyth, Northumberland.

Mr Ingham said the incident unfolded on February 16, 2022, after the victim suspected that his own vehicle had been damaged by the pensioner.

He confronted Fisher about this and stood in the middle of the road to prevent him driving away.

“(Fisher) then pauses (his vehicle) in front of (the victim) before driving straight at him,” added Mr Ingham.

“It ended up with the (victim) on the bonnet of Mr Fisher’s car. He pleaded with him to stop, but the defendant kept going and came to the end of the street with a give-way junction and drove out into a main road without stopping until he reached a roundabout where (the victim) could roll off the vehicle.”

Fisher, of Newcastle Road, Blyth, was charged with dangerous driving and damaging the victim’s own vehicle but denied both allegations.

He was convicted of dangerous driving following a trial but was acquitted of the criminal- damage charge.

The victim said the incident had “shaken” him and left him with ongoing pain in his hip.

He said that as Fisher carried on driving despite his pleas to stop, his ankles were “catching on the road” and he feared he would go under the car.

“He says he feels exceptionally lucky to have escaped the incident in one piece,” added Mr Ingham.

He said that Mr Fisher, who had health issues, had no previous record for bad driving.

The pensioner who was wearing a hearing loop in the dock, chose to represent himself in court.

He said he “totally rejected” the prosecution’s account of events based on the victim’s evidence.

Fisher said that “the most delightful thing of my life” was driving out into the countryside, but “all that’s finished now” due to the inevitable driving ban.

He said he had lived a blameless life for 89 years and been driving for 71 years “without a single conviction or a single blameworthy accident”.

“I’m now a convicted criminal in my 90th year and I’ll go to my grave a convicted criminal,” he added.

Judge Sean Morris reminded the pensioner that he had been convicted by 12 jurors who were sure that the victim’s account of events was accurate.

He told Fisher: “It wasn’t a speedy affair, but it was a shockingly bad piece of driving and…one of the most telling things is that you deliberately chose not to go on the motorway (after driving from the scene) to go back north (because) you were staying away from the automatic-number-plate-recognition cameras.

“You know what you had done and you tried to hoodwink a jury. Twelve members of the public just did not accept your explanation.”

Fisher received a nine-month jail sentence, but this was suspended for a year.

The judge ordered him to pay £1,000 to the victim for the “fear you put him through” and gave the pensioner a 12-month driving ban.
