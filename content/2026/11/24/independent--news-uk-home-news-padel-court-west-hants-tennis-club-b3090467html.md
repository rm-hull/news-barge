Neighbours of a prestigious tennis club are fighting plans to install more padel courts after outrage at the amount of swearing coming from them.

Objectors claim they frequently hear foul-mouthed tirades that would make John McEnroe blush during games on the already existing padel courts at the West Hants Tennis Club.

The sweary language combined with the loud, continuous thwack of a ball hitting a hard bat is disturbing the tranquillity in the leafy Talbot Woods suburb of Bournemouth.

Padel, a cross between tennis and squash, is the world's fastest growing sport, with over 25 million players across the globe.

But some outdoor padel courts have triggered noise complaints because the racket is solid with no strings meaning it makes a loud noise when striking the ball.

West Hants Tennis Club, which was founded in the 1930s, has three padel courts alongside its indoor and outdoor tennis courts.

The £1,300 a year private club is seeking planning permission from BCP Council to install two more doubles-sized padel courts with flood lights to capitalise on the sport's popularity.

One of the courts will be just 30 yards away from luxury £1m homes on Dunkeld Road.

Local residents, some of whom are members of the club, fear they will only lead to an increase in noise pollution and more expletive-filled matches.

![Neighbours of a prestigious tennis club are fighting plans to install more padel courts after outrage at the amount of swearing coming from them](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/24/9/29/BNPS_WestHantsClubPadel_17.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Matt Gillett is a local councillor who has been contacted by several concerned neighbours over the proposals.

He said: "I have been talking to residents in Dunkeld Road and they need some mitigation in respect of the noise and overbearing light.

"They complain of shouting and often swearing when shots are missed and far more noise comes off the padel courts than the normal tennis courts, probably because there are many more players per square metre.

"This application will mean that the residents will be unable to enjoy their gardens."

Neighbours said it was not unusual to hear 'f-bombs' being dropped from the courts.

Retired IT specialist Alex Stevenson, 73, who has lived in the area for 36 years, said: "When we moved here it was a small tennis club and since then it has grown a lot.

"We want the club to continue to be profitable but padel courts need to be covered and not outdoors.

"The noise when the bat hits the ball is like the crack of the gun.

"It is a sociable sport which seems to attract younger people who are not worried about making noise.

"There has been no mitigation from the club whatsoever to tackle it.

"A lot of people around here are concerned about these proposals."

One neighbour, who wished to remain anonymous, said: "I believe the LTA (Lawn Tennis Association) recommends padel courts should be built no less than 30 metres from residents' homes but there is one woman whose garden is 18 metres away and she can't use it because of the noise.

"It's had a big impact on her standard of life.

"It's not unusual to hear swearing including the 'F-word' from players frustrated at missing shots."

One member of the club who has submitted a letter of objection said the existing padel courts are already 'intrusive'.

Bronwen Dean said: "I am a member of the West Hants Club and I feel that this application is excessive.

"The existing padel courts are intrusive and additional courts, especially floodlit will increase this burden.

"The club is over-subscribed but I feel that expansion will take from the residents and have a negative affect on the neighbourhood."

![Objectors claim they frequently hear foul-mouthed tirades that would make John McEnroe blush during games on the already existing padel courts at the West Hants Tennis Club](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/24/9/56/BNPS_WestHantsClubPadel_13.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The local neighbourhood residents' association has also opposed the plans, with the noise from the padel courts one of the major concerns.

Stephen Chappell, chairman of Talbot and Branksome Woods Residents Association, said: "We think that to those residents trying to enjoy a quiet relaxing afternoon in the privacy of their back gardens, the persistent cracking noise of ball on padel bat from multiple padel courts will have a very definite adverse impact.

"We cannot see any assessment has been made of the fact that the proposed enlarged seating area will produce greater audience participation during padel competitions – a participation which is already clearly heard by some Dunkeld Road residents in their gardens."

The proposal states that the padel courts will be enclosed by 13ft high screens, with playing hours restricted to 8am to 9pm on weekdays and 8am to 8pm at weekends and on Bank Holidays.

The plans, if approved, will also see the club's gym extended and a new outdoor patio area built.

A Design and Access Statement prepared on behalf of the club states: "Padel is one of the fastest-growing sports at the club.

"The two additional doubles courts would take the total to five, and the enlarged outside area would provide viewing of the four padel courts close to the clubhouse, with more space for families and friends to sit, relax and spend time together.

"It would not result in unacceptable noise, lighting, outlook or privacy effects for neighbouring occupiers."

West Hants Tennis Club has been approached for comment over the complaints about swearing.
