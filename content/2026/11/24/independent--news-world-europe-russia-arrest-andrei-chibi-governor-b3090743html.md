Russian officers have detained one of the country’s regional governors as part of a criminal investigation into fraud and embezzlement on a “particularly large” scale.

The Interior Ministry said on Monday that Andrei Chibis, the governor of the northern Murmansk region in the Arctic Circle, was arrested on suspicion of involvement in a group crime case.

The case includes allegations ‌of embezzling more than 39 million roubles (approximately £362,631) in budget funds from ‌the regional construction ‌ministry.

Investigators were also looking into the alleged misappropriation in the region, a strategic military point home to the Russian navy’s Northern Fleet, of more than one billion roubles earmarked ‌for reconstructing a highway ​linking the towns of ‌Apatity and ⁠Kirovsk, funds allocated through the ⁠regional transport ministry.

![In this pool photograph distributed by the Russian state agency Sputnik, Murmansk region Governor Andrei Chibis speaks with Russia's President during a meeting in Moscow on August 6, 2024](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/24/15/2165039706..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Interior Ministry spokesperson Irina VolkVok said that investigators were also probing suspected illegal currency transactions.

Local journalists were unable to contact Mr Chibis for comment.

According to the BBC, his wife Yevgenia Chibis said “anyone who knows our family understands how ridiculous this is”.

On social media, she said her husband sincerely believed in the Murmansk region and welcomed comments of support – adding: “We really need them right now”.

![Mr Chibis was re-elected as governor two years ago](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/24/15/2165039915..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Mr Chibis worked as deputy minister for construction and housing before becoming governor in 2019. The 47-year-old was re-elected two years ago after surviving a knife attack and is deemed an active supporter of Russia’s war against Ukraine.

Governors in Russia function mainly as executives aligned with and accountable to the Kremlin rather than independent representatives of local interests.

Mr Chibis, who has been sanctioned by the UK and EU over accusations of illegal transportation of Ukrainian children to Russia and their adoption by Russian families, is the first local governor to be detained since Russia’s full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022.

However, during this time, many high-ranking officials have been arrested, including former Deputy Minister of Defence Timur Ivanov, who was sentenced to 13 years in prison in 2025 over accusations of accepting large bribes and embezzlement.

According to local news, a court will now decide whether Mr Chibis will remain in custody at a hearing on Wednesday.
