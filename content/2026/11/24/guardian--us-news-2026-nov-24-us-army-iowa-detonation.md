The US army has released new maps showing the projected path of a toxic lead dust plume from the planned detonation of 68 barrels – five tons – of lead azide at an Iowa ammunition dump.

That detonation has been scheduled for 10am ET on Wednesday.

Projections show a plume moving south-west of the Iowa army ammunition plant in Middletown, stretching through a south-eastern portion of the state before extending into northern Missouri.

The army sent an emergency notification to cellphones on Tuesday confirming the time of the controlled explosion – and warning that some people within 5 miles south-west of the plant may be evacuated.

The “imminent threat alert” told those up to 20 miles away to remain indoors with windows closed “to avoid airborne lead dust, noise and vibration”.

The army said it had set up additional “blast barriers” around the bunkers where the lead azide will be detonated. It also said it would apply ballistic gels and foams to absorb the lead dust and douse the explosive materials with thousands of gallons of water.

Adam Telle, acting secretary of the army, said in a post on X that there was no reason for the public to panic. He said it was “unanimous judgment” of various government entities that the army “should proceed with an extensively mitigated, carefully controlled detonation”.

“We are proceeding with the controlled removal in full coordination with all state, local and federal authorities, because the safety of Iowa’s citizens comes first,” Telle said.

The decision came after a federal appeals court cleared the way for the detonation to take place after the date had been changed three times after an outpouring of public concern. Environmentalists sued to stop the detonation, arguing that the planned explosion had not been issued permits correctly.

An attorney for the environmentalists emphasized that their legal challenge centers on the validity of the Environment Protection Agency permit – not the army’s decision to move forward with the detonation under the emergency provision.

The army said it needs to blow up the stockpile to prevent a possible chain-reaction explosion, which would be more dangerous.

“We don’t believe that the public should be in a panic,” Telle said. “Our goal is to keep as much of it as we can here on our Iowa army ammunition plant to prevent it from traveling.”

The order to go ahead came after an extraordinary intervention by Donald Trump, who said on social media that in addition to the court’s go-ahead, he wanted Rob Sand, Iowa’s governor-elect, to approve the detonation.

Sand, a Democrat who does not take office until January, has said the decision is fully up to the president’s administration.

Outgoing governor Kim Reynolds said that after speaking with military leaders, including US defense secretary Pete Hegseth, she supports the planned detonation.

“I trust the army will do everything in its power to protect the people who could be affected,” Reynolds said.

Evacuated residents will be notified by phone once they may return home, the Des Moines Register reported.

The army and the Iowa department of health and human services are recommending that those in the area take protective measures. Those include closing all windows and exterior doors; turning off or placing heating, ventilation, and air conditioning systems in recirculation mode; avoiding unnecessary travel; and cleaning indoor and outdoor surfaces that may be contacted by children.
