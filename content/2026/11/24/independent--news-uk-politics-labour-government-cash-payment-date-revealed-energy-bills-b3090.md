More than 20 million households across the UK will begin receiving a £100 cash payment from January 4 to help with the rising cost of energy bills, ministers have announced.

The measure, unveiled at the autumn Budget, gives an automatic £100 rebate to properties in council tax bands A to D.

But with a typical household set to pay £62 a week for energy from January, the money will only cover the first 11 days of the new year, researchers have warned.

![More than 20 million household in council tax bands A-D to get the payment into their bank accounts next year](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/18/13/18125259-b75d19bd-7da1-486e-b2c6-2c47aee9f830.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Councils will begin making payments from 4 January, the Treasury said as it set out more details of how the rebate will work.

A total of 13 million households who pay by direct debit will automatically receive the payment directly into their bank accounts, with no action required.

Those who pay in other ways will be contacted and given the choice of receiving the £100 into their bank account or as credit against their council tax bill.

In a bid to ensure no-one loses out, the scheme will remain open until the end of May.

Chancellor John Healey said: “Families are worried about energy bills rising again this winter.”

![Chancellor John Healey said he was providing ‘breathing space’ to households](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/09/00/aaef79b54adbe6e0a653f503d386c215Y29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzk0MDcwODgw-2.86329203.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

In what appeared to be a reference to Donald Trump’s war in Iran, he added: “Whilst we can’t control movements in global energy markets, we’re doing what we can to protect household finances and give families some breathing space.

“That’s why 20 million households will receive £100 from January. Today we’re setting out how money will be paid to families straight into their bank accounts.”

Ofgem’s energy price cap is set to rise by £443 in the new year, forecaster Cornwall Insight has predicted, taking the average bill from £1,723 to £2,166 – a 26 per cent increase.

This would mark the highest level since 2023.

The bleak outlook for bills comes after escalating violence in the Middle East sparked a fresh jump in oil and gas prices last month.

Energy secretary Miatta Fahnbulleh said: “Too many families up and down the country are worried about the costs of putting on the heating this winter.

“That’s why we’re giving millions of people breathing space with their energy bills, and supporting councils to act quickly to ensure everyone eligible gets the support they are entitled to.

“We will continue to do everything we can to support people and bring bills down for good, by breaking the link with fossil fuels and delivering more clean, homegrown power.”
