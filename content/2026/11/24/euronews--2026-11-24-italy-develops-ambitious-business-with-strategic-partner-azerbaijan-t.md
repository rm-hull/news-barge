## Italy's Deputy Prime Minister and Foreign Minister Antonio Tajani told Euronews that Rome has sought to expand its business relations with Baku now that peace has brought stability and improved connectivity to the region.

Italy has sought to invest in the emerging Middle Corridor transit route connecting Asia to Europe through the South Caucasus and boost its business volumes with its "strategic partner" Azerbaijan, Italian Foreign Minister Antonio Tajani told Euronews in Baku on Monday.

With Italy as the top EU importer of energy from Azerbaijan and one of its largest commercial partners, Tajani and Azerbaijan's Economy Minister Mikayil Jabbarov led an Azerbaijan-Italy Business Forum that brought together business leaders from both countries, with the two countries sharing a "strong relationship built on trust and common interests."

During a Euronews interview at the forum, the Italian foreign minister, who is also deputy prime minister, told Euronews that "we must be ambitious," describing the forum's objective as "a leap in quality" and calling for a stronger economic partnership between Italy and Azerbaijan.

"Azerbaijan is at the heart of a region that is increasingly important to our foreign policy," Tajani said in a statement, adding that he "reaffirmed Italy’s full support for the peace process between Azerbaijan and Armenia, which is essential to the region’s stability and growth."

Tajani announced that Italian exports to Azerbaijan increased by 14% in the first 8 months of this year.

Tajani described Italy's strong interest in Azerbaijan’s role in the Middle Corridor transit project, saying Italian companies can bring their engineering expertise and technology, together with AI, machinery, agri-food and railways.

"We devoted considerable attention to connectivity and the diversification of energy and logistics corridors: from the Middle Corridor to the Green Energy Corridor, which could bring energy to Europe and is already attracting strong interest from our Italian companies," Tajani said.

"We are working together in a spirit of friendship towards a common goal: strengthening links between Europe and Asia and creating new opportunities for growth for our countries," the Italian foreign minister added.

The Middle Corridor is an emerging strategic transit and trade route between Asia and Europe and a strong Eurasian alternative amid global supply chain disruptions in the Middle East.

EU countries are actively exploring new investment opportunities along the Middle Corridor, a rail and maritime transport network that will connect China, Southeast Asia, and Central Asia to European markets via the Caspian Sea and the South Caucasus, then to the Black Sea and onward to the Mediterranean.

Tajani also met with Azerbaijan’s President Ilham Aliyev and shared on X that he was on a “mission” to Azerbaijan, describing the country as “a strategic partner of Italy with which we share a strong relationship built on trust and common interests.”

Italian and Azerbaijani officials and business delegations discussed investments and cooperation in manufacturing, infrastructure, transport, technology, and critical raw materials.

Tajani said the two countries should work together on critical raw materials, including in Africa and other parts of the world, after signing a Joint Declaration on Strategic Cooperation in Critical Raw Materials in Rome in October.

"If you want to compete at a global level", Tajani told Euronews, "you need to reduce the price of raw materials for example."

"It's also possible to invest in Italy for companies coming from Azerbaijan, but also for Italian companies to export to Azerbaijan", he said.

Tajani also reflected on the role peace might play in the future of business, saying "peace is a good choice for paving the way for new investments."

"I want to thank Azerbaijan for its engagement in favour of peace, not only with Armenia but also in this part of the world, during the difficult situation", he said.

"We need countries working for peace," the Italian foreign minister added.

## **Economic diversification and development**

Azerbaijan’s Jabbarov told Euronews that the two countries signed several agreements during the forum covering AI, industrial production, construction, public transport and investment cooperation.

As both countries aim to increase Azerbaijan's gas supplies to Italy, Jabbarov said economic diversification and business developments in the non-oil sector were also a focus of the talks, including industrial parks and free economic zones, adding that “cooperation with Italian companies opens up very good opportunities for our companies.”

Jabbarov told Euronews that Azerbaijan wanted to improve the competitiveness of locally produced goods and increase their access to foreign markets.

"The initiatives are aimed at modernising industrial enterprises, expanding production capacity, facilitating access to international markets for industrial products, accelerating the adoption of digital technologies and strengthening business and investment ties," Azerbaijan's minister of economy said.

Italy’s Leonardo, a major aerospace, defence and security company, and Azerbaijan’s Ministry of Economy signed a memorandum on cooperation in high-performance computing systems and artificial intelligence.

Italy’s development finance institution, Cassa Depositi e Prestiti (CDP), signed an agreement with Azerbaijan’s Business Development Fund, and its Head of International Markets Development Laurent Franciosi described the platform as being "capable of connecting the different dimensions of the bilateral economic relationship between the two countries.”

Speaking about the agreement with Azerbaijan’s Business Development Fund, Franciosi said: “We don’t want it to be just an institutional framework.”

## \*\* Role of small and medium-sized businesses \*\*

Vice President of Confindustria’s Small Industry section Fausto Mazzali led the talks on cooperation among smaller businesses, telling Euronews that “for the Italian industry, Azerbaijan does not just want to be a market for development, but a country with which to build new investment opportunities and collaboration.”

Mazzali highlighted potential cooperation in infrastructure, transport and logistics, critical materials, metallurgy, automation, robotics, electronics, pharmaceuticals, and agricultural machinery, and said Italian companies could contribute through exports, but also by sharing technology, expertise, and industrial solutions.

Head of Foreign Relations at the Italian Union of Chambers of Commerce Pietro Infante said that he would like to propose "a change of paradigm,” calling for businesses to move “beyond the traditional commercial partnerships” and the “logics of pure import and export”, towards joint ventures, industrial partnerships and technology development.

Società Italiana per le Imprese all'Estero (SIMEST), majority-owned by CDP, also discussed support for Italian companies seeking to expand abroad.

Its Chief External Relations Officer Francesca Alicata outlined the institution’s role in helping businesses enter new markets, saying their mission was to support the companies, and in particular, " small and medium-sized companies.”

Alicata explained that SIMEST provided financial instruments to support international expansion, including financing, grants and support for joint ventures.

She also described supplier credit, which allows Italian companies to offer overseas customers extended payment terms for machinery and industrial equipment.

SIMEST signed an agreement with the Italy-Azerbaijan Chamber of Commerce and the Small and Medium Business Development Agency of Azerbaijan; Italy’s Investment and Export Promotion Agency and the Italian Trade Agency signed a memorandum to support trade and investment links.

Attracting investment into Italy was another subject covered at the forum.

Augusto Reggiani, Director of the Foreign Investments Attraction Unit at Italy’s Ministry of Enterprises and Made in Italy, explained the support available to international investors.

Meanwhile, Baku Steel Company signed an agreement with Italian companies Dismas Trading and Edgar H. Greenham to promote exports of locally manufactured industrial products.

The Azerbaijan Construction Products Manufacturers Association and Italy’s FINCO agreed to develop cooperation in construction and building materials.

Iveco and AZ Tex Import signed an agreement in the public transport sector.

Another contract, between North West Construction and Italy’s Falorni Tech, concerned the modernisation of the Garadagh Glass Plant.

The business forum follows Italian Premier Giorgia Meloni's talks with President Aliyev in May, focusing on expanding Azerbaijan's oil and gas supplies to Italy, which Meloni said had been "decisive for the energy security" of Italy since Russia launched its full-scale invasion of Ukraine in early 2022.

At the time, Meloni said she wanted Italy to serve as Azerbaijan's "privileged gateway to the European market" as a hub between Europe and Asia.

Bilateral trade reached €10.2 billion last year, making Italy Azerbaijan's largest commercial partner, while Azerbaijan has invested around €3 billion in Italy.
