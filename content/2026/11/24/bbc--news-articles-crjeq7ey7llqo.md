**A former police chief inspector who once worked with convicted murderer Wayne Couzens has been sentenced to 10-and-a-half years in prison for two counts of rape and one of sexual assault.**

Glen Mahony, 54, who was convicted at Maidstone Crown Court in September following a 10-day trial, carried out two attacks while his victim was asleep.

Mahony worked in the Civil Nuclear Constabulary (CNC) and previously worked with Couzens, who is serving a whole life tariff for the rape and murder of Sarah Everard in 2021.

In his sentencing remarks on Tuesday, Judge Gareth Branston dismissed Mahony's claims that he was targeted because he had once managed Couzens.

"You were not targeted. You were arrested because of what you did," he said.

He described Mahony's behaviour as "appalling", saying it had left his victim, who was known to him, suffering panic attacks and unable to work.

The judge said that when arrested in March 2022, Mahony told Kent Police officers that he had been Wayne Couzens's overall manager, though not his line manager.

The CNC, which guards the UK's nuclear facilities, is the force where Couzens served for six years before joining the Metropolitan Police.

Mahony, who was an inspector in charge of day-to-day police operations at the Dungeness power station before being promoted, was his commanding officer for some of that time.

Mahony was suspended on full pay by the CNC in March 2022 and resigned the day before a disciplinary hearing in January of this year.

The CNC said he would have been dismissed for gross misconduct if the disciplinary process had been carried out.

In an impact statement read to the court, his victim said she was made to feel worthless, and that "it was all about control for him".

"His actions didn't just hurt me at the time but have continued to do so each day. He has stolen my confidence," she said, having described ongoing nightmares and mental health struggles.

Det Ch Insp Tracy Evans, of Kent Police, said: "To take advantage of a woman while she is asleep and at her most vulnerable is utterly disgraceful and to then deny it happened and force the victim to relive her ordeal during a trial makes things even worse."

She also praised the "tremendous courage" of the victim in coming forward to report Mahony.

CNC Chief Constable Simon Chesterman said his immediate thoughts were with Mahony's victim, who he also commended for her bravery.

"Former officer Glen Mahony's crimes were despicable, committing the most serious sexual violence to a vulnerable female victim, who was not able to consent," he said.

"Mahony's behaviours are entirely contrary to the high standards required of a police officer with the CNC. And his actions go on to undermine the trust and confidence that the public, and particularly women, have in the police service."

Two WhatsApp groups to which Mahony belonged were also investigated by CNC professional standards.

"In these groups, Mahony sent sexist and misogynistic messages, and he did not challenge or leave the group when others sent similarly offensive messages," Chief Constable Chesterman said.

"This further underlines his disgusting opinion of women."

Mahony was promoted to chief inspector for the south division in 2020. It meant he was number three in the division's chain of command for the four nuclear sites in the south - Dungeness, Sizewell and Hinkley Point power stations, and the nuclear research centre at Harwell, Oxfordshire.

He spent most of his working life as an armed officer based at the Dungeness nuclear plant in Kent.

He joined the force in 2006, becoming a sergeant in 2011 before being promoted to inspector in 2016. This put him in operational charge of security at the site. He would have worked with Couzens, who was a constable at Dungeness between 2012 and 2018.

Couzens was also in a separate WhatsApp group with six other officers who transferred to the Met from the CNC. The messages they shared were found to be sexist, misogynistic, racist and violent, raising questions about the culture among officers at both the CNC and the Met.
