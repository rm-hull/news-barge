## Two men were arrested on the Sandringham estate on suspicion of burglary and trespass and remain in custody, Norfolk Police said. The force added that the property was unoccupied at the time of the incident.

Two men have been arrested on suspicion of burglary after a reported break-in at the Prince and Princess of Wales' country home on the Sandringham estate in the UK.

Police were called out shortly before 6 pm on Monday following a report of "suspicious activity at the address," Norfolk Police said in a statement to Euronews.

British media outlets are reporting that the property was Anmer Hall, which Prince William, his wife Catherine and their three children occasionally use as a retreat in the holidays. Their main residence is Forest Lodge in Windsor.

"Officers attended and specialist resources, including police dogs, drones and the National Police Air Service helicopter, were deployed to assist with searches in the area," police said.

The force added that two men were arrested on the estate on suspicion of burglary and trespass and remain in custody.

The property was said to have been unoccupied at the time of the incident. Inquiries are ongoing.

News of the break-in came as William travelled to Westminster on Tuesday to meet with British Prime Minister Andy Burnham in Downing Street - marking his first ever visit to the PM's residence.

The Sandringham estate, located in Norfolk in the East of England, is the private country retreat of King Charles III and Queen Camilla.

Sandringham House, the Jacobean-style jewel of the estate, has been open to the public since 1977. The house and gardens are open from 28 March to 9 October each year.
