The goal is to better coordinate the development and deployment of autonomous vehicles in Europe and to support the transition from testing to regular traffic.

Illustrative image

Berlin is joining a European initiative for autonomous mobility. The initiative stems from an action plan for the automotive industry issued by the European Commission, according to the Senate Department of Transportation.

A key component is closer coordination of approval and licensing procedures. A coordinated approach is intended to create a more reliable regulatory framework. In addition, the participants are working on the practical implementation of autonomous transportation services for both passenger and freight transport. "Autonomous vehicles don’t stop at national borders, but their registration requirements do - at least so far," said Transportation Senator Ute Bonde (CDU), according to the press release. "Common rules are now being developed in the European test bed, and Berlin wants to help shape them."

The European exchange is intended to help transfer proven solutions more quickly and develop sustainable offerings. At the same time, it would create new opportunities for collaboration among Berlin-based companies and research institutions. In addition to Germany, numerous other European countries are participating in the initiative. Just last week, the U.S. electric car manufacturer Tesla announced plans to produce so-called "cybercabs" at its plant in Grünheide near Berlin within a few years. Around three billion euros are to be invested in the construction of a new production facility for this purpose. It is unclear when these driverless robotaxis might be on German roads.
