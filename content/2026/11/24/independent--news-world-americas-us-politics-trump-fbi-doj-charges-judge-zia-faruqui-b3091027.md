A federal judge lambasted Donald Trump’s administration for a series of “botched” politically motivated prosecutions and “slapdash” charges targeting the president’s critics and allies that have fallen apart in court.

In a damning 48-page assessment of the Justice Department’s attempts to prosecute Trump’s perceived enemies, U.S. District Judge Zia Faruqui said the administration has “treated the weaponization of law enforcement like a horror movie villain coming for them.”

“Now the threat is finally real. Only the call is coming from inside the house — the White House,” she wrote. “Perhaps, less time on the pool and more on reflecting.”

Faruqui delivered his rebuke in a written order that tossed out a case against two former FBI officials who briefed Congress about the bureau’s involvement with investigations into Hunter Biden.

Nikki Floris and Bradley Benavides, two former intelligence specialists, were charged with one count each of making false statements to Congress. The charges, which were announced by FBI Director Kash Patel last month, were not brought before a grand jury.

![A federal judge lambasted the Trump administration for a series of ‘botched, politicized cases where the government has rushed to charge cases, only to have them fall apart’](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/24/21/2306565916..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Faruqui’s ruling follows a years-long campaign from the president and his allies who have alleged a so-called “deep state” plot to suppress allegations of corruption involving former President Joe Biden and his family.

The case against Floris and Benavides — a rare attempt from the Justice Department to prosecute former employees — stemmed from an investigation into the now-defunct FBI Foreign Influence Task Force, which sought to prevent foreign actors from unlawfully influencing 2020 elections. Floris had previously faced scrutiny from Republican members of Congress and other Trump allies over recently declassified emails in which she discussed her intelligence work.

Such prosecutions “can pose a risk of abuse and injustice,” as they can apply to “virtually any statement an individual makes to virtually any federal government official — even when the individual making the statement is not under oath,” the judge noted.

“This risk is most acute when the government weaponizes [such prosecutions] against its perceived enemies,” he said. “This risk is not theoretical. It is an unprecedented reality now before this Court.”

The government “is not free to use this weapon unchecked” and “the rule of law is still a shield against it,” he wrote.

After failing to obtain a grand jury indictment, the government then failed to establish probable cause in a preliminary hearing, according to Faruqui.

“This case is not a one-off mistake,” he wrote. “This court has seen a line of botched, politicized cases where the government has rushed to charge cases, only to have them fall apart.”

Prosecutors may still try to seek a grand jury indictment, but Faruqui said that the government is “afraid” of trying. noting that grand juries have “refused to indict more cases in the last year than in the prior 10 years combined.”

![The Justice Department, where massive images of Trump are on display, ‘has demonstrated a lack of independence and impartiality from the political desires of the administration,’ a federal judge said November 24](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/24/21/2306157430..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The Trump administration has seen an unprecedented streak of losses and prosecutorial failures over the last two years, including an eight-week period in which the Justice Department moved to dismiss 21 percent of all cases brought during a surge of federal troops into the nation’s capital last year.

The government’s failed case against two former FBI officials is a “betrayal” of the “independence and impartiality" that the Justice Department lists among its values, according to Faruqui.

“The slapdash prosecution of Ms. Floris and Mr. Benavides in response to a public command from the President demonstrates a lack of independence and impartiality from the political desires of the Administration,” he added.

“Moreover, the rampant errors and misstatements stemming from the slapdash nature of this investigation and prosecution further undercuts public confidence in the work of the Department of Justice,” Faruqui said. “If a high-profile case like this is so poorly executed, what can the public expect in the run-of-the-mill case with far less resources and oversight.”

The subsequent erosion of public confidence in the Justice Department “further harms public safety,” Faruqui wrote.

The judge “confirmed what we said from the start,” according to Nikki Floris’ legal team, which included Mary Dohrmann, Kyle R. Freeny, and Kirby Mayo of Washington Litigation Group and Margaret Donovan and Chris Mattei of Koskoff Koskoff & Bieder.

“This was a rushed, error-ridden, and politically motivated prosecution that never should have been brought,” they said in a statement shared with *The Independent.*

“While Nikki should never have had to endure this disgraceful public attack, we hope the courts continue to be a refuge for all citizens unlawfully targeted by the Trump administration,” they added.
