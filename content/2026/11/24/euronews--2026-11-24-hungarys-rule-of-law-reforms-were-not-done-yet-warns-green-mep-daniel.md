German Green MEP Daniel Freund has warned against ending the EU's rule-of-law procedure against Hungary too soon. He said that while Péter Magyar’s government has made significant progress since taking power in April, it still needs to comply with key EU court rulings.

"We're not done yet," Freund told Euronews ahead of Tuesday's European Parliament debate on the restoration of the rule of law in Hungary.

Freund welcomed the pace of Magyar's reforms, but pointed to Poland as a warning against easing EU pressure before changes take full effect. There, "the [European Union] sanctions mechanisms were stopped, and the money was unfrozen very quickly," yet Hungary continues to flout the rule of law, according to the MEP.

Elections in April resulted in a victory for Magyar's Tisza party, ending 16 years of rule by Viktor Orbán. Orbán's Fidesz party has faced multiple charges of corruption since first taking power in 2010. During its time in office, Fidesz channeled funds and state contracts to business people close to the party. The party also engaged in a spending spree during Orbán's final months in office, ballooning Hungary's budget deficit. In response to risks to EU values, the European Parliament triggered Article 7 proceedings against Hungary in 2018.

On 20 November, the European Commission stated that Hungary had met 126 out of 127 milestones under its request to unblock nearly €10 billion in frozen recovery assets. A separate €4.2 billion in cohesion funding was unlocked in October. The Article 7 procedure is a separate process.

Freund told Euronews that Article 7 should not be closed until "all major problems are addressed," including Hungary's continued failure to comply with an EU asylum judgment. In 2024, the EU’s top court ordered Hungary to pay a €200 million fine plus €1 million per day of delay for failing to comply with an earlier ruling.

Freund added that these remaining issues could still be "wrapped up in a matter of months."
