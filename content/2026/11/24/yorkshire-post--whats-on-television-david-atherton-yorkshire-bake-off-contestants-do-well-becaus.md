# David Atherton: ‘Yorkshire Bake Off contestants do well because we don’t take ourselves too seriously’

## The Great British Bake Off winner David Atherton has told The Yorkshire Post he believes the reason why Yorkshire bakers do well on the show is because they don’t ‘take themselves too seriously’.

David Atherton, from Whitby, competed in the Great British Bake Off in 2019 and went on to win the series. He has since worked on various projects relating to baking on top of his main job as an NHS practice nurse.

Over the last seven years he has been invited to various events, from food festivals to the more recent A Dickens Christmas at Castle Howard, and he has achieved his dream of writing and publishing five children’s cookbooks.

The 40-year-old has also been collaborating with another former Bake Off contestant Michael Chakraverty on their podcast Sticky Bun Boys since 2022.

![David Atherton with one of his kids' cookbooks.](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/jpim-static/image/2026/11/24/11/45/NLYP-23-11-2026-David-Atherton-with-one-of-his-kids-cookbooks-YORupload.jpg?trim=55,55,184,54&width=640&quality=65)


Alongside his baking endeavours, David works three days a week as a nurse.

When discussing why Yorkshire contestants often get far in the Great British Bake Off, he believes it’s in their approach to baking and being on the show.

“I think it's because we don't take ourselves too seriously and we don't have any high opinions of ourselves,” David said.

“The most important thing in Bake Off is staying calm and just plowing on through if things are going wrong.

“It’s not always going to go correctly and you cannot be a perfectionist. If you look at Tom this year, he's got a dry sense of humour.

“When things are going wrong, he's got a brilliant attitude to getting on with it and I think that's probably why Yorkshire people do so well.”

Both David and Michael were on the same series of the Bake Off and they have been rewatching previous eras as well as the current series and reacting to them on their weekly podcast.

They have aired more than 220 episodes so far.

“It’s called the Sticky Bun Boys because it's myself and Michael who was on my year of Bake Off as well,” he said.

“It’s been quite interesting re-watching old series because obviously at the time you're a viewer of The Great British Bake Off and when you watch it a second time, having been on the show, you see a lot more things.

![Bake Off 2019 contestants.](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/jpim-static/image/2026/11/24/11/59/NLYP-23-11-2026-Bake-Off-2019-contestants-YORupload.jpg?trim=84,57,159,58&width=640&quality=65)


“You start looking at some of the more structural things and the production side of things. You also look for those times where it might be the edit is slightly different and things are done in the wrong time.

“It's definitely very interesting watching old series back and it's quite interesting to reflect on your own performance if you look back at how you were back then.”

The final of the Great British Bake Off this year will air tonight (November 24) at 8pm with 25-year-old hairdresser Tom, from West Yorkshire, among the finalists.

This year’s judge Nigella Lawson has proven to be popular with the audience and David.

“I love Nigella. I think she's brilliant and she's really giving Paul Hollywood a run for his money and it's just so nice to see,” he said.

“I think Tom has a very good chance of winning; we could be seeing another Yorkshire winner but it seemed like a really fun group this year.

“I've really enjoyed it. It seems like the production team are playing around with the formats of the technicals, which we didn't have for our series.”

Outside of the show, David said it was an ‘absolute dream’ to write children’s cookbooks.

“I'm just very lucky that they're still selling now as well,” he said.

“There's a certain romance to people writing a book or opening a cafe and writing the books ticked all the boxes.

![David Atherton.](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/jpim-static/image/2026/11/24/11/55/NLYP-23-11-2026-David-Atherton-YORupload.jpg?trim=70,53,170,58&width=640&quality=65)


“Writing a book isn't one person writing a book; there's a huge team of people and the publisher was great.

“One of my best friends, who is the illustrator in my first kids book, got the deal; she was going to the publisher to talk about doing another book and she mentioned that I was on Bake Off.”

David also has a piece of advice for aspiring bakers wanting to compete on the show in future.

“I think after the show my biggest bit of advice is not to get into your head because one of the things that happens is everyone comes out to the show at the same time,” he said.

“Just because you were the winner doesn't mean you're the most popular or that brands will want to work with you necessarily.

“I think sometimes it gets a bit competitive between the bakers because they see other people getting certain jobs and they don't get it, but the thing is there's lots of jobs out there.

“There's lots of reasons why people get asked to do certain things.

“So I would always say try not to spiral in your own brain with those things and just stay positive.

“I think a lot of people expect things to come to them but the reality is the people that have done well after the show in a commercial sense are the people who have hustled and gone after it,” he said.

“For next year I would always say just enjoy yourself in the tent because like I say it's not about winning anyway it's about showing people fall in love with you on the show because you're a lovable character.”

David encourages people who want to apply to do so without worrying about getting their bakes perfect.

“I have a lot of people who come up to me and say ‘I've applied lots of times for Bake Off, can you show me what I’m doing wrong? I keep on practising more and more’,” he said.

“Then I look at their bakes and they're too good; it's an amateur baking show and the TV production company wants good bakers but they don't want perfect bakers.

“So don't worry about practising every single bake, what they want to see is your personality; everyone has different personalities, it's not about being a loud personality it's just about letting yours show through.

“Your bakes are in some way a secondary thing. If you've got through the first round then they already know you can bake.”
