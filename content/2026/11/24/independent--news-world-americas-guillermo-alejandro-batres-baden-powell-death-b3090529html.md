A hiker fell 500 feet to his death on an icy California mountain while trying to help his son, his family says.

​Guillermo Alejandro Batres died on November 22 while hiking to the summit of the 9,400 foot Mount Baden-Powell in Los Angeles County.

​Batres’ son slipped and fell during the climb, prompting the father to try to help him, the late hiker’s niece Jennifer Morse wrote in a GoFundMe campaign.

​“As Guillermo tried to help him up, he slipped on ice and fell approximately 500 feet down a steep gulch,” Morse explained. “Guillermo died in the fall, leaving his family shocked, grieving, and facing an unimaginable loss.”

Morse launched the campaign to help her family pay for funeral costs, with the fundraiser already receiving over $24,000 in donations toward its $40,000 goal.

![Batres’ family said they were facing an “unimaginable loss”](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/24/11/31/Screenshot-2026-11-24-at-11-02-18.png?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

​Preliminary information obtained by local outlet AV Scanner News stated that the fall unfolded three miles from Vincent Gap, a route at the base of Mount Baden-Powell.

​The incident was reported at around 10 a.m., with officials from the Los Angeles County Fire Department and the Los Angeles County Sheriff’s Department’s Palmdale Station responding to Batres’ death, the outlet reports. An air rescue team was requested to retrieve his body.

​Batres shared his love of the outdoors on social media, often posting on Instagram about ultrarunning - a sport involving foot races exceeding the length of a standard marathon. ​The late father was certified as an ultrarunning coach back in July 2025.

​In a post uploaded in July, Batres can be seen posing with an American flag and a sign reading “Mt. Baden-Powell.”

![The 9,400 foot Mount Baden-Powell is a popular spot for hikers](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/24/11/59/Screenshot-2026-11-24-at-11-07-24.png?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&trim=55%2C0%2C39%2C3&auto=webp)

​“I love our mountains!” he wrote. “Everytime I go out for (sic) new expedition a new spot I can feel fear and joy it’s a weird mix of feelings I can’t describe it.

​“What I know is, this bring me peace and happiness. Let’s go outside.”

​Mount Baden-Powell is located in the San Gabriel Mountains, which often become covered in snow and ice during the winter. Speaking to *Outside*, hiking expert Cris Hazzard explained that hikers need to take precautions if they try to scale the mountain.

​“On this mountain, there’s no room for error if you slip when it’s snowy or icy out,” he said. “If you slip in the summer, you probably just fall on your butt, maybe go down some gravel.

​“You slip in the winter, you’re going down hundreds, if not thousands, of feet.”
