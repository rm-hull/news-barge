## The neighbourhoods attracting the most anti-social behaviour complaints

North Yorkshire Police officers during a recent operation. Photo: NYP.

THE parts of Scarborough experiencing the highest levels of anti-social behaviour have been identified in a new council report amid growing concerns about disorder in the town centre and areas with HMOs.

Anti-social behaviour (ASB) remains a major concern across the town, with Castle ward and North Bay recording the highest levels of reported anti-social behaviour.

Public concern has also increased in areas with high concentrations of houses in multiple occupation (HMOs). However, a new report stressed that homelessness and anti-social behaviour were not always linked.

Speaking in Parliament earlier this month, Alison Hume, Labour MP for Scarborough and Whitby, said some people living close to temporary accommodation sites had been left feeling “anxiety, frustration and fear”.

She said the problems were increasingly concentrated around a small number of town-centre properties used for emergency temporary accommodation.

The MP told ministers: “To be clear, I do not oppose emergency accommodation being located in Scarborough, but we are seeing an over-concentration in one town out of a large county.”

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21705210.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Alison Hume MP, Westminster Hall Debate On ASB. 10.11.26

During the second quarter of the year, nuisance behaviour remained the most commonly reported form of anti-social behaviour.

Overall reports increased compared with the previous quarter, but incidents fell steadily through the summer, making September the quietest month of the year so far.

Among the issues highlighted by officers were alcohol-related disorder in the town centre, anti-social behaviour involving children and young people during the school holidays and ongoing problems in known hotspot locations.

The council’s Community Safety Hub received 81 new referrals between July and September, with 58 identifying anti-social behaviour as the main issue.

Mental health concerns were identified in 21 cases, while alcohol or substance misuse featured in 18.

The report also details enforcement action taken during the three months, including 12 Community Protection Warnings, five Community Protection Notices and two closure orders obtained by North Yorkshire Police.

Officers carried out 87 multi-agency visits and patrols, with more than a third focused on anti-social behaviour hotspots.

The update comes amid ongoing debate about the impact of emergency accommodation and HMOs in parts of Scarborough.

The report notes that concerns have been raised about areas including Prince of Wales Terrace, Esplanade Gardens and Valley Road.

However, officers said investigations had shown that not all incidents in those locations were linked to residents living in emergency accommodation.

According to the council, Scarborough is facing particularly high demand for temporary accommodation, with 76 households currently living in emergency housing compared with 53 in Harrogate.

The authority said it was seeking to expand its supply of temporary accommodation, particularly self-contained units for single people, to reduce reliance on bed-and-breakfasts and hotels.

Council officers also highlighted ongoing efforts to improve the appearance of the town through the “Sprucing Up Scarborough” programme.

The initiative is backed by £940,000 in Pride in Place funding and includes improvements to buildings, shopfronts, green spaces and proposals for new pocket parks.

Meanwhile, a recent roundtable meeting involving businesses, councillors, police and council officers agreed to establish a regular town centre forum focused on anti-social behaviour and develop plans to improve engagement with local businesses.

The report will be discussed by members of the Scarborough and Whitby Area Committee on Friday, November 27.
