## The criticism comes as Prime Minister Mark Carney diversifies defence spending and weighs alternatives to US defense kit amid strained relations with the Trump administration.

The Pentagon has accused Canada of failing to be a credible partner in defending North America in a sharp rebuke over its military spending and its rethink of an American-made F-35 fighter jet purchase.

Set out on Monday in an unusually blunt fact sheet from the US Department of Defense under Secretary Pete Hegseth, the criticism says Ottawa has shied away from the difficult choices needed to modernise its forces and meet its NATO obligations.

"Canada's defense shortfalls speak for themselves," it states. "Canada must get serious and focused."

Hegseth is "laser focused" on modernising Canada's military after decades of American taxpayers funding its defence, it adds.

## Ottawa has no costed route to 5% NATO target, says Pentagon

Canada met NATO's old defence target of 2% of gross domestic product and has joined allies in pledging 5% by 2035, including related security spending.

Its newest budget puts combined defence and security outlays at 4.1% of GDP by 2027-28, yet just 2.6% would fund core defence.

According to the Pentagon, Ottawa has no credible, fully costed route to 5% and is deferring its biggest increases until after the 2029 federal election. Canada's parliamentary budget watchdog has questioned how it will get there.

The rebuke follows a classified Hegseth directive that Republican Senator Mike Rounds says largely curbs US military officials from taking part in a Canadian think tank and social events.

Ties are strained by President Donald Trump's trade war and threats to make Canada the 51st state.

The fact sheet says just 41.9% of Canada's air force fleet and 53.9% of its naval fleet were serviceable in 2025-26, with army readiness at 48%.

It takes aim at Ottawa's review of its plan to buy 88 American-made F-35s, warning that a mixed fighter fleet could aggravate personnel shortages. Canadian leaders, it says, are putting domestic economic interests before military effectiveness, leaving the jointly run North American Aerospace Defense Command (NORAD) in uncertainty.

## **The criticism comes as Carney** diversifies defence spending

Prime Minister Mark Carney ordered the review as Trump threatened Canadian sovereignty and imposed tariffs. Canada has looked at Sweden's Gripen jets, which could bring manufacturing jobs.

Neither Canada's Department of National Defence nor Carney's office commented on the fact sheet, and the defence minister's office did not reply to a request for comment.

Carney has conceded readiness problems, saying only one of Canada's four submarines is seaworthy; his government plans to buy up to 12 replacements. "We should no longer send three-quarters of our defense capital spending to America," he said in June.

Other NATO members also lag on the 5% goal, yet Colby on Sunday hailed Germany's military buildup as a "tectonic shift" and called Poland a "true model ally."

Pentagon officials were already barred from the Halifax International Security Forum in Canada under 2025 restrictions imposed by the Trump administration.

Forum president Peter Van Praagh, a former adviser to a previous Canadian conservative government, warned before the 3 November midterms in the US that Trump might undermine the results after heavy Republican losses and that if he succeeded, it would mean "the end of the United States as we know it."

At the forum on Saturday, Mike Rounds said the directive was not expected to affect joint military operations or intelligence sharing.
