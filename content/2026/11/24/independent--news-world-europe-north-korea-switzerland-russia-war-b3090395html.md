North Korea has threatened to shut down a Swiss diplomatic site only weeks after it opened in Pyongyang, reacting angrily to sanctions imposed on a youth complex over allegations that it helped move children from Russian-occupied parts of Ukraine.

In a statement published by state media, Kim Yo Jong, the influential sister of North Korean leader Kim Jong Un, denounced the measures against the Songdowon International Children’s Camp in Wonsan as an "open hostile act" and a "serious infringement" of her country’s sovereignty.

The prominent foreign policy figure maintained that the eastern coastal centre facilitated "legitimate" co-operation between Pyongyang and Moscow. She failed to directly respond to allegations from the West that Ukrainian children seized by Russian forces had been taken there for re-education.

Pyongyang has forged increasingly close relations with Moscow throughout the invasion of Ukraine, deploying thousands of soldiers and supplying vast quantities of weaponry to bolster Russian President Vladimir Putin’s military campaign, likely receiving economic support and military tech in return.

![Swiss Foreign Minister Ignazio Cassis holds a talk with North Korean Foreign Minister Choe Son Hui at Pyongyang Assembly Hall in Pyongyang, Friday, Oct. 30, 2026](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/24/07/North_Korea_Switzerland__3977.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Strengthened ties with Moscow and Beijing have been key drivers of Kim Jong Un’s increasingly assertive foreign policy as he positions North Korea as part of a united front against the United States.

Swiss Foreign Minister Ignazio Cassis traveled to North Korea in late October as Switzerland opened a diplomatic office in Pyongyang and held talks with senior North Korean officials, including Foreign Minister Choe Son Hui, over “promoting dialogue and cooperation,” North Korean state media said at the time.

Kim Yo Jong said that North Korea would need to “comprehensively reconsider the relations with unreliable Switzerland” following the sanctions on the camp and called on North Korean diplomats to take unspecified “corresponding” countermeasures.

“It is my view that the Swiss diplomatic office has spontaneously lost justification for its existence, which was opened in Pyongyang under the pretext of improving the relations, given that the Swiss Confederation has clarified its stand not to respect the sovereignty of the DPRK,” she said, using the initials of North Korea’s formal name, the Democratic People’s Republic of Korea.

In adding the Songdowon camp earlier this month to its broader sanctions related to Russia’s all-out invasion of Ukraine, Swiss authorities held the camp responsible for the “forced assimilation, including indoctrination, and militarized education of Ukrainian minors,” according to a document by Switzerland’s State Secretariat for Economic Affairs.

It said the camp participates in programs involving the transfer of Ukrainian children from Russia-occupied territories to North Korea and exposes them to propaganda and activities designed to promote pro-Russia narratives.

The United Kingdom in May and the European Union in September imposed similar sanctions on the Songdowon camp, freezing its assets and prohibiting financial transactions with the facility, although the practical impact of such measures is unclear given North Korea’s limited financial ties with European countries.
