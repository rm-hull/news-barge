A postal worker was sacked by Royal Mail after he was caught dumping mail in a rubbish bin by a member of the public.

A photograph of Oliver Despard putting unaddressed door-to-door [D2D] marketing mail into the bin on a residential street in Glaisdale, North Yorkshire, was handed in as part of a complaint, an employment tribunal heard.

When questioned by a manager, Mr Despard admitted to putting “five or six” surplus or damaged pieces of the mail in the bin – but later said he thought it was acceptable based on a lack of training he had received.

Mr Despard, who had six years’ of service at Royal Mail, was subsequently dismissed for gross misconduct.

At the employment tribunal hearing in Nottingham last month, Mr Despard claimed the sacking was unfair. But Judge Holbrook dismissed his case, stating it was fair for Royal Mail to consider the action due to potential for serious reputational and commercial damage.

D2Ds, the tribunal heard, are a vital source of income for the Royal Mail, which is already under pressure over deteriorating delivery standards, with just 82.5 per cent of first-clas mail arriving the following day.

![The Royal Mail said the delivery of door-to-door marketing provided vital income. Image is a stock, and does not show Oliver Despard.](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/14/11/40/iStock-2263170391.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The case also comes several months after it emerged Royal Mail was investigating allegations that a postal worker claimed to have “dumped” Reform UK campaign leaflets in a bin.

In Mr Despard’s case, the complaint was made against him after a delivery round with a “high-capacity” trolley on 28 October last year. The following morning, he was informed there had been a complaint made by a member of the public who “had reported seeing him put mail into a public bin close to the Post Office”, the tribunal decision recorded.

At a “fact-finding meeting” a week later, the postal worker said he thought it was acceptable to put the D2Ds in a bin based on his experience, but he also acknowledged that it would “potentially not look good” to a member of the public.

At a further meeting, evidence was supplied from a manger who said they went to the bin and found 50 D2Ds, including advertising from Sky, a pizza shop and a pub.

Mr Despard maintained he placed five to six D2Ds in the bin, and accused bosses of carrying out an inadequate investigation of the incident. He said they failed to take into account a lack of training that made him aware D2Ds fell under the definiton of mail, and that by throwing it them in a public bin he was breaching business standards.

He even said he had seen other staff throwing away D2Ds.

But Royal Mail told the hearing that Mr Despard undertook training that included mail integrity, and that it issued guides to employees which stated the definition of mail.

In his decision, Mr Holbrook wrote: “I conclude that the dismissal was fair because Royal Mail acted reasonably in, on the accepted fact that Mr Despard had put 5 – 6 D2Ds in a public bin on 28 October 2025, to consider this gross misconduct in light of its policies and the potential for serious reputational and commercial damage and to treat this as a sufficient reason for dismissing Mr Despard, having conducted an ultimately fair investigation and procedure.”
