Andy Burnham has hailed new limits on non-disclosure agreements (NDAs) that will allow victims of crime to speak more freely.

The Prime Minister said the aim was to root out a culture of silencing victims to protect perpetrators.

The new ruling will now come into force in January after Mr Burnham announced he was bringing planned changes forward.

Russell Brand’s rape conviction and allegations against David Walliams, which he has denied, have reignited conversation about how such agreements are used in recent weeks.

Mr Burnham said: “Too many victims have suffered in silence, feeling like they can’t speak out because their perpetrator is too powerful. That must end.

“We must draw a line in the sand on the idea that powerful men are immune to facing justice because they think they can silence their victims.”

He said the government will also consider recent calls to introduce a staggered age of consent to prevent older people using their status to have sex with much younger partners.

Mr Burnham said: “I not only want to make sure victims can speak out about their own abuse, but also have the opportunity to challenge the status quo.

“That’s why we will also be looking whether or not to stagger the age of consent, asking survivors, parents, young people, victim support services and the wider public to come forward to share their experiences and inform this process.”

Changes under the Employment Rights Act and the Victims and Courts Act that void clauses in NDAs if they are used to silence workers over harassment or discrimination or stop victims reporting information about criminal conduct were due to come into force later in 2027.

They will now take effect in January.

This means victims of crime will be able to speak freely to police, family, friends, the media or anyone they choose even if they have signed an NDA.

Similarly, confidentiality clauses between employers and workers will no longer be valid in cases where someone experiences or witnesses harassment at work – but this only applies to new NDAs.

The Prime Minister wants to make sure no loopholes are left and also plans to set out steps next year to ban NDAs used to stop people from reporting harassment in situations outside of the workplace – including for freelancers and fans.

Campaigner Zelda Perkins, who breached an NDA to become a whistleblower against Harvey Weinstein, said: “These promises have the potential to transform lives, help reshape how we treat one another as a society and uphold the integrity of a legal system that must never be used to protect perpetrators at the expense of victims.

“I am really keen to see how this develops and look forward to working with the Government to make sure these protections go far enough, and genuinely cover everyone win society who needs them.

“These promises are hugely significant. Now we need to make sure they become reality.”
