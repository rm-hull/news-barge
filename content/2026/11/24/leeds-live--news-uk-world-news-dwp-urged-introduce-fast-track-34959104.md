# DWP urged to introduce fast-track PIP process for 2 groups of claimants

## The Timms Review has recommended the DWP introduce an 'immediate' fast-track PIP pathway for people with severe conditions

The Department for Work and Pensions has been instructed to implement an "immediate" change for Personal Independence Payment claimants following the publication of the Timms Review. The wide-ranging review into PIP, commissioned by the Labour government, was released today.

It has put forward a complete overhaul of the system to enable disabled people to "achieve better health, higher living standards and greater independence". One of the immediate changes proposed is an expedited route for those with severe conditions and terminal illnesses, reports Birmingham Live.

Reviews for claimants whose conditions are unlikely to improve will also be scaled back. The review states: "There should be 2 fast-track pathways: one for people with serious, well-evidenced conditions whose impact on their daily life is clear, and a quicker route for people receiving end-of-life care."

The DWP adds that those on these pathways should receive an award without the need for a full assessment. Craig James, a PIP claimant from Birmingham, shared his thoughts with the BBC News team regarding the proposed changes

"The first time it was very daunting to me, I didn't know the process whatsoever," said the 23-year-old, who lives in Birmingham. I didn't know there were charities that could help you, I just went in without any information whatsoever. I knew I needed it but I didn't know how much."

Craig was first admitted to hospital in September last year and is currently "under multiple specialists" with several medical appointments "every week now" while awaiting a formal diagnosis. He states that he requires PIP to assist with transport expenses and grocery costs, and believes there "should be a fast-track process because it's not an easy thing to apply for".

He added: "It shouldn't exclude the people that genuinely do need the help there, it shouldn't be [a choice] between employment support or disability support, there should be something in place to be able to do both."
