*60 Minutes* is beset with troubles as it suffered another week of low ratings in the latest blow to CBS News boss Bari Weiss.

CBS News’ flagship newsmagazine has seen ratings tumble ever since Weiss, who took on the role of editor-in-chief in October 2025, began a massive overhaul of the core-three broadcast news network.

*60 Minutes*drew an average of 8.3 million viewers for the nine episodes that aired from Sept. 9 through Nov. 15, a 16-percent drop from last season, according to preliminary Nielsen data obtained by the* Wall Street Journal.*

That also includes a nearly 25-percent drop among viewers ages 25 to 54, which is the key demographic for advertisers.

The bad news comes as the show opened its 59th season in September with its smallest premiere audience in at least a quarter century, with just 6.5 million viewers tuning in, according to Nielsen numbers.

![CBS News’ 60 Minutes has seen ratings drop this season under the new leadership of Bari Weiss](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/09/26/16/04/GettyImages-2253294251.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The show’s continuous fall in ratings follows a tumultuous year at CBS News, after billionaire David Ellison acquired Paramount — CBS News’ parent company — and appointed Weiss, an “anti-woke” conservative columnist, as editor-in-chief in October last year.

Weiss, who came into the role with no prior broadcast experience, ousted several veteran *60 Minutes* correspondents, including Scott Pelley and Cecilia Vega. Another longtime correspondent, Sharyn Alfonsi, left after her contract expired and amid a dispute with Weiss.

Weiss, who founded *The Free Press,*a conservative-leaning news and opinion site on Substack, later hired former* New York Times*and* Vanity Fair*writer Nick Bilton to lead* 60 Minutes,* though he also had no prior experience in TV news.

Weiss later filled the empty roles with more conservative-leaning hires, including New York Times opinion columnist Ross Douthat and British broadcaster Sir Trevor Philips, who has defended Jeffrey Esptein-tied Lord Peter Mandelson.

Weiss has also been accused by critics of swaying the network’s coverage to be more favorable toward the Trump administration and Israel. However, she has denied those claims.

![After David Ellison took over Paramount and named Bari Weiss as editor-in-chief of CBS News, the network has seen its ratings tumble](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/04/21/57/GettyImages-2194604196.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The network’s struggling news ratings also come as routine discussions over editorial decisions have repeatedly been leaked to the media, fueling speculation over troubles at the show under Weiss and Bilton’s leadership.

Bilton told the *Journal* that his focus is on the show’s content, not its ratings.

“I can control how good the stories are, the mix of the stories,” he said. “And I truly believe that [if] you continue to do that, you continue to put out great journalism, great storytelling, your audience comes.” Weiss declined to comment to the *Journal.*

*The Independent* has contacted CBS News for comment.
