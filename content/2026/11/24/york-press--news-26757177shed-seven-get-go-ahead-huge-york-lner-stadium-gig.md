Shed Seven are set to play the biggest headline show of their career at the York LNER Community Stadium after a licence was approved allowing the venue to host major concerts.

Council planners gave the green light for the stadium in Huntington to host three music concerts for up to 14,500 people per day from May to June next year.

The decision will officially allow the Sheds to take to the stage on the LNER pitch for their homecoming concert on May 29, 2027.

It also means Sir Tom Jones can headline the LNER the day before the Sheds as part of the Welsh icon’s Come Gather Round World Tour.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21725262.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Sir Tom Jones is set to become the first music artist to headline at York's LNER Community Stadium on May 28, 2027. *(Image: Submitted)*

Richard Taylor, the stadium’s lawyer, apologised to the planning panel for tickets for the gigs already going on sale before the licence had been granted.

Mr Taylor said there was the “unfortunate situation that the promoters have announced what is happening” before the licence was approved.

He said the LNER had asked the promoters not to announce the gigs, but tickets were already on sale before the stadium had obtained the licence.

“We have not announced what is happening,” he told the panel. “We have asked them [the promoters] not to announce what is proposed.

“There are no contracts signed. I understand that if you try and buy the tickets, it does say in some very small print somewhere ‘subject to licensing’.

“Certainly, no disrespect from our side here – we can ask people not to do things, but they can ignore us.”

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21584303.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Shed Seven at York's LNER Community Stadium where they will be playing in May 2027 *(Image: Tom Oxley)*

Mr Taylor said the stadium was approached by Live Nation around 18 months ago, proposing that the LNER hosted the homecoming concert for Shed Seven.

The application received two objections from residents but Mr Taylor noted that the experts, North Yorkshire Police and City of York Council, had not objected to the plans.

A future resident of Castra Park development, under construction in New Lane, objected to the LNER hosting music concerts, saying it would “create sustained and intrusive noise for future residents”.

The second objector said the “very close proximity of these large crowds to the adjacent Castra Park residential walkways raises critical safety, security, and antisocial behaviour concerns for the incoming families”.

But the panel’s chair, Cllr Richard Watson, said the two objectors’ concerns were not “substantiated by evidence”, as the application was granted.

He said the panel considered the objections “very carefully” but “we have not considered in the light of the relationship between the objectors and the applicant. And we feel that the relationship is not relevant to our decision-making process.

“Our job is a balancing act. And we conclude that three concerts per annum, at a venue already used for major sporting events, is not an unreasonable request.”

Shed Seven’s frontman, Rick Witter, said the LNER concert will be the “biggest and best Shed Seven show we’ve ever put on”.
