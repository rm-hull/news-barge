Democratic Senate leader Chuck Schumer on Tuesday accused Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang of violating federal laws limiting advanced technology exports to China.

In a post on X, Schumer said Huang "shipped extremely advanced chips to China, violating US export control law, in what amounts to the equivalent of selling rocket engines to the Soviets during the Space Race."

Nvidia and the White House did not immediately comment.

Schumer's statement marked an escalation of congressional criticism against Huang, who was reinstated as chief executive of Nvidia on Monday despite claims regarding his relationship with a woman accused of being a Chinese government agent. Lawmakers from both parties are increasingly questioning Nvidia's trade with China, pressing for tighter controls while investigating whether Huang's conduct endangered national security.

Lawmakers have also faulted President Donald Trump for pushing Nvidia to reinstate Huang as CEO.

Schumer and other legislators insist Congress must act rapidly to curtail American chip sales to China. Senator Dave McCormick, a Pennsylvania Republican, said: "Congress must get to the bottom of what happened, and then strengthen our export controls to ensure China can't access our most advanced chips through the back door."

In a separate move on Tuesday, leading Democrats on four House committees wrote to Trump demanding answers about his awareness of Huang’s ties to the suspected agent.

![](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/24/23/2305747014..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

"Your personal relationship with Mr. Huang and your willful blindness to the economic and national security risks posed by this scandal have put the American people at great risk," wrote Representatives Frank Pallone, Gregory Meeks, Jamie Raskin and Robert Garcia, requesting all records of administration contact with the Nvidia board.

"Upon learning that Huang was in a relationship with a suspected agent of the Chinese government, what actions, if any, did Trump take to limit risks to America’s economic and national security?" the lawmakers inquired.

Senator Kevin Cramer, a North Dakota Republican, told CNBC on Tuesday that more protections are needed on exports to China. "We might even have to put a babysitter on Jensen Huang," he stated, adding it would be "just to make sure that he doesn't do something that is going to risk the United States."

Huang will appear on December 3 before the US Senate Banking Committee for a public hearing, panel's chair Tim Scott announced Monday.

Huang is also slated for a private briefing on December 1 with three House panels.

Representative Pat Harrigan, a North Carolina Republican, said Huang's "reinstatement today at this juncture is a mistake." He noted: "We sent a signal that one man’s financial impact on our economy outweighs the importance of good governance, good citizenship, and adherence to the law. That is a dangerous precedent to set."
