* **Tidal says it's banning all 100% AI-generated music from its service**
* **The policy will start from December 9 and lead to 29 million track deletions**
* **Meanwhile, Deezer research has discovered most music streaming users unknowingly have AI music in their playlists**

As Spotify gears up to launch its AI-powered remix and covers feature in 2027, Tidal is moving in the opposite direction — announcing today that it’s banning all AI-generated tracks from its platform, starting on December 9.

The news, announced on X, follows its decision back in July to demonetize and label 100% AI-generated recordings. This time, it’s getting rid of them completely. This will result in roughly 29 million songs being deleted, and an estimated 80,000-85,000 rejected songs per day.

It's also a move that makes sense for Tidal, with the streaming service revealing that roughly 6% of its catalog is currently AI-generated, and that roughly 43% of all tracks added over the last 90 days have been AI.

However, there’s clearly not much appetite for those tracks, because 96% of all AI-badged tracks got zero streams in the last 90 days.

## Unpopular yet hard to avoid

Recent research from rival Deezer suggests the public have little interest in listening to AI music — and that when AI music is listened to, it’s often done so unknowingly. The streaming service's "international barometer", conducted by Ipsos across nine countries this month, concluded that while 57% of people now have fully AI-generated music in their playlists, 74% of people don’t think they do.

It also found that 67% of people fear that AI could reduce creativity in music production, and 69% believe that AI-generated music threatens the livelihood of musicians.

Based on those numbers, it’s safe to say that fully AI-generated music isn’t particularly popular and risks clogging up music services. The more AI-generated tracks there are in circulation, the more people will unknowingly listen to them — or become more accepting of the idea, which could harm the industry in the long term.
