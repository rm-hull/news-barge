President Donald Trump may have made as much as $15 million from Nvidia’s decision to reinstate Jensen Huang as its CEO, which sent its share price soaring by more than 30 percent Monday, according to a report.

*The Financial Times* reports that the president’s latest financial disclosures, filed on November 5 with the Office of Government Ethics, reveal that he owned as many as 370,000 shares in the chipmaker as of September 30.

Welcoming the news of Huang’s reinstatement Monday, Trump posted: “The Country needs Jensen’s leadership at Nvidia in order to ensure the Success and Prosperity of not only the Company, but the American Economy.”

But Arizona Democratic Sen. Ruben Gallego was among those quick to voice unease over the prospect of the president appearing to profit from Nvidia’s announcement, writing on X: “We need to investigate what stock positions took place before the announcement. This White House is the most corrupt in history.”

*The Independent* has reached out to the White House for comment.

![President Donald Trump may have profited substantially from Nvidia’s decision to reinstate Jensen Huang as CEO Monday, according to an estimate of his shareholdings](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/12/09/2196376751..?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&auto=webp)

The 370,000 share figure does not account for any share sales or purchases Trump may have made in October and the first three weeks of November – the period during which the extramarital affairs that forced Huang’s resignation on November 12 were first reported.

The *FT* also qualifies its estimate by stating that the filings do not contain precise figures on the shares and only provide wide bands, explaining that its estimate is based on the assumption that Trump “always buys the largest amount in the range and sells the smallest.”

But it was previously reported that the president’s personal stake in Nvidia grew from an estimated $615,000 to $1.3 million at the end of 2024 to as much as $62 million over the last year as the company’s role in driving the global AI boom became ever-more pivotal.

The president had been loudly lobbying for Huang’s return for more than a week, insisting he is a “genius” who has been treatment unfairly and dismissing any concern that one of the women with whom he was alleged to have had an affair, Megaspeed executive Alice Huang (no relation), was considered a Chinese spy by U.S. intelligence.

He even mentioned Huang’s exile at his trilateral summit with Chinese Premier Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin in Shenzhen, at a D.C. press conference Friday and again on Truth Social Sunday.

![Trump reacted jubilantly to Huang’s return, posting a meme on Truth Social satirizing his surviving the sex scandal that forced his resignation on November 12](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/23/18/02/jensen-huang-truth-social.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Following Huang’s reinstatement, the president posted a parody version of the famous photograph of himself rising up with a defiant fist clenched after he was shot in the ear by a sniper in Butler, Pennsylvania, in 2024, now featuring Huang brandishing a red bra below a billowing Nvidia flag.

Trump then admitted to CNBC he had leaned on Nvidia’s board of directors to reinstate him, saying: “I spoke to them, let me put it that way. And I convinced them… I made the deal… The board decided to do what we wanted to do.”

Asked about internal concerns over Huang’s conduct, the president insisted the tech boss “did nothing wrong” and added: “We gave him assurance, at least with everything we know, and we know a lot. People say, ‘Is it a threat to have him?’ No, it’s a threat to lose him.”

Nvidia said in a statement it had received input from “senior levels” of the U.S. government before making its U-turn. “Based on that input, the board is confident that Huang’s reinstatement poses no national security risk.”

Among those expressing doubt about that assessment was MAGA influencer Laura Loomer, who warned the decision was likely to “blow back on our national security.”

“People can like the guy personally, but why are we pretending like his relationship with an alleged Chinese spy isn’t the same thing (if not worse) that we all attacked Eric Swalwell for?” she said, invoking a similar scandal involving the disgraced California Democratic congressman.

At least three Republican senators – Tom Cotton of Arkansas, John Curtis of Utah and Pete Ricketts of Nebraska – have also come forward to express disquiet about Huang’s return, saying they were not satisfied that questions over whether or not he had compromised U.S. national security had been conclusively addressed.
