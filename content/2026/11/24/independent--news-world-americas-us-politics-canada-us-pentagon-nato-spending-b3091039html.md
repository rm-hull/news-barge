A war of words between the United States and Canada has erupted again after the Pentagon accused Ottawa of failing to adequately contribute to defending North America.

"Canada is a sovereign nation. I’ll be very direct. We don’t report to the United States," Canada’s Defense Minister David McGuinty told reporters Tuesday, a day after the Pentagon issued an unusually blunt fact sheet.

"Canada’s defense shortfalls speak for themselves," the Pentagon memo said. "Canada must get serious and focused."

The Pentagon claimed Canada has no realistic strategy for meeting NATO military targets and accused Ottawa of depending on U.S. taxpayers to fund its defense for decades. The Pentagon also accused Canadian officials of delaying major defense funding boosts until after their 2029 election.

McGuinty maintained Ottawa is on schedule to allocate 5% of gross domestic product toward defense and security by 2035, highlighting that NATO members previously agreed to reassess that target during a 2029 review.

"We also said by 2029 we would be examining that 5% commitment," McGuinty said.

![Canada's Defense Minister David McGuinty speaks to journalists before a meeting of the federal cabinet on Parliament Hill in Ottawa, Tuesday, Nov. 24, 2026](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/24/22/download..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

McGuinty noted Canadian projections reach 4% of GDP by 2030, describing it as a "significant, significant step" up from the 1.25% recorded 14 months earlier.

NATO’s 2025 accord requires member nations to spend at least 3.5% of GDP on core defense alongside 1.5% for security by 2035. The 2029 evaluation will gauge expenditure growth against evolving global threats and operational requirements.

## Pentagon memos spark outrage

The Pentagon’s criticism coincides with a secret memo by Pete Hegseth that curtails American officials from joining Canadian think tank panels and gatherings.

Under rules enacted in 2025 limiting policy forum attendance, the Trump administration prohibited defense staff from joining the Halifax International Security Forum. The annual summit, running for 18 years, attracts roughly 300 defense personnel, diplomats, lawmakers, and policy experts. Past administrations across party lines routinely dispatched senior Pentagon leaders, including defense secretaries.

Attending the weekend event in Halifax, Democratic U.S. Sen. Andy Kim publicly denounced the administration's policy in a social media message.

"Hegseth did not allow a single American servicemember in uniform to show up," he wrote.

On Saturday, Republican U.S. Sen. Mike Rounds said that the policy order should not disrupt routine bilateral military operations or intelligence exchange.

McGuinty emphasized that operational collaboration with Washington via the North American Aerospace Defense Command, or NORAD, as well as NATO, continues unabated.

"We’re making the investments we announced. We’re executing on those plans," he said.

American officials also slammed Ottawa's decision to reassess its scheduled acquisition of 88 U.S.-built F-35 fighter jets, warning a split fleet risks compounding staffing challenges and creating operational uncertainty for NORAD.

Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney commissioned the evaluation after Trump made threats against Canadian sovereignty and levied trade tariffs. Canada has evaluated Swedish-produced Gripen aircraft, offering domestic manufacturing advantages.
