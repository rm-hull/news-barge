Goldie the Gritter is getting into the Christmas spirit once again, thanks to the incredible handiwork of volunteers from the Ripon Community Poppy Project.

The much-loved gritting vehicle from Econ Engineering has been given its enormous festive makeover, complete with a knitted Christmas jumper and Santa hat, ready to spread some seasonal cheer.

The jumper, believed to be one of the biggest ever created, is made up of hundreds of knitted panels featuring Christmas trees, snowmen, gingerbread men and Father Christmas.

It's the third year Goldie has worn the festive outfit, with the creation previously travelling around the UK, including visits to schools in Wales and Scotland, where it has delighted children.

Stuart Martin and Hazel Barker, co-founders of the Ripon Community Poppy Project, explained how the unusual project first came about.

Stuart said:

> "We were asked about three or four years ago if we'd knit a Christmas jumper for somebody at Econ.
>
> "Foolishly, we thought it was just a person we were going to knit it for until we got told the size of it!
>
> "It turns out they wanted a full-size jumper for a gritter to cover the whole gritter, with a Santa hat as well."

The volunteers set about knitting individual panels, which were then sewn together to create the enormous garment.

Hazel explained that the panels are knitted in squares of around ten inches, although differences in knitting styles and tension mean they don't always turn out exactly the same size.

![](https://mmo.aiircdn.com/409/6b05c9a5b614f9f72c2.png)

The finished panels were brought together on netting, with Hazel using her conservatory and dining room to lay everything out and assemble the creation.

The project also has a special connection with Econ Engineering, which has supported the Poppy Project in the past.

The company produced the metal silhouettes seen around Ripon, including in the Remembrance Garden and Spa Gardens, free of charge.

Stuart said:

> "It's good for us to be able to give something back to a company like this.
>
> "It's just right and proper that we say thank you, and this is our way of saying thank you."

Fernando Sarmento de Alencar, Maintenance and Production Efficiency Manager at Econ Engineering, said he was proud to see the relationship between the company and the community project continue.

Fernando, who has worked at the Ripon factory for 20 years, said Goldie had become a familiar sight when travelling around the country.

He added:

> "It's amazing. You see the Christmas jumper over a gritter truck and the children are absolutely happy when they see it.
>
> "It's nice to see at this festive time of year, closer to Christmas and the winter.
>
> "I'm very proud of that."

Goldie was given to Econ Engineering as a present to celebrate the company's 50th anniversary and has become an important part of its story.

Fernando added that supporting local organisations and charities was an important part of the company's identity.

He said:

> "For us, that's everything. Econ has always been helping the community and helping charities.
>
> "Being part of the community makes the community part of the business as well, and that's what's helping our business grow all over the country."

Goldie's Christmas jumper will be on display from November 24, with another opportunity for people to see the festive gritter coming up at Ripon's Winter Wonderland.

The event takes place in the Spa Gardens on December 18, 19 and 20, with Goldie expected to be parked in the grounds of the Ripon Inn nearby.
