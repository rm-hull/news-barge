A typical household's dual-fuel bill is on course to jump by £443 from 1 January, according to analysts at Cornwall Insight.

The consultancy has projected that the energy cap will rise by 26 per cent to £2,166 for the three months from January to March.

The energy regulator Ofgem is expected to announce the increase, which would take the cap to its highest level in four years, on Wednesday.

The rise would add to already high energy costs, after the cap rose by 4 per cent at the start of last month.

Campaigners have stressed that it is “not too late” for further government intervention to help hard-hit households.

![Chancellor John Healey announced in his autumn budget that gas standing charges will be scrapped in England, Scotland and Wales](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/20/07/e66fd0e31127cfd401e90aa3a5096ecbY29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzk1MTg1MDY0-2.86331177.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The bleak outlook for bills follows escalating violence in the Middle East, which sparked a fresh jump in oil and gas prices through October.

It also comes amid deepening fears of gas supply levels in Europe.

Wholesale prices have since eased back, helping to support a reduction in petrol prices.

But that easing came too late to affect the calculation for the new price cap.

Households will ultimately see a rise in bills even once they receive a government payment of £100 towards their costs from next year.

The payment, for up to 20 million households, comes alongside a temporary 5 per cent VAT cut from electricity bills until June next year.

![But campaigners have said current support will still leave millions of people facing a surge in their bills](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/08/20/11/02/iStock-1345466784.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The Chancellor also announced in his autumn budget that gas standing charges will be scrapped in England, Scotland and Wales.

But campaigners have said current support will still leave millions of people facing a surge in their bills.

Simon Francis, coordinator of the End Fuel Poverty Coalition, said: “Households are set to pay more for gas than ever before with changes coming in the depths of winter.

“For millions of people that equals cold homes, mounting debt and having to make impossible choices.

“It means parents going without so their children can stay warm, people who rely on medical equipment rationing their electricity and households on prepayment meters left with no heat at all once their credit runs out.

“The Government should be investing more in helping households get off gas for good and ending our exposure to these volatile prices, and it is not too late for Ministers to also take action that would make a difference this winter.”
