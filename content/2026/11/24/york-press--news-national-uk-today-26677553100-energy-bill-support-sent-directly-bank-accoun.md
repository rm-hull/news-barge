Letters are due to start arriving for Chancellor John Healey's £100 energy payment for 20 million households in England in council tax bands A to D, with the money paid directly into bank accounts to help with rising energy bills this winter.

The £2.6billion scheme is designed to give families breathing space during the coldest months, as households face pressure from energy costs and the impact of conflict in the Middle East.

Announcing the measure in his Budget, Mr Healey told MPs: “Madam Deputy Speaker, let me say this to families at home: I know that with the conflict in the Middle East, rising energy bills are a real worry.

“I know that people look to government at times like this not to solve all their problems, but to give them a bit of breathing space.

“So today, I'm taking one further step. This winter, for 20 million households in council tax bands A to D, I'm announcing a £100 energy payment for households, paid directly into people's bank accounts to deal with rising energy bills.

“That's a £2.6 billion scheme to take the pressure off in the coldest months and see people through to the spring.”

The Chancellor said the Scottish, Welsh and Northern Ireland governments would receive their share of the funding, and urged them to introduce similar support for households in their nations.

He added: “Madam Deputy Speaker, 50 pounds off VAT, 100 pounds off council tax, for this winter two tax cuts for next year fairer bills, and for the long term radical reform of the energy market to produce cheaper power.”

## Who will receive the £100 energy payment?

The payment is intended for households in council tax bands A to D, with the money paid directly into bank accounts.

The announcement sets out a £100 payment for eligible households this winter, but further details on how payments will be administered and when the money will arrive have not been provided in the statement quoted above.

The Chancellor said the devolved governments would receive funding to support households in Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland, but called on them to take their own action to provide similar help.

Households should wait for further official guidance on eligibility and payment arrangements before assuming they will receive the money automatically.

## Healey says Budget keeps Labour's tax pledge

The energy payment is part of a wider Budget package that Mr Healey said would support working people while maintaining the Government's commitment not to raise taxes on “working people”.

The Chancellor told MPs that the Budget was designed to provide breathing space on the cost of living and the cost of doing business, support businesses and growth, and tackle longer-term challenges including energy, national security, public services and housing.
