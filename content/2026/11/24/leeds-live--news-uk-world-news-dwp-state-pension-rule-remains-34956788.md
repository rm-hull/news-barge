# DWP state pension rule 'remains in place' with people over 60 warned

## The Department for Work and Pensions has issued an update on State Pension rules

The Department for Work and Pensions has provided an update for widowed women expecting to receive their late partners' state pensions at 60 years of age. The DWP delivered the update in the House of Commons following questioning from opposition MPs.

Hannah Spencer, Green Party Spokesperson for Business, Innovation, Science and Trade, Culture, Media and Sport, Education and Housing, Communities and Local Government, raised the matter in Parliament.

Ms Spencer addressed the chamber "to ask the Secretary of State for Work and Pensions, whether he has made an assessment of the potential impact of the State Pension age increase on widowed women who expected to inherit some of their deceased partner's State Pension at the age of 60?".

Responding to the query, DWP minister Torsten Bell explained: "The new State Pension, for people reaching State Pension age on or after 6 April 2016, is based on an individual's own National Insurance record."

The Labour MP for Swansea continued: "In general, it is not possible, as it was under the old State Pension system, for a person to increase their State Pension or qualify for a State Pension based on their spouse or civil partner's National Insurance record, although there is some protection under the transitional arrangements for the new State Pension.

"It remains the case that a person cannot become entitled to inherit State Pension until they reach their own State Pension age."

Under DWP and Labour Party guidelines, you may be eligible to receive an additional payment on top of your new State Pension if you have been widowed. However, should you remarry or enter into a new civil partnership prior to reaching State Pension age, you will forfeit any entitlement to inheritance.

If your marriage or civil partnership commenced before 6 April 2016, you could be in line to inherit a portion of your late partner's Additional State Pension.

Furthermore, one of the following conditions must also be met: either your partner attained State Pension age before 6 April 2016, or they passed away before that date but would have reached State Pension age on or after it. Any inherited amount will be paid alongside your own State Pension.
