Indonesian authorities confirmed Tuesday 26 workers were killed in a fire three days ago at a candy factory where rescuers said most victims died on the second floor trying to reach the emergency exit.

More than 600 rescuers, including soldiers, police officers, Red Cross personnel and volunteers, were searching the burned three-story building for eight more workers missing since the fire Saturday at the PT Fonusa Agung Mulia factory in Serang district of Banten province near the capital, Jakarta.

Teams focused on the rear of the factory, where debris, lingering smoke and occasional flare-ups complicated recovery efforts, said Al Amrad, head of the Banten search and rescue agency.

Many victims were found near an access route leading to an emergency staircase close to the area where the fire is believed to have started.

“Most victims were recovered from the factory’s second floor, and many of the remains were severely burned,” said Jajuli, a search-and-rescue worker who uses a single name.

He said workers trying to reach the emergency exit had to navigate corridors and turns, while the staircase’s proximity to the fire may have made escape difficult as flames spread through the building. Some victims were found clustered together, apparently trapped while attempting to flee. Elevator problems also may have hampered evacuation efforts, he said.

Preliminary examinations showed the remains recovered from the site so far belonged to 26 victims, Banten Police medical chief Commissioner Dr. I Gusti Gede Dharma Arimbawa said. Seven were men and nine were women, while 10 others were too badly burned for visual identification. DNA samples would be used to match the remains to the list of missing.

During a meeting with families Monday, company officials apologized and pledged support for affected workers and relatives.

“The company will take responsibility for helping victims and their families,” chief executive Steven Erwin Wijaya said.

About 300 workers were inside the factory when the fire started, possibly in a cooling unit on the second floor. Many of the workers escaped, and rescuers saved 29 of them, five of whom were hospitalized with injuries.

The workers said no alarm sounded and their fellow workers shouted warnings that allowed them to escape.

Police have opened a criminal investigation after finding preliminary evidence that negligence may have contributed to the fire.

“We have found indications of a criminal offense related to negligence,” Zain Dwi Nugroho, deputy chief of Banten police, told reporters Tuesday, adding that investigators have questioned 19 witnesses and seized safety records, permit documents and camera equipment as they seek to determine who could be held criminally responsible.

The company earlier said the factory regularly conducted evacuation drills and maintained evacuation routes and that its fire-safety systems, including hydrants and emergency exits, were functioning when the fire occurred.

Indonesia’s ombudsman urged the company and authorities to clarify issues involving wages, employment status, social security benefits and workplace accident compensation, and called for a review of workplace safety standards.

The factory produces Milkita, an Indonesian brand of milk-based candies and snack products exported to more than 40 countries.
