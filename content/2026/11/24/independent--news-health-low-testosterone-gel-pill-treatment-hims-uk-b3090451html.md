Hims & Hers has begun offering testosterone treatments in the UK, the telehealth company said on Tuesday, marking a further expansion in its international business.

Treatment options include enclomiphene, an unlicensed pill that stimulates natural testosterone production, and Testogel, a testosterone gel that is applied to the skin and sold by Besins Healthcare.

Hims already offers the treatments in the United States, where it also sells injectable testosterone. The company also sells diagnostic tests and other hormone treatments.

Enclomiphene is not approved for general commercial sale in the UK, Europe or the US. Hims did not immediately respond to a query about how it will negotiate offering a drug not approved for general use.

In small clinical trials, enclomiphene achieved testosterone levels comparable to transdermal gels. It did so without causing the decrease in sperm production seen with traditional testosterone replacement therapies.

However, it did not show clear beneficial effects on symptoms, which can include low sex drive, fatigue, low energy, weight gain and brain fog.

Dr. Kultar Garcha, chief medical officer for Hims' UK and EMEA business, said the company's offerings could improve access to earlier intervention and preventive care.

He noted that approaches to diagnosing and treating low testosterone vary across UK providers, creating an opportunity for more standardised telehealth care.

"Guidelines and recommendations are there, but we need to move away from the framing of 'I need to wait for somebody to be sick before I really take action'," said Garcha. "We're being forward-thinking and proactive."

Garcha said he expects both existing and new customers to use the treatments, as the company looks to broaden its offering and treat a wider range of patient conditions.

Testosterone therapies are now the second-fastest-growing business for Hims in the US, the company said.

Health experts have expressed concerns about unnecessary testosterone treatment.

Risks that have been flagged include infertility, testicular shrinkage, blood clots, and cardiovascular complications.

![Patient eligibility would be based on symptoms, testosterone levels and frequent reassessments, Garcha said](https://static.independent.co.uk/2023/06/19/16/newFile-1.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Patient eligibility would be based on symptoms, testosterone levels and frequent reassessments, Garcha said.

Testosterone levels would include whether hormone levels had declined over time.

Treatment plans for individual patients may also include vitamin supplements and the erectile dysfunction drug tadalafil, the generic of Eli Lilly's LLY.N Cialis, Hims said.

International business has helped boost subscriptions and increase monthly revenue per subscriber by 30 per cent from a year earlier, but has weighed on gross profit margins.

In 2025, Hims bought Zava, a London-based company that prescribes and delivers weight-loss drugs in the UK, Germany, France and Ireland.

Hims has said it expects continued pressure on its profit margins, as the company invests in launching new product lines. Hims said it aims to reach $6.5 billion in revenue by 2030.
