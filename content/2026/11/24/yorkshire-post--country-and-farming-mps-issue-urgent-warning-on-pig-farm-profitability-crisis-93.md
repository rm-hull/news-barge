# ‘Time is running out’: MPs issue urgent warning on pig farm ‘profitability crisis’

## The Farming Minister is set to convene a summit for the pig farming sector.

Ministers need to swiftly tackle a “profitability crisis” in pig farming otherwise smaller farmers and independent producers will start going under, MPs have warned.

The Environment, Food and Rural Affairs (Efra) Committee of MPs have written to the Farming Minister pushing for definitive action at an impending summit for the pork sector.

Stephen Morgan is set to convene and chair the meeting in the coming months, which the Government said would bring producers, processors and other supply-chain stakeholders together.

The Efra Committee urged for a swift response to the sector’s profitability crisis.

It said that the sector has been in decline since autumn 2025 due to an oversupply of pigs, and stagnant demand for pork and bacon.

At the same time, Spain’s African Swine Fever outbreak has led to export restrictions, which are hitting British farmers hard.

On top of this, feed prices have increased significantly. The National Pig Association believes that producers are losing between £20 and £50 per pig.

Efra Committee member Charlie Dewhirst, who comes from a family of pig farmers in Driffield, in the East Riding, said the sector was struggling across Europe.

“There is no easy fix to this, there’s no silver bullet,” the Bridlington and the Wolds MP told The Yorkshire Post.

The Efra Committee is particularly concerned about smaller producers, many of which are on the verge of going out of business.

Yorkshire, in particular, is home to a significant number of these.

Committee chair Alistair Carmichael said: “No one should take the continued survival of the UK pig sector for granted. Our smaller independent producers are in a particularly vulnerable position.

“Time is running out to keep the pig sector on its feet. Multiple challenges have made 2026 a tumultuous year, and the industry is now approaching a crisis point.

"The interconnectedness of rural economies means that the pig sector’s problems are being felt further afield.

“This matters to livelihoods and the UK’s food security so the government must respond urgently.

“They need to make both practical and financial interventions and to do it with some urgency.

“Lose the primary producers and you risk losing abattoirs, hauliers, feed companies and vets. That way, you create problems for other farming sectors and the wider rural economy.”

The Committee is urging the Government to do more to promote British pork and increase its use in hospitals, schools and prisons.

It also said the Home Office needs to release visa for several hundred skilled workers to tackle staff shortages in abattoirs and major processors.

The key request was for swift action to be taken following Mr Morgan’s upcoming summit.

A Defra spokesperson said: “We are strengthening pig farmers' position in the supply chain, this includes new rules to give British pig producers fairer contracts, greater certainty and protection.

“The Farming Minister will also chair a summit to explore the pressures across the pork supply chain, from farm to fork, and potential solutions to build resilience – including ways to spot market shifts sooner and how to get more people choosing British pork."
