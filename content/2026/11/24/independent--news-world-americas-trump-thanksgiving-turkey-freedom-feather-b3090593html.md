A pair of turkeys called Freedom and Feather will be spared the Thanksgiving table on Tuesday when President Donald Trump carries out the annual White House pardoning.

However, only one of the two birds will receive the ceremonial pardon in person from Trump. The public is invited to vote online to decide which West Virginia native gets that honor. Both turkeys will retire to Texas A&M University, where they will live out their days.

The White House says Tuesday's event is the 79th turkey pardoning, although the ritual only became a yearly fixture under George H.W. Bush's administration in 1989. The pardoning usually offers a brief departure from politics and policy, serving as a light-hearted moment ahead of the quintessential American holiday.

But politics is not always absent from the ceremony. In 2025, the first year of Trump's second term, he spared the turkeys while spending part of the event attacking his political opponents. He said then that the birds should be named Chuck and Nancy, after Democratic leaders Chuck Schumer and Nancy Pelosi.

“I would never pardon those people,” Trump said at the time. The White House Historical Association traces an early story of presidential turkey pardoning to Abraham Lincoln's administration, when he granted a reprieve to a bird destined for Christmas dinner after his son Tad intervened on the gobbler's behalf.

![Two turkeys named Freedom and Feather are set to get Thanksgiving pardons from President Donald Trump on Tuesday](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/24/11/2306519375..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Nearly a century later, in 1954, a photographer captured a smiling Dwight Eisenhower feeding a cranberry to a speckled turkey. In 1963, days before he was fatally shot in Texas, John F. Kennedy pardoned a turkey wearing a sign around his neck that said, “Good Eating, Mr. President.”

Joe Biden relished word-play in 2024 while pardoning Peach and Blossom, two birds from a farm in Minnesota. Peach, Biden said, “lives by the motto, ‘Keep calm and gobble on.’” Blossom has a different motto: “No fowl play. Just Minnesota nice.”
