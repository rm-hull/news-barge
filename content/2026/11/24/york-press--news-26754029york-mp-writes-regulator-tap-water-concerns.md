The drinking water regulator is investigating a chlorine taste and smell in York’s tap water.

Yorkshire Water has said the taste and smell is a result of “some small changes” it made to treat York’s water supply, insisting the water “continues to meet our high drinking water standards”.

But Luke Charters, MP for York Outer, asked the Drinking Water Inspectorate to investigate the issue, which he said remains three months after he reported it to Yorkshire Water.

A spokesperson for the Drinking Water Inspectorate told The Press it has received “complaints from consumers about chlorine taste and odour in York, and Yorkshire Water has reported two events in the area, both of which we are investigating”.

“As these are live investigations, we cannot comment in detail on our findings while we gather and assess the information.”

They said the inspectorate recognised that a “noticeable change in the taste or smell of drinking water can be concerning for consumers”.

"Consumers with concerns about their water should continue to contact Yorkshire Water,” the inspectorate’s spokesperson said.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21050678.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Luke Charters, MP for York Outer *(Image: Supplied)*

Mr Charters wrote to the inspectorate requesting details of inspections and investigations into Yorkshire Water's supply, including checks on chlorine levels following maintenance work.

He has also asked for advice on the safety of the water supply for pets and livestock, after constituents raised concerns.

Mr Charters has asked to meet with the inspectorate to discuss the situation and what further action may be needed.

The Labour MP said his inbox is “full of emails” about concerns over tap water, adding: “People are genuinely worried about their tap water, and frankly, I'm worried too.

"My own water at home has been cloudy and tasted strange, on and off, for months. Just last week, I had an incident where the water had to be flushed out.

“I first raised this with Yorkshire Water back in August, and three months later we're still having the same conversations.

"They've told me chlorine levels are within normal limits, but that doesn't explain why so many people are noticing a difference.

“The lack of answers from Yorkshire Water hasn't reassured anybody. People deserve to know what's going on and whether their water is safe.

“I've now written to the Drinking Water Inspectorate and I'm due to meet them soon. I'm also asking constituents to share their experiences with my office so we can get to the bottom of this.

“I'll keep holding Yorkshire Water to account. In my view, it's urgently time for stronger public control of our region's water. People in York deserve better, and I'm not letting this drop.”

A Yorkshire Water spokesperson said: "We’ve recently made some small changes to the way we treat York’s water supply network and, as a result, some customers may notice a slight difference in the taste or smell of their drinking water.

“Chlorine is an essential part of the water treatment process and has always been used to disinfect drinking water supplied to York. Chlorine concentrations within the York supply area are carefully controlled, continuously monitored and remain within the normal range expected for a public water supply.

“We know that minor changes in the taste or odour of drinking water can be concerning for customers and we investigate concerns raised with us. However, the recent change has not altered the quality or safety of the drinking water supply, which continues to meet our high drinking water standards."
