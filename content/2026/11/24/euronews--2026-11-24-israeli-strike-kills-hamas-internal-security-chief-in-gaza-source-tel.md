## Two policemen died when a police station in southern Gaza was hit, while coalition talks in Israel leave caretaker prime minister Benjamin Netanyahu in charge of military operations.

Israeli airstrikes in the Gaza Strip killed seven people on Tuesday, including Hamas's director of internal security, according to Palestinian officials, despite a ceasefire that has been in place since October 2025.

Gaza's interior ministry, which operates under Hamas authority, said an airstrike in Gaza City killed Brigadier General Naeem Abu Nahal, director-general of the Internal Security Service.

Israel's military said it had "struck a senior terrorist" from Hamas.

Mahmoud Bassal, spokesman for Gaza's civil defence agency, said several other people were in critical condition after the same strike in Gaza City's Rimal neighbourhood.

## **Police station hit**

Bassal said three people, including a 17-year-old, had been killed earlier on Tuesday in a separate Israeli airstrike near Gaza City.

Three more were killed in a third strike, on a police station in the Al-Mawasi area of southern Gaza, he added.

Gaza's police force, which also operates under Hamas, said two policemen aged 23 and 33 were among the dead. It condemned the strike as "part of an ongoing campaign targeting the police apparatus".

Israel's military said it had "struck terrorists in the area" of the police station, but did not confirm the strike that is believed to have killed the 17-year-old.

## **Fragile truce**

Israel and Hamas regularly accuse each other of violating the ceasefire, which came into effect after two years of war triggered by the Hamas-led attack on Israel on 7 October 2023.

More than 1,650 Palestinians have been killed since the truce began, according to a tally based on data from Gaza's health ministry, which also operates under Hamas authority and whose figures are considered reliable by the United Nations.

Over the same period, the Israeli military says seven of its soldiers and one civilian contractor have been killed.

Most of Gaza's 2.1 million people are displaced and living in tents or makeshift shelters. Israel's military says it controls more than 60% of the territory.

## **Coalition talks in Israel**

The strikes come as talks continue in Israel to form a new government after legislative elections in October produced no clear winner.

The centrist Gadi Eisenkot is seeking to build a coalition that would end the long tenure of Benjamin Netanyahu, who is now caretaker prime minister.

But Netanyahu, who has staked his reputation on ensuring Israel's security since the 7 October attack, remains in charge of military operations while negotiations go on.
