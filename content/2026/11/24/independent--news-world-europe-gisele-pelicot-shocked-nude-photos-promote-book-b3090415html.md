Gisèle Pelicot has said that she was left “humiliated” after naked images of her were sent to the media to promote a book.

Ms Pelicot’s ex-husband, Dominique Pelicot, was jailed for 20 years for drugging and raping her and allowing other men to rape her while she was unconscious, in abuse that lasted nearly a decade.

She turned into a global icon against sexual violence when she waived her right to anonymity during the 2024 trial of her former husband and 50 other men involved in the abuse.

French newspaper *Le Canard Enchaîné* reported that Tinbad, a publishing house in Paris, had sent nude photographs of Ms Pelicot, 73, to the media to promote a book authored by philosopher Sabine Prokhoris.

“I am deeply shocked that images from the most intimate part of my private life are being displayed and exploited for commercial purposes,” Ms Pelicot told BFM TV. “I refuse to let my privacy become an advertising tool.”

She added: "Today I find myself once again humiliated, exposed and hurt. As if the ordeal of the trial had not been enough."

Ms Pelicot said she would take legal action against the publisher.

![](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/24/08/2280281131..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Tinbad has apologised for the incident, describing it as an "unfortunate communication error". A spokesperson said the publisher had not sent "any images of rape or any pornographic videos" to the press, but did not deny sending the nude photos.

Prokhoris, a writer who has previously criticised the MeToo movement, said she placed "full responsibility" for the "inappropriate" campaign on her publisher.

AFP reported that the blurb of the book says that coercive control is "a cornerstone of #MeToo orthodoxy".

"These psychological situations, fundamentally ambiguous in nature, involve each of the parties in an equally responsible, though not symmetrical, way," it said.

In December 2024, Dominique Pelicot, and 50 other men were convicted of sexually assaulting Gisèle between 2011 and 2020 while she was under chemical submission. Dominique Pelicot was sentenced to 20 years in prison, while the other defendants received sentences ranging from three to 15 years. An appeals court later increased to 10 years the sentence of the only defendant who challenged his conviction.

![Pelicot became a global icon against sexual violence](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/24/08/2306037038..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Dominique Pelicot, whom Ms Pelicot had been married to for nearly 50 years, acknowledged that for years he mixed sedatives into her food and drink so he could rape her and invite other men to do the same.

In October 2025, France passed a law defining rape and other sexual assault as any non-consensual sexual act in the wake of the Pelicot case, joining many other European nations that have similar consent-based laws, including neighbouring Germany, Belgium and Spain. Until then, rape under French law was defined as penetration or oral sex using “violence, coercion, threat or surprise.”
