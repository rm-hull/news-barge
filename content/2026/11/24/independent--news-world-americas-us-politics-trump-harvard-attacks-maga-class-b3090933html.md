After the Trump administration targeted Harvard with threats to research grants and lawsuits, the Ivy League university has finally hit back, offering students a class on the Make America Great Again movement.

When President Donald Trump returned to the White House in January 2025, his administration began going after higher education over DEI initiatives, antisemitism on campus, foreign funding and international students on campus. However, Harvard has become the 80-year-old president’s biggest target as he frequently complains about the university’s “woke” practices.

After withstanding Trump’s attacks, Harvard’s John F. Kennedy School of Government this fall launched its first for-credit course about MAGA, *The New York Times* reported.

“Make America Great Again: The Ideas Behind the Movement” was a half-semester course this fall with the goal of helping students understand the MAGA phenomenon.

The course was taught by Stephen Richer, a fellow at the Cato Institute, a libertarian public policy group, and a former Republican elected official in Arizona.

![Students at Harvard were able to take a course about the MAGA movement this fall, according to a report](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/24/18/2026-11-02T201318Z_1951589874_RC2VZHAHWBF9_RTRMADP_3_USA-TRUMP-HARVARD.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Richer told the *Times* he sees MAGA as “the most consequential political movement of the 21st century” for its role in U.S. politics and how it has influenced right-of-center groups in other countries.

About 50 students enrolled in the course, which served as a follow-up to a more informal study group Richer led last year on the topic. Students in the class read “The Flight 93 Election,” an essay that characterized the 2016 presidential election in apocalyptic terms, and studied Trump’s 2017 inaugural address.

Richer, who previously was elected as a Republican to the office of Maricopa County Recorder, which oversees voter registration and early voting, saw MAGA’s wrath firsthand following Trump’s claims that the 2020 election was stolen.

While voters overwhelmingly supported Democrats during the November midterm elections, Richer said he doesn’t think the ideas are going away any time soon.

Students in the course told the *Times* that they found value in learning about the MAGA movement, even if they don’t necessarily agree with it.

Isha Bodas, 27, who is pursuing a master's in public policy, said she took the class to engage with other perspectives, after spending much of her life in more liberal spaces.

She said studying “the strongest versions” of MAGA political arguments didn’t change her views, but taught her to become more thorough when thinking about politics.
