The smell of your significant other could become more arousing as your relationship develops, a new study has found.

Researchers in the Netherlands have looked at how people respond to their partner’s natural scent and analysed how this changes as the relationship matures.

The study, which was published in *Archives of Sexual Behaviour* this week, found that people who enjoyed the smell of their partner’s body odour also believed they had greater sexual intimacy.

Scientists from Radboud University analysed a group of 150 men and women who had been in relationships between one month and 37 years.

They were first asked to evaluate their partner based on their appearance, voice, touch, personality, and smell.

![Participants were asked to smell one of their partner’s worn T-shirts](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/24/11/2011422118..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Participants were then made to smell one of their partner’s worn T-shirts and rate its odour based on its pleasantness, intensity, and whether it made them feel safe, soothed or sexually aroused.

Researchers found participants who enjoyed their partner’s smell also reported greater sexual intimacy. There was a stronger effect for people who were in longer-term relationships.

The study said the findings suggested there was “a statistically significant but subtle influence of odour evaluations on sexual intimacy”.

Participants who liked their partner’s smell and were in average-length or longer relationships also reported a greater sense of emotional safety, the study said.

![People who like their partner’s smell may be more likely to report greater sexual intimacy, the study claims](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/24/11/2175841108..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Meanwhile, people’s evaluations of their partner’s looks, voice, personality, and competence did not change after smelling the T-shirt.

The study added: “Although the correlation analyses showed that odour pleasantness was associated with relationship satisfaction and emotional intimacy as well, these associations did not hold when controlling for the pleasantness of the other partner traits.”

The researchers noted the size of the effect and the test group warranted some caution, and did not determine cause and effect.

It concluded: “While evaluations of a partner’s odour may be less impactful than personality or physical appearance for general relationship satisfaction and emotional intimacy, it plays a unique role in sexual intimacy, highlighting the importance of sensory experiences in romantic connections.

“Our findings indicate that a partner’s scent enhances intimacy both directly and indirectly, especially as relationships deepen. By strengthening mechanisms of safety and sexual arousal that foster connection in long-term bonds through repeated, positive associations, body odours play a fundamental role in enhancing relationship chemistry.”
