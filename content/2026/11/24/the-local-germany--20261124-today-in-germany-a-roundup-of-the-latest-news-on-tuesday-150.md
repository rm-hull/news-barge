**Tuesday's top story: Budget week begins in the Bundestag**

For four days starting Tuesday, Germany's parliamentarians will debate the budgets for each individual ministry for the coming year one more time.

When Finance Minister Lars Klingbeil takes the podium on Tuesday morning, the budget is technically still not yet finalized. The last-minute compromise to reduce the electricity tax for consumers and businesses still needs to be incorporated.

The first order of business will be Klingbeil defending the proposed budget as it is, including all of the recently approved changes.

Then the individual budgets of the other 15 ministries, from the Digital Department to the Ministry of Transport should be discussed over the coming days.

Finally, on Friday afternoon, the budget for 2027 should be approved by parliament.

There will be a special debate on Wednesday morning during the discussions on the Chancellery's budget.

The so-called 'general debate' is traditionally opened by the opposition leader – in this case the AfD – then the Chancellor responds. The general debate is not only about money, but about the entire government policy.

**German politicians voice alarm over reports of Russian nuclear weapons in space**

German media reports by WDR, NDR and the *Süddeutsche Zeitung* suggested that the Federal Intelligence Service (BND) and the Bundeswehr believe Russia could launch a satellite carrying a nuclear warhead within two months. This could potentially destroy other satellites, and the electromagnetic pulse could also damage equipment on Earth.

Politicians from both the coalition and the opposition have voiced serious alarm.

Siemtje Möller, deputy chair of the SPD parliamentary group, said that if the reports are confirmed it would be "a dangerous breach of a taboo with significant consequences for international security".

She warned it would drive the militarisation of space and endanger military and civilian satellites, on which daily life, the economy and security depend.

Desiree Becker, the Left Party's spokesperson on peace and disarmament, called such a step "another blow to international arms control".

She added that the US has also announced it has deployed weapons systems in space, though what they are is unknown.

Asked about the German media reports, NATO chief Mark Rutte said Monday the alliance would defend against threats in space.

![Merz sternly looks at NATO leader Mark Rutte as they greet each other in front of the Chancelry.](https://apiwp.thelocal.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/AFP__20261123__D8D87BM__v1__MidRes__GermanyNatoDiplomacyDefence.jpg)

"We will always make sure that we defend every inch of NATO territory, and that includes making sure that we are safe when it comes to the space element," Rutte said.

German Chancellor Friedrich Merz, speaking alongside Rutte, did not confirm or deny the media reports but voiced concern.

"Reports that Russia is planning to introduce nuclear weaponry into space are not new, yet they remain no less shocking," he said.

**Tech start-up investment booms in Germany**

Investment in cutting-edge German start-ups, from robotics to defence technology, is forecast to almost triple this year to €8.1 billon, according to a study published on Monday.

The study, by the German Start-up Association and the Joachim Herz Foundation, said the figure compares with just €2.9 billon last year. It called the surge a "major success for Germany as a business location".

The boom was driven by a handful of mega funding rounds, including for Neura Robotics, AI drone maker Helsing and defence tech group Quantum Systems.

About 300 new tech start-ups are expected to be founded this year, up 40 percent on 2025, mostly clustered around Berlin, Munich and Hamburg.

Still, major challenges in the sector remain: Only 28 percent of capital has come from domestic investors, and support is weaker when it comes to taking innovations from lab to market.

**Five dead in suspected gas leak in western Germany**

Two adults and three children have been found dead in a suspected gas leak in western Germany, police said on Monday.

![Investigators in hazmat suits stand by a police car next to an apartment building that is taped off.](https://apiwp.thelocal.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/AFP__20261123__D8D23GW__v1__MidRes__GermanyAccidentPolice.jpg)

The incident occurred in a flat in the border town of Kehl, across the Rhine River from the French city of Strasbourg.

Police in nearby Offenburg said the apartment belonged to a family with several children. Two children were treated by emergency services.

Officers were called to the scene at around 11:45am, alerted by the children's absence from class, according to the police statement.

The regional Badische Zeitung newspaper reported that carbon monoxide was detected at the scene, citing fire brigade official Viktor Liehr.

Other flats in the block were checked by the emergency services, with police confirming that there was no longer any danger.

**German president disappointed with outcome of COP 31**

Federal President Frank-Walter Steinmeier regretted the failure of the World Climate Conference (COP 31) in Turkey.

"This is disappointing, especially in a year when, I believe, climate change has become more pronounced in Europe than ever before," Steinmeier said during his visit to Ivory Coast in Abidjan.

"It is clear that we must act. And it is also clear that we can only take a global path to greater climate protection and combat climate change together," Steinmeier emphasized.

The climate conference ended without the states being able to agree on a final document. Many countries refused to support the deal that had been proposed because it did not include fossil fuel reductions.

Speaking about the conference's failure, Green Party leader Franziska Brantner accused the federal government of a lack of commitment.

Brantner said before the federal executive board meetings in Berlin that neither Germany nor the EU had been sufficiently involved on the ground.

The Greens had already criticized that Chancellor Friedrich Merz was not present at the climate conference. Brantner said that in Germany, dependencies on fossil fuels must be reduced instead of continuing to rely on gas and oil.

"That is the order of the day," he said, yet the federal government has weakened the heating law.

Brantner said setbacks were looming in the expansion of renewable energies, referring to reforms planned by the government to the Renewable Energy Act.
