Democrat Victoria Broderick has won Tennessee’s fourth congressional district, unseating eight-term Republican incumbent Scott DesJarlais in one of the biggest House upsets of the cycle. The victory expands Democrats’ historic House majority in the 120th Congress to 259 seats, the party’s largest advantage in three decades, and flips a seat that Donald Trump carried by more than 20 points in 2024.

Broderick, 34, a former technology and logistics professional who previously ran for Congress in 2024, told the Guardian in an interview before her race was called that she connected with constituents who had not voted in years and met Republicans concerned about affordability who appreciated the “integrity” she brought to the race.

“We’ve been neglected in this district,” she said earlier this month. “People want representatives who understand what they are going through, who understand the costs of going to the gas station or grocery store. I’m that representative.”

Tennessee’s congressional map was redrawn as part of the GOP’s mid-decade gerrymandering effort before this year’s midterm elections, and election forecasters had rated the district as “solidly Republican”. Yet Broderick prevailed by fewer than 400 votes, according to the Associated Press race call. DesJarlais, 62, who aligned himself closely with Trump during his time in office, conceded to Broderick last week after the razor-thin contest but the race was officially called on Tuesday.

“We all leave here with our heads held high, and we hope the Fourth District continues to receive the attention and care it so richly deserves,” DesJarlais wrote in a statement. “All of us hope that the new Member of Congress feels the same level of awe and gratitude for this tremendous undertaking that I felt every day I had the privilege of serving.”

DesJarlais’ stunning defeat comes despite significantly out-fundraising Broderick in the run-up to the election and with many national Democrats dismissing her campaign as a long shot. Broderick will now be the sole Democrat in Tennessee’s congressional delegation on Capitol Hill, underscoring the scale of an upset few in either party saw coming.

Broderick said that she managed to pull off the upset without support from the Democratic Congressional Campaign Committee, the party’s House campaign arm. “The Democratic party wasn’t paying attention to this race,” she said. “I knew I was the underdog.”

A day after the election, on 4 November, Broderick had already declared victory. “We did not have the political machine behind us. We didn’t have corporate Pac money, we didn’t have powerful insiders. We had you, the people,” she wrote on social media. “That turns out that is all we needed.”

DesJarlais, a physician, was also dogged by controversy during his time in office, particularly after it was revealed that he urged his ex-wife to have two abortions before their marriage despite his anti-abortion stance. Divorce trial transcripts also showed that he admitted to sexual relationships with patients.
