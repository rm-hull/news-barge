Amelia Dimoldenberg, the host of Chicken Shop Date, has said she decided to end the popular YouTube series which had increasingly left her feeling “isolated” and made her search for real relationships more challenging.

The comedian, 32, also revealed she had questioned the future of the interview series after suffering two panic attacks, saying she wanted to end it “before I start resenting it”.

Dimoldenberg announced in September that Chicken Shop Date was concluding, with the final episode due to be released on YouTube on Friday.

The presenter began the series aged 17 as a magazine column before transitioning to YouTube in 2014, interviewing celebrities in local chicken shops with her unconventional questions and deadpan silences.

Dimoldenberg wrote in The Cut magazine: “When you are a child who desires fame, you are really a child who yearns for connection.

“I thought the more famous I became, the more likely it was [that] someone would fall in love with me.

“If anything, playing a decade-long role of the serial dater has made my real dating life challenging.

“People often meet you with a preconceived idea of who you are, their curiosity misplaced. I am so proud of my success, but it has isolated me in some ways.”

Dimoldenberg said when she first started the show in 2014, initially interviewing figures from the UK rap and grime scene, part of her believed she could fall in love with someone on-camera, which she believes “grew into a delusion”.

She wrote: “Why wouldn’t a hot celebrity want to be with me? I wondered. I started this show when I was a teenager, so of course I’ve had crushes on guests.

“I have gone through every emotion a twentysomething woman might experience: fear that they might not like me back, overthinking if and how I should reach out to them again, wondering if they will be at the same glamorous awards show.”

She added: “To actually date a guest would have been transgressive and implausible in this constructed reality.

“Nevertheless, this belief has been what kept me going and I think kept the audience believing too. Now that the prophecy has gone unfulfilled, a new one must take shape.”

The presenter’s interviewees have included Ed Sheeran and Jennifer Lawrence. Paul McCartney turned up for a date in a vegan chicken shop, declining Dimoldenberg’s suggestion that he adopt a pet otter.

The comedian also spoke of the panic attacks she has experienced.

She wrote: “I knew my perspective on Chicken Shop Date was changing around 18 months ago, when I had my first and then second panic attack ever in the space of a week.”

Dimoldenberg continued: “I had stopped drinking at the time because I was trying to pinpoint exactly why I felt so unlike myself.

“All of my emotions felt so much more visceral; I figured that’s what happens when you cut out a sedative.”

Dimoldenberg told the Edinburgh TV festival earlier this year she is writing a Hollywood romcom, in which she will star as a journalist who finds romance with a celebrity after interviewing them.
