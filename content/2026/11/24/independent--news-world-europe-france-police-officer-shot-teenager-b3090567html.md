A French police officer is set to stand trial for murder in the killing of a teenager in 2023 that triggered huge protests across the country, an appeals court said on Tuesday.

Officer Florian M shot 17-year-old Nahel Merzouk in the Paris suburb of Nanterre on June 27, 2023, after the teenager didn’t comply with an order to stop his car following allegations that he’d been driving dangerously.

He later died from his wounds.

The officer’s charge was originally lowered to voluntary assault leading to death earlier this year, which carries a lesser maximum prison sentence.

However, following a successful appeal to France’s highest court, the Versailles court re-examined the case and a judge ruled there was sufficient evidence for him to stand trial for murder.

![People hold a banner that reads ‘Justice pour Nahel 27/06/2023’ during a march against police impunity](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/24/11/2268125384..?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&auto=webp)

Under French law, the charge carries a maximum sentence of 30 years in prison, whereas the previous charge carries a maximum sentence of 15 years.

The officer’s lawyer Laurent-Franck Lienard told local press that his client would appeal against the decision again.

Yasmina Belmokhtar, a lawyer for Merzouk’s family, said she would not comment on the news until she had read the decision in full.

Merzouk’s death unleashed violent protests in Nanterre that spread and morphed into nationwide riots after footage of an officer firing his weapon as the driver pulled away circulated on social media.

Human rights groups have long accused the ‌French police ​of systemic racism, and those accusations ‌were at the heart ​of the unrest.

Protestors who gathered en masse with posters denouncing police violence demanded tougher rules limiting the use of firearms by police, along with higher investment in low-income neighbourhoods and an independent body to replace the internal agency tasked with investigating police abuses.

The French government has denied systemic racism or brutality by police.

More recent protests have filled the streets in France as demonstrators rebel against high living costs and worsening public services and education services in the country. The demonstrations have triggered the worst civil unrest France has seen in years.

Earlier this month, Education minister Edouard Geffray condemned the “criminal behaviour” after people threw fireworks that started a large fire at a high school near the city of Toulouse in the southwest.
