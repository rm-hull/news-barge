A senior officer who acted as commanding officer to Sarah Everard’s killer has been jailed for over 10 years for raping a sleeping woman.

Former chief inspector Glen Mahony, 54, was convicted of two counts of rape and one count of sexual assault targeting a woman at his home in Rochester, Kent.

Kent Police, the investigating force, confirmed that one rape and a sexual assault on another occasion were carried out while the victim was asleep.

Mahony was a serving Civil Nuclear Constabulary (CNC) chief inspector at the time of his arrest and was later found to have posted sexist messages in two WhatsApp groups alongside six fellow officers.

He was handed a ten-and-a-half-year sentence at Maidstone Crown Court on Tuesday and placed on the sex offenders register for life, according to Kent Police.

Detective Chief Inspector Tracy Evans, from Kent Police, said: "To take advantage of a woman while she is asleep and at her most vulnerable is utterly disgraceful, and to then deny it happened and force the victim to relive her ordeal during a trial makes things even worse.

![Mahony acted as commanding officer to Wayne Couzens (Metropolitan Police/PA)](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/05/15/12/9046ec90c12f270af5da5aa70f5b1c95Y29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzc4OTI4NTk1-2.75485426.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

"His behaviour was vile and he will now serve a significant period of time behind bars where he can cause no further harm to his victim or any other woman who may have crossed his path in the future."

Mahony served alongside Wayne Couzens for around four years within a small CNC unit based at the Dungeness power station in Kent.

Couzens held the rank of police constable while Mahony was an inspector tasked with overseeing operations and acting as his commanding officer.

Mahony earned a promotion to chief inspector around the period Couzens transferred to the Metropolitan Police in 2018.

Following his arrest by Kent Police on 29 March 2022, Mahony was immediately suspended by the CNC, a force comprising roughly 1,800 armed officers responsible for protecting nuclear sites across Scotland and England.

He resigned a day prior to his CNC misconduct hearing in January this year, where a panel concluded he would have been dismissed for gross misconduct had he still been serving.

The six officers implicated in the WhatsApp group chats faced misconduct meetings, a lower tier than misconduct hearings.

Each was issued a written warning, with no requirement for case details to be published.

Chief Constable Simon Chesterman, of the CNC, described Mahony’s crimes as "despicable" and "the most serious sexual violence to a vulnerable female victim, who was not able to consent".

He added: "My immediate thoughts are with his victim, who must endure the impact of his crimes for the rest of her life.

![Mahony was sentenced at Maidstone Crown Court (Tom Pugh/PA)](https://static.independent.co.uk/2024/11/08/16/9a5e9084413830304d8376fd6bd5bdf7Y29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzMxMTY4MDg4-2.12313461.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

"I commend her for the bravery she has displayed in supporting Kent Police and in giving evidence to the court to secure this conviction."

He said Mahony's actions were "entirely contrary to the high standards" required of a CNC officer and "go on to undermine the trust and confidence that the public and particularly women have in the police service".

Mr Chesterman added: "On his arrest, the CNC immediately suspended Mahony. Subsequently, Kent Police also passed to us evidence of police misconduct to investigate, associated with WhatsApp groups that Mahony had been a member.

"In these groups, Mahony sent sexist and misogynistic messages, and he did not challenge or leave the group when others sent similarly offensive messages. This further underlines his disgusting opinion of women.

"Mahony will have no place in policing again", he said.

Detective Chief Inspector Evans said Mahony’s victim "displayed tremendous courage in coming forward to report the terrible offences committed against her".

"I am pleased that with her support we were able to prove beyond all reasonable doubt that he was guilty and will now have to face the consequences."
