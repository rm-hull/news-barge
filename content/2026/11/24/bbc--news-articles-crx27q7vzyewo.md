**A nephew of twin brothers killed by a man suffering a psychotic episode who was released from custody hours before says he has little confidence similar cases could be prevented.**

The deaths of Roger and Dick Carter, both 84, and Anthony Payne, 80, in Exeter by Alexander Lewis-Ranwell on 10 February 2019 followed a catalogue of failings by police, healthcare professionals and G4S-employed staff, an inquest jury found.

Alex Carter told BBC Radio 4's Today programme that "any number of people could have said releasing this guy is not right".

"What I want to see is visible improvements," he said. "I want somebody to tell me this has happened. I want to be able to see it or hear it."

![Anthony Payne is holding a white and brown cat standing in th emiddle of what looks like a cleared allotment. He is weaning blue trousers and a brown jumper with a red top underneath.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/e7fc/live/ca52ae20-e806-11f1-95bb-3f37d3e026b0.jpg)

Anthony Payne was also killed by Alexander Lewis-Ranwell in Exeter in February 2019

The jury found Lewis-Ranwell, who had a history of paranoid schizophrenia, was released from custody without a Mental Health Act (MHA) assessment despite warnings about his deteriorating mental state.

Within hours, he killed Mr Payne and later the Carter brothers.

Carter said the inquest had revealed more problems than he had expected and he remained unconvinced enough had changed since 2019, despite hearing during the inquest that some improvements had been introduced.

He also said agencies should examine in particular how information was recorded, shared and acted upon.

He said: "Police, Devon County Council, G4S have all got to look at the processes that are in place because it fell down in so many different places."

He added: "I'm not seeking apologies. Apologies don't mean anything to me.

"We've gone through this really lengthy process. If nothing changes, then this will happen for nothing."

Alexander Lewis-Ranwell was arrested twice in the days before the killings

The inquests heard evidence of the then 28-year-old Lewis-Ranwell's interactions with various health professionals and police officers during his arrests between 8 and 10 February, which included:

* After his first arrest, on 8 February, Lewis-Ranwell's mother phoned Barnstaple police station saying she would be "extremely concerned should he be released, not just for the violence and the way he was but for his own safety". He was charged and bailed in the early hours of 9 February
* Following his second arrest for attacking a farmer with a saw later on 9 February, one custody officer described Lewis-Ranwell as "one of the most mentally unwell individuals I can remember encountering"
* Forensic medical examiner Dr Mihail Pichiu - a doctor employed by the now defunct G4S Health Service - deemed Lewis-Ranwell was not "acutely unwell" and did not require an MHA assessment.

Lewis-Ranwell was released from Barnstaple police station at about 09:30 on 10 February. He travelled to Exeter, where about three hours later he killed Payne with a hammer at his home on Bonhay Road.

Less than three hours after that, he scaled the wall of the Carter brothers' home in Cowick Lane, took a spade from the garden and killed them both with it.

Lewis-Ranwell, who had a history of paranoid schizophrenia and had been sectioned under the Mental Health Act twice before, was arrested again on 11 February following an incident at an Exeter hotel, the inquests heard.

This time, an MHA assessment was completed and he was found to be acutely psychotic.

Lewis-Ranwell was charged with murdering Payne and the Carters, but found not guilty by reason of insanity. He was given a direct hospital order.

Coroner Philip Spinney said he would be considering writing Prevention of Future Deaths reports.

Such reports are sent when a coroner thinks action is needed to protect lives.

Devon and Cornwall Police said its Professional Standards Department liaised with the Independent Office for Police Conduct (IOPC) on 13 February 2019 and requested that the matter be considered as a voluntary referral.

The force also said: "Areas of learning and change identified during the inquest and prior reviews are being, and have been, addressed."

An IOPC spokesperson said: "We received a complaint referral from Devon and Cornwall Police in March 2020, in relation to the contact the force had with Alexander Lewis-Ranwell prior to the deaths of three men, in Exeter, in February 2019.

"After assessing all the available information provided by the force, we decided that an investigation was required and it was appropriate for the investigation to be carried out by Devon and Cornwall's Professional Standards Department.

"The complainant would have had the right of review to the IOPC should they have been dissatisfied with the investigation outcome.

"To date, we have not received a request to carry out a review."

G4S Health Services no longer operates. A G4S spokesperson said: "We offer our condolences to the families affected by these tragic events."

The Devon Partnership NHS Trust said: "We would like to extend our sympathies to the families of Richard and Roger Carter and Anthony Payne.

"Our organisation will reflect on the jury's conclusions and we remain committed to continuous learning and ensuring that improvements are embedded in our systems and practice."
