The Prince of Wales held talks with Prime Minister Andy Burnham at 10 Downing Street on Tuesday morning, in what is believed to be the first visit to the address by a member of the royal family in 14 years.

William was welcomed outside Number 10's famous black front door by Mr Burnham as the pair met to discuss shared priorities, including tackling homelessness.

It comes a day after the prime minister backed *The Independent*’s Christmas appeal, which will help to lift young people out of homelessness by transforming some of the UK’s empty buildings into safe and comfortable homes.

In an impassioned article, Mr Burnham urged the country to “put hope in the hearts” of Britain’s disadvantaged young people by getting behind the Project Nest appeal, which has been launched in conjunction with the charity EveryYouth.

The meeting between William and Mr Burnham has been billed as the latest example of the evolution of the future king's public life, marking another step in his role as a future head of state.

![William meets Andy Burnham outside 10 Downing Street](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/24/12/2306135237..?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&auto=webp)

It is believed that no royal has called at Downing Street since Queen Elizabeth II attended a cabinet meeting there during her Diamond Jubilee in 2012.

The late Queen also dined there with Sir Winston Churchill on the eve of his resignation in 1955, among other visits.

William's meeting – described as a bilateral – was to discuss issues such as homelessness, rather than being a social or celebratory engagement.

He launched his Homewards initiative in 2023 – a five-year project aimed at making homelessness “rare, brief and unrepeated”.

![Charles and Diana, the then-Prince and Princess of Wales, at 10 Downing Street with Margaret Thatcher and husband Denis in 1989](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/24/11/54627dd1789610a1da380c185057ddf1Y29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzk1NTQxNTk0-2.1245215.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Mr Burnham pledged to end rough sleeping in his first speech as prime minister.

The visit follows William's bilateral talks with Indian prime minister Narendra Modi in Delhi last week, at the request of the British government, during his Earthshot tour with the Princess of Wales.

The prince met Mr Burnham at the Omani state visit in October.

The two men had also met previously, when the now-premier was mayor of Greater Manchester, during a visit to the Hideaway Youth Project in Moss Side in 2023.

In January 2025, the prince held a meeting with the then-prime minister Sir Keir Starmer at Kensington Palace.

![Sir Winston Churchill welcomes Queen Elizabeth II and the Duke of Edinburgh to Downing Street in 1955](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/24/11/db5e2204293501f073ed4499eb99660bY29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzk1NTQxMjQ2-2.1226414.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

It is a convention for a future head of state to hold an annual meeting with the prime minister, and William had been due to have his first official one with Mr Burnham, royal sources said.

Senior members of the royal family, including the reigning monarch, have often visited the prime minister's official residence, posing in front of the front door, as part of what is described as the longstanding constitutional relationship between the sovereign and the prime minister.

William's father King Charles, as the previous Prince of Wales, went to Downing Street numerous times, including with the then-Princess of Wales, Diana, for a dinner with Margaret Thatcher in 1989.

The prince almost went to Number 10 as an unborn baby.

![The then-Duke of York and Business Secretary Peter Mandelson at a Downing Street reception in 2009](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/24/11/2a86f9afa1308e11bb8a8c55624fc24fY29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzk1NjAzNDU0-2.7277886.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

When William's mother Diana was nearly five months pregnant with him in 1982, she visited 11 Downing Street, the chancellor's residence next door, for a British Film Institute reception.

In 2009, the then-Duke of York, now the disgraced Andrew Mountbatten-Windsor, was pictured in his role as the UK's trade envoy with Lord Peter Mandelson, then the Business Secretary, attending a reception in 10 Downing Street to coincide with the opening South Hook Liquefied Natural Gas Terminal in Milford Haven.

Both Andrew and Lord Mandelson were arrested in February this year on suspicion of misconduct in public office over their connections to paedophile Jeffrey Epstein.
