# Football pitches could be created at miners’ welfare ground hit by flooding

## Football pitches could be created at a miners’ welfare ground that was hit by flooding after a village colliery closed.

Plans have been drawn up for five new pitches to boost junior sports facilities in Allerton Bywater.

Leeds City Council is working with environmental charity Groundwork on plans for the site on Station Road.

A planning report said a newly-formed junior football club would benefit if planning permission is approved.

![Allerton Bywater Miners' Welfare Ground](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOmQzMGJhYTJiLTIxNTgtNDAyZC1iY2Y4LWFlNjA5MmE1ZjBkZTphYTFmMzdjMC02ODkxLTQwZjctOWI1Yy1hNmYwMGZkZjVmOWU=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


It said: “Despite its rural setting, Allerton Bywater has a shortage of formal junior sports pitches, and demand has grown sharply following a resurgence in youth participation.”

The report said attempts to re-instate the pitches in the late 1980s and early 2000s were unsuccessful due to an increase in flooding.

But work by the Environment Agency and the council’s Flood Risk Management team had reduced the risk of excess surface water.

The project would involve the clearance of overgrown vegetation, levelling the ground and installing new drainage.

The report said: “Historically, the miners’ welfare ground offered a vibrant mix of recreational uses including football, cricket, bowls and tennis.

“However, following the closure of the local pit, the site fell into under-use and the football pitches became overgrown.”

Parish and city councillors, along with community groups, worked together to find space for additional pitches in the village.

Their longer-term aim was to bring existing sports facilities together as part of an integrated Allerton Bywater Sports Club.

The report said: “There are currently 150 junior members with no clear means of progression and the club is danger of losing these members if no infrastructure can be provided.

“The club currently trains on the cricket outfield, which is not sustainable in the long term.”

A full planning application is being considered after a public consultation found there was strong support for the project.

The report said: “The junior football club have carried extensive consultation, contacting over 300 people locally.

“This has gone out to via Facebook to over 600 people and out to wider community groups totalling over 3,000 members. There has not been one negative comment to date.”
