Numerous schools across Germany received bomb threats on Monday. According to police, 14 schools in Berlin received threatening emails, while in Brandenburg, the state police headquarters reported that 17 schools were affected. Schools in Potsdam, Oberhavel, Havelland, Dahme-Spreewald, and Barnim were among those targeted.

The police, who were informed around 9:30 a.m., do not believe the threats pose any danger. There was no evidence of any specific threats or dangers to the schools contacted or to any other schools. The Berlin police were in close contact and communication with the affected schools and the responsible Senate Department, according to a press release issued Monday afternoon.

The majority of affected schools returned to regular operations later that day, the state education ministry announced. Brandenburg’s Education Minister Gordon Hoffmann (CDU) advised schools to act prudently in such cases and to take the police’s assessments into account.

The police asked people not to spread the threats further. “Don’t let those responsible succeed in spreading fear and terror among our children and young people.”

Schools in many other German states and cities also received emails containing threats and announcements of an attack. There, too, the respective police authorities assumed there was no real danger. Hundreds of schools nationwide are said to be affected.

In recent weeks, there have been repeated mass bomb threats against schools in other European countries. In Italy, more than 6,000 schools were evacuated in a single day as a result.
