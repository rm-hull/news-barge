In a letter to the mayor, the council says it remains opposed to the introduction of a tourist levy in York and North Yorkshire

**North Yorkshire Council has said it opposes the introduction of a tourist tax for visitors staying in the county, with its concerns only increasing as plans for the levy develop.**

In a letter to Mayor of York and North Yorkshire David Skaith, the authority's deputy leader Mark Crane said the option of not introducing such a tax "must remain genuinely open".

Crane said his main concern was the potential for a "percentage-based levy with no national ceiling" to be introduced, which he said many in the tourism sector found "particularly difficult and unfair".

In response, Skaith said he was "committed" to addressing any concerns before a decision about a tourist tax was made.

Crane's letter comes after the Visitor Levy (England) Bill, which would give regional mayors the power to create a tourist tax on overnight stays, was introduced in parliament earlier this month.

Meanwhile, in September a report commissioned by Skaith recommended imposing a £1 per night tourist tax across York and North Yorkshire - a move which the report claimed could raise £26m for the region each year, which could be ring-fenced for tourism-related purposes.

## 'Concerns increased'

Crane's letter stated that the announcement of legislation "does not establish the case for imposing another charge on visitors and another administrative burden on businesses already under considerable pressure".

"Our concerns have increased, not diminished, as the proposals have developed," it said.

Despite the council's opposition, it would "engage constructively" to protect businesses and communities, the letter said.

"Our engagement should not be mistaken for support or acceptance that a levy is inevitable," the letter added.

In response, a spokesperson for Skaith said: "Residents across York and North Yorkshire have to live with, and pay for, the additional pressures that the high number of visitors place on our county's public services.

"It is only fair to ask them to contribute towards those costs and enable us to invest in our towns, cities and villages to make them even more attractive to tourists."

The spokesperson added that a full public consultation and impact assessment would be carried out and engagement with the tourism sector would continue.

"The mayor is not content with the continued lack of investment into the things that matter most to residents, like transport and making our high streets the best they can be," they said.

"He will always put our county, and its residents, first."
