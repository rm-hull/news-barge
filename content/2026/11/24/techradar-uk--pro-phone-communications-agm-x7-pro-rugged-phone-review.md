## AGM X7 Pro: 30-second review

The X7 Pro is the thermal imaging model in AGM's X series. It sits above the standard X7, which has no thermal camera. But the price difference because of that one feature is significant.

The headline feature is a 512 x 384 thermal sensor running at 25fps, although SGM doesn’t say what the sensor is, or if that resolution is native. It reads temperatures from minus 20 to 550 degrees Celsius. If the resolution is native, that’s amazing, but it’s more likely that it uses something lower spec and uses multiple passes to increase the resolution. Whatever it does, the apparent resolution is well above what many budget thermal modules use.

Around that, it's a competent but conventional rugged phone. The MediaTek Dimensity 7300 is a sensible mid-range chip. The 6.72-inch screen runs at 120Hz. The 10,000mAh battery should last days, and it charges at 33W wired or 15W wireless.

If all this seems remarkably similar, this is effectively the same phone as the AGM G3 Pro, but with less RAM and storage. And it costs $100 less because of those limitations.

This is a tool first and a phone second. For electricians, plumbers, inspectors and builders, that is exactly what they might need. But for everyone else, it is an expensive way to buy a good rugged phone, though not as costly as the G3 Pro. Since this is mostly a rehashed design, it probably won’t make it into our best rugged phone collection.

## AGM X7 Pro: price and availability

* **How much does it cost?** $529/£399/€599
* **When is it out?** Available now
* **Where can you get it?** You can get it in most regions directly from AGM or from many online retailers.

AGM lists the X7 Pro at €599 on its European site. US pricing is $599 on Amazon, and AliExpress lists it at around $563. But if you are in the US, you can get this phone directly from the makers for only $529. And, as a special offer, you also get some AGM Buds headphones included.

The value case rests on the thermal camera. Strip that feature out and you are paying $529 for 6GB of RAM, 256GB of storage and a mid-range SoC. The standard X7 does most of that for $399.

## AGM X7 Pro: design

* **Large, but not impractical**
* **Identical to G3 Pro layout**
* **Wireless charging**

The X7 Pro looks like a rugged phone and doesn't apologise for it. A reinforced frame surrounds the display, the back is textured, and the camera cluster sits in a circular module, with the thermal sensor placed below the main lenses.

This is all unchanged from the G3 Pro, with the exception of a few styling differences on the back, which you can see in my comparison photo.

A giveaway to this phone's origins is that all the camera positions, including the thermal sensor, match those on the G3 Pro.

![AGM X7 Pro rugged camera with thermal sensor](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/o9mC8UVhtgEXDN3W6TxSSh-1200-80.jpg)

At 16mm thick and 365g, it is not slim by normal phone standards. It is lighter than many rugged rivals with this much battery, though. AGM calls the design slim, which is generous, at best. Compared with the 11.9mm WP68 Air, it is a brick, some might say.

The side button can launch an app, open the camera, or switch on the torch, among other functions. That is a good use of a physical control, and I'd like to see more makers do it rather than welding it to some push-to-talk function.

There is also a camping light and a 5W speaker. Both are practical on a job site, though the light is relatively modest compared to some rugged phones, and it's about 40% the size of the one on the G3 Pro.

What also carried over from the G3 Pro is wireless charging. Many rugged makers still skip it, which I have never understood. AGM includes it here at 15W, and the flat-ish rear panel supports it properly.

Overall, the phone that spawned this one wasn’t a poor layout, and this one doesn’t make sweeping changes to the concept.

## AGM X7 Pro: hardware

* **Common Dimensity 7300 SoC**
* **Workable screen**
* **Less RAM and storage**

The Dimensity 7300 is a good fit for this phone. It is built on a 4nm process, and it uses A78 and A55 cores with an ARM Mali G615 GPU.

That matters because I have complained about the Dimensity 7025 repeatedly. That chip is a 6nm design from 2022, and its PowerVR GPU struggles with OpenGL and Vulkan. The 7300 has none of those problems, as it is a full generation ahead.

This phone has the same display as its G3 Pro predecessor, an IPS panel with a decent resolution of 2400 x 1080. That’s enough for 1080p video playback, and the 120Hz refresh results in a slick interface experience. The biggest issue with this display is outside, where the glossy surface tends to reflect the sky, so a textured screen protector might be a solid purchase for it.

The weak spot is memory. 6GB of RAM is modest by today's standards, and AGM adds 6GB of virtual RAM to make the number look better. That is storage pretending to be memory. Ignore it.

Storage is a healthy 256GB, and it supports microSD cards if you need more.

The battery is the real highlight. 10,000mAh is a big step up from the 8,000mAh in the WP68 Air. AGM claims several days of use, even with heavy thermal camera work.

Charging is quick enough at 33W. Wireless charging at 15W is slower, but handy at a desk.

I’d contest that this phone has enough battery for at least three working days, and maybe some more if you curate it well. But it wouldn’t last a whole week away from civilisation, if that’s where you want to take it.

## AGM X7 Pro: cameras

* **Rear camera:** 64MP Sony IMX682 primary camera, Camera, 2MP BYD BF2257 Macro Camera, Thermal Sensor
* **Front camera:** 50MP Samsung S5KJN1

![AGM X7 Pro rugged camera with thermal sensor](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/z7am6g8tHTb99gUK47R4Sh-1200-80.jpg)

I talked previously about the normal photography capability of these sensors in my G3 Pro review, so I won’t repeat all that here. Safe to say the 64MP rear Sony IMX682 can take some decent pictures, even if it only offers digital zooming. The macro camera is junk, in case you wondered.

The thermal camera is the reason to own this phone. With a resolution of 512 x 384, with a 25fps frame rate, this all sounds excellent. The temperature range runs from minus 20 to 550 degrees Celsius, so that covers most requirements unless you like to forge steel.

It doesn’t work with the standard Android camera app; instead, it uses a dedicated Thermal AGM app that looks eerily similar to the ones Thermal Master uses on their thermal add-on cameras.

In my G3 Pro review, I theorised that there is only one chip that can do this in common use, and it’s the one used in the InfiRay P2 Pro, which has a resolution of 256 x 192 pixels, and has a thermal range of -20°C to 550°C. Since I wrote that review, nobody has come to me and told me I’m wrong, and given how keen people are to say that to reviewers, I stand by that assessment.

AGM claims a 512 x 384 super resolution, which hints that it’s an interpolation or multi-sample from the real resolution, but most thermal products tend to do that so that images don’t look like 8-bit games.

One thing I noticed using it is that while the image updates relatively quickly at 25 fps, the overlays where temperatures are displayed update less often.

Another issue that I noticed is that the better phones with this feature make sure that the thermal sensor and the primary camera are close together, and that the normal light view is calibrated to be zoomed appropriately for those images to be combined.

This sensor has that feature, but unless the object being captured is at least 10 or 20ft away, the positional sync isn’t good. You can move one of the images manually, in an attempt to overlay them, but if the subject is close, that doesn’t work well.

And, that’s the most important takeaway about the thermal camera, which is that it's good for boiler installations and larger scenarios, but it's not ideal for small things like electronics.

I think this would have been better had the Sony primary sensor and the thermal one been closer, but I’m sure the engineers who designed this had other issues to contend with.

![AGM X7 Pro rugged camera with thermal sensor](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/ADLcP5iTpxm4qnXeXq6xRh-1200-80.jpg)

Compared to professional dedicated thermal cameras, one in a phone is always going to be a compromise in some respects; it's just important you realise that before spending on one.

Overall, this phone's photo and video capabilities are fine, but AGM typically takes the standard Chinese phone-maker's view that Widevine L1 encryption isn’t necessary, reducing streamed show quality to 480p with Widevine L3. What makes that an extra surprise on this device is that the AGM web page for this device declares that the “X7 Pro is certified for Google Widevine L1 so that you get full HD streaming on your favourite platforms without quality restrictions.”  
My review phone didn’t have L1, only L3, so it appears marketing didn’t get that memo.

## AGM X7 Pro: Final verdict

The AGM X7 Pro does one thing very well. It puts a high-resolution thermal camera in a tough phone that you can actually use every day. The battery is large, the charging options are generous, and Android 16 is a welcome sight.

However, other than some minor tweaks and a slight restyling of the back, this phone is blatantly the G3 Pro revisited.

Looking at the current AGM lineup, this brand had the G3 Pro with a thermal camera and the G3 without one, but both with 12GB of RAM and 512GB of storage. And so the X7 Pro takes the thermal camera from the G3 Pro, reduces the memory/storage sizes, and voila, new phone.

Most phone makers would simply have two G3 Pro SKUs, but AGM decided this phone deserved a whole new designation in a different series, confusingly.

It needs to be said that this spec at the asking price doesn’t exactly make this a bargain. The RAM capacity isn’t massive, the SoC chip is mid-range, and the standard X7 costs €200 less. If you never use the thermal camera, you have paid for something you don't need.

For tradespeople and inspectors, that equation reverses. A thermal camera of this quality usually costs much more as a standalone tool. Having it in your pocket, with a phone attached, is a useful trade. But, would they prefer the extra RAM and storage in the G3 Pro, for that extra €100? Or would they like one where the fusion of the conventional visible light sensor and the thermal camera works better?

If you like this phone and the company is paying, buy the G3 Pro, as it’s the better version.

## Buy it if...

**You need a phone for outdoors.** The water- and dust-resistant AGM X7 Pro is suitable for the harshest environments. It's a large phone, but not too big to fit in a pocket, and easy enough to handle. But if you don't need the thermal camera, cheaper options exist.**You need a decent life on battery.**   
The power-efficient SoC used in the AGM X7 Pro makes the most of a battery that isn't the biggest available. It should be capable of running for at least three days, if not longer, and it can also be recharged wirelessly.

## Don't buy it if...

**You are looking for a cheap option.**  
For phones with a thermal image sensor this isn't the cheapest option. But you need to decide if you need the quality of the one in here, or if a lower resolution sensor will do the job.
