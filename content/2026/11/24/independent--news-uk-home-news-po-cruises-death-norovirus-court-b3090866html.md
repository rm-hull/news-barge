A widow is suing the operator of P&O Cruises at the High Court over the death of her husband following a cruise from Southampton.

Alan Forster, a 77-year-old retired teacher and grandfather from Paignton, Devon, was on board the cruise line’s *Arcadia* ship with his wife Diana Forster, 79, when he fell ill with gastroenteritis.

He was taken off the ship in Canada and admitted to a local hospital, but died on 16 October 2024.

Ms Forster is seeking damages, claiming that Carnival PLC caused the illness which resulted in her husband’s death by failing to deal with an outbreak of similar illnesses onboard.

The couple paid £9,590 for the cruise, which began on 3 September 2024 and was due to visit Newfoundland, Boston and New York before returning to Southampton.

![The Arcadia, seen off the coast of Bournemouth](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/06/23/1264463140..?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&trim=26%2C0%2C26%2C0&auto=webp)

In a document detailing the claim to the court, Lorna Badham, representing Ms Forster, said that the cruise line operator's fault or neglect had caused Mr Forster's illness and contributed to its severity and extent.

Ms Badham said the couple had informed reception of Mr Forster's illness on 19 September and called 999 on 24 September.

She said a nurse placed Mr Forster back on his bed, gave him paracetamol and lowered the room temperature, but no further examination was given until he was seen by a doctor the next day.

The next day he was seen by a doctor.

On 25 September he was taken to hospital, where he later died.

Ms Badham said that Carnival's records show that 128 passengers and 19 crew members fell ill with diarrhoea or vomiting during the cruise, with the likely cause being norovirus.

She argued that the company did not have a sufficient plan to minimise the risks of gastroenteritis and to deal with it appropriately, including failing to adequately clean following a spike in cases and to take reasonable hygiene measures.

She said that food was served undercooked and the company failed to give adequate medical care and monitoring.

No written defence to the claim is currently available.

A P&O Cruises spokesperson said: "We can confirm that the matter is the subject of ongoing legal proceedings and that it would therefore be inappropriate to comment on the allegations.

“P&O Cruises takes the health and wellbeing of our guests extremely seriously and have established and approved health and hygiene protocols in place.”
