### TechRadar Verdict

The LiTime Lares K1’s split design let me fit a 2,304Wh battery and inverter into a narrow space beside my under-counter freezer. The steel casings feel solid, the battery handles make it easier to move, and the built-in touchscreen is useful for a quick check. Its useful for charging on cheap overnight electricity to use during the peak day.

### LiTime Lares K1: Design and availability

The Lares K1 arrived in two deliveries around mid-October 2026. The inverter came first and the battery followed about a day later. Product information was scarce when it arrived, and the regional availability still puzzles me. My review unit came with European sockets and a continental mains lead, yet the only listing I could find was on LiTime’s US storefront. None of the European LiTime sites I checked mentioned the K1**.**

I had to source a UK mains lead with an IEC C13 connector to fit the inverter’s C14 inlet. At the output, I needed a EU-to-UK adaptor to connect the freezer’s three-pin plug. It works, but buying two extra pieces is an awkward start for a product sold as plug-in backup. The adaptor also adds bulk at the side of the inverter, where space is already tight in my installation.

Both the inverter and battery have steel outer casings, which I liked as soon as I unpacked them. The battery has two retractable carry handles, one on each side. They give you a proper grip when you’re moving it and tuck away once it’s in place.

LiTime includes hardware to wall-mount the units, fit the inverter above the battery or mount them back to back. A longer linking cable lets you place the battery separately, such as above a fridge, while keeping the inverter lower down where you can reach the screen. I fitted mine vertically in my attached garage, between the timber framing and existing cabling. You can just see the under-counter freezer behind the K1 in the installation photo.

The separate battery is rated at 2,304Wh and uses LiFePO₄ chemistry. The inverter-charger can recharge it from the mains or connected solar panels. LiTime quotes up to 1,500W of mains charging and 800W of solar charging.

### LiTime Lares K1: Setup

Putting the K1 together was straightforward once I knew the order. Every screw I used took the same Phillips bit, and the rubber feet have peel-off backing so you can stick them on yourself. The printed manuals cover specifications, troubleshooting and the touchscreen, but I couldn’t find instructions for assembling the hardware.

I ended up following LiTime’s installation video. That’s where I learned to connect the necessary cables before mounting the inverter and to follow a specific start-up sequence. Someone setting up in a detached garage or a motorhome with unreliable internet might have a harder time. The assembly order and start-up sequence belong in a printed quick-start guide.

### LiTime Lares K1: Performance and daily use

The only appliance connected to my K1 is a small under-counter freezer. I charge the battery overnight at the cheaper rate and run the freezer from the battery during the day. If the battery reaches the low-charge threshold I’ve set (at 20%), the K1 switches the freezer back to mains power.

One early app reading showed about 40W going to the freezer and 60W coming from the battery. In a separate snapshot, the inverter showed about 20W coming from the battery while the displayed AC load was zero. Those are rounded, momentary readings. With a small freezer that spends part of the day with its compressor off, the inverter’s own draw could make a meaningful difference to how long the battery lasts.

I started the test at 05:00 with the Lares K1 fully charged. My cheap overnight rate ends at 05:30, but Peak Saving Mode only lets me schedule charging in whole hours, so 05:00 was the latest I could tell it to stop charging without running into the higher rate. I left it to run my under-counter freezer all day and checked it again at midnight, 19 hours later. The battery still had 73% charge left, so it had used 27% of a full charge to run both the freezer and the inverter over that period.

LiTime quotes a switchover time of 10ms or less, 2,000W continuous output and a 4,000W surge rating for one second. The 19-hour run measures battery use in my everyday setup. A separate power-cut test would show how the freezer behaves if the mains drops while its compressor is running and what happens when power returns.

### LiTime Lares K1: Display, app and charging schedule

Logging into the app was easy, and I could connect over Bluetooth alone or use Wi-Fi. Both the app and touchscreen show where power is coming from, what the freezer is drawing and how much charge is left. Once I got into settings such as output priority, charging mode and battery reserve, I found myself reaching for the paper manual. A short explanation beside each setting would make the app easier to use.

Peak Saving Mode lets you charge the battery when electricity is cheaper and use that stored energy to run your appliances during peak-rate hours. Unfortunately, this is where LiTime dropped the ball. It only accepts whole hours on the version I tried, but Intelligent Octopus Go’s standard overnight window runs from 23:30 to 05:30. I can set the K1 to charge from 00:00 to 05:00 and stay within the cheap window, but that gives up 30 minutes at each end. Bluetti offers custom charge and discharge schedules on the FridgePower down to the minute. Even half-hour increments would solve the problem on my tariff.

I also had to set the clock on the K1 itself, and I couldn’t find an option to sync it with my phone. That matters when the clocks go forward or back. If the unit stays an hour out, a schedule that was safely inside the cheap window could spill into the higher rate. A phone-sync option and a clear indication of whether the unit adjusts automatically would make it easier to trust the schedule.

### LiTime Lares K1: LiTime Lares K1 vs Bluetti FridgePower

The Bluetti FridgePower is the direct rival. It puts the battery and inverter into one slim enclosure, with an optional magnetic display. I managed to tuck mine away behind the kitchen plinth, but if I had to leave both products on show side by side, I’d pick the Bluetti hands down. Its single enclosure looks more elegant. The K1’s two boxes and visible linking cable look more at home in a utility space like my garage.

The LiTime has advantages of its own. The separate battery and inverter gave me more freedom over placement, and the touchscreen comes as standard. The steel construction and retractable handles also make a good impression in person. The difference between my 3.5 stars for the K1 and 4.5 for the FridgePower comes down to how the whole product feels to set up and live with, as much as how it looks. The LiTime took a video, a replacement UK mains lead, an adaptor to get running, and its charging schedule can’t match the half-hour boundaries of my tariff.

### LiTime Lares K1: Final Verdict

The Lares K1 fits the space I had, and charging it overnight gives it a useful everyday job alongside its role as freezer backup. The hardware feels better thought through than the experience of getting it running. A UK lead and socket option, a printed assembly guide and a scheduler that follows the tariff and the clock would make it easier to recommend. For buyers who need to work around an awkward space, the split design is the strongest reason to choose it.
