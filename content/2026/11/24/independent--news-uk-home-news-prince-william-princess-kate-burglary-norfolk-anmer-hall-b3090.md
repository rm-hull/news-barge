Police have detained two men following an alleged burglary and trespass at the country estate belonging to the Prince and Princess of Wales.

An investigation is under way by Norfolk Police into the incident at Anmer Hall on Monday night.

The 10-bedroom retreat was presented to William and Kate by Queen Elizabeth II following their wedding.

No members of the royal family were at the property during the reported break-in, it is understood.

The force said a police helicopter, drones and police dogs were called out to assist searches after a report of suspicious activity at the address just before 6pm.

Norfolk Police said in a statement: “Two men have been arrested on suspicion of burglary following an incident at a property on the Sandringham Estate last night (Monday 23 November).

![No members of the royal family were at the property during the reported break-in, it is understood](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/24/15/2306142190..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“Police were called just before 6pm following a report of suspicious activity at the address.

“Officers attended and specialist resources, including police dogs, drones and the National Police Air Service helicopter, were deployed to assist with searches in the area.

“Two men were arrested on the estate on suspicion of burglary and trespass. They remain in police custody.

“The property was unoccupied at the time of the incident.

“Inquiries remain ongoing.”

According to tracking site Flightradar 24, a police plane left Nottingham East Midlands Airport at 6.25pm on Monday, and circled an area close to Sandringham before landing back at the airport almost three hours later.

![The 10-bedroom retreat was presented to William and Kate by Queen Elizabeth II following their wedding](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/24/15/2306144134..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Anmer Hall, which is a secluded red brick Georgian mansion sits on the private Sandringham estate, is used as a country retreat by the family.

It has its own private swimming pool, a full-size, artificial grass tennis court and vast grounds.

Kate and William, and their children George, Charlotte and Louis, spend the majority of their time at Forest Lodge, the grade II listed mansion in Windsor, which they have described as their “forever home”.

On Tuesday morning, William was pictured visiting No 10 Downing Street for the first time in the latest example of the evolution of the future king’s public life.

There have been several occasions in which intruders have breached royal estates over the years.

In 2025, an intruder was arrested for breaking into the grounds of Windsor Castle, where senior members of the Royal Family reside.

The year before, masked burglars scaled a 6ft fence and stole farm vehicles from the Windsor Castle estate.

Meanwhile, in 2021, a man armed with a crossbow was detained on the castle grounds after being encouraged to kill the Queen by his AI chatbot “girlfriend”.

*The Independent* has approached Kensington Palace and Norfolk Police.
