Drivers using Gatwick Airport may have had their personal details exposed following a data breach involving a third-party drop-off payment provider.

Contact details, personal details and, "in some very limited cases", partial card payment data may have been accessed in the incident, according to the airport authorities.

An investigation has been launched after Times24 UK Limited, the firm responsible for managing drop-off areas at the West Sussex airport site, stated that they had experienced "unauthorised access" to AutoPay, an external automated drop-off payment system.

A London Gatwick spokesperson said: "Our investigation is ongoing. We have identified that the incident impacts some customer information and those who have been affected have been informed.

![An investigation has been launched following the payment system breach](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/24/20/2293751079..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

"The data that has potentially been accessed includes AutoPay login information, customer contact information and personal details, vehicle and drop-off information, and in some very limited cases, partial card payment information.

"As a precaution and to help protect affected accounts, the AutoPay system has been disabled."

Passengers can continue using drop-off zones with alternative payment channels, while the airport’s main car parks remain open and functioning as normal.

The spokesperson said: "We take the security of our customer information extremely seriously and apologise for any inconvenience or concern that this may cause."

Lisa Webb, legal expert at Which?, said: "People who have paid for a Gatwick drop-off using this system will understandably be worried that their personal data could be in the hands of criminals, so it is essential that Gatwick now provides clear and timely updates to those affected, and supports them in protecting themselves.

"Anyone suspecting they could be affected should keep an eye on bank accounts and credit reports for any suspicious activity in the near future.

"Customers should also watch out for unexpected phone calls, emails or suspect ‘customer support’ messages popping up on social media regarding the payment system breach, as scammers might try to take further advantage."

A spokesperson for data protection regulator the Information Commissioner’s Office said: "We can confirm we have received a report and are assessing the information provided."
