Jensen Huang is back in charge at Nvidia, 11 days after the company's board removed him as chief executive over extramarital affairs, one of them with a Chinese businesswoman suspected of working as a spy for Beijing.

The reinstatement, announced on Monday, came after President Donald Trump successfully pressured the chipmaker to reverse its decision. For Democrats weighing White House runs, Trump's closeness with tech executives was already a potent target before the Nvidia episode.

His intervention has now crystallized their concerns about the growing power of artificial intelligence companies and his blending of government and business interests.

![Nvidia's decision sends a message “that the national security of this country is not as important as your relationship with President Trump,” said Sen. Ruben Gallego](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/24/1/12/GettyImages-2297774940.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Although Democrats lack a unified vision for handling the spread of artificial intelligence, the episode gave them an opportunity to speak largely in unison, calling Huang's return a threat to national security and describing Trump's role as corrupt.

Nvidia's decision sends a message “that the national security of this country is not as important as your relationship with President Trump,” said Sen. Ruben Gallego, an Arizona Democrat considering a White House campaign, in an interview with The Associated Press.

“We also have to figure out how corporate America doesn’t take a bad lesson from this, and thinks that somehow it’s OK for them to evade national security for the purposes of just selling more,” Gallego said.

Trump's intervention also put a spotlight on his own financial stake in the company, which his latest financial disclosure report says was worth at least $12.5 million at the end of 2025.

It also drew attention to the millions of dollars Huang contributed to Trump's ballroom project.

## Trump argues that Huang’s leadership at Nvidia is necessary

Nvidia is separately under scrutiny over whether it is complying with U.S. restrictions on exporting its most advanced semiconductors to China and on their use by Chinese tech firms.

The company's share price dropped sharply during Huang's brief absence, dragging down the broader stock market to one of its worst weeks since the 2008 financial crisis, before recovering with Monday's announcement of his return.

![Still euphoric over their sweeping midterm victories, many ambitious Democrats are still searching for the right message on artificial intelligence](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/24/8/21/tag_reuters-com-2026_newsml_RC2TTOAGX1RZ_603820255-reuters.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Trump had worried that Huang's ouster was not only tanking the stock market but also risked putting the U.S. behind Beijing in the race for tech dominance.

“I’m doing it for the country,” Trump said on CNBC.

Writing on social media that “rules should apply to everyone,” California Rep. Ro Khanna noted that “the rich & powerful, like Jensen, do not get to evade export controls or accountability just because it props up the stock price.”

Still euphoric over their sweeping midterm victories, many ambitious Democrats are still searching for the right message on artificial intelligence, a technology that carries both promise and peril as it becomes increasingly intertwined with the economy, infrastructure and daily life.

Huang's reinstatement reinforced the message many Democrats delivered in the midterms: the system is rigged against average Americans, said Sacha Haworth, founder and CEO of The Tech Oversight Project and a former aide to Democrats.

![Trump said in an interview with CNBC that “I spoke to them, let me just put it that way. And I convinced them.”](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/01/21/21/32/cnbc_youtube_126.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Big tech leaders can be a powerful foil.

“They are the most unlikeable men in America, and yet it seems like the system is working very well for them,” Haworth said. “The news this week just reinforced that idea. So the mission that Democrats have now is to continue to prove to voters that they are fighting against this corrupt system, against the influence of tech CEOs and on behalf of Americans.”

Nvidia's board and Trump himself left no ambiguity over who drove the decision.

Nvidia said the decision was based on input from “senior levels of the United States government,” in a press release announcing Huang's return.

Trump said in an interview with CNBC that “I spoke to them, let me just put it that way. And I convinced them.”

## Democratic candidates see corruption, security risks

Pennsylvania Gov. Josh Shapiro tied Huang's reinstatement to “the sheer corruption and weakness” of the president, calling it “another example of the dangerous combination governing this country: a rigged system that protects those at the very top no matter what, and a corrupt White House that cannot be trusted to put the economic well-being and safety of American families first.”

![Illinois Gov. JB Pritzker compared Huang's reinstatement to Democrats' broad wins in this month's midterm elections](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/24/12/2026-11-05T204554Z_1023610772_RC2THOAGS34Q_RTRMADP_3_USA-ELECTION-CONGRESS.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Sen. Elissa Slotkin of Michigan said that Trump's pressure campaign made clear that, to the president, “making money is more important than America’s national security or the rule of law.”

Slotkin, a former CIA analyst, added, “If ever there was an issue that deserved national security oversight, it’s this one.”

Illinois Gov. JB Pritzker compared Huang's reinstatement to Democrats' broad wins in this month's midterm elections, calling the CEO's return an example of “the greed and lack of accountability that the country just rejected.”

Pritzker urged Democrats “to speak with one voice and not be afraid to fight the Trump administration,” saying Trump and Vice President JD Vance “will move mountains for tech billionaires.”

Huang's return to the top of Nvidia came 11 days after the board removed him, following pressure from the president that Democrats have described as corrupt.
