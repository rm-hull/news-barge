A House Ethics Committee report has found that Democratic Rep. Jimmy Gomez of California engaged in sexual misconduct by making unwelcome sexual advances and sexual contact toward a House staffer.

Gomez announced Monday that he will resign from Congress in response to the report, saying he would leave the House on Dec. 1, even though he has “strong disagreements with the Committee’s report and its conclusions.”

The report said the conduct caused the House aide significant and ongoing distress and contributed to her decision to leave House employment altogether.

![The House Ethics Committee says in a new report there is “substantial evidence” that Democratic Rep. Jimmy Gomez of California engaged in sexual misconduct toward a House staffer](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/04/11/2/36/GettyImages-2251913386.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Gomez was already due to leave Congress at the end of the year, after losing his bid for reelection this month to a fellow Democrat, Rep.-elect Angela Gonzales-Torres.

The committee said “Gomez’s conduct also reflects a broader environment of sexual misconduct and toxic working conditions in some Capitol Hill offices which the Committee is determined to address.”

Gomez said he recognizes the seriousness of the Ethics Committee's report and takes full responsibility for the “personal mistakes I committed outside of my marriage.”

## Panel leaves issue of punishment to the House

He also said he cooperated with the investigation and responded voluntarily to requests for information.

He had previously said he would cooperate with the investigation but also stressed that his actions were consensual in nature and did not violate the law or House Ethics rules.

![Rep. Jimmy Gomez (D-CA) speaks during a House Select Intelligence Committee hearing on March 19, 2026](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/24/09/2267402013..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

He also apologized to his family and constituents.

Gomez objected to the report's conclusion, saying the committee omitted key portions of his testimony essential to a full and fair understanding of what occurred.

“In my interview, I answered the Committee’s questions only to see my statements selectively quoted, disparaged, and disregarded throughout the final report, to which I was given no opportunity to respond,” Gomez said. “With this in mind, I see no other alternative than to resign, effective December 1, to allow for my staff to close out important constituent casework and needs.”

The committee said that while Gomez had sought to characterize his actions as personal mistakes that affected those he cared about most, the inquiry was more concerned with the impact on the staffer who said Gomez's conduct contributed to her decision to leave work on Capitol Hill.

![Gomez speaks at the rally to Say NO to Tax Breaks for Billionaires & Corporations at US Capitol](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/24/09/2209606220..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“Representative Gomez’s conduct caused a traumatic and ongoing chain of interactions for Witness 1 which, combined with a broader culture of sexual misconduct on the Hill, drove her from government service,” the committee said.

The committee said it began its investigation after a report that Gomez was seen making out in public with a House staffer.

Gomez disclosed interactions with two women. With one of them, he described one occasion of consensual kissing. The committee contacted the aide, whose recollection differed sharply from Gomez's.

The aide did not work in his office or on a committee he served on, but for a “leadership office” where, from her point of view, lawmakers were the “constituents.”

## Ethics report describes Gomez’s behavior with a staffer

She described unwanted sexual advances on two occasions, including an incident in which Gomez made sexual contact with her. The committee found that the interactions violated House rules by making unwelcome sexual advances. It also said that Gomez's behavior toward the aide could constitute criminal misdemeanor sexual abuse.

The committee also interviewed a second woman and House staffer after Gomez had disclosed a past consensual sexual relationship. Gomez said that relationship ended in July 2022. The woman denied to the committee that she ever had a “nonconsensual sexual encounter” with Gomez.

The committee did not recommend any particular sanction for Gomez, who is serving his fifth term in the House. The panel said the Constitution charges the House with policing its own members' behavior. “The House should take whatever action it deems appropriate in light of the Committee’s findings,” the Ethics panel said.

Gomez may head off severe sanctions from his colleagues by leaving office early. The finding is just the latest involving allegations of sexual misconduct by a House lawmaker. Two lawmakers resigned earlier this year rather than face an expulsion vote.

## Congress weighs harassment reforms

Another member was censured for his interactions with two female aides on his staff. Democratic leaders in the House, who are preparing to take the majority in the next Congress, issued a joint statement calling the report's findings “serious and deeply troubling.”

“It is appropriate that he is stepping down,” the statement said. “Decisive action to set a new standard for reform is an urgent priority for House Democrats.” The woman's attorneys, Lisa J. Banks and Sarah E. Nesbitt, applauded the committee for the investigation.

“However, Representative Gomez’s conduct did not occur in a vacuum, and immediate steps must be taken by Congress to rein in what the report describes as a ‘toxic and predatory culture,’” Banks and Nesbitt said. “Democracy cannot function properly when women must endure harassment and abuse as a condition of public service.”
