## The sport's top court ruled that the referee's decision to restart the final after Senegal's walk-off could not be challenged, while Morocco has raised questions over alleged contact between one of the judges and Senegal's federation.

Senegal have been confirmed as winners of the 2025 Africa Cup of Nations after the Court of Arbitration for Sport (CAS) overturned a ruling that had stripped them of the title and handed it to hosts Morocco.

The court, based in Lausanne, Switzerland, upheld an appeal by the Senegalese Football Federation (FSF) against a decision by the Confederation of African Football's (CAF) appeal board. The board had declared that Senegal forfeited the final and recorded Morocco as 3-0 winners. The original 1-0 result in Senegal's favour now stands.

"By blowing his whistle to restart play, the Referee considered that the conditions for resuming the match had been met - both teams were on the pitch again, and both expressed their willingness to resume," the court said.

"In the Panel's view, there is no doubt that the effects of the Referee's decision were limited to the pitch. As such, the Panel considers that the Referee's decision was covered by the 'field of play' doctrine."

Under the doctrine, CAS does not review decisions taken on the pitch by referees unless the rules were applied arbitrarily or in bad faith.

"The Panel acknowledged that the Referee had to make a decision in an atmosphere of extreme tension. However, these circumstances do not justify describing the decision as 'arbitrary'," CAS added.

The three-member panel's decision was unanimous.

## **Morocco to challenge ruling**

The Royal Moroccan Football Federation (FRMF) said it would "pursue all necessary legal avenues" to contest the ruling.

The FRMF said it objected to the rejection of its request to postpone the decision. It added that it could take the case to Switzerland's Federal Supreme Court, which rules on procedural matters rather than the merits of a case.

In a statement, the FRMF referred to what it said was "contact" between Ulrich Haas, a German arbitrator who was one of the three CAS panel members, and the president of the FSF 48 hours before the decision was announced.

## **Chaos in Rabat**

The final was played on 18 January at the Prince Moulay Abdellah Stadium in Rabat, Morocco. The score was 0-0 deep into stoppage time when the Congolese referee, Jean-Jacques Ngambo Ndala, awarded Morocco a penalty after a VAR review.

Senegal's players, led by coach Pape Thiaw, walked off the pitch in protest for 15 minutes, and fans tried to storm the field.

When play resumed, Brahim Díaz's penalty was saved by Senegal goalkeeper Édouard Mendy. Pape Gueye then scored in extra time to win the match for Senegal.

## **Title stripped**

CAF's disciplinary board first fined and banned players and officials from both Senegal and Morocco, with fines totalling more than $1 million, but it left the result unchanged.

Morocco appealed. In March, CAF's appeal board ruled that Senegal's conduct fell under Articles 82 and 84 of the tournament regulations, declared that they had forfeited the final, and awarded Morocco their first African title since 1976.

## **The appeal to CAS**

The FSF lodged its appeal with CAS on 25 March. Morocco's football federation argued that the referee's decision was not protected by the "field of play" doctrine, that by leaving the pitch Senegal had breached Articles 82 and 84, and that the referee should have ended the match early.

The panel rejected those arguments. It found that the two articles apply only when a team's conduct leads a referee to abandon a match permanently, so they did not apply in this case. A hearing was held behind closed doors in Lausanne on 8 October.

CAS noted that the FSF, Thiaw and some players had already been sanctioned by CAF in separate disciplinary proceedings.
