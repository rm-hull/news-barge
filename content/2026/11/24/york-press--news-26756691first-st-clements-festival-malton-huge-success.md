## Crowds gather for festive celebration of crafts, music and community spirit

The St Clement’s Festival took place in Malton on Saturday, November 21, bringing together families, businesses, and visitors for a day of seasonal cheer and traditional activities. *(Image: Nick Fletcher)*

Crowds gathered for a festive celebration of crafts, music, and community spirit.

The St Clement’s Festival took place in Malton on Saturday, November 21, bringing together families, businesses, and visitors for a day of seasonal cheer and traditional activities.

The Horseshoe Trail guided attendees through the town and into local shops, many of which reported strong footfall, a lively atmosphere, and excellent sales.

A spokesperson for the Festival Steering Committee said: "We’re so grateful to all the local businesses who got behind the festival, whether by sponsoring and decorating Christmas trees, taking part in the Horseshoe Trail or generously donating goodies for the prize hamper.

"We really hope it was a day to remember for everyone who came along, but also a great day for our independent businesses.

"Malton is incredibly lucky to have such a wonderful mix of shops and traders, and seeing the town buzzing with people enjoying and supporting them is exactly what we hoped for."

St Michael’s Church hosted performances from choirs and brass bands, while the Milton Rooms offered live music, crafting, children’s activities, and demonstrations of traditional skills.

Four working forges gave festival-goers the chance to try blacksmithing, and Acorn Community ran apple pressing sessions.

Gilly from The Cook’s Place added a culinary element to the day’s events.

The Woodhams Stone Collection exhibition at The Parish drew interest from visitors, as did guided walking tours from Malton Museum.

The day ended with a performance by the Salvation Army Band and a Son et Lumière show, with films and illuminations projected onto St Michael’s Church.

The festival was organised by the Malton St Clement’s Steering Group and supported by funding from David Skaith, Mayor of York and North Yorkshire, through the York and North Yorkshire Combined Authority.

The celebrations are set to continue with the Open Finkle Street Festival taking place on Saturday, November 28.
