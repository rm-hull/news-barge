A grieving mother has said justice has come “too late” for her son, who died behind bars serving a “venomous” indefinite jail term as a landmark legal challenge reaches court.

Josh Mcrae, 34, had spent more than 16 years behind bars on a controversial Imprisonment for Public Protection (IPP) sentence when he was found unresponsive in his cell.

He had previously said the only way out of prison was in a body bag as he lost hope of being freed from the open-ended punishment he was handed for a pub fight when he was 17.

This summer, almost two years after his sudden death, the United Nations ruled that Mr Mcrae’s detention and that of four other men trapped on jail terms was “arbitrary” and unlawful under international human rights standards.

A major habeas corpus legal challenge over their punishments is being heard in Cardiff on Wednesday, which could see the surviving prisoners freed if the case succeeds, potentially paving the way for scores of other IPP inmates to follow suit.

Mr Mcrae’s mother, Theresa, told *The Independent* the finding by the UN Working Group for Arbitrary Detention (UNWGAD) confirms what they have long known, that “this sentence violates human rights and causes profound suffering”.

“It devastates us that Josh won’t be here to see the legal action through or be here for the outcome,” she said. “It feels unfair that Josh suffered for so long, only to pass away before he could be part of this. Josh spoke out to help ensure other IPPs facing the same injustice were heard.

“This has all come too late for Josh, our family and his friends, but we hope it will bring others closer to justice. We can only hope that, whatever comes next, the government will finally show some humanity, end this venomous sentence once and for all, and give those still serving a chance to heal and rebuild their lives before it’s too late.”

![Top: Abdullahi Suleman, Shaun Anton Lloyd, Joshua McRae. Bottom: Wayne Williams, Leroy Douglas](https://static.independent.co.uk/2025/09/09/10/52/ippnewsplit-newwww.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

An inquest jury last month concluded Mr Mcrae, who used to be an ambitious footballer, died as a result of drug-related misadventure from spice toxicity, a potent type of synthetic cannabinoid.

They found the failure to impose sufficient measures to stop drugs getting into HMP Long Lartin, a Category A prison in Worcestershire, probably contributed to his death.

Prison staff had visited his cell three times on the morning he died on 6 September 2024, but failed to check that he was alive and well, the inquest found.

Mr Mcrae was handed an IPP sentence with a four-year minimum tariff for grievous bodily harm in 2007, but maintained his innocence.

He served almost six years before he was first released in 2012, but was recalled to prison six months later after he was further convicted of actual bodily harm, where he languished for another 11 years until his death.

![An inquest found there were not sufficient measures to stop drugs getting into HMP Long Lartin](https://static.independent.co.uk/2025/07/11/14/7bb16182ee49e8950d8a3c47d2f8d243Y29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzUyMzI1MTI2-2.45571825.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

His case formed part of a complaint to the UN on behalf of five men who served a combined total of 84 years incarcerated under the IPP jail terms, which were outlawed in 2012.

In a scathing legal opinion, the human rights watchdog called for Britain to urgently act, concluding the prisoners’ long incarceration beyond their original tariffs amounted to arbitrary detention under international human rights law.

The four other cases include Leroy Douglas, who served almost 20 years for street robbery of a mobile phone; Abdullahi Suleman, who is still in custody 20 years after he was handed an IPP for a laptop robbery; Wayne Williams, who has spent more than 20 years in prison without release for a 23-month jail term for attempting to injure a police officer in a fight; and Shaun Lloyd, 39, who was recently hauled back to prison for the fifth time, having served more than 13 years for street robbery.

*The Independent* has previously reported each of their cases as we called for all remaining IPP prisoners to have their sentences reviewed.

Human rights lawyer Karen Todner, representing the prisoners, has said “enough is enough” as she called for the men to be freed. She accused the government of “hypocrisy” and “defending the indefensible” for disputing the prisoners’ legal challenge, despite pledging to bring legislation to end the “unfair” punishments by the end of this parliament.

“IPP is the biggest blight on our justice system for the last 30 years and these, mainly men but also some women, have been completely forgotten and it needs to stop,” she told *The Independent.*

![Andy Burnham has pledged to ‘end the injustice’ of IPP jail terms](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/20/14/2305467801..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

In written submissions to the court, the applicants argue the fallout of the IPP regime is an “urgent matter of national importance” and these men have been “forgotten by the state”.

Their treatment has created “a profound sense of injustice” that their treatment is so different from that of those who “avoided the ill-fortune of being sentenced between 2005 and 2012”, the seven-year period in which IPP sentences were handed out.

The written submissions also point to “an utter failure” to resolve the IPP crisis “in spite of a legal and political consensus that the operation of the scheme has been a disaster”.

In August, prime minister Andy Burnham pledged to “end the injustice” of IPP jail terms for more than 2,000 still languishing in prison before the end of this parliament. A further announcement is expected to be made later this year.

A Ministry of Justice spokesperson said: “The government inherited prisons on the brink of collapse, but we are pulling every lever to restore order across the estate. This includes investing £75m in physical security measures to help tackle drugs and other contraband that fuel violence behind bars.

“It is right that IPP sentences were abolished and as the prime minister and Lord Chancellor announced, we will set out plans to end the historic injustice of these sentences.”
