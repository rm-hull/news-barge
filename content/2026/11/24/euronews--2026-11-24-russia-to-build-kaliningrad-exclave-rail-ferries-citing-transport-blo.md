## The ferries, due in 2030 and costing around 50 billion roubles, will run between Baltiysk and Ust-Luga and be built in occupied Crimea. Most freight to Kaliningrad has already moved to sea routes, since Lithuania and Poland restricted transit after the 2022 invasion.

Russia is building two rail ferries to reinforce supply lines to its Kaliningrad exclave, Prime Minister Mikhail Mishustin said Monday, citing what he described as attempts by unnamed countries to impose a transport blockade.

Speaking at an operational meeting of deputy prime ministers, Mishustin said "unlawful actions by some countries" were disrupting logistics chains and complicating business in the region.

The ferries will operate on the sea crossing between the ports of Baltiysk and Ust-Luga, linking the Kaliningrad and Leningrad regions.

According to official documentation, the ferries will be built to a Russian design at the B E Butoma Shipbuilding Plant in Kerch, in Russian-occupied Crimea, with completion scheduled for 2030.

The project has been assigned to state enterprise Rosmorport and included in the list of infrastructure works financed from Russia's National Wealth Fund. The authorities have allocated around 50 billion roubles (€450 million) for the construction.

Poland and Lithuania separate Kaliningrad from the rest of Russia, but it has access to the Baltic Sea. Major naval facilities and other military infrastructure are based there, making sea transport a crucial logistical pathway.

After Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine in early 2022, Lithuania restricted transit of sanctioned goods through its territory. The European Commission later allowed rail transit within set quotas, but road transport of sanctioned goods remains banned.

Poland has reinforced its border with the region with military defences and a fence equipped with cameras and motion sensors. Lithuania recently drew up plans for further reinforcement measures.

Border crossings with both countries operate under strict limitations, with Russian citizens barred from tourist entry and local cross-border traffic suspended.

By November the bulk of freight to Kaliningrad had shifted to sea routes from the Leningrad region, with around 30 ships and ferries operating on those crossings.

Russian Post now ships parcels from the region by sea after Lithuania began inspecting them for possible military and dual-use goods.

On Saturday, an air-raid warning was declared in the Kaliningrad region for the first time since the start of the war following a drone threat, with temporary restrictions imposed on operations at Khrabrovo airport.

In Finnish waters, drones attacked Russian dry cargo vessel Sparta II, which observers have linked to Russia's military logistics.

Ukraine has asked EU and NATO countries to shut down Moscow's supply routes in the Baltic Sea and has called for action against Russia's shadow fleet.
