Beau Lamarre-Condon’s fate is in the hands of his peers as the 12-person jury in his double murder trial has been sent off to deliberate.

The former police officer is accused of fatally shooting Jesse Baird and his new partner, Luke Davies, with his police service weapon in Baird’s Paddington home on 19 February 2024.

He has pleaded not guilty to two counts of murder and one charge of aggravated break and enter.

But it will be up to 12 jurors, made up of seven men and five women, who have viewed every exhibit and listened to every witness – including the accused – to deliver their verdicts.

They were sent off to deliberate on Wednesday morning, 10 weeks after the trial first began.

The jury was empanelled in September at Sydney’s King St courthouse, with three additional people picked to account for potential juror dropout due to reasons such as illness.

But the entire 15-person jury stood strong through two months of evidence, and shortly before deliberations began, three jurors were eliminated by ballot.

Justice Natalie Adams earlier said the process could be “a little brutal”.

She acknowledged jurors on long trials often form friendships with each other, and can become emotional if they do not make it into the final jury.

As the 12 verdict jurors departed the courtroom, some offered pats on the back to those who hadn’t been selected.

The eliminated jurors have been barred from contacting the jury until they come to a verdict.

But Adams maintained they had were still integral to the trial.

“I thank you sincerely for the service you have performed,” she said on Wednesday.

“I know how disappointing it must be having come this far but please understand the important role you have played.

“You have actually saved the court a considerable degree of time by performing the service you have to a very high standard.”

The jury began their deliberations at 9.40am on Wednesday.
