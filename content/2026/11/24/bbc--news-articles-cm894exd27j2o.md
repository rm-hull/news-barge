* **Spoiler warning: This story reveals details of The Great British Bake Off finalMoyin Odeniran said he was "in shock" after being crowned the winner of the 17th series of The Great British Bake Off.**

The 22-year-old from Loughton, Essex, described it as a "very busy time" as he juggled competing in the Channel 4 baking competition with completing his university studies.

Despite his dissertation being due a month after filming wrapped, Odeniran achieved a first in his political economy degree at King's College London.

He beat fellow finalists Tom Whittaker, from West Yorkshire, and Birmingham student Mo Mahir to the title in Tuesday night's episode.

"I don't know how that happened, I don't know how I got here. I can't believe it – I am in shock," the talented baker said.

"I have made friends for life and I have just loved every single second of it, and I don't think I could have asked for anything more."

Judge Paul Hollywood described Odeniran's efforts as "pure magic" after watching his jubilant celebrations with family and friends.

"He has done an amazing job because out of all the bakers he struggled at the beginning, and he actually got better and better," Hollywood said.

Fellow judge Nigella Lawson praised Odeniran as a "worthy winner", but also said the other finalists were "sensational".

![Odeniran smiling as he holds a glass cake stand in front of a large white tent.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/1a3a/live/e39318b0-e84d-11f1-aa88-031e8213533d.jpg)

Odeniran said he would never forget his time on the show

The high-stakes final saw the three contestants tasked with making a citrus celebration cake, creme brulee donuts and a "spectacular tribute to iconic British landscapes".

Reflecting on his performance in the final, Odeniran said: "I didn't want to put too much pressure on myself, but I knew I had put my best foot forward.

"I couldn't have done anything better than I did and I had a great time doing it."

He said he got "quite exhausted" while filming the series and praised his mother, sister and close friends for their support.

"Earlier on, some of the challenges were quite difficult for me and I was shocked that I kept going through," Odeniran said.

"Before you get there you think you know how to bake, and by week two you question whether you know anything about baking."

He shared his gratitude at being able to learn from Lawson and Hollywood.

"I will never forget my time there and the friendships made which will always continue," the winner added.

Prior to the final, Odeniran told BBC Essex he planned to view it at a huge "watch party" at his local church.
