## Danish Prime Minister Mette Frederiksen drew sharp criticism after making controversial remarks on migration in a domestic podcast, emphasising Denmark's "ethnically Danish" population and appearing to echo the far-right "great replacement" theory.

Danish Prime Minister Mette Frederiksen's hardline stance on migration has fuelled a growing rift within the European Parliament's S&D group, putting her Socialdemokratiet party at odds with its centre-left allies.

On Tuesday, the S&D leader Iratxe García Pérez said the group would hold internal discussions on the issue "as soon as possible", after several MEPs brought it up during a group meeting.

“There is no doubt about the positions defended by our political family. The respect of international law is a crucial element,” she said.

Frederiksen sparked a backlash with recent comments on migration in a domestic podcast, touting the “ethnically Danish origin” of her country's population and even echoing the “great replacement” theory, a far-right conspiracy theory alleging a plot to dilute Europe’s white population.

“This podcast was a disaster,” an S&D MEP told Euronews on condition of anonymity, summing up a widespread view in the group.

Copenhagen has adopted one of the EU's toughest approaches to irregular migration, joining forces with right-wing governments, including Italy and Belgium to advance its agenda.

Notably, Denmark is one of five EU countries that struck a partnership with Rwanda to deport irregular migrants to the first-ever return hub outside the bloc. The S&D group has campaigned against the EU law enabling return hubs, called the “return regulation,” and voted overwhelmingly against it in the European Parliament last June.

“We can open up again an internal debate on migration policy. But the group already has a position on return hubs: they have no legal ground and are not in line with international law,” García Pérez said at a press conference the same day.

She said the majority of the group opposes the Danish line on migration, a claim confirmed to Euronews by S&D MEPs from other countries.

“For now, no one is taking [Frederisken’s] side”, a senior Socialist lawmaker told Euronews.

Another MEP likened her statements on migration to the “worst propaganda from [Italian Prime Minister] Giorgia Meloni.”

Only a few S&D lawmakers, from Malta, Romania, Latvia and Sweden, broke ranks with the group’s line by voting in favour or abstaining on the return regulation. But none of them has so far spoken out in defence of Frederiksen’s stance, according to several sources in the group.

The discussion will probably take place in the coming weeks, after the Strasbourg plenary session, according to an S&D official.

Although Denmark's position conflicts with the group's stance, no sanctions or concrete action are currently expected, as any decision on the matter would need to be taken at a higher political level.

Any disciplinary measures against Danish S&D members could be considered only within the Party of European Socialists (PES), the European centre-left umbrella party whose parliamentary expression is the S&D group, García Pérez said.

Euronews reached out to the Danish S&D delegation for comment, but had not received a response at the time of publication.
