**A triple killer who murdered the notorious child-killer Ian Huntley in prison has been given a second whole life jail term.**

Anthony Russell, 44, bludgeoned Huntley, 52, to death with a metal bar at the high security Frankland Prison near Durham in February, Newcastle Crown Court heard.

Russell, who was jailed for life with no chance of parole in March 2022 for raping and murdering a pregnant woman and killing a mother and son in the West Midlands, admitted Huntley's murder on the second day of his trial.

Sentencing him for what prosecutors called a "cold-blooded" and planned attack, Mr Justice Hilliard KC said Russell would never be freed from prison or eligible for parole.

Huntley was serving a life sentence at the high security Frankland jail for murdering Holly Wells and Jessica Chapman in Soham, Cambridgeshire, in 2002.

He and Russell, from Coventry, were both on A-wing and there was "tension" between the killers, the trial had heard.

At about 09:00 GMT on 26 February, Russell attacked Huntley in the prison's recycling workshop, repeatedly beating him about the head with a metal bar.

As a "natural lifer" who would never get parole, Russell said he had "nothing to lose" and intended to murder Huntley, with the attack planned for several weeks, the court heard.

Huntley died nine days later at Newcastle's Royal Victoria Infirmary from unsurvivable brain and skull injuries.

Two incidents showing "tension" between Huntley and Russell had been recorded in Frankland's logs.

In March 2025 the pair were separated while arguing and shouting threats at each other.

In January, weeks before Huntley's murder, Russell was seen with a black eye which prison guards believed might have been caused by the Soham child-killer.

After the fatal attack in which Huntley sustained at least six blows the head with the metal bar, Russell told prison staff: "Hopefully I've killed him."

He said he "caved" Huntley's head in as we was "sick" of the murderer, who had been "getting in my face the last few weeks".

Russell had admitted manslaughter on the grounds of diminished responsibility, claiming he was hearing voices and under the influence of childhood post-traumatic stress disorder when he attacked Huntley.

But at the start of the second day of his trial, after prosecutors had opened the case, he changed his plea to murder.

Ahead of sentencing, prosecutor Matthew Bean KC told the court Russell was serving a whole life term for three murders which he admitted committing.

On 21 October 2020, at his flat in Coventry , Russell beat and strangled David Williams, 32, out of "jealousy" due to the mistaken belief the victim had slept with Russell's girlfriend.

Having concealed the body, Russell took his victim's phone and messaged his mother, Julie Williams, in a bid to make her think her son was alive, the court heard.

Three days later he went to the 58-year-old's flat in Coventry and strangled her because he knew she suspected he had murdered her son.

To evade arrest he went to the Leamington Spa area where he met and befriended Nicole McGregor, 31, who was five months pregnant, the court heard.

Hours after meeting her on 26 October 2020, he raped and murdered her, Bean said, to stop her reporting what he had done.

## 'Nothing to lose'

The murderer accepted the killing of Huntley was "deliberate and planned", his barrister Tyrone Smith KC said.

Russell told prison staff Huntley was a "horrible narcissistic person" who "walks around the wing like he's something big and hard and special", Smith said.

Russell felt Huntley was protected by prison guards because of "how high profile" he was, the court heard, and the child-killer had been even more "arrogant" and "cocky" since Russell walked away from an earlier argument.

He told guards he had "nothing to lose" and was "never getting out", so wanted to do the job of murdering Huntley "properly", the court heard.

Smith said Russell was "frank" in expressing "no remorse" towards Huntley, but he apologised to those who witnessed the attack and had to deal with the aftermath.

Hilliard said it was "hardly surprising" there would be tension between two such murderers, telling Russell: "I'm sure you were able to give as good as you got."

He said the "background of antagonism" was the reason for the killing but it in no way reduced the sentence in a case of "exceptionally high seriousness".

"You will never be released."
