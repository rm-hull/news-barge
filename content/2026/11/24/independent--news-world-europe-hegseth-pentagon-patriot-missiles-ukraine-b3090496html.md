US defense secretary Pete Hegseth has held up the delivery of roughly 60 Patriot air-defence interceptors already pledged to Ukraine, according to a report.

The stalled shipment includes both advanced PAC-3 interceptors — Kyiv’s primary shield against incoming ballistic missiles — and around 20 older PAC-2 models, the *Financial Times*reported*.*

Although Ukraine’s Western allies have already paid for the hardware, and several of the missiles are sitting in European stockpiles, US export rules require formal sign-off from Washington before any American-designed munitions can be transferred or re-exported.

According to the newspaper*,* Ukraine was scheduled to receive eight PAC-3 interceptors in September, 10 in October and 10 in November, with another 12 due in December. None of the batches has been cleared.

![The Pentagon has delayed signing off on Patriot interceptor systems for Ukraine’s war effort](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/08/11/09/2156557079..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The bottleneck comes amid a steep escalation in hostilities and growing concerns that the conflict may be opening a Mediterranean front, after Kyiv targeted Russian ships in Libyan waters on Monday.

Russian forces have since intensified their aerial bombardments campaign, sparking fears that without Patriot interceptor systems, major Ukrainian cities will be left increasingly vulnerable to ballistic missile attacks.

In a video address delivered on Sunday, president Volodymyr Zelensky urged Washington to release the paid-for munitions, before warning on Monday night that Ukrainian intelligence had detected preparations for a fresh wave of Russian strikes.

Kyiv has also been waiting on separate Pentagon approval to establish domestic production lines for Patriot interceptors — a move Donald Trump publicly promised to back in July — but this has not yet materialised.

![President Donald Trump publicly granted approval for Kyiv to domestically produce its own interceptors in July](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/07/08/14/2285204322..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

While Washington has recently cleared a separate backlog of Nasams air-defence missiles, which are now reportedly en route to Ukraine, the Patriot freeze has fuelled alarm in Kyiv that the Trump administration is withholding critical hardware as diplomatic leverage.

Relations between Zelensky and his American counterpart have soured in recent weeks. The US president has criticised Ukrainian strikes on Russian oil refineries, suggested Kyiv needs a “new president,” and turned down a Ukrainian request for a 300-missile “winter package” due to global shortages triggered by his war on Iran.

According to Zelensky, Russian forces fire between 30 and 40 ballistic missiles during each large-scale assault — meaning the entire 60-missile backlog would barely cover two major barrages.

![The aftermath of a Russian strike on Ukraine on Tuesday, as Kyiv pleads for Patriot interceptors](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/24/08/2306119953..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

In further blows for the Ukrainian president, Mr Trump has doubled down on warmly receiving Vladimir Putin as his “friend” after meeting doing so in China last week — only hours before Russian ballistic missiles struck central Kryvyi Rih, Zelensky’s hometown, killing a seven-year-old boy and a medic. An additional 58 people were wounded.

Combined with new speed restrictions on Starshield — the military tier of Elon Musk’s Starlink satellite network — direct US support for the Ukrainian military appears to be waning.

For officials in Kyiv, the Patriot hold-up comes as part of a broader push to weaken Ukraine’s battlefield footing and pressure it into territorial concessions ahead of a new, Democratic-led US Congress taking office in January.
