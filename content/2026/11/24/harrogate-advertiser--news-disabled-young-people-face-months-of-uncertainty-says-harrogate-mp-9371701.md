Harrogate and Knaresborough’s MP has warned that disabled people and carers across North Yorkshire face months of uncertainty over the future of Personal Independence Payment (PIP).

Tom Gordon’s comments follow Disability Minister Stephen Timms’s announcement of the findings of his new report on the main disability benefit in England and Wales, just a week after Alan Milburn’s review .

While Tom Gordon, Liberal Democrat MP for Harrogate and Knaresborough, welcomed the Government's decision to fast-track support for people with the most serious conditions, he said that, because the Government had now agreed two recommendations, there was a risk of uncertainty for disabled young people, in particular.

"Ending repeated, stressful reassessments for people whose conditions will never improve, and fast-tracking those with the most serious illnesses, is the right thing to do,” said the Liberal Democrat MP for Harrogate and Knaresborough.

“Credit for that belongs to the disabled people who helped write this review.

"But disabled young people now face months of uncertainty, with Stephen Timms recommending one thing and Alan Milburn another.

“The Government must not waste time in setting out its approach.”

Approximately four million people claim PIP at the moment at a cost of about £32 billion this financial year.

This is projected to rise to £41.5 billion in 2030–31.

Disability Minister Sir Stephen Timms’s review was co-produced with disabled people and their organisations and concluded that the current system was "no longer fit for purpose".

The Government has agreed two recommendations straight away: a fast-track route, without an unnecessary full assessment, for people with serious and well-evidenced conditions; and an end to repeated award reviews for people whose conditions are lifelong, progressive or unlikely to improve.

The rest of the review will be considered alongside Alan Milburn's review of young people and work, with a White Paper due early next year and legislation to follow.

Milburn's proposals would take £3.6 billion from disability support for 18- to 24-year-olds, despite the Joseph Rowntree Foundation’s warning that young people are already at disproportionately high risk of destitution.

Harrogate MP Tom Gordon said: "Families in Harrogate and Knaresborough who rely on PIP, and the carers for whom PIP is the gateway to Carer's Allowance, deserve to know where they stand.

“Last year the Government's own figures showed its original PIP plans would have pushed 250,000 people, including 50,000 children, into poverty. We cannot let that happen again by the back door.”

The Liberal Democrats are calling on the Government to:

"We need to manage the welfare bill after it soared thanks to the Conservative Party's economic chaos," said Mr Gordon.

"But the way to do that is to tackle the root causes: grow the economy to create jobs, bring down the cost of living, and fix the NHS and social care so health problems are treated early.

"That means guaranteeing everyone can see a GP within seven days, setting up mental health hubs for young people in every community, and giving disabled people who can work the support they need to do so."
