Jensen Huang revealed “additional affairs” to Nvidia’s board during a lengthy, high-stakes meeting Sunday afternoon before they reinstated him as CEO following pressure from President Donald Trump, according to a report. Upon returning, he told staff he is in therapy.

It has been a rollercoaster two weeks for billionaire Huang and his AI-chipmaking company after he admitted being unfaithful to his wife. He resigned as CEO on November 12 after Nvidia was rocked by allegations of his extramarital affairs, including with an alleged Chinese spy and another with a married subordinate Nvidia executive.

Huang made an astonishing comeback Monday after Trump declared that the U.S. government “has no reason to believe that Jensen’s return to Nvidia poses any risk to national security.”

It has since emerged that the board reinstated Huang despite a confession during Sunday’s meeting that he had more affairs beyond the two that were already in the public domain, *The Wall Street Journal* reported, citing people familiar with the matter.

Huang was reportedly “contrite” about his behavior during the crunch talks, and the board discussed “the importance of candor and honesty” with him, according to the newspaper.

When Huang returned to the office Monday to a rockstar’s welcome, he apologized to his employees — many of whom own stakes in the company — and pledged to “be a better CEO the second time around.”

“I’m terribly sorry for putting all of you through all of this,” Huang said in a video. “I’ve made some very terrible mistakes and I’m going to work on coming back.”

During an all-hands meeting Tuesday, he again apologized to employees and told them he is in therapy, *The Wall Street Journal*reported.* The Independent* has contacted Nvidia for comment.

Huang has admitted being unfaithful to his wife of 41 years, Lori Huang. One of the reported affairs that prompted security concerns was with former Megaspeed director Alice Huang — no relation — whom U.S. intelligence has said facilitated shipments of Nvidia’s microchips to Chinese firms as part of a foreign intelligence operation.

Then, Huang was accused of having a relationship with Kimberly Powell, his company’s vice president of healthcare, who is also married.

Neither women have publicly commented on the allegations and his wife, Lori Huang, is yet to break her silence. If she divorces Huang, she could net a windfall settlement of $75 billion, according to *Forbes*.

Trump — who may have made as much as $15 million from Huang’s return, according to a report — and Vice President JD Vance intervened over the weekend to push for Huang to be reinstated. The president’s push to restore Huang intensified late last week after he returned to Washington from a trip to China and watched the stock market decline Friday.

The president admitted to CNBC he had leaned on Nvidia’s board of directors to reinstate him. “I spoke to them, let me put it that way,” Trump said Monday. “And I convinced them… I made the deal… The board decided to do what we wanted to do.”

Asked about internal concerns over Huang’s conduct, the president insisted the tech boss “did nothing wrong.”

Nvidia said in a statement it had received input from “senior levels” of the U.S. government before making its U-turn. “Based on that input, the board is confident that Huang’s reinstatement poses no national security risk.”

Despite the White House’s backing, the decision drew immediate pushback from lawmakers on Capitol Hill, where concerns linger over national security and potential export control violations.

*The New York Times* reported on Sunday that Alice Huang maintained contact with an official from China’s primary intelligence agency for over a year during their relationship. Additionally, her employer, the Singapore-based firm Megaspeed, is currently under Justice Department investigation for allegedly smuggling restricted Nvidia chips into China.

In its Monday announcement, Nvidia stated it would hire a chief compliance officer and that its internal investigation into the matter would continue with Huang’s cooperation.

Huang is scheduled to testify before the Senate Banking Committee next week.
