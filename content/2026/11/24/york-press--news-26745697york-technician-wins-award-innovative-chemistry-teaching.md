A laboratory technician from the University of York has won a Royal Society of Chemistry award.

Kieran Godfree, who works at the university, received the Early Career Prize for Excellence in Higher Education in recognition of his innovative teaching methods and dedication to creating an inclusive and supportive environment for students.

The award is part of the Royal Society of Chemistry’s Education Prizes, which honour outstanding contributions from teachers, technicians, lecturers and education researchers.

Mr Godfree said: "I'd not been aware of these prizes before, and if I had been, I don't think I would have considered the possibility that I could have won a teaching award as a technician.

"I love supporting and teaching students in the lab, and the challenges involved in developing the practical course.

"Seeing students developing their skills during their time in the lab is a constant reward, but it's also really gratifying to be recognised through this award."

Dr Helen Pain, CEO of the Royal Society of Chemistry, said: "Exceptional chemistry education changes lives.

"I extend my warmest congratulations to Kieran.

"Winning an RSC Prize is a remarkable achievement.

"Our winners are inspiring role models for our communities, and we’re so pleased to be celebrating such a brilliant cohort this year."
