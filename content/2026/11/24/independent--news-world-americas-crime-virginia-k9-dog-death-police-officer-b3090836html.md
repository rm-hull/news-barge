A Virginia police officer has been charged with animal cruelty after his K-9 partner was found dead inside a patrol car parked at the officer’s home.

Charlottesville Police Officer Darius Nash is facing a misdemeanor charge in connection with the death of K-9 Blue, according to the department. The Greene County Commonwealth’s Attorney’s Office notified the department of the charge Thursday.

Blue was found unresponsive inside a Charlottesville police vehicle at Nash’s Greene County home on the evening of August 16. The bloodhound was later pronounced dead.

Virginia State Police’s Bureau of Criminal Investigation, through its Appomattox Field Office, led the criminal investigation, which has since concluded. Nash has been on leave since Blue’s death, according to local reports.

The Charlottesville Police Department said it will now conduct a separate administrative investigation in accordance with department policy.

![Charlottesville Police Officer Darius Nash is facing a misdemeanor animal cruelty charge after his K-9 partner, Blue, was found dead inside a patrol car at his Virginia home](https://static.the-independent.com/2026/11/24/16/05/Work-900-px-x-600-px-(15).png?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“Blue was part of this department and part of this community,” Police Chief Mike Kochis said in a statement. “The criminal process will run its course in Greene County. Our job now is a thorough, fair administrative investigation.”

The department directed questions about the criminal case to Virginia State Police and the Greene County Commonwealth’s Attorney’s Office.

Blue joined the department as a 4-month-old puppy in 2023 and was paired with Nash. The two completed certification to handle tracking calls in April 2024.

“K-9 partners are more than working animals; they are members of our department family,” the department said in an August statement. “They are trained and trusted to serve alongside their handlers in keeping our community safe. The loss of a K-9 partner is felt deeply throughout the entire agency.”

The department also thanked community members for their cards, messages and other gestures of support following Blue’s death.

A celebration of life honoring Blue is expected to be announced at a later date.
