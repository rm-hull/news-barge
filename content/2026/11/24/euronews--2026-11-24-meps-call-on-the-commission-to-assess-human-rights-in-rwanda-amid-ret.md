## The letter signed by 76 MEPs targets a controversial plan to set up a centre in Rwanda for migrants with no legal right to stay in Europe.

A total of seventy-six MEPs have challenged the legality of plans by five EU countries to deport irregular migrants to Rwanda from 2027, and are asking the European Commission for an assessment of the country's human rights record.

In a letter seen by Euronews and sent on Tuesday to EU foreign policy chief Kaja Kallas and Migration Commissioner Magnus Brunner, the MEPs warned that Rwanda may not meet the human rights standards required under EU law for return hubs outside the bloc.

“Besides general human rights concerns, Rwanda has a particular reputation for serious maltreatment of refugees and asylum seekers in the recent past,” the letter says, challenging the initiative launched by Austria, Germany, Greece, the Netherlands and Denmark.

Most of the signatories come from centre-left and left-wing political groups, which have consistently opposed the EU legislation paving the way for the creation of return hubs.

They fear that Rwanda is “unable to fulfil the conditions” required to host a return hub, namely compliance with international human rights standards and international law.

Under the law enabling EU states to set up return hubs outside the bloc, only non-EU countries “where international human rights standards and principles in accordance with international law are respected” are eligible.

The MEPs consider this unlikely to be the case in Rwanda, although the final details of the deal are still under negotiation.

“The Rwandan government under President Paul Kagame is notorious for its harassment, espionage, detention and transnational repression of dissidents and political opposition,” reads the letter.

The lawmakers also condemned Rwanda’s involvement in the conflict in eastern Democratic Republic of Congo (DRC), which has led to “violations of international law and the displacement of millions of persons”.

The European Parliament has previously adopted a firm stance towards Rwanda.

In February 2025, it called for the suspension of an EU-Rwanda memorandum of understanding on critical raw materials, citing alleged war crimes and the occupation of Congolese territory by Rwanda’s defence forces.

Under EU law, the European Commission can initiate an assessment of Rwanda's human rights record, but it has no formal role in approving the plan.

The deportations, expected to begin in 2027, could nonetheless face legal challenges before European courts.
