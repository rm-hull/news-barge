A daughter accused of attempting to murder her 100-year-old mother by giving her morphine told her to “hurry up and die”, a court has heard.

Sarah Pickering, 72, and her brother, Browns Brasseries founder Jeremy Mogford, 79, are charged with the attempted murder of Pamela Mogford, who died on February 15 2022 after she was given a dose of oral morphine solution by her children.

Giving evidence at Oxford Crown Court on Tuesday, Pickering said she believed her mother had suffered a stroke the day before she died and was in a “terrible state”, drifting in and out of consciousness.

The defendants travelled to be with their mother at her house in Bledlow Ridge, Buckinghamshire, on 14 February 2022.

Recalling how Mrs Mogford seemed when she arrived, Pickering told the court: “She was not like my mother – she wasn’t sparky, she was lying on her back, moaning and groaning a bit, and she was very grey.”

![Pamela Mogford died on 15 February 2022 after she was given a dose of oral morphine solution by her children](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/23/14/01M84A3AFD7JF1F4QQFA1Q7070.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Mrs Mogford was attended to by paramedics, who classed her as “end of life”, as well as her GP, Dr Thomas Neale, who gave her two doses of Oramorph – a liquid form of morphine designed to be swallowed.

The drug had been prescribed to Mrs Mogford to ease back pain, and was kept in a kitchen cupboard, the court was told.

Jurors previously heard that when Dr Neale left the house that evening, he advised the defendants not to give their mother any more of the medication.

But Pickering said no such instruction had been made, rather she and her brother had been “left to our own devices”.

She said her mother, who was “clearly in pain”, had been agitated and restless when Dr Neale visited and was much the same after he had left, so the siblings decided to give her another dose of Oramorph.

Mrs Mogford was then visited by a family friend, Stephen Cox.

Pickering’s defence barrister, Benjamin Newton KC, put to her that she had “surprised” Mr Cox during his visit, by saying: “For f\*\*\*’s sake mother, hurry up and die.”

Newton asked: “Is it the kind of thing you would say?”

“Absolutely,” Pickering replied, prompting laughter from the public gallery packed with the siblings’ supporters.

![Sarah Pickering, 72, and her brother, Browns Brasseries founder Jeremy Mogford, 79, are charged with the attempted murder of Pamela Mogford](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/23/14/01M84A3BTPVFPETC1AGHZSH9J2.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Asked what she had meant by the comment, Pickering said: “More really for her, so she’s not suffering anymore.

“It’s not that I wanted her to die but I knew that she wanted to die.”

Pickering added that she thinks her mother, with whom she shared a “very similar” sense of humour, would have found the remark amusing.

She said Mrs Mogford again became “very agitated” after Mr Cox left, so she and her brother gave her another dose of Oramorph, which she said calmed her mother down.

Jurors heard Pickering had texted Mogford after he had returned home, to get his agreement to give their mother a third dose of Oramorph.

She had told him their mother’s breathing had become a “death rattle”, which was “awful” to listen to.

Mogford replied: “Yes, go ahead.”

In her response, Pickering told Mogford she would “wait a little while she’s fast asleep”, jurors heard.

In messages sent to various friends and family members, Pickering described the situation as a “waiting game”.

![Pamela Mogford was attended to by paramedics, who classed her as “end of life”, as well as her GP, Dr Thomas Neale, who gave her two doses of Oramorph – a liquid form of morphine designed to be swallowed.](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/12/15/01M786PH6TM0PYC800942WQCVB.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

She told the court: “We knew mother was dying. It was just a matter of time. Neale had told us, the paramedics had told us.”

Asked whether she had intended to do anything to “make that waiting game shorter” by hastening her mother’s death, Pickering replied: “Absolutely not.”

Asked about a message she had sent to her husband, in which she told him, “I am actually killing her”, Pickering told the court she knew morphine could be a dangerous drug when used inappropriately but said they had been using it “to manage her pain”.

“I think after two glasses of wine I was probably out of my brain a bit,” she added.

Asked what she had meant by the message, Pickering replied: “I can’t answer that question.”

In another message shown to the jury, Pickering told her husband: “I shall wait until Foyle is over then I can go to my grave knowing I have topped someone.”

Asked what the intention behind the message had been, Pickering said there was no intention, adding: “I had had too much to drink.”

She told the court she did not end up giving her mother any more Oramorph because Mrs Mogford fell asleep, adding she would have done, had she shown signs of being in pain.

Mrs Mogford died shortly after midnight.

Asked how she felt after her mother had died, Pickering said she was “very sad”, but relieved that her mother was no longer suffering.

The trial continues.
