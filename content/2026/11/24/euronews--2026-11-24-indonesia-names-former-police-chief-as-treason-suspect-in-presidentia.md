**Dogyu Lee & Euronews Doha bureau**

## An investigation into alleged surveillance of Indonesia’s president has widened into a treason case against senior police figures. It has already led to dismissals and parliamentary scrutiny, with police alleging a plot to undermine Prabowo’s government.

Indonesia’s former national police chief Listyo Sigit Prabowo is among six current and former police officers named as treason suspects in an investigation into alleged illegal surveillance of President Prabowo Subianto.

Indonesian police announced on Monday that they had expanded the wiretapping investigation to cover an alleged plot to undermine the country's government.

Asep Safrudin, head of the National Police’s Criminal Investigation Agency, accused the group of trying to keep Listyo in his post as police chief by weakening public confidence in the president’s administration.

Safrudin alleged that Listyo had ordered the wiretapping and received surveillance reports from the other suspects. Police also accused some of the suspects of organising anti-government protests and producing online content critical of the police.

Investigators seized electronic devices, cash and materials associated with demonstrations as part of their probe.

Safrudin said the offences under investigation carry penalties including life imprisonment and the death penalty.

Listyo and two other senior officers were arrested on 3 November. Police allege that the surveillance operation ran during 2025 and 2026, targeting the Indonesian president and other public figures, including government officials, politicians and journalists.

Listyo became Indonesia's national police chief in January 2021 under then-president Joko Widodo, whom he had previously served as an aide.

He left the police chief’s post on 1 October this year and became presidential chief of staff. Prabowo dismissed him from that role on 4 November, the day after his arrest.

Two other officers have already been dismissed from the force following internal disciplinary proceedings. Listyo is due to face a police ethics hearing on Thursday, 26 November.

On 4 November, the Indonesian military announced plans for a security operation involving nearly 21,000 personnel in Jakarta. The first phase began two days earlier, with troops already stationed in the capital.

Military spokesperson Muhammad Nas said the troop movements were part of routine efforts to maintain national security and territorial integrity.

Authorities have not confirmed a connection between the operation and the wiretapping investigation.
