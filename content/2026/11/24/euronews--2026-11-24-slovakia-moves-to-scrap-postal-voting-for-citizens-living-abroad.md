## The proposed changes have prompted protests across the country. The opposition has said the measure targets pro-Western voters and that it could stop tens of thousands of Slovaks from voting.

Slovak lawmakers backing the country's ruling coalition on Tuesday approved Prime Minister Robert Fico's controversial plan to scrap postal voting for citizens living abroad.

The bill, which would see expats able to vote only at Slovak embassies, passed by 79 votes to 71 in the 150-seat National Council.

Fico, who returned to power in 2023, has argued that the legislation is designed to "prevent fraud and manipulations," without providing evidence.

The proposed changes have prompted protests across the country. The opposition has said the measure targets pro-Western voters and that it could stop tens of thousands of Slovaks from voting.

President Peter Pellegrini's signature is required for the bill to become law.

Around 59,000 Slovaks living overseas reportedly voted by mail in the 2023 parliamentary election, with Fico's Smer party receiving 6.1% of those votes compared to more than 80% for opposition groups.

The move comes after thousands of people took to the streets of Slovakia last week to demonstrate against Fico's government.

Fico has repeatedly clashed with Brussels since returning to office and has maintained close ties to Russian President Vladimir Putin.

Earlier this month, Fico reiterated that Slovakia would have no part in a military loan for Kyiv.

"Slovakia will support peace talks, not further funding of the war," he added in a post on X. "We will continue providing humanitarian aid to Ukraine, but not a single cent for weapons and killing."

Slovakia is due to hold its next parliamentary elections in 2027.
