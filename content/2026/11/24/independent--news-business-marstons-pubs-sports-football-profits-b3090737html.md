Pub operator Marston’s has announced plans to inject further capital into transforming over 100 venues into sports venues after reporting increased profitability.

The decision comes as the business, which manages a portfolio of more than 1,300 pubs across the UK, recorded a slight fall in annual sales. Overall revenues declined by 1.6 per cent to £883.4 million for the year ending 26 September, compared with a year earlier.

Like-for-like sales across managed and partnership premises dropped by 1.2 per cent year-on-year, alongside "broadly flat" performance during the final quarter.

The Wolverhampton-based company stated that income was affected by "increased amounts of closure periods" following temporary shutdowns across dozens of locations to complete conversion projects, mainly in the first half of the year. Results were also partly impacted by a "small number" of pub disposals.

Marston’s has been pushing forward with a raft of refurbishments to its venues, with 60 conversions in the latest financial year.

![Marston’s has said it hopes pub refurbishments and the World Cup will boost sales (Carlsberg Marston’s Brewing Company/PA)](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/05/12/13/fa420ace329483ad0d8456e0017932ecY29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzc4NjczODI1-2.74764548.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

This included 31 pubs converted to its Grandstand sport-focused format, where venues saw a 30 per cent jump in like-for-like revenues, including a 17 per cent rise in drink volumes.

The company said it plans at least 100 more conversions in the new financial year, focused on the Grandstand format, in a bid to accelerate sales growth.

The conversions helped drive a rise in capital spending to £73.8 million for the past year, compared with £61.2 million a year earlier.

This is expected to rise again to around £80 million for the current year.

Meanwhile, Marston’s reported that pre-tax profits climbed by 18.4 per cent to £85.4 million for the year.

Justin Platt, chief executive of the group, said: “We’ve delivered another excellent set of results this year, executing on our strategy to be a high-margin, highly cash generative local pub company.

“For the third year running, profit is significantly up, cash flow is strong and margins have improved, as we continue to deliver exceptional guest experiences every day.”

Shares in the company were 2 per cent lower on Tuesday.
