## The IRGC claimed three tankers hit mines in the crucial strait while under US escort, which the Joint Maritime Information Centre said was unconfirmed, as Qatar's prime minister pressed Rubio and Vance to restart talks.

Iran's army commander-in-chief ordered his navy on Monday to close the Strait of Hormuz "further day by day" until Washington meets Tehran's conditions, warning that any new US military action would draw blows that were "more severe, more crushing and without geographical limitation."

"Until the seven conditions of the Islamic Republic of Iran are fulfilled, not only will the Strait of Hormuz not be opened, it must be closed further day by day, conditions for the enemy must become more difficult, and stronger control will be exercised over it," Major General Amir Hatami said, addressing senior commanders of Iran's armed forces.

Citing orders from Ayatollah Mojtaba Khamenei, Hatami added that if the US "commits a new folly and starts another war, in the new phase the blows will be more severe, more crushing and without geographical limitation."

The elite paramilitary IRGC said on Monday that three oil tankers in the Strait of Hormuz suffered "powerful explosions and massive fires" after hitting naval mines.

One tanker was hit off the southern shores and two more in the east of the strait, "while under extensive air escort by the US army," an IRGC statement said.

The IRGC said it would publish images of the explosions and warned shipowners that "military escort by the US army is no substitute for compliance with maritime regulations."

The Joint Maritime Information Centre (JMIC), in a report covering 19 to 21 November, said it recorded 114 passages through the strait "with the assistance of the United States" and that claims of tankers hitting naval mines were unconfirmed.

Iranian Parliament Speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf said Washington was seeking to impose a neither-war-nor-peace situation on Tehran, threatening there were "military ways to break the blockade that, God willing, we will pursue in the near future."

As head of the Iranian delegation in the Islamabad talks, Ghalibaf said there would be no further negotiations or action before Washington moved.

"Before the implementation of Iran's seven conditions there will be no action and no negotiation, and in response to the enemy's moves we are also ready to give a decisive answer and to change the geography of the battlefield in the event of an enemy mistake," Ghalibaf said.

Foreign ministry spokesman Esmaeil Baghaei told reporters a naval blockade was "tantamount to military aggression" and that Iran had "the right in this regard to use any available means to defend itself."

"We will certainly not tolerate this situation, and we will use all our tools to confront this US aggression," he said.

## Qatar wants both sides at the table

Qatar's Prime Minister Mohammed bin Abdulrahman Al Thani visited Washington on Monday and met US Secretary of State Marco Rubio and Vice President JD Vance to discuss efforts to restart negotiations between Tehran and Washington.

After his talks with Rubio, Al Thani said the region "cannot bear a renewed escalation of tensions between the United States and Iran" and that Doha was working to bring both sides back to the table.

Qatar and the US were also seeking to strengthen their military cooperation, he said. According to AP, citing a source familiar with the proceedings, the meeting with Vance lasted about 10 minutes.

The visit followed US President Donald Trump's statement on Sunday that military strikes on Iran could resume after the New Year. Trump had said earlier this month that he had asked Vance to shift his focus from Iran to domestic policy.

In recent days, Iran has attacked US military bases in Bahrain and Kuwait on several occasions, and Tehran's armed forces have reported almost daily that tankers it describes as violators have struck naval mines.

Qatar hosts the largest US military base in the Middle East and the regional headquarters of CENTCOM, and plays a key mediation role between Tehran and Washington.
