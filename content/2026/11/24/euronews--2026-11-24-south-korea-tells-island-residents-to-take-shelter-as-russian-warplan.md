**Dogyu Lee & Euronews Doha bureau**

## South Korea has repeatedly scrambled fighter jets in response to Russian military flights. Tuesday’s approach to an inhabited island brought a rare shelter warning for civilians, although the aircraft stayed outside the country’s territorial airspace.

South Korea scrambled fighter jets and told residents of an eastern island to take shelter on Tuesday after Russian air force aircraft came close to entering its territorial airspace, the military said.

The aircraft entered the Korea Air Defence Identification Zone and approached Ulleungdo island before turning away. The Joint Chiefs of Staff said no violation of South Korean territorial airspace occurred.

An air defence identification zone extends beyond national airspace and is used to identify approaching aircraft. Entering it does not by itself mean crossing into a country’s territorial airspace.

“Airspace intrusion was anticipated, therefore in order to protect the safety of our people, we issued a warning preemptively,” Jang Do-young, a spokesperson for the Joint Chiefs of Staff, told a briefing on Tuesday.

The interior ministry issued the shelter warning at around 7:52 am local time (11:52 pm CET Monday), telling residents to move underground or inside buildings. It was lifted within minutes.

Conflicting messages added to the confusion. The initial alert said Russian aircraft had entered South Korean airspace. A later message from North Gyeongsang provincial authorities misidentified the aircraft as possibly North Korean. The military clarified that they were Russian and had remained outside territorial airspace.

South Korean news agency Yonhap reported that the aircraft spent about 20 minutes inside the identification zone. It said this was the first time South Korea’s military had issued a precautionary civilian alert over a possible airspace violation by Russian warplanes.

South Korea’s defence ministry summoned Russia’s military attaché Colonel Nikolai Marchenko later on Tuesday to protest and demand measures to prevent a repeat.

The ministry said repeated unannounced entries into the identification zone and the latest close approach could unnecessarily raise tensions on the Korean Peninsula and across the region.

The foreign ministry also conveyed its objections to Moscow through diplomatic channels, Yonhap reported.

## Separate incidents in Poland and Japan

Poland also reported a brief Russian drone incursion late on Monday. Polish officials said the aircraft turned east and that forces had decided against shooting it down because of the potential risk to civilians.

That followed an incident on Friday in which three Russian Kh-101 missiles landed in Poland, according to Polish prosecutors.

NATO has authorised the deployment of an airborne early-warning aircraft to Poland in response to the missile incident, an alliance spokesperson confirmed on Monday. The aircraft uses radar to detect and track activity over a wide area.

Japan separately scrambled fighter jets last Thursday after two aircraft believed to be Russian briefly entered its territorial airspace near Okinoshima, off Fukuoka. Tokyo lodged a diplomatic protest with Moscow.
