Donald Trump’s administration has reportedly reached an agreement to let Kilmar Abrego Garcia leave the U.S. voluntarily, ending a nearly two-year legal battle over the fate of a Salvadoran immigrant whose story has been at the center of the president’s mass deportation campaign.

He is expected to travel to Spain as soon as Tuesday night.

Abrego Garcia’s case has been a lightning rod in the Trump administration’s efforts to rapidly arrest and deport tens of thousands of people from the country despite legal safeguards and humanitarian protections that the president has also eroded since returning to the White House.

The administration has spent the last year trying to deport Abrego Garcia a second time after the government unlawfully deported him to a brutal prison in his home country, triggering the high-profile legal battle for his return.

Abrego Garcia has an American wife and child and has lived in Maryland for years after fleeing gang violence in El Salvador and entering the U.S. illegally as a teenager. An immigration judge blocked the government from deporting him to his home country in 2019.

![The Trump administration has reached an agreement to allow Kilmar Abrego Garcia to leave the US voluntarily after a months-long legal saga that has been a lightning rod for the president’s anti-immigration agenda](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/24/23/2026-11-24T230621Z_56539716_RC2WXNAXHINC_RTRMADP_3_USA-TRUMP-MIGRATION-ABREGO.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

But last year, federal agents abruptly deported Abrego Garcia to El Salvador’s notorious CECOT prison where he says he endured torture and “severe” abuse for several weeks before he was transferred to a separate jail.

Government officials admitted in court that his removal was due to an “error,” and several federal judges and a unanimous Supreme Court ordered the administration to “facilitate” his return.

The government spent weeks battling those orders while White House officials launched a barrage of public attacks against him and declared that he would never again step foot in the country.

He was returned to the U.S. last June — only to face allegations that he illegally moved other immigrants across the country. He pleaded not guilty.

A federal judge later dismissed the criminal case against him after finding that the administration unlawfully targeted him as part of a smear campaign.

“Objective evidence” has shown that federal prosecutors only brought charges against Abrego Garcia after he won his lawsuit challenging his arrest and removal, the judge ruled.

![Abrego Garcia’s wrongful removal from the US to the notorious CECOT prison in El Salvador — and ICE’s attempts to re-detain and deport him a second time — launched high-profile legal battles at the center of the Trump administration’s attempts to rapidly deport thousands of immigrants](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/24/23/2253185139..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The administration has, at the same time, tried to deport him to at least five different countries, including four in Africa, and refused to let him leave the country for Costa Rica.

In a memo issued in March, ICE’s then-acting director Todd Lyons argued that sending him to Costa Rica would be “prejudicial to the United States.”

In April, the administration “analyzed and eliminated all other options” from the table and settled on Liberia as “a final country of removal,” despite his own attempts to voluntarily remove himself to Costa Rica.

Government lawyers argued in court documents that the U.S. has expended significant political capital and entered “high-stakes” and “extensive negotiations” to send him to Liberia.

But the judge overseeing his deportation case had blocked Immigration and Customs Enforcement from re-deporting or detaining him, noting earlier this year that the government has made “one empty threat after another to remove him to countries in Africa with no real chance of success.”

Both sides were scheduled to discuss his case at a hearing in October, but U.S. District Judge Paula Xinis abruptly ended the hearing after she met privately with attorneys in her chambers.

Democratic Senator Chris Van Hollen of Maryland, who traveled to El Salvador in an attempt to secure Abrego Garcia’s release from prisons there, said his constituent is now “traveling to a country of his choice, a free man — unharmed and with dignity.”

“While the Trump administration had been pursuing their vindictive prosecution against Kilmar and desperately trying to ship him against his will to countries known for human rights abuses, the administration was forced to come to this agreement that respects Kilmar's will and the due process rights guaranteed to him and to all in our nation,” Van Hollen said in a statement Tuesday night.

“I am relieved that he will be safe, in a country of his choosing, and free — including from this administration’s constant persecution,” he added. “This is a victory for due process and our Constitution.”
