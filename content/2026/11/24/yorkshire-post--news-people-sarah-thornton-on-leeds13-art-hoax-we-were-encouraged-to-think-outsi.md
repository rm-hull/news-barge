# Sarah Thornton on Leeds13 art hoax: 'We were encouraged to think outside the box - I think a lot of media felt duped'

## ​Leeds artist Sarah Thornton speaks to Laura Reid about her lifelong creative endeavours and how she creates vibrant work inspired by things that bring her joy.

From the minute that Sarah Thornton could hold a crayon, she was off in her own world. Always drawing, always making things, as she grew up on the cusp of Harehills and Chapeltown in Leeds. “It was just like a drive in me,” she says. “And it hasn’t stopped since.”

It was somewhat inevitable she’d find a creative streak. Her father was musical, with a gift for singing, and her mother came from a large Irish family where music and art “were just part of people”. It took a while, however, for Sarah to find her feet. But since launching her art business more than 20 years ago, the now 58-year-old has been creating unique and vibrant art and interior items inspired by the things in life that bring her joy.

“I work in lots of different styles and that’s a good indicator of who I am,” she says. “Every phase of life has been reflected within my practice really...I am really interested in chaos and order because that really reflects the human condition. I’ll do very detailed, controlled drawings, and then I’ll do one where I go mad and completely let myself go. It’s like an experiment with my own mind and productivity.”

As a self-described ‘nature junkie’, a portion of her work draws from the natural world and the emotions it provokes. If she’s painting a bird, she’s thinking about its song, whilst her florals are bright and bold, fused with elements from different eras. Sarah’s also known for her Discoball Diva range, born out of her love of music and of dancing under the glinting allure of a mirror ball. As well as producing fine art paintings, she creates prints for cards, wallpaper, make up bags, cushions and tea towels, proud that her work finds is way inside other people’s homes.

This weekend, Sarah will be showcasing some of what she creates at the Haworth & Yorkshire Creatives Christmas Makers Market, organised by Rosalia Ferrara, the face behind Brontë-inspired brand Brontë Bitch. The event, which celebrates independent artists, makers and creative small businesses from across Yorkshire, is back at Haworth’s historic Old School Room and Sarah will be part of the featured artists stage.

“I used to be really shy and unable to do creative work in a live situation but now I’m over it,” she reflects. “A lot of artists, including myself, really enjoy making the art and even have a great presence on social media but as far as pushing yourself, that doesn’t come naturally….Rosalia has a brightness and spark that can make things happen. She’s got that drive and tenacity to take the event forward to new audiences.”

It will be the first time Sarah has appeared at the makers market, despite her long presence in the art world – and her journey to get there. It began with the study of art at A-level but Sarah soon dropped out of college and went into full time employment in the jewellery and antique trade, needing to earn a living.

![Artist Sarah Thornton at her home studio in Leeds.](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOmQwZWE0YTA4LTc5OTUtNDhhYS04NzBhLWI4OTY5NDBlZWQwZTpmOTliMDU5Zi03NjE0LTQzODEtYjkxYy1iYzE5YTQ0ZmE3Yjg=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


“That was really fascinating. I’ve got a photographic memory so every little thing that I saw stayed with me,” she says. “I was absorbing all these amazing things I was seeing and having the experience of going from being pretty shy and unable to talk to people to being able to talk to anyone.”

Later, Sarah decided to return to education, first doing an access course in sociology and equality before beginning a degree in the history and theory of art and design. That wasn’t for her, however; and so at the age of 28 she found herself returning to college for a more practical qualification in art and design. It helped her then to secure a place on the fine art degree course at the University of Leeds in the late 1990s.

Whilst there, Sarah became part of the art collective known as Leeds13, made up of students on the course who decided in their third year to work collaboratively. They’re perhaps best known for their art-world hoax Going Places in 1998, which fooled the media into believing they’d spent exhibition donations on a holiday to Spain. One group member later revealed on national radio that the entire trip was an elaborate simulation, a conceptual art experiment which achieved what it set out to do – start a debate on the nature of art and explore media reporting on truths and untruths.

“We were encouraged to think outside the box,” recalls Sarah. “At that point, painting and illustration weren’t the done thing, it was (all about) conceptual art. We thought can we disappear for a week and before this art show, do a set of photographs that look like reality? And it turned out we could and we did.”

“All our art tutors loved it. They were very supportive,” she continues. “It went even more bonkers than I thought it would...I think a lot of places felt duped. But that was part of it. It was (showing how) things get taken up and whisked into a frenzy and then get spat out onto the front pages.”

In the years after graduating in 1999, Sarah, who is still in touch with many of the members of Leeds13, then began to slowly make a living as an artist, finding solace in illustrations and paintings during a difficult period of her life after losing her brother. In 2003, came the launch of her art business, which today she runs from her attic studio at home in Chapeltown.

“Art means many things to me. Art, poetry and music I think are the essence of the human experience put into something else. The good, the bad, the ugly, the political,” she muses. “I think we need more hands-on art from a very young age...It’s a really absorbing, wonderful thing.”
