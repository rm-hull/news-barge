## Glorious GMMK Eternal: Two-minute review

Sometimes simple really is better. That seems to have been the ethos behind the new Glorious GMMK Eternal, a 96% form factor gaming keyboard that I could immediately tell would be worthy of standing among the best gaming keyboards on the market.

There are no fancy bells and whistles here; no tiny LED display, no multi-device connectivity, no optical key switches with adjustable actuation. Glorious has focused on the basics, delivering a product that simply functions exceptionally well for both typing and gaming.

![The Glorious GMMK Eternal gaming keyboard, pictured on a dark marble worktop.](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/WV2edser5dJ6Ku4eVWdeGi-1200-80.jpg)

A big part of this is the build quality. While it might look fairly pedestrian in aesthetic terms as far as gaming keyboards go, the GMMK Eternal feels like a high-end device straight out of the box despite its **$99.99 / £89.99 (around AU$150)** price tag.

Beyond the per-key RGB lighting (which is practically a given for any decent gaming keyboard these days), the design is not at all ostentatious. The backplate is solid aluminum with a very slim bezel around the edges, and the keycaps are double-shot ABS plastic, both in matte black. There's no in-your-face branding, save for the Glorious logo stamped on the underside.

As I mentioned earlier, the GMMK Eternal uses a 96% key layout; in other words, you get the functionality of a full-size keyboard, but with a much smaller overall footprint. The numpad, arrow keys, and function row are all still in place, but some of the navigation keys are eliminated, and there are no dedicated media controls – one thing I do like to see on gaming keyboards, so it's a shame they weren't included here, even if it does allow for a more svelte overall device.

![The Glorious GMMK Eternal gaming keyboard, pictured on a dark marble worktop.](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/m3A3dFUW67oqfZGt4Av3Hi-1200-80.jpg)

On the underside, there are double-level feet that allow for three different inclines. Connectivity is wired-only, via an included USB-C to USB-A cable. Anyone looking for multi-device functionality should look elsewhere – as I noted before, Glorious is keeping things as simple as possible here.

Simple doesn't mean basic, though. The linear red Glorious Fox key switches are excellent, providing a solid amount of actuation force (55g, a little beyond the average linear switch) with a pleasingly muted striking sound thanks to the triple-layer sound-damping underneath, which uses a silicone base with two layers of PU foam surrounding each switch.

As a linear switch enjoyer, I was very enamored with the GMMK Eternal's typing experience. It doesn't quite give the 'thwock' you might expect from a more expensive mechanical keyboard, but nonetheless the sound is very satisfying. If you prefer a different type of switch, like clicky blue or the classic tactile brown, every switch on the Eternal is easily hot-swappable with an included tool.

![The Glorious GMMK Eternal gaming keyboard, pictured on a dark marble worktop.](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/xBuuooTdnBRNcC9Bciq8Hi-1200-80.jpg)

In terms of actual use, I had a great time with the Glorious GMMK Eternal. It felt reliable and responsive both for everyday typing and for all kinds of games, including competitive shooters like *The Finals*and* Marathon*and MMOs like* World of Warcraft Forever.* I also tested out the Glorious Core utility software, which lets you tweak the (somewhat limited) settings of the keyboard

I did use my own wrist rest for some of my time testing the GMMK Eternal, since it doesn't come with one included in the box (unsurprising, given the stripped-down approach here), but I did actually find that it was perfectly comfortable to use without one on the lowest incline.

Overall, the Glorious GMMK Eternal is an easy recommendation, purely because it's such good value for money.

## Glorious GMMK Eternal review: Price & availability

* **$99.99 / £89.99 (around AU$150)**
* **Available now in the US and UK**
* **Excellent value for money** The Glorious GMMK Eternal will cost you**$99.99 / £89.99 (around AU$150)**, and is available now in the US and UK; shoppers in Australia will have to try their luck with third-party retailers.

There's only one configuration available, which uses the manufacturer's own Fox Red key switches, but these are hot-swappable so you're always free to replace or upgrade them later on.

It's not exactly cheap for a wired mechanical keyboard, but it notably does come in at a lower cost than gaming-oriented competitors like the HyperX Origins 2 1800, and it's a lot cheaper than the (admittedly excellent) Razer BlackWidow V4 Pro 75%. In terms of bang for your buck, the GMMK Eternal offers a great value proposition.

## Buy the Glorious GMMK Eternal if…

**You love quiet switches** The GMMK Eternal only comes with Glorious Fox Red switches, but they're so delightfully quiet and thunky that you won't want to swap them out.**You want value for money**  
For the premium build quality and typing experience it offers, this keyboard is very sensibly priced indeed.

## Don't buy it if…

**You want lots of features** Without a built-in display or dedicated media controls, the GMMK Eternal is pretty basic as far as gaming keyboard go.**You want something super cheap**  
Although the GMMK Eternal is very competitively priced, PC gamers with very tight budgets might still want to look elsewhere.
