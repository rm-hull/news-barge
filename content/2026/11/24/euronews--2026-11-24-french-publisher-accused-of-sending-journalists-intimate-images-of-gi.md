**Laure Valentin**

## Publishing house Tinbad is accused of having used intimate images of Gisèle Pelicot to promote a book, three years after the start of her legal fight against sexual violence.

A French publishing house, Tinbad, has reportedly sent journalists intimate photographs and videos of Gisèle Pelicot that were recorded without her knowledge by her former husband, Dominique Pelicot, according to several French media outlets.

The images were allegedly used to promote *Emprises, les impasses d'une success story*(* Coercive Control: The Dead Ends of a Success Story*), an essay by the French philosopher and psychoanalyst Sabine Prokhoris. In it, Prokhoris questions the concept of "emprise", a French term for one person's psychological hold over another, and even challenges the idea that Gisèle Pelicot is a victim.

Prokhoris is known for books that take a critical stance on the #MeToo movement. She told the French daily Libération that she was not behind the decision to send the images.

Gisèle Pelicot's lawyers condemned the episode as a fresh assault on her.

"Today I find myself once again humiliated, exposed and hurt," Gisèle Pelicot told the French news channel BFMTV. "I have fought to regain my dignity and my freedom. I will not let anyone take them away from me."

## Publisher denies having distributed pornographic photos

Tinbad has said it will not reprint the book and that Prokhoris will no longer promote it. It described the episode as an "unfortunate communication that was not suited to the content of the book".

In a statement on its website, the publisher apologised to Gisèle Pelicot and to Prokhoris, insisting that "absolutely no image of rape, nor any video of a pornographic nature" had been sent to the press. Euronews has confirmed that the book is no longer available on the website.

According to the publisher, around 280 copies are still in circulation.

Gisèle Pelicot became an international figure in the fight against sexual violence after the Mazan rape trial, named after the town in south-eastern France where the couple lived. Between 2011 and 2020, her then-husband drugged and raped her and recruited dozens of men online to rape her while she was unconscious.

In December 2024, Dominique Pelicot and 50 other men were convicted, and he was sentenced to 20 years in prison.

The case has particular resonance in the United Kingdom, where a similar trial has just concluded. Joanne Young, 49, has been described as the "British Gisèle Pelicot".

For 13 years, the mother of three was drugged and raped by her then-husband, Philip Young, who also recruited other men online to abuse her at their home in Swindon, in southern England. He pleaded guilty to dozens of sexual offences against her earlier this year.

On 19 November, a jury at Winchester Crown Court in England found four of those men guilty of sexual offences against her. Like Pelicot, Young chose to waive her anonymity, which is guaranteed for life to victims of sexual offences in the UK.
