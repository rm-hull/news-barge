Wednesday’s turkey pardoning ceremony was a low-stakes event, but it went awry in the way that only a Trump administration event can. And naturally, it came just a couple hours after the president gave Rudy Giuliani the Presidential Medal of Freedom.

The ceremony itself was a fairly representative illustration of the current state of American government. Trump began by observing that the new helipad was "locationally perfect” for the job, with an excellent view of his own renovation work on the facade of the White House. “Do you mind if I turn and look?” he said, before doing just that. Because when does a president ever get the chance to just stand and admire his handiwork?

He then, of course, launched into a rambling tour of his administration's other, less important achievements, including bolstering the stock market, re-energizing the military, putting in place that spectacularly brutal and unpopular line of immigration enforcement, doing something about the price of eggs (don’t mention gas or literally any other grocery), and doing something unclear about the Iran war he started. He finished off by praising his own electoral performance in the place where the turkeys had been born and raised, West Virginia.

“We won it by 45 points,” the president said, when reading off his script at the podium. He then looked up, presumably remembered that the midterms had happened, and added, “At least I did when I was on the ballot… I don’t know what the hell happened with this last election.”

Not long after that, Trump announced that a special guest-cum-turkey suite would be available at the White House for next year's ceremony, apparently because the traditional accommodation arrangements for presidential turkeys — a fancy DC hotel room — were due for an upgrade. It’ll be right next to the “ballroom military complex,” a distinctly dystopian phrase that has been repeatedly forced upon us over the past few months as Trump has incessantly talked about the construction work surrounding his very expensive ballroom.

![A giddy President Trump and Melania Trump with one of two freed birds](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/24/20/2306706118..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

But don’t worry! The ballroom was financed by the tech oligarchy, not *just* the American taxpayer. And that includes Jensen Huang, ex-but-now-also-new CEO of Nvidia, possible boyfriend of a Chinese spy and worst husband ever, who contributed a reported $25 million toward the project and recently starred in an AI-generated Truth Social image on the president’s account where he holds a bra triumphantly in the air. Anything to keep the turkeys comfortable, eh?

Freedom and Feathers, the president told onlookers today, would receive a “full pardon” for whatever crimes they’re supposed to have committed. They probably weren’t the only ones at the event who will require one. Their brother-in-turkeyness, Giuliani — also recently pardoned, but in his case for trying to help overturn a democratic election — gets a shiny lifetime achievement award. And the cycle of giving ceremonial thanks to the birdbrains continues.

In a slightly faltering voice, Trump added that he’s asking God to look after the US military — and if he keeps leaning on boards to bring back CEOs who are a threat to national security, the military is probably going to need all the help it can get. He thanked Melania “on behalf of our country” for her work with foster children (“You’ve done a fantastic job. Thank you, honey.”) And he briefly resurrected a couple of vintage Trump talking points: the idea that he never got enough credit for the Covid vaccine, and the story about the White House doctor supposedly declaring him the healthiest president who ever lived.

![Trump grants a presidential pardon to "Freedom" the National Thanksgiving turkey as first lady Melania Trump and Speaker of the House Mike Johnson look on during the 79th annual National Thanksgiving Turkey Presentation on the South Lawn of the White House Thursday.](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/24/20/2306703990..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The mics cut out as he called up Melania and wandered over to Freedom, who he pardoned singly because Feathers had wandered off into the greenery. Then the music started up again and Donald awkwardly took Melania’s gloved hand. They walked back toward the White House — he leaned over to say something to her; she didn’t appear to reply — and she dropped his hand as soon as they arrived in the doorway. The large sunglasses never came off her face. Her Louboutins glinted in the sunlight.

And so Rudy Giuliani, once America’s mayor and then America’s weirdest hair dye incident outside Four Seasons Landscaping, has been given a medal.

Never mind the disbarment or the defamation lawsuit from Georgia election workers that he lost, or the fact that he went broke having to pay those election workers a settlement. Never mind that he did what so many MAGA figures do — fell under the spell of Trump, worked tirelessly as Donald’s attack dog to the cost of his own reputation and his career, before getting cast aside — because now he has a lovely gold consolation prize he can take home and turn over in his hands in the early morning hours when he thinks about what could have been.

Three turkeys have been given the chance to live out their lives, in Trump’s words, “in solitude and peace.” Giuliani likely gets the solitude. I’d wager that it’s Feathers and Freedom who’ll get the peace. Somewhere in the corridors of the White House, Melania peels off her gloves and throws them in the sanitizer cycle.
