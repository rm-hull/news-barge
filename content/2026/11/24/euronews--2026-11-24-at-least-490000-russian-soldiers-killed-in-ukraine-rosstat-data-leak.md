## Two hacking groups extracted the data from the statistics agency's systems on 1 November. The figure comes from death certificates registered up to the end of August, so it excludes missing servicemen and deaths not yet recorded.

A hack of Russia's federal statistics agency Rosstat has revealed that at least 490,000 Russian military personnel have been killed in Ukraine since the start of Moscow's full-scale invasion in February 2022, according to data obtained by hackers and analysed by multiple independent news outlets.

Two hacking groups, Cyber Partisans of Belarus and Silent Crow — which also carried out a 2025 attack on Aeroflot's servers — extracted the data in a breach on the night of 1 November.

The hackers accessed Rosstat's electronic document management system and database, which contains around 23 terabytes of statistical, economic and personal data, including census materials.

The figure of at least 490,000 deaths derives from official death certificates registered between February 2022 and the end of August 2026.

Meduza, applying actuarial and epidemiological modelling to the data, estimated at least 720,000 irrecoverable losses when it includes those missing in action. Rosstat's documents alone cannot capture the full toll, as the database does not include servicemen whose deaths have not yet been registered.

Researchers and journalists justify including the missing in total loss figures because, according to Mediazona, confirmed survival is recorded in fewer than 10% of missing servicemen cases.

Under current battlefield conditions, with drone warfare making the evacuation of bodies increasingly difficult, the death tally is likely to grow.

Deaths are also registered with an average delay of 3.5 months, and those of missing soldiers with a delay of about a year, Novaya Evropa reported.

Using the conservative estimate of 490,000 to 500,000 dead, Russia sustained an average of 280 to 300 fatalities a day — one death every five minutes — according to Important Stories, which examined the leak alongside more than a dozen other independent outlets.

The outlet found that 92% of Russian servicemen died from explosions and shrapnel, with firearms accounting for only 1.3% of deaths. The Insider identified more than 370,000 of the dead by name.

The BBC Russian Service and Mediazona said at least 7,421 foreigners have been killed fighting on Russia's side.

## Suicides, alcohol and drug use prevalent

Journalists also analysed deaths among Russian servicemen from non-combat causes. Around 2,500 died as a result of alcohol and drug use or suicide.

According to confidential Rosstat data analysed by the international news outlets, more than 1,000 Russian servicemen took their own lives since the start of the invasion.

The deaths of another 885 soldiers in Ukraine or near its borders were registered as firearm injuries "with undetermined intent," a classification experts believe is used in some cases to conceal suicides.

Together, the two figures represent a number comparable to a full Russian regiment.

Military analysts note the problem is deepened in the Russian armed forces by intense pressure from fellow soldiers and commanders, fear of assignment to high-casualty fighting areas known as "meatgrinder assaults," and contracts that in practice prevent soldiers from leaving the war.

## Toll highest away from Moscow

The number of Russian soldiers killed in Ukraine is the highest in Russia's Far East and Siberia relative to their populations.

Russia's Jewish Autonomous Region in the Far East recorded nearly 1,000 deaths per 100,000 inhabitants, Zabaykalsky around 800, Chukotka 760 and Tuva 650.

Moscow, which the authorities have worked to insulate from the visible effects of the war, recorded 63 deaths per 100,000 inhabitants, according to Important Stories.

The Kremlin does not publish or recognise figures for Russian losses from the invasion. Russian authorities have restricted access to official databases and prosecuted those conducting independent counts.

Ukraine's General Staff estimated Russia's losses since February 2022 at 1,639,641 servicemen, encompassing both killed and wounded, although the figure has not been independently verified.

The Economist, in a study published at the end of October, put the number of Russian servicemen killed at between 374,000 and 770,000, and total losses including the wounded at between 1.4 and 2.1 million.

The Ukrainian armed forces' General Staff reported that October of this year was the bloodiest month for Russia since the invasion began.

The Insider said the Russian armed forces suffered their heaviest losses in spring 2025, during fighting on the Pokrovsk axis, when more than 61,000 were killed over three months.
