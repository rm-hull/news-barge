Now you can say yes to the dress — without committing to a hefty price tag.

David’s Bridal is testing a new rental program that lets shoppers rent wedding gowns, bridesmaid dresses and special-occasion outfits without committing to buying them.

The new Something Borrowed program launched last week at 11 of the nationwide chain’s Pennsylvania stores, giving customers the chance to try on dresses in person before taking their favorite look home for 14 days.

Unlike some online rental services, the program lets shoppers see how a dress fits before renting it. David’s Bridal employees, known as Dream Makers, can also help with styling and fit, including minor reversible alterations such as strap adjustments and wrist loops, the company says.

Renting a dress costs 50 percent of its regular price for Diamond Loyalty members and 55 percent for other customers. For example, a $500 dress would cost $250 to rent for members and $275 for nonmembers.

![The new Something Borrowed program at David’s Bridal includes dry cleaning and costs 50 percent to 55 percent of a dress’s original price, depending on loyalty membership](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/24/14/2270149908..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Customers must sign a rental agreement and pay with a credit or debit card. Normal wear and tear is covered, and anyone who falls in love with their rental can purchase it at its regular retail price. Otherwise, dresses must be returned to the same store within the 14-day rental period.

The pilot program is being tested out at 11 Pennsylvania shops. Customers can book an appointment online, call a participating store or walk in to browse available dresses.

David’s Bridal has not announced when the program might expand to additional locations. The company is using the pilot to gauge customer interest and determine whether in-store rentals could become a bigger part of its business.

The move comes as shoppers increasingly look for alternatives to buying outfits they may only wear once. David’s Bridal estimates the fashion rental market could reach $2.8 billion, citing industry data that suggests 62 percent of shoppers prefer renting clothes for one-time occasions. The company also said one in three brides now considers renting a dress or choosing a second wedding gown for their celebrations.

"We've all experienced it - a closet full of dresses we've worn once, but nothing to wear for the next big occasion," David’s Bridal CEO Kelly Cook said in a statement. "With Something Borrowed, we're giving our guests more freedom and flexibility to dress for life's biggest moments without the commitment of owning every look."
