Proton and Surfshark regularly rank among the best VPNs, and right now their mid-tier plans are closer than ever.

Thanks to Black Friday, Proton VPN Plus and Surfshark One are $2.49/month on two-year terms, so choosing between them suddenly isn’t about price at signup.

Instead, the question is what happens later, and what you actually get: Proton leans into privacy-first credentials, while Surfshark One bundles a whole security suite.

Here, then, is everything you need to know to decide which deal is worth your money this Black Friday.

## Proton VPN Plus vs Surfshark One: Features

![Smartphone with Surfshark Web Content Blocker running](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/NZiFtMBwxxp3uew9fduisK-1200-80.jpg)

Surfshark and Proton manage their VPN plans differently. Surfshark offers three plan tiers, with Surfshark One as the middle tier, while Proton only offers one VPN plan.

As Proton's only VPN plan, Proton VPN Plus includes everything your VPN needs: Tor over VPN (a rare feature among mainstream VPNs), the Stealth protocol to slip past censorship and firewalls, NetShield ad and malware blocking, and a VPN Accelerator to boost speeds.

Surfshark One, by contrast, is less a VPN plan than a security bundle: antivirus, data breach alerts, and Alternative ID, which generates masked email addresses and personal details, so you can sign up for services without exposing your real details in a breach.

Neither plan includes a fully fledged password manager: Surfshark doesn’t have one, and although Proton offers Proton Pass as part of its wider ecosystem, VPN Plus includes only the free version.

As far as VPN fundamentals, both cover the bases well. Both have a reliable kill switch, split tunneling - though Surfshark’s Bypasser is the more flexible and comprehensive of the two - and obfuscation – Proton via Stealth, Surfshark via Camouflage and NoBorders modes.

Both also offer multihop, though with different benefits. Proton’s Secure Core wins on security architecture, since traffic passes through Proton-owned, hardened servers in privacy-friendly jurisdictions, while Surfshark’s MultiHop wins on configurability, letting you pick your own entry and exit pairs from *every* Surfshark location.

One difference security-conscious buyers should note: Surfshark has already rolled out post-quantum encryption on WireGuard and uses RAM-only servers; Proton’s VPN hasn’t shipped post-quantum support yet and relies on full-disk encryption.

Otherwise, only Proton’s apps are fully open source, but both VPNs have a solid independent audit history.

## Proton VPN Plus vs Surfshark One: Speed and unblocking

Tested over WireGuard, Surfshark measured 2005 Mbps to the nearest server and 1739 Mbps connecting to the UK from the US; Proton followed at 1783 Mbps and 1397 Mbps, respectively. Either way, they consistently rank among the fastest VPNs we’ve tested.

Streaming is similar: two strong performers with different blind spots. Both unblocked multiple Netflix libraries, BBC iPlayer, and 9Now. Beyond that, it’s a split decision – Proton struggled with ITVX and TVNZ+ and failed to access Prime Video across several countries, while Surfshark handled those but stumbled on UK Disney+ and YouTube.

## Proton VPN Plus vs Surfshark One: Pricing

![Proton VPN website download page on windows laptop](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/SJtS4QJrmUdgrg55RYwNJc-1200-80.png)

Where price is concerned, the two-year plans look identical this Black Friday at $2.49/month. Yet look closer, and they’re not quite the same.

Proton VPN bills $59.76 for the first 24 months, then renews at $83.88 every 12 months, while Surfshark One runs 27 months for $67.23 – the same rate plus three bonus months – then renews at $99/year (neither price includes tax).

Zoom out, and Proton edges ahead. You commit to three fewer months, and the renewal rate runs about $15 a year cheaper.

Then again, Surfshark’s three bonus months delay that steeper renewal by a quarter, and the deal comes with a 7-day free trial ( you’ll still need a payment method, and remember to cancel).

As mentioned already, Surfshark splits into three tiers – Starter, One, and One+. On this Black Friday deal, Surfshark’s Starter and One cost the same.

This makes One a near-free upgrade: antivirus and breach alerts for effectively nothing. One+ adds Incogni data removal (US, UK, EU, Swiss, and Canadian residents only) and identity theft coverage (US only).

Proton offers Plus and Unlimited, the latter being a bargain at $6.49/month if you’d regularly use the included premium Mail, Drive, and Pass services. Otherwise, Plus is the smarter buy.

The price gap widens on shorter-term plans. Both providers offer one-year plans, but monthly plans have the biggest gap: Proton VPN Plus is just $9.99/month, whereas Surfshark One is twice the cost at $19.95/month.

But Proton VPN Plus covers 10 simultaneous connections; Surfshark offers unlimited on every tier. For a solo user, 10 is plenty – but for a whole household pooling one subscription, Surfshark’s policy could prove a deciding factor.

Both offer apps for all major platforms, and Proton’s recent app improvements have helped put it on a par with Surfshark for ease of use.

## Verdict

With Proton VPN Plus and Surfshark One at $2.49/month this Black Friday, the initial price won’t decide this one.

Overall, Proton VPN Plus takes it. It boasts cheaper renewal, a shorter commitment, and fully open-source apps – plus Secure Core and Tor over VPN for privacy purists.

However, Surfshark One is the right pick for plenty of people – especially households where unlimited simultaneous connections matter most, or anyone wanting antivirus or breach alerts thrown in. It also edged Proton in our latest speed tests. With savings this deep, it’s hard to go wrong with either.
