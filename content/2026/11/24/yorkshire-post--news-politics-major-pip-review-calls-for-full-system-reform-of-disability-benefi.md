# Pip review: major report calls for ‘full system reform’ which could lead to less money for some

## Some disability benefit claimants might get less money under potential changes to eligibility, the co-author of a major review into the issue, which called for “full system reform”, has said.

The year-long Timms review into personal independence payment (Pip) said reforms could involve a “rebalancing process” to ensure payments better reflect a person’s needs.

Disability Minister Sir Stephen Timms said the report did not make recommendations about eligibility, instead saying that would be up to the Government in a white paper next year.

The Department for Work and Pensions has accepted its two main changes – reducing unnecessary award reviews for people with lifelong or degenerative conditions, and fast-tracks for people with serious, well-evidenced conditions and those receiving end-of-life care to have Pip payments made without the need for a full assessment.

However, review co-chair Sharon Brennan said such any “rebalancing” would likely lead to some people getting more based on their needs “and some people therefore might get less”.

The review has called for a “more personalised” approach when people are being assessed as to whether they should get a payment and how much, suggesting it should focus on what support can be provided to help someone participate in society rather than the current system, which looks more at “what people cannot do”.

Pip – currently paid to someone with a long-term physical or mental health condition or disability, and intended to help with everyday tasks and extra living costs – is a “valued and important benefit” but the process for claiming is “complex, hard to navigate, burdensome” and sometimes “dehumanising”, the final report said today.

The benefit was introduced in April 2013 and, under the current system, recipients can get between £30.30 and a maximum of £194.60 a week.

Total spending on Pip in 2026/27 is forecast to be £32.1 billion, looks set to rise each year for the rest of the decade and is expected to reach £41.5 billion in 2030/31.

A think tank report published last month found around one in six working-age people getting disability benefit do not face extra costs because of their conditions.

Warning against “waste” in the system, the Resolution Foundation estimated £4.4 billion could be saved if payments were stopped or lowered depending on the level of limitations reported by those who said they faced no extra costs.

Among its recommendations for reform to the system, the Timms review has called for “an award that is responsive to need”.

It said: “Eligibility criteria should ensure that the benefit is fit for purpose and better responds to real-life need. This is likely to require a rebalancing process to ensure that awards more accurately reflect individual needs.”

Louise Murphy, senior economist at the Resolution Foundation, said the wording leaves “flexibility for when the Government comes to do its White Paper to say ‘well, the review was clear that to have a better system there will be some winners and losers’”.

Ms Brennan, who co-chaired the review with disability minister Sir Stephen Timms, told reporters: “We’ve looked at some principles around what eligibility should look like, and it should be much more tailored to real-life extra costs and the associated needs that come with that.

“As a result, it’s likely there will be a rebalancing process in which some people will get more related to their needs, and some people therefore might get less. But it’s for the Government to make decisions based on our report about eligibility and what that cost should look like.”

James Taylor, executive director of strategy at disability equality charity Scope, said: “The Government's commitments to fast-tracking claims for people with severe, well-evidenced conditions and cutting unnecessary reviews are positive steps.

“Disabled people with lifelong conditions shouldn’t have to keep proving they need Pip.

“The review is right to ask what it costs disabled people to live independently. But a fairer assessment must recognise what people are already going without.

“The review’s recommendation to keep cash at the heart of Pip is the right one. Life costs more if you’re disabled, whether you’re in work or not. Pip is there to help offset the extra costs of disability.

“We urge the Government to consider its full response carefully and continue to protect Pip for all disabled people who need it.”
