A 13-year-old boy stabbed two children in the neck at school while shouting “Allahu Akbar”, a court has heard.

The alleged attack was launched in front of “terrified” children at Kingsbury High School in Brent, north-west London, on 10 February, a jury at the Old Bailey was told on Tuesday.

The defendant, who had been excluded from school, allegedly targeted one boy in a classroom before stabbing a second boy in the playground.

Prosecutor Alison Morgan KC said: “Miraculously, notwithstanding that both children had been stabbed in the neck by the defendant, they both lived.”

Ms Morgan said that the prosecution case is that the defendant attempted to murder the two boys “as an act of terrorism”.

She said that the defendant was armed with a knife and insect repellant spray as he walked through school corridors.

![The boy, who cannot be named for legal reasons, is on trial at the Old Bailey](https://static.independent.co.uk/2025/10/14/13/ac18c62908f84c52e85bef2790369213Y29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzYwNTI5ODY3-2.40523080.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

He stopped at a classroom where a lesson was taking place and repeatedly knocked on the door, jurors heard.

When a child answered, the defendant sprayed that child in the face with the insect spray and entered the room, the court was told.

He was allegedly looking for a boy he knew and demanded: “Where you at fam?”

Ms Morgan said: “He found (the boy) and stood behind him, grabbing at him and then stabbing him repeatedly.”

The boy was stabbed in the head, neck and once in the shoulder area, the court was told.

The defendant then swore and twice exclaimed “Allahu Akbar” (“God is the Greatest”), the court was told.

As children screamed, the defendant allegedly watched the panic he had created and said: “You wanna die fam. Come here!”

![Two children were injured in the incident at Kingsbury High School in London](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/08/25/17/01KH61TH0PGHAEKVMDJXA1EC3D.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

He shouted “Allahu Akbar” again and moved towards the terrified children, who raced to get away from him, Ms Morgan said.

She went on: “Still armed with the knife, he left the classroom, went along the corridor and down the stairs.

“At the bottom of the stairs, he could see out into a playground area where other children were playing at break time.

“He went through the open door and approached a child he did not even know.

“He grabbed the child to the neck with his left arm, turned the child’s head to expose his neck and then he stabbed the child in the neck, before walking back into the school building.”

Ms Morgan told jurors that they would view footage recorded by the defendant of what he had done.

The defendant was described in court as an “angry child”.

He was not a pupil at the school at the time of the incident, having been excluded because of his “poor behaviour”, jurors were told.

The youth, who is now aged 14 and cannot be named for legal reasons, has denied two charges of attempted murder.

The Old Bailey trial continues.
