**The sacked boss of London Pride has been handed a suspended prison sentence after "harvesting" confidential documents to disrupt an investigation into his alleged misconduct and set up his own rival company.**

London's High Court heard how Christopher Joell-Deshields had downloaded more than 50 private documents just days after agreeing to a court order to hand over all company property.

Mr Justice Cotter said the 55-year-old had downloaded documents on a "large and significant scale" with the intention of "disrupting the organisation" and "advancing his own causes".

Lawyers for Joell-Deshields said he had mental health issues and couldn't recall deliberately downloading private information.

The former Pride in London (PIL) chief executive was held in contempt on two separate counts, after admitting failing to hand back company property and failing to provide a witness statement confirming he had done so.

He was sentenced to six months imprisonment for the first count, and four months for the second.

Both sentences were suspended for 18 months, with Cotter warning the defendant the court would "not be in any way likely to be as lenient if there were to be any further breaches".

The sentencing brings to a close a long-running legal battle between the organisers of the UK's largest pride event and its former boss.

Joell-Deshields was suspended in August 2025 after being accused of bullying and misappropriating vouchers donated by a sponsor in order to purchase luxury perfumes and Apple products. He denies those claims.

The court heard how within days of his suspension, employees of PIL were frozen out of key operational systems needed to run the organisation, including financial accounts, emails and social media, which led to the organisers seeking an injunction in the High Court.

At a hearing on 12 September last year, Joell-Deshields agreed to a court order to hand back various documents and company property, including plans for holding a Pride event in London's Vauxhall Pleasure Gardens.

Evidence provided to the court by Pride in London showed how, within days of signing the order, Joell-Deshields went on to download more than 50 private documents, including some relating to the internal investigation into his alleged misconduct.

Caley Wright, representing Joell-Deshields, told the court that, despite his earlier admissions, his client did "not recall" that he "set out to deliberately harvest documents".

Handing down the sentence, Cotter told the court that Joell-Deshields had "persistently failed to be fully open and honest about the extent of his breaches" and that his conduct amounted to "wilful, flagrant and deliberate disobedience".

He added: "Anyone who chooses to act in such a fashion, harvesting documentation rather than giving them back, must face the court recognising it as a serious and flagrant breach which requires the court to act to protect its own rules and orders.

"Such behaviour will not be tolerated, and it does not overstate matters to say the rule of law will suffer otherwise."

In April 2026 it was announced Joell-Deshields had been dismissed from his role at Pride in London following an internal investigation and an unsuccessful appeal.

The court heard how his dismissal was subject to separate ongoing employment tribunal proceedings.
