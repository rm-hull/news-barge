## The rule change will mean pet owners no longer need to fork out for an animal health certificate each time they travel.

Travelling to the EU with cats, dogs and ferrets as a Brit is about to get easier – and cheaper – thanks to the reintroduction of pet passports.

From 1 March 2028, pet owners will be able to get a multiuse passport which is valid for EU travel rather than securing a new animal health certificate for each trip.

The details around how to get a multiuse pet passport will be shared in 2027. You can sign up for updates on the UK government website (or keep your eyes peeled on Euronews Travel).

The British Veterinary Association said in a statement: “This new agreement is broadly positive news and one that we've been pressing the UK government for. But it is important that the transition to the new trade rules is smooth and doesn’t negatively impact vets working across all parts of the profession.”

The news comes after the UK and EU agreed to align on sanitary and phytosanitary (SPS) rules, with a number of other changes introduced.

Cats, dogs and ferrets microchipped from 1 March 2028 will need to have a code that shows the country where the animal was initially identified. This will not apply retroactively.

The UK is also set to work to ensure its microchip database system works with EU systems.

## What are the current rules for travelling with pets to the EU?

Following the closure of a loophole in April that allowed Brits with holiday homes in EU countries to continue to use EU pet passports from before Brexit, all cats, dogs and ferrets have needed an animal health certificate to travel to the EU.

To get the health certificate, your pet must have either an up-to-date rabies vaccination or only need booster doses to keep the vaccination up to date. You’ll also need to take proof of your pet’s microchipping date, and vaccination history.

The certificate will be valid for 10 days, with day one being the date it is issued. It will also be valid for six months for onward travel within the EU after entering, as well as for re-entry into Great Britain.

Pets who need a rabies vaccination will need to wait for it to work, which could take as long as 30 days, before the certificate can be issued.

Only official veterinarians can sign the certificates, and prices can vary between £90 (€108) and £250 (€299), depending on factors like turnaround times and vet location.
