After flying back from Israel and Egypt on Oct. 25, 2017, President Donald Trump made sure he got back to the White House by 4 p.m. to present Charlie Kirk’s widow with the Presidential Medal of Freedom in the East Room of the White House.

He had promised a “very big crowd” for the event a month earlier.

In February, during the State of the Union, Trump announced that he would present the medal to Connor Hellebuyck, goalie for the gold medal-winning U.S. Olympic hockey team, after Hellebuyck brought several of the players into the Capitol with him.

You would think the man once heralded as ”America’s Mayor” would get similar treatment when receiving the same honor, but you’d be wrong. Trump presented Giuliani with the medal on Tuesday in the Oval Office, at a small ceremony that was purposefully closed to the press.

The White House isn’t saying why, but keeping the former Mafia-fighting U.S. attorney, New York City mayor during 9/11 and Trump attorney away from reporters and cameras is more than a bit uncharacteristic for the president, who rarely passes up an opportunity to hand out shiny baubles within range of television cameras.

But Giuliani is different ... because of his connections to Trump’s worst defeats and darkest moments.

![Rudy Giuliani (pictured as he dripped hair dye and pushed a false stolen 2020 election narrative) was awarded the nations’ highest honor Tuesday — behind closed doors](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/05/03/23/19/GettyImages-1229683394.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The ex-prosecutor turned politician was head of Trump’s legal team in the days immediately following the 2020 election, when it was clear to everyone but Trump and his inner circle that he’d lost to Joe Biden.

Losing that election cost Trump the presidency for four years, but it cost Giuliani his reputation — and his law license.

First came the scores of meritless lawsuits — including one in Pennsylvania that would require him to make his first in-court arguments in decades. Then came the shambolic press conference at the Republican National Committee alongside other Trumpworld attorneys in which the hair dye ran down his head as he sweat.

Then, days later, was the even more farcical presser at Four Seasons Total Landscaping outside Philadelphia, called right as the networks were calling the race for Biden.

And in between there were the dozens of television appearances in which he lied about what was happening during the counting of ballots, including when he defamed a pair of Georgia election workers who would go on to own most of his assets after they sued him for defamation.

Then, finally, there was the unhinged Jan. 6, 2021 speech on the Ellipse, where he called for Game of Thrones-style “trial by combat” just hours before a horde of pro-Trump rioters breached the Capitol in a last-ditch attempt to prevent Biden’s victory from being certified.

In the years since, Trump hasn’t been shy about downplaying the events of that day or continuing to insist, despite all evidence, that he won the 2020 election.

He pardoned roughly 1,500 people on his first day back in office, and this past June he told aan interviewer that he would pay the Jan. 6 rioters “the kind of money that they deserve” through a $1.776 billion “anti-weaponization fund” that would have paid out to Jan. 6 rioters, had it not been blocked by a federal judge.

![Rudy Giuliani poses for his booking photo on Aug. 23, 2023 in Atlanta, Georgia.](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/05/04/15/1619649891.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Trump’s Justice Department continues to search for evidence of the 2020 election having been rigged. In May, Acting Attorney General Todd Blanche said there was a “ton of evidence” of the election having been rigged, but in July, Trump scheduled a 9 p.m. “Speech to the Nation” that contained evidence of absolutely nothing.

And in the aftermath of his party’s midterm election massacre this month, it’s possible that Trump isn’t willing to highlight his own losses by putting another infamous loser on television.

As for why he was being honored, Giuliani told Steve Bannon on Monday that he did not yet know what the citation for his medal would say. “I’ll have to see what the citation says, but I mean, if I would just view it, I would assume it would be September 11,” he said.

To that end, the White House’s own account of the honor skips 2020. Assistant press secretary Olivia Wales said Trump “will proudly honor Mayor Giuliani” for a lifetime of service, and her statement cites his prosecutions of mob bosses, his record as mayor and his response to the Sept. 11 attacks.

The White House also publicized the medal beforehand with a video recapping his career, and Trump announced the honor himself in a Truth Social post in September 2025, calling Giuliani “the greatest Mayor in the history of New York City, and an equally great American Patriot.”

But Giuliani’s most devoted service to Trump — the part of his career that will be the first line in his obituary and eclipses his well-deserved reputation for heroism on September 11. is being memory-holed just as it was when Trump pardoned him last November, along with Sidney Powell, Kenneth Chesebro, Mark Meadows and 73 others, in a post from Trump’s “clemency czar,” Ed Martin, at 10:54 p.m. on a Sunday.

The first two people to receive the Presidential Medal of Freedom in Trump’s second term were honored posthumously. Kirk, the widow of the late Charlie Kirk, and Welles Crowther, the trader who died while rescuing people from the South Tower on 9/11, were both awarded the medal by Trump in the East Room of the White House in October 2017.

Giuliani is the first living recipient of the medal in Trump’s second term, but as far as Trumpworld is concerned, he might as well be dead.
