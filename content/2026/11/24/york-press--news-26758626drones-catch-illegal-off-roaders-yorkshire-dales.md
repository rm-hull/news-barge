## Drones to be used in crackdown on illegal off-roaders

Damage caused by illegal off-roaders. *(Image: Yorkshire Dales Moorland Group)*

Thermal imaging drones could be deployed to catch illegal off-road drivers and motorcyclists damaging sensitive moorland habitats in the Yorkshire Dales.

The Yorkshire Dales Moorland Group says it is stepping up efforts to identify offenders and bring them before the courts amid growing concerns about damage to protected landscapes.

The group says illegal off-roading has become an increasingly serious problem over the last two years, with vehicles ripping up peatland, destroying sensitive vegetation and disturbing wildlife.

It is now working with the Yorkshire Dales National Park Authority, North Yorkshire Police, Natural England and the National Gamekeepers Organisation on measures to tackle the problem.

These will include increased vigilance, with thermal imaging drones used in some circumstances to help identify those responsible.

The group warned that offenders could face prosecution and potentially have their vehicles confiscated.

Darren Chadwick, from the group, said: “This is a big, big problem for upland managers here in the Yorkshire Dales.

Over the last couple of years it's got worse and the damage to upland habitats is completely unacceptable.

“These images show the long-term, sometimes irreversible damage inflicted on our landscape caused by selfish and irresponsible off-road drivers and riders.”

The group said much of the damage was occurring in protected areas, including Sites of Special Scientific Interest (SSSIs), where landowners and gamekeepers must comply with strict environmental regulations.

It said those responsible for managing the moors could face legal action for breaching conditions designed to protect the habitats, while illegal off-roaders were causing damage without facing similar consequences.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21758603.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Illegal off-roaders stuck on moorland. *(Image: Yorkshire Dales Moorland Group.)*

The organisation added: “This senseless abuse of our moors has to stop.”

It said incidents would be logged and reported to the relevant agencies as part of a coordinated effort to bring offenders to justice.

The group also highlighted a recent successful prosecution in Wales involving illegal off-roaders who damaged an SSSI with 4x4 vehicles.

It warned: “Extra vigilance will be introduced and thermal drones employed in certain instances to capture offenders.

“If you are an illegal off-roader reading this, please be aware that we are looking forward to getting our first convictions secured.

"Make sure it's not you."

The Yorkshire Dales National Park Authority confirmed it was working with North Yorkshire Police to tackle illegal vehicle use.

A spokesperson said: “Illegal off-roading can cause significant damage to sensitive habitats, historic features and access land within the national park.

“We already work closely with North Yorkshire Police through initiatives such as Operation Byway, which focuses on education and enforcement to promote the lawful and responsible use of green lanes and byways.”

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21758604.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Damage caused by illegal off-roaders. *(Image: Yorkshire Dales Moorland Group.)*

The authority said its ranger teams supported police operations to check that vehicles were using legal routes and that drivers and riders were behaving responsibly towards other countryside users.

The spokesperson added: “We have a strong and constructive working relationship with a number of responsible off-road user groups.

Their support plays an important role in promoting good practice and helping to protect these special places.”

The moorland group says it is determined to secure prosecutions as part of its efforts to prevent further damage to the Dales' protected landscapes.
