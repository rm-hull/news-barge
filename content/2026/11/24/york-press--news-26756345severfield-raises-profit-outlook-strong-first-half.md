North Yorkshire's Severfield has raised its full-year profit expectations after delivering a strong first-half performance.

The structural steel specialist reported a 20 per cent rise in revenue to £239.1 million for the six months to September 26, 2026, along with underlying pre-tax profit of £7 million and a significantly improved order book.

Charlie Cornish, chairman of Severfield, said: "The board and I are encouraged by the initial progress in delivering our refreshed strategy.

"It’s great to see the assembly of an experienced, capable and dynamic new executive leadership team, to deliver the group’s strategy and long-term growth."

In the UK and Europe, the Thirsk-headquartered company’s order book reached £629 million as of November 1, 2026, covering a mix of large, multi-year projects and higher-margin contracts.

Severfield said it now expects full-year underlying pre-tax profit to be towards the upper end of its previously guided £12 million to £15 million range.

Paul McNerney, chief executive officer, said: "We have delivered a strong set of results in the first half of FY27, with revenue up 20% and underlying profit up 289%."

"We have secured significant new contracts across our priority markets, alongside a growing contribution from India, which continues to deliver record results and offers substantial growth potential."

"Our order book reflects the strength of our engineering and delivery capabilities across our target sectors and provides confidence for the remainder of FY27 and into FY28."

The company reported record production and profitability in India through its joint venture JSSL, which contributed £2.4 million in profit in the first half, up from £1 million in the same period last year.

JSSL’s output reached 85kt for the period, supporting a full-year forecast of more than 200kt.

Additional investment in India is being directed toward capacity expansion, including plans for new facilities in Gujarat and Odisha.

Mr McNerney said: "In India, our phased investment plans offer the potential for a step change in sustainable cash generation, positioning us to capture an increasing share of this rapidly growing market."

Severfield also reported progress in its cost reduction programme, delivering £6 million in annualised savings.

The company’s net debt (excluding IFRS 16 lease liabilities) stood at £44.8 million, up from £28.2 million last year, due to the timing of contract receipts and bridge remedial outflows.

Legacy contract receipts totalling £14 million are expected in the second half of the year, supporting working capital improvements.

Severfield said it remains on track to achieve broadly flat net debt for the full year.
