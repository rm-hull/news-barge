Parents have told the *Harrogate Advertiser* they fear for their children’s safety if plans for a £15.7m care hub close to two of the town’s schools go ahead.

Since North Yorkshire Council revealed its plans earlier this year for a new three-story Harrogate Care Hub on a brownfield site off Ainsty Road, the potential implications for neighbouring St Robert's Catholic Primary School and Harrogate High School - and their pupils - have been the subject of growing apprehension.

Tom Peacock, a parent of two children attending St Roberts Primary School, said there was deep anxiety among the school community over the proposals which went beyond the inevitable noise and disruption of the construction period.

Primary among parents’ concerns over the care hub development were the potential safety risks to pupils from increased road congestion, as well as the threat to the privacy of youngsters in the school playground caused by the height of the three-storey building.

"Both St Roberts and Harrogate High are already accessed by only one congested road, with limited parking provision,” said Mr Peacock.

"As for the new hub itself, the three-storey care home site is immediately next to the school grounds so would have elevated views from bedrooms and communal spaces towards children’s playgrounds.”

North Yorkshire Council said it was unable to comment on people’s concerns as the application was currently being considered in the planning process.

Earlier this year the council said its aim was to improve adult care facilities in the county as it faced a perfect storm of rising care costs, increasing demand for specialist services and ongoing challenges linked to the sustainability and quality of ageing local authority care homes.

If approved, the multi-million pound new facility would provide 60 beds and offer care designed to help people regain independence while also providing long-term support for those living with dementia.
