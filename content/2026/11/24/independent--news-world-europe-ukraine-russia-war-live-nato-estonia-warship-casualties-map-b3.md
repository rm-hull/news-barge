Russia has threatened Estonia with “open war”, accusing the Nato and EU member state of a series of hostile actions, including allowing Ukrainian drones to use its airspace to attack Russian targets.

The threat follows Estonia’s announcement that it will close its border crossings with Russia for at least six months from 1 December, after accusing Russian security services of attempting to set fire to an Estonian defence company.

Moscow summoned an Estonian envoy on Monday and told him that “such provocative and extremely dangerous actions are on the verge of triggering open war with Russia”.

Separately, Russia threatened “painful consequences” after Ukraine confirmed it carried out devastating strikes on two Russian ships at a port in Libya.

Ukraine sank a Russian naval corvette using naval drones in Benghazi and also hit the Russian dry cargo ship MV Angara, which president Volodymyr Zelensky claimed had been used to transport weapons.

A Russian Ka-27 helicopter was also destroyed along with the corvette, Zelensky said.

The attack came only a day after a drone strike on a Russian-military linked cargo ship in the Gulf of Finland, which Zelensky claimed responsibility for on Tuesday.

## EU proposes ban on firms helping repair Russian energy infrastructure

The European Commission has proposed banning European companies from helping Russia build, operate or repair its energy infrastructure, including oil refineries, as part of its 22nd sanctions package.

Announcing the proposals on 23 November, Commission president Ursula von der Leyen said: “There cannot be business as usual in our economic relations with Russia.”

We are "giving Ukraine strength to secure peace, and Russia every reason to choose it," she said.

She added that the EU would continue imposing sanctions until Moscow showed a genuine willingness to pursue lasting peace in Ukraine.

The proposal comes as Ukrainian strikes continue to target Russian energy facilities. The Novoshakhtinsk oil refinery in Russia’s Rostov region suspended operations on 23 November following a missile and drone attack, regional governor Yuri Slyusar said.

## Tajikistan withdraws from ICC on day Putin visited country

Tajikistan has formally notified the United Nations of its decision to withdraw from the International Criminal Court, submitting the notice on 20 November – the same day Russian president Vladimir Putin visited the country.

ICC member states are required to arrest Putin if he enters their territory.

The ICC said in March 2023 that “there are reasonable grounds to believe” that president Putin “bear criminal responsibility for the unlawful deportation and transfer of Ukrainian children from occupied areas of Ukraine to the Russian Federation”.

In a statement on its website published on Monday, the ICC said it has “taken note of the situation with concern”.

“The Presidency regrets these developments. Decisions by States Parties to disengage from the Rome Statute risk undermining the collective pursuit of justice and weakening global efforts to end impunity. The ICC stands at the heart of the international system of accountability, and its effectiveness depends on sustained and unequivocal support from the international community. The pursuit of justice is strengthened through unity of purpose.

“The Presidency calls on Tajikistan to remain committed as State Party to the Rome Statute and to continue engaging constructively within the framework of the Assembly of States Parties. The Presidency reiterates that all States Parties are entitled to raise their concerns within the Assembly and encourages Tajikistan to take part in a meaningful exchange on this matter.

“The Presidency recalls that withdrawal from the Rome Statute does not relieve a State Party of obligations arising during the period in which it remained a Party to the Statute.”
