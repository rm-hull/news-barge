Cases of the flu are surging in the United States, in an early start to the season.

There have already been 3.7 million infections and 2,800 deaths reported, including seven child deaths, from late August to last week. Southern states are being the most heavily impacted including Texas, South Carolina and Tennessee.

It has led doctors to reissue calls for people to get their flu vaccine, which can prevent infection and protect against severe infection from circulating strains.

Doctors had advised people to get their shot in September and October but it’s “not too late” to get vaccinated, Dr. Cheryl Hug-English, of the University of Nevada Reno, said Friday.

“It does take about two weeks for the vaccine to become effective. And so, it’s good to plan that knowing the likelihood of exposure goes up in those prime months,” she said.

![Flu season has been surging earlier than usual. Now, doctors have a key warning for Americans](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/24/15/25/GettyImages-2258844347.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“But it doesn’t mean that if you haven’t gotten your flu shot by the end of October of November that it’s too late to get it; we do flu shots into the spring and often times our flu season goes into the spring as well.”

Dr. Jason Bowling, chief epidemiologist at University Health, shared the view.

“So even though we’re seeing higher levels of flu right now, we’re likely gonna continue to see high levels of flu for quite some time,” he told KSAT last week.

This year’s flu shot in the U.S. targets the H1N1 swine flu and the B/Victoria virus, as well as subclade K, a variant of H3N2 influenza that also spread last year.

It’s too early to know how effective this year’s shot will be, the U.S. Centers for Disease Control and Prevention says. However other medical experts say there are early indications the vaccine is a good match for circulating H3N2 and B/Victoria.

Less is known about H1N1.

“That third component, we don’t really know at this point,” Scott Hensley, a professor of microbiology at the University of Pennsylvania, told NBC News last month.

Even if protection isn’t perfect, the vaccine still helps to prevent severe illness and death. Flu causes 9.4-51 million illnesses a year in the U.S. and some 6,300-52,000 deaths, according to the CDC.

![Flu is surging across much of the U.S. South. The states with the most cases include Texas, South Carolina, Tennessee and Oregon](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/24/15/36/FLU-MAP-CDC.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“They are very effective in reducing the severity of illness and preventing illness, ultimately, with influenza,” Dr. Nike Shoyinka, medical health officer for the Ingham County Health Department, told Michigan’s News 10 on Monday.

Just over 120 million doses of flu vaccine had been distributed by the end of last month, CDC data shows. That’s less than the number of doses distributed at the same time during the past two seasons.

Vaccination rates have fallen in recent years, despite widening availability. It’s free to get a flu shot and people can get them from local pharmacies and drugstores, mobile clinics or at doctors’ offices.

In the 2024-2025 season - the most recent data reported by the CDC - 41.9 percent of adults were vaccinated against the flu, the lowest rate in seven seasons.

Vaccine hesitancy and misinformation about the flu virus and vaccine have played a role, according to experts, including about how severe an infection can be.

“It’s not just a cold,” Dr. Ryan Stanton, president-elect of the American College of Emergency Physicians, said in October, according to AARP. “There is just a cold out there. I still don’t want to have that one either. But a lot of these [respiratory viruses] are much, much more serious.”
