# Linton-on-Ouse: scrap male migrant super-camp plan before it's too late

![RAF Linton-On-Ouse in North Yorkshire. PIC: Owen Humphreys/PA Wire](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOjRkNzAwM2ZlLWFiYWEtNGMzZC1iOGViLTE1NGU3MGQzMjNiMzplYjUyYzg4Ny1iYTk0LTQxZWYtOTAzNS03ZTUxOGM1NjQxOTk=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=990&quality=70&enable=upscale)


## The next bus arriving at Linton-on-Ouse is not the number 80 to York, which operates just five times per day, it will be one full of migrant men who, by and large, have breached the borders of Great Britain illegally. And then another, and then another until some 1,300 new young men born of distant cultures have been dropped off by bus and left to live just yards from a children’s primary school and their village playground. Thirteen hundred men who, by the very definition of ‘asylum seeker’ may well have fled persecution, war or life-endangering violence.

Chancellor of the Exchequer, John Healey, when asked by this newspaper whether or not he supported the plan, despite the genuine concerns of the local community and the clear-and-obvious valid reasons for closing down the idea of using the former RAF base as a super-camp for illegal aliens, said: ‘the village will play a part’ in tackling the country’s asylum problem.

It is little wonder, then, that those living in Linton feel ignored; Mr Healey’s assertion that the village ‘shall play its part’ gives the impression that the go-ahead for this ill-conceived initiative is a fait accompli. Sir Alec Shelbrooke, MP for Wetherby and Easingwold, in a Parliamentary exchange with Immigration Minister Anna Turley, MP - who still has not been to see for herself the risks posed should this plan go ahead - said this: “When looking back through Hansard tomorrow, it will seem as though some of the decisions have already been made on Linton-on-Ouse, because the Minister just said that these are the most appropriate places to house people.”

Two things could readily lead one to a similar conclusion; that is, that the genuine concerns held by the residents of Linton-on-Ouse, for both themselves and for those whom this Government intends to squeeze into the village - it is important to remember, this community did not object to a proposal to give over 16 homes in the village to the Afghan Refugee Resettlement Programme - are being ignored: firstly, no Government Minister has visited Linton-on-Ouse to see for themselves the make-up of the village, which essentially comprises a single farm track in and out, with small residential streets branching off. There is no shop, no Post Office, few - if any - employment prospects and a bare-bones bus service to York and back. There is a small primary school that sits about 600m away from the perimeter fence of the 680-acre former RAF station. In the interests of those children and their families alone, the proposal is entirely inappropriate.

Secondly, on 1st October, the Leader of North Yorkshire Council, Carl Les, penned a courteous note to Prime Minister Andy Burnham that congratulated Mr Burnham on his coming to high office, extended the hand of the county by way of an offer to assist and collaborate on the work that needs to be done in regards to Linton-on-Ouse and, tellingly, was signed off: ‘Time is critical, so I invite you to join me on site as soon as possible.’

Save for an acknowledgement of receipt of said letter, and so perhaps even more tellingly, Coun Les has received no meaningful reply to his most urgent letter. Given what is at stake, here, that is unfair.

And so we move to that which comes next: if this Government really does care about place-based decision making, it should listen to the people of Linton-on-Ouse and stop this plan in its tracks. Of course, if placed-based decision making is in fact nought but disposable, meaningless rhetoric, and no decision making power is available to the people of Linton-on-Ouse, at a local level, then a Minister, preferably the Prime Minister, must turn up, look this community in the eye and take full responsibility for the forthcoming consequences of this terrible error of judgement.

James Mitchinson, Yorkshire Post editor.
