A huge search operation in Istanbul to track down a suspected lion found the animal was ⁠neither a lion nor any other type of big cat, the Istanbul governor's office has said.

On Sunday evening, a sighting on surveillance cameras of "an animal resembling a ⁠lion" by personnel ​at ⁠a police station in the Zekeriyakoy area, north of the European side of Istanbul, sparked ⁠a search by Turkish security and wildlife ​personnel.

Turkish ⁠television broadcast surveillance and ‌thermal camera footage showing what appeared to be a large cat walking through undergrowth and teams ‌searching through woodland in outer Istanbul's ‌Sariyer district, which borders the Bosphorus strait and the Black Sea.

"Teams equipped with thermal imaging cameras were dispatched to the area to allow more comprehensive searches, and residents living nearby were given the necessary ‌warnings," the parks authority statement said at the time.

"Following ​operations in the wooded area, ‌the animal’s location was ⁠identified. Efforts to capture it ⁠are continuing, with a team that includes specialist ‌veterinarians on ​site," it added.

![Turkish gendarmerie officers block a road as police and animal welfare teams search for a lioness spotted roaming in Sariyer district in Istanbul](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/23/11/download.-1..jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The Istanbul governor's office said late on Monday ⁠that the area was scanned from the air with drones equipped with thermal cameras and on the ground by search teams. Additionally, bait was placed, and motion-activated camera traps were deployed, it said.

"No concrete evidence indicating the presence of a lion ‌was found during the field operations," the Istanbul ​governor's office said.

It said that based ‌on new footage, captured ⁠from various angles, "it has been determined that the ⁠animal in the footage is neither a lion nor any ‌other species ​of big cat."
