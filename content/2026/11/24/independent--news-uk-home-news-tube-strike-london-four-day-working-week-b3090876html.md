London Underground drivers will strike as scheduled following the failure of negotiations regarding a proposed four-day week.

Services across the capital face disruption when members of the Rail, Maritime and Transport (RMT) union stage a 24-hour walkout from noon on Thursday.

A meeting between the union and London Underground at conciliation body Acas on Tuesday ended without agreement.

**Here’s everything you need to know aboutThursday’s London Underground strikes**

The RMT said: "Talks at Acas ended after management refused to budge on demands that the union had put to the company consistently since March 2025 when the ‘offer’ was first made.

"RMT had warned that the proposals would force drivers to surrender hard-won protections regardless of whether they choose to work four or five days a week.

![The 24-hour strikes will begin at noon on 26 November (PA)](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/06/11/21f671836835dcacfdc02354cf9ec28eY29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzk0MDQ4NzQ3-2.28637321.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

"Under the plans, drivers face increased time spent driving trains, longer duties, earlier starts and later finishes, short-notice changes to shifts and attacks on existing transfer rights and annual leave arrangements."

Nick Dent, director of customer operations at London Underground, said: "We met with the RMT today to discuss their ongoing concerns around the terms and conditions of our proposal for a voluntary four-day week.

"As part of those discussions, we committed to working on proposals to address several of their concerns and asked them to postpone any industrial action until we had done so. They have refused to withdraw the action.

"We have amended our proposals to guarantee that drivers who wish to continue working a five-day working pattern can do so, and have worked hard, alongside the RMT and our other union Aslef, to resolve many of their other concerns with the proposals and provide our colleagues with a real, meaningful choice on their own working patterns.

"Drivers who choose a four-day week, and those who choose to work a five-day pattern with the same terms and conditions as the four-day pattern, will spend less time at work per year, while delivering a more efficient, reliable Tube service for our customers.

"We continue to believe that the majority of the concerns from the RMT have now been resolved and we urge the RMT to put this proposal to their members."

An Acas spokesperson said: “The parties have adjourned for today. Acas is in contact with both parties and our services remain available.”
