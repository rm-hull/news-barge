## Environment Agency approves North Yorkshire gas exploration

Alison Hume MP, "No To Europa". Courtesy AH.

FRESH opposition has been voiced to controversial plans for gas exploration near Scarborough after the Environment Agency granted a permit allowing a disputed extraction technique to be used at the site.

The regulator has approved an environmental permit for Europa Oil & Gas’s proposed Cloughton-2 exploratory borehole near Burniston, despite strong local opposition and the earlier refusal of planning permission by North Yorkshire councillors.

The decision allows for the potential use of a well stimulation technique known as a “proppant squeeze”, which involves injecting a water-based fluid and sand mixture underground under pressure to improve the flow of gas from rock.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21006482.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Anti Fracking Protest March In Burniston. Courtesy Numminen/LDRS

While the Environment Agency said the technique is distinct from the hydraulic fracturing covered by the Government’s fracking moratorium, opponents argue it carries many of the same environmental risks.

Independent councillor Rich Maw criticised the decision and said the distinction between fracking and proppant squeezing was unconvincing.

He said: “I’m really annoyed that the Environment Agency has granted Europa Oil & Gas an environmental permit for its proposed gas exploration at Burniston.

“I’ve opposed this from the start, and nothing has changed.

“The Government’s insistence that a proppant squeeze isn’t technically fracking doesn’t wash with me. Calling it something different doesn’t make the potential environmental risks disappear.”

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21269554.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Europa Oil And Gas Website.

Scarborough and Whitby MP Alison Hume also expressed disappointment at the regulator’s decision.

She said: “I remain firmly opposed to this development, on the edge of the beautiful North York Moors, and will continue working closely with the many parties also determined to protect our picturesque coastline.

“My position has always been clear. I define fracking by its purpose and effect, not by the volume of fluid injected underground.”

The Labour MP said she had been campaigning to change the legal definition of fracking and close what she described as a loophole allowing some lower-volume operations to proceed outside existing restrictions.

The Environment Agency said its decision followed a detailed technical assessment and two public consultations.

Pat Kelly, the agency’s area environment manager, said: “We recognise the significant public interest in this application and we have very carefully reviewed the comments and evidence from both public consultations.

“Our role is to assess whether the application meets the requirements of environmental legislation and whether the proposed activities can be carried out without unacceptable risks to people or the environment.”

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21006481.jpg?type=mds-article-620)View East Of Proposed Burniston Drilling Site. Courtesy Nyc

The regulator stressed that planning permission and environmental permitting are separate legal processes and that it was legally required to issue a permit if the applicant met all environmental requirements.

The permit decision comes as Burniston Parish Council prepares to fight an appeal by Europa Oil & Gas against North Yorkshire Council’s refusal of planning permission for the scheme.

The parish council said it had written to the authority urging it to ask the Planning Inspectorate to hold a public inquiry into the appeal.

In a statement, the council said a public inquiry would provide the “best way to ensure an open, fair and thorough examination of the highly complex facts”.

North Yorkshire councillors rejected the application earlier this year, but that decision is now subject to an appeal under consideration by the Planning Inspectorate.

Despite the permit being granted, Alison Hume said the project still faced significant hurdles.

She added: “The Environment Agency’s decision is not the end of the matter.

“I will continue to oppose this mini-frack proposal and stand with local residents in making the case that our area’s future lies in protecting its natural environment.”

Europa has said the scheme would create local employment and that residents would not notice the presence of the drilling rig. It has also argued there is no ‘loophole’ in the moratorium on fracking.

William Holland, the CEO of Europa Oil & Gas, previously said: “Developments of this nature can be carried out responsibly, and we are committed to working constructively with the local community throughout.

​“The development is limited, and it does not determine any future development.

“Let’s be clear, if we are successful, we will make another application, and it will create jobs and support the UK economy; it will also create gas that will be consumed here in Yorkshire.”
