# University of York laboratory technician honoured with prestigious Royal Society of Chemistry Prize

Posted on Tuesday 24 November 2026

![Kieran Godfree in  the Department of Chemistry at York.](https://www.york.ac.uk/media/chemistry/news/images/2026/k-godfree1000x500.jpg)

The award, which includes a medal, certificate and £3,000 cash prize, was given to Kieran for “improving the laboratory learning environment for students at the University of York through innovative teaching approaches, online resources and a supportive, inclusive culture”.

**Celebration**

The Early Career Prize for Excellence in Higher Education is one of the RSC’s Education Prizes, given in celebration of exceptional teachers, technicians, lecturers, and education researchers.

Kieran said: “I'd not been aware of these prizes before, and if I had been, I don't think I would have considered the possibility that I could have won a teaching award as a technician.

“I love supporting and teaching students in the lab, and the challenges involved in developing the practical course. Seeing students developing their skills during their time in the lab is a constant reward, but it's also really gratifying to be recognised through this award.”

**Practical**

Kieran prepares and delivers undergraduate practical classes within the teaching laboratories at the University of York. This involves advanced setup including the procurement and organisation of chemicals, solvents and equipment. The running of more open-ended project work in Year three has seen Kieran develop improved systems to manage and educate students in the preparation of detailed risk assessments across a wide variety of project areas.

Kieran says he has a strong belief in inclusive education, working closely with teaching staff to improve teaching in an inclusive and supportive environment, as well as working closely with both the department disability officer and student experience teams to further facilitate students with additional needs in the lab. He is heavily involved in the development of both new and existing practicals, along with co-supervising summer internship students who assist in this work, particularly in addressing pedagogical and sustainability considerations.

**Exceptional**

Dr Helen Pain, Chief Executive Officer of the RSC, said: “Exceptional chemistry education changes lives. Across schools, colleges and universities, dedicated teachers, technicians and researchers help young people develop the knowledge, skills and confidence to support society and tackle the challenges facing our planet. Our winners empower the scientists of the future, strengthening the chemistry community for generations to come.

“I extend my warmest congratulations to Kieran. Winning an RSC Prize is a remarkable achievement. You join the ranks of a prestigious roll call stretching back over 150 years, including several dozen who went on to win Nobel Prizes. Our winners are inspiring role models for our communities, and we’re so pleased to be celebrating such a brilliant cohort this year.”
