In today's series of subscriber-only nostalgia deep dives, we look back at a time when York City had derby day delight.

York haven’t locked horns with rivals Scarborough many times in recent years, with the two teams only meeting on three occasions since phoenix club Scarborough Athletic were formed in 2007.

The newly formed Scarborough side and City first met in 2008, when goals from Mark Robinson and Adam Boyes gave the Minstermen the win in the North Riding Senior County Cup.

Prior to Scarborough’s reformation, there were plenty of meetings between the two rivals, with their last coming in April 1993.

But on this day in 1989, there was derby day delight for York.

With 3,602 in attendance at the McCain Stadium in Scarborough on a chilly Friday night, City fans travelled in their numbers to cheer on John Bird’s side.

It was only the third time the two teams had met in Division Four, but both sides had met in the FA Cup three times prior, in 1932, 1954 and 1978, and once in the Sherpa Vans Trophy, where Scarborough came out on top.

In the Minstermen’s two previous league encounters with Scarborough, both matches had finished 0-0.

But the game on this day in 1989 was the first league fixture to produce goals between the two rivals – and it was York who celebrated a first ever league win over Scarborough.

Ian Helliwell had fired City in front after just 20 minutes, heading the Minstermen into a 1-0 lead.

It was a quickfire double for Bird’s side, as Gary Howlett scored York’s second just four minutes later.

It wasn’t all plain sailing for City, with Scarborough pulling one back before half-time through Paul Dobson.

But the points and bragging rights were sealed for York when Tony Barratt scored City’s third after some neat footwork from Helliwell.

Like any fierce derby, this one didn’t end without some form of controversy.

Both Bird and Scarborough manager Ray McHale were shown red cards in the second half after clashing on the touchline, in what was the latter’s first match as permanent manager in the home dugout.
