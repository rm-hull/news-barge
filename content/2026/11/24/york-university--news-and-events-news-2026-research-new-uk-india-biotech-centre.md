# Waste-to-energy research at heart of new UK-India biotech centre

Posted on Tuesday 24 November 2026

![](https://www.york.ac.uk/media/news-and-events/pressreleases/2026/tech-summit-1000.jpg)

University of York researchers will work with the BBC to accelerate research, innovation, entrepreneurship and commercialisation in industrial biotechnology to turn organic waste into clean energy.

Following the MoU signing the York team met with Karnataka’s Minister for Home Affairs, Information Technology, Biotechnology, and E-Governance, Priyank Kharge to discuss how working together could contribute to Karnataka’s Global Innovation Alliance, a partnership initiative that connects Karnataka's tech and startup ecosystem with global innovation hubs, foreign governments, research institutions, and international startups.

One of the first projects to be explored under the new partnership is the opportunity to apply engineering biology approaches to anaerobic digestion technologies for the green energy sector.

### **Anaerobic digestion**

The project will be led by Professor James Chong, Director of the Centre of Excellence for Anaerobic Digestion (CEAD) - a leading research centre at the University of York.

Anaerobic digestion turns waste materials into renewable energy and nutrient-rich fertiliser and is seen as a crucial technology to increase the sustainability of industry and reduce carbon emissions.

Researchers are developing waste-recovery systems tailored to operate efficiently in both British and Indian climates.

![](https://www.york.ac.uk/media/news-and-events/pressreleases/2026/tech-summit-2-1000.jpg)

Whilst Garden City University, Bangalore, will host the initial pilot study at its Hoskote campus, the new Centre of Excellence at the Bangalore Helix Biotech Park is designed to investigate the commercial implications of the research and accelerate the development of a scalable proof of concept to develop larger scale facilities.

Professor James Chong, Director of the University of York’s Centre of Excellence for Anaerobic Digestion, said: “Working alongside the Bangalore Bioinnovation Centre gives us a vital bridge to test how our anaerobic digestion technologies perform under different environmental and feedstock conditions, accelerating our work in the laboratory to commercially viable green energy solutions that will benefit the planet.”

BBC, a joint venture between India's Department of Biotechnology and the Karnataka state government, will provide laboratory space, researcher placements, and access to its network of life-science start-ups to look at the commercialisation opportunities for CEAD's technology in India, and other biotechnology projects at York.

### **Biotech ecosystem**

Dr Mohamed Adil A.A, Managing Director of the Bangalore Bioinnovation Centre, said: “The partnership reinforces the growing York, United Kingdom, and Karnataka India Biocorridor and reflects shared commitment to building resilient, sustainable, and globally connected biotechnology ecosystems.”

The announcement of the Centre follows the University of York’s signing of a Letter of Intent (Lol) with the Department of Electronics, Information Technology and Biotechnology, Government of Karnataka to collaborate on research into the bioeconomy, agri-tech, and clean energy.

The Lol was overseen by Home Minister of Karnataka Priyank Kharge and David Skaith, Mayor of York and North Yorkshire, during the Great North Trade mission to India in October.

### **Bioinnovation corridor**

Joan Concannon, the University of York’s Chief Reputation and Stakeholder Relations Officer, said: “Not only is this a significant step for particular research projects, it also lays the groundwork for a wider Bengaluru–Yorkshire Bioinnovation Corridor.

“This will assist in bringing together Karnataka’s thriving biotech ecosystem with the BioYorkshire and BioIndustrial Cluster North (BICN) initiatives - key academic, industry, and regional government alliances aimed at turning biotechnology and biological research into commercial products, sustainable farming practices, and green manufacturing.

“The aim of the BioInnovation Corridor will be to create a ‘knowledge partnership’ between the Centre of Excellence at the BBC, University of York and the wider BICN Integrated bioeconomy ecosystem, and spanning industry in Bengaluru and our regional economy. It also offers opportunities for the newly opened University of York Mumbai to create a route for future joint research, doctoral training, student mobility, and industrial scale-up.”
