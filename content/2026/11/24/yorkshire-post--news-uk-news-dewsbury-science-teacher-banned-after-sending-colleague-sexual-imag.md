# Dewsbury science teacher banned after sending colleague sexual images on Snapchat

## A West Yorkshire secondary school teacher who sent unwanted sexual pictures of himself to a colleague has been banned for five years.

Asif Pandor, a science teacher at Westborough High School in Dewsbury, was accused of ‘behaving in an inappropriate and/or harassing manner’ towards a female colleague at the school between September 2023 and April 2024.

The ‘dedicated and valued’ teacher sent images of himself of a sexual nature, and said to her, ‘Does it not make you want it?’

Mr Pandor also engaged in unwanted physical contact with the woman, the Teaching Regulation Agency ruled.

The unnamed colleague said Mr Pandor asked to kiss her several times during an incident in February 2024, having touched her bottom and started kissing her neck.

She said Mr Pandor asked several times to kiss her, but that she refused.

In a separate incident during a parents evening later the same month, Mr Pandor said he shared a ‘mutual kiss’ with the colleague in a classroom.

The woman later said in an interview that while in his classroom, Mr Pandor placed his hands on her back, touched her face then kissed her - adding that she felt ‘pressurised’ and ‘unable to stop (Mr Pandor)’.

She said she wasn’t alleging sexual assault, but did turn her face away and didn’t reciprocate the kiss.

The same day, Mr Pandor asked to send the woman a picture, ‘for her eyes only’, to which she replied ‘What do you mean? Nothing crazy please’.

Mr Pandor went on to send her three ‘overtly sexual’ images of himself to the colleague via Snapchat.

Mr Pandor had denied behaving in an appropriate and/or harassing way towards the colleague, and also denied that his conduct relating to this allegations was sexually motivated.

The Teaching Regulation Agency found that the images he sent were unwanted, and that he should’ve known this before sending them.

Mr Pandor expressed regret for sending the pictures shortly afterwards, the agency found.

He had previously taught at the school from September 2016 to July 2017, before later returning in September 2023.

Following the allegations, he was suspended in April 2024, before losing his job in August the same year after a school investigation.

The Teaching Regulation Agency found that Mr Pandor’s behaviour amounted to ‘unacceptable professional conduct’ which ‘may bring the profession into disrepute’.

He was described in evidence as a ‘dedicated and capable educator and successful in engaging pupils’ and was ‘valued by both pupils and staff’, but that his behaviour ‘fundamentally breached the standard of conduct expected of a teacher’.

Mr Pandor was banned from teaching indefinitely, and can only apply for the prohibition order to be set aside in 2031.
