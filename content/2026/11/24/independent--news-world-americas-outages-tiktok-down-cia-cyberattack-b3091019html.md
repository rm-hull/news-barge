Americans endured yet another tech outage on Tuesday when TikTok went down, shortly after a new CIA cyberattack came to light.

The issues are the latest in a series of tech meltdowns across several sectors in the past few weeks, from a crippling Microsoft outage to bank issues and a communications glitch that grounded all US flights.

The problems have sparked fears that cyberattacks are becoming more common and could take out the technology that millions use for everyday tasks and work.

Here is a roundup of the latest high-profile outages to strike the US.

## 1. Global Microsoft outage

Office work ground to a halt on Thursday, Nov. 12, when Microsoft products, including Outlook, Teams and AI assistant Copilot, went offline and were not restored for around a full day.

Over 430 million people use Microsoft 365 apps, according to Microsoft. The outage sparked global chaos, affecting everything from hospitals and police forces to schools.

![A global Microsoft outage crippled many workplaces across the world last week](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/13/08/2304840726..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Downdetector, an online outage tracker, noted outage reports from users in more than 70 countries and territories.

The company apologized on Friday and said the outage was “not related to cyber” or artificial intelligence agents.

## 2. FAA ground stop

As Microsoft was still working to restore access, a second major outage was reported in the United States.

The Federal Aviation Administration issued a ground stop for all US flights after a newly installed router malfunctioned, overwhelming the aging air traffic control system.

Almost 25,000 flights were delayed and over 9,000 were canceled on Friday, per flight racking site FlightAware. The disruption lingered into Saturday morning, when more than 4,000 cancelations recorded, comprising of roughly 18% of flights departing the US.

![The FAA issued a ground stop for all US flights on Friday due to a tech problem](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/17/17/56/GettyImages-2305015185.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Tens of thousands of passengers across the US were stranded after the rare nationwide ground stop, only the second of its kind since the Sept. 11 attacks.

Officials said the FAA was still investigating the exact cause of the error.

## 3. Bank issues

Americans rushed to downdetector again on Monday, Nov. 16, just days after the Microsoft and FAA meltdowns, to report issues accessing their banking apps.

They reported outages affecting a handful of major US banks but it was determined that the issue stemmed from Chase Bank.

Chase said Monday that the technical problems were not the result of a cyberattack and that it was working to bring banking services back online by the end of the day.

“JPMorgan Chase has experienced a temporary issue related to a software bug in an on-premise hardware system — it is not cyber-related,” said Joe Evangelisti, spokesman for the bank.

![Chase Bank customers were unable to access some services on Monday because of an outage](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/16/20/11/AP26320714643957.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

## 4. United Airlines ground stop

Hours after the banking issues emerged, United Airlines was forced to issue a ground stop for all of its US flights.

A total of 600 flights were delayed by Monday evening as the airline worked to resolve the outage, which lasted roughly an hour and 40 minutes.

The carrier said there was "no indication that this was a cybersecurity-related issue” and apologized on social media, though it did not share specific details regarding which of its internal systems failed.

Pilots told air traffic controllers that the disruption involved a program used to establish an aircraft's final weight.

## 5. NSA issue

Another outage happened on Tuesday, Nov. 17, when a highly classified network that the US National Security Agency uses for communications went down.

“The National Security Agency experienced temporary degradation during scheduled routine maintenance,” the agency wrote in a statement on Tuesday afternoon.

“While some internal systems were affected, mission-critical capabilities remain fully operational and our ability to defend national security continues. NSA's infrastructure is designed with multiple layers of redundancy to maintain operations during planned maintenance or unexpected events.”

## 6. Apple and Marriott services

Many of Apple’s online services, including Apple Music, the App Store and other iCloud services, were affected by technical issues Tuesday afternoon.

The iCloud troubles affected the ability to sign in, including on new devices, meaning that they could not be activated after purchase as usual, and also stopped users from signing up for the AppleCare service to protect those devices.

The company said an internal software update caused the disruptions, with most systems being restored shortly after while teams worked on remaining issues.

“Many of our systems are back up and running after an internal update caused some outages this morning,” the company wrote. “We’re working to get the remaining systems online as soon as possible.”

Marriott International, the largest U.S. hotel operator, was also having tech issues that impacted reservations and check-ins, among other problems, according to users posting on Reddit.

![Asked whether he was worried about Thanksgiving week travel, Trump said ‘not at all’](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/16/21/59/GettyImages-2305377088.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

President Donald Trump brushed off the series of outages in remarks on Tuesday despite Americans’ growing worries.

“It’s life, it’s life,” he told reporters during a trip to the Philippines. “When you’re big and powerful and you have all the money, things like that happen. It’s fine.”

Danielle Lemons, vice president of operations at Nashville-based IT firm DirecTech Connect, told *The Independent* that people should be “concerned enough to plan, not to panic.”

“The real lesson is how many separate services quietly depend on the same few providers,” Lemons said. “When one of them goes down, email, file sharing, sign-ins and even phone systems can fail together, because they were all built on the same foundation.”

## 6. Delta Air Lines outage

A technical glitch in an automated system used to calculate aircraft weight caused delays for more than 1,000 Delta Air Lines flights on Sunday, Nov. 22, impacting travelers during a busy period directly ahead of the Thanksgiving holiday.

The FAA issued a ground stop lasting around an hour for selected flights heading into Hartsfield-Jackson Atlanta International Airport, Delta’s primary hub and one of the world's busiest airports.

The airline said that its operational teams were using manual workarounds to dispatch departures while efforts were underway to restore the automated weight-calculation system, which ensures planes are not too heavy to fly. Hours later, Delta confirmed the system was functioning again, though schedules remained far from normal.

Delta said it was assisting passengers in need of hotel rooms or meals while expressing hope that all delayed flights would be airborne by the end of the day.

## 7. TikTok and Chicago airport hit by outages

TikTok users flooded downdetector with outage reports on Tuesday, Nov. 24, with more than 26,200 reporting issues accessing the social media app.

TikTok told *The Verge* that a “brief technical issue” impacted access to the platform.

Just a short while later, the FAA issued a ground stop at Chicago O'Hare International Airport. The FAA blamed an equipment outage but did not immediately offer more information.

Hours earlier, it came to light that the CIA was hacked in the latest cyberattack targeting the United States’ intelligence agencies.

An unclassified CIA system was breached and the attack did not involve any highly sensitive information, a source told *The Associated Press*.

The issue has since been resolved but it adds to anxiety around recent hacks involving the FBI, which was targeted for a second time in recent months over the weekend.
