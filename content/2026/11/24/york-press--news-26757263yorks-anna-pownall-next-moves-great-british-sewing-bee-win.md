## What York’s Anna Pownall is doing after her Great British Sewing Bee victory

York’s Anna Pownall who won The Great British Sewing Bee on the BBC. *(Image: BBC)*

York’s Anna Pownall has been putting her creative talents to good use since being crowned winner of The Great British Sewing Bee.

The 27-year-old triumphed in the BBC programme’s 12th series after impressing judges Patrick Grant and Esme Young with her imaginative designs and technical skill.

Her success included three Garment of the Week awards and eight transformation challenge victories.

Since lifting the show’s coveted golden sewing machine trophy, Anna has continued to champion sustainable fashion and the possibilities offered by second-hand materials.

Working with St Leonard’s Hospice, where she is a member of the retail team, Anna produced a range of upcycled outfits for Secondhand September and Recycle Week.

Her creations used preloved curtains, shirts, bedsheets and tablecloths sourced from a hospice shop.

Anna has also stepped into York’s wider fashion scene.

![The 27-year-old said she had learnt ](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21461731.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The 27-year-old said she had learnt "so much" through the competition *(Image: BBC)*

She recently attended the inaugural Golden Thread Awards, organised by the Company of Merchant Taylors, and presented the Emerging Designer Award to Daniel Zak Roberts of Birmingham City University.

For the event, Anna wore an outfit made entirely from second-hand materials found in a St Leonard’s Hospice shop.

She said sustainability was a major issue for the fashion industry and hoped people could be inspired by the clothes and fabrics already available.

Anna also welcomed the opportunity for York and the North to showcase emerging designers outside London.

Marina Rose, who runs York based House of White Rose, was presented with the Golden Thread Award by Gary James McQueen, chair of the jury, on November 22 at The Milner York.

Further ahead, Anna has ambitions to turn her passion into a professional career.

She has spoken of her dream of working in costume design, particularly on period drama films, combining her love of corsetry, fashion history and storytelling through clothes.
