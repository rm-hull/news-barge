Members of the Senedd have rejected an attempt by Reform UK to remove the Welsh Parliament’s presiding officer from his post.

The decision follows an announcement earlier on Tuesday by Helen Jenner, leader of the party in Wales, who outlined plans to table a vote of no confidence against the Llywydd (presiding officer), Huw Irranca-Davies.

The standoff began earlier this month after Reform MS Joe Martin was barred from speaking over remarks made in the Senedd, where he claimed white British citizens were being replaced in the UK.

Mr Irranca-Davies regarded the statements as "unnecessarily provocative and deliberately offensive", ruling that Mr Martin could not speak in debates without first issuing an apology.

Maintaining that Reform had "no choice but to escalate this situation", Ms Jenner proceeded with the motion, which was voted down by Senedd members by 55 to 29.

Speaking in the Senedd, Ms Jenner said: “Free speech is vital in a democracy, and yes, if you support free speech, you absolutely have to be prepared to listen to ideas that you disagree with.

![The standoff began earlier this month after Reform MS Joe Martin was barred from speaking over remarks made in the Senedd](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/11/17/18/bigpic.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“I accept that some people have completely misunderstood, I’d argue wilfully in some cases, what Joe Martin was espousing in this chamber, but that is not his fault at all.

“Joe was in fact prepared to acknowledge that, but he was not prepared to apologise for speaking on behalf of voters.

“Nobody inside or outside this chamber should have immunity from offence caused by the words of an elected member of this place,” she added.

Responding, Mr Irranca-Davies said: “Parliamentary privilege has never meant that anything goes.

“It has never meant speech without standards, it has never meant rights without responsibilities.

“Every democratic legislature recognises this reality.

“Members will occasionally overstep the mark,” he added.

“The chair intervenes, a discussion takes place, an apology is made where necessary, business resumes.

“The point is not that members never make mistakes, the point is that they recognise the authority of this institution where those mistakes occur.

![Welsh Reform UK Leader Helen Jenner](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/11/17/2296381248..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“No party has a monopoly on virtue, no party has a monopoly on error.

“The rules apply equally to all of us, government and opposition, front bench and backbench, senior members and newly elected members, they apply equally to everyone, and they must continue to do so.”

Reform, the largest opposition group, began a boycott of Senedd proceedings on November 11 in a show of support for Mr Martin, but returned to the debating chamber on Tuesday.

The speaking ban followed comments made by Mr Martin in the Senedd on November 10.

He said: “Can I have a statement from your government on the demographic replacement of white British people taking place in the UK?

“Because the scale of change has been breathtaking.

“Mass immigration and differing birth rates mean that, in just 37 years’ time, in 2063, we will be a minority in our own country.

“That change has already taken place in London, Birmingham, Leicester, Slough, Luton and Manchester, and I don’t want it to happen here.

“Indeed, at every opportunity, the majority of the electorate have wanted less immigration so that their nation can be preserved.

“A nation is not an economic zone, it’s not a hotel, and neither is it a charity.

“It’s a family, which is capable of absorbing people of all races if done at a sensible rate, but not if it’s replaced altogether.”

Welsh minister Heledd Fychan responded by asking for a ruling on whether the comments were in line with Senedd rules.

Later that afternoon, Mr Irranca-Davies said he considered the comments made by Mr Martin to be “unnecessarily provocative and deliberately offensive”.

He said: “Whilst it is always possible to raise legitimate policy matters in relation to immigration in this Siambr, the focus of that contribution on race and ethnicity was clearly discriminatory, offensive, and detracted from the dignity of the Senedd.

“This is not the first time the member has pushed the boundaries in his contributions, and I have previously warned him about his conduct.

“Until I receive a clear indication that the member understands the boundaries of acceptable parliamentary conduct and apologises to the Siambr, we will not be calling the member to speak.”
