# Richmond Library opens in new Mercury House home despite concerns over move

## Richmond Library has opened in its new home at Mercury House, with North Yorkshire Council promising improved facilities and savings for taxpayers.

The library has moved from its long-standing premises on Queens Road to the council-owned building on Station Road, where it now shares space with other North Yorkshire Council services.

Critics have previously questioned whether the steep walk back into town could discourage some people from using the service, particularly those who previously visited the library while shopping in the Market Place.

However, the council says the new premises have an accessible entrance, disabled parking and bus stops outside the building.

![The existing library in Richmond.](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOjQzYTk1YzQyLTA2YzEtNDg0NC05NWIzLTljYzg0MmE3NDhmMzo5ZDZmYjA5Ny1iMmYzLTQ4YjMtYTIxOC00MzQ0ZjdmMTVmNmQ=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


Staff and volunteers have spent recent weeks transferring books and other resources to Mercury House, which also provides customer and registration services.

The council says the move will reduce running costs by making use of a building it already owns, rather than continuing to occupy premises on Queens Road which were partly leased from a private landlord.

The former library building had also suffered maintenance problems in recent years, resulting in some areas being closed to the public.

North Yorkshire Council’s executive member for culture and arts, Cllr Simon Myers, said: “We are delighted to welcome users into Richmond Library’s new home after a smooth transition over the last few weeks which has been months in the planning.

![Richmond Library is now housed in the council-owned Mercury House on Station Road.](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOjg3MWQ4YjIxLTg1NjYtNDE3Zi05NTNjLTFkYzk4YzYxYzhiZTpmZGVhMjMzOS1mMGEzLTRjNjQtYmNkZC00M2IyYTk2NTZkMTQ=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


“The building on Queens Road was partly leased from an external landlord, so utilising a building that we already own helps to secure its future.

“It also means that the library is now under the same roof as other important council services.”

He said staff and volunteers hoped the new premises would encourage more people to join the library and volunteer to help run it.

The new facilities include an improved children’s section, designed to accommodate more school groups, a dedicated IT area and ground-floor space for the local studies collection.

![The new-look library is now open, offering a more modern, fit-for-purpose space.](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOjc4ZWIwYTgyLWNiMmEtNGEyMy1hOWM5LTJhZjJjNzUwNTM1Nzo3N2ZkMzRlNC04MTlhLTRhOGMtODI4Zi0wNjRhZWI5MDYxY2Y=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


Since 2017, Richmond Library has been run in partnership with community organisation CRACCL, which also manages libraries in Colburn and Catterick.

The organisation relies on volunteers to help deliver day-to-day services, supported by council staff.

Library user and volunteer Caroline Stott said she was impressed by the new facilities.

“I walked into the building and was immediately impressed with how modern, light and airy it is,” she said.

“It is a much-improved space than the previous library building, with welcoming surroundings for existing and new customers.”
