A £750,000 payment made to the former Duke of York by a Turkish woman for help with her passport was “on any view corrupt”, the High Court has heard.

Nebahat Isbilen, 81, made the payment to Andrew Mountbatten-Windsor in 2019, but claims that she was misled by her former advisor, Selman Turk, about its purpose.

Ms Isbilen is suing Mr Turk at the High Court, alleging that he misappropriated tens of millions of pounds of her funds and did not explain where the money went.

The former duke, who is due to give evidence on 14 December, is not accused of wrongdoing and has since repaid the £750,000 sum.

Ms Isbilen’s lawyers have told a trial in London that Mr Turk dishonestly claimed the money paid to Andrew was needed as a “gift” for the former prince’s help with Ms Isbilen’s Turkish passport.

![The former Prince Andrew, now known as Andrew Mountbatten-Windsor](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/30/10/aa3aacffa81e98a17e5e8f2a54eee4d1Y29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzkzNDQwNDk2-2.83533534.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

But Mr Turk denies dishonesty and is defending the claim, arguing that Ms Isbilen knew the money was being paid in the hope that Andrew could help her imprisoned husband in Turkey.

Mr Turk’s lawyers have also told the court that it should not find that he breached a duty to act in Ms Isbilen’s best interests, known as a fiduciary duty.

Joshua Hitchens, for Mr Turk, said his client “acted only with (Ms Isbilen’s) express authorisation or under her direct instruction”.

In court on Tuesday, Mr Hitchens said that while it was “not implicit that the Duke of York agreed to accept a bribe”, the payment to him was “corrupt”.

He said: “It actually matters not if the claimant’s case or the defendant’s case is correct about the Duke of York payment.

“The claimant understood that she was paying £750,000 for a payment that on any view was corrupt.”

![Selman Turk arriving at court](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/23/14/01M84AFJTQZ2XG3870BET92Z96.jpg?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&auto=webp)

He continued: “My client’s case is that there was a £750,000 payment for the Duke of York, on the expectation, on the understanding, that the Duke of York would seek to influence a foreign government on behalf of a woman he hardly knew.

“This was a payment which offends the public interest because it involves someone in public life, in a position of power, receiving payments from an individual he did not know… on the expectation that he would use that position in public life either to secure a residency permit or to lobby a foreign government.”

Opening her case on Monday, Simon Colton KC said that Ms Isbilen is part of the Evyap family, which owns a major soap and cosmetics firm in Turkey.

In written submissions, Mr Colton said that Ms Isbilen met Mr Turk in 2017 and he “persuaded Ms Isbilen that her assets were at risk and that he was able to transfer them to a safe place”.

The barrister continued that Mr Turk “deliberately and systematically abused” Ms Isbilen’s trust and transferred the money to companies he owned or controlled, for his own benefit, leaving her the victim of a “major, cynical fraud”.

![Andrew Mountbatten-Windsor will give evidence in December](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/19/23/51d29a8f15ade955fb50c27b8dcc68a6Y29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzk1MDkxMjk2-2.75468787.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Mr Colton also said that “all of the transactions were procured by (Mr Turk) in breach of trust or fiduciary duty” and that Ms Isbilen “remained in the dark” about how her money was being used.

He continued that Ms Isbilen was asked to sign documents written in English, which she did not understand, and of which some were “bogus”.

He said: “The transactions themselves, and/or what was subsequently done with the money, were inconsistent with Ms Isbilen’s express instruction to keep her assets safe.

“Even if the court does not find that those instructions were given, the transactions were manifestly inappropriate for a person in Ms Isbilen’s position, ie an elderly lady, in a vulnerable position, with no dependents, no earning capacity, and a consequent need for low-risk passive income rather than chasing high-risk returns.

“This was not rocket science.”

The trial before Mr Justice Adam Johnson is due to conclude in March, with a written judgment at a later date.
