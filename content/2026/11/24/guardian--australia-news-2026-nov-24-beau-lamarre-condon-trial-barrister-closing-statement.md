Beau Lamarre-Condon could have sat in silence and let the crown “do its worst”, but he made the choice to take the “long walk” to the witness box, a jury has been told.

Lamarre-Condon is accused of fatally shooting Jesse Baird and his partner, Luke Davies, with his police service weapon in Baird’s Paddington home on 19 February 2024.

The former officer has pleaded not guilty to two counts of murder and one charge of aggravated break and enter.

His barrister John Stratton SC urged the jury to accept Lamarre-Condon’s testimony as truthful, as he wrapped up his closing statement on Tuesday morning.

“He didn’t have to give evidence. He could have said to the crown, ‘Do your worst but I’m not going to help you’,” Stratton told the New South Wales supreme court.

“He didn’t do that.

“He took that long walk to the witness box, and he did it voluntarily and he subjected himself to a detailed, fierce cross-examination by a very experienced crown prosecutor that went over six days.”

Stratton claimed the cross-examination did not break down his client, nor did it establish he was telling new lies.

“The fact the accused has chosen to give evidence does not mean he needs to prove his case … the onus of proof is always on the crown,” he said.

The double murder trial is expected to wrap up in the coming days, with the jury soon set to begin deliberations.

Justice Natalie Adams will spend the afternoon giving her instructions to the jurors.
