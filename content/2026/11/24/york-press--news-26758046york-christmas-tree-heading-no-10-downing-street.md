## 20ft giant grown in York is heading for Britain’s most famous street

Kirstie, Olly and Joe Combe, of York Christmas Trees, with the impressive 20ft Nordmann Fir chosen to stand outside No 10 Downing Street, before it was felled. *(Image: Nadia Jefferson-Brown)*

York is sending some festive cheer to Downing Street after a magnificent 20ft Christmas tree was chosen for one of the country’s most famous addresses.

The towering tree destined for No 10 has been cut down in York today, Tuesday, ahead of its journey to London.

The giant Nordmann Fir was felled at York Christmas Trees at Wigginton Lodge, Wigginton Road, before being prepared for the trip south to the capital.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21758066.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Olly and Kirstie Combe with their son Joe, of York Christmas Trees, with the Downing Street Christmas tree before it is felled. *(Image: Nadia Jefferson-Brown)*

The tree will become one of the most prominent Christmas trees in the country after the family-run Wigginton business was named Christmas Tree Grower of the Year by the British Christmas Tree Growers Association (BCTGA).

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21758075.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Olly Combe, of York Christmas Trees, after felling the Nordmann Fir chosen for No 10 Downing Street. *(Image: Nadia Jefferson-Brown)*There were more than 100 entries vying for the title this year, with the standard described as "exceptionally high".**Recommended reads:**

York Christmas Trees won the BCTGA's national competition, in which its Nordmann Fir took first place in its category as well as the overall title.

The 20ft tree has taken 20 years to reach its impressive height, having been planted in 2006 when Labour's Tony Blair was Prime Minister and Taylor Swift released her self‑titled debut album in the UK.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21758065.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Olly and Kirstie Combe's son Joe, of York Christmas Trees, marking the Downing Street Christmas tree with a number 10 before it was netted ready to be transported to the capital. *(Image: Nadia Jefferson-Brown)*

Olly Combe runs York Christmas Trees with his wife Kirstie, who grew up on the farm when it was her family home with her late parents Nigel and Gillian Stephenson.

Olly is due to begin transporting the tree to London on Wednesday. It will be delivered to a holding bay on Thursday morning before the Downing Street team takes charge of its final journey to No 10.

Standing in front of the tree in Cottage Field, at York Christmas Trees, Olly told The Press: "I planted this in 2006 with a good friend from the Christmas Trees Growers.

"I nurtured this one; I've cut all the trees round it, and I kept it for a good customer but I never realised how special that customer would be."

![A giant 20ft Nordmann Fir was felled at York Christmas Trees in Wigginton, destined for No 10 Downing Street after the family-run business was named Christmas Tree Grower of the Year by the British Christmas Tree Growers Association.](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21758062.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The Nordmann Fir being carefully netted ahead of its journey south. *(Image: Nadia Jefferson-Brown)*

It is not the only Christmas tree from the York grower heading to a prominent destination this year.

A 7ft Nordmann Fir is also being supplied to stand outside No 10 in the North in Manchester.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21758064.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Olly and Kirstie Combe, of York Christmas Trees, carrying a 7ft Nordmann Fir which they are supplying to stand outside No 10 in the North in Manchester. *(Image: Nadia Jefferson-Brown)*

It is the second time in six years York Christmas Trees has earned the honour of supplying the tree for Downing Street, having previously won in 2020.

However, Covid restrictions meant the family had been unable to attend the lights switch-on that year.

Olly said they were incredibly proud to have won the privilege again.

This time, Olly, Kirstie and their children Tom, 25, Lottie, 22, and Joe, 18, are due to attend the light switch-on on Monday, November 30.

Olly's 84-year-old mother is also hoped to be among the family contingent at the event, which is due to be attended by the Prime Minister Andy Burnham.

Kirstie said: "It is quite something to be recognised by your peers who grow trees for a living and who all aspire to grow better trees every year."

She said needles fastness, colour and shape were the main characteristics of their trees which each take about eight years to grow in the field.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21758067.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Wreath makers Michelle Shaw, Trudi Lister and Elaine Yorke, who are part of the York Christmas Trees festive team. *(Image: Nadia Jefferson-Brown)*

All the company's trees are grown on its own farmland within two miles of its Christmas Barn Shop and are pruned by hand twice a year to promote healthy growth and shape.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21758071.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Kirstie Combe in the York Christmas Trees festive barn on Wigginton Road, Wigginton. *(Image: Nadia Jefferson-Brown)*

York Christmas Trees was founded in 1998 when the family planted its first trees, and it has also supplied Christmas trees to York Minster – including this year's four towering trees as well as 102 smaller ones for the upcoming Christmas tree festival – as well as to Castle Howard, Grantley Hall and numerous pubs, restaurants and hotels.

"The first year we were open we had 73 trees to sell over the three weekends we were open," said Kirstie, adding that they now plant 15,000 a year with each tree taking seven to 10 years before they are harvested.

"We put up this barn about eight years ago and then had to extend it. At busy times we have two teams working in the field.

"Agricultural workers who are quieter at this time of year help us. In the barn, we have four to five staff replenishing trees, helping people to choose, netting the trees, and we also have a team of wreath makers."

The company also received the BCTGA Innovation Award in 2023 for developing the "Doziroller", a tool designed to help produce the ideal Christmas tree shape.
