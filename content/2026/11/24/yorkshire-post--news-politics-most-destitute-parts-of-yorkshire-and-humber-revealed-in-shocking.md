# Most destitute parts of Yorkshire and Humber revealed in shocking research

## The York-based Joseph Rowntree Foundation said: “Without action, we risk destitution becoming the norm for millions in the UK.”

Record numbers of people continue to experience destitution, including more than one million children, new research has found.

The study, funded by the York-based Joseph Rowntree Foundation and conducted by Heriot-Watt University with Verian, found that around 3.8m people experienced destitution in 2025.

This is defined as being unable to afford the basic necessities that we all need to eat, stay warm and dry, and keep clean.

It has remained at the record levels seen since 2022, when the number of people affected more than tripled compared with 2017.

This has meant that exceptionally high levels of destitution is becoming the norm for many people.

People are increasingly reliant on emergency support services – such as food banks – to meet their basic needs on a longer-term basis, the JRF said, rather than using them only for short-term crisis support.

In particular, disabled people, migrants, ethnic minority communities and single working adults are likely to be caught up.

Hull has the highest levels of destitution in Yorkshire and the Humber, followed by Bradford and Sheffield.

Julian Hartley, group chief executive of the JRF, said: “With living standards stagnating across the board, there is a risk of forgetting about those experiencing the very deepest forms of hardship.

“But today’s figures show that far too many people are going without life’s most basic necessities, while support services are buckling under the weight of need.

“Without action, we risk destitution becoming the norm for millions in the UK.”
