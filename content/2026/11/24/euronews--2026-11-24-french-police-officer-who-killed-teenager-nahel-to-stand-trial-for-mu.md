## Judges reversed an earlier downgrade of the charge, ruling that a shot to the chest fired from 16cm pointed to an intent to kill. The officer now faces up to 30 years in prison.

The Versailles Court of Appeal in France ruled on Tuesday that Florian M., the police officer who shot dead Nahel Merzouk on 27 June 2023, should stand trial for murder before an assize court.

He faces up to 30 years in prison, which would have been capped at 15 years for violence resulting in death without intent to kill, before any aggravating circumstances are taken into account.

Florian M. will therefore be tried by an assize court with a lay jury, rather than by a criminal court made up exclusively of professional judges.

The ruling comes after a series of legal twists in a case that has become a symbol of police violence.

On 27 June 2023, Nahel Merzouk, 17, was killed in Nanterre, in the Hauts-de-Seine department, when Florian M. shot him at point-blank range during a traffic stop. The scene was filmed and widely shared on social media, and it led to several nights of violence across France.

After a two-year investigation, two investigating judges ordered the officer to stand trial for murder before the Hauts-de-Seine assize court, in line with the recommendation of the Nanterre public prosecutor's office.

Florian M., who spent five months in pre-trial detention before being released, appealed against the decision.

In March, the investigating chamber of the Versailles Court of Appeal reclassified the case. It ordered the officer to stand trial before the Hauts-de-Seine departmental criminal court on the lesser charge of violence resulting in death without intent to kill, to the dismay of Nahel's family, who are civil parties in the case.

In their ruling, the judges found the evidence "insufficient" to establish that Florian M. had intended to kill. They also upheld the dismissal of the case against the second police officer present during the traffic stop.

The reclassification satisfied no one. The prosecutor general's office, the civil parties and the defence, which had hoped for the case to be dropped, all appealed to the Court of Cassation, France's highest court.

## **The question of intent to kill**

In a new twist, the Court of Cassation overturned the reclassification in June, once again paving the way for Florian M. to be tried for murder. It also confirmed the dismissal of the case against the other officer.

The Court of Cassation, which rules solely on points of law and not on the facts of a case, found that the appeal court had "not justified its decision" to reclassify the charge.

The case was therefore sent back to the investigating chamber of the Versailles Court of Appeal. In September, a different panel of judges re-examined whether Florian M. had intended to kill.

On Tuesday, the court found that his use of a firearm, the short distance of the shot and the part of the body struck pointed to an intent to kill. The shot was fired 16cm from the windscreen of the car Nahel was driving, and it hit him in the chest, a vital area.

The ruling, which was delivered behind closed doors, can still be appealed. Franck Berton, the lawyer for Nahel's mother, had argued that the officer should stand trial for murder. The officer's lawyer, Laurent-Franck Liénard, had declined to comment before the ruling.

Before Tuesday's decision, the Justice for Nahel committee, of which his mother Mounia Merzouk is a member, had called on the court to deliver a "firm decision" by upholding the murder charge. Louise Tort, who represents several members of the family, said they had been waiting "impatiently" for the ruling.
