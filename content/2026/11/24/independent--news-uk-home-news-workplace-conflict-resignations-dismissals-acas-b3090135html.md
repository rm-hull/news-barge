Workplace conflict is costing employers an estimated £51.2 billion annually, according to new research.

Findings from conciliation service Acas, produced alongside researchers at the University of Westminster, indicate that 14 million employees experienced conflict in 2025.

More than one in 10 of those affected subsequently left their jobs, forming the largest share of costs, with resignations and dismissals accounting for an estimated £29.5 billion a year, Acas stated.

Baroness Maggie Jones, chair at Acas said: “Workplace conflict is becoming more complex and comes at a substantial cost.

“Our research shows that 14 million employees across the UK experienced conflict at work in 2025, costing employers £51.2 billion annually.

“Conflict can do serious damage to working relationships, productivity, wellbeing and staff retention.

![Resignations and dismissals account for an estimated £29.5 billion a year, Acas said](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/23/17/41/iStock-2242952093.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“Rising demand for Acas services, growing case complexity and more discrimination-related disputes all point to the need for prompt action.

“These costs are not inevitable. Investing in training and effective management, and promoting healthy relationships and early resolution, are good for people and business.”

Ann Francke, chief executive of the Chartered Management Institute, said: “Losing £51 billion a year to workplace conflict is a wake-up call for British business.

“Too many disputes spiral into costly tribunals simply because line managers lack the confidence to spot tensions early and tackle difficult conversations head-on.

“When one in three workplace disputes goes without even a conversation to resolve it, we’re seeing the rise of the accidental manager: people promoted for their technical expertise, but woefully lacking the training and people skills to lead a team.”

In an Acas report released last year, capability and performance issues were named as the most common causes of workplace conflict, followed by personal disagreements and relationship problems.

Stress, anxiety and depression were the most common impacts of conflict, the report added, followed by a drop in motivation or commitment.

Speaking at the time, Niall Mackenzie, Acas Chief Executive, said: “For 50 years, Acas has been at the heart of workplace relations, helping employers and employees navigate change and find common ground even in the most challenging circumstances.

“Our report into conflict prevalence shows that there is still a need for Acas’s experts to bring their experience and cool heads to workplace disputes.”
