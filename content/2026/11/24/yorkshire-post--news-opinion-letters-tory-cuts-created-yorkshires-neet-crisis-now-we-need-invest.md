# Tory cuts created Yorkshire's NEET crisis - now we need investment in our young: Yorkshire Post Letters

## Dr Peter Williams, Malton.

The five Conservative governments since 2010 cut Sure Start centres, the Connexions youth service, careers guidance, youth clubs, playing fields, leisure centres, education maintenance allowance and further education funding.

Schools saw their resources and specialist staffing cut for music, sports, arts, drama and technology.

Mental health services for children disadvantaged by Covid were slashed.

![British Chancellor of the Exchequer George Osborne (R) listens as British Prime Minister David Cameron pictured in 2016 (Photo by Daniel LEAL / AFP)](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOmI0ZTMyYWMyLWZhOWMtNDE3ZC04MzNlLWRjMjhlNzQzOWIxZTowNzQ0MzVjOS03MzdhLTQ1YTItYTYzMC1kMTI1ZTRhMjVhY2Y=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


This failure to invest saved money in the short term but stacked up huge future costs in probation, police, justice and prison service.

Another casualty was the national shame of one million 16-24 year-olds not in education, employment or training (NEET). In Yorkshire and The Humber the NEET proportion (15.6 per cent) is the third highest in England. Contrast this with 4.8 per cent in the Netherlands.

The Yorkshire and Humber region has the highest child poverty rate in England.

Young people in our coastal and rural areas face the additional disadvantage of widely scattered resources and poor public transport.

Their stark choice is to stay NEET and reliant on benefits or to move away for city jobs, leaving our villages and small towns hollowed out.

The present government is the first in decades to not duck this problem, but 14 years of cutbacks can't be reversed by 'quick fixes' over one parliament.

The proposed youth hubs, operating under local direction, will improve coordination between schools, colleges, job centres, NHS services and employers.

As with all investments, this has upfront costs but savings in the long run.

It will help a disadvantaged generation develop as earners and taxpayers, boosting the economy and revitalising local communities. If we love our children, this overdue initiative must be given time to work.

**Peter Auty, Great Hatfield.**

It has taken 12 months for the review into youth unemployment to conclude. The conclusion that wages are too low not benefits too high is typical blinkered Labour.

It is time benefits were only available as a lifeline not a lifestyle.

What's wrong with a 40-hour working week then add any necessary benefits?

Around 8.75 million (this figure was 1.25 million in 1992) people now work part time in the UK plus benefits, its not right.

It is time to stop private equity backed companies offering to help gain a diagnosis for a fee to gain benefits, this should be outlawed immediately.

Employers should not be constrained by tax and NI costs against employing younger workers.

Work and the work ethic should andmust come to the fore. Working age people should want to work where ever possible for their own peace of mind and mental health.

Interaction with others is a great benefit for being employed.

**Phil Baggaley, Hull.**

Could Alan Milburn let us know what benefits the youth of families who have never worked for generations and have no intentions of working themselves will be entitled to?
