CNN’s resident conservative Scott Jennings told his fellow pundits to “lighten up” after they objected to President Donald Trump’s meme of Nvidia boss Jensen Huang holding up a red bra.

Huang, 63, resigned from the chipmaker on November 12 after he was alleged to have had extramarital affairs with three women, one of whom is accused of being a Chinese spy by U.S. intelligence.

Trump lobbied hard for his friend’s reinstatement and celebrated his success by posting an AI-generated image on Truth Social, which parodied the infamous ‘Butler assassination attempt’ photo. The image was taken seconds after the president was shot in the ear by sniper Thomas Matthew Crooks in Pennsylvania, on July 13, 2024, raising his fist in defiance while being held by Secret Service agents.

On *CNN NewsNight*, guest host Elie Honig had already overseen a heated debate between Jennings and his colleagues about the Huang affair in which the right-winger had argued the Nvidia leader was crucial to ensuring the U.S. won the AI arms race against China, prompting Democratic strategist Ameshia Cross to accuse him of putting the CEO “above the law.”

![President Donald Trump posted a meme of Nvidia boss Jensen Huang holding up a red bra after he was reinstated as CEO Monday](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/23/17/22/trump-nvidia-truth-social.png?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Returning later to Trump’s meme sending up the situation, Honig asked Jennings: “I get the reference to Butler, Pennsylvania, here, but what the hell is up with the red bra? What does that mean? Interpret for us.”

The conservative appeared reluctant to do so, answering: “Do you actually need me to tell you what it means? Do you really need me to spell it out for you?”

When Honig insisted, Jennings answered: “I mean, you could just lighten up a little and chuckle. I mean, it’s a meme.”

The host then invoked Corey Comperatore, the 50-year-old retired fire chief who was killed at Trump’s Butler rally as he attempted to shield his family from the sniper’s bullets.

“Who’s Corey Comperatore, Scott?” Honig asked. “Do you think he thinks that’s funny? His family thinks that’s funny? You think his family’s laughing at that image with the bra? ‘Haha?’”

![CNN Newsnight pundit Scott Jennings laughs off Trump's Huang meme](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/24/16/38/Scott-Jennings.png?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&trim=0%2C291%2C0%2C171&auto=webp)

“Well, his family, I think, is very grateful for how the president has interacted with them – one,” Jennings answered. “Two – are you seriously telling me that you don’t see a little bit of humor in this on a day like today?

“I mean, the president was shot. Does he not have the right to make light of his own situation?”

It was left to fellow panelist Ashley Allison to interject, addressing Honig: “We can’t even agree that a man who might have been compromised by a Chinese spy shouldn’t be running this company and you think you’re going to get a concession on a meme?”

Earlier in the day, Trump had welcomed Huang’s return by congratulating Nvidia’s board of directors on making a “correct and proper decision” and declaring: “The Country needs Jensen’s leadership at Nvidia in order to ensure the Success and Prosperity of not only the Company, but the American Economy.”

![The Huang meme is parody of Trump’s pose after he was shot at in Butler two years ago](https://static.independent.co.uk/2024/12/17/14/SEI231651580.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

He also dismissed concerns about Huang potentially being exploited by adding: “The United States Government has no reason to believe that Jensen’s return to Nvidia poses any risk to National Security.

“The Attorney General of the United States has confirmed that the U.S. Government does not possess evidence that he has engaged in any unlawful conduct.”

He subsequently confirmed his role in prompting the board to change course during an interview with CNBC: “I spoke to them, let me put it that way. And I convinced them… I made the deal… The board decided to do what we wanted to do.”

It has been estimated the president may have earned as much as $15 million from the share price rebound that greeted Monday’s news and sent Nvidia’s stock value soaring by 31.5 percent.

White House spokesman Davis Ingle told *The Independent* that Trump’s investment portfolio is “independently managed by third-party financial institutions,” adding: “Neither President Trump nor any member of his family has any ability to direct, influence, or provide input regarding how the portfolio is invested or when investments are bought or sold.”
