## Latest on investigation into alleged sexual assault near York car park

A police cordon in the Coppergate Centre, York, on October 1 *(Image: Ben Hardy)*

A police investigation continues nearly two months on following an alleged sexual assault near a York city centre car park.

The incident was reported to have taken place at around 7pm on Wednesday, September 30 on a public footpath connecting Piccadilly to Castle Car Park which led to the closure of Coppergate Car Park.

North Yorkshire Police said in an updated statement issued on Tuesday (November 24) that the investigation remains ongoing.




Detectives said a white man seen in the city centre at the time wearing a pink T-shirt with a black circular logo on the front may have important information to help the investigation.

A police spokesperson said the alleged victim cannot recall the exact location of the reported assault.

Police said at the time that the alleged victim was being supported by specially trained officers while detectives carried out urgent enquiries to establish the full circumstances surrounding the incident.

Senior investigating officer Matt Wilkinson said: "The man seen in the city centre at the time of the incident may have important information which could assist our investigation, and I would urge him to come forward and speak to us.

"I would also urge anyone who was in York city centre at around 7pm on September 30 and saw or heard anything that could assist our investigation to contact us.

"Even if you think the information you have is insignificant, please get in touch.

"It could prove important to our enquiries."

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21527560.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Police were in Piccadilly on Thursday, October 1 after a reported sexual assault the previous evening *(Image: Haydn Lewis)*

A cordon remained in place next to Coppergate Car Park the following day, and the car park was closed.

Anyone with information is urged to contact police on 101, quoting the reference 12260192537.

Workers in the Piccadilly area were greeted with the sight of a police cordon as they arrived for their shifts on the morning of October 1.

An officer from North Yorkshire Police would be seen giving directions and answering questions from members of the public and shoppers who were wondering what was going on while the cordon was in place.

Nearby businesses including Shopmobility York issued an apology to customers at the time as they were forced to close while the police cordon was in place.

A spokesperson for Shopmobility York said: "We sincerely apologise for any inconvenience caused. Unfortunately, this situation is beyond our control, and we appreciate your patience and understanding."

North Yorkshire Police guidance on receiving support after rape and sexual assault says: "If you've been raped or sexually assaulted, we're here for you. If you're ready to, you can report rape and sexual assault to us online.

"But if you don't want to talk to us, or if you want extra support, there are lots of people who can help.

"You're in control, and it's up to you who you talk to and what help you get.

"You can speak to these people and organisations privately and confidentially, and unless they think someone is in serious danger, they won't share anything with us."

The force's general guidance also states a Sexual Assault Referral Centre (SARC) is a place you can go after a rape or sexual assault to get confidential medical, practical and emotional support from specialist doctors, nurses and support workers.
