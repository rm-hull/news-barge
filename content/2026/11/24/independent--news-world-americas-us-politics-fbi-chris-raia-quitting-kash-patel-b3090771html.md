Deputy FBI director Chris Raia is reportedly retiring next month, making him the third person in the role to exit the bureau under Kash Patel’s controversial leadership.

Raia, considered to be a stabilizing presence at the FBI amid Patel’s turbulent tenure, is expected to leave the agency in December after less than a year in the job, according to *The Washington Post.*

The deputy, a longtime FBI career official, was responsible for the day-to-day running of the bureau and his reported departure leaves a critical vacancy to fill.

Raia was tapped to replace MAGA podcaster Dan Bongino, who left the bureau last December after reported clashes with former Attorney General Pam Bondi over the administration’s handling of the Jeffrey Epstein files.

There was a second deputy alongside Bongino, former Missouri attorney general Andrew Bailey, who resigned in September. Neither Bongino nor Bailey had previous FBI experience before they landed the No. 2 role at the agency, the *Post* noted. Bailey has not been replaced.

People familiar with Raia’s exit told the *Post*that he brought a “calmer and more professional” atmosphere to the FBI, and noted his experience of overseeing counterterrorism cases.* The Independent* has contacted the FBI for comment.

Under Patel’s leadership, the FBI has been dogged by controversies and dozens of staff purges. In February, he fired at least 10 employees who worked on the investigation into classified documents at President Donald Trump’s Mar-a-Lago estate.

Patel accused the fired agents of “weaponizing” the FBI during congressional testimony, which they refuted in a lawsuit they filed against him in October.

Reports of the FBI director’s personal conduct have dominated his tenure at the agency, including allegations of excessive drinking, which he vehemently denies, and accusations of using government jets on personal trips.

Patel is locked in a legal battle against *The Atlantic* after the outlet published a bombshell report claiming the director is deeply paranoid about being fired and often drinks to excess, alarming officials at the law enforcement agency and beyond.

He is suing the outlet in a $250 million defamation lawsuit. *The Atlantic* said it stood by its reporting and “will not be intimidated by acts of politically motivated retaliation.”

![](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/24/15/2294916094..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Raia filled in for Patel at the House Homeland Security Committee’s annual “Worldwide Threats” hearing last week after the director “refused” to appear, lawmakers said.

GOP committee chairman Andrew R. Garbarino said he was “disappointed” by Patel’s absence.

A former Coast Guard officer, Raia joined the FBI in 2003 and during the course of his two-decade career has investigated violent crime, drugs and gangs as well as overseen counterterrorism and national security investigations.

Raia helped lead the response to the deadly truck attack in New Orleans on New Year's Day last year and was picked to run the New York office in April after having served as a top counterterrorism official at FBI headquarters.
