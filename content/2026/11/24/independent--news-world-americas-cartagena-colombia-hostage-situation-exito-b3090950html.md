Twenty-five people are being held hostage inside a grocery store in the Colombian city of Cartagena after a failed hijacking of an armored cash truck.

Five older women who were being held have been released, according to President Abelardo De La Espriella. Military and police forces have been deployed to the store and are working to resolve the situation.

The chaos began when a group attempted to take control of an armored car transporting cash from a mall. Security camera operators advised police via a panic button, De La Espriella said.

They then "had no option but to take refuge in one of the stores," he said.

![Military personnel stand guard near an Exito supermarket as authorities respond to a reported hostage situation, in Cartagena, Colombia, November 24, 2026](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/24/19/17/2026-11-24T165933Z_1782930985_RC25UOALO9HX_RTRMADP_3_COLOMBIA-HOSTAGES.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

*Reuters* images showed heavily armed military and police gathered outside an Exito supermarket in the La Castellana neighborhood.

"Thirty people were kidnapped, five of whom have already been released. They are older women," said De La Espriella from the city of Villavicencio, where he traveled following an overnight car bombing there.

"All the capabilities of our various security forces have now been deployed, including special units," he added.

Images on social media showed some people kneeling against the windows of the store earlier on Tuesday, their hands seemingly bound. *Reuters* could not immediately independently verify the images.

![People watch a police operation to release 25 hostages held by gunmen at an Exito supermarket in Cartagena, Colombia](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/24/19/2306189627..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

"For Grupo Exito, protecting the people's lives and safety is our priority," Exito supermarket said in a statement. "The situation at our store in Cartagena is being handled by the relevant authorities. As a company, we are following their guidance and their handling of the situation."

Cartagena, especially its walled colonial historic district, is a popular tourist destination for both Colombians and people from other countries.

The Rafael Nunez airport in Cartagena briefly suspended landings as the armed forces used one of the runways in connection with the incident. Landings were reopened about 45 minutes after the suspension.
