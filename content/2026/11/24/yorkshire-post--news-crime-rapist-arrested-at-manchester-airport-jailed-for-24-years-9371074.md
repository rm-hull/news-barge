# Rapist arrested at Manchester Airport jailed for 24 years

## A rapist who was arrested at Manchester Airport earlier this year as he arrived back in the UK has been jailed for 24 years.

Mohammed Khalid, 55, was found guilty of eight offences including rape and sexual assault.

The offending took place between 2003 and 2006 in Kirklees against two teenage girls.

Khalid had fled the country in 2020 while under investigation by officers conducting wider enquiries into sexual offences committed in Kirklees in the late 1990s and early noughties.

Numerous enquiries were undertaken to find Khalid, who was arrested at Manchester airport on April 23, 2026 as he arrived back in the UK.

Khalid was taken into custody, charged and put before magistrates in Kirklees the very next day.

At a trial in October a jury found him guilty of five counts of rape, and an offence of sexual assault against one survivor, with offending taking place in the Kirklees area. He was also found guilty of providing Class A drugs to a second victim in 2005.

In a statement read in court, the survivor of sexual offences, now a mum, said she was proud of the educational success she had achieved in the years since Khalid’s abuse. She said she often wondered, however, how different her life would have been if not for his acts.

In extracts from her statement, she said:“For a long time, I believed that the abuse was my fault and that I deserved it.

“Those beliefs have affected my sense of self-worth and the way I behave with others. I judge myself harshly, struggle to say no and frequently put other people's needs before my own. My mental health has affected every aspect of my life, from my relationships and parenting to my education, career opportunities and physical wellbeing.

“I often isolate myself at home because it feels safe, but that safety comes at the expense of friendships and experiences“Even having to attend trial has taken something from me. The months leading up to it are blurry, and the experience has changed me. I resent being put through the distress of giving evidence and going through a trial when Khalid could have accepted responsibility.

“I cannot change what happened to me, and I will never know the person I might have become. But I do know that I deserved a childhood free from abuse, and a life that was not made harder by its consequences. I want to move forward, to feel safe and to be happy without my past continuing to shape my future.”

DCI Phil Davis of Kirklees District Police, said Khalid committed some “dreadful offences” and the powerful impact statement from one of the survivors in this case really makes clear the lasting trauma he has caused.

DCI Davis continued: “As she has so eloquently said, by refusing to accept his guilt he put both women through the distress of a trial, forcing them to relive the criminality he subjected them to. “We welcome his sentencing today and hope it may help bring both women a measure of closure.

"Their courage in supporting the prosecution has been instrumental in making this man answer for his crimes.”
