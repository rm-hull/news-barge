Waist-deep floodwaters have turned streets into waterways across **Puerto Ayora** on**Santa Cruz Island**, where aerial images reveal whole neighbourhoods submerged and ground footage captures residents evacuating by boat or straining to rescue furniture, appliances and personal belongings from inundated homes and shops. Alongside** military teams and community volunteers**, who are serving** meals from community kitchens**and helping displaced people into** emergency shelters**, locals have thrown up** makeshift barriers** against rising water and started the grim task of clearing out sodden properties.

The deluge is thought to be tied to **El Niño**, the climate phenomenon marked by unusually warm equatorial** Pacific**waters that frequently brings heightened rainfall to** Ecuador**and the** Galápagos archipelago**, even as scientists stress that individual weather events cannot simply be attributed to** El Niño**in isolation. For residents left counting the cost of lost belongings and damaged businesses,** emergency shelters offer immediate food and refuge** ahead of a gruelling clean-up.
