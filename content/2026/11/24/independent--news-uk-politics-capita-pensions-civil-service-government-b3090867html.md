Fallback plans have been established for the UK’s Civil Service pension scheme should Capita fail, the government has said, after the firm’s chief executive resigned on Monday over operational problems.

Cabinet Office minister Sally Jameson refused to outline the back-up strategy when questioned in Parliament, but assured MPs that ministers had learnt lessons from Carillion’s collapse in 2018.

Shares in Capita plummeted by about 70 per cent yesterday following the announcement that Adolfo Hernandez would leave with "immediate effect".

The resignation follows widespread criticism over delays in administering the 1.7 million member scheme, which left thousands of civil servants awaiting payments and retirement quotes.

Ms Jameson said she would meet Mr Hernandez’ replacement Pablo Andres in the coming days.

Capita has revealed plans to raise capital from shareholders, warning that annual profits will take further hits due to ongoing scheme management issues.

![Capita said it was now expecting its underlying operating profit for 2027 to be knocked by between £85 million and £95 million, having previously expected the hit to be up to £40 million](https://static.independent.co.uk/2023/04/03/09/03090232-8e52ee47-3f5c-4e79-bcbb-e32c400d7245.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Reform UK deputy leader Richard Tice, who also holds the party’s business brief, said he feared Capita could fold.

As well as running the pension scheme, Capita is also responsible for running the recruitment process for the British Army, and has contracts for the Royal Navy, Department for Work and Pensions and NHS England.

Mr Tice said: “This company is too big to fail, and we cannot face an equivalent of Carillion PLC. But that’s where this is going, and it’s all very well saying we must rely on the commercial company and the shareholders. There’s no guarantee that the shareholders will pony up.

“This company could well go bust in the next three months, and the reality is the government needs to have a plan B and a plan C and a plan D that may involve ensuring that it can merge with another outsourcing company.”

Ms Jameson acknowledged Capita’s performance had been “abysmal”, but said she believed the firm had the resources to carry out its plan to cut down on the backlog of cases.

She said: “The government routinely monitors the financial health of all of its strategic suppliers, particularly ones as big as Capita, and we do work across government to make sure that relevant contingencies are in place.

“I’m not in a position to discuss that in detail in this chamber now, but it is routine now, and lessons were learnt on the back of what happened with Carillion to make sure that government doesn’t find itself in that position again, particularly around transparency.

“Capita up to this point have been transparent with government and let us know what their position is. And while ever that continues, we will work with them.”

Capita had previously failed to meet a deadline of 30 June to deliver the terms of a £239 million pensions contract after missing earlier goals.

![Reform UK deputy leader Richard Tice, who also holds the party’s business brief, said he feared Capita could fold](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/27/13/2298453777..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

It has left some civil servants unable to get pension payments, or having to delay their retirement because they cannot get pension statements.

The government withheld nearly £10 million in payments to Capita for its poor performance over the Civil Service scheme, amid calls for the firm to be stripped of the contract.

SNP MP Lara Bird said one constituent in Arbroath and Broughty Ferry had been left with less than £100 to live on a month due to Capita’s pension failings.

Conservative chair of the Public Accounts Committee Sir Geoffrey Clifton-Brown was among those to call for the government to have a back-up plan.

He said: “If the worst really happens, and I know she doesn’t want to discuss it in this chamber this afternoon for understandable reasons, but she must have a proper contingency plan in place if, for some reason or another, Capita are not able to carry out the remainder of this contract.”

Labour MP (Sheffield South East) Clive Betts said: “We have to seriously look at the plan to take this scheme in-house because nobody believes, I think, in this chamber that in six months’ time we’ll be in any different position if Capita carry on with it.”

Ms Jameson replied to Sir Geoffrey: “It’s important that we are able to stabilise the situation and that Capita are able to continue delivering the pension scheme. That is really important.

“But I want to give him confidence that the government is working across government, as it would with all strategic suppliers, to make sure that we have the relevant contingency plans in place for this.”

Ms Jameson later told MPs that “severe breaches of contract or poor performance” could lead to “debarment”, so companies are blocked from being awarded public contracts under the Procurement Act 2023.

“I’m not in a position to confirm or deny whether a supplier is under active consideration for or subject to a debarment investigation,” the minister said.

“But I would just reiterate that it is an option that Government now has for poor performance to take into account for any strategic suppliers.”
