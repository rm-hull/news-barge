December is the month for *Glühwein*, Christmas markets and getting cosy as the days draw in. But this year it also brings changes and deadlines that could affect your wallet, your commute and even your holiday travel plans.**Key dates in December**

If you're dealing with German authorities, it's worth keeping an eye on the month's public holidays too, both official and unofficial.

This year, Christmas Day and New Year's Day fall on Fridays, while Boxing Day is a Saturday, so there's no bonus weekday off. But while the Christmas Eve on the 24th and New Year's Eve on the 31st – both Thursdays this year – aren't official public holidays, many offices will also be shut then as well.

At least one of Germany's immigration offices (*Ausländerbehörde*) is urging anyone whose residence permit expires over the holidays to get in touch well before December 23rd.

Whatever your own background or beliefs, there are a few dates are worth having in your diary this month as they come with traditional celebrations that will be see around Germany.

On the Christian calendar, the second, third and fourth Sundays of Advent fall on December 6th, 13th and 20th.

St Nicholas Day is on December 6th, Christmas Eve is on December 24th and Christmas Day is on the 25th. Notably almost all shops and businesses will be closed on Friday the 25th for Christmas Day.

Jewish families will be marking Hanukkah in December – the eight-day Festival of Lights – which begins on the evening of Friday, December 4th and runs until Saturday, December 12th.

Public menorah lightings take place in many cities, including at Berlin's Brandenburg Gate, where the central ceremony is planned for December 7th.

On Tuesday, December 8th, some Buddhist traditions observe Bodhi Day, which commemorates the day Siddhartha Gautama attained enlightenment under the Bodhi tree.

**Deutschlandticket price increase**

The monthly cost of the Deutschlandticket – Germany's all-in-one transport subscription pass – is set to go up from €63 to €66.80 at the start of next year. So passengers who currently have a subscription, and would like it to continue into the new year need to agree the new price and updated terms before then.

Deutsche Bahn has asked passengers to do so by November 30th, noting that those who fail to agree can expect their subscription to end on December 31st.

However, if you miss the deadline, you can still reactivate your ticket until December 31st.

Whatever you do, don't let your subscription lapse by accident. Travelling without a valid ticket could mean a fine of around €60.

Around 14.5 million people use the Deutschlandticket, and the price is now set to be adjusted each year according to a cost index.

**New Deutsche Bahn timetable**

Deutsche Bahn's new timetable takes effect on December 13th.

According to DB's chief executive, fares on long-distance routes won't rise before May 2027 and the new timetable is said to include more than 320 international trips a day, reaching more than 200 cities in 14 countries.

Faster Sprinter trains are also due to link Hamburg and Munich in about five hours 20 minutes, stopping only in Hanover and Nuremberg.

The downside is continued construction work. Passengers between Hanover and Berlin may be among the worst affected: from December 13th until December 2027, trains on this route will be diverted via Magdeburg, adding around 80 minutes to journey times.

Drivers have benefitted from a temporary cut in fuel taxes since October 1st, brought in after the Iran war pushed prices up. It takes 14.04 cents a litre off the tax on petrol and diesel, which works out at about 17 cents once VAT is included.

That cut ends on December 31st, which means that pump prices could potentially rise by up to 17 cents a litre on January 1st.

The government is currently planning to bring in a fuel price cap from January 1st, with the cabinet due to decide on the details of the draft law on December 2nd.

At the moment the cap would only limit how much stations can charge – which means it's unlikely to make up for the end of the tax cut.

**Keep eyes on you health insurance contributions**

The average additional contribution for public health insurance is set to remain at 2.9 percent for 2027.

But each fund sets its own rate, mostly in November and December. The board of one of the biggest funds – Techniker Krankenkasse (TK) – is due to decide on December 18th, for example.

If your fund raises its rate on January 1st, it has to tell you in writing beforehand – and you get a special right to switch. That means it could be worth checking your post in December and then comparing rates.

Generally speaking, people who are in Germany's statutory health insurance system should have until January 31st to change funds if they want to.

Even if you aren't required to file a tax return in Germany, you can still choose to submit one voluntarily up to four years later.

The deadline for the 2022 tax year falls on December 31st – and taxpayers who had substantial deductible expenses that year could be eligible for a refund.

There's also a separate deadline for people with investments.

If you have accounts at more than one bank and made a loss at one while making gains at another, you have until December 15th to ask the bank for a loss certificate. This would be needed in order to offset the loss through your tax return.
