## York business secures £1m funding to create 10 jobs and expand into new premises

Some of the new vehicles at Kridan Handling *(Image: Pic supplied)*

A York business is creating around 10 new jobs as part of a major expansion.

Kridan Handling, based in Elvington, has secured more than £1 million in funding from Lloyds to acquire neighbouring premises and invest in new electric forklift trucks.

The family-run company, which rents, sells, and services material handling equipment, says the funding will support long-term growth and create new employment opportunities.

Daniel Wilkinson, director at Kridan Handling, said: "Purchasing the neighbouring premises was an opportunity we couldn’t afford to miss.

"The acquisition gives us long-term security and the space to keep growing at the right pace."

Founded nearly 33 years ago by David and Anne Wilkinson, the company operates within a 100-mile radius of York and currently employs more than 20 people.

It manages a rental fleet of nearly 400 machines—more than double its size six years ago—and maintains around 1,700 customer-owned assets.

The expansion is supported by nearly £1 million in funding from Lloyds for the property purchase, along with £180,000 in asset finance for new equipment.

The new premises have provided additional space for future growth and enabled the business to lease several units to other companies.

Income from the units is being reinvested in improvements across the site, including roofing, electrics, heating, lighting, warehouse refurbishment, and extensive yard works.

The additional asset finance has allowed Kridan Handling to replace older diesel and gas-powered machines with new electric forklift trucks from manufacturers including Mitsubishi and Hyundai.

The company has already recruited four new employees since the expansion and plans to hire five more over the next 12 months, with senior engineering roles also under consideration.

Mr Wilkinson said: "Modernising our rental fleet is equally important.

"Customers increasingly want electric equipment, while replacing older machines means our engineers can spend more time supporting customers rather than maintaining our own assets.

"We’ve grown organically throughout our history and want to continue doing that safely and sustainably.

"Lloyds took the time to understand the business and proactively presented funding options we hadn’t previously been offered.

"Its support has given us the confidence to invest in our premises, fleet, and people."

Kridan Handling currently generates a turnover of around £3.2 million and expects this to grow by between 5 and 10 per cent over the next year.

Alexandra King, relationship manager at Lloyds, said: "Kridan Handling has built a successful family business by investing carefully in its people, equipment, and customer service.

"Acquiring the neighbouring premises gives the team the space and security it needs for its next phase of growth."
