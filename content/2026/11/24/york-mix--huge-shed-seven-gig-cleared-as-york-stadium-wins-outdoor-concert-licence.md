York’s LNER Community Stadium has been granted permission to host up to three major outdoor concerts a year.

It means the already announced gigs, including a Shed Seven homecoming concert, can now go ahead.

City of York Council’s licensing sub-committee approved the new premises licence for York Stadium Management Company yesterday, allowing open-air live music for crowds of up to 14,500 between 1 May and 30 June 2027.

During the hearing, stadium solicitor Richard Taylor revealed the bid was sparked when promoters Live Nation approached the venue about staging a Shed Seven homecoming show next summer.

However, Mr Taylor issued an apology to councillors after tickets were put on sale for both the Shed Seven gig and another one featuring Sir Tom Jones before the licence was granted.

“This application comes with an apology from the stadium for matters out of our control that these really shouldn’t have been announced now,” he told the panel.

“There is the unfortunate situation that the promoters have announced what is happening.

“Now we have not announced what is happening. We have asked them not to announce what is proposed.

“There are no contracts signed. I understand that if you try and buy the tickets, it does say in some very small print somewhere subject to licensing.”

Two future residents of the neighbouring Castra Park housing development objected, warning of intrusive bass noise, antisocial behaviour, and safety risks near an adjoining ancient monument.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/york-community-stadium-april-2026.webp)

Chair of the hearing Cllr Richard Watson responded to this by saying: “Amongst the evidence is the evidence of two objectors who basically object on all four of the grounds pertinent to licensing, but their objection is not substantiated by evidence.”

The panel noted that neither North Yorkshire Police nor council noise officers objected after the stadium agreed to submit a strict noise management plan and dropped earlier plans for outdoor boxing and wrestling.

Granting the licence, Cllr Watson said: “Our job is a balancing act and we conclude that three concerts per annum at a venue already used for major sporting events is not an unreasonable request.”
