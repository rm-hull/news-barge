# We need good connectivity in every postcode - no matter how rural: Ian Murray

## Access to high-quality, reliable and secure digital connectivity is not a luxury, but part of the internal fabric of everything that we do. It is as important as energy and water, it is how people stay in touch, how businesses grow and how the opportunities of the modern economy spread to every part of the country.

If an area does not have connectivity as good as we would expect anywhere in the country, it could be hampered in economic growth. The Prime Minister has challenged us all to deliver good growth in every postcode.

For that, we need good connectivity in every postcode, because it underpins what we need to do on our economy.

The impact on communities of poor mobile connectivity should not be underestimated.

![Ian Murray responded to a recent Westminster Hall debate](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOjE0MTM0Mjg0LTU2MTItNDQ0OS04NGFmLTQ5ZGIwZTg5YTk2NjozNDA1MjgwMy1hMmRiLTQ4ZjAtYjI2OS0zZWQzMTQ4MWY2ZDI=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


For most of us, staying connected to modern life starts with the phone in our pocket.

What matters to people is whether they can make calls for work, use data reliably, access services, stay connected to others and have that security blanket that we have heard about in relation to the emergency services and the emergency call buttons that elderly people use.

We have big ambitions on connectivity and are having to work very closely with the industry to make sure that they are delivered.

I think everyone in this debate, including the Government, is of the same view: we need to get this sorted, and as quickly as possible.

By 2030, we want people across the UK to see tangible improvements in connectivity in their everyday life, and we want economic growth and opportunity to be more widely shared.

As part of that, we have ambitions for high-quality stand-alone 5G connectivity everywhere by 2030 – in villages and rural communities, as well as towns and cities – and that must be delivered commercially, without Government subsidy.

All three mobile network operators have committed to significant investment plans that align with those ambitions.

We fully share the ambitions to push on as quickly as possible and to see those deadlines not as a target but as the backstop, with everything done much more quickly.

Although stand-alone 5G is already available outside 93 per cent of premises across the UK, we understand that we need to go much further and we continue to work with operators to support that.

We see this almost through a rural, topographical or digital inclusion lens, but it is everywhere in the country.

Whether we are in central Stirling, in central Edinburgh, which I represent, or in rural Wales or Northern Ireland, there will be notspots, so we have to make sure that there is coverage everywhere. It is about quality coverage and about having resilience in that coverage.

Connectivity is an issue for all of us. The Government’s ambition is clear: we want the whole country to be connected.

The Prime Minister has told Ministers and the Government that he wants growth in every postcode, and that can be delivered only through connectivity in every postcode. That is the Government’s ambition.
