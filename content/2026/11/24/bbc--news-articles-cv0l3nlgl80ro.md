**Andy Burnham has been accused of turning the UK into "a joke" on the world stage by Conservative leader Kemi Badenoch, amid a row about social media videos posted from an event in Downing Street.**

Social media influencer Max Balegde has deleted a video filmed outside the door of Number 10, which was posted on TikTok, in which he mimed along to explicit lyrics.

Badenoch said it was a "disgrace" that the prime minister allowed 10 Downing Street to be "degraded".

The prime minister's spokesperson said the content was clearly not acceptable and it was "right" that Balegde had taken down the social media posts.

Balegde's video appears to have been filmed earlier this month at an event for around 60 social media influencers hosted by Burnham, who is eager to use social media to reach younger voters. Balegde also gave a speech to those present at the event.

In the video, the influencer, whose real name is Max Trobe, mouths the words "bomboclaat pussywagon chicken nuggets", using audio from a meme that is trending online.

Bomboclaat is a Jamaican profanity referring to a menstrual cloth, while pussy is a slang term for female genitals.

It is unclear when Balegde's video was deleted but it no longer appears on his TikTok or Instagram accounts.

The creator has 3.6 million followers on TikTok and 640,000 on Instagram.

Videos which were filmed inside the Downing Street premises were recorded on government phones, the BBC can reveal.

Attendees at the event have confirmed they had to hand personal devices in and were given a phone and a camera to film with, with the footage they recorded sent to them after the event.

One of the distinctive features of Burnham's time in office has been his enthusiasm for engaging with social media influencers as a means to communicate with different groups of voters, especially younger voters.

It was an approach begun by Sir Keir Starmer, under whom a "new media unit" was formed, but has significantly intensified since July.

The engagement with influencers has elicited criticism of Burnham from those who fear it means reduced scrutiny and diminished opportunities to hold the prime minister to account.

Speaking at a press conference on Tuesday, Badenoch said she had been "pretty shocked" by the videos coming out of the event, questioning whether they were in good taste.

She said that while Burnham might think he was connecting with young people, many people around the world "will think our country is a joke".

She said: "I do think Andy Burnham should apologise for the way he is using Number 10 as his personal studio to carry out these silly TikToks.

"Vladimir Putin, President Xi and all sorts of other people who are hostile to the UK will see these things, they will get reports of them, and they will see that our prime minister is not a serious man and they will think we are not a serious country."

In a reference to Balegde's video, she said: "I think it is a disgrace that Number 10 – that door which is a symbol of British democracy, power and authority in the world, dignity, that's where we receive people like President Zelensky, showing them that they have the support of people in this country, is being degraded in this way."

Reform UK leader Nigel Farage also criticised the use of the Downing Street door as "puerile" and "demeaning" but said he wouldn't ban influencers from Number 10.

"I wouldn't let them behave quite the way that it happened," he said.

"It showed a slight lack of respect for the place and for the office and was really very inappropriate indeed."

Responding to questions about Balegde's video, the prime minister's spokesperson said "the content was clearly not acceptable", but added "content creators are responsible for what they choose to post".

Asked repeatedly whether Number 10 had requested for the video be removed, the spokesperson said: "It is right the creator took down that content.

"It's clearly not acceptable and that is reflected by the fact that it has been taken down."

They also defended the decision to invite influencers to Downing Street, saying it was "right that the PM listens to" social media content creators who "reach millions of people who don't get their news from traditional media".

Balegde also gave a speech inside the influencers' event, which was held by Burnham in an attempt to build links with a growing sector of the economy.

A smaller group of creators were asked to arrive early to film videos with the prime minister.

One attendee in this group said they had to run ideas past the prime minister's team for approval.

A government spokesperson said they would not comment on security and whether the footage captured on the government phones was reviewed, but said it was "not abnormal" for attendees of Downing Street to use government phones.
