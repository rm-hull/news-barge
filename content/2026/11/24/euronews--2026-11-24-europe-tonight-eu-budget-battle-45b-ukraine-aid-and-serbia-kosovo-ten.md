## Europe Tonight unpacks the fierce clash in Brussels over the EU’s next seven-year budget and a proposed €45 billion loan package for Ukraine, examines Serbia-Kosovo diplomatic tensions ahead of the Western Balkans summit with former Kosovo President Vjosa Osmani, and features an exclusive interview

### **Brussels clashes over seven-year spending and agriculture**

A major showdown is underway over the European Union’s next seven-year budget, pitting the European Parliament against EU member states while deeply dividing national capitals over proposed spending cuts, digital levies, and agricultural subsidies. As negotiations intensify, governments remain locked in a tug-of-war over how to balance traditional farm aid with new strategic priorities.

### **The €45 billion fault line: Financing Ukraine’s future**

Perhaps the most contentious sticking point in the EU’s financial planning is securing long-term support for Kyiv. The European Commission has proposed an additional €45 billion package to help cover Ukraine’s military and civilian needs, but growing frustration and resistance from several member states threaten to derail consensus; prompting sharp warnings from Berlin over potential budget vetoes.

Tonight, our Ukraine Correspondent Sasha Vakulina speaks with Ukraine’s former Minister of Economic Development to assess what is at stake for Kyiv’s economy and defense.

### **Diplomatic friction ahead of the Western Balkans Summit**

Ahead of the upcoming EU-Western Balkans summit, European officials are voicing growing concern over ongoing diplomatic controversies between Serbia and Kosovo, following recent disputes over regional representation and Brussels' enlargement strategy.

We will speak live with former President of Kosovo Vjosa Osmani about the state of the Belgrade-Pristina dialogue and Kosovo's path toward European integration.

### **Exclusive: Marina Litvinenko reflects 20 years after London poisoning**

Twenty years after her husband Alexander Litvinenko was fatally poisoned with radioactive polonium in London (in what became one of the most notorious Russian intelligence operations on foreign soil) Marina Litvinenko has spoken exclusively to Euronews.

Coinciding with the release of her new memoir, she reflects on her two-decade fight for accountability and what her husband's murder warned the West about the Kremlin.
