Iranian singer Parastoo Ahmadi has been flogged for performing without a hijab during a live-streamed YouTube concert in 2024.

Ahmadi and four members of her production team were each given 74 lashes in the city of Qom and handed a two-year ban on artistic activities and travel.

They were convicted in June and sentenced for "offending public decency by producing and publishing obscene and immoral content online", an Iranian news website reported on Monday.

Their appeal was rejected by a Qom court in September.

Ahmadi, 29, clad in a black sleeveless dress and sporting a necklace in the shape of the map of Iran, wore her long hair loose over her shoulders while performing with four male members of her band.

They were detained in December 2024 following their performance of “*Az Khoone Javanane Catam”*, a patriotic song meaning “From the Blood of the Youth of the homeland”.

The video of the performance racked up millions of views on YouTube.

Iranian laws mandate women to wear modest clothes, including a hijab, and ban public performances by women.

The stringent hijab rules were a flashpoint during protests in 2022 after the death in custody ‌of Kurdish woman Mahsa Amini, who ​was arrested for allegedly violating the dress ‌code.

Western governments ‌and human rights groups have ‌criticised flogging as torture and a violation of international law. Iran has rejected accusations over its human rights record, saying ⁠Western nations should focus on their own human rights problems.

![](https://static.independent.co.uk/2024/12/16/07/Screenshot-%28483%29.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Ms Ahmadi and her male co-defendants were flogged at the Criminal Sentence Enforcement Office in Qom, one of their lawyers told the BBC.

"We did everything in our power to have the verdict overturned, or at least to secure a reduction in the sentence or a suspension, but it didn't work out,” he said.

"The other lawyers and I even tried to get the enforcement of the sentence postponed, but the enforcement branch insisted on carrying it out immediately."

Mahmood Amiry-Moghaddam, director of Iran Human Rights, condemned the flogging and pointed at the government’s failure to address the country’s economic and infrastructure crises.

“She sang, the Islamic Republic responded with the lash,” he said.

“A regime that cannot solve the crises of livelihoods, water and electricity, yet doesn’t hesitate to flog a woman for her voice, has reached the end of its life.”

US-based journalist Masih Alinejad also slammed the Iranian government. “This regime is so afraid of a woman singing,” she said. “And where are the Iranian male singers and musicians?"

"Women in Iran and Afghanistan are flogged for exercising their most basic rights. This is what it looks like to live under Islamic regimes.”
