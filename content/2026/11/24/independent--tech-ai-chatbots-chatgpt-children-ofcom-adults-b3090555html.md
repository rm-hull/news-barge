Surveys for the online safety regulator show that around four in five children and nearly two-thirds of adults have used chatbots in recent months.

Ofcom warned that these digital tools are becoming increasingly common and "can pose risks".

A survey of 2,004 UK children and their parents found 81 per cent of young people aged eight to 17 reported using an artificial intelligence (AI) chatbot in the past six months.

Of those, more than a third (35 per cent) said they use them daily, with 82 per cent using them at least weekly.

Just over half (54 per cent) ask questions and explore interests via chatbots, while 53 per cent use them for schoolwork or homework.

Just over a third of teenagers aged 13-17 said they had engaged in roleplay with a chatbot, including telling it to act as a person and make decisions.

Meanwhile, 64 per cent of the 2,081 adults surveyed in Great Britain said they had used chatbots recently.

![A survey of 2,004 UK children and their parents found 81 per cent of children aged between eight and 17 told how they had used an AI (artificial intelligence) chatbot in the past six months.](https://static.the-independent.com/2026/10/15/19/OPENAI_DEMANDA_36251.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Around one in 10 (12 per cent) said they had interacted with AI-generated characters, and 8 per cent said they had used them for mental health or wellbeing-related purposes.

A quarter used them for medical advice while 12 per cent sought personal advice, such as relationship help.

Of adult users who had interacted with an AI character, half said they felt emotionally connected to them and one in seven said they sometimes forgot it was an AI and not a real person.

The social media ban for under-16s, which is due to come into force in spring, is not expected to include an outright ban on AI chatbots.

But the government has previously said it will consider banning particular chatbots if they are deemed to provide dangerous, misleading or unverified mental health advice as these could “pose a serious threat to children’s safety and wellbeing”.

The government said it recognises that AI ”can bring benefits to children” and is focused on ensuring youngsters “can benefit from these technologies safely”.

![Just over a third of teenagers aged 13-17 said they had engaged in roleplay with a chatbot, which would include instructing it to act as a person and make decisions](https://static.the-independent.com/2026/11/24/11/01KF0KQTFN5T8XMAA15GAFWH4Q.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Andrew Breeze, Ofcom’s director of tech policy, said: “AI chatbots are becoming an increasingly common part of people’s digital lives.

“While these tools can provide positive experiences for users, they can pose risks.

“It’s crucial that we understand how and where children and adults are using these services so that we can ensure everyone can benefit from them safely and the appropriate safeguards are in place.”

Ofcom, which commissioned the research to be carried out in August and September, said it will continue to examine how adults and children in the UK are using AI companions and chatbots, focusing on both the risks and the benefits.
