## Dogs and York Christmas Market don't mix

Thousands of people visit York Christmas Market every day (except Tuesday when it's closed).

But, alarmingly, a lot of visitors choose to bring their dogs with them into town.

I'm sorry but dogs and crowds of people simply don't mix in the confined market and narrow streets of York and should be discouraged before someone or something is injured.

I realise that some visitors to the city have no options but to have their animals with them.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21753272.jpg?type=mds-article-620)York Christmas Market. *(Image: Newsquest)*

But surely the husbands or partners would be happier walking in the Museum Gardens or down by the riverside than tethered outside the shops (I mean the husbands), rather than out exercising with the dog.

Recently I've heard of several dog-on-dog attacks, usually between male animals but occasionally by female dogs.

It's something you rarely heard of but it's started to become more common nowadays. I wonder why?

But perhaps it's time for all dogs, irrespective of size, breed or temperament, to be forced to wear a muzzle if running off the lead.

## Why bring back the two-child benefit limit?

I was pleased to read the article on page 13 of The Press (Saturday November 21) that stated that 2,200 children now living in North Yorkshire have been lifted out of poverty since the present government scrapped the two-child benefit limit.

Now the Tories have declared that one of the first things they would do if elected to power would be to reinstate this limit.

So you are still the "Nasty Party" Ms Badenoch!

**John TaylorLycett RoadYork**

## Save our high streets and give young people a chance

In Saturday's paper on page 2, Rachael Maskell MP welcomed Alan Millburn's report aimed at identifying ways to get almost a million young people not in education, employment or training (NEETs) into work and off benefits and goes on to say, "... this is going to need a whole city effort."

However, turn to page 5 and there's an article about a shopkeeper's business rates that have "more than tripled" and you suddenly start to think that it's all well and good having these positive goals but, what's the point when grasping landlords and councils seem to see only the short-term benefits of lining their own pockets while they can, and not the longer term realities of these NEETs having nowhere to work because, for example, there is no longer a high street full of shops to get started on the employment ladder.

The high street started its death knell when home deliveries took over.

I find it ironic that a number of people I have spoken to about buying online have "green" credentials but wouldn't dream of walking into town to do a bit of shopping because it's easier to sit on your backside and wait for someone to bring stuff to you!
