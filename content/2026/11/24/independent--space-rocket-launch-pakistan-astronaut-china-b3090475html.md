China has announced the crew for the upcoming space launch to its Tiangong space station, including a Pakistani astronaut, who will be the first foreigner to board the orbiting outpost.

Muhammad Zeeshan Ali, who served as a pilot in the Pakistan Air Force, will board China’s Shenzhou 24 spacecraft powered by the Long March 2F rocket along with Chinese colleagues Tang Hongbo and Wu Yi, the China Manned Space Agency (CMSA) announced on Tuesday.

The trio is scheduled to launch from the Jiuquan Satellite Launch Centre in northwest China at 8.18am local time on Wednesday, the agency said.

Shenzhou-24 will be China's 18th crewed launch and marks the first joint space flight mission conducted by Chinese and foreign astronauts in the history of Beijing’s crewed space mission programme.

![A staff member looks on as journalists film a site near the Shenzhou 24 spacecraft](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/24/09/download.-1..jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Mr Ali, 40, would spend about a week aboard the Chinese space station as a payload specialist and return to Earth aboard the Shenzhou 23 spacecraft, along with crew members Zhang Zhiyuan and Lai Ka-ying, returning after their six-month rotation.

“I’m not flying for myself; I carry the name of Pakistan on my shoulder, the hope of 250 million Pakistanis,” he told reporters at a press briefing on Tuesday.

The Pakistani astronaut hoped space cooperation between the two Asian countries would go from one astronaut in one flight to multiple joint missions to the Moon, Mars and beyond.

Mr Ali has trained for seven months since he was selected in April, including learning Mandarin for better communication with his crewmates.

“The labels and operating instructions aboard the spacecraft and space station are all in Chinese, and the Pakistani astronaut began training with virtually no foundation in the language," Mr Hongbo said.

![Pakistan’s astronaut Muhammad Zeeshan Ali speaks during a meeting with the press at the Jiuquan Satellite Launch Center in Jiuquan, northwest China, Tuesday](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/24/09/download.-2..jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

On board the space station, Mr Ali would conduct scientific projects in the fields of biotechnology and materials, as well as an in-orbit experiment on photosynthetic microorganisms, Chinese state broadcaster CCTV reported.

“To every child who’s watching while I’m in orbit, look up at the sky and remember that I am up there with your dreams,” he said.

All facilities and equipment are in good condition for Wednesday’s launch, CMSA said.

![Chinese astronauts Tang Hongbo, Wu Yi and Pakistani astronaut Muhammad Zeeshan Ali gesture as they attend a press conference before the Shenzhou-24 spaceflight mission](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/24/09/2026-11-24T040214Z_1286380670_RC23UOAB1PY8_RTRMADP_3_SPACE-EXPLORATION-CHINA-SHENZHOU.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The three-module Tiangong space station was completed in late 2022, and ever since, rotating six-month-long missions have kept the orbiting outpost constantly crewed ever since.

China hopes. to double the size of the space station in coming years, and facilitate more joint missions with crews from other countries.

This comes as the US and China are locked in a space race to the Moon, with both countries aiming to land boots on the lunar surface by 2030.
