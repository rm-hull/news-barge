A Pennsylvania teen who died from a brain tumor could become the first Gen-Z saint.

​Maria Middleton died aged 18 in October 2020, around two years after receiving her diagnosis in her sophomore year of high school. The United States Conference of Catholic Bishops says visitors have flocked to her grave and there have even been reports of “physical blessings and healings received through her intercession.”

​On November 17, the USCCB announced that its bishops had voted in a plenary assembly to advance the beatification and canonization process for Middleton.

Her parents told ABC News their daughter shared the same interests as many girls her age, including Taylor Swift, making jokes, pets and dancing. She also had an “extraordinary” outlook on faith, her father Brian said.

​“Our favorite Maria quote: ‘You don’t have to be weird to be holy,’” he told the outlet. “She was in every way normal, but she had this extraordinary faith and pursuit of virtue.”

![Maria Middleton, a teenager from Pennsylvania, (pictured) could become the first Gen-Z saint](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/24/8/30/Maria-Middleton.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

​Born in September 2002, Middleton took part in charity work from a young age, organizing her school and parish’s annual coat drive in elementary school. Information from the Archdiocese of Philadelphia shared with the USCCB stated that Middleton created an annual fundraiser to support children with cancer even before her own diagnosis.

​The teenager was diagnosed in her sophomore year of high school and began experiencing a loss of balance, headaches, blurred vision and a decline in her fine motor skills, the USCCB says.

​“It was devastating. It was a nightmare. It was a no-way-out situation,” Susan Middleton, the girl’s mother, told ABC News.

​Despite her diagnosis, Middleton traveled to Uganda on a mission trip and continued raising money for a school there upon returning to the U.S.

​Middleton described her illness as her life’s purpose in her journal, with the final lines of her diary reading: “Think hope.”

![Carlos Acutis was canonized as the first Millennial saint in 2025](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/24/08/2205837358..?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&auto=webp)

​The vote by the USCCB is just the first step in a lengthy process to become a saint, which also needs the support of the Vatican. That precedes an examination of her writing and proof that two miracles can be attributed to her.

​Currently, there are no canonized Gen-Z saints.

​Carlo Acutis was canonized as the first Millennial saint in 2025, following his death in 2006 from leukemia.

​Two miracles by Acutis, who died at the age of 15, were recognized by Pope Francis, with Pope Leo XIV presiding over the canonization ceremony in September last year.
