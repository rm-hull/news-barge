A leading Democratic lawmaker in the House has expanded an inquiry into 1789 Capital, the venture capital firm tied to Donald Trump Jr., requesting documentation from four companies that secured funding shortly before benefiting from lucrative federal contracts or policies.

On Monday night, Rep. Jamie Raskin—who serves as ranking member and incoming chair of the House Judiciary Committee—sent letters to defense firm Hadrian Automation, e-cigarette company Juul Labs, prediction market venue Polymarket, and online gun vendor GrabAGun.

The Maryland congressman ordered all four businesses to hand over two years of records detailing interactions with 1789 Capital, along with specific corporate files. Raskin said the records are necessary to examine if U.S. President Donald Trump’s oldest son used insider information to orchestrate or anticipate government actions that boosted the companies and his own finances.

Both Trump Jr. and Palm Beach, Florida-based 1789 Capital have strongly denied committing any wrongdoing.

![Both Trump Jr. and 1789 Capital, which is based in Palm Beach, Florida, deny any wrongdoing](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/24/16/2294060524..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The requests intensify a probe that Raskin initiated in August into the firm, which he alleged "suddenly struck investment gold" following its 2024 addition of the president’s son as a partner. Set to lead Democratic oversight efforts when Congress shifts leadership in January, Raskin identified exposing financial misconduct and bribery as primary objectives.

## Don Jr. addresses corruption fears

Trump Jr. has consistently dismissed allegations that he capitalized on political influence for financial gain. Speaking at an Axios conference last week, he recognized public anxieties about corruption but asserted they were unfounded, pointing out his multi-decade record as an investor.

"I can see what they are saying. It doesn’t make it so," he said. "The notion that I should somehow sit in a corner, suck my thumb and wait, you know, eight years — in this case 12 years — till we’re out of the political game, it’s not real."

1789 Capital has likewise rejected allegations of improper conduct. In a September letter sent to Raskin, legal counsel for the firm argued its investment strategy existed prior to Trump’s administration, adding that while the company "welcomes legitimate congressional oversight," it "has no intention of being held hostage to political gamesmanship."

Bill Burck, an attorney representing the investment firm, noted Monday that the prior correspondence addressed misconceptions in Raskin’s claims, but the lawmaker "never bothered to reply."

"Facts are facts and Congressman Raskin has them wrong here, but persists in his tall tales against 1789," Burck stated. "These are personal attacks untethered to actual evidence."

## Don Jr. named a partner

Established in 2022, 1789 Capital formally named Trump Jr. as a partner in November 2024.

In correspondence sent to the four firms and obtained by *The Associated Press,* Raskin detailed how government policy or procurement decisions helped their operations, contending that 1789 Capital exhibited suspiciously "serendipitous timing" with its financial backing.

According to Raskin, Hadrian Automation secured almost $1 billion in U.S. Army and Navy contracts in March—marking its initial federal deals—following 1789 Capital joining as an investor in 2025.

Regarding Juul, the lawmaker pointed out that the Food and Drug Administration approved the company to keep selling its e-cigarettes in July 2025, four months after 1789 Capital acquired an equity position.

![The letters ramp up an investigation that Rep. Jamie Raskin (D-MD) opened in August](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/24/16/2304031307..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The congressman further highlighted that GrabAGun utilized assistance from 1789 to complete a public listing and appointed Trump Jr. to its board prior to the Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco, Firearms and Explosives drafting a rule simplifying direct firearm deliveries to residences.

Raskin additionally cited concerns regarding the Justice Department and the Commodity Futures Trading Commission ending probes into Polymarket in July 2025, occurring after 1789 Capital had already backed the trading venue.

In a released statement, GrabAGun maintained that Raskin’s letter "is a political document, and its allegations are baseless."

"ATF’s review of its regulations was well known across the firearms industry, and the agency published numerous proposed rules for public comment. GrabAGun’s only input came through public comments on several of those proposals," the response stated.

Juul Labs indicated it was carefully evaluating the inquiry.

"As always, we intend to cooperate with any Congressional effort to understand our company, our commitment to combating underage vaping, and our mission to help adult smokers of combustible cigarettes transition completely away from smoking," the firm stated in an email.

The remaining companies targeted in the inquiry did not immediately respond to requests for comment.

The Trump administration has moved to counter Democratic oversight efforts, issuing an executive order on Friday instructing government contractors and subcontractors to confer with executive officials regarding congressional requests and refrain from disclosing materials deemed privileged by the White House.

This directive could hinder response efforts for at least one entity involved, Hadrian Automation, owing to its status as a federal contractor.

Raskin characterized the presidential order as "blatantly unlawful," emphasizing that defense and public contractors carry a "duty and a right to speak truthfully when Congress calls."
