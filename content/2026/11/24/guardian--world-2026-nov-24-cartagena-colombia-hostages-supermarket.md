Armed and masked men have been holding people hostage in a supermarket since early Tuesday morning in Cartagena, one of Colombia’s most popular tourist cities.

The country’s president, Abelardo de la Espriella, said 30 people had initially been taken hostage, but five had been released, and hours later local media reported that another 10 had also been freed.

There have been no reports of injuries so far.

The far-right president added that, despite the context of growing violence perpetrated by criminal groups involved in the country’s decades-long armed conflict, the hostage-taking appeared to have stemmed from a botched robbery carried out by “common criminals”.

The past few days have been particularly challenging in the South American country, with a car bomb and a drone attack killing one person and leaving nearly 30 injured.

The supermarket where the hostages are being held is just six miles from the old city and its 500-year-old fortress walls overlooking the Caribbean. Hundreds of thousands of visitors are drawn to Cartagena every year.

The gunmen reportedly arrived at about 8.30am local time at an Éxito supermarket in La Castellana neighbourhood and, according to Colombia’s president, the group planned to rob an armoured cash-in-transit vehicle that was due to collect the previous day’s takings from the store.

After spotting the movement on security cameras, employees reportedly alerted the national police, who surrounded the supermarket.

“My son is there,” Jacob Perez, the father of one of the workers reportedly held hostage, told Agence France-Presse. “He didn’t leave work and he’s not answering his phone. I think they took it from him,” he said.

Customers were also believed to be among the hostages. The five people initially released were “older women”, according to De la Espriella. Caracol Radio later reported that another 10 had been freed.

Footage shared on social media showed dozens of people rushing out of the supermarket, while local media outlets showed snipers positioned on nearby buildings in what appeared to be a standoff between the armed men and police.

De la Espriella, known for his populist rhetoric and an “iron fist” approach to crime – he sometimes wears a gun on his waist and has posed beside the bodies of people killed by police – has been in office for just over three months, and said the hours-long standoff at the supermarket “will turn out well for the hostages, but I’m not so sure it will turn out quite so well for the hostage-takers”.

He addressed the case at a press conference in Villavicencio, about 684 miles inland, where he was dealing with the aftermath of an attack on Monday night in which a car bomb was detonated outside the headquarters of the Meta departmental government.

More than 20 people were injured and a security guard was killed, and the president attributed the attack to a dissident faction of the Revolutionary Armed Forces of Colombia (Farc).

“Narco-terrorism is desperate because we are confronting it decisively. It intends to intimidate us and force us to retreat in search of another false peace process,” the president said, referring to the landmark 2016 peace deal between the government and the Farc – whose signing marked its 10th anniversary on Tuesday.

Internationally recognised for substantially reducing violence, the deal alone could not bring the decades-long armed conflict to a definitive end. Subsequent administrations were slow to implement the agreement, which was rejected by Farc dissidents and other rebel factions.

In the early hours of Monday, in another part of the country, gunmen believed to belong to the ELN, an armed group that never signed a peace deal, used drones to drop bombs on the local police command in the city of Cúcuta. Instead they hit homes and injured three civilians, including an 18-month-old baby.

Although drone attacks by criminal groups have become increasingly common, it was the first time they had targeted a departmental capital.
