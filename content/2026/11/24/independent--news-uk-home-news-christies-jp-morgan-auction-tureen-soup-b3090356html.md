A silver soup tureen made in 18th-century Paris could sell for as much as £6million, auction house Christie's said.

The Meissonnier Tureen, once owned by the financier J.P. Morgan, goes under the hammer in London on Dec. 1.

It is the star lot of the sale.

The piece measures more than a foot and a half (46 centimeters) long and weighs 44 pounds (20 kilograms).

Juste-Aurèle Meissonnier, a French artist and goldsmith, made the tureen in the 1730s in a flamboyant rococo style.

(AP Photo/Kirsty Wigglesworth)](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/23/13/Britain_Auction_91511.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Christie's specialist Isabelle Cartier-Stone said it was “designed to astonish,”

The tureen is shaped like a stylized shell.

Its cover is encrusted with detailed silver plants and animals, including “a duck, a crab, a fish, an oyster, and a snail among an artichoke and other leafy vegetables,” according to the catalog.

Its past owners have included British aristocrats, Russian statesmen and the American banker John Pierpont Morgan.

It is one of a pair that was split up after being auctioned together in 1977.

The other tureen is in the Cleveland Museum of Art in Ohio.

![A pair of Louis Xv silver wine-coolers on display estimated between GBP 800,000 - GBP 1,200,000 at Christie's Auction House on November 23, 2026 in London, England](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/24/06/2306017535..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The Blaquier-Arrieta family, wealthy Argentine collectors, are selling the tureen as part of a trove of silver.

Its presale estimate is between 4 million pounds and 6 million pounds ($5.25 million and $7.86 million).

The same family sold a group of a dozen paintings at Sotheby's last week for $412 million, including a Vincent van Gogh work, “Châtaigniers en fleurs” (Chestnut Trees in Flower), which fetched $190.4 million.
