# Grandad, 77, who 'hated' gyms eyes elite weightlifting months after new hip

## The 77-year-old is back in the gym just months after surgery

An Otley grandad is refusing to let age or major surgery stand in the way of his ambitions.

Jan Osinski, 77, is back lifting weights just seven months after a hip replacement, and he now has his sights set on an ambitious new target.

The grandfather-of-two is hoping to lift 136kg, a weight he believes would put him in the elite category for his age group.

Before his operation he was already lifting an impressive 120kg, but when severe pain forced him to stop training, he feared he might never return to the gym.

Now, after surgery at Nuffield Health Leeds Hospital and months of rehabilitation, he is back doing what he loves and has no intention of slowing down.

Jan’s journey into weightlifting began later in life and he admits he was initially far from convinced about joining a gym. But what started as an attempt to improve his health quickly became one of the biggest passions of his life.

Speaking about his latest challenge, Jan said his biggest concern before undergoing surgery was whether he would ever be able to lift weights again.

Jan’s problems with his hip date back 42 years to a serious motorcycle accident which left him with a shattered pelvis, two broken vertebrae, three broken ribs and a displaced femur.

At the time, he was warned he would probably need a hip replacement within 15 to 20 years. However, he managed to carry on for more than four decades before the damage finally caught up with him.

Jan said: "I managed to get 42 years out of it, so I don’t think I did too badly."

But earlier this year, Jan began experiencing severe groin pain which became so debilitating that he could no longer continue his regular weightlifting sessions.

An X-ray revealed severe degeneration of his hip joint, and he was referred for a hip replacement at Nuffield Health Leeds Hospital as an NHS patient. He underwent the operation in April, but his main concern was not the surgery itself. It was whether he would ever be able to lift weights again.

He said: "The first question I asked the surgeon was whether I’d be able to go back to weightlifting. He asked how many times a week I trained and said there was no problem. He even told me he wanted to see videos of me weightlifting afterwards!"

Jan was discharged just 24 hours after his operation and began working towards getting back on his feet with support from his wife and a physiotherapy programme at Nuffield Health.

Within two months he was walking without crutches, and after three and a half months he was back in the gym. His determination is all the more remarkable given that he only discovered weightlifting later in life, and he admits he was initially far from convinced about joining a gym.

The accident hasn’t been Jan’s only setback. Around 20 years ago, he suffered a heart attack, and six years ago he decided it was time to take his health and fitness more seriously.

He said: "I was seizing up, and the effects of the motorcycle accident were catching up with me. I hated the idea of going to a gym. I really did."

That changed after he was introduced to personal trainer Mark, who helped him discover a passion for exercise. Starting with weights of around 40kg, Jan gradually built up his strength and confidence until he was eventually lifting three times that amount.

What started as an attempt to improve his health quickly became one of the biggest passions of his life, and he says it has improved his mental health as well as his physical health.

He said: "It’s one of the best things I’ve ever done. It makes me feel better. I get this sense of self-worth and wellbeing from it."

Alongside his 136kg target, Jan has another goal that is hopefully a little easier to achieve. He wants to build up enough strength and stamina to walk from his home into Otley again, a journey of around a mile and a half.

For now, he is taking his recovery steadily, listening to his body and gradually building up his strength as he works towards his next milestone.

After everything he has been through, including a serious motorcycle accident, a heart attack and major hip surgery, the 77-year-old is proof that it is never too late to discover a new passion or set yourself another challenge.
