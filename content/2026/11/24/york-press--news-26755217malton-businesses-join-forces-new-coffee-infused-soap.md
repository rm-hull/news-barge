## North Yorkshire town finds new use for spent coffee grounds

Sue Jefferson from Circular Malton & Norton holding a bar of the soap *(Image: Circular Malton & Norton)*

Three independent businesses in a North Yorkshire town have joined forces to brew up a new way to use spent coffee grounds.

Circular Malton & Norton, Leoni Coffee House and Cosy Cottage, all based in Malton, are working together to transform an everyday waste product into a new coffee-infused all natural soap.

Leoni Coffee House supplies the spent coffee grounds from its everyday coffee-making, which are collected and given a new purpose as an ingredient in Cosy Cottage’s natural coffee-infused soap.

Inspired by ReYorkshire, an initiative championing reuse across the region, Circular Malton & Norton helped bring the collaboration to life, showing how local businesses can work together to turn waste into something useful and desirable.

Sue Jefferson from Circular Malton & Norton said every day, 103 million cups of coffee are drunk across the UK.

‘‘That’s a whole lot of caffeine and a lot of spent coffee grounds. But what happens to all those grounds once you’ve enjoyed your morning coffee? More often than not, they end up in the bin.

‘‘This project is about so much more than making soap. It’s about bringing local businesses together, finding clever ways to make use of what would otherwise go to waste and keeping value within our local economy. Most importantly, we’ve created a really lovely product that people will enjoy buying and using. It’s a great example of what can happen when we work together and think a little differently.’’

There’s even a little coffee-inspired twist. The team invited people to try the soaps and choose their favourite packaging, and discovered that those who like their coffee strong and black tended to favour the dark packaging, while milky coffee drinkers preferred the lighter option.

Sue added: ‘‘The 130g bars cost £7.95 and make a lovely Christmas gift. Whether it’s a Secret Santa surprise, a stocking filler or a treat for a coffee lover, they’re also a great way to shop local and support independent Malton businesses this Christmas.

And there could be more to come. As the project takes off and 5,000 bars are sold, it could help create new jobs, support the development of the product range and inspire even more innovative ways to reuse everyday waste.’’

Clara Challoner-Walker, founder of Cosy Cottage Soaps, said: ‘‘Coffee is well known for its exfoliating properties. We are delighted to bring our soap making expertise to create this highly appealing all natural, coffee infused soap, all made in Malton.’’

Dom Nesbitt, owner of Leoni Coffee House, said: ‘‘In my opinion, spent coffee grounds make the best soap. They scrub away the day, smell like your morning brew and they prove that even after you’ve given everything to creating one cup, you’ve still got something left to give.’’

ReYorkshire is supported by David Skaith, Mayor of York and North Yorkshire, with funding from the York and North Yorkshire Combined Authority’s Carbon Negative Challenge Fund.

Gilli Hobbs, ReYorkshire Lead, said: ‘‘Businesses and communities in the region have embraced ReYorkshire. This is the first of our pilots bringing to life how reuse ideas can be a smart part of the economy.’’

The coffee-infused soap will be available from Leoni Coffee House, Cosy Cottage and Circular Malton & Norton from Saturday, November 28.
