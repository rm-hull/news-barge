Jensen Huang made a triumphant return to Nvidia's California headquarters on Monday, less than two weeks after he resigned as CEO following revelations of extramarital affairs, including one with an alleged spy for China.

Insiders have revealed that the return was the result of two weeks of desperate lobbying by a small group of loyal allies, who worked to convince the Trump administration that Huang was a vital to the $4 trillion company’s long-term prosperity.

The firm, which had been thrown into chaos by Huang’s departure, meanwhile fought to keep employees on side, bringing forward pay rises and blaring Tom Petty’s “I Won’t Back Down” to anxious staff bracing through the turbulence.

It was US president Donald Trump that finally broke the tension this week, announcing the return of the “Great Patriot and National Treasure” Huang after piling pressure onto the board of executives to “exonerate” him.

![In a lengthy post on Truth Social, Trump said the board had made the “correct and proper decision to return Jensen Huang to the position of CEO”.](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/24/08/2026-11-23T170750Z_603820255_RC2TTOAGX1RZ_RTRMADP_3_NVIDIA-CEO-TRUMP.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The 63-year-old Huang, clad in his trademark black leather jacket, was welcomed back to the fore by doting employees in California as he apologised: “I’ve made some very terrible mistakes, and I’m going to work on coming back.”

Nvidia shares surged 31.5% as the market opened on Monday, adding around $950bn to the company’s value and reportedly making Trump up to $15m (£11.5m) on his holdings.

## Huang’s ‘war room’ in Silicon Valley

Huang’s surprise return to the role capped a turbulent fortnight for the world’s second largest company.

People familiar with the backroom conversations told the *Financial Times* that Trump and his vice president JD Vance were both personally involved in the rescue mission, urged on by a ‘war room’ of Huang’s allies in Silicon Valley.

Supporters were said to include Uber founder Travis Kalanick, Goldman Sachs executive John Underwood, and venture capitalists Gavin Baker, Brad Gerstner and Marc Andreessen.

They argued that Huang, who founded Nvidia in 1993, was vital to the success of the company, which in turn is essential to the health of America’s wider AI industry that has powered the US stock market.

Huang announced he would step down on Thursday, 12 November following the bombshell revelations that he had had an affair with Alice Huang, a businesswoman accused of being a Chinese spy.

![Jensen Huang greets Chinese businesswoman Alice Huang after delivering his keynote speech at COMPUTEX 2024 in Taipei, Taiwan, June 2, 2024](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/16/8/13/tag_reuters-com-2024_newsml_RC2138AXSI4C_141698297-reuters.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

US intelligence officials believe Alice Huang, the former boss of Singaporean cloud computing firm Megaspeed, was funnelling information to China as she courted Nvidia’s CEO.

Authorities had been investigating Megaspeed since late 2025 over suspicions that it was helping to divert advanced AI chips to Beijing.

And earlier this month, Nvidia’s own board launched a probe into whether Jensen had been targeted by a foreign intelligence operation. It expressed confidence in him at the time, but reports of the alleged affair with Alice piled on pressure.

Huang finally gave in to the pressure but allies were quick to rally around him, making the case in public and in private for him to be reinstated, insiders told the FT.

![Jensen Huang reacts as employees applaud his return to office on Monday](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/24/08/2026-11-24T063636Z_1120649052_RC26UOAXL3SX_RTRMADP_3_NVIDIA-CEO.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

## Nvidia executives ‘tried to boost morale’

The top executive team met last Wednesday, 18 November for an ‘all hands’ meeting to boost morale and address concerns as Nvidia’s market value lost around $500bn.

The firm unveiled plans to bring forward pay increases to December, instead of the spring, and raised spirits by playing Tom Petty’s “I Won’t Back Down” as employees waved torches on their phones, the newspaper reported.

Those close to Huang meanwhile began lobbying their contacts in Washington, insisting on his importance to the firm, reports said.

Huang is known for his hands-on management style and overseeing some 60 direct reports, nearly all in engineering roles, including experts in memory chips, networking and graphics and central processors.

But critics have suggested such an approach left Nvidia with a dysfunctional management structure, that made it more difficult for the firm to function without Huang at its core.

![Huang is one of the technology executives who is closest to Trump and. other members of his administration (pictured in September)](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/23/19/2026-11-23T151536Z_862976933_RC27TNAMYK5I_RTRMADP_3_NVIDIA-CEO-TRUMP.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

He has dug deep into the startups Nvidia has invested in, helping them secure customers and has even joined fundraising calls to attract investors, according to startup founders, venture capitalists and a former Nvidia employee.

Behind the scenes, an ally had managed to secure a call between Huang and Vance, during which the ousted Nvidia boss explained his dealings with Megaspeed and talked out his positions on semiconductor export controls.

## Vance and Trump ‘decided Huang was not national security risk’

It was then up to Vance and Trump to convince the Nvidia board that Huang’s affair with Alice Huang did not make him a national security risk to the United States.

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent walked back criticism on Sunday and said he would not oppose Jensen’s return to Nvidia, a week after calling on the board to implement a “dramatic shake-up” and look elsewhere for a new leader.

The intervention seemingly worked, as Huang was returned to his post on Monday. Trump said he had played a key role in the decision and insisted: “He did nothing wrong.”

In a lengthy post on Truth Social, Trump said the board had made the “correct and proper decision to return Jensen Huang to the position of CEO”.

“The Country needs Jensen’s leadership at NVIDIA in order to ensure the Success and Prosperity of not only the Company, but the American Economy,” he said, adding: “The United States Government has no reason to believe that Jensen’s return to NVIDIA poses any risk to National Security.”

Nvidia’s board, which only last week said it did not plan to reinstate him, finally conceded that “Jensen is the right person to lead” the firm, after receiving “input from senior levels of the US government”.

![Trump shared an edited picture of Huang victorious as he returned to the CEO role](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/23/17/22/trump-nvidia-truth-social.png?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Trump went on to share the famous picture of himself with a fist raised after the July 2024 assassination attempt in Butler, edited to show a bloodied Huang raising a bra in front of a Nvidia flag.

Despite the certainty with which the US government was willing to pardon Huang, the US Commerce Department added Megaspeed to its trade blacklist on Monday.

And both Democratic and Republican lawmakers were furious that Huang had been “exonerated”, even after the US attorney general’s office concluded a “thorough” investigation into his conduct, according to Trump.

Huang made his triumphant return to the Santa Clara office on Monday.

“This is my first day of work. I don’t have a computer, so the IT department is going to give me a new PC,” he joked, before adopting a more serious tone and apologising to staff for putting “all of you through all of this”.
