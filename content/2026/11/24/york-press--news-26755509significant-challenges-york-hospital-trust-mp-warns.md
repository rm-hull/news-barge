The cash-strapped NHS trust that runs York hospital is facing a "significant challenge" to provide health services as the underfunded organisation faces further strain heading into winter, an MP has said.

Rachael Maskell told The Press extra pressure is being placed on the trust’s staff who "have given their all in providing the very best care possible under the circumstances".

It comes as the trust ruled out cutting the opening hours of urgent treatment centres to save money, despite the organisation admitting it faces "significant financial pressures".

![York Hospital](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21573383.jpg?type=mds-article-620)York hospital *(Image: Newsquest)*

Reducing the hours of the treatment centres, which help prevent overcrowding of A&E units, had been suggested in York, Scarborough and Malton to save £200,000 during a consultation.

But the York and Scarborough Teaching Hospitals NHS Foundation Trust confirmed there are currently no plans to reduce the treatment centre hours.

"There are currently no plans to change the opening hours of our urgent treatment centres," a trust spokesperson told The Press.

"Like many NHS trusts nationally, we are facing significant financial pressures and have been asked to identify further savings.

"As part of this, we worked with colleagues to suggest potential opportunities for efficiencies, with changes to opening hours being one of the ideas put forward for consideration.

"These were initial suggestions to help inform discussions, rather than formal proposals for change."

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/19715684.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Rachael Maskell, MP for York Central *(Image: UK Parliament/Press Association)*

Ms Maskell said the pressures facing staff at the trust are "not helped by the condition of [York] hospital, the paucity of funding to primary care to prevent admissions and a 35 per cent shortfall in public health funding since 2015".

"We need a serious dialogue with the ICB [integrated care board] and government as to why funding is not meeting need, so that people in our city can have the healthcare they need in a timely way," the Labour MP for York Central said.

The York and Scarborough trust is seeking an £8 million support package from NHS England as it battles a growing deficit and attempts to deliver tens of millions of pounds of planned savings.

A finance report said the trust had an adjusted deficit of £18.4 million at the halfway point of the financial year, around £9.2 million behind its planned position.

The trust is currently forecasting a deficit of £30.5 million under its mid-range scenario and £45.5m under its worst-case scenario.

Andrew Bertram, the trust’s finance director, has said continued shortfalls in the trust’s financial recovery programme remained one of the biggest pressures on the organisation’s finances.

He told a board meeting in October that the trust’s position had deteriorated further during the first six months of the year.

"We’ve delivered £24.7 million against the £31.8 million year-to-date position," he said.

Meanwhile in August, the trust announced that the care unit at Bridlington Hospital is to shut and move to a community-based model of care for patients who no longer need acute hospital treatment.
