A married vicar who it was claimed had a secret relationship with a woman who sought his religious guidance after the breakdown of her own marriage has been banned by the church.

Rev Dr Johnny Douglas, 51, who was vicar at St Paul’s Swanley in Kent, was said to have been “very intimate” on a rocking chair with the woman in one instance raised during a bishop’s disciplinary tribunal.

The hearing also heard how the woman, after suspecting he was then seeing another woman from the church, found her hiding under a desk when she went to confront him. The hiding woman told her “didn’t want her to find out like this”, according to the tribunal record.

In his decision banning Rev Douglas from exercising functions of his orders for life, chairman of the tribunal Peter Collier wrote: “He clearly took advantage of her when she sought his counsel as she returned to God in the midst of her marriage falling apart.”

He added: “They embarked on what became a sexually intimate relationship. However our judgment is that it was not genuine on his part – blowing hot and cold according to his whim.”

The tribunal heard how Rev Douglas was ordained as a deacon in 2011, before becoming a priest the following year and marrying his ex-wife Julie. Under his leadership, the ministry at St Paul’s, which had two churches, grew in number and was described as “fruitful and effective”.

In 2021, when the pair separated, the tribunal heard it came as a “great shock” to parishioners who had seen them as a happy couple.

But the tribunal heard that Rev Douglas behaved toward his ex-wife Julie in a coercive and controlling manner, subjecting her to emotional and financial abuse.

She told the tribunal that the pair had received counselling after a complaint against Rev Douglas led to stress between them, but during the process, she claimed, he repeatedly misrepresented her and her concerns, and threatened to report her to her employers to claim she was damaging his ministry and being unfaithful.

She also told the tribunal he had been dishonest with her, claiming in one instance that he was attending a residential medical trial, but instead she found in his diary a restaurant booking at a hotel.

By September 2021, they were living apart, the tribunal heard, although they did not divorce until 2024 due to a financial dispute.

The tribunal next heard from a woman who claimed to have enjoyed an on and off intimate relationship with Rev Douglas between 2022 and 2023.

After appearing devastated following the end of his relationship, the woman claimed, the pair began to message each other frequently and started one-to-one meetings at the start of 2022 as he encouraged her to grow her faith, the tribunal heard.

He told her the relationship must remain secret, she told the tribunal, and on his WhatsApp he had her saved as “ECG Ken Nimes”, a reference to “everyday courage girl” and Nimes, where she had gone on holiday after he dropped her off at an airport.

In evidence, Rev Douglas denied there was sexual intimacy of any kind.

In 2023, the pair travelled to a birthday party together in his car and he touched her hand and her knee, she told the tribunal. When they got out of the car they kissed each other, she added.

In March 2023, he messaged her saying “Loved the rocking chair togetherness.... very beautiful and delicious”, which she told the tribunal was in relation to her sitting next to him on a rocking chair where they were both clothed but “it was very intimate”.

Rev Douglas claimed the reference was in fact related to two other people on the rocking chair at a party, but the tribunal panel judged that did not tally with the wording of the message, and “unhesitatingly [we] accept her account”.

After Easter in 2023, there were two separate occasions of “some intimacy” between them, in April and in July, she said.

But late in 2023, after she suspected Rev Douglas was in a relationship with another woman who attended the church, she went to confront him at a house where she found the woman hiding under a desk.

In response to the claims, Rev Douglas’ special counsel suggested that all the intimacy described was what she would like have happened, but never did.

But Mr Colliersaid: “We find, and each of us is sure about it, that in the light of the WhatsApp messages and voice notes – their number, their timings, and their content – that it is impossible to come to any conclusion other than that she has spoken truthfully about that relationship.”

The panel said the relationship was “clearly improper and inappropriate” given the woman was a member of the congregation with vulnerabilities seeking his pastoral support.

Mr Collier concluded: “His [Rev Douglas] behaviour, as we have found it to be in relation to controlling and coercive behaviour with Julie and conducting a secret intimate relationship with XY [the woman] whilst still married to Julie, is behaviour which we consider any right member of the public would regard as misconduct by a priest.”
