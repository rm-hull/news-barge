A playground in Harrogate and plans for a new car park at a GP surgery in Ripon are among nine projects across North Yorkshire that could share more than £5 million in developer funding.

North Yorkshire Council has identified the schemes as priorities for investment through the Community Infrastructure Levy (CIL), with councillors due to decide whether to approve the allocations at an executive meeting next week.

For residents in the Harrogate district, the proposals include upgrading The Glebe playground in Killinghall to create a safer and more inclusive play area, while Park Street Surgery in Ripon is in line to receive funding towards the creation of a new car park.

The projects form part of a wider programme of investment in transport, healthcare, leisure and community facilities funded through developer contributions.

Unlike Section 106 agreements, which require developers to pay for infrastructure directly linked to individual housing developments, the Community Infrastructure Levy allows councils to pool contributions from multiple developments and invest them in projects that benefit the wider community.

North Yorkshire Council's deputy leader and executive member for open to business, Councillor Mark Crane, said: "We know that CIL funding is making a real difference in our communities.

"A wide range of projects have already benefited from this investment, and one of the key advantages of CIL is that it allows us to pool contributions from multiple developments and direct them towards infrastructure projects that are not site-specific and can benefit more residents.

"The projects identified for this year's funding programme cover a range of priorities, from transport and healthcare improvements to leisure and community facilities.

"The proposals will be considered by members of the executive next week as we continue to make sure investment is targeted where it is needed most and where it can deliver the greatest long-term value."

Elsewhere in North Yorkshire, proposals include £2 million towards a new neighbourhood healthcare facility in Northallerton, repairs to Bedale Hall and improvements linked to the Selby Station Gateway scheme.

Other projects include the refurbishment of Sheriff Hutton Bridge Cricket Club's pavilion, new 24-hour prescription dispensers at surgeries in Thirsk and Sherburn and Rillington, and a new padel tennis facility at The Galtres Centre in Easingwold.

During 2025/26, North Yorkshire Council collected more than £3.7 million through the Community Infrastructure Levy.

More than £3.1 million has been allocated for strategic infrastructure projects, while almost £593,000 has been passed directly to town and parish councils for local community schemes.

A further £7.2 million was secured through Section 106 agreements for site-specific improvements linked to new developments.

The proposed funding allocations will be considered by North Yorkshire Council's executive committee when it meets on Tuesday, December 1.
