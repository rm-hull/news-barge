# ‘My happy place’: Rishi Sunak hails North Yorkshire people and countryside

## Rishi Sunak has admitted that the country did not see “the best side of me” as Prime Minister, but said now as a backbench North Yorkshire MP he is “more relaxed”.

Speaking to Sky News from his Richmond and Northallerton constituency, Mr Sunak described the countryside as “his happy place”.

He admitted that he is much “more relaxed” now compared with his 19-month spell as prime minister.

In an interview to be aired today, Mr Sunak said: “If I look back on it now, I was not as at ease with myself as I could have been, should have been.

“You know, I don't think the country probably saw the best side of me and you know how I am here, you know, I'm more relaxed… I was wound quite tightly during it.”

Describing himself as an “apprentice Yorkshireman”, the 46-year-old said: “I love it … it's a very special place to represent.

“I feel very fortunate to have had the privilege to do it for over a decade, and hopefully some years to go to.

“It's an extraordinary place. Not just because it's beautiful like this but the people are what makes it special actually.

“That is the thing that I think is probably underappreciated because everyone always says oh you're so lucky to be MP for here and they all come on holidays here and do walking holidays here and they know how beautiful it is but it's the people that make it truly special.”

Mr Sunak said his wife - Akshata Murty - and their two daughters “absolutely love it”, hence the couple naming their numeracy charity, The Richmond Project.

“It's so special that we named our charity after it, which we thought was an appropriate thing to do actually because this is home to Akshata and I, it means a lot to us,” Mr Sunak explained.

“And when we're thinking about what to call our charity, just inspired by our life here and what this place means to us and what it's done for our family, it seemed very appropriate actually.”

There had been speculation that Mr Sunak would move back to California - where he met Ms Murthy - after losing the 2024 general election.

Speaking about this, he said: “You want to think through everything because it's still an important decision, but it wasn't a particularly difficult decision, put it that way.

“It wasn't a difficult decision. I love doing it. I get fulfilment from it and I was thinking about this point in my life.”

He said that expanding services at Friar Ridge Hospital, in Northallerton, was his “proudest achievement” in the constituency.

However, the former prime minister and chancellor did not rule out returning to frontline politics in the future.

When asked if he would return to the Shadow Cabinet or Cabinet under Kemi Badenoch, he said: “I'm 46 and I hope the future will be - touch wood - a long future ahead of me. And who knows where it will take me, right? And I don't know.

“But I do know that the job I'm most focused on is continuing to be a Member of Parliament here and making a difference.

“But look, there's lots of different ways people can contribute and that hasn't left me.”
