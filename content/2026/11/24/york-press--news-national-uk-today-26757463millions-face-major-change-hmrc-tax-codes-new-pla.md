Millions of taxpayers will no longer receive their annual HMRC tax code notice through the post under a major change coming into force next year.

HM Revenue and Customs (HMRC) is moving more of its communications online, with around 3.5 million people set to receive their annual tax code notices digitally from February 2027.

The change will apply to people who already use HMRC's Personal Tax Account or app but have not yet switched to paperless communications.

Instead of receiving a letter through the post, they will be able to check their tax code online and receive a notification by email when new information is available.

HMRC is urging customers to make sure their email address is up to date so they do not miss important tax updates.

Those who do not currently use HMRC's online services will continue to receive their tax code notices by post.

The move comes as the tax authority looks to reduce the cost of printing and sending millions of letters.

Of the 17.5 million tax code notices issued ahead of April 6, 2026, just 34% were sent digitally, while the remaining 11.5 million were posted at a cost of more than £8.2million.

HMRC has committed to saving £50million a year in printing and postage costs from the 2028/29 tax year.

James Murray, Financial Secretary to the Treasury and Paymaster General, said: "We are digitising the way HMRC communicates with members of the public. This will modernise the service it provides and will reduce the amount of taxpayers’ money being spent on printing and postage."

He added: "Making annual tax code notices digital by default, where people already use HMRC’s digital services, is the first step to reduce print and postal costs by £50 million annually from 2029."

Customers can check their tax code through the HMRC app or their Personal Tax Account.

Those who want to receive digital communications should download the HMRC app or sign in to their Personal Tax Account and provide an up-to-date email address. They will then be notified when new communications are available.

## Why are the changes being made?

HMRC says there are also practical benefits to checking tax codes online. Customers can see changes immediately rather than waiting for a letter, view all their Pay As You Earn (PAYE) information in one place and tell HMRC if any details are missing.

Recent improvements to the digital tax code service, including explanations when tax codes change, have led to a 40% reduction in the need for customers to contact HMRC.

However, not everyone will have to switch to digital communications. People who request information in alternative formats, including braille, large print or audio, will continue to receive it in their chosen format.

Anyone whose tax code has changed can also still request a paper notice if they need one.

HMRC will monitor how the 3.5 million affected customers respond to the change, including whether they view their tax code online, contact the department or request a paper copy. This will help inform the wider rollout of digital communications.
