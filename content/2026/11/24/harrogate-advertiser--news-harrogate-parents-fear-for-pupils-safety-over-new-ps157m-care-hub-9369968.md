North Yorkshire Council is facing growing opposition from worried parents in Harrogate over its latest efforts to tackle pressure on adult social care services.

Since the council first submitted plans in June for a new three-storey £15.7m care and support hub off Ainsty Road, close to two Harrogate schools, it has been hit by a different sort of pressure - concern over the choice of location and the potential impact on children’s safety and traffic congestion.

Like many councils across the country, North Yorkshire says it is facing rising care costs, increasing demand for specialist services and ongoing challenges linked to the sustainability and quality of existing care accommodation.

The contentious development, known as the Harrogate Care Hub, would be built on vacant land between St Robert’s Catholic Primary School and Harrogate High School and provide 60 bedrooms, split equally between three types of care – 20 dementia care places, 20 intermediate care beds and 20 short-term stay beds.

But parents of youngsters at both St Robert’s Catholic Primary School and Harrogate High School say the proposed new adult care facility, while welcome in principle, should not be at the expense of pupils’ safety, the schools and the interests of the local community.

A flurry of objections lodged on North Yorkshire Council’s planning portal outlines a lengthy and varied list of worries over a development which would happen next door to a congested part of Harrogate which has already experienced seven years of house building.

Using information ,which parents say was provided by St Robert’s Catholic Primary School, one parent wrote: "Whilst we acknowledge the wider community benefits that a specialist care facility may provide, these should not come at the expense of the safety, wellbeing or educational opportunities of our pupils.

"Unless the applicant can demonstrate that the development will not adversely affect pupil safety, safeguarding, school amenity, existing operations or the school's reasonable potential for future development, we respectfully request that planning permission be refused.”

Tom Peacock, a parent of two children at St Roberts Primary School, said: "Although the care home proposes to add 36 parking spaces this will not be nearly enough for staff, visitors, residents, contractors, deliveries and emergency vehicles.

"The access road will become extremely congested and hazardous for children coming in and out of school.

"The development would also mean our children working and playing next to a noisy, dusty construction site for a couple of years."

The saga has been brewing quietly ever since North Yorkshire Council approved a strategic business case to build to five new‑build Care & Support Hubs (CSH) across the county in January 2025.

Objectors say part of the problem with the plans is that the proposed development would share the existing access arrangements serving St Robert's Catholic Primary School and Harrogate High School, which are already experiencing considerable pedestrian and vehicle activity during school drop-off and collection periods.

They are also concerned that residents in the new care hub may require assistance during an emergency, adding to traffic problems in the area of the two schools, especially during arrival and departure periods.

They also say the three-storey care facility would be located immediately adjacent to school grounds, creating the potential for elevated views from bedrooms, communal spaces and circulation areas towards our playgrounds and external teaching facilities.

Responding to the *Harrogate Advertiser*, North Yorkshire Council’s corporate director for community development, Nic Harne, said: “As the application is currently being considered through the planning process, we are unable to comment at this stage.

“All representations submitted as part of the application process will be carefully considered.”

The council has previously said that the brownfield site near the two schools had been chosen because of its proximity to local services and public transport links, with shops, medical facilities and bus services all within walking distance.

The proposed new Harrogate Care Hub is part of a wider council programme designed to replace ageing local authority care homes including Station View, in Harrogate, Silver Birches, in Filey, and Larpool Lane, in Whitby, to alleviate hospital discharge delays by providing specialist dementia, intermediate, and short-term rehabilitation care.
