An "unknown substance" that sparked a mass evacuation at a US rapper’s concert in Manchester could be linked to robberies and reports of jewellery being stolen, police say.

The Co-op Live Arena was evacuated during rapper Don Toliver’s show and a major incident declared on Sunday night, with concertgoers describing a “horrible smell”, which police initially believed to be tear gas.

According to Greater Manchester Police (GMP), 89 people displayed symptoms believed to be linked to the exposure.

One woman was taken to Manchester Royal Infirmary for further assessment and treatment.

The remaining 88 people underwent dry decontamination at the scene and were subsequently accommodated within the Etihad Stadium while the incident was managed.

The major incident status ended in the early hours of Monday, police said, and the scene had been lifted on Tuesday.

GMP said that, as part of progressing enquiries, detectives are exploring the possibility that release of the substance could be linked to reports of a small number of robbery offences at the arena on Sunday evening.

![Police officers outside the Co-op Live in Manchester](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/24/19/01M87EN26VY64Y63T0EXEZQSH8.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

It comes after the force received nine reports of jewellery being stolen during the same concert. No arrests have been made at this time.

Assistant Chief Constable Tom Harding said: "We know that what happened on Sunday night and the imagery seen on social media will bring back painful memories of 2017.

"Thankfully, we are not dealing with an incident of such a devastating nature or motive and we maintain our belief that, based on the information available to us now, there is no wider ongoing risk to the public.

"We are aware similar robberies have been reported at two European concerts for the same artist and will be linking in with our European counterparts to better understand the wider picture of this suspected offending.

"We'll also continue to work closely with the venue and local authority partners over the coming weeks as events begin to return to the Co-Op Live arena.

"I'd like to thank everyone who attended the concert on Sunday evening for their patience and cooperation while emergency services responded.

"We're continuing to advise anyone who has developed symptoms or become unwell since attending the event to seek advice from their GP, NHS 111, or to attend their local hospital if necessary."

The rapper’s show at London’s O2 Arena – which had been scheduled for Monday night – has also been postponed.

![Don Toliver (James Manning/PA)](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/23/16/fe5c0a5d1bcfc55f566bbf944692254bY29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzk1NTM2NDk1-2.67691950.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

A representative for the venue said in a statement yesterday: “Due to production delays following last night’s show at Co-op Live in Manchester, tonight’s Don Toliver concert at The O2 is rescheduled to December 15. All tickets for tonight’s show at The O2 will be valid for December 15.

“We are sorry to everyone affected by last night’s events in Manchester and full refunds for that show will be automatically processed to ticket holders.”

Posting on Instagram on Monday, the rapper said: “I’m very sad to sit here and say I have to reschedule the O2 London show to December 15 due to the unfortunate incident last night in Manchester.

“I just wanna make sure everyone is safe and the next show goes on properly. My heart and prayers are with everyone that was injured last night at Co-op Live.

“The only thing I love more than creating music is performing for my fans. STOP BRINGING BULL\*\*\*\* INTO MY SHOWS. It ain’t cool. See you soon London. Love you guys.”
