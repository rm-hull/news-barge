Hundreds of mourners gathered at the Cincinnati Zoo Thursday night to pay tribute to Fiona, the beloved 9-year-old Nile hippo whose death has devastated fans in Ohio and around the world.

Visitors left flowers, handwritten cards and stuffed animals near a statue of Fiona outside the zoo's entrance, remembering the hippo who became an international sensation after surviving a premature birth in 2017.

Among the tributes was a handwritten card that 10-year-old Mikey read aloud.

“We love our Cincinnati Zoo. Fiona, we will miss you always,” he read his letter to WXIX.

Fiona died Thursday from complications after swallowing a visitor's hat that accidentally ended up inside her habitat, the zoo said. She ingested the hat November 8, prompting her veterinary and animal care teams to work around the clock to help her recover. Despite their efforts, Fiona died after the object could not be successfully moved through her digestive system.

![Visitors left flowers, cards and stuffed animals Thursday at the Cincinnati Zoo to honor Fiona, the hippo who captured hearts worldwide after surviving a premature birth in 2017](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/20/13/download.-1..jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“I can’t imagine what her caretakers are going through, everything they’ve done in the past 10 days,” one visitor told WXIX.

This wasn’t the first time Fiona fought for her life. She first captured the internet’s heart in January 2017, when she was born six weeks prematurely at the zoo, weighing just 29 pounds.

Her survival was far from certain, and zoo staff worked around the clock to care for the newborn hippo. Millions of people followed her progress as she grew from a fragile baby into one of the zoo's most recognizable residents.

Fiona’s journey inspired a wave of merchandise sold at the zoo, by local businesses and online retailers, including everything from seasonal mugs to children’s books and holiday ornaments. She even inspired her own limited-edition Graeter’s Ice Cream flavor, Chunky Chunky Hippo.

For some visitors, Fiona's story became a reason to make regular trips to the zoo.

“I remember when she was born. I remember the fight, all of the nurses from Cincinnati Children’s feeding her around the clock,” a visitor told WXIX. “I started buying our family season passes because of Fiona.”

![Fiona amassed millions of fans when the zoo shared her fight for her life on social media in 2017, and she had since become a tourist attraction](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/19/21/20/Fiona-(2).jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Another visitor told the outlet that their son and Fiona grew up side by side.

“I watched him kind of grow with Fiona because I was staying at home with him, and she was born a month later,” the parent said.

The growing memorial comes just weeks after it put up its giant Fiona the Nutcracker statue. Standing 24 feet tall and 8 feet wide, the statue has stood guard at the zoo’s Vine Street Village since the 2022 holiday season.

One visitor said the circumstances surrounding Fiona’s death made the loss especially heartbreaking after everything she had overcome early in life.

“It’s just so sad with her story and how hard she fought and how long she made it and then to lose her tragically. It’s heartbreaking for us and the whole city,” the visitor said.

![The growing memorial comes weeks after the zoo unveiled its 24-foot-tall Fiona the Nutcracker statue, a fixture at its Vine Street Village since the 2022 holiday season](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/19/21/US_Fiona_the_Hippo_53461.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Many downtown Cincinnati buildings lit up purple Thursday night in Fiona’s honor. The large LED-illuminated “Cincinnati” sign outside the Convention Center was in purple with hippo visuals after her death.

Ohio Governor-elect Amy Acton even released a statement.

"So sad to hear about the loss of Fiona the Hippo,” Acton said. “Fiona captured the hearts of millions and became one of Cincinnati’s most beloved icons. Her story brought joy, hope, and a little extra magic to our state.”
