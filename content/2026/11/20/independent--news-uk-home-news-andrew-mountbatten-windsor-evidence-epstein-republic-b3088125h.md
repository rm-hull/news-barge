A new dossier of evidence on Andrew Mountbatten-Windsor has been handed over to the police and Crown Prosecution Service by campaign group Republic.

Republic is now calling for a full investigation into “any and all suspected offences” by the former duke.

The campaign group said it had set out “a number of areas” where investigation into Mr Mountbatten-Windsor was warranted and stressed they should be examined “without fear or favour”.

Republic, which campaigns for an elected head of state, added that the details in the 80 pages of evidence have not been made public following legal advice.

However, it is understood they relate to allegations of misconduct in public office.

Republic previously announced it would bring a private prosecution against Mr Mountbatten-Windsor, but, as a result of his arrest, it said it was trusting the police and CPS to follow the evidence.

Mr Mountbatten-Windsor was arrested on his 66th birthday in February this year following claims he shared sensitive information with the paedophile Jeffrey Epstein during the former prince’s time as the UK’s trade envoy.

Graham Smith, Republic chief executive, said: “It is important that all evidence is presented to the police and that police follow all evidence to its natural conclusion.

![Mr Mountbatten-Windsor was arrested in February following claims he shared sensitive information with the paedophile Jeffrey Epstein during the former prince’s time as the UK’s trade envoy](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/23/15/9c0d9c6139b1bc4185899c3bfe7943b4Y29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzkyODUxOTM4-2.84707749.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“Private prosecutions are usually prohibitively expensive and difficult to pursue. So, we are trusting the police and prosecutors to step up and follow the evidence, wherever that leads.”

He added: “We make no claims as to the guilt or innocence of anyone involved, but it is vital that evidence is pursued and investigations followed, and that everyone is treated equally in law.

“At the moment, we have done what we can to ensure available evidence is put in front of the proper authorities.”

A Thames Valley Police spokesperson said: “Our misconduct in public office investigation is ongoing. We cannot go into specifics of the investigation, but we are following all reasonable lines of inquiry.”

The Metropolitan Police has also been approached for comment. The CPS said it was a matter for the police.

Republic announced: “Republic has handed over evidence to the police and CPS setting out a number of areas where investigation into Andrew Mountbatten-Windsor is warranted.”

It added: “Republic has now submitted further evidence to the police and CPS, and called on them to investigate any and all suspected offences without fear or favour.”

Last month, it was found that police raids on Mr Mountbatten-Windsor’s royal homes before his arrest were unlawful.

The former prince, who was not in attendance at the hearing, launched a legal claim against Thames Valley Police over the lawfulness of search warrants granted to them by a judge at the Old Bailey in February.

The warrants allowed officers to raid his residence on the Sandringham Estate and his former Windsor home, Royal Lodge, before his arrest on suspicion of misconduct in public office, following claims he shared sensitive information with the late sex offender Jeffrey Epstein during his time as the UK’s trade envoy.

Mr Justice Hilliard said a “significant error was made in the application for and grant of the search warrants” and that was “enough to render the warrants, the searches and the material seized unlawful.”

It emerged in May that detectives investigating Mr Mountbatten-Windsor will also consider allegations of sexual misconduct in their inquiry into potential misconduct in public office.

Multiple forces, including Thames Valley Police, the Metropolitan Police and Surrey Police, have live investigations linked to the release of the Epstein files.

The release of millions of pages by the US Department of Justice relating to Epstein in late 2025 and early 2026 sparked a fresh round of allegations against Mr Mountbatten-Windsor.

They ranged from claims a second woman was sent to the UK by Epstein for a sexual encounter with Mr Mountbatten-Windsor and that the former prince and Epstein asked an exotic dancer for a threesome in the latter’s Florida home.

Other allegations included that Mr Mountbatten-Windsor shared confidential reports of official visits to Hong Kong, Vietnam and Singapore in his role as the UK’s trade envoy.

In 2025, it emerged Mr Mountbatten-Windsor had emailed convicted sex offender Epstein in 2011 after he had already been jailed, telling him “We’re in this together” – three months after the then-duke insisted he had broken off all contact.

Meanwhile, Police Scotland is investigating the time Mr Mountbatten-Windsor, Epstein and Ghislaine Maxwell, who was convicted of luring young girls for Epstein to sexually abuse, spent at Balmoral amid claims women were trafficked to the private estate, which then belonged to the late Queen Elizabeth II.

The late Virginia Giuffre alleged she was forced to have sex three times with Mr Mountbatten-Windsor, which he vehemently denies, including when she was 17 and also during an orgy, after she was trafficked by Epstein.

Mr Mountbatten-Windsor paid millions to settle a civil sexual assault case with Ms Giuffre in 2022, despite insisting he had never met her.

Ms Giuffre died by suicide in 2025.

Republic’s latest steps were announced on the eve of the seventh anniversary of the late Queen’s second son announcing he was stepping back from public duties after his disastrous Newsnight interview.

In the televised sit-down in 2019, Mr Mountbatten-Windsor said he did not regret his friendship with Epstein and had no recollection of ever meeting Ms Giuffre.

He was heavily criticised for failing to show sympathy with the paedophile’s victims.
