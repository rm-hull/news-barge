**It's been 14 years since the release of the first Hunger Games movie, which helped establish the careers of talented young actors Jennifer Lawrence, Josh Hutcherson, Tom Blyth and Rachel Zegler.**

This latest instalment, prequel film Sunrise on the Reaping, looks to do the same with its leading trio: 21-year-old Joseph Zada, 20-year-old Mckenna Grace and 23-year-old Whitney Peak.

Sitting down with BBC News at the start of a gruelling press run, the group were buzzing with a mixture of anticipation, nerves and excitement ahead of the release of the much-talked-about film.

Zada, who is taking on his first major role as lead Haymitch Abernathy, said they had been "clinging together" and "using each other, making sure we all feel supported".

## 'Nothing like anything I've experienced'

Grace is the most experienced of the three, having grown up as a child star with appearances in TV series The Handmaid's Tale, The Haunting of Hill House and two of the recent Ghostbusters films.

Even then, she said joining The Hunger Games franchise is "so nerve-racking", adding "I don't think anything can prepare you for working on it - this is nothing like anything I've experienced."

![Still from The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping showing Joseph Zada and Mckenna Grace participating in the games, wearing beige outfits in a wooded forest](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/66c6/live/3d022da0-e3fd-11f1-99a1-253766e7ad8b.jpg)

Joseph Zada and Mckenna Grace play District 12 tributes Haymitch Abernathy and Maysilee Donner

Peak, who starred in Netflix's Chilling Adventures of Sabrina, jokes that she and Zada have sought out Grace every time they have felt "stuck or confused about what's happening" while promoting the film, and Zada says she has been a "mentor" to the new actors.

Sunrise on the Reaping is the sixth film in the series, which to date is one of the highest-grossing film franchises of all time, bringing in more than $3.3bn (£2.51bn) worldwide since 2012.

It is set 24 years before the original The Hunger Games film and is based on the 2025 book of the same name by author Suzanne Collins.

Zada takes on the role of a teenage Haymitch, who is played by Woody Harrelson in the original films - a District 12 tribute in the games, chosen alongside Grace's character Donner.

Peak plays Lenore Dove, Haymitch's girlfriend.

## 'I'm a bit traumatised'

![Still from The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping of Whitney Peak and Joseph Zada, sat in a field with ducks next to them](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/7adc/live/94e7db50-e3fd-11f1-99a1-253766e7ad8b.jpg)

Whitney Peak stars as Haymitch's girlfriend Lenore Dove, who is left behind in District 12 while he fights in the games

Reviews of the latest film have been generally positive, with several outlets praising Zada's performance, noting the difficulty of stepping into Harrelson's shoes.

Zada says he spent months "completely invested" in Harrelson's performances in the first four The Hunger Games films, "watching everything I could find and then making notes" which he used "as a base layer to colour with my own ideas".

He didn't meet the Oscar-nominated actor until halfway through filming, but says his advice was to watch two films - Cool Hand Luke and Harold and Maud, with the former "helping a lot to ground my performance" and the latter serving as "a fun watch".

Jesse Plemons, who plays a games producer in the film, told the BBC he knew Zada's role would be "a massive undertaking" after reading the script but says he was "amazed by his stamina and grace".

This was echoed by Glenn Close, who plays an over-the-top representative from the ruling Capitol, who said she "loved working with" Zada.

"When I met them [Zada, Grace and Peak] for the first time, they'd done all the games and been through it all.

"The first thing Joe said to me was 'I'm a bit traumatised' and he wasn't even done - there was still so much to go," she added.

The film, which includes an ensemble cast of Ralph Fiennes, Maya Hawke, Kieran Culkin and Elle Fanning, is set during the 50th edition of The Hunger Games - with most elements of the brutal dystopian universe that fans have come to recognise from the previous films returning in this iteration.

Adapted from Collins' novels, the series is set in a post-apocalyptic North America, which is known as Panem, with the first trilogy focusing on a teenage Katniss Everdeen, who was played by Lawrence in the films.

Panem has 12 districts that participate in the yearly Hunger Games, produced and run by the governing area, the Capitol, as a punishment for rebellion.

Peak notes that being on set was an intense experience for the young cast and could "get overwhelming very quickly" on set, so they had to make sure to "keep it light between all the hard stuff".

"I don't think people realise how intensely bonding an experience like this is because we all spend days doing extremely physical and deeply emotional things," Grace says.

"It's like a little family on set; we've all been thankful to have each other," she added.

*The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping is in cinemas now.*
