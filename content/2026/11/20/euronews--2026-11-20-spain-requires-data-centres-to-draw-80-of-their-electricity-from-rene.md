## Spain tightens regulation of data centres with new requirements on renewable energy, efficiency and digital sovereignty to limit their impact on the power grid and attract sustainable investment.

The Spanish government has approved new rules for data centres that set **renewable energy consumption requirements**, water and energy efficiency standards and digital sovereignty obligations, with the aim of aligning the growth of these infrastructures with the capacity of the electricity system and the country’s environmental commitments.

The **Royal Decree**, approved on Friday by the Council of Ministers, requires data centres** to prove that 80% of their electricity consumption comes from renewable sources**, with hourly matching between generation and consumption and additionality criteria to drive new clean production capacity.

The regulation provides for **long-term power supply contracts, self-consumption and energy storage**. It also establishes a voluntary transitional regime for operators that agree to the possibility of being disconnected with priority when this is necessary to safeguard the stability of the electricity grid.

## Stricter energy and environmental requirements

The rules introduce **provisional limits on water and electricity use** in data centres: they will have to use water more efficiently and cut the energy needed for their operation, while European criteria are finalised. The text also incorporates into national law other transparency and water and energy efficiency requirements laid down by European regulation, adapting them to Spain’s specific circumstances.

The regulation introduces sustainability reporting obligations for the largest facilities and sets **specific data protection and digital sovereignty requirements** for high-category information systems under the National Security Framework.

In these cases, the **most sensitive data** must remain within the European Economic Area and controls will be tightened on access from third countries to administration, maintenance and support tasks.

Failure to comply with certain obligations may lead to financial compensation and sanctions, including suspension of grid access and connection rights for up to three years. Projects that are already being processed will generally have **six months to adapt to the new conditions.**

## 35 GW between permitted capacity and projects in the queue

The decision comes at a time of strong growth in demand for digital infrastructure in Spain. Third Vice-President and Minister for Ecological Transition, Sara Aagesen, said that data centres currently have 15 gigawatts (GW) of granted access capacity to the electricity grid, while a further 20 GW remain on the waiting list.

The minister warned that, without control measures, the expansion of these facilities could generate an additional cost of up to 5,000 million euros per year in the electricity system, according to government estimates: **4,000 million from the impact on wholesale prices and another 1,000 million from capacity mechanisms.**"In the absence of this Royal Decree, we would see an impact of 5,000 million euros on our bills," said Energy Transition Minister Sara Aagesen. "We have different scenarios, but it would lead, for example, to industrial consumers having to pay 25% more for the electricity they use," she added. These figures are** government forecasts for a hypothetical scenario**, not costs already incurred or savings guaranteed by the new rules.

## 17,142 million euros of investment in the electricity grid

At the same time, the government has approved new electricity grid planning up to 2030, providing for investments of 17.142 million euros **to expand and reinforce infrastructure and meet rising demand**. The plan includes capacity for new forms of consumption, including data centres, although it is not specifically dedicated to these facilities.

Spain is seeking to consolidate its position as a **destination for international investment in data centres**, drawing on its renewable resources, connectivity and strategic location in Europe.

The new regulation is designed to **tie that growth to greater energy and environmental efficiency**, in a context where the availability of electricity, water and connection capacity has become one of the key factors in the development of these projects. The impact on ongoing projects and future investment will depend on how the new obligations are applied and on the deadlines set out in the final text.
