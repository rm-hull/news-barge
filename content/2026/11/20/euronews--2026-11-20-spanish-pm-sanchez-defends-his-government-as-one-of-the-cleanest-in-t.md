## Sánchez's PSOE party trails in most polls to the mainstream right-wing People's Party (PP), which is predicted to win the most seats on November 29 but is expected to fall short of securing a majority.

Spanish Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez has defended his government as "one of the cleanest in the history of democracy" after criticism from opposition leader Alberto Núñez Feijóo over corruption scandals.

The pair clashed over topics including corruption, the economy, housing, immigration and foreign policy as they faced off in a televised debate on Thursday.

Feijó, the leader of the mainstream right-wing People's Party (PP), pointed to corruption scandals that have affected Sánchez's relatives and senior Socialists while accusing the prime minister of not doing enough to avoid the housing crisis.

He also blamed Sánchez's government for two deadly high-speed train collisions this year.

"Spain deserves a respectable prime minister, and (Pedro Sánchez) is not one," he said.

For his part, Sánchez acknowledged having made mistakes and said his government had "not been perfect.

"But I am also fully aware that we have always governed in the right direction," he said.

He also argued that Spain was in better shape than when he took power in 2018, referencing economic growth and employment figures - a claim Feijóo rejected.

"Having a job does not guarantee you can make ends meet. Having a job does not guarantee you can afford a home either. You have crushed the middle class," he told the prime minister.

"The Spanish economy is posting growth figures, but many families are still having to perform financial gymnastics to make ends meet."

Sánchez's PSOE party trails in most polls to the PP, which is predicted to win the most seats on November 29 but is expected to fall short of securing a majority.
