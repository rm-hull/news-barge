# Drax: Yorkshire power station operator forges forward with solar and wind drive as it makes £9m acquisition

## North Yorkshire power station operator Drax is set to further bolster its solar and wind division after agreeing to a £9m acquisition.

Dax has agreed to acquire Bluefield Services Limited for £9.15m, following its acquisition earlier this year of fellow Bluefield firm, Bluefield Solar Income Fund.

The Yorkshire firm completed the £561m takeover of Bluefield Solar Income Fund in July as part of efforts to broaden its range of power generation.

Drax said that in connection with the acquisition in July, it had made post-offer intention statements around acquiring all or part of Bluefield Group.

In an announcement to the London Stock Exchange yesterday, the company confirmed it had now agreed to acquire Bluefield Services, which provides asset management, operations and maintenance, construction management and pipeline development services to the wider Bluefield group.

Drax said in a statement: “The acquisition will expand Drax’s operations and maintenance and pipeline development expertise, which it will utilise across its dedicated solar and onshore wind business.”

Speaking in July, Drax Group CEO Will Gardiner described the first Bluefield deal as “transformational” for Drax, with the move adding solar and wind assets to the firm’s generation portfolio for the first time.

In a trading update in September, Drax said that it expects to operate Bluefield as a “dedicated Solar & Wind segment within the group”.

Mr Gardiner added that the move was part of “transitioning Drax into a broader business”.

Drax’s North Yorkshire power plant has been the subject of much controversy in recent years. The site burns biomass wood pellets, a method of energy production which has had its green credentials brought into question by multiple scientists and campaign groups.

Drax maintains that the use of biomass plays a key role in reducing the UK’s emissions.

The latest announcement comes after November saw Drax announce a partnership with Brookfield to develop one of the UK’s largest data centres at its North Yorkshire site.

Brookfield estimates that total investment in the development could potentially exceed £20bn, including an initial £2.5bn as part of the first phase.

It is planned that the first phase of the scheme will be operational from the first quarter of 2028.

Drax’s plans for a data centre were central to North Yorkshire’s bid to become one of the Government’s ‘AI Growth Zones’, dedicated zones where reforms to planning rules and energy access are put in place to speed up the building of data centres.

North Yorkshire Combined Authority, the main body behind the region’s AI Growth Zone bid, confirmed in October that the bid had not been successful.

Drax subsequently confirmed that it would still go ahead with plans for the data centre, adding that its scheme “never relied” on the growth zone bid.
