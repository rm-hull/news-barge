# Castle Howard: ‘I had great expectations for the Charles Dickens themed Christmas event at the Yorkshire stately home and it surprised me’

## My expectations were high when I attended Castle Howard’s Charles Dickens-themed event this year and it surprised me.

I was invited on behalf of The Yorkshire Post to attend the Charles Dickens-themed Christmas event at Castle Howard with a friend.

The last festive Castle Howard event I went to was its Alice In Wonderland Christmas extravaganza two years ago.

Due to the fantasy element of that event, there was a lot of room to make very elaborate and otherworldly decorations and my jaw was on the floor as I walked through.

![Liana Jacob at Castle Howard's Christmas event 2026.](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/jpim-static/image/2026/11/20/2/59/NLYP-19-11-2026-Liana-Jacob-at-Castle-Howards-Christmas-event-2026-YORupload.jpeg?trim=146,55,91,50&width=640&quality=65)


Naturally my expectations were high but part of me did consider that the vibe would be different this time.

I knew that this theme would be more grounded based on Charles Dickens’ very gritty books surrounding dark themes and realism such as the life of an orphan, that capture the authentic atmosphere of London.

After seeing the effort and details from the last occasion, I had a strong feeling that CLW Event Design and imitating the dog would be pulling out all the stops again.

Walking through the various hallways and rooms, I was right - the decorations made me feel like I had stepped into each of Charles Dickens’ novels.

Each room was modelled after a Charles Dickens novel - from the classics and well known titles such as Oliver Twist to the lesser known titles such as Bleak House.

There were a few memorable moments that stuck in my mind; the overall room was tastefully decorated with minimal decorations but what stood out the most was the interactive doll house that included moving projections of the characters from the book. It was fascinating to watch.

![Charlotte Benedict dressed in period costume takes time out from the festive arrangements and relaxes in The Crimson Dining Room.](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOmQ3ZGM2Nzc4LWJlZmMtNGM5OC05NmQ3LTA0NDVmYjI4MWQ4OTo4OGQ5NmNkNS0wMjRlLTQyMjctOGI5ZS1iYjFjMjcyYzU3YmU=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


It was lovely reuniting with a few familiar faces including former Great British Bake Off contestants David Atherton, Helena Garcia and Freya Cox. They were all in festive spirits and it was nice to hear their thoughts on the event.

As always, I loved the Great Hall with the large 26 ft Christmas tree displayed as the centrepiece directly below the magnificent dome ceiling. The height and elaborate decorations of the tree always blows me away.

![Charlotte Benedict dressed in period costume places a bauble on the Christmas Tree in the Great Hall at Castle Howard.](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOjM5M2YzOGY0LTFhZDUtNDNhOS1hNjM5LTRmOGE1MWI4M2IxMzowMjY1ODdlYS0wODgzLTQ3MmEtYjcwMy03NWZkYTYyODNlNjM=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


This time, there were intricate cardboard cut-outs of Charles Dickens characters hanging from the balcony - they added a lot of whimsy to the Great Hall.

I felt a chill in more ways than one when I entered the room inspired by A Christmas Carol; there was a sculpture of Ebeneezer Scrooge presiding over a food stall with a large screen behind it.

The ghosts appeared on the screen, very life-like, with one of the ghosts blowing air and I felt a chill breeze past. It felt very real.

I really enjoyed the aesthetic of the Great Expectations room with the clever effect of the veils draped over the Christmas tree and the table of treats. It gave the effect of a spider’s web.

![Model Taggie Pridden in the Dining Room at Castle Howard.](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOjNlOTAyOGNmLTZmMGUtNDUwZC1iNTlhLWJkZjQwNzQxNWVkMTpkNDBhN2QyNC1iODI4LTRlYjYtOTI5Ny02YWRkNGU1MWE5OTc=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


The long gallery was also a remarkable room with an elaborate feast and a vivid design that looked like real cakes and treats.

We were treated to a delicious afternoon tea finale with sparkling wine courtesy of UK pastry chef Florian Poirot, originally from France. The sandwiches were delicious and melted in my mouth - the perfect end to an extravagant festive day.

My friend and I popped into the Christmas shop afterwards and got into the Christmas spirit browsing all the trinkets and baubles.

Charlotte Lloyd Webber told me: “If only half of the people went away today and felt inspired to pick up a copy of Great Expectations today, that would be amazing.”

Rest assured that I did feel inspired to revisit Charles Dickens novels after attending the event.
