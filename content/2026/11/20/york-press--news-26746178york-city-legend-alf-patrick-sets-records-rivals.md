In today's series of subscriber-only nostalgia deep dives, we look back at a time when a York City striker made history.

Ollie Pearce might be the name in the headlines as he continues to climb his way up the all-time leading York scorers list, but 78 years ago today another City striker was also making history.

On a day where three records were set at Bootham Crescent, the Minstermen smashed Yorkshire rivals Rotherham United 6-1 in a Third Division match.

Their first record set was the attendance – an incredible 19,216 came through the Bootham Crescent turnstiles, 4,000 more than York’s previous highest crowd at the time.

It had only been exceeded twice in City’s history at that point, both of which were FA Cup ties against Huddersfield Town and Middlesbrough.

The club received £1,271 for the crowd, a record amount for a league fixture.

But arguably the most impressive record set of the day belonged to striker and club legend Alf Patrick.

Patrick would have an outstanding career with the Minstermen, bursting onto the scene in November 1946 at the age of 25, not too long after World War Two had ended.

In a six-year spell for York, Patrick was recorded to have played 241 times, scoring 117 goals in that period.

His first season as a City player delivered an impressive 17 goals in 23 appearances, including a goal on his debut against Stockport County.

But his best season in a York shirt came in the 1948/49 campaign, scoring 26 times and setting club records.

In that match against Rotherham where City broke attendance and income records, Patrick scored a remarkable five goals – a club record for the Minstermen in the Football League.

He remains the only player to have done so, and at the time it took Patrick’s run in the league to 14 goals in 18 matches.

It was Rotherham’s first defeat in seven games, and only their second of the season, while the Millers had also only conceded 15 goals in their 17 games so far in that campaign.

Also in the stands on that day was Runcorn manager Albert Malam, who was scouting York ahead of an FA Cup showdown.

Patrick would go on to end his career with Scarborough, playing one season there, before returning to York to help train the next generation of City talents.

He sadly passed away in 2021, aged 100, but has since had a street named after him at the old Bootham Crescent ground.
