## A former senior Interior Ministry official is suspected of sharing details of investigations into National Rally's election campaign finances, according a French newspaper.

French authorities are investigating whether a former senior Interior Ministry official leaked confidential information about judicial investigations to Marine Le Pen's far-right National Rally party, months before the presidential election.

According to Le Monde newspaper, Hugues Moutouh, now prefect of the Centre-Val de Loire region, is suspected of sharing information about investigations into the financing of the party's 2022 and 2024 election campaigns.

### Judicial investigation

Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez confirmed on Friday that an investigation had been opened and that he had summoned Moutouh for a meeting.

The Paris prosecutor's office told The Associated Press news agency it was investigating possible breaches of professional confidentiality and investigative secrecy, as well as the handling of information obtained through these offences.

No one has been formally charged, prosecutors said, adding that investigators searched the National Rally's headquarters on 9 November.

Le Pen has not commented on the case.

"If the facts are confirmed, they are obviously very serious," Nuñez told BFM TV.

### Further controversies ahead of the election

The investigation adds to a series of controversies involving Le Pen's party less than six months before France's presidential election.

In September, Jordan Bardella, Le Pen's choice for prime minister if elected, was accused of making antisemitic remarks as a young party member. Bardella, 31, strongly denied writing the comments reported by investigative website Mediapart.

Le Pen, 58 is also facing legal challenges after being sentenced in July to a year of home detention with electronic monitoring following an embezzlement conviction. She has appealed to France's highest court and believes she will not have to wear the device.

Despite her legal troubles, Le Pen leads opinion polls ahead of next year's presidential election to succeed Emmanuel Macron.
