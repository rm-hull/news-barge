A Liverpool fan who was accused of racially abusing Antoine Semenyo has been found not guilty after the footballer withdrew his support for the prosecution.

Semenyo was due to give evidence at Liverpool Magistrates’ Court on Friday in the trial of Mark Mogan, 47, who was accused of shouting abuse during the opening match of the Premier League season at Anfield on 15 August last year.

But Simon Jones, prosecuting, said: “Following recent contact made with Mr Semenyo, he has indicated he is no longer supportive of proceedings and the prosecution do not consider it is appropriate to compel him.

“In those circumstances we offer no further evidence in the case.”

District Judge Mary Dowrick recorded a verdict of not guilty to the charge of racially aggravated behaviour.

Semenyo, who was a Bournemouth player at the time of the incident but is now signed for Manchester City, had been due to give evidence in October, but the trial was adjourned halfway through when Mr Mogan was taken to hospital after suffering a seizure.

![The court had heard that Mr Mogan swore at Semenyo](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/20/11/2230483011..?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&auto=webp)

The court had heard that Mr Mogan, in a wheelchair, shouted at Semenyo as he went to take a throw-in about 30 minutes into the game.

He was alleged to have said: “Hurry up and take the throw-in, you black b\*\*\*\*\*\*.”

Opening the case in October, Mr Jones said: “That racial abuse was shouted directly at Antoine Semenyo, who was close to him.

“Mr Semenyo briefly froze, as he couldn’t believe what the male had said.”

Mr Mogan then shouted “I’m talking to you, you c\*\*\*”, the court heard.

![Mark Mogan, who had an intermediary with him in court, told police in interview that Semenyo had called him a cripple after he shouted at the player to ‘hurry up’](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/14/15/d8f8d7ed30749410298cbedc6a154a5fY29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzkyMDcyOTE1-2.86181758.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Witnesses also said they saw him throw his chewing gum at the footballer.

Semenyo reported the incident to the match referee and Mr Mogan was removed from the stadium at half-time.

Mr Mogan, who had an intermediary with him in court, told police in interview that Semenyo had called him a cripple after he shouted at the player to “hurry up”.

Mr Mogan, of Templehill Close in Dovecot, Liverpool, declined to comment as he left court.
