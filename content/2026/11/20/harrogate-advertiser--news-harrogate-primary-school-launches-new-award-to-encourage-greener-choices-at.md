Pupils at St Peter’s Church of England Primary School in Harrogate are being rewarded for their eco-friendly efforts through a new Creation Care Award.

Launched by the school’s Climate Crew, the award recognises children who are making a positive difference through practical actions at school and at home.

The scheme celebrates everyday green habits, from reducing waste and cutting energy use to caring for school plants and looking after the environment.

The first awards have already been presented, with Year 1’s Kingfishers class recognised for overhauling their classroom recycling system and making their own recycled paper.

Year 4’s Starlings class also received an award for their dedication to caring for their classroom plants and creating a positive learning space.

To earn the award, pupils are encouraged to share examples of how they are helping the planet at home, helping to build sustainable habits beyond the school gates.

Rachel Patefield, Climate Education Lead at St Peter’s Church of England Primary School, said: “It has been wonderful to see the children taking genuine pride in their actions and sharing practical ideas with one another.

“The Creation Care Award shows them that small everyday changes at school and at home really do add up.”

Paul Griffiths, Headteacher at St Peter’s Church of England Primary School, said the initiative reflects the school’s values of caring for the world around them.

He added: “The Creation Care Award goes right to the heart of our school vision to Love, Learn and Shine - showing care for our environment, learning how our everyday choices make a difference, and giving our children the confidence to lead the way.

“Seeing their enthusiasm spread across the whole school, and beyond the school gates to their families, is wonderful.”

The Climate Crew plans to present more Creation Care Awards throughout the academic year as part of the school’s wider environmental work.
