With a fine brush and hand-mixed colours, traditional painter **Karma Wangchuk** brings plain timber to life through elaborate dragons and ornate motifs, practising**Shing Tshon,** a centuries-old wood-painting craft that gives**Bhutanese** architecture its signature vibrancy. From**private homes and monasteries to public buildings and footbridges**, these colourful designs, frequently woven with** Buddhist prayers and sacred symbolism**, are an ever-present sight across the Himalayan kingdom.

Protected by government building codes that champion traditional styles, **Shing Tshon** is deeply rooted in Bhutan’s national identity. To keep the craft thriving alongside rapid modernisation, the**National Institute for Zorig Chusum** in**Thimphu** instructs young trainees in all**13 traditional arts and crafts**, ensuring that the country’s distinctive painted woodwork remains visible on new builds as well as ancient monuments.
