Denmark’s oldest living person was a ‘faithful’ Nazi party member during the German occupation, it has been claimed.

Kirsten Schwalbe, from Struer, made headlines in October when she became the longest-living Dane in recorded history at the age of 112 years and 218 days.

Ms Schwalbe, who was born in March 1914, is today the subject of a best-selling biography and has become a TV celebrity of sorts in recent years.

But during celebrations in her name last month, it emerged she had been a member of the Nazi party during the occupation in World War II.

![Kirsten Schwalbe sits in her kitchen in Struer, Denmark in March last year](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/20/09/2203769355..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Ms Schwalbe told local media at the time that joining the party had been her late husband’s idea and claimed she did not know what they were signing up for.

“I barely knew what it was, because I've never been interested in politics,” she told TV Midtvest, adding: “Back then, wives didn't get involved in things like that – they left that to the men.”

But this week, a bombshell report claimed to have uncovered evidence that she had a much greater role the party than she had let on.

Documents from the Danish national archives retrieved by Frihedsbrevet suggest Ms Schwalbe was acting department group leader and women's group leader of the Danish arm in Holstebro from August 1943 to July 1944.

Historian Claus Bundgård Christensen told the outlet: “In other words, there is no doubt that both she and her husband were prominent Nazis locally.”

![Kirsten Anna Schwalbe was born in Midtjylland on March 10 1914 (pictured last year)](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/20/10/2203769040..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

In one document, where she was formally nominated for the two roles, she was described as a “faithful member” of the party.

Ms Schwalbe declined to comment to Frihedsbretet.

It had previously been reported that Ms Schwalbe had joined the party during the Nazi occupation, which lasted from April 1940 until 1945.

Her name appeared in the Bovrup registry, which lists people who were members of DNSAP, the National Socialist Workers' Party of Denmark.

Membership alone does not show level of involvement, and Ms Schwalbe said at the time she felt it unfair that she should have to “pay for it” after so much time.

![Kirsten Schwalbe sits on a sofa as she knits in her home in Struer](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/20/10/2203769109..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

During the occupation, around 6,000 Danes volunteered as soldiers for Hitler's Wehrmacht on the eastern front. Around 20,000 Danes also joined the anti-Nazi resistance.

The Danish resistance managed to smuggle almost all of its Jewish population from the hands of the Nazis by smuggling them out in fishing boats to neutral Sweden.
