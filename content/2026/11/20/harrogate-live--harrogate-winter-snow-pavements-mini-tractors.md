Town centre pavements will be salted before forecast heavy snow and three mini tractors can clear footways, after Harrogate's January 2025 freeze.

Town centre pavements in Harrogate and Knaresborough will be salted in advance when exceptional snow is forecast this winter. It is one of several changes North Yorkshire Council has made since the snow and freeze of January 2025.

The changes are set out in a report for the Harrogate and Knaresborough Area Committee on **Thursday 26 November**. Councillors there had asked what would be done differently, especially on footways.

## What went wrong in January 2025

Heavy snow began on 4 January 2025 and lasted several days. Intense cold followed straight away, with road surface temperatures as low as **minus 10C**. The report says snow froze and compacted on roads and pavements, and the cold made conditions worse even where they had been treated. It accepts Harrogate was “particularly impacted”.

## What has changed

The report lists these measures, all already in place or agreed:

* **Pre-salting:** town centre footways are salted before an exceptional snow event, with extra council staff to help.
* **Longer treatment of priority pavements:** in prolonged snow and ice, treatment of the main priority footway routes is extended.
* **Main shopping streets:** footways can be treated in advance when black ice or continuous frost is expected overnight, a council decision of October 2025 confirms.
* **Three mini tractors:** fitted with salt spreaders and small ploughs for pavements. They cover the whole county, not only Harrogate, and trim hedges in summer.
* **More hands:** the council’s parks and countryside staff, North Yorkshire Highways, its subcontractors and farming contractors can be called in when conditions demand.
* **Earlier warning:** five to ten day forecasts are watched, and a forecast of snow triggers talks on how many staff are needed.
* **Grit bins:** stickers with QR codes for requesting a refill have been put on bins.

The rules on where a grit bin can go have not changed. Councillors, parish councils and town councils can still pay for a bin at a spot that does not qualify.

## Who pays

Any extra cost comes out of the existing winter service budget, the report says. It does not put a figure on it, because the cost depends on how much snow and ice there is.

The council has a legal duty under the Highways Act 1980 to make sure, as far as reasonably practicable, that safe passage along a highway is not endangered by snow or ice.

## What it means for you

* **If you walk into town in bad weather**, the main priority footways should now be treated before and for longer during heavy snow.
* **If your street has a grit bin**, the QR sticker on it is the quickest way to ask for a refill.
* **If you want a grit bin your street does not qualify for**, ask your parish or town council or your county councillor, who can fund one.
* **If you belong to a business group or residents’ association**, the council asks volunteers who want to help clear snow to contact its local highways office.
