## Speaking exclusively to Euronews, the Reform UK leader admitted Brexit had failed to deliver on immigration, but accused Prime Minister Andy Burnham of betraying voters by bringing Britain closer to the EU.

Nigel Farage has accused British Prime Minister Andy Burnham of betraying Brexit voters by bringing Britain back into parts of the EU single market, as the two sides prepared to seal a series of agreements at a summit in Brussels on Friday.

Speaking to Euronews' Europe Today as Burnham arrived for his first EU summit since taking office in July, the Reform UK leader and longtime Brexit campaigner said he was "pretty angry" at having to fight the Brexit battle all over again.

"What is happening this morning in Brussels is Andy Burnham is bringing us back into the single market in the areas of foodstuffs and farming," Farage said.

He argued that Britain would have to accept EU regulations without having a vote on them, describing the move as a betrayal of Labour's promise not to rejoin the single market.

But Farage struggled to offer a convincing rebuttal to Burnham's argument that Brexit had damaged Britain's economy and international standing.

He claimed Britain's global influence had actually increased since leaving the EU, citing its support for Ukraine and the AUKUS nuclear submarine agreement with Australia.

"There's no question we stand taller," he insisted.

On the economic damage, however, Farage argued that the Covid pandemic made it difficult to assess Brexit's impact. He claimed Britain had prospered in sectors such as artificial intelligence and financial services, while dismissing the EU single market as a "failing, backward model".

Farage was more candid about immigration, acknowledging that Brexit had failed to deliver one of its central promises.

"It was the biggest," he said when asked whether the failure to control immigration was Brexit's greatest disappointment.

He blamed successive Conservative governments for allowing record immigration despite regaining control of Britain's borders.

Farage also acknowledged that many Britons felt Brexit had failed to deliver, but insisted this did not mean they wanted to reverse it.

He warned that reopening the question of EU membership would divide the country and demanded another referendum if Burnham sought to rejoin.

"If Labour, if Burnham, fights the next election on a ticket of rejoining the EU, he'd have to have a referendum," Farage said.

## **EU would welcome Britain back, says MEP**

German Socialist MEP Tobias Cremer offered a sharply different assessment, telling Europe Today that Brexit had imposed enormous economic costs on Britain and that a majority of voters now favoured rejoining.

"£260 billion a year, £10,000 per household," he said. "And immigration as well. Yes, EU immigration went down by 100,000 or 150,000, but overall non-EU immigration has gone up by three million since then."

Cremer said the geopolitical landscape had transformed since 2016, citing Russia's war in Ukraine, China's growing assertiveness and an increasingly unpredictable United States.

"It's in both the British and European interest to stick together," he said, arguing that closer cooperation on security, defence and trade would benefit both sides.

Asked whether Britain could be back in the EU within five years, Cremer replied: "That would be my wish."
