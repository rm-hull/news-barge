It’s fitting that the young but brilliant Cuore Chamber Orchestra should opt for two Mozart pieces written before he had even reached the age of 20 on their homecoming at Harrogate’s glittering Royal Hall.

If Yorkshire’s newest professional orchestra promised a lot in their debut year, their return to the stage amid the fireworks of Guy Fawkes Night delivers in spectacular fashion.

Having impressed in their inaugural year at three Yorkshire venues in which they showcased works by British composers such as Elgar, Parry, Purcell and Arnold, their latest Harrogate concert suggests new breadth to their vision of creating a broad-ranging and inclusive space for classical music in the county.

Under the baton of William Dutton, the Cuore have already gained a reputation for being masters of the light touch.

The orchestra’s Founder and Artistic Director’s sophisticated and understated approach perfectly suits the twists and turns of Edvard Grieg’s light and lively Holberg Suite (1884) and, even more so, Michael Berkleley’s Meditations for Strings (1975) where both the conductor and the players bring an innate subtlety to the tidal surges of contemplation and melancholy in this modern composer’s first acknowledged orchestral work.

That this British great and BBC Radio 3 presenter, who was made a CBE in 2012 and a life peer the following year, appears inside the red and gold of the Royal Hall to receive the accolades after what is this special piece of music’s Yorkshire premiere, says a lot about the standing the Cuore is now held in just 18 months after making its debut.

So far in its short but remarkable life, Cuore Chamber Orchestra has appeared to be at its very best in the spaces and silences, the rising and falling of unspoken feelings buried deep in the music.

The second half of their homecoming concert isn’t about that and demonstrates how far they have travelled and how quick.

There’s a sprightly feel in the air as the Cuore launch into Mozart’s lyrical and playful and rhythmic Violin Concerto No. 3 in G major, K. 216 (1775).

Leading soloist Jennifer Pike, who makes brilliance on the Stradivarius look as natural as breathing, seems to inspire the young orchestra and its conductor, the latter smiling like the cat who’s got the musical cream in a perfect double act.

The Cuore’s performance of Mozart’s early landmark Symphony No. 29, K201 (1774) is just as impressive, navigating its mix of serenity and impulsive energy with the right blend of dynamism and grace.

At the end, the ovation for the young faces of the Cuore Chamber Orchestra’s goes on long and hard in the packed auditorium as the rockets continue to fly outside.

This may only be the opening shot of their second season but it's not too early to suggest that at some point London awaits for this superb new orchestra.

Cuore Chamber Orchestra will play Merchant Taylor’s Hall in York on Friday, November 26.
