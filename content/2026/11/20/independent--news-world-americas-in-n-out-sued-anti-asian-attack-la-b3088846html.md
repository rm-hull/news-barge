A California mother has filed a lawsuit against In-N-Out Burger, accusing employees at a Los Angeles restaurant of failing to intervene or contact police during a prolonged anti-Asian attack against her and her 17-year-old daughter.

Julie Choi filed the complaint Wednesday in Los Angeles County Superior Court, seeking unspecified damages for emotional distress as well as punitive damages for alleged misconduct by the fast-food chain, as reported by the *Los Angeles Times*.

The lawsuit stems from a May incident at a Sherman Oaks location, where Choi had taken her daughter to celebrate the end of a difficult school exam. According to the complaint, the altercation began when a female customer pushed Choi’s daughter into people standing in line ahead of her.

When Choi turned to the woman and asked, “Did you push my daughter?” the woman allegedly replied, “Yeah, b\*\*\*\*,” used an anti-Asian slur and advanced toward them aggressively.

A security guard stepped between the woman and the pair, though the complaint states the assailant continued to “menacingly approach and taunt” them. Choi said she asked the guard to remove the woman from the building, but he told her he was not permitted to physically handle the customer.

![In-N-Out Burger is facing a lawsuit from a California mother who claims staff at a Sherman Oaks location stood by as an aggressive customer shoved her 17-year-old daughter, shouted anti-Asian slurs and poured soda on them before being served food](https://static.independent.co.uk/2024/02/22/10/iStock-1222630411.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

For several minutes, the woman allegedly trailed the mother and daughter inside the restaurant, yelling racist remarks, mocking East Asian accents and languages, and calling them “entitled Asian b\*\*\*\*\* s,” according to the court filing.

Despite the ongoing harassment, employees allegedly allowed the angered woman to place a food order, pay in cash and take an empty fountain drink cup.

Bystander video reviewed by the *Los Angeles Times* captured an In-N-Out worker handing the woman her change while she continued to shout expletives and a racial slur. The woman then filled the cup at the soda machine and dumped the liquid over Choi and her daughter before walking out of the restaurant while still yelling slurs, the lawsuit states.

After the woman left, Choi spoke with the restaurant manager, who claimed he had already phoned the police but was told officers would not respond because neither Choi nor her daughter required paramedics. Addressing the guard's inaction, the manager told Choi that “security can’t touch anyone unless an aggressor touches someone first,” according to the complaint.

![A complaint filed in Los Angeles County Superior Court claims a restaurant manager falsely told Julie Choi he had called the police, despite LAPD confirming no calls were logged from the business that night](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/20/16/54/GettyImages-1025440958.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&trim=0%2C372%2C0%2C0&auto=webp)

Choi pointed out that the assailant had shoved her daughter twice and thrown soda on them in direct view of the guard. The manager said he would file an internal report, advised them to leave and walked them to their vehicle, later sending Choi a text message stating that officers never arrived.

However, when Choi contacted the Los Angeles Police Department the following day, she learned that no calls had been placed from the restaurant the previous evening, the complaint alleges. LAPD Lt. Alondra Garcia confirmed to the *Los Angeles Times* that no call was logged from the restaurant's address that night, though police did receive a 7:52 p.m. report regarding a battery investigation at the nearby intersection of Van Nuys Boulevard and Moorpark Street. Garcia noted there were no additional records detailing that call or whether officers were dispatched.

Police later took a statement at Choi’s home and informed her that the incident constituted a hate crime, according to the lawsuit, though the suspect remains unidentified.

![According to the lawsuit, an on-duty security guard stood by as a woman followed the Korean American mother and her teen daughter around the Los Angeles restaurant, stating he was not permitted to physically remove the aggressor](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/20/16/26/GettyImages-1955867930.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&trim=0%2C4%2C82%2C0&auto=webp)

Choi, a Korean immigrant who moved to the U.S. in the 1980s, said her daughter collapsed in tears after the confrontation and remains anxious in public spaces six months later.

“I felt just so sad for her that she had to see this ugly side of humanity,” Choi told the *Los Angeles Times*. “Whenever we go anywhere now, she looks around the room to see if there’s anything she should be concerned about. What happened to me and my daughter shouldn’t happen to anyone else because of the color of their skin or where they’re from.”

In-N-Out spokesperson Patty Peña declined to comment on the allegations, citing the pending litigation.

The lawsuit follows a separate high-profile incident of anti-Asian harassment at a Northern California In-N-Out in 2022, in which a man was arrested on two hate crime charges in Contra Costa County after directing racist and homophobic remarks at two Korean American college students filming a video.
