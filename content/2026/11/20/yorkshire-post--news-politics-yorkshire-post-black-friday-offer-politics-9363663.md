# Black Friday 2026 Offer: Yorkshire Post £12 for a year - here’s 12 politics stories worth subscribing for

## The Yorkshire Post has launched a limited time Black Friday deal offering a digital subscription for just £1 a month – only £12 for an entire year.

It gives readers access to all our paywalled stories, which includes almost all of our politics content including exclusive interviews with the Prime Minister, Chancellor, mayors and the region’s MPs.

We’re really proud of the award-winning reporting we do at The Yorkshire Post and are excited to be able to share it with even more people thanks to this offer.

People often ask why some of our stories are behind a paywall – the answer is that by focusing on subscribers rather than chasing clicks we can continue to pursue the original reporting and high-quality journalism that The Yorkshire Post has delivered for more than 270 years.

For several hundred years The Yorkshire Post has had a correspondent in Westminster, and I’m really proud to continue to fulfill that historic role.

At a time when almost all other regional newspapers have cut their reporters in Parliament, The Yorkshire Post chooses to continue to invest in that coverage which means we continue to hold power to account on behalf of the region.

However, that costs money so if you value the work that we do please consider subscribing to allow us to keep on pursuing original reporting. To give you a flavour of what new subscribers can expect, here are 12 of our recent premium politics stories – everything from investigations to in-depth interviews:

![The Great Northern Conference 2024 saw Chancellor Rachel Reeves interviewed by Yorkshire Port Westminster correspondent Ralph Blackburn](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOjFiMzAzZDBiLThkNDgtNDZlYy05MjMzLWIzNWEzNGQxNjA3Yjo1ZjNlMzk0My05MTUxLTQ5ZWYtOTE0ZS1hNDMzMjFiMTdiYTE=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


In January, The Yorkshire Post reported that Yorkshire rail upgrades would be prioritised in Northern Powerhouse Rail, with an exclusive interview with then-prime minister Sir Keir Starmer.

This story revealed that NPR would not be the high-speed line, first put forward by then-Chancellor George Osborne in 2014, but instead will aim to create a “turn up and go” railway across Leeds, Liverpool, Manchester, York and Sheffield, that mirrors London’s transport network.

In February, The Yorkshire Post spoke to a number of parents who have lost babies relating to poor care within Leeds Teaching Hospitals NHS Trust, and all said they had lost faith in then-health secretary Wes Streeting.

This reporting led to Mr Streeting U-turning on his choice for chair of the independent review into maternity and neonatal services at LTHT and backing the bereaved parents’ candidate Donna Ockenden.

Later that month, The Yorkshire Post reported how the Prime Minister suspended a Bradford councillor with a violent criminal conviction from the Labour Party, after the case was raised in Parliament.

Following this reporting, Coun Mohsin Hussain told this paper that he would not be standing in the upcoming local elections.

In March, The Yorkshire Post reported how Reform UK’s Luke Campbell was facing criticism after the Olympic gold medallist missed the last two mayoral council meetings with senior Cabinet ministers. This precipitated the media scrutiny which uncovered a police investigation.

In April, The Yorkshire Post revealed that the secret Whitehall review into West Yorkshire’s proposed mass transit system has urged mayor Tracy Brabin to consider using buses over trams due to a “significantly less” cost.

Ahead of the local elections, The Yorkshire Post exclusively reported how Reform deputy leader Richard Tice defended the party’s selection of a Sheffield City Council candidate accused of praising the Nazis, saying “we’re all human”. Nathaniel Menday, who was eventually elected, was suspended by the party following further reporting.

![Kevin Hollinrake, Thirsk and Malton MP, being interviewed by Ralph Blackburn, Yorkshire Post Westminster Correspondent, in Harome. After the May elections, The Yorkshire Post exclusively revealed that Leeds United had signed a memorandum of understanding with West Yorkshire Mayor Tracy Brabin to support with her tram plan. We also unveiled an exclusive new image of the Elland Road redevelopment, which shows how intertwined the mass transit project is.

After Andy Burnham became Prime Minister, The Yorkshire Post was one of the first regional papers to interview him, getting his thoughts on the Doncaster Sheffield Airport reopening. This came after earlier in the year we had revealed details of the punitive lease agreed, putting public scrutiny on the amount of taxpayer money and risk that was being put into the airport.

Later that month, I carried out an exclusive interview with Alison Lowe ahead of her peerage. From fighting in Seacroft to representing abuse victims on the national stage, she told me how she wants to take her “Leeds values” to the House of Lords.

A special Yorkshire Post investigation into the transport difficulties between Sheffield and Manchester revealed that South Yorkshire Mayor Oliver Coppard is considering private sector proposals for a road tunnel under the Woodhead Pass.

In October, The Yorkshire Post exclusively revealed that Bradford Interchange would be the site for a new station in the city. Speaking to the paper, Transport Secretary Heidi Alexander said it would mean more frequent trains, improved journey times and better reliability.

As part of a special investigation into the maternity services in Leeds, The Yorkshire Post revealed that the new midwifery director at Leeds Teaching Hospitals held senior roles at another NHS trust during the time of a critical review into its maternity ward. Sascha Wells-Munro also signed an open letter which appeared to dispute that “normal birth” ideology exists in the NHS.
