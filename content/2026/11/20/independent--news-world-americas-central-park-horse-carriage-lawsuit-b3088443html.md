A federal lawsuit filed in Manhattan is seeking to stop New York City from banning horse-drawn carriage rides in Central Park. The union representing the carriage drivers filed the case on Thursday, minutes after Mayor Zohran Mamdani signed a bill to wind down the industry.

The law is set to end the carriage rides by June 2028. It was approved after the death of a teenager last spring. The union argues the city is taking away its members' livelihoods and wants the ban struck down.

“The City’s approach can be summarized in twelve words: immediately cripple the owners, ignore property interests and contracts, and maybe compensate,” the lawsuit reads. The lawsuit argues the law is unconstitutional and that the city violated local legal procedures when it approved it.

It names the mayor and various other city officials as defendants. A spokesperson for the city's law department said it is reviewing the case. The legal challenge is the latest turn in a long fight over the carriage rides.

Animal welfare activists and industry supporters have tussled over whether the rides amount to animal cruelty or are simply a romantic throwback to the city's past.

At a bill-signing ceremony at City Hall on Thursday, Mamdani lauded the role horses played in the city's development. But he said the animals should no longer be hauling tourists around a perpetually busy Central Park among joggers, cyclists and other passersby.

“Our city has changed and now it is time for our laws to change, too,” said Mamdani, a Democrat. City lawmakers reignited their push for a ban earlier this year in response to the death of 18-year-old Indian tourist Romanch Mahajan.

Mahajan died after jumping from a runaway carriage in what appeared to be the first fatality in a horse carriage accident since they were introduced in Central Park. Over the years, horses have collapsed or gotten startled and run off into cars or other objects.

In response, the city has put various safety regulations in place for the industry, including veterinary checkups, vacation time for horses and a retirement age for the animals. City Hall has promised to release a career transition plan for those in the carriage industry next April.

The plan includes job training, educational pathways and stipends. Drivers argue their future remains uncertain. "Despite the good intentions of the mayor, the protections he outlined recently should have been written into the legislation," said John Samuelsen, head of the Transport Workers Union, which represents carriage drivers.
