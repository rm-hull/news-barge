A federal judge stated Friday she will issue a preliminary injunction blocking construction of President Donald Trump’s proposed triumphal arch, remarking during a hearing that it was a "rushed job" that lacked a legal basis.

During a court hearing, U.S. District Judge Leonie Brinkema cited multiple defects in the federal government’s economic and environmental impact assessments, including an inadequate explanation regarding nearby Reagan Washington National Airport.

"That just hasn’t been done in the case because it’s been such a rushed job," the judge said.

In September, the Federal Aviation Administration concluded the arch would not pose an aircraft hazard, though it required an "eternal flame" on top.

The FAA determination mandates a light atop the structure alongside floodlight projectors pointed directly at the arch to remain visible to pilots.

The proposed arch would sit 3,000 feet from Reagan National within the primary approach and departure corridor, prompting safety concerns from several aviation experts.

![](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/09/21/12/17/GettyImages-2284394681.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Brinkema said the government’s argument that a 1925 statute meant Congress had authorized the project made no sense.

"It hurts your credibility with the court by focusing on that," the judge told government lawyers. "This is a project that lacks congressional approval."

Earlier this month, US District Judge Tanya Chutkan in Washington issued a separate order temporarily blocking construction of the proposed 250-foot arch near Arlington National Cemetery, marking another setback for Trump's architectural plans in the capital.

The White House and FAA did not immediately comment.

Trump has encountered notable judicial resistance to projects pursued in Washington during his second term, including a White House ballroom, a Lincoln Memorial Reflecting Pool renovation, and a proposed championship-level golf course 2 miles from the White House.
