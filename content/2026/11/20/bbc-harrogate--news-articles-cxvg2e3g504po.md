Repairing Viaduct Terrace in Knaresborough is said to be complex due to the location beside the river and beneath a railway line

**The expected cost of rebuilding a collapsed riverside walkway in Knaresborough has risen to more than £1m, councillors will be told.**

A wall and part of the riverbank fell into the River Nidd in the North Yorkshire market town in February following a landslip.

"People are always asking me when it's going to get fixed. It does look awful," said Liberal Democrat councillor Matt Walker, citing "delay after delay".

North Yorkshire Council officers said the operation was complex because of its location and that "extensive" design and engineering work had taken place.

Walker, who represents Knaresborough West, previously called for repair work to be carried out before the winter to prevent further weather damage.

Speaking on Friday, he said: "I wouldn't be surprised if when we've got high waters things get washed away.

"That's the nature of rivers, they're quite fierce when they flood."

Repair work is due to start in July, 17 months after the collapse took place.

Walker said this would cause disruption during the busy summer period, when tourists flock to cafés, shops and boat hire businesses near the viaduct.

"We need to make sure the impact is minimised so that any people going down there, first of all they're safe, and they can access it and go to those shops."

He said he had been told the construction work would take approximately 100 days.

![A man, Matt Walker, wears a yellow jacket and looks into the camera. He is standing next to the collapsed walkway.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/a17c/live/8adcc7c0-e41f-11f1-99b6-a35a62fa4264.jpg)

Knaresborough residents are "very frustrated", councillor Matt Walker says

In September, North Yorkshire Council appointed a contractor to repair the failed retaining wall at Viaduct Terrace, with an estimated cost of £350,000.

A recently published report said it would now be likely to cost more than £1m, with the aim to "develop a permanent solution that addresses the underlying causes of the failure rather than providing a short-term repair".

"I think it will overrun on budget and overrun on time, as these projects often do," Walker said.

"They originally talked about £350,000. We're now at over £1m. It'll creep up and I wouldn't be surprised if it takes them at least six months to resolve it."

He said he understood that the council was facing complications, including "environmental issues".

"They've got to make sure that the river is OK for fish spawning, things like that.

"So there are some considerations that they need to take into account, but the sooner they can get it done the better."

According to the Local Democracy Reporting Service, council officers said the scheme was unusually complex because of its location beside the River Nidd, beneath an operational railway viaduct and within a sensitive heritage setting.

The area also contains listed structures, homes and businesses, while the riverside is a popular destination for visitors, the report added.

![A collapsed wall and walkway with fencing at the top, fallen into the river below. There is a yellow floating fencing around it.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/2661/live/0e684280-e426-11f1-a094-5fdc692f3e33.jpg)

Historical structural issues have been identified since the walkway collapsed, a report says

Officers said historic structural issues, erosion around the adjacent viaduct foundations and challenging ground conditions all contributed to the collapse of the wall, which was constructed in 2010.

This was the reason the terrace was closed following the collapse, they explained, adding that surveys, inspections and engineering assessments had since been undertaken.

A ground investigation was scheduled to begin on 7 December, with planned work still subject to approvals from "key partners and stakeholders" including the Environment Agency and Network Rail.

The report is due to be considered by North Yorkshire Council's Harrogate and Knaresborough Area Committee on Thursday, with further updates expected at future meetings.
