# Andy Burnham: Europe ‘ready to enter’ Ukraine war negotiations

## Andy Burnham has said Europe is “ready to enter negotiations” on ending the Ukraine war and that a peace deal must come before talks on Russia’s “unrealistic security demands”.

The Prime Minister was in Berlin to co-chair a meeting of the Coalition of the Willing, a collection of European countries prepared to enforce any peace agreement put in place to end the conflict.

Mr Burnham was joined by Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky and US envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner, while Donald Trump was meeting Vladimir Putin and Xi Jinping in China.

Worryingly for the Europeans, Mr Trump greeted Mr Putin warmly and called him his “friend” ahead of the one-on-one talks.

Mr Trump spoke to him for around 40 minutes in a private meeting, their first since talks in Alaska that failed to broker a peace deal, and also held trilateral talks with the Russian leader and China’s President Xi.

He then dialled into the Coalition of the Willing, with Mr Kushner saying he would be prepared for a Nato-style security guarantee for Ukraine.

Mr Burnham said the US President spoke about “the horrific loss of lives, including the loss of young Russian lives, and he spoke quite passionately about that subject”.

After the meeting, Mr Burnham confirmed that Europe was prepared to enter the negotiations.

Speaking alongside Mr Zelensky, German Chancellor Friedrich Merz and French President Emmanuel Macron, he said: “Not only are we strong and organised, we are also ready to enter negotiations.

“Europe has a clear and consistent and united negotiating position.

“It’s not for the aggressor in this conflict to set out wide-ranging and unrealistic security demands. That much we are all absolutely clear about.

“However, we are ready to enter serious discussions on European security, as long as it is matched with a serious discussion, and crucially, only first if there is a peace agreement between Ukraine and Russia.”

Any peace deal still appears far off, with Ukrainian authorities saying that Russia launched anti-ship missiles and 98 drones at Ukraine, with hits recorded at 18 locations.

At least 10 people were reported wounded as Russian strikes caused damage in 12 regions, Prime Minister Serhii Koretskyi said.

Meanwhile, Ukraine continued targeting the Kerch Bridge between Russia and occupied Crimea.

![Donald Trump and Vladimir Putin greet each other in Shenzhen, China (Vyacheslav Prokofyev, Sputnik, Kremlin Pool Photo via AP)](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/jpim-static/image/2026/11/19/14/AP-2026-11-19-4bc0a73923de4d3d9c70bea77fc90de6.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


Train traffic across the bridge stopped after the strike overnight on Wednesday while authorities assessed the damage, but it was restored yesterday afternoon.

The Kerch Bridge is a key route for military and civilian supplies to Crimea, which Moscow seized in defiance of international law in 2014 and uses to support frontline troops.

It is a potent symbol of Russia’s illegal annexation and has been a prime target for Ukraine.

After the strikes, Mr Putin said: “We will respond to these attacks. Our response will be far more painful than Kyiv’s attack on the bridge.”

Speaking about the war, Mr Burnham said Russia is “losing the battle to break Ukraine”, as he vowed to support Kyiv “for as long as it takes”.

The Prime Minister told the press conference: “Our commitment is there and it will be there going forward, working with all of our colleagues in the coalition of the willing for as long as it takes. And I hope that message is heard very clearly in Moscow.

“For as long as it takes, we will be there at the side of Ukraine and we will not falter.

!Our support will be unwavering, and that’s the biggest weapon we have together: The solidarity between all of Europe, which has been on display today, and I hope that has been seen around the world.

He continued: “Let’s be clear: Russia is losing men, Russia is losing ground, and Russia is losing the battle to break Ukraine because, from what I’ve seen of the resilience of the president (Volodymyr Zelensky), and from what I have seen of the resilience of the Ukrainian people, they are unbreakable, and they should know that with us at their side, they can carry on to fight this with everything that we’ve got.”

In a sign of potential escalation, a Russian drone damaged the Dutch embassy in Kyiv after Moscow suggested the Netherlands was involved in the Kerch Bridge attacks.

Dutch arms firm Destinus revealed this week that its Ruta missiles were used in the strikes.

The Russian embassy in the Netherlands posted on X on Wednesday: “The confession has been well noted. Is The Hague openly joining the conflict?”

Then yesterday, the drone hit the embassy in Kyiv while another was “neutralised” as it circled above, according to Dutch media.

It is unclear if it was intentional, however the incident raises the possibility that Moscow chose to strike a Nato ally inside Ukraine.
