Casa Del Alma has applied to sell alcohol until midnight every day at 10 Princes Square, Harrogate. Comments to North Yorkshire Council close on 4 December.

A venue called Casa Del Alma has applied for a licence to sell alcohol from 10am until midnight, seven days a week, at 10 Princes Square, Harrogate, HG1 1LX.

The applicant is Blue Events 2024 Ltd. Its public notice appeared in the Harrogate Advertiser on 19 November. The application is also on North Yorkshire Council’s list of open Harrogate-area licence applications, where it is a new premises licence.

## What the licence would allow

The council’s register lists:

* sale of alcohol for drinking on the premises only, 10am to midnight, Monday to Sunday
* late night refreshment, 11pm to midnight, Monday to Sunday

Late night refreshment means serving hot food or hot drinks after 11pm. The newspaper notice mentions only the alcohol sales. The council’s entry adds the late night refreshment.

The notice does not ask for live or recorded music.

## Who is behind it

Blue Events 2024 Ltd was registered at Companies House on 14 November 2024. Its registered office is in Darlington. It lists its business as licensed restaurants, takeaway food, event catering and the retail sale of food in specialised shops.

Casa del alma is Spanish for “house of the soul”. We could not find a website or social media page for the venue, and it has not said in public what it will serve or when it plans to open. The notice gives no opening date.

## What it means for you

The deadline for comments is Friday 4 December. You can:

* email [licensing.central@northyorks.gov.uk](mailto:licensing.central@northyorks.gov.uk)
* write to North Yorkshire Council Licensing Team, PO Box 787, HG1 9RW

The full application can be seen at the council’s Civic Centre on St Luke’s Avenue, Harrogate, HG1 2AE. It has been available there since 7 November.

Comments on a premises licence have to relate to the four aims of the licensing law: preventing crime and disorder, public safety, preventing public nuisance and protecting children from harm. If nobody comments, the council must grant the licence as applied for. If comments are made and not settled, the council’s licensing committee decides at a hearing.

“It is an offence knowingly or recklessly to make false statement in connection with an application,” the notice says.

We will report the council’s decision and the opening date once they are published.
