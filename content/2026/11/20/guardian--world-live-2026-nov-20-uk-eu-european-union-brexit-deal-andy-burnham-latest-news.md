'Deal is done,' No10 spokesperson says, but talks 'will continue in some areas'

Peter Walker

Senior political correspondent

The UK and EU have agreed a reset deal on food and drink, emissions trading and a new youth mobility scheme, Downing Street has said, although talks on the level of tuition fees EU students will pay remains unresolved.

“The deal is done – negotiations have concluded,” a No 10 spokesperson said, less than an hour after Andy Burnham arrived at the summit venue in Brussels, where he was greeted by Ursula von der Leyen, the European Commission president, António Costa, the European Council president.

EU’s Ursula von der Leyen, Britain’s Andy Burnham and EU’s António Costa during the EU-UK summit in Brussels. Photograph: Thierry Monasse/Getty Images

It went on:

“Now, both sides will work towards implementation of the youth mobility scheme, the SPS deal and the emissions trading scheme. Both sides have agreed when these will come into force. And those dates are set out in the joint statement, which will be published in due course.

As with all international agreements, there is a legal process in each respective country which will now take place. And talks will continue on some areas – these will be set out in the joint statement later.”

Talks “will continue on some areas”, it added (10:40).

UK officials push back against suggestions reset deals could be jeopardised by tuition fees disagreement

Peter Walker

Senior political correspondent
in Brussels

After the deal was announced, UK officials pushed back strongly against the idea that the three plans could be jeopardised if there was no agreement on tuition fees, saying they were completely separate.

Earlier, EU officials had indicated that progress on the main deals would be contingent on tuition fees.

But while talks on tuition fees are scheduled to last another six months, even if there was not agreement by March 2028, when the new arrangements on food and agriculture come into force, this would still happen, UK officials insisted.

They said the UK had also won agreement in principle for sign-up to the EU’s still-to-be finalised “Made in Europe” policy, a protectionist plan intended to limit China’s increasing influence on European industry, with one official calling this “a big win”, especially as this had happened before a deal on tuition fees.

The two sides are now taking part in a plenary session with all relevant commissioners, ministers and other officials.

We are expecting to hear from Britain’s PM Burnham and EU’s von der Leyen and Costa about the details of their discussions later today.

Tuition fees hold up mostly political - snap analysis

Jakub Krupa

Just to highlight Jennifer’s point there (10:40): the “hold-up” over tuition fees is nowhere near as catastrophic or dramatic as some headlines elsewhere would have you think – and, in fact, was pretty anticipated.

The three sectoral deals are still getting signed today and it counts as a genuine step forward in the post-Brexit relationship; it’s just that their full implementation get suspended until the tuition fee issue is resolved.

The explicit link on their implementation does create a new high-pressure political deadline in six months’ time, adding urgency for the two sides to resolve the tuition fees issue and unblock the whole process.

Ah, classic EU-UK negotiations stuff. Nothing is agreed until everything is agreed.

Just like the good old days (gets Brexit flashbacks).

Three sectoral deals to be signed, but won't enter into force until tuition fees issue resolved

Jennifer Rankin

in Brussels

Andy Burnham’s Brexit reset deals will not materialise unless the UK reaches a settlement with the EU over tuition fee rates for European students.

EU sources said on Friday that the three deals – on food and drink, emissions trading and youth mobility – will not enter into force without equal treatment for European students at British universities.

In a statement seen by the Guardian from the EU council and European Commission spelt it out:

“The Council and Commission consider that this agreement is to provide for equal treatment with nationals as regards tuition fees for all students. The Council and Commission acknowledge that these four agreements form a package of the 2026 EU-UK summit.”

The EU wants to ensure its students pay UK domestic rates of tuition fees, rather than the far higher international rate. In a concession last night, the bloc agreed to defer agreement on tuition fees by six months, but that does not mean they have accepted the UK’s position that their students pay higher rates

As the Guardian has reported, it intends to use its leverage, by making the three deals contingent on an acceptable agreement on tuition fees.

An EU official said:

“I think our position has been clear. We think this is an issue that needs to be addressed.”

The European Commission president Ursula von der Leyen and the European Council president António Costa are now in position and waiting for UK’s Andy Burnham.
