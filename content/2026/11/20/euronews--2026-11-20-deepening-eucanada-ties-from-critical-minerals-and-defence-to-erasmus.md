**Andrzej Halicki, Zeljana Zovko, Łukasz Kohut and Loucas Fourlas of the European People's Party Group (EPP, Christian Democrats) in the European Parliament**

## ‘We are determined to drive the renewed political momentum between the EU and Canada forward,’ write four Members of the European People’s Party (EPP) Group in the European Parliament in an op-ed for Euronews, published on the occasion of a visit by an EPP delegation to Canada on 19–21 November.

Europe and Canada have always been natural allies, through both good times and bad, and are growing even closer. It´s not only politics, trade and security, but culture, sports and way of life that joins us. Next year will mark a historic milestone when Canada will compete at the Eurovision Song Contest for the first time. May the best song win!

## Canada is a trusted democratic partner

For Europe, Canada is a trusted democratic partner. As Canada´s Prime Minister Mark Carney said when addressing the European Parliament: “Our commitment to the rule of law, and our unyielding defence of human dignity, individual freedom, and democracy share the same deep roots, the same unwavering dedication”. And those shared values are translating into concrete cooperation.

Trade is obvious area where the partnership has delivered tangible results. Since the provisional application of Canada-European Union Comprehensive Economic and Trade Agreement (CETA), bilateral trade in goods and services has grown by around 80%, rising from €72.1 billion in 2016 to €130 billion in 2025.

## More opportunities lie ahead: critical raw materials and defence

And more opportunities lie ahead. For example, Europe is seeking to reduce its dependence on China, particularly in the supply of critical raw materials. Fortunately, Canada is one of the few democratic partners with the resources and capacity to supply the EU with minerals essential to batteries, semiconductors, defence, aerospace, renewable energy and telecommunications.

Canada is also a major producer of uranium, cobalt, nickel and zinc, as well as the world's leading producer of potash.

During this time of Russian aggression, defence and security are of the utmost importance. Europe must build a circle of trusted friends around our continent and, here, Canada plays a vital role. Canada has stood firmly with Ukraine and contributes directly to European security through its leadership of NATO operations in Latvia.

Canada’s defence spending reached 2.0% of GDP this year, and its new federal budget sets out a path towards more than 4% by the end of the decade. This is an ambitious commitment, bringing Canada closer to the level of investment pursued by frontline allies like Poland.

Earlier this year, Canada also became the first non-European country to join the €150 billion defence procurement instrument, SAFE, though more remains to be done to better integrate Canada into European defence markets and defence industry development.

## Education and research offer other chances for mutual benefits

Education offers another chance for mutual benefits. Canada consistently performs well in international education, remaining among the strongest OECD countries. According to PISA 2025, it outperforms most EU Member States in science and reading.

We can deepen this relationship by welcoming Canada into the Erasmus+ exchange scheme, one of the most tangible benefits of European integration for young people. Following the launch of formal negotiations at the recent Canada-EU Summit in Montréal, Canada is poised to become the first non-European country associated with the programme, which funds people and organisations to study and train abroad.

Research cooperation is also moving forward. In 2024, Canada became associated with Pillar II of Horizon Europe, which supports collaborative research across a wide scientific and technological fields. Although this is an important milestone, there is still room to strengthen Canada’s participation. Negotiations on deeper participation have now begun and we fully support Canada’s closer integration into Horizon Europe.

## Europe and Canada have every reason to stand together

Generations of Europeans have made Canada their home, contributing to Canadian society while maintaining ties with their countries of origin. These communities help sustain cultural links, connect businesses and create opportunities for younger generations to study and work on both sides of the Atlantic.

The case for a stronger EU–Canada partnership is clear. From critical minerals and trade to defence, education and research, there is considerable scope to work closer together. Great partnerships, like great oaks, do not grow overnight. But with the right foundations, they can weather even the roughest political storms. We must make full use of the opportunities available under our existing treaties and agreements. Whether we describe this relationship as an alliance or an association matters less than what we achieve together.

As the strongest political force in the EU political system with majorities in the main institutions, the Commission, the Council and the Parliament, the EPP is an effective broker. We are determined to drive the renewed political momentum between the EU and Canada forward. In an increasingly uncertain world, Europe and Canada have every reason to stand together, whatever the political weather. The timing of our visit couldn’t be better.

*Andrzej Halicki, MEP, Vice-president, EPP Group*
