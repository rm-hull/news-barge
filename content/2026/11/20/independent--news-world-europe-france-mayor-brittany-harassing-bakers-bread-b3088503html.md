A French mayor has been given an eight-month suspended prison sentence for harassment against local bakers for refusing to slice their loaves of bread.

Pierre Thomas was issued the sentence by the Rennes Criminal Court on Thursday, was fined €5,000, ordered to pay €11,200 in compensation to the couple owning the bakery, and banned from contacting them for three years, *actu.fr* reported. Mr Thomas denied any harassment.

The 67-year-old was alleged to have criticised the business practices of the couple, including the quality of their baked goods and how they were presented to customers. The couple were left with “traumatic stress”, the court heard.

Thomas stepped down as mayor of Saint-Ouen-des-Alleux (Ille-et-Vilaine) in March, choosing not to run again following his growing unpopularity among several shop owners in the town.

The disagreements began soon after Henri Virmouneix and Lorenn Piekowsky-Delarue took over the bakery in the Place de l'Eglise in late 2022.

![Saint Ouen des Alleux town hall](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/20/9/32/Screenshot-2026-11-20-085932.png?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

After the village council bought the bakery from its previous owners, a dispute over the lease of the bakery and an adjoining residential accommodation.

“Disagreements” soon arose between Thomas and the couple on the “practical operating procedures” of the bakery.

Mr Thomas criticised them for “not slicing the bread” and not offering “traditional pastries”, with Mr Virmouneix and Ms Piekowsky-Delarue saying they were “incessantly harassed and hounded” by Thomas.

“I never expected someone who is an elected official of the Republic to do things like that,” Ms Piekowsky-Delarue told the court in October.

Mr Virmouneix added: "He imposed things on us that have nothing to do with freedom of trade”, criticising the mayor’s "systematic and permanent interference".

They were not the only shopkeepers in the town to file a complaint against the mayor accusing him of “interference”, giving him the nickname of “the king”.

Thomas claimed that he was the victim of a “conspiracy”, according to *Ouest France*. and denied harassing the bakers. He said only “made a remark” to the “very, very aggressive, anti-business” bakers. He reportedly told the court that they “lost all their customers” through their own fault,* actu.fr* added.

He has claimed the harassment allegations had been fabricated by a “cabal” of “incompetent shopkeepers”, who mounted a campaign against him.

The couple closed down their bakery in July 2023, just two months after opening.

In October that year, Thomas and three local officials turned up with a drill to try and force open the lock of the shop, arguing the premise belonged to the council, the court heard.

The judge described this as “questionable coming from elected officials”, according to *The Times*.

The state prosecutor said Thomas’ defence in court demonstrated his “feeling of being all-powerful” and accused him of a “punitive expedition”, the outlet added.
