The US has issued sanctions on an Australian motorcycle gang with alleged links to drug trafficking.

The US Treasury has hit alleged members of the Comanchero motorcycle gang with financial sanctions, accusing them of assisting criminal cartels to traffic drugs and grow their operations worldwide.

The move was announced on Thursday.

According to the Treasury, the Comanchero has links to US-sanctioned criminal groups in Mexico and South America.

Those ties, it said, have facilitated the shipment of hundreds of kilograms of cocaine from South America to the United States, Australia and Europe.

The gang has also engaged in assassinations, arms trafficking and money laundering, said the US Treasury, ⁠which also sanctioned Italian mafia syndicate 'Ndrangheta ​in connection ⁠with Comanchero.

"Cartels do not operate in isolation. They rely on other criminal organisations like Comancheros and 'Ndrangheta to move drugs, launder illicit ⁠proceeds, and ultimately poison Americans with narcotics," Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said.

The Australian Federal Police said in a statement on Friday that it is the first time the US has sanctioned individuals linked to an Australian-based organised crime group.

![New Zealand Police carried out a series of raids across Auckland this year, the culmination of a year-long investigation into the activities of the Comanchero Motorcycle Gang](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/20/09/1141821574..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Any assets the individuals hold in the US, or that people in the country control on their behalf, are frozen under the sanctions.

Their Australian assets are unaffected.

However, Australian banks, which access the US financial system to move money around the world, may be cautious about providing services or processing transactions for individuals subject to US sanctions.

"These sanctions will constrain the Comanchero's access to the international financial system and increase the risks for those who enable or do business with it," Australian Federal Police Assistant Commissioner Stephen Nutt said.

"Our objective is to make it harder, riskier and ‌more expensive for the Comanchero to operate."

US President Donald Trump has escalated his crackdown on drug trafficking since returning to the presidency in January 2025.

He has designated cartels as foreign terrorist organisations.

He has also authorised dozens of strikes on vessels in the Caribbean and eastern Pacific that US officials have said were carrying drugs destined for the United States.

Human rights groups have contended the strikes, which have killed more than 220 people, constitute extrajudicial killings.
