## Lana Nusseibeh, the Emirati star diplomat running to succeed António Guterres, told Euronews the UN must return to its core purpose of peace, development, and prosperity. She has pledged relentless diplomacy, a leaner UN judged on results, and early action on wars from Ukraine to Gaza if chosen.

As the race to elect the next UN Secretary-General enters its final phase, UAE Minister of State Lana Nusseibeh, who is running to succeed António Guterres, has pledged to serve as the UN's chief mediator and take political risks for peace if chosen for the organisation's top post.

Speaking in a wide-ranging interview with Euronews, Nusseibeh, who served as the UAE's ambassador to the UN for more than a decade, said the organisation must get back to its core purpose of bringing governments and people together for peace, development and prosperity to restore its credibility.

"I think it's a turning point moment for the United Nations," she said.

"I am a relentless diplomat and I intend to offer a candidacy that is about relentless diplomacy," Nusseibeh told Euronews.

Nusseibeh said the UN was too often seen as absent from the 65 active conflicts she said exist worldwide, and that the next secretary-general should act as mediator in chief, pursuing humanitarian truces and aid access first, then a political strategy to make them last.

"It's not about political theatre, it's about political risk-taking," she said.

With official development assistance down 23%, Nusseibeh said the UN would have to narrow its scope, partner with others where they are better placed, and publish a scorecard of results for member states and donors.

"It's not around organograms or design structures of the system, but it's about impact and cost of impact and delivery," she said.

In her first 100 days, she said she would agree on a shared vision with member states and civil society, cut duplication across the thousands of mandates given to the secretariat and move the UN's focus from headquarters to the field.

## First woman to lead the UN?

If chosen, Nusseibeh would become the first woman to lead the UN since its establishment in 1945, the first of Palestinian descent, and the second secretary-general from the Arabic-speaking world after Egypt's Boutros Boutros-Ghali.

"But I'm not running because I'm a woman. I'm running because of over two decades of experience and judgment in international diplomacy," Nusseibeh said.

Asked about critics who have questioned her independence, Nusseibeh pointed to the UN Charter. She has taken leave from her post as UAE minister of state to campaign.

"Article 100 of the UN Charter stipulates the independence of a secretary-general," she said.

"So the allegiance of a secretary-general is to the UN Charter and to international law and that is what I would pledge in that role," she said.

"I welcome the criticism, I welcome feedback, I will always welcome the feedback if I am in this role as well," she added.

Nusseibeh said the next secretary-general must combine public advocacy for peace with discreet diplomacy.

"Sometimes quiet diplomacy is needed, keeping channels open to all parties when the doors are closed," she said.

She said she would set up a mediation board of outside experts and UN officials, draw on local community leaders and make women's networks the first point of contact for her mediation team. Nusseibeh would also create a focal point in her own office for the protection of aid workers, 300 of whom she said had been killed in the past year.

"I would continue to try relentlessly until there is an outcome that serves the people on the ground better," she said.

## Approaching conflict 'with a lot of humility'

On Ukraine, she said any settlement must rest on the UN Charter's principles of sovereignty and territorial integrity.

"I would offer my good offices immediately," she said. "Where that is welcomed, I would come with options, practical options for how to move from first agreements to a political strategy."

Nusseibeh said 38% of the Security Council's agenda concerned the Middle East, a region she said had seen generations of war.

A fluent Hebrew speaker and a highly regarded diplomat who grew up between Abu Dhabi and the UK in a family of well-known Palestinian political figures, Nusseibeh previously served as the UAE ambassador to the UN and is credited with elevating the UAE's profile during that time.

"One of my driving purposes in seeking this role is to try and pass on a region that is more stable, more prosperous for my own children," she said.

On Gaza, Nusseibeh cited Security Council Resolution 2720, which she negotiated in December 2023 after taking council ambassadors to the Rafah crossing. She said Resolution 2803, which endorsed a 20-point plan for Gaza, had stalled.

"As Secretary General, I would want to see that through in terms of implementation," she said.

Nusseibeh, who is of Palestinian heritage with family roots in Jerusalem, said the issue was both professional and personal for her.

"I come to every conflict with a lot of humility that what we have done in all of these situations has not been nearly enough for the people who've needed it the most," she said.

The Security Council has held repeated informal straw polls, with no candidate yet clearing opposition from all five permanent members, and Guterres's term is due to end on 31 December.

## Race remains tight

In the closely watched UN race, former Costa Rican Vice President Rebeca Grynspan still holds a challenging lead as Argentina’s International Atomic Energy Agency Director-General Rafael Grossi and UAE Minister of State Lana Nusseibeh actively campaign to emerge as winners.

The growing excitement around the race is also fueled by the prospect of a female UN secretary-general, something that has never happened in the UN's more than 80-year history.

Campaigning alongside Grynspan, Nusseibeh and Rossi are Guyana’s former Foreign Minister Carolyn Rodrigues-Birkett, former Ecuadorian Foreign Minister Maria Fernanda Espinosa, former Senegalese President Macky Sall, former UN Under-Secretary-General Olara Otunnu of Uganda, and Ecuadorian diplomat Ivonne A-Baki.

Costa Rica's former Vice President Rebeca Grynspan, 70, was born to Jewish parents who fled Poland before World War II. She lost her grandparents in the Holocaust after they decided to remain in Poland.

In a May interview with Euronews, Grynspan said, “I know exactly what wars take away from you and what peace allows you to do.”

“I am a generation that have lived in peace. I have never experienced war directly. But millions of people in the world today live in war zones, in conflict. And that is why I am running, because I know the difference, what peace and opportunity can do for you and your family,” Grynspan told Euronews at the time.
