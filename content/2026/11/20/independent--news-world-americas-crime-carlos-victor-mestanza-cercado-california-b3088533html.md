A ringleader behind a $100m jewellery heist in California has pleaded guilty to a federal robbery charge, in what prosecutors described as the largest theft of its kind in US history.

Carlos Victor Mestanza Cercado, 33, admitted his role in stealing approximately $100m (£76m) worth of precious gems, fine jewellery and luxury watches from a Brink's armoured vehicle in July 2022.

The Ecuadorean national was one of seven men indicted over the robbery, which took place at a highway rest stop in Kern County, north of Los Angeles.

Mestanza was arrested two years ago in Panama while on a stopover during a flight from Guatemala to Ecuador.

The robbery took place on 11 July 2022, when an armoured truck transporting 73 sacks of jewellery and precious stones from a major exhibition in the San Francisco Bay Area to a storage facility in Southern California was targeted.

According to authorities, Mestanza and his accomplices tracked the shipment for approximately 300 miles (480km), using multiple vehicles and coordinating their movements by cellphone.

The group broke through the locks of the truck while it was parked at a rural rest stop in Kern County, making off with 24 bags of valuables.

The stolen collection included gold, diamonds, rubies, emeralds and luxury watches, with its value estimated at approximately $100m.

The US Attorney's Office in Los Angeles described the theft as the largest jewellery heist on record in the country.

Under a plea agreement signed earlier this month, Mestanza pleaded guilty to one count of interference with commerce by robbery, a felony carrying a maximum sentence of 20 years in prison.

In exchange for his guilty plea, prosecutors agreed to drop several other charges he faced. He is due to be sentenced on 15 April.

Mestanza also admitted taking part in a series of highway robberies targeting trucks carrying electronics in March 2022, alongside his co-defendants.

A statement from the US Attorney's Office announcing the plea described him as the “lead defendant in the case”.

Three of his co-defendants are due to stand trial in federal court in Los Angeles in March 2027. Two other accused members of the group remain at large, while a seventh has been deported.

It remains unclear whether any of the jewellery stolen in the $100m robbery has been recovered.
