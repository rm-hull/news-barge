# M18: Tributes paid to “truly special” father-of-two after crash involving horses

## The family of a “truly special” man killed in a collision on the M18 involving horses has paid tribute to him and said he “made people’s lives better”.

On November 13 2026, at 4.44am, two collisions occurred at Junction Four of the motorway involving two loose horses.

Train manager Johnny Brown, 50, from Epworth, was the driver of a grey Skoda Superb that collided with one of the horses in the carriageway.

He suffered fatal injuries and was pronounced dead at the scene, South Yorkshire Police said.

On Thursday (Nov 19), his family shared a tribute to their much-loved son, partner, dad, brother, uncle and friend.

They said they were devastated by his death and struggling to come to terms with the loss.

The statement continued: “As a brother to David, Kath and his twin Eddie, he was at the heart of our close-knit family and a constant source of love, support and laughter. He was devoted to his partner, Kay, and to his children, George and Emily, who were the centre of his world.

“He was also a devoted son to our mum, Liz, and a constant source of love, support and care in her life. A loving and hands-on father, he cherished every moment spent with his family and loved exploring the Peak District and surrounding countryside with those closest to him.

“Johnny had a remarkable gift for connecting with people. His warmth, humour and kindness made everyone feel valued, and he had a unique ability to lift people's spirits.

“One lifelong friend described him as someone who always made you feel ‘at the centre of his orbit’. The tributes we have received from friends, colleagues and those whose lives he touched have been overwhelming. One colleague said they were ‘better for knowing him’, while another described him as ‘a legend on the railway’.

“They all confirm what we already knew, that Johnny was a truly special man who made people's lives better simply by being in them. We are heartbroken that he was taken from us in such tragic circumstances.”

South Yorkshire Police continue to appeal for anyone who knows who owns the horses, that are described as one being black and one being grey in colour.

Officers said they want to be able to provide answers for Johnny’s family who added: “If anyone has information that could help provide answers about how this happened, we urge them to do the right thing and come forward. Johnny will be missed beyond words and remembered with love every day.”

If you can help officers progress their enquiries, get in touch quoting incident number 94 of November 13, 2026.
