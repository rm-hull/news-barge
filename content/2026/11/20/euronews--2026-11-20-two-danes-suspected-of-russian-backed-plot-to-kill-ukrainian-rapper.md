## Two men linked to one of Denmark's most notorious criminal gangs have been charged over a plot to kill a Ukrainian rapper, prompting a nationwide crackdown on the outlawed group.

Denmark will expel Russia's defence attaché over an alleged Russian-backed plot to assassinate a Ukrainian rapper on Danish soil, Denmark's foreign minister said at a press conference on Friday.

Two Danish men have been charged and remanded in custody in connection with the attempted assassination.

The plot was planned and initiated by Russian intelligence services, according to Denmark's Security and Intelligence Service (PET).

The two men have links to one of Denmark's most notorious criminal gangs, Loyal to Familia (LTF), a statement by Danish police said.

Foreign Minister Lars Løkke Rasmussen said the case would have diplomatic consequences for Moscow.

"He must leave Denmark within seven days," Rasmussen said, referring to the Russian defence attaché.

The alleged plot appears to have been thwarted when the men were arrested shortly before the rapper's scheduled concert on 25 September, according to Danish public broadcaster DR.

The two men have been held in pre-trial detention since September after they were found with weapons in their car in Hellerup, an affluent residential area north of Copenhagen.

Both men have reportedly pleaded not guilty.

Kyivstoner's Danish concert, part of a European tour, was subsequently cancelled.

The rapper said in an Instagram post, which is now unavailable, that Danish intelligence officials had warned him of a threat to his life and prevented him from performing in Denmark, according to local media.

Kyivstoner was also reportedly the target of an assassination plot in Poland earlier this year.

## Denmark steps up crackdown on outlawed gang

Danish police announced intensified nationwide operations against Loyal to Familia on Friday, targeting both its violent activities and sources of income.

"Police will now further intensify their overall efforts in response to this particular case," Lasse Boje, national police commissioner of Danish police, said in the statement.

The operation will also target key figures within the gang, including those based abroad, in an effort to weaken the organisation.

The gang has been involved in several violent conflicts with rival criminal groups and remains active despite being permanently banned by Denmark's Supreme Court in 2021.

Over the past five years, Danish police have brought more than 5,000 criminal charges against members of the gang, involving offences including violent crime, drug trafficking, weapons offences, fraud and money laundering, according to the statement.

Police estimate that around a third of the gang's members are currently behind bars.
