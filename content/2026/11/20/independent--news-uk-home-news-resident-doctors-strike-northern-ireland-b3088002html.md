Resident doctors across Northern Ireland are preparing to go on strike this Friday amid an ongoing dispute over pay.

The 24-hour walkout will run from 7am on Friday until 7am on Saturday November 21, as resident doctors withdraw their labour from both GP surgeries and hospitals.

This industrial action will be followed by specialist and consultant doctors in the region, who are set to stage a strike on Monday November 23.

It marks a second round of strikes over demands for full pay restoration, following a previous 24-hour walkout held by doctors in June this year.

According to Dr Steven Montgomery, chairman of BMA NI resident doctors’ committee, doctors "should not have to take strike action for a second time", though the problem leading them to picket lines during the summer remains unresolved.

“Our pay is eroding and we need to address that in order to retain young doctors in Northern Ireland,” he said.

“When I talk to colleagues it’s clear they want the best for the health service in Northern Ireland and for our patients.

“But it is also clear that they are facing burnout and moral injury and what we are paid does not reflect that.

![‘Our pay is eroding and we need to address that in order to retain young doctors in Northern Ireland,’ Dr Steven Montgomery said](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/06/29/18/2a6bdd2d8c9963e14657e727f72d5d15Y29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzgyODM5MjQy-2.85123583.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“Doctors train for many years, carry enormous responsibility and are asking for the value of their pay to be restored.

“Our members have been very clear that this dispute is about full pay restoration. Receiving the pay award doctors are overdue for this year will not restore the value of the pay that has been lost over many years.

“We are more than willing to discuss with the minister and the department how we can address pay erosion and the steps they need to take to satisfy our members that they are committed to addressing the issue.”

Over the summer, resident doctors in England voted to accept a government offer on pay and working conditions, bringing an end to a year of industrial action.

The decision concludes a dispute that saw the British Medical Association (BMA) Resident Doctor Committee (RDC) orchestrate 21 days of strikes since July 2025.

Resident doctors in England had threatened a four-day strike in June but called it off at the last minute after the government’s offer.

Dr Jack Fletcher, chairman of the BMA’s resident doctors committee (RDC), expressed the union’s long-standing position on industrial action.

He said: "We have always been clear that no strikes needed to go ahead if we received an offer appropriate to put to our members."
