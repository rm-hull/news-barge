Lauren Boebert's time in Congress has ended after voters declined to send her back to Washington, D.C. The Colorado Republican lost to Democrat Eileen Laubacher in a district Boebert had won by double digits two years ago.

About a week after that result, she sat in a U-Haul van in the nation's capital on Thursday, having packed up her congressional office and her “dungeon of an apartment”. She spoke via Zoom from the van, in one of her first interviews since the loss.

“I think about it every day,” Boebert said, referring to the midterm elections in which American voters sent Democrats to Congress en masse. “This could be a referendum on President Trump. It could be the referendum on Congress or the Republican Party,” the Colorado Republican said. Then, lowering her voice, she added, “I think it’s all of it.”

![“I think about it every day,” said Boebert, referring to the midterm elections where American voters sent Democrats to Congress en masse](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/14/16/54/GettyImages-2264752004.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“I’m not mad at the president,” she said, but “the president did some things that hurt my voters.” Boebert was first elected in 2020 and became one of the most vocal and visible members of the “Make America Great Again” movement.

From the time she arrived in Congress, her right-wing righteousness on television and the House floor made headlines. Her years in D.C. roughly traced the arc of that movement – and its supporters' more tenuous relationship with Trump of late – through its apogee in 2024 and its more recent midterm losses.

Four years ago, she described herself on Fox News as “ultra MAGA”, but she has since become less inclined toward definitions. “When Jesus comes back and I’m raptured up, like all these labels are just going to fly off anyway,” she said. “I respect President Trump, I still work with him, I want to continue to work with him. Not for him, with him.”

Boebert says she hasn't changed much in her six years, aside from a few more gray hairs and maybe some “personal growth” on bipartisanship. She said she wouldn't have done anything differently – besides, she joked, “not gone to the theater.”

![Boebert (R-CO) speaks to the members of the media as she leaves the Chappaqua Performing Arts Center on the day of former Secretary of State Hillary Clinton appearance for a deposition in the House Oversight Committee investigation of late financier and convicted sex offender Jeffrey Epstein,](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/20/09/2026-11-13T221735Z_421837789_RC2XTJAYWDEX_RTRMADP_3_USA-ELECTION-COLORADO-BOEBERT.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

She was referring to an incident at a Denver theater where she was caught on camera vaping at a 2023 performance of “Beetlejuice” and escorted out of the venue. The clip was replayed in campaign ads against her, and she partly credits it for her loss.

She felt it was an unfair dredging up of a past she'd already “owned up to.” But she said there could be “a great number of” unaffiliated or Republican voters who, when it came to the scandal, just said, “‘I don’t care if any of this is true or not, I’m tired of the drama.’”

Her former district covered western Colorado, and at the time she apologized for the incident up and down it. She later moved to a more conservative House seat covering the state's Great Plains, where she won in 2024 before losing two years later.

Boebert's opponent raised far more campaign cash than she did. “I didn’t raise millions of dollars and I kinda refused to,” she said. “Colorado voters know me, and, you know, if they know me enough to get rid of me, then I guess that’s my answer.”

![Boebert responded to reporters' questions while pushing a child on a tricycle outside of the building where Hillary Clinton is being deposed](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/02/26/20/52/SEI286912708.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Boebert has broken from Trump at times, typically on topics other Trump supporters also opposed, such as the war in Iran and the administration's treatment of the Jeffrey Epstein files. But she didn't lay the blame for her defeat or other GOP losses squarely on Trump.

“I think it’s more all of us. It’s all of the Republican Party,” she said, citing high gas prices, GOP infighting and unfulfilled promises. “We had solutions and we didn’t take action.” Boebert said she doesn't take her loss as an "L," and instead sees it as a “launching pad” for the future.

She didn't specify what that was, but hinted at it. “Too many people come to Congress and get rich,” she said. “I am not one of those, and I’m gonna go make some money now.”
