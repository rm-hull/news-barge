**The owner of an independent game shop says he has only sold five copies of the highly anticipated open-world action-adventure Grand Theft Auto 6.**

GTA 6, as it is known, is estimated to become the biggest entertainment launch in history following the blockbuster video game's debut on Thursday, after a 13-year wait.

But Richard Tewksbury, who runs Super Game Shack in the Silver Arcade in Leicester, said he had seen "embarrassing" sales of the game because it was only released as a digital download and not on disc.

"People are super, super, super hungry for this game - so it's already doing crazy numbers already - but that's not translated for us," he said.

"I've only sold four copies on PS5 and one copy on Xbox," added Tewksbury.

"It's because it's all digital - no-one sadly wants to buy a code in a box. We gave it a little go, but they just knocked it dead with making it digital. For game shops, it's a write-off.

"I'm really behind the game - I think it's amazing - but for shops, it's just not great."

In June, developer Rockstar Games said physical copies would contain a code for a digital download rather than the more traditional format. The company has been contacted for comment.

Tewksbury said, while he thinks the game is "amazing", the choice to not sell physical copies with discs was "not great" for smaller stores.

The release of the blockbuster came after two delays to its initial launch date in autumn 2025.

In advance of the launch, GTA 6 generated an estimated £1.25bn ($1.65bn) in revenue based on an estimated 17.07 million units sold, sales tracker SensorTower said, external.

However, Tewksbury said sales at local stores had been poor because physical editions contained download codes rather than discs.

"It is possibly the biggest game hype there's ever been - 13 years people have been waiting for this," he said.

![A GTA 6 case](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/4d44/live/179a67f0-e414-11f1-b37c-b3a71d7200af.jpg)

GTA 6 generated an estimated £1.25bn ($1.65bn) in revenue based on pre-sales

GTA 6 is one of the first major games to only be released without a traditional disc version.

However, Tewksbury said the reality for game shops was that this style of release would be the future of the industry.

Many consoles are now without physical disc drives and PlayStation announced in July that its games would no longer be released on discs from January 2028.

Sony, which owns PlayStation, said the move came "as consumer preferences and the broader entertainment industry continue to shift away from physical discs to digital".

"This is the way it's going to be," Tewksbury said.

"It's a big effect for us because it was such a massive game - everyone wants it.

"A lot of people aren't even aware that you could actually get it as a code in a box in a shop.

"Suddenly everyone's already pre-ordered it months in advance because the download is so large and [they] aren't thinking about us."

There was frustration from many gamers when it was announced that there would be no disc copies of GTA 6.

Rob Spence, physical media collector and TikToker, said there was "outrage" over more games being released without a disc.

"There're so many great benefits to physical media," he told BBC Radio Leicester.

"For me personally, it's that ability to touch, to hold, to really admire the artwork that goes into the casing and how it's all put together.

"I always think there's nothing better than walking into a room and seeing a library or a shelf full of great pieces in a collection that can't ever be taken away from you.

"There are so many great retailers out there, like the shop in Leicester, people should go and support them.

"Nothing beats holding the game."

![A headshot of Rob Spence](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/5ef7/live/2f21cce0-e4d4-11f1-b045-89fda867dd9a.jpg)

Rob Spence said the gaming community could lose its "sense of belonging"

Spence, from Rothley in Leicestershire, said town and cities might see "another wave" of closures, if stores did not find ways of further diversifying as sales of physical games decreased.

"It's a very sad thought and unfortunately, this is just the first wave of these types of releases," he said.

"We are now going to see more of this in 2027, and then we're going to see a lot more of it in 2028."

He added the gaming community could lose its "sense of belonging" that came with the stores.

"We all remember that community feel these shops should have, whether you came in at 10am on a Saturday with your pocket money to buy a latest release - we're going to miss all that," he said.

"But we're also now going to miss the midnight releases, when again, there was that sense of community, that sense of belonging.

"That sort of party atmosphere to be one of the first to get your hands on a game - that will all go."
