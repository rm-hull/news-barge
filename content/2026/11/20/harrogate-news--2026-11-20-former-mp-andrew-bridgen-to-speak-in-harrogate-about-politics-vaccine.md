Former Conservative MP Andrew Bridgen is due to speak in Harrogate on Saturday 21 November, bringing with him a political career that has included involvement in the Post Office Horizon scandal and a controversial campaign questioning the safety of Covid-19 vaccines.

Bridgen represented North West Leicestershire from 2010 until 2024. During that time, he became involved in the campaign for justice for sub-postmasters affected by the Post Office Horizon system. Later, his outspoken criticism of the Covid-19 vaccination programme led to the withdrawal of the Conservative whip and his eventual expulsion from the party.

He continues to campaign on vaccine safety, pharmaceutical industry influence and what he sees as a lack of accountability within government and regulatory institutions. His current campaigning positions are set out on his personal website [1].

## Andrew Bridgen and the Post Office Horizon scandal

Bridgen became involved in the Post Office scandal after being approached by constituents affected by the Horizon computer system.

One of those constituents was Michael Rudkin, a former sub-postmaster who became involved in challenging the Post Office over the reliability of the system and the treatment of branch operators.

Bridgen worked alongside other MPs, including James Arbuthnot and Mike Wood, to raise concerns about the Post Office’s treatment of sub-postmasters and the handling of complaints about Horizon.

On 17 December 2014, Bridgen contributed to a Westminster Hall debate on the Post Office Mediation Scheme. He raised concerns about the experiences of sub-postmasters and allegations surrounding the operation of the Horizon system, including the possibility of remote access to branch accounts [2].

He returned to the issue in Parliament in June 2015 during a debate on the Post Office Horizon system, continuing to press for answers about the treatment of affected sub-postmasters [3].

The Horizon scandal subsequently became one of the most significant miscarriages of justice in modern British history, with hundreds of sub-postmasters having been prosecuted or otherwise financially and personally damaged as a result of errors in the system and the Post Office’s actions.

## Bridgen’s challenge to the Covid-19 vaccination programme

Bridgen’s later political profile became increasingly associated with his criticism of the Covid-19 vaccination programme.

He questioned the safety and effectiveness of the vaccines, the way risks were communicated to the public, and whether the balance of benefit and risk had been adequately considered for different age groups.

He also raised questions about natural immunity, the collection and assessment of reports of adverse reactions, and the influence of pharmaceutical companies on public policy.

On 24 October 2022, Bridgen used a Westminster Hall debate to raise concerns about Covid-19 vaccines and their safety. His contribution questioned aspects of the vaccination programme and called for greater scrutiny of the available evidence [4].

On 13 December 2022, he secured a further adjournment debate entitled *Vaccines: Potential Harms*. During the debate, he raised concerns about reported adverse reactions, the monitoring of vaccine safety and the support available to people who believed they had been harmed.

Bridgen called for the suspension of mRNA vaccines, arguing that potential risks were not being given sufficient attention. The then health minister Maria Caulfield responded by defending the regulatory and safety-monitoring processes and the role of the Medicines and Healthcare products Regulatory Agency (MHRA) [5].

## What does the research say about vaccine-related harm?

Some of the concerns raised by Bridgen relate to recognised adverse reactions to Covid-19 vaccines. Myocarditis and pericarditis, which involve inflammation of the heart muscle or surrounding tissue, are recognised rare adverse reactions associated with mRNA vaccines.

The risk is not uniform across the population. Research has identified differences according to age, sex, vaccine type and dose, with young males among the groups in which the association has been most closely examined.

A 2025 systematic review and meta-analysis published in *Epidemiologic Reviews* examined age- and sex-stratified risks of myocarditis and pericarditis attributable to Covid-19 vaccination [6].

The existence of recognised adverse reactions is an important part of the safety evidence. It does not, however, establish every wider claim made about vaccine injury or prove that all reported long-term health problems were caused by vaccination.

The issues Bridgen has continued to raise include how potential harms are identified, how individual cases are investigated, whether people reporting serious reactions receive adequate recognition and support, and whether the public has been given a sufficiently clear account of both benefits and risks.

## The MHRA and questions of regulatory independence

Bridgen has also questioned the independence and accountability of the UK’s medicines regulator, the Medicines and Healthcare products Regulatory Agency.

The MHRA is an executive agency of the Department of Health and Social Care. It has operational autonomy in its regulatory decisions, but it operates within a framework agreed with the department. That framework covers matters including governance, finance, policy and ministerial oversight [7].

This arrangement does not mean that ministers direct individual licensing decisions. However, it does provide the context for questions about the relationship between the regulator, government and the wider pharmaceutical industry.

Bridgen’s criticism has focused on whether the regulatory system has been sufficiently transparent and independent, particularly during the pandemic, and whether concerns about vaccine safety have received adequate scrutiny.

## Expulsion from the Conservative Party

Bridgen’s position on Covid-19 vaccines brought him into conflict with his own party.

In January 2023, he published a social media post comparing the Covid-19 vaccination programme with the Holocaust. The comparison was widely condemned, including by Jewish organisations and political figures.

The Conservative Party withdrew the whip before subsequently expelling him. The controversy marked a decisive break between Bridgen and the party he had represented in Parliament for more than a decade.

He joined the Reclaim Party in May 2023 but left in December of that year. He continued to sit as an independent MP until the 2024 General Election [1].

## Legal dispute with Matt Hancock

Bridgen’s public statements about Covid-19 vaccines also led to a legal dispute with former health secretary Matt Hancock.

The dispute followed a post published by Hancock on 11 January 2023, in which he described Bridgen’s comments as antisemitic, anti-vaccine and anti-scientific conspiracy theories.

Bridgen brought proceedings concerning Hancock’s reference to antisemitism. Hancock relied on the defence of honest opinion under the Defamation Act 2013.

On 29 July 2026, the Court of Appeal ruled in Hancock’s favour in *Andrew Bridgen v Matt Hancock* [2026] EWCA Civ 991 [8].

The judgment concerned the legal issues in the defamation proceedings. It was not a determination of the scientific merits of Bridgen’s wider arguments about Covid-19 vaccines.

## Bridgen’s continuing campaign

Bridgen continues to campaign on vaccine safety and the influence of pharmaceutical companies on public policy.

His current website uses the heading “Expose Vaccine Harm” and advocates the suspension of mRNA treatments in humans and animals. His campaigning also addresses government decision-making, regulatory institutions and what he regards as a lack of accountability within the pharmaceutical industry [1].

These remain controversial positions, particularly his calls for the suspension of mRNA vaccines. They are central to the public profile he has developed since leaving the Conservative Party.

## The 2024 General Election

Bridgen stood as an independent candidate in North West Leicestershire at the 2024 General Election, seeking to retain the seat he had held since 2010.

He received 1,568 votes, approximately 3.2% of the vote, finishing sixth out of seven candidates. Labour’s Amanda Hack won the constituency [9].

The result brought his parliamentary career to an end, although he has continued to speak publicly and campaign on the issues that became central to his final years in Parliament.
