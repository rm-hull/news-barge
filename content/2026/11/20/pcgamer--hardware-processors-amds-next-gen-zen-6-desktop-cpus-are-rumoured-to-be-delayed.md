We got our first technical deep dive into AMD's next-generation Zen 6 CPU architecture last week, in the form of the company's new Venice range of server chips. Now there's a rumour that the consumer Ryzen chip launch may have been pushed back to the second half of 2027.

The new Ryzen offerings were originally thought to be launching in the first half, but Chiphell leaker Zhang Zhonghao says otherwise. In a post spotted by Videocardz, Zhang Zhonghao claims that the desktop chips, codenamed Olympic Ridge, have been switched to TSMC's N2X manufacturing process, and as a result the "timeline has been pushed back" (via machine translation).

Zhang Zhonghao makes mention of Computex in their post, which some are speculating means a launch date after the show in June of next year.

Olympic Ridge was originally though to be using TSMC's N2 or N2P process node, but a switch to the performance-focussed N2X would line up with previous rumours that the chips are targeting very high clockspeeds, potentially all the way up to 6.5 GHz.

N2X is scheduled to enter production in 2027, which would explain the longer timeframe if this rumour proves out. A delay to the second half of next year would mean that Intel's Nova Lake chips, said by the company to be "tracking ahead of schedule" in a recent earnings call, would potentially have the first half of 2026 all to themselves if they launch before Zen 6.

![A photograph of a silicon wafer from TSMC, showing multiple rows of individual dies](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/dack3ubkkCLzGNpiVehom7-1200-80.jpg)

In the battle of the next generation CPUs, Intel would end up with a significant headstart. However, for those thinking about a potential upgrade, it's worth noting that Intel's new chips will use an entirely new socket in the form of LGA 1954. AMD's new offerings will use the existing AM5 socket.

AMD's Jack Huynh posted a cryptic video on X earlier this week, alongside a message declaring that "the next era of computing is going to be special," and that he "can't wait to share a glimpse of what's coming at CES." So, hopefully we might learn more about what's going on with Zen 6 desktop silicon on January 5.
