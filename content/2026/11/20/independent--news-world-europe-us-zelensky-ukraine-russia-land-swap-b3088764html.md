Donald Trump’s top foreign envoys have suggested that Ukraine could still join the Nato military alliance in closed-door talks over the past few months, according to a shocking new report.

The idea of granting Ukraine membership to the bloc has long been considered dead in the water, a red line for a Russia that has opposed even the idea of deploying Nato peacekeepers in a post-war settlement.

But sources familiar with the matter told the *Wall Street Journal* that Trump’s son-in-law Jared Kushner has suggested that Kyiv could still join if it bends to Moscow’s central demand for it to give up territory.

They said he had recently taken the idea right to Ukrainian president Volodymyr Zelensky and other European officials in an effort to break the deadlock in peace talks.

![Jared Kushner, Volodymyr Zelensky and Steve Witkoff in Dublin on November 12](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/20/14/download.-3..jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The idea apparently emerged in August, some six months after a round of talks in Geneva ended without any clear breakthroughs.

It since came up at a meeting with both sides in the UAE, although it was unclear from the report what the Kremlin had made of it.

Ukraine maintains that it cannot and will not give up control of its eastern lands, which are mineral rich and host its defensive line of ‘fortress cities’ - as well as thousands of Ukrainian citizens.

But Nato membership could go some way to ensuring Ukraine gets its desired security guarantees against a future invasion.

![Volodymyr Zelensky, French President Emmanuel Macron, and US Special Envoy Steve Witkoff attended a conference in Berlin on Thursday](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/20/15/2305353712..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The US, Russia and the UK offered security assurances when Ukraine gave up its nuclear weapons in 1994, but those failed to stop Vladimir Putin annexing Crimea in 2014 and launching a full-scale invasion in 2022.

And Trump has since cast doubt on whether he would even help a Nato ally in the event of an attack, saying last year he would not defend countries that failed to meet his “requirements” on defence spending.

In 2024, before his second term, he drew criticism for saying he would “encourage” Russia to attack any Nato member that fails to pay its bills.

An attack on Nato without the US - the bloc’s largest contributor - would leave Europe and Canada a weakened force against any potential adversary.

![A Ukrainian serviceman of the 66th Separate Mechanized Brigade walks along a road in the village of Serednie](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/19/12/2026-11-18T085827Z_101325980_RC2JHOAMTBAG_RTRMADP_3_UKRAINE-CRISIS-VIVALDI.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The growing rift between Washington and Europe since Trump’s return to office was felt this week as he met Putin on the sidelines of the Apec summit in Shenzhen, China.

Trump greeted the Russian leader by asking “Who is he? He’s my friend” before the two settled into discussions on the Ukraine war.

It was later reported that Trump reportedly had urged Europe to stop funding Ukraine, just hours after the warm bilateral with Putin.

Trump was said to have called into a meeting with the so-called Coalition of the Willing in Germany, attended by UK prime minister Andy Burnham and Ukraine’s president Volodymyr Zelensky, among others.

He raised concerns about billions in aid provided to Ukraine from Europe, according to European officials in *Politico*and* Telegraph* reports, stressing countries could save money if the war ended.

An unnamed US official later told *Politico* it was “not accurate” to say Trump told the leaders to end funding to Kyiv, but rather repeated his desire to bring the conflict to an end.
