# 'Cancer showed me a new level of kindness': Christine Talbot hosts Yorkshire Cancer Research fashion show

## The Yorkshire Cancer Research Fashion show kicked off its Christmas shopping campaign as 15 models, each with their own cancer story, wore outfits sourced from its 18 shops, as Yorkshire Post Fashion Editor Stephanie Smith finds out. Pictures by Jonathan Pow.

When Janea Maitland took to the catwalk this month to model a whisper-grey, sequin-embellished, pre-loved chiffon dress at Yorkshire Cancer Research’s inaugural pre-Christmas fashion show, it was a moment of mixed emotions.

In 2020, Janea from Doncaster was pregnant with her daughter, Amelie, when she was diagnosed with malignant melanoma after noticing a bleeding sore on her leg.

After Amelie's birth, she learned the cancer had spread to her lymph nodes, needing surgery and a year of immunotherapy treatment. As soon as she found out, she phoned her mum, Janet. Janea says: “ When I got home, she was waiting for me on the drive and gave me the biggest hug. She looked after me, drove me to appointments and helped care for Amelie. I honestly don't know how I would have got through it without her."

![Christine Talbot wearing sequin maxi dress to host the Yorkshire Cancer Research Christmas fashion show at the charity's superstore at Foss Island, York. Picture by Jonathan Pow](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOmI5NWViOWY2LWU3NjAtNDlmMi1hOGZlLWQ1MGRiYzNiNDllYjo2M2E1ZjRkZC00NjhhLTQ0YTItOTAwZC1jN2YxZmU0Y2FiM2Q=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


But while Janea was being treated, Janet was diagnosed with late-stage lung cancer, and began a course of chemotherapy and radiotherapy, so they were both having cancer treatment at the same time.

"It didn't feel real,” Janea says. “Mum hadn't shown any symptoms and was still her usual outgoing self. One minute she was supporting me through treatment and the next, our family was trying to process her own diagnosis."

Janet's health deteriorated and she died in August 2023, aged 56, weeks before her daughter completed her own treatment. "Getting involved in the Yorkshire Cancer Research fashion show gave me something positive to focus on,” Janea says. “My mum won't see my little girl grow up, but I'm hopeful advances in cancer research will mean more children get to grow up with their grandparents."

Every 15 minutes, someone in Yorkshire is diagnosed with cancer - almost 31,000 people. Reflecting that, Janea was one of 15 models - each with their own cancer story - who took part in the special fashion show at Yorkshire Cancer Research’s new superstore in York.

The event, which marked the launch of the Christmas campaign, saw models wearing outfits sourced entirely from the charity’s 18 shops, where more than four million items have been sold so far this year, transforming second-hand treasures into pioneering research and cancer services.

The models included those rebuilding confidence after a cancer diagnosis, while some were receiving treatment, or were family members celebrating milestones together or, like Janea, remembering their loved ones.

The show was hosted by presenter and journalist Christine Talbot, who was herself diagnosed and treated for a fast-growing type of breast cancer. "When I was told I had cancer in 2012, I was live on TV every night and very much in the public eye,” she says. “The support I received from colleagues and the public was overwhelming. Cancer is not something you would ever wish on anyone, but it showed me a whole new level of kindness I didn't know existed.

"At this time of year, family and community take on even greater importance, and events like this are a wonderful reminder of the strength and generosity of people across Yorkshire."

Lynn Hall and her daughter Jemma, also from Doncaster, walked down the catwalk together. In December 2025, 58-year-old Lynn went to her GP with abdominal pains. In January, she was diagnosed with a fast-growing type of oesophageal cancer.

“We spent the Christmas before on edge,” she says. “We tried to carry on the usual festivities, but it was hard. Christmas is all about family and togetherness, so it makes you think about everything you could lose."

Jemma says: “Mum is my beautiful, incredible, selfless, caring best friend, so to hear the word ‘cancer’ connected to her was hard to accept.”

Lynn was referred to the Yorkshire Cancer Research Active Together service, where a team helped her prepare for her operation to remove the tumour and rebuild her strength after.

“The difference exercise has made physically and mentally has been enormous,” Lynn says. “My consultant told me, because of the scale of the surgery, I should expect to spend at least two weeks in hospital. But I was home in seven days - to think that time was effectively halved through cancer exercise treatment pioneered here in Yorkshire is remarkable.

“The only reason I can look forward to life together with family again is because of cancer research, treatment and support here in Yorkshire.

“I never expected to be in a fashion show at 58 years old, but it has been one of the big lights at the end of a very dark tunnel. Being involved in the Yorkshire Cancer Research fashion show meant so much to me.”

For Claire Charlton and Mohan Nagaraj, from Gildersome, the Yorkshire Cancer Research fashion show was an opportunity to reflect on everything they had experienced together. Claire, 37, had been feeling tired and struggling with muscle pain for months before a lump on her neck was spotted during a routine dental appointment. Scans confirmed she had thyroid cancer.

“I felt disorientated and in shock,” she says. “The results from my surgery showed I’d been living with cancer for quite a long time and, because of that, I would need intensive treatment.”

Mohan says: “You go from planning your future together to waiting for answers about what the future holds.”

The two years since Claire’s diagnosis have been a cycle of scans and treatments, leaving her often unable to leave the house. “I’ve experienced recurrence,” she says. “I’ve had lymph nodes removed and three operations. Mohan was present every step of the way.“

They married in November 2025 and had a traditional wedding celebration in India. Mohan says: “Cancer has changed our lives, but it hasn’t stopped us living them. Christmas is such a special time for our family, so we want to make sure it’s another one to remember.”

Claire says: “I’ve had some incredibly difficult moments, but alongside them I’ve found love, celebrated a proposal and a wedding, visited Lapland and been surrounded by people who never stopped showing up for me. That’s what I choose to focus on.”

The outfits on the catwalk were styled by Yorkshire-coast based businesswoman and supporter Tina Boden and image consultant Jackie Crawford, from York.

Jackie says: "I help people embrace positive change by using colour and personal styling to build confidence and self-esteem. As a champion of sustainable fashion, I love the idea of the new adventures that pre-loved clothes will enjoy with their new owners.”

Tina says: "Raising awareness of the impact cancer research has on people and families across Yorkshire is so important, and the Yorkshire Cancer Research fashion show was a truly magical way to have those conversations while supporting life-saving research."

Tony Graham, director of Retail at Yorkshire Cancer Research, says: “Every item donated, every purchase made and every volunteer hour helps turn pre-loved goods into life-saving research and innovative cancer services that are changing lives across the region.”

Jemma Hall says: “Last Christmas, we didn’t know what the future held. This Christmas, thanks to vital research and services in Yorkshire, Mum can be with her family.”
