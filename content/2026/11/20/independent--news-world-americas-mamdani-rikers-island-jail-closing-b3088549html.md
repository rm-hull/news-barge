New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani has announced a plan to shut the Rikers Island jail complex by 2032, betting on a substantial cut in the number of people held behind bars to finally close the notorious facility.

Mamdani has been a vocal proponent of closing Rikers, though the longstanding plan to build new jails has met pushback, including from his predecessor, former Mayor Eric Adams, who opposed federal oversight and borough jails.

The plan hinges on lowering the city's inmate population from the more than 6,500 people housed at Rikers to around 4,500.

“It is time to finally close Rikers Island,” Mamdani said in a statement.

“For decades, New Yorkers have demanded a correctional system that is safe and humane.”

![Rikers Island, in the East River, contains New York City's largest jail](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/20/10/2293529639..?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&auto=webp)

For years, Rikers Island has stood as a symbol of dysfunction, with the network of jails so plagued by violence and overcrowding that a federal judge, citing “abnormal and unacceptable” conditions, appointed an outside manager to restore order and implement reforms.

Criminal justice activists have pushed for decades to close Rikers Island, which has housed inmates since the 1930s.

The city is all but certain to blow past a legal requirement to close Rikers and replace it with four smaller, more modern jails by 2027.

In 2019, the New York City Council voted to close the jail complex and replace it with newer jails located in the city's boroughs, with local lawmakers invoking the names of former prisoners who died, including Kalief Browder, who took his own life at age 22 after being held for three years at Rikers.

At the time, city officials had also called for shrinking the jail population to accommodate the closure, but ran into opposition from critics who argued doing so would mean more violent criminals on city streets.

![Mamdani says it is ‘time to finally close Rikers Island’](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/20/10/2305768937..?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&auto=webp)

To get the numbers down, the Mamdani administration is proposing a new slate of diversion and supervised release programs, expanding housing and social services to prevent recidivism, along with restoring an online bail payment system and reducing case delays through various measures.

“We have a legal and moral obligation to close Rikers. Working alongside the City Council, Courts and nonprofit partners, we will meet it — for the people in our custody, the staff who serve them, victims of crime, and every New Yorker,” Mamdani said.

Instead of housing prisoners at Rikers – an island in the East River near LaGuardia Airport – the city will shift people to jails in the Bronx, Queens, Brooklyn and Manhattan, as well as a handful of medical facilities for people who need healthcare or psychiatric treatment.

The city also plans to reduce the number of beds for women, diverting them into community-based programs.

The borough jails are expected to open in Brooklyn in 2029; the Bronx and Queens in 2031; and then Manhattan in 2032, according to City Hall.
