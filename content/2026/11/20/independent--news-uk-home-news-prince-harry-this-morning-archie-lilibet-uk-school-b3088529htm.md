Prince Harry has said his children Archie and Lilibet are “loving” their return to the UK, and that their confidence has “gone through the roof” since being in a British school.

The Duke of Sussex and his wife Meghan returned to England in August, after spending the last six years living in California since stepping down as members of the royal family in 2020.

Speaking on *This Morning*, where he is promoting next year’s Invictus Games, Harry said: “They love being back – I say "back", they don't really know… It's a big culture shift. I think the kids in school uniforms is a big change for them but it's certainly helped.

“The confidence in both of them has gone through the roof since being in a UK school, which is great as well. I'm squeezing the accents out of them and so is the school.”

![Prince Harry on ITV’s ‘This Morning’](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/20/12/13/prince-harry-this-morning.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

In September, Archie, seven, and Lilibet, five, moved schools after just two days over security concerns regarding the transport route from their home.

Asked of his return to the UK, he said: “It’s great being back on home soil and I’m throwing myself into the new winter weather, which is also nice.

“It’s always interesting when you’re living overseas and looking back in, you can see lots of stuff happening, but when you’re back everything feels quite similar. Obviously there’s lots of change, but as far as London, as far as the UK… it’s home.”

![](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/07/10/18/48/meghan-instagram.png?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

He also gave his seal of approval to Robert Pattinson’s casting as James Bond.

Harry said he is looking forward to seeing The Batman star play 007, after the British actor was announced as the successor to Daniel Craig earlier this week.

He was joined by two veterans who are set to compete in the Invictus Games in Birmingham next July, which features wounded service members from 25 countries competing in sports.

![The Duke of Sussex is promoting the Invictus Games in Birmingham in 2027](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/09/28/14/2295283063..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

On Wednesday, the duke, who served in the armed forces for 10 years from 2005 to 2015, said he would rejoin the military to fight Russia if needed.

Harry took part in a conversation for The Telegraph’s Ukraine: The Latest podcast while on a surprise visit to the war-torn country last weekend.

In footage released on the Telegraph’s website on Wednesday afternoon, former soldier Harry, who spent a decade in the forces and served on the front line in Afghanistan, said: “There is a lot that we can learn from Ukraine. I think there’s a lot that the whole world can learn from Ukraine.

“If asked to sign up again, if the need is there, then I will 100% be there to serve my country, alongside my fellow veterans and service personnel.”

![Prince Harry visited Ukrainian war veterans in Lviv this week](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/20/12/2305168750..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Harry suggested Britain had been in conflict with Russia for a decade, telling the paper’s Dominic Nicholls: “Just because there’s not drones, bullets and rockets flying at us, that doesn’t mean that we’re not at war.

“I feel like we’ve been at war for the last decade.”

News of Harry’s conversation was trailed on social media on Tuesday, minutes before his estranged brother the Prince of Wales took to the green carpet with the Princess of Wales in Mumbai for his glittering Earthshot Awards.
