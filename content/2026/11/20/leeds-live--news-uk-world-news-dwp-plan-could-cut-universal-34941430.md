# DWP plan could cut Universal Credit payments to £0 for claimants

## The DWP's Jobs Guarantee scheme is now available across England, Scotland and Wales

Universal Credit claimants across England, Scotland and Wales could see their payments reduced to £0 under the new Jobs Guarantee Scheme. The Department for Work and Pensions (DWP) Jobs Guarantee scheme is now fully operational throughout England, Scotland and Wales.

The Labour government describes the scheme as offering "guaranteed opportunities to young people furthest from the labour market." It will provide fully funded, six-month paid employment to all eligible 18 to 24-year-olds who have been claiming Universal Credit and seeking work for 18 months.

The Jobs Guarantee is anticipated to support in excess of 90,000 young people over the next three years. Eligibility criteria will be broadened to allow young people claiming Universal Credit and actively seeking employment to access the scheme after 12 months, rather than the current 18-month threshold.

This change means young people will be placed into work sooner, addressing the damaging effects of prolonged unemployment. The scheme aims to assist more than 90,000 young people into employment by March 2029, reports Birmingham Live.

The Jobs Guarantee targets 18 to 24-year-olds who are currently claiming Universal Credit and actively looking for work. At present, young people who have been claiming Universal Credit and out of work for 18 months are referred by their work coach to a local delivery partner.

From April 2027, this timeframe will shorten to 12 months, allowing young people to benefit from the Jobs Guarantee sooner. The partner organisation gets acquainted with participants and their abilities, then pairs them with suitable employment opportunities.

Prior to starting work, participants receive bespoke pre-employment assistance to address any obstacles preventing them from working. This might involve confidence-building, interview coaching and CV support.

Throughout their employment, they continue receiving ongoing support while developing workplace skills. The government covers the full cost of up to 25 hours of paid employment per week for 6 months, at the applicable minimum wage rate.

Money Helper says: "If you get a job under the Job Guarantee Scheme, you'll usually be offered work of 25 hours per week at the national minimum wage. As with any paid work, your pay is taken into account when calculating your Universal Credit amount.

"If you get the standard Universal Credit allowance for single people under 25 of £338.58 per month, your paid employment under the Job Guarantee scheme would mean you would be earning too much to be eligible for Universal Credit, and your allowance would reduce to £0."
