# DWP Universal Credit scheme explained as payments could be reduced to £0

## A new Department for Work and Pensions (DWP) programme will be introduced at the end of this month

Thousands of young Universal Credit claimants will soon have access to "guaranteed" employment through a new Department for Work and Pensions (DWP) scheme launching later this month.

The Jobs Guarantee scheme provides a six-month paid role for 18 to 24-year-olds who are receiving Universal Credit while job hunting. Participants will benefit from bespoke support both prior to and during their placement, reports the Mirror.

While the scheme doesn't promise permanent employment, it's designed to help young people build skills and gain valuable experience to boost their prospects in future roles.

Rolling out at the end of November, the programme is open to 18 to 24-year-olds across England, Scotland and Wales who have been on Universal Credit for 18 months. In a significant overhaul of youth support, from April 2027 onwards, qualifying young people will access employment assistance sooner - after 12 months rather than 18.

### Who is eligible for the Jobs Guarantee and when does it start?

From late November 2026, if you're between 18 and 24, live in England, Wales or Scotland and have been unemployed while claiming Universal Credit for 18 months, you'll be offered a six-month placement tailored to your abilities. These positions will generally involve up to 25 hours per week, paid at the minimum wage appropriate for your age bracket.

### What are the earnings and duration of the placement?

Your wages will be determined by your age. The Government is funding just 25 hours per week, although your specific employer may opt to provide extra hours. For those aged 21 and over, 25 hours at minimum wage amounts to £317.75 each week. For people aged 18 to 20, this sum is £271.25 weekly. Minimum wage rates are anticipated to increase in April 2027.

### What will happen to Universal Credit payments?

If you obtain work via the Job Guarantee Scheme, you'll generally be provided with 25 hours of employment weekly at the national minimum wage. Like any paid work, your earnings are factored in when determining your Universal Credit sum.

For those claiming the standard Universal Credit allowance for single claimants under 25 - presently £338.58 per month - income earned through the Job Guarantee scheme would indicate you would be receiving too much to qualify for Universal Credit, and your allowance would drop to £0.

However, certain exceptions exist. For example, if you have responsibility for a child, or possess a limited capability for work due to illness or disability, you'll be entitled to a 'work allowance'.
