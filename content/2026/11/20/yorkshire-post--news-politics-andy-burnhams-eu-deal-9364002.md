# Andy Burnham’s EU deal: what it means for food and drink exports, tuition fees and youth mobility

## Andy Burnham is in Brussels today finalising the agreement, which covers food and agriculture exports, emissions trading and a youth experience scheme.

Andy Burnham has said a “landmark” deal with the European Union - which includes slashing red tape on food and agricultural exports - will benefit every person in the UK.

European Commission chief Ursula von der Leyen said the deal marks a “turning point” in post-Brexit relations between the UK and the bloc.

Mr Burnham is in Brussels today finalising the agreement, which covers food and agriculture exports, emissions trading and a youth experience scheme.

The latter will open the doors for 100,000 young Europeans to temporarily work in the UK, with Britons able to do the same on the continent.

The Prime Minister also said the UK would participate in the EU’s action plan to tackle small boat crossings in the English Channel, marking a “very significant step forward”.

Ms von der Leyen said the UK-EU summit in Brussels was a sign of “good old friends that are rejuvenating the friendship”.

### **Tuition fees**

But tensions still remain, with a six-month deadline put on reaching an agreement over EU demands to cut tuition fees for students from member states studying at British universities.

Officials in Brussels had indicated that implementation of the deal announced today could be affected if the tuition fees issue is not resolved.

But the UK insisted the deal is done and dates for when the agreements come into force would be set out in a joint statement with the EU and are not conditional on the fees issue.

The Russell Group - which includes the universities of Leeds, Sheffield and York - said that “any reduction in EU student fees would put pressure on UK student places and take more money out of UK higher education”.

Mr Burnham intends to follow up from the summit by starting a national conversation around the UK’s future relationship with the EU, something which could potentially include rejoining the bloc.

He told EU chiefs: “I think we are working together in a world that is changing around us to come back closer and, in doing so, make ourselves stronger, both our economies and our security.

“It’s a pleasure to be with you today to make this landmark agreement.”

The outcomes of the summit “will benefit every single person” in the UK and EU, he said.

Naomi Smith, chief executive of Best for Britain, said: “Today marks one small step in our economic fortunes but one giant leap for young people’s opportunities, and British businesses who for too long have had their horizons narrowed and wings clipped by needless Brexit red tape.

“Having drawn a line under a decade of drift and division, the Prime Minister must now build on the bold leadership he has shown so far, and take the essential and laudable step towards restoring our EU membership, as the majority of voters the length and breadth of Great Britain now demand.”

### **Agri-food exports**

The deal on food imports and exports “holds out the real prospect that the cost of the weekly shop will begin to come down”.

This will see Britain align with the EU on trade rules on sanitary and phytosanitary (SPS) goods, such as food and plants, from March 2028.

Under the deal, Export Health Certificates – which can cost up to £200 each – will be scrapped. The Government said that British favourites that were blocked from European markets under the Brexit deal will be sold in Europe again. That includes fresh sausages and burgers, Welsh mussels, and Scottish seed potatoes.

The deal will also remove the vast majority of paperwork and checks on food and plants moving from Great Britain to Northern Ireland, reducing friction within the UK internal market and strengthening our Union.

![From right, European Council President Antonio Costa, Britain's Prime Minister Andy Burnham and European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen. Credit: AP](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/jpim-static/image/2026/11/20/13/19/01M7WCRMPQVR3DM0M9APYSM0ZS.jpg?trim=0,0,0,0&width=640&quality=65)


The managing director of Marks & Spencer Food, Alex Freudmann, has said that the deal will “remove bureaucracy” from the current regulations.

He wrote: “How hard can it be to get a spaghetti bolognese from a factory in Lincolnshire onto a shelf in Dublin?

“Today, it takes hundreds of extra miles, mountains of paperwork, a vet’s signature and the Latin name of a cow (Bos taurus, if you’re wondering).

“This is what food trade looks like after Brexit. And it’s why we need the UK-EU food trade deal announced today.”

But Country Land and Business Association (CLA) President Gavin Lane said: “In the short term, there will be significant benefits to exporters but it's naive to think that there won't be challenges in the long term.

“When the EU comes up with a new regulation that may damage British farmers or producers, we will have no recourse. This doesn't bode well for democracy.

“The political instability this will cause risks overshadowing many of the benefits the deal will bring.”

### **Youth Mobility Scheme**

Young people from the UK will be able to travel to the EU to live, work and study. Those aged 18 to 31 will be able to move to Europe for three years, with no need for a job offer.

Mr Burnham said the youth mobility deal with the EU made it a “good day for young people in Britain”. A final number still needs to be ironed out, but it is likely to be between 80,000 and 100,000.

“Gen Z, millennial kids, I think they arguably were the biggest losers from the changes of a decade ago,” Mr Burnham said.

“They lost opportunity when Brexit came and then Covid.

“You know they’ve been the generation I think that has been really really unlucky in many ways.”

Ms von der Leyen told Mr Burnham “we are at a turning point” and “we have made a political choice to strengthen our partnership”.

The UK and EU were “good old friends that are rejuvenating the friendship”, she said.

Welcoming the youth experience deal she said the young people of the UK and EU would be “the best ambassadors for our friendship”.

However, an agreement on tuition fees will have to be found in the next six months.

EU member states want to cut the fees faced by their students in the UK to the same level as domestic undergraduates.

That would mean a cut from fees of up to £38,000 a year to £9,970 – something the UK has resisted because of the financial impact it would have on universities.

An EU official said: “I think this is an important element, so let’s hope that everything can go ahead.”

### ‘**Brexit betrayal’**

Reform UK leader Nigel Farage accused Mr Burnham of “betrayal”, and added: “I’m afraid that the great Brexit debate has just started again.

“We don’t need to be in political union of any kind at all to do business and have good relations with our neighbours.

“That’s the betrayal that Burnham is committing today and he’s trying to do it incrementally, but it’s perfectly clear that this very, very soon becomes a debate about: do we rejoin or do we stay out?”

He acknowledged that for food exporters a deal on agricultural standards will make things “a little bit easier”, but said it comes at the cost of giving up control over regulation.

The Reform leader told BBC Radio 4’s Today: “The Labour Party was elected saying they would not rejoin the single market. Well, today we’re rejoining a chunk of it.”

The food and agriculture deal, aimed at ending post-Brexit export checks, would see Britain align with the EU on trade rules on sanitary and phytosanitary (SPS) goods, such as food and plants, from March 2028.

The Conservatives accused the Government of being prepared to “sell out” British farmers by becoming a rule taker.

Shadow environment secretary Victoria Atkins said: “They are forcing farmers to follow rules over which we have no say, no vote, and no veto, that benefit highly subsidised foreign farmers, and taxpayers are going to end up paying for the privilege.”
