**Two British nationals connected to an alleged Iran-linked plot at RAF Fairford in September will go on trial at Woolwich Crown Court next year.**

At the Old Bailey, legal representatives for both men indicated they are likely to enter pleas of not guilty.

Nouman Essghir and Mohmed Abdulkarim, both from London, are charged with National Security Act offences after they drove vans loaded with petrol to the airbase, operated by the US air force, allegedly on the orders of Iran.

Julia Faure Walker, prosecuting, said both defendants were in contact with a person named Ali who was acting "in the interests" of Iran.

The four-week week trial is set to begin on 22 November 2027.

A major incident was declared after three suspicious vans were seen near the Gloucestershire airbase on 27 September.

Dozens of residents were evacuated and a large cordon was put up in the area around the base.

No explosive materials were found in the vans by police but a quantity of petrol was seized.

During the hearing on Friday, both defendants confirmed their identities and their lawyers indicated both would deny the charges against them.

Abdulkarim, 24, from Westminster, has been charged with assisting a foreign intelligence service, which police earlier said they believed to be Iran.

Faure Walker told the court Abdulkarim acted as an "intermediary" for Ali who allegedly directed the Fairford operation, buying equipment and receiving multiple payments from him totalling around £70,000.

Essghir, 25, from Notting Hill, has been charged with entering a prohibited place for a purpose prejudicial to the UK on 22 August, weeks before he was arrested near the airbase in Gloucestershire alongside four other men.

He is accused of filming the perimeter of RAF Fairford at Ali's request.

Essghir was among the five men arrested on 27 September, and was then re-arrested on 20 October.

One of the men involved in the mission called police in the early hours after the organiser warned a bomb was in the vans, the court heard.

Both defendants appeared in court via a video link. In Essghir's case it was from Wandsworth jail in south London, and he was remanded into custody, while Abdulkarim was granted continued bail.

Iran has previously denied any involvement in the incident at RAF Fairford.

The base has been used as a forward operating location by the US Air Force for decades.

In recent months, the US has flown B-1 and B-52 bombers from the base to the Middle East, after the UK approved a request for it to be used for defensive strikes against Iran.

After the incident in September the US pulled its bombers out of the base and returned them to America.

US President Donald Trump said the aircraft were removed due to "some threats" to the base, without giving specific details.
