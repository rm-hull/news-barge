# Black Friday 2026: My favourite Yorkshire Post stories of this year that have been some of the most read by subscribers

## Many of our stories this year have caught the attention of The Yorkshire Post subscribers - from historic businesses and pubs to the insights of a Michelin-starred chef.

Our Black Friday deal returns this month and with a variety of stories for you to choose from this is your chance to subscribe to The Yorkshire Post for only £1 per month.

Yorkshire has a special place in my heart. Throughout my university studies and career I’ve lived in other areas in the UK - but they never felt like home.

![The Owl Inn, Hawnby, North Yorkshire.](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOmU4NWYwNzY3LTRjOWQtNDNmZS04ODk2LWU0NGQzYjNhYmUwMjo3ZDZhZmQ4Yy00MTU4LTQ0YjYtOWI4Ny00YWY1MDMzODVmYmI=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


Working for The Yorkshire Post has brought me back to where I always felt I belonged.

There’s so much going on in the region that finding stories is like finding your favourite sweet in a traditional sweet shop. Possibilities are endless.

In light of the Black Friday £12 annual subscription offer, I’ve had a look at the stories that stuck out to me over the last year and you’re in for a treat.

## **Best Yorkshire Post stories of 2026 that were most read by subscribers**

Back in January, Elaine Lemm, a food writer and restaurant critic, decided to brave the extremely cold weather with her husband to indulge in a meal at The Owl Hawnby. Was it worth the trip? Our subscribers certainly wanted to know.

Our senior reporter, Alexandra Wood, often covers stories around Hull and East Yorkshire, though at times she ventures out further. Back in July this year she got acquainted with a family in Scarborough who had to close their business of 181 years. This clearly struck a chord with our subscribers.

In October this year, Yorkshire historian, author and photo archivist Peter Tuffrey explored the thoroughfare on Blossom Street in York that stretches as far as 300 yards and there was a lot to share.

I enjoyed interviewing Pamela Howorth in March, who is the owner of a Haworth business with connections to the Brontë sisters. There is something truly magical about delving into the history of a building and discovering hidden treasures - or in this case secret doorways to the past. I was glad to see that our subscribers enjoyed reading it too.

The longstanding Michelin-starred chef Andrew Pern, who has been the chef patron at The Star Inn At Harome since 1996, admitted he was ready to hang up his apron professionally speaking and this piqued many of our subscribers’ interest.

When I went to Malham for my birthday, I underestimated just how far I would be climbing and also how much it would be worth it. It was a terrifying experience as someone who is not much of a hiker or climber and it was an experience I had to share.

The best pleasures I have working in this job is when I get to interview ordinary people who do extraordinary things. Many of them pour their heart and soul into building a shop that will wow visitors when they walk inside. This business in Whitby was no exception.
