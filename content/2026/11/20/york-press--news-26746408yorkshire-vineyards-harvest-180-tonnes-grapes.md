## Bumper harvest to deliver 125,000 to 140,000 bottles of Yorkshire wine

Tim Spakouskas of the Yorkshire Heart Vineyard *(Image: Darren Greenwood)*

Yorkshire vineyards are raising a glass to one of their best-ever grape harvests.

Nineteen vineyards across the region collectively picked more than 180 tonnes of grapes in 2026, according to the Yorkshire Vineyards Harvest Report.

James Townsend of Dunesforde Vineyard at Upper Dunsforth, between York and Boroughbridge, said: "After a nervous start to the year with frost damage affecting all varieties, the prolonged warm and dry weather led to an excellent recovery and good yields.

"The dry weather enabled us to hang the grapes where we wanted and push for excellent ripeness.




![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21741305.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Tim Spakouskas of the Yorkshire Heart Vineyard *(Image: Darren Greenwood)*

"Overall, we’re really pleased with the 2026 harvest and are excited to see the quality of wines from this year."

The harvest, which took place mainly between September 20 and October 15, was about a week ahead of schedule, contributing to improved ripeness and quality.

Disease-resistant varieties such as Seyval Blanc, Solaris, and Rondo performed particularly well, accounting for around 70 per cent of the total crop.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21741273.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Antonia Chasney of the Goose Beck Vineyard at Bilton-in-Ainsty *(Image: Darren Greenwood)*

Peter Kirk from Towthorpe Vineyard, north of York, said: "Mildew was a challenge in the Bacchus, but across the wider vineyard yields on the mature PIWI grapes have been exceptional, and the flavour profiles seem to be more intense given the smaller berries.

"This hopefully sets us up for good 2026 wines."

Despite the overall success, some vineyards faced challenges due to early frosts and drought conditions, especially newer sites.

Antonia Chasney of Goose Beck Vineyard at Bilton-in-Ainsty, west of York, said: "As a newer vineyard we had a mixed year with frost and drought playing their part in reducing the yield, but good clean grapes have been harvested."

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21741303.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Peter Townsend and his father Ian at the Dunesforde vineyard in the Vale of York (Image: Darren Greenwood) *(Image: Darren Greenwood)*

Tim Spakouskas from Yorkshire Heart Vineyard at Nun Monkton, near York, said: "2026 harvest was our biggest and best harvest yet.

"The quality and quantity of the grapes coming in was brilliant, the weather during our harvest period was excellent which helped with the processing and fermentation.

"The wine in tanks is tasting very good, we are excited to see what the finished product is like when bottles and released in 2027."

More than 500 volunteers supported the harvest, taking part in events and celebrations throughout September and October.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21741274.jpg?type=mds-article-620)A bumper harvest has taken place in Yorkshire *(Image: Darren Greenwood)*

The 2026 harvest is expected to produce between 125,000 and 140,000 bottles of Yorkshire wine.

The industry’s call for duty relief on wine sold through the cellar door would deliver much-needed investment funds for the Yorkshire wine industry.

This relief would bring the wine industry in line with beer and cider producers.

Cellar door activity contributes significantly to local tourism in Yorkshire.

Yorkshire’s wine industry continues to expand, with ongoing experimentation in classic French varieties such as Cabernet Franc, Gamay, and Pinot Noir.

These varieties successfully ripened in 2026, further demonstrating the region’s winemaking potential.

The full Yorkshire Vineyards Harvest Report 2026, including detailed data on grape varieties, harvest dates, volumes, and quality, is available at yorkshirevineyards.co.uk/insights.
