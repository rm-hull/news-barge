# The tide is turning on screen time as children and adults rediscover real-world play - Ismail Mulla

## Parenting is difficult at the best of times. But in a world where every other child is mindlessly gawping at screens because their parents have no care in the world about the impact that could have on their offspring, it must be doubly challenging for mums and dads that are invested in their child’s wellbeing to keep them away from screens.

That is not helped by the fact that there is no getting away from them. I’m staring at one right now as I bash away at my keyboard. While the majority of us have no choice but to stare at one for work, there’s no getting away from screens when it comes to social lives as well.

But we’re at the inflection point where more and more people are asking about the pernicious effects that staring at screens all day can have on individuals and society as a whole.

Therefore it is no surprise that parents are prioritising screen-free toys this Christmas. The Entertainer said every one of its predicted top 10 toys this Christmas was screen-free, while Smyths said screen-free play “continues to make a comeback” as parents seek to keep children entertained away from their devices.

Geoff Sheffield, buying director at The Entertainer, said: “We’re seeing a real appetite for toys that get children hands-on, using their imaginations and finding their own ways to play.

“Screens are naturally a big part of children’s lives in today’s world, but there’s still a real desire for toys that offer something different.

“We’re seeing strong demand for creative, imaginative, and more traditional educational toys, and it’s coming from both children and those buying for them.”

That’s exactly how it should be. Unlocking a child’s imagination and getting them to be creative are traits that they can carry through the rest of their lives. Even STEM (science, technology, engineering, maths) industries require people to think outside of the box.

If everything is spat out for them at an instance and their brains are not given the room to wander then they are unlikely to exercise these muscles. Dare I say it, even the technology industry needs creative minds to enable it to continue developing.

Earlier this year, former Prime Minister Sir Keir Starmer announced a social media ban for under-16s. It is not an insignificant policy. Indeed Australia has already taken the steps to protect its young people from the dangerous brain rot that is being peddled by social media firms.

This is very much a turning point and if Starmer can be proud of one thing, it is the fact that he may well have set Britain on the course to protect its young people. There is now a general consensus that you can’t let the market rip at the cost of young children’s lives. Regardless of how much social media firms may wail.

People have a tendency for overestimating the impact of film and television but there are certain franchises that hold sway over significant swathes of the population. One of them is Toy Story.

The fifth installment of the film franchise was released earlier and its plot revolved around a Lilypad smart tablet who forces the toys to work together to try to save their owner Bonnie from electronic devices.

It’s an important message just as much for the millions of children who have watched the film as it is for adults.

Aldi’s Christmas range of more than 50 wooden toys comes with a “two per product per person” purchase limit on certain lines to meet anticipated demand. It shows that the tide is really beginning to turn.

Toy retailer Smyths has seen a “particularly strong interest in Lego, games and creative toys for 2026, from hands-on drawing and craft sets to laugh-out-loud family games”. I still fondly remember as a child how Lego used to provide a blank canvas for us to let our imagination run wild. These days a lot of the sets come with a set template but even that is better than gawping at a screen.

“There’s something brilliantly nostalgic about everyone gathering around a game or enjoying an activity together, creating those shared moments and memories that Christmas is all about,” says Sinead Byrne, European marketing director at Smyths.

Mr Sheffield says “analogue play is having a real moment as we head towards Christmas”.

Hopefully this is a trend that goes beyond Christmas. Not just for the sake of young children but also adults. Everyone would benefit from spending less time on social media and more time interacting with each other in the real world.
