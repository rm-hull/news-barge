# Leeds beats Manchester in station rankings as UK's quietest gets just 94 users

## There were 28.0 million entries and exits at Leeds between April 2025 and March 2026, according to figures

Leeds has pipped Manchester Piccadilly to become Britain’s second-busiest train station outside London, new figures show.

There were 28.0 million entries and exits at Leeds between April 2025 and March 2026, according to new data from the Office of Rail and Road.

That’s an increase of 700,000 passengers on the previous year. Leeds overtook Manchester Piccadilly partly because of that rise, and partly because Manchester’s numbers fell by a similar amount.

A total of 26.6 million people used Manchester Piccadilly last year, down 760,000 on the year before. The fall at Piccadilly will have been affected by the station closing for nine days in February for major engineering works.

Birmingham New Street was the only station outside London busier than Leeds. It had 38.8 million entries and exits, 2.2 million more than a year earlier.

Liverpool Street in London is still Britain’s busiest station. Almost 102.7 million people used it in the year to April, an increase of 4.7 million passengers.

Paddington came second with 72.9 million entries and exits, up 3.0 million. Waterloo was third with 71.6 million passengers. Next came Tottenham Court Road with 67.2 million, London Bridge with 57.0 million and Farringdon with 55.3 million.

Leeds ranks 15th in the whole of Britain.

At the other end of the scale, fewer than two people a week used Britain’s quietest train station last year.

Reddish South in Greater Manchester had just 94 entries and exits over the 12 months. That’s fewer passengers than any other station in the country, and works out at one passenger every four days.

Denton, also in Greater Manchester, was the next quietest. There were just 100 passes through the turnstiles there last year, which is just under two a week.

In total, 16 stations averaged fewer than one entry or exit a day last year.

Elton and Orston in Nottinghamshire came next with just 102 entries and exits. After that came Chapelton in Devon with 148, Shippea Hill in Cambridgeshire with 154, Altnabreac in the Highlands with 172, Ince and Elton in Cheshire with 216 and Coombe Junction Halt in Cornwall with 246.
