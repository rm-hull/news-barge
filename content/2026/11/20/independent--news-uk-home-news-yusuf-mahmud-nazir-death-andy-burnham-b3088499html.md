The family of a five-year-old boy who died after he was sent home from A&E say they want to meet Andy Burnham and discuss what they describe as “system failures”.

Yusuf Mahmud Nazir died at Sheffield Children’s Hospital on 23 November, 2022, eight days after he was seen at Rotherham Hospital and sent home with antibiotics.

His family believes his death was a result of system failures across two hospitals and other services.

A fresh report into Yusuf’s care was published in July last year with a range of recommendations for the NHS, but his family said at the time there were still many unanswered questions.

The report said in its conclusions the “primary finding is that the parental concerns, particularly the mother’s instinct that her child was unwell, were repeatedly not addressed across services”.

One recommendation for Rotherham NHS Foundation Trust was to introduce structured documentation protocols for recording parental concerns.

Family-requested recommendations included ensuring families are informed that when their children are on ventilators, they may still be able to hear, and should be encouraged to talk to and interact with them and ensuring that any items such as clothing or equipment can be saved so families can keep as memories when a child dies.

Yusuf’s mother, Soniya Ahmed, and uncle, Zaheer Ahmed, met health secretary Yvette Cooper at the Department of Health and Social Care in London on Thursday morning.

![Yusuf’s mother, Soniya Ahmed, and uncle, Zaheer Ahmed, met health secretary Yvette Cooper at the Department of Health and Social Care in London on Thursday morning](https://static.independent.co.uk/2025/12/10/12/23/Yusuf-Mahmud-Nazir-family-meet-Wes-Streeting-86i8b1rh.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Mr Ahmed said it was a “very good meeting” and they discussed recommendations for the NHS made in the report.

He said: “We discussed the recommendations, how the recommendations are being implemented throughout the NHS, national recommendations and recommendations for Rotherham Hospital and Sheffield Hospital, so we spoke a bit about that.

“The health secretary seems very, very keen on concentrating on children’s care at the moment. It’s a very big focus for her at the moment throughout the A&E for children’s care and the children’s specialist hospitals, so it is something she’s really focusing on, and it’s good to see that there’s a lot of focus being put in that area because it is an area that needs to be improved dramatically.”

He said: “It does seem from her reactions that she is very committed to following these changes, making these changes.”

Mr Ahmed said the family have also called for a meeting with Andy Burnham and want him to “listen to a grieving family and how it’s affecting us”.

“We want the head of government to listen to the voice of a family, a grieving family, after losing a child,” he said.

“Yusuf wasn’t just a number. He wasn’t just a patient. He wasn’t just another complaint. He was a living child.

“So we’re here to make changes. We don’t want any other family to go through what we’re going through, and ultimately, it’s about working together to implement these changes, follow these changes.”

The fresh report said it “is impossible to make a statement on whether (intravenous) antibiotics or earlier detection would have prevented Yusuf’s death”.

Mr Ahmed added that “one of the key recommendations is to listen to parents’ voices, listen to parental concerns, and take relevant action from there”.

Mr Ahmed said the last four years have been “very very tough for the family”, adding: “A day doesn’t go by where we don’t think about Yusuf, and then on top of this, we’ve had two investigations to fight for, and we seem to be fighting for the truth.

“We’re fighting for the truth consistently, it shouldn’t be difficult to fight, you know, to know how your child’s died, but we’re still fighting for answers.”

Mr Ahmed described Yusuf as “a very very happy, healthy child, full of energy”, and said he was “very special”.

Yusuf, who had asthma, was taken to a GP with a sore throat and feeling unwell on 15 November 2022.

He was prescribed antibiotics by an advanced nurse practitioner.

Later that evening, his parents took him to Rotherham Hospital urgent and emergency care centre, where he was seen in the early hours of the morning after a six-hour wait.

He was discharged with a diagnosis of severe tonsillitis and an extended prescription of antibiotics.

Yusuf’s family have always said they were told “there are no beds and not enough doctors” in the emergency department at Rotherham, and that Yusuf should have been admitted and given intravenous antibiotics.

Two days later, Yusuf was given further antibiotics by his GP for a possible chest infection, but his family became so concerned they called an ambulance and insisted the paramedics take him to Sheffield Children’s Hospital rather than Rotherham.

![Yusuf’s family have always said they were told ‘there are no beds and not enough doctors’ in the emergency department at Rotherham, and that Yusuf should have been admitted and given intravenous antibiotics](https://static.independent.co.uk/2025/07/31/13/31130921-c2490e25-30b9-4798-a7a5-14c0a55ea0d0.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Yusuf was admitted to the intensive care unit on 21 November but developed multi-organ failure and suffered several cardiac arrests, which he did not survive.

The July 2025 report said in its conclusions: “Our primary finding is that the parental concerns, particularly the mother’s instinct that her child was unwell, were repeatedly not addressed across services.

“A reliance on clinical metrics over caregiver insight caused distress for the family.

“This led to a lack of shared decision-making and there was limited evidence of collaborative discussions with Yusuf’s family around clinical decisions, leading to a sense of exclusion and reduced trust in care plans.”

The fresh report by Peter Carter, former general secretary of the Royal College of Nursing, said it was agreed that Yusuf had pneumonia and sepsis, which led to respiratory failure and his death, but “no causative agent” could be found.

It said: “It is impossible to make a statement on whether (intravenous) antibiotics or earlier detection would have prevented Yusuf’s death.

“We understand that this is not the information that the family obtained initially and appreciate how this difference of professional opinion has led to confusion and emotional distress for the family.”

The report said Yusuf had 23 separate healthcare contacts across four organisations “with no single, co-ordinated record or oversight, contributing to fragmented and disjointed care”.

Last year, an inquest into Yusuf’s death was opened and adjourned by Sheffield’s senior coroner, Tanyka Rawden.

A Department of Health and Social Care spokesperson said: “The secretary of state was honoured to meet Yusuf’s family today, to share her sadness over his loss, and to thank them for the great determination they have shown in fighting for answers and change. No other family should have to go through the same heartbreak that Yusuf’s family have endured.

“The failings identified in Yusuf’s care must lead to real improvements, and the Secretary of State told his family that she would ensure NHS England continues working with local organisations to put those improvements into practice, and make sure that the voices of patients and families are always listened to when their children are in need of help.”
