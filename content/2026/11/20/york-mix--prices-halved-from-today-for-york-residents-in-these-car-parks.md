Parking prices for York residents will be halved for York residents from today (Friday, 20 November) at community car parks across the city.

These include Bishopthorpe Road, Rowntree Park and East Parade car parks.

City of York Council has made two changes to reduce the parking charges for York residents.

For Minster Badge holders, the hourly cost of parking will be reduced from £3 per hour to £1.50 per hour and the cost of a Minster Badge will be reduced from £45 for two years to £15 for one year.

### Community car park charges

| Length of stay | Monday to Sunday | Minster Badge holder Monday to Sunday |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Up to 1 hour | £3.00 | £1.50 |
| Up to 2 hours | £6.00 | £3.00 |
| Up to 3 hours | £9.00 | £4.50 |

It follows 18 months of campaigning by traders and residents to bring back affordable parking for people to access their local shops and support their local community.

In March 2025 car parking prices across the city were increased, most noticeably by 500% at the Bishopthorpe Road shopper’s car park.

It was part of the council’s strategy to reduce traffic congestion in the city by encouraging people to switch to public transport.

However, shop owners in areas such as Bishy Road, the Groves and East Parade say they have seen a drop of up to 20% in customers after the charge hike.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/parking-sign-micklegate-york-sept-2025-2.webp)

After a lengthy review process York council agreed to lower the car parking prices at community car parks following a 36% drop in car parking transactions together with the impact felt by both residents and traders.

The reduction will also be available at on-street parking on Lawrence Street, Micklegate, Priory Street, Clifton, Garden Street, Huntington Road, Lowther Street, Marygate, Park Grove, Penley’s Grove Street, The Crescent, The Mount, Townend Street and Union Terrace.

Joe Nasson of the Bishopthorpe Road Traders Association said today: “We are pleased that York council have listened to traders and residents and lowered the car parking prices at the Bishy Road shopper’s car park.

“We hope that the reduced car parking prices will encourage customers back to the street who were driven elsewhere by the extortionate high parking charges.

“We are not pro-car and we support the council’s aim to reduce car dependency. However, we need to improve the bus services and cycle racks before huge hikes to car parking.

“The last 18 months have shown that increasing charges alone doesn’t lead to a change to more sustainable travel instead this has led to York residents no longer shopping local.”
