## Hungary is set to lift its objections to Ukraine’s last two EU accession clusters on Friday, diplomats say, allowing Kyiv and Moldova to start negotiating on every aspect of EU membership.

Hungary is set to drop its veto on opening Ukraine’s final two EU accession negotiating clusters at a meeting of EU ambassadors on Friday, two diplomatic sources have told Euronews.

András Bíró-Nagy, Hungary’s Secretary of State for EU Affairs at the Prime Minister’s Office, signalled the move during a discussion on enlargement ahead of Thursday’s General Affairs Council in Brussels.

Budapest has long held up Ukraine’s accession bid by blocking the opening of negotiating clusters. Its veto has also stalled Moldova’s membership bid, which remains linked to Kyiv’s.

Hungary’s position began to shift after Péter Magyar ousted Viktor Orbán following 16 years in power. The first negotiating cluster was opened in June, a second in July and two more in October.

Last month, Kyiv and Budapest reached an agreement on the rights of the ethnic Hungarian minority in western Ukraine, which the Ukrainian parliament subsequently passed.

The Hungarian government was then ready to move forward but held off for several more weeks on opening the final two clusters. Years of anti-Ukrainian rhetoric under Orbán had made the move politically difficult at home.

Magyar and Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy held their first bilateral meeting on the margins of the last European Council summit in Brussels on 16 October, marking another step in the warming of the relations between the two neighbours.

Once Hungary confirms on Friday that it is lifting the veto, Ukraine and Moldova are due to open all negotiating clusters, including some of the most sensitive chapters, on the environment and agriculture.

Agriculture will be particularly sensitive for Kyiv. The European Commission has proposed to carve out access to EU funding and the European market for certain Ukrainian agricultural products, under pressure from countries such as Poland and France.

Ukrainian Prime Minister Sergii Koretskyi told Euronews Kyiv could not accept a permanent exclusion of its farmers from the European market, but that the measures had to be transitional and proportionate.

Opening the clusters is only the beginning. Candidates must then align with EU legislation and practice, and throughout the process, member states can veto progress more than 100 times.
