The owner of P’Nut the Squirrel, the internet-famous pet whose tragic killing by New York wildlife officials sparked outrage in 2024, has been arrested on 14 felony charges, including mortgage fraud and grand larceny.

Mark Longo, 36, was arrested Wednesday following an investigation into allegations that he used falsified financial documents to secure a $720,000 residential mortgage, New York State Police said and reported by the *Associated Press reports*.

The arrest comes weeks after Longo’s wife, Daniela Bittner, who is originally from Germany, was detained by federal immigration authorities in October while traveling through a small airport in upstate New York.

Longo is accused of submitting fraudulent pay stubs, letters and mortgage application documents to obtain the loan. The charges include second-degree residential mortgage fraud, second-degree grand larceny, eight counts of first-degree falsifying business records and two counts of fourth-degree conspiracy. He also faces attempted mortgage fraud and attempted grand larceny charges.

The investigation began in December 2024, weeks after state officials seized and euthanized P’Nut, when police received information about alleged financial irregularities involving Longo, New York State Police spokesperson Beau Duffy told the *AP*.

![Mark Longo, the former owner of the internet-famous P’Nut the Squirrel, has been arrested on 14 felony charges, including mortgage fraud and grand larceny](https://static.the-independent.com/2026/11/20/20/12/PNut-the-squirrel-owner-arrested.png?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Longo, who was living in Chemung County, New York, pleaded not guilty to the charges. He was released on his own recognizance and is scheduled to return to court February 4.

Longo and Bittner, who operate P’Nut’s Freedom Farm Animal Sanctuary in upstate New York, have been in legal trouble before. The couple made national headlines in October 2024 after New York State Department of Environmental Conservation officials seized P’Nut and a raccoon named Fred from their property following complaints that the animals were being illegally kept as pets.

P’Nut was a social media sensation, attracting hundreds of thousands of followers through videos showing the squirrel climbing on Longo’s back, playing with him and wearing tiny cowboy hats.

He became the center of a national controversy after wildlife officials seized him. During an inspection, the squirrel reportedly bit a biologist’s gloved thumb, prompting officials to euthanize him for rabies testing. Fred was also put down.

Both animals later tested negative for rabies, sparking widespread backlash.

Prominent Republicans, including Elon Musk and then-vice presidential candidate JD Vance, criticized the decision. Vance famously claimed that “Democrats murdered the Elon Musk of squirrels” at a 2024 campaign rally.

The backlash escalated into threats against state environmental officials, prompting the agency to temporarily let employees work remotely.

In August 2025, Longo sued the New York State Department of Environmental Conservation for $10 million, accusing officials of government abuse over the animals’ seizure and deaths.

The charges against Longo are unrelated to the P’Nut investigation.

Bittner says Immigration and Customs Enforcement threatened to deport her Thursday and bar her from returning to the U.S. for 10 years, despite federal protections against her removal, according to her attorney, Reuben S. Kerben.

The Second Circuit granted her an emergency stay Wednesday while it considers her appeal.

“Daniela was terrified that she would be put on a plane and separated from her husband for potentially 10 years,” Kerben said in a statement to *The Independent*, questioning whether the threat was an intimidation tactic or a breakdown in communication within ICE.

Although she was not deported Thursday, Bittner remains in ICE custody in Texas, separated from Longo and their animal sanctuary.
