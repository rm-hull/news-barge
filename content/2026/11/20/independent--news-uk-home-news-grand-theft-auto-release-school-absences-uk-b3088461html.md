More than 35,000 teenagers skipped school on Thursday morning after the latest Grand Theft Auto (GTA) game was released.

Figures from Arbor, which provides school management software to 1,415 secondary schools in England, shows attendance rates fell from around 91.3 per cent from Monday to Wednesday to 88.8 per cent on Thursday morning, a difference of around 35,000 pupils.

Thursday also saw the lowest attendance of any school day over the last two weeks, despite significant efforts from some schools to ensure pupils attended. Multiple schools across the country wrote to parents ahead of the release encouraging them to ensure pupils attended classes the next day, and others said they would enter students into prize draws if they missed no lessons this week.

One school located in Birmingham said it would deploy a minibus to pick up absent students and bring them onto the campus.

GTA 6 is the highly-anticipated next instalment in the gaming franchise, which was released to UK users at midnight on Thursday. Some 2.8 million UK adults were set to play the game on launch day alone, according to a Uswitch poll commissioned earlier this month.

![The launch of Grand Theft Auto VI broke more broadband records as gamers rushed to download it on Wednesday night](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/20/08/01M7T9XSDJAZZXZDZ1YV81C4Y5.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Bradford Forster Academy, a West Yorkshire secondary school, requested that families uphold routine sleep schedules to ensure that children "attend school on time" this week.

In a letter to parents and carers ahead of the release of GTA 6, the school said: “We would encourage parents to discuss not only whether their child is accessing the game, but also when they are accessing it and how this may affect their well-being, sleep and readiness for school.

“We would also encourage families to review parental controls and monitor gaming activity where appropriate.”

The letter added: “Regardless of interest in the game’s release, all students are expected to attend school as normal on Thursday.

“We will continue to monitor attendance closely and follow up any absence in line with our usual procedures.”

The number of people off sick or requesting annual leave also soared on Thursday, according to HR software firms.

BrightHR, which serves more than one million employees at around 70,000 companies in the UK, said it recorded a 56 per cent increase in sickness absence on Thursday, compared to Thursday last week.

Deel, which provides HR services for thousands of UK firms, said there had been a 21 per cent increase in the number of requests to have this Thursday off work, compared to Thursday November 20, 2025.

Virgin Media O2 said its traffic record between 8pm and 10pm on Wednesday was 9.4% higher than the previous evening record, set last week by those “pre-loading” the software ahead of the release.

BT and EE networks also recorded its highest-ever home broadband traffic peak in the lead up to the midnight release.
