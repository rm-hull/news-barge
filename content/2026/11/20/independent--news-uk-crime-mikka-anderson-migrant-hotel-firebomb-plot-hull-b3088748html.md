A white supremacist has been found guilty of plotting to firebomb a migrant hotel in Hull and kill as many occupants as possible.

Mikka Anderson, 24, attempted to recruit an accomplice to his scheme, telling him he would fire petrol bombs at the Royal Hotel in Hull, which had been the scene of previous protests.

However, that man went to the police and informed them of Anderson’s plan, telling jurors he thought terrorism was “disgusting” and that innocent people should not be killed.

The defendant, from Hull, denied preparing an act of terrorism including by manufacturing Molotov cocktails and by conducting “hostile reconnaissance” on the hotel.

He also denied possessing knives and other weapons for terrorist purposes, but was convicted of both counts on Friday following a trial at Newcastle Crown Court.

![Knives were found during a search of Anderson’s home](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/20/15/01M7WTS1FJ610GWDEDW61JE57G.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Mrs Justice Lambert told the defendant he will be sentenced next year once a psychological report is completed and an assessment of his dangerousness is made.

She added: “It is very likely indeed, if not inevitable, that I will be imposing a very significant custodial sentence.”

Anderson first reached out to his would-be accomplice through social media, knowing that the man – who cannot be identified by the media – had been involved in the anti-migrant disorder in the city.

On a secure messaging platform, Anderson informed the man he was in a group of “white Christian nationalists” whose “main objective is fighting back against Islam, paedophile elites and Jews”.

The two men met up in person in the city centre and Adam Birkby, prosecuting, told jurors how Anderson outlined “his plan to attack the Royal Hotel by the city’s railway station, and to kill as many of the migrants who were housed inside it as possible”.

Mr Birkby said that during the meeting, where the two men laid down their phones, Anderson revealed “he had a crossbow and that he had created some form of device that he would use to launch the Molotov cocktails through the hotel windows”.

During the trial, the would-be accomplice told jurors Anderson explained his rationale for targeting the hotel, saying: “He said it was full of nonces, rapists, people that shouldn’t be in our country.”

![Mikka Anderson has been told he can expect a ‘very significant custodial sentence’](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/20/15/01M7WTSD3F54VP54AS1DZPXG29.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

After the informer told police about the plot, Anderson was arrested and knives, a crossbow and petrol bombs were found during a search of his home.

He also had a copy of the Anarchist’s Cookbook, which the prosecution described as “a how-to guide for the wannabe terrorist”.

He had made or attempted to create napalm, gunpowder and fuses, the court heard.

He carried out reconnaissance on the hotel, which housed migrants until September this year, checking for weaknesses in security.

In February this year, the media reported that a 13-year-old girl had been repeatedly raped by an Iranian asylum seeker at a different hotel.

Mr Birkby said: “As the defendant later explained in his police interview, his reaction to that news was to tell a friend that he was going to attack the Royal Hotel.”

Anderson denied being a terrorist or racist, although he had said racist things online.

He claimed to have been playing a role and “bigged himself up” with extreme right-wing supporters online.

He also said the cache of weapons was linked to his interest in the outdoors and “prepping”.

Anderson believed in an antisemitic conspiracy theory, the Great Replacement, jurors were told, which claims politicians and global elites plotted to swap the white population with non-white immigrants.

Mr Birkby said Anderson told a friend last November that the UK Government was building an army of “cultist, fanatic, inbred lunatics”, and added: “There’s rumours the Government have been shipping guns into Scotland and the weapons are being transferred and stored in mosques.”

![The prosecution said Anderson admired Hitler and referred to himself as a Nazi](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/20/14/23/Mikka-Lee-Anderson.png?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The prosecution said Anderson admired Hitler and referred to himself as a Nazi.

Mr Birkby said Anderson told an associate in September 2025: “I am stocking up and preparing for violence… Muslims aren’t here to assimilate, they’re here to take over.

“They’re a literal bioweapon. One day they will turn this country into a warzone.”

During the trial, it emerged that Anderson, who grew up in France, Spain, Cornwall, Devon and finally moved with his family to the North York Moors, had tried to join the Royal Marines.

He also studied to become a gamekeeper at agricultural college, but worked for a kitchen manufacturer and lived in Hull with his Polish fiancee.

Anderson had tried to buy a piece of land in Devon with fellow right-wingers, the court heard, saying they could use it as a shooting ground and a site to practice unit tactics.

Earlier, he had looked into Islam but turned away from it and had also previously been a Mormon.

He developed an interest in the extreme-right following the Southport riots, the court heard, finding a sense of “brotherhood” with others after experiencing a somewhat isolated childhood.

![Anderson had a copy of the Anarchist’s Cookbook](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/20/15/01M7WTRXSH656G79H5Z1PCB7QW.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The judge reminded the jury how Anderson had claimed he was not extreme right-wing, as he believed in democracy and “he was not a racist although he said racist things”.

Before the trial, Anderson admitted possessing information likely to be useful to a person committing or preparing an act of terrorism, and possession of a blade in a public place.

Outside court, Detective Chief Superintendent James Dunkerley, head of Counter Terrorism Policing North East, said: “Mikka Anderson’s extreme right-wing views led him to plan a terrorist attack in order to spread fear amongst the migrant community.

“Counter Terrorism Policing officers and our partners conducted an in-depth investigation to understand the threat that Anderson posed and were able to apprehend him before anyone came to harm.”
