If we're using Metacritic as a metric, Grand Theft Auto 6 is currently the best-reviewed game of all time. Between preorders and launch-day sales, it's already made over $3.7 billion in sales revenue. It's being lauded as a landmark achievement in game design, storytelling, and engineering. So how, exactly, did Rockstar pull it off?

Well, the billion-dollar production budget certainly helped. But a Bloomberg report offers an important insight into how GTA 6 was able to deliver on its promises: According to a spokesperson, 21% of Rockstar's staff—roughly 1,000 people—have been employed by the company for over 10 years. For many of its development leads and directors, that figure is closer to 20 years, or higher.

![Jason inserting his card into an ATM in GTA 6.](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/Wq64uRf6b7bSqqUsy6zRjb-1200-80.jpg)

By retaining its employees for so long, Rockstar is able to capitalize on the kind of shared, institutional knowledge and honed creative process that developers build through years of collaboration. We've seen that same kind of continuity at other studios who've delivered some of the last decade's biggest hits: FromSoft director Hidetaka Miyazaki has attributed its wins with Elden Ring and Armored Core 6 to its developers growing with the studio, while the breakout sales successes of Baldur's Gate 3 and Helldivers 2 were delivered by developers who'd spent years sharpening their craft at Larian and Arrowhead.

That lesson—that making successful games is easier when you keep the people who know how to make games around—isn't one that the games industry at large has been adhering to: In its latest game developer survey, GDC reported that 34% of its respondents had been laid off in the last two years. 20% of laid-off respondents said they'd been laid off twice or more in 24 months.

From Halo to Dragon Age, we've seen how beloved game franchises can suffer when their studios endure employment turmoil. After it laid off 1,600 Xbox workers in July, Microsoft's own employees said that myopic job cuts at major game companies were weakening the games industry as a whole by dismantling its ability to produce quality games.

It's a bell we've been ringing a lot around here.

Still, while Rockstar boasts a deep roster of veteran employees, those devs aren't confident that they won't be the next ones suffering from mass layoffs: We've seen plenty of successful launches lead to job cuts in recent years. As the company enjoys GTA 6's record-breaking launch, fears like those are why over 1,600 Rockstar employees in the UK are currently voting on unionization.
