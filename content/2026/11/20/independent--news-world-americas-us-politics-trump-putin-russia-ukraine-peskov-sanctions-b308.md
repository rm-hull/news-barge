Donald Trump offered a warm greeting to Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov at the Apec summit in China Thursday, patting him on the arm and telling him: “You’re doing a good job.”

The president appeared to have no qualms about showing support for Peskov, despite the Treasury sanctioning him in response to the invasion of Ukraine in February 2022.

Under Trump’s predecessor Joe Biden, the U.S. government included the “lead propagandist and spokesperson of the Russian Federation” on a list of Kremlin elites and oligarchs who enabled the war.

Peskov was accused of routinely lying about Russia’s intentions towards Ukraine before the war, about atrocities carried out by its soldiers in the city of Bucha in the ensuing conflict and over the poisonings of dissidents Alexander Litvinenko and Sergei Skripal in 2008 and 2018 respectively.

Trump was joined by his treasury secretary Scott Bessent, the head of the same department that sanction Peskov four years ago.

![President Donald Trump offered a hearty greeting to Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov at the Apec summit in China](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/19/15/2305268353..?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&auto=webp)

The commander-in-chief was even more friendly with Russian President Vladimir Putin when he encountered him in a meeting room at the Qilin Shanzhuang residence in Shenzen, pointing a finger and jokingly asking: “Who is he?”

Marching up to Putin with a grin, he answered his own question: “He’s my friend! Nice to see you.”

Trump went on to say of his Russian counterpart: “We’ve had a great relationship for many years. We’ve known each other for a long time, and I think a lot of wonderful things can come out of this, or at least start. But I think they’ll come out of this.”

The exchange, which was followed by a hearty handshake, has already seen the president branded “an embarrassment for America” and the moment labeled “beyond nauseating.”

The president had trailed his meeting with the Russian by saying it would largely concern the Ukraine war, declaring: “I think things are happening, I think [Putin] and hopefully the other side would like to see an end to that war.”

After they had emerged from 40 minutes of private talks, the Kremlin briefed the press that the two men had indeed discussed Ukraine and that Putin had left in an “excellent” mood.

![Trump joined Chinese Premier Xi and Russian President Vladimir Putin for a ‘historic’ trilateral in Shenzen, China, and left with an commitment to blocking nuclear proliferation](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/19/15/2305279486..?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&auto=webp)

That detail is likely to be met with apprehension by Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky, who has endured a rollercoaster relationship with Trump ever since they clashed in the Oval Office in February 2025.

Zelensky revealed last week that U.S. negotiators had proposed his country cede the entirety of its eastern Donbas region – previously rejected as a red line by Kyiv – in exchange for Nato membership, an outcome Moscow has long opposed. He said he would be prepared to hold a referendum to allow the Ukrainian public to vote on the proposition should the U.S. and Nato support it.

Trump said in a Fox News interview in Shenzen that Zelensky and Putin “have got to get along” and insisted that the conflict would never have erupted if he had been in the White House at the time, also expressing indifference to its outcome: “It doesn’t affect us. In fact, I guess you could say… we make a lot of money.”

Trump, Putin and Xi ultimately emerged from their summit with a pledge to hold further top-level discussions on curtailing nuclear proliferation and agreed on a four-point “peace declaration,” reaffirming their shared opposition to weapons of mass destruction.

They also promised to advance reforms of the United Nations, to increase supplies of refined petroleum products for all countries and to uphold the immunity enjoyed by state officials in international courts.

The latter point is particularly significant for Putin because the International Criminal Court in The Hague has issued arrest warrants for him and five other Russian officials.
