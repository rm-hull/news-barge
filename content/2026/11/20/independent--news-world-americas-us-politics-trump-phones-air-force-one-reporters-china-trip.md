White House reporters traveling aboard Air Force One had their phones temporarily confiscated before President Donald Trump’s plane took off after his trilateral summit with Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin, raising the specter of an unscheduled detour on his way back to Washington D.C.

U.S. officials collected reporters’ devices as the president’s plane prepared to leave Shenzhen, China, Thursday evening.

“At 6:45 pm consider this your wheels up,” the White House pool report read. “Our phones are being taken away for now for security reasons.”

No one on the trip has provided more information. The White House did not immediately respond to a request for more information.

It is highly unusual for reporters’ phones to be sequestered outside of situations when the president is traveling to a war zone, such as when Trump flew to Al Asad Air Base in Iraq at Christmas in 2018, and to Bagram Air Field in Afghanistan for Thanksgiving in 2019.

![The decision to sequester reporters’ phones touched off speculation over whether Trump would be extending his trip for a meeting with Kim Jong Un](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/19/14/2026-11-19T105235Z_1043529915_RC2YQOARQN7L_RTRMADP_3_APEC-SUMMIT-TRUMP.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Fox News correspondent Jacqui Heinrich, the current president of the White House Correspondents’ Association, pointed out on X that the pool’s phones were not taken when Trump left Beijing after his state visit in May.

The move was later explained as a cybersecurity measure, as the phones used by journalists in China were placed in a metal-lined Faraday bag to prevent them from emitting any signals while the aircraft was aloft.

Most of the devices that were collected were so-called burner phones used only for the travel to China, as journalists had previously stored their regular devices in similar bags aboard Air Force One for use during the Philippines leg of the trip and while en route to Washington.

Separately, a flight restriction that had been imposed over Alaska around the time Air Force One had been scheduled to refuel there was lifted Thursday, raising questions as to the plane’s destination.

The most obvious candidate for a detour was North Korea. Trump spent the days before the summit musing that Kim Jong Un might join him when he met with China’s Xi and Russia’s Putin on the sidelines of the Asia Pacific Economic Cooperation summit.

On Wednesday, Secretary of State Marco Rubio told reporters in Manila that the president was “prepared” to meet the North Korean leader.

“It may not happen, but he’s prepared to do it,” Rubio said.

![Trump described the Russian and Chinese dictators as his 'friends' at the unprecedented trilateral meeting, the first between Russian, Chinese and American heads of state](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/19/10/download.-5..jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

However on a call with reporters before the trip, senior U.S. officials said there were “no plans at all for engagement with North Korea.”

Trump relished the pageantry and attention that were lavished on his first two meetings with Kim, though neither sit-down resulted in the denuclearization deals he had promised to reach with the brutal autocrat.

He also met Kim for a third time during a visit to the Korean Demilitarized Zone and briefly became the first American president to set foot in North Korea.

The intrigue surrounding the president’s departure from China followed Trump’s participation in the first-ever meeting between an American president and the autocrats who have led the other two major nuclear powers for a combined 41 years.

Trump gave Putin a warm reception when he met with him for a bilateral meeting immediately preceding the three-way sit-down.

The U.S. president pointed, smiled and called out “Who is that? He’s my friend!” as he walked into a room with his Russian counterpart. The scene was captured by TV cameras that had been permitted in, even as journalists were barred from attending to prevent the leaders from being asked any uncomfortable questions, though a Russian official attributed the move to space constraints.

Trump said of Putin: “We’ve had a great relationship for many years. We know each other for a long time, and I think a lot of wonderful things can come out of this, or at least start. But I think they’ll come out of this.”

The Kremlin said after the meeting that the pair had discussed the ongoing war in Ukraine, and that Putin left in an “excellent” mood.

The White House offered no official readout of the sit-down, which lasted approximately 40 minutes.

Following the trilateral meeting, the Russian, Chinese and American governments released a four-point “peace declaration” reaffirming that a nuclear war should never be fought and vowing to engage in dialogue on reducing nuclear risks.

The three leaders also pledged to advance reforms of the United Nations, to increase supplies of refined petroleum products for all countries and to uphold the immunity enjoyed by state officials in international courts.

The International Criminal Court in The Hague has issued arrest warrants for Putin and five other Russian officials. The Trump administration has been working to delegitimize and defund the ICC out of fear that Trump and his associates could be subject to war crimes charges over America’s campaign of airstrikes against alleged drug smugglers or an airstrike against an Iranian school that left over 100 children dead.
