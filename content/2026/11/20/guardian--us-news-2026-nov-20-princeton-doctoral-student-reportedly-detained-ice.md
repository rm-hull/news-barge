A Princeton University doctoral student has reportedly been taken into custody by US Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) after traveling to Washington DC, for a neuroscience conference.

Eva Yi Xie, a third-year PhD candidate at the Princeton Neuroscience Institute, was detained Thursday at a Washington-area airport, according to university officials and her colleagues.

The Daily Princetonian, the university’s student newspaper, reported that Xie is believed to be held at an immigration detention facility in Chantilly, Virginia.

The Brody Lab, a Princeton neuroscience research group, identified Ronald Reagan National airport as the location of her detention in a post on X. According to the lab, Xie had been returning from the Society for Neuroscience conference, where she presented a research poster, when immigration authorities detained her.

Princeton has since secured legal representation for Xie, university spokesperson Michael Hotchkiss confirmed to the Daily Princetonian.

The detention also prompted a late-night message to graduate students from Rodney Priestley, dean of Princeton’s graduate school. Priestley sent the email to students at the Princeton Neuroscience Institute late Thursday night, acknowledging the situation and outlining the university’s response.

“We hope she is able to soon resume her studies at Princeton,” the email, obtained by the Daily Princetonian, read. “Please be assured that we will stay in close contact with the student’s family, her legal team, and her PNI colleagues as this situation is resolved.”

Xie’s current immigration status has not been publicly disclosed.

Xie moved to the US from China in 2016. She earned degrees in computer science and mathematics from the Massachusetts Institute of Technology in 2024, alongside a concentration in economics, according to her portfolio.

Her professional experience includes internships at Meta, Microsoft AI and IBM Research, among other organizations. At Princeton, her doctoral work focuses on the “neural basis of decision-making,” according to the Daily Princetonian.

The Guardian has reached out to ICE and Princeton for comment.

Xie’s detention comes amid an aggressive nationwide immigration enforcement campaign under Donald Trump, whose administration has made mass deportations a defining priority.

Department of Homeland Security (DHS) figures show that ICE deported 483,200 people and conducted more than 491,000 immigration arrests during fiscal year 2026, setting new annual records for the agency.

The Trump administration maintains that its enforcement efforts are intended to uphold immigration laws and remove dangerous offenders from the country. However, federal figures and independent reviews indicate that substantial numbers of people kept in ICE detention have no criminal convictions.

The enforcement campaign has affected international students and immigrants who originally entered the US legally. Xie’s detention now joins a string of prominent immigration cases that have drawn worldwide scrutiny and raised serious questions about the administration’s tactics.

Among those cases is Rümeysa Öztürk, a Tufts University student detained by immigration agents in Massachusetts last year. Videos of Öztürk’s arrest spread rapidly online, drawing widespread attention to the administration’s treatment of international students.

She was among numerous foreign students targeted over pro-Palestinian expression and protest during demonstrations against Israel’s military campaign in Gaza. Those protests have been particularly prominent on American university campuses.

Another high-profile case involved Mahmoud Khalil, a 30-year-old Palestinian permanent resident and Columbia University graduate student who emerged as a leading figure in campus protests last year.

Khalil was arrested without due process in front of his pregnant wife and subsequently detained at an immigration facility. Authorities denied him permission to leave detention to attend the birth of his child.

The administration’s enforcement policies also came under intense scrutiny after the wrongful deportation of Kilmar Ábrego García, a Salvadoran immigrant who had been living in Maryland. Ábrego García was deported from the US to El Salvador in March 2025, despite the Trump administration’s admission that the removal was an error.
