A letter sent to employees from the Lufthansa Airlines Board, which has been obtained by The Local, confirmed the cuts.

"At our Frankfurt and Munich hubs, we now plan to reduce a total of 1,000 full-time positions across Lufthansa Airlines," the letter from Lufthansa CEO Jens Ritter and other board members said. The cuts amount to around 2.5 percent of its roughly 40,000 employees.

The airline added that it was looking at other measures including reducing short and medium-haul flights further and reviewing structures "across the different areas of Lufthansa Airlines with a focus on efficiency and long-term competitiveness".

Lufthansa, which also operates carriers including Austrian, Swiss and Brussels Airlines, last year announced plans to cut 4,000 jobs across the whole group by 2030, mainly targeting administrative roles.

It was not immediately clear whether the cuts disclosed on Friday were included in this round of layoffs, or in addition.

"We have achieved a great deal through the turnaround programme," the airline said to staff in the letter.

"We have stabilised our operations and improved customer satisfaction. Since 2024, we have also succeeded in generating more than €1.5 billion in earnings contributions."

However, the board members said the measures announced so far are "no longer enough to secure our future".

"After two years of the turnaround, however, we have to acknowledge that the original measures are no longer enough to secure our future," the letter says.

"In fact, 2026 has set us back: the war in the Middle East, persistently high fuel costs, nine days of strikes and our already very high cost base have made the situation even more difficult.

"If, like us, you do not achieve sufficient profitability in good times, difficult times hit you all the harder. We therefore need to make even bigger changes. Lufthansa Airlines is currently not competitive in terms of its cost structure, efficiency and productivity.

"We have reached a point where we need to make even more far-reaching changes to our company."

During the first nine months of the year, the airline was losing on average €20 million a month, it said.

However, overall the wider Lufthansa group appears to be in good financial health.

It reported a 30-percent jump in profits in the July-September period, beating forecasts, boosted by demand for premium tickets on long-haul routes.

It remains unclear when the cuts will take place and how they will be implemented.

The board said it would provide a "spotlight" for employees to ask questions about the planned cuts on November 24th.

A spokesperson from the Lufthansa Group told The Local: "We have nothing to add to the statements made by the divisional board member of Lufthansa Airlines at this time."
