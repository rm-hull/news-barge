A former Trump administration official claims that Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth has done “a lot of damage” at the helm of the Pentagon and has “hurt” the president in the process.

John Ullyot, who briefly served as a Pentagon spokesperson under Hegseth in early 2025 and has worked for Trump since 2016, said he “felt the need to speak out” after the former Fox News host left the Defense Department “in disarray.”

“Hegseth is the least qualified and most polarizing Defense Secretary in modern memory,” Ullyot told *The New Yorker* while promoting his new book titled “The President Deserves Better.”

“And as a Marine veteran, and as someone who has spent a lot of time in both the Senate Armed Services Committee and at the National Security Council working with senior members of the military and senior Pentagon leaders, I felt the need to speak out, because he was doing real damage and it was hurting the President as well,” Ullyot said.

In response to *The Independent*’s request for comment, White House spokeswoman Anna Kelly simply said, “Who?”

![A former Trump administration official claims that Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth has done ‘a lot of damage’ at the helm of the Pentagon and has ‘hurt’ President Donald Trump in the process](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/27/18/24/GettyImages-2287743633.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The Pentagon did not immediately respond but deputy press secretary Joel Valdez previously likened Ullyot to “a crazy ex.”

“John Ullyot, who was exiled from the MAGA movement by Donald Trump Jr., is acting like a crazy ex, and quite frankly it’s embarrassing,” Valdez previously told *The Independent.* “John was terrible at his job and now lashes out at the Secretary any chance he gets.”

In the near two years that Hegseth has presided over the Pentagon, he has been embroiled in a scandal that endangered the lives of U.S. troops by sharing sensitive military plans in a Signal group chat; been accused of war crimes for issuing a lethal “double-tap” airstrike on alleged drug boats; and been blamed for pushing Trump into the war in Iran.

Hegseth narrowly escaped impeachment when the GOP-led House blocked a vote on eight articles against him on November 10, but he remains under intense scrutiny after the disastrous midterm results.

Ullyot, a former White House deputy assistant in the first Trump administration who also served as chief National Security Council spokesperson, said he does not believe that the president “has paid attention to the damage that Hegseth has done on either the cultural front or the self-promoting front” or to leaders of the military.

He also criticized the president for not doing his “due diligence” when selecting Hegseth as his Defense Secretary. “I think President Trump just saw him on Fox News, liked what he saw, and didn’t say, OK, let’s look into his background,” Ullyot told the *New Yorker.*

The former adviser pointed to the president’s decision to hire people based on loyalty in his second administration, whereas in his first term he hired people based on outside recommendations. “And he felt, I believe, that he was not well served by several of them,” Ullyot said.

![John Ullyot, who briefly served as a Pentagon spokesperson under Hegseth, said his former boss ‘is the least qualified and most polarizing Defense Secretary in modern memory’](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/20/16/2203664500..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Ullyot called for Hegseth to resign in a recent op-ed for *The Atlantic*, citing the recent embarrassing incident when his group was reprimanded by an Army sentinel at the Tomb of the Unknown Soldier on Veterans Day.

“It should not take a soldier to remind a secretary of defense about the decorum his office demands,” he wrote. “The president deserves better, as do the members of our military.”

Following the Republican wipeout in the midterm elections, Hegseth mounted a defense campaign when reports began to swirl that Trump had asked aides whether to fire him.

In the articles of impeachment, Hegseth was accused of unlawfully waging war in Iran and Yemen, capturing former Venezuelan President Nicolás Maduro and striking suspected drug trafficking boats. At a press conference, Hegseth implied he was just following Trump’s orders to defend his wartime actions.

“Iran, the cartels, Maduro, Yemen — every one was a lawful operation ordered by the commander-in-chief, then ordered by myself, notified to Congress, and executed by American warriors who did their jobs,” Hegseth told reporters.

Trump voiced support for Hegseth ahead of the House vote that ultimately failed.

“He’s guilty of two things, basically winning two wars,” Trump said. “I believe we saved the Middle East and Israel combined. And it’s awfully hard to say let’s impeach somebody who did that.”
