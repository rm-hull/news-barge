Less than a day after returning from a whirlwind trip to Asia that was long on pageantry but short on substance, President Donald Trump turned a traditional post-travel press conference into a meandering free-association session in which he appeared to have difficulty remaining focused on any topic for more than a minute or two.

Over the course of roughly 90 minutes, Trump threatened to punish foreign countries for allowing U.S. companies to build data centers within their borders, claimed detailed knowledge of air traffic control systems due to his “genetics,” waxed on about the relative greatness of Chinese roads versus American ones, and mused aloud about the aesthetic quality of filled potholes.

At one point, Trump appeared to catch himself. “They'll say, "Oh, he rambled, he rambled terribly — I don't ramble," he said.

The president’s rambling remarks about his tendency to ramble came about the 40-minute mark, most of which was taken up by a long soliloquy consisting of prepared remarks and lengthy digressions he has taken to calling a “weave.”

He was flanked on stage in the White House briefing room by Vice President JD Vance and Secretary of State Marco Rubio. Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent and White House Chief of Staff Stephen Miller watched from stage right.

![Trump claimed he didn’t 'ramble' even as he wound through a long list of topics over 90 minutes at the White House Friday afternoon](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/20/19/2026-11-20T192528Z_130355272_RC2UROA3K99Q_RTRMADP_3_USA-TRUMP.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

At the top of his remarks, Trump began with a boast about the length of his return flight from Shenzhen, China, which he called a record-breaking journey by the new Air Force One aircraft — a Boeing 747-800 that was gifted to him by the Qatari government last year.

He immediately turned to claiming that the U.S. economy is “growing faster than any other major developed nation on earth by a lot” and suggested that a recent 20 percent drop in gasoline prices was politically motivated and timed so as to prevent him from getting credit before the midterm elections earlier this month.

He said future gas prices were dependent on “how we settle up with Iran,” nearly a year after he started a war that has wreaked havoc on the global economy and upset energy markets by turning the Strait of Hormuz into a war zone.

“They want to make a deal, but they don't want to make a deal that's acceptable to me. They want to make a real deal,” said Trump. He did not elaborate on what would make any agreement “real” versus not.

Trump also hailed the Senate for passing a long-delayed permitting reform bill that could speed up construction of the data centers that are now opposed by large majorities of voters in most jurisdictions across the U.S.

He suggested that communities should “take advantage” of data centers by forcing developers to pay residents and warned that other countries would want the data centers that are rejected by Americans.

![Trump hailed the Senate for passing a long-delayed permitting reform bill that could speed up construction of the data centers that are now opposed by large majorities of voters in most jurisdictions across the U.S](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/20/19/2306099705..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

At the same time, he threatened to impose consequences on any country that allows U.S. companies’ data centers to be built within their borders and said he wanted to stop American firms from building any abroad.

“We're not going to allow our companies to build data centers outside of our country. We're not going to allow that to happen. You know, they allowed that to happen with chips and everything else, and it was very destructive to our country,” he said.

Trump added that he’d impose “tremendous taxes” on companies that expand their data centers beyond U.S. borders.

“We're not allowing data centers to go into other countries because China will take all of them, and we're not allowing that to happen. And we're going to put tremendous penalties on companies that try and do it,” Trump said.

The president returned to his prepared remarks as he recounted his four-day trip, which started at the Association of Southeast Asian Nations summit in Manila, the Philippines, before moving to China for the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation forum and a series of meetings with China’s Xi Jinping and Russia’s Vladimir Putin.

It was a bilateral sit-down with the longtime Russian dictator that has attracted global scrutiny after footage of the meeting showed a delighted Trump describing Putin as “my friend.”

When pressed on whether he believed Putin to be a friend of the United States, Trump demurred, telling reporters: ”I can't speak to America.”

”I've been dealing with Putin during the fake Russia, Russia, Russia hoax, and to be honest, he was very nice about it,” the president said, repeating language he has long used to downplay findings from multiple U.S. government investigations which found Russia ordered a “sweeping and systematic” campaign to interfere in the 2016 election to Trump’s benefit.

“It was a rigged hoax. It was a hoax that was made up by the Democrats,” he said, though he conceded a moment later that “it probably helped me win the election.”
