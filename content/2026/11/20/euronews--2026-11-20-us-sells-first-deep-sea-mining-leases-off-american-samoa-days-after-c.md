## The Trump administration pressed ahead with the auction despite a federal judge’s finding that regulators broke wildlife protection law and conservation groups vowed to have the leases voided.

The Trump administration on Thursday sold the first deep-sea mining leases in US history, offering seabed areas off American Samoa just days after a federal court found the approval process unlawful.

The sale covered two zones totalling about 31 million acres (12.5 million hectares) in the Pacific, in the face of strong opposition from local residents and environmental groups.

Each zone fetched $3 million (about €2.8 million), for a combined $6 million (about €5.5 million). The winning bidders have not been named.

On Monday, a federal judge in Hawaii sided with community and conservation groups, finding that the National Marine Fisheries Service broke the Endangered Species Act when it cleared the sale.

But despite the court's ruling, Interior Secretary Doug Burgum called it “the first time the United States has held an offshore critical minerals lease sale in the Pacific and a historic step for America’s energy and mineral security.”

He said the sale “opens a path to learn more about the resources off the coast of American Samoa and assess whether they could contribute to a more reliable domestic supply.”

Mining firms want to harvest polymetallic nodules from the deep ocean floor. The potato-sized lumps contain nickel, copper and cobalt, metals used in batteries and electric vehicle systems.

The sale follows an executive order Trump signed in April 2025 describing leadership in deep-sea science and technology as “a core national security and economic interest.”

It also answers a request from the US company Impossible Metals to open leasing off the territory, which lies east of the independent nation of Samoa.

## **Opposition from locals and scientists**

Critics warn of damage that could not be undone, with clouds of waste smothering marine life and noise from heavy machinery disrupting animal migrations.

“This premature push to exploit the deep sea before we even fully understand how vital it is will have catastrophic consequences that reverberate for generations,” said Julia Singer, Oceana’s Defense Campaign Manager.

American Samoa banned deep-sea mining in its own territorial waters in 2024, but the auction covers the federally controlled Outer Continental Shelf beyond that limit.

In a public comment in September, Governor Pulaali’i Nikolao Pula said he “does not support commercial deep-sea mineral extraction at this time.”

In Monday's ruling, Judge Jill Otake wrote, “NMFS failed to even attempt to consider the potential effects on ESA-listed species”

The lease areas lie close to the vast Rose Atoll marine sanctuary, home to many endangered or threatened species, including whales, turtles, sharks and corals.

Earthjustice attorney Chris Eaton condemned the decision to go ahead.

“In deciding to move forward with today’s scheduled sale, the Trump administration has performed a renegade, unlawful act,” he said. “We will use the full extent of the law to vigorously push for the leases to be voided.”
