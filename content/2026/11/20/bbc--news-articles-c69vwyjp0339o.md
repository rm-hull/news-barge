The killing of 13 women around Johannesburg earlier this year sparked a national outcry

**Nearly 600 women were killed in South Africa between July and September this year, according to the country's latest crime statistics.**

This figure includes 13 women whose partially naked and bruised bodies were found dumped in public areas in and around Johannesburg.

The murders triggered national outrage and police faced pressure to find the culprits, in a country with one of the highest rates of gender-based violence and murder rates in the world.

In the latest shocking killing, five women and a toddler were gunned down in Free State province earlier this week.

The latest crime statistics, released on Friday, revealed that 581 women were killed in the country between July and September, including 131 in the Gauteng province which includes Johannesburg.

The same statistics revealed a slight decline in the number of murders.

Police reported 5,590 murders, 204 fewer than in the same period last year. This still come to an average of about 60 murders a day.

It is not clear whether the number of women killed is an increase or decrease because the police had stopped giving these figures recently due to what they said were "significant anomalies" in the statistics.

Acting Police Minister Firoz Cachalia said there needed to be "greater efforts to prevent... violence" against women and children.

According to police in Free State province, south of Johannesburg, three masked men opened fire on a family in the township of Botshabelo on Wednesday night.

The trio were allegedly looking for the owner of the house and when they realised he was not there, "forced their way into the house and started shooting at the people inside", police said.

The owner's wife was also shot in the stomach and was taken to hospital. No arrests have been made.

On Thursday, the police chief for Gauteng province said that eight people had now been arrested in relations to the 13 murders which sparked a national outcry earlier this year.

General Tommy Mthombeni said three of the arrests related to the murder of Elizabeth Moselakgomo, an avid runner whose disappearance prompted a highly publicised nationwide search for her.

The 38-year-old went missing while out for her daily run near her home in Kempton Park.

Her body was found badly bruised and partially clothed on 12 September.

The police chief said two other people had been detained in connection with the death of Jabulile Ntimba, 33, who was discovered half-naked and wrapped in a sheet on the side of a road in Boksburg on 17 September. Ntimba's was the ninth body found in the Ekurhuleni region.

He said another person had been arrested for one of the Kempton Park cases while a suspect was arrested over Ntombifuthi Nxumalo's death in Tembisa, north-east of central Johannesburg.

Nxumalo was discovered at a cemetery on 26 September. She was found naked with serious head injuries as well as stones near her body.

Another arrest was made back in July, in relation to the death of Busisiwe Mahlangu, whose body was found in Kempton Park on 15 July. Hers was the first of the 13 bodies discovered in the area.

In September, President Cyril Ramaphosa vowed that "no stone will be left unturned" to find those behind the killings.

Last year, he declared that violence against women was a "national disaster".
