A specialist vessel recovered the Kilda on Thursday night

**A fishing boat that went missing near the Isle of Lewis a month ago has been recovered after it was spotted semi-submerged in the North Sea off Orkney.**

Three men were on board the Kilda when it disappeared on 13 October.

The body of crew member Douglas Craigie was found by search teams the following day but two other men - Norman Murray and Murdo Morrison - are still missing.

The wreck was spotted east of Orkney on Wednesday morning, and a specialist vessel recovered from about 40 miles (64km) out to sea on Thursday before bringing it to Kirkwall.

The Marine Accident Investigation Branch (MAIB) has started an investigation into the loss of the Kilda.

It is understood it will be kept in a secure yard while investigations are carried out.

![The men are pictured in a composite image.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/144f/live/6f304600-c88b-11f1-bc2e-018d645d8d21.png)

Douglas Craigie, Murdo Morrison and Norman Murray had been fishing off the north coast of Lewis

Insp Neil Taylor, from Police Scotland, said: "Our thoughts remain with everyone affected by this tragic incident.

"The vessel recovered has been confirmed as the FV Kilda and we will continue to work with partners, including the MAIB, Maritime and Coastguard Agency, as well as local maritime experts, as our enquiries continue."

He said it was a very difficult time for the families and local community, and asked for patience while a full examination of the vessel was carried out.

"This will be a complex operation and we would ask that officers and specialists are given the time and space to carry out their enquiries," he added.

An HM Coastguard aircraft had confirmed the sighting of a semi-submerged boat on Wednesday.

Local firm Leask Marine was able to recover the Kilda in tricky conditions at about 18:00 on Thursday then brought the vessel into Kirkwall.

It was a cold, sombre scene when it arrived just before 23:00.

The Kilda was partly covered by a tarpaulin, but its name and its registration number were clearly visible. Two police officers went on board briefly with torches, then watched as it was lifted.

BBC Scotland understands part of the wheelhouse had been torn off and the rudder was gone, but the boat was still floating.

The Kilda was recovered about 200 miles (322km), in a straight line, from where it went missing.

One theory is that the vessel may have floated round the north coast of Orkney before being blown back south.

![A map of northern Scotland showing the locations of Lewis, Orkney, Port of Ness, Kirkwall and Inverness.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/fa38/live/362de030-e4d1-11f1-99a1-253766e7ad8b.png)

Earlier this month, an empty life raft from the Kilda washed up 200 miles away in Shetland.

The Kilda was last known to be operating close to the Port of Ness in Lewis.

Coastguard personnel, lifeboat crews, police and volunteers have been involved in searches since its disappearance.

A specialist Northern Lighthouse Board ship was brought in to carry out surveys of the seabed off north Lewis, and it identified two sites which were searched but nothing was found.

![The Kilda after it was lifted out of the water](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/5286/live/69266ee0-e4c6-11f1-a094-5fdc692f3e33.jpg)

Speaking at the time of the Kilda's disappearance, Duncan Macinnes, secretary of the Western Isles Fishermen's Association, said the incident has had a devastating impact in the local community.

He told BBC Scotland: "It is just an unimaginable tragedy that happened just over a week ago and we have heartfelt sorrow for the grieving families who have suffered such loss and we pray that Tam and Murdo will be found.

"We are thankful for the rescue services that have taken place by the Coastguard, the Marine Accident Investigation Branch, Police Scotland, and all the others that have gone and assisted in any way possible to try and find those that have been lost.

"With a tragedy like this which is one of the worst fishing tragedies that we have experienced in the Western Isles in a generation, it is going to leave broken hearts.

"It is just very difficult and total devastation for the industry and for the community."

A minute's silence was observed for the crew at the Royal National Mòd in Glasgow

Ness is a sparsely populated area on the northern tip of Lewis.

It is an area of rugged coastline, with many of its roughly 1,300 people living in crofting townships - small holdings where families can raise livestock and grow crops while working other jobs.

The parish has one of the highest concentrations of Gaelic speakers in the Western Isles.

A minute's silence was held at the Royal National Mòd, Scotland's annual celebration of Gaelic culture and language, which was taking place in Glasgow at the time.

Flowers were also left on the plinth of the Ness Fishery Memorial at Port of Ness.
