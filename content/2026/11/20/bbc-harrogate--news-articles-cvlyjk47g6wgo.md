Andrew Whitaker said postal delays meant he missed a mental health appointment

**Andrew Whitaker says being able to rely on his post being delivered is essential.**

The Harrogate resident, who is receiving NHS support for issues with his mental health, explains that missing or late post can be the difference between knowing about an appointment or finding himself at the back of the queue.

He says "ongoing" delivery delays in his area have caused issues.

"I've had a couple of appointments run late. Thankfully they've been OK about it. If you miss so many appointments you get put back on the waiting list, or get taken off the list, which means you have to re-refer."

He's now used to the irregular rhythm of when post will drop through his door.

"Usually you hear nothing for quite some time then you get an abundance of mail. Important letters - like from the NHS - don't come as quickly as they should do, which is quite a nuisance when it comes to the appointments.

"It's frustrating when you've got something important you're waiting for," he says.

"I see the mental health team quite a bit. It's worrying enough thinking about a letter coming late. If I had to re-refer, and go through a long time on a waiting list, the stress would be massive, it would put huge pressure on my mental health."

A Royal Mail spokesperson said first class deliveries had improved across the HG postcode area.

"We know there's more to do," they added.

The issue of postal problems is regularly reported to the BBC by residents across Yorkshire.

Our research shows that Royal Mail's first class next working day delivery performance has yet to return to pre-pandemic levels.

The best performance is in the York, or YO, postcode area, where 92.2% of first class post arrived on the next working day so far this year - that's down from 92.9% in the 12 months from 2019.

In 2025-26, 85.4% of the YO postcode's first class mail was delivered the next working day.

The poorest performance in our region is in Bradford, where so far this year, the rate has been 80.6% - down from 94.3% in the year to April 2020.

Ken Smith, 88, from Keighley says he is considering posting his 20 Christmas cards in Leeds because the post in Bradford is so poor.

"We were getting mail every two weeks. One Friday we got 18 letters including a hospital letter," he fumes.

"I get a weekly magazine and sometimes I get several weeks of the magazine in one go. Some people get the Radio Times after the programmes have already been broadcast."

![A graph showing the delivery of post by postcode area in Yorkshire.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/a7a5/live/72cf9520-e41c-11f1-99a1-253766e7ad8b.png)

During a one-hour period in the Jennyfields area of Harrogate, I speak to five people and all report going for days without any post - then a large amount arriving at the same time.

Matthew Moseley, who lives in the HG3 area, tells me: "You get nothing for about four days, then you get five lots of post through your letterbox at once.

"It's frustrating. I'm missing out on my post, your bills are sometimes late, and they don't care when you call them up and say I haven't received my letters."

Neighbour Richard adds: "It's unreliable, it's hit-and-miss. Sometimes nothing, sometimes a great pile behind the door that then all needs to be sorted."

The Royal Mail spokesperson said in the three months to September this year, 89.4% of first-class letters sent in the HG postcode area arrived the next working day.

"That's up from 72.7% in the same quarter last year," they added.

Harrogate and Knaresborough's Liberal Democrat MP Tom Gordon, who has raised the issue in parliament, says Whitaker's situation was "all too common," and comes at a cost to both the person receiving treatment and the NHS.

"It's really worrying. It means that time when people could be getting the help and support they need is wasted. The answers we get around this are largely individual, 'the postman or woman was off, stuff was sitting on a shelf'.

"If that is the case, there will be illness, holiday and other absence, then there needs to be contingencies in place to make sure people are still getting their post," he adds.

Kevin Hollinrake, the Conservative MP for Thirsk and Malton and a former Post Office minister, says he has had "complaints galore" from his constituents about delivery reliability.

They include a parcel which went to Scotland instead of Scarborough, and another which was meant to go to Leeds, but got there via Leicester and Skipton.

His constituents have also reported NHS appointment letters arriving a week late, and a bank card and its PIN arriving in the same delivery.

Hollinrake believes Ofcom - which can fine Royal Mail - should investigate.

"The service is just not good enough. Ofcom need to look at this, they have the ability to fine Royal Mail, to make sure they are more focused on their performance, and to make sure they recruit the right number of people.

"I don't blame the posties. I blame the Royal Mail management for not making sure they have enough properly trained, full-time staff to be able to deliver a decent service."

The Royal Mail spokesperson said the firm knew people "need to be able to count" on their mail arriving on time.

"Especially when they're waiting for a letter about a hospital appointment," they said.

The spokesperson said Royal Mail was investing £500m over five years and "rolling out" a new delivery model to make deliveries more reliable.

"For Christmas, we're recruiting almost 22,000 seasonal workers across the UK and adding 7,500 hire vans and extra transport capacity to help us deliver the extra mail and parcels," the spokesperson added.

An Ofcom spokesperson said: "We've fined the company more than £37m in recent years and we're currently investigating its failure to meet its latest delivery targets.

"In response to our demands, Royal Mail has publicly set out how it plans to turn things around by April 2027 and committed to invest £500m over the next five years.

"Progress is now being made, and we'll hold it to account for delivering on its improvement plan."
