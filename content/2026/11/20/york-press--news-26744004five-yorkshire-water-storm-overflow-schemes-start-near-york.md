Yorkshire Water has launched a new round of investment to tackle storm overflow pollution.

The company has started five projects totalling £7.8 million in villages around York as part of its wider £1.5 billion programme to reduce storm overflow discharges.

The projects are in Askham Bryan, Tholthorpe, Shipton, and Sheriff Hutton, where contractors OCU Group and United Infrastructure are installing more than 2.6km of new surface water sewers.

Qasim Elahi, project manager at Yorkshire Water, said: "Starting these five projects shows our commitment to delivering improvements for the watercourses around York, and in Yorkshire more broadly, at pace.

"Last year, we reduced the operation of our storm overflows by 24.5 per cent as a result of our investment programme, and we're working hard to continue that downward trend."

The sewers will carry rainwater away from the combined system, which handles both rainwater and wastewater.

This will help prevent combined storm overflows from discharging during heavy rainfall.

The work will benefit Derrings Beck in Tholthorpe, Askham Beck in Askham Bryan, Moor Gutter in Shipton, and Howl Beck in Sheriff Hutton.

The Sheriff Hutton scheme will last three months, with Tholthorpe and Askham Bryan due for completion in spring 2027 and Shipton in summer 2027.

In Fulford, work is underway to install an underground storage tank on Fordlands Road to improve water quality in the River Ouse.

The tank will hold 33,000 litres of rainwater and wastewater to prevent discharges during heavy rain.

Ward and Burke are delivering the seven-month project, which aims to cut discharges from Fulford Mains Street CSO to fewer than ten per year.

Residents have been informed of some disruption.

Work will pause over the festive period in Askham Bryan, Sheriff Hutton, Shipton, and Tholthorpe.

Further works are taking place across North Yorkshire, with schemes ongoing at Staxton, Crambe, Hebden and Malham.
