European leaders have expressed their readiness to negotiate with Russia to end the war in Ukraine, insisting that Europe must have a seat at the table.

Speaking at the Coalition of the Willing summit in Berlin, British prime minister Andy Burnham said Europe had a “united negotiating position”, while German chancellor Friedrich Merz argued Russia’s “maximalist demands” were unrealistic.

“The US understands that there can be no peace without us. We, in turn, know that with the United States by our side, the prospects for peace increase,” Mr Merz added.

US envoy Jared Kushner said Donald Trump wanted to reach a deal with Europe’s support, adding the president believed “peace is achievable” following his meeting with Russian leader Vladimir Putin and Chinese president Xi Jinping at the Apec summit in Shenzhen.

Mr Kushner and fellow envoy Steve Witkoff travelled to Berlin to brief some 40 European leaders on Mr Trump’s efforts to end the nearly five-year war following his historic trilateral talks with Mr Putin and Mr Xi this week.

Mr Kushner said Russia was open to European participation in peace negotiations and appeared “comfortable” with Ukraine receiving security guarantees enshrined in Nato’s Article Five, which requires allies to defend a member under attack.

“I think we have gotten the Russians comfortable with that concept where any security guarantee for Ukraine would be based on Article 5,” he said. “And I think that comes with at least what the Russians are saying, which is that their intention when this war is over is to not have another war in the future.”

“Any deal we do will be in close consultation with Ukraine and with Europe,” he added. “And that’s something that he also asked us to come here today and reassure people in that regard.”

However, Ukrainian president Volodymyr Zelensky remained sceptical, saying Mr Putin had repeatedly rejected proposals for peace. “Putin has always said no,” he said.

Mr Merz said European leaders were willing to hold “serious discussions on European security” with Russia, but only after a “peace agreement with Ukraine” and on terms acceptable to both Kyiv and its allies.

French president Emmanuel Macron urged allies to do the “maximum” to strengthen Ukraine’s air defences and “reopen the question” of the country’s membership in Nato.

Mr Kushner said Mr Trump used his talks with the leaders of Russia and China to “send a very strong message to both of them on the desire for peace.

While declining to disclose details of the “private discussions”, Mr Kushner said the US president had emerged from the talks convinced a resolution was possible.

“I believe he also came away believing that peace is achievable and we need to all work together to try and achieve it,” he said.

Mr Putin said in Shenzhen that Moscow was open to involving European countries in the negotiations, but “we need to understand with what Europe will come to these negotiations”.

He said the Kremlin had employed diplomatic channels to invite European officials to Moscow “to determine their position”, but did not offer further details. No date had been set for the proposed meeting.

Despite these diplomatic efforts, there are few signs that Moscow is prepared to scale back its military campaign. Russia continues to attack Ukraine’s energy infrastructure as winter approaches and Mr Putin maintains his territorial demands. He also threatened a stronger response to Ukraine’s latest strike on the Crime bridge.
