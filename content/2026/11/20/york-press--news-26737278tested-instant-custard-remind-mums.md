## I loved my mum's custard - did any of these instant varieties remind me of it?

Custard with a pudding is popular comfort food

FOR many years I steered clear of custard. I blame it on school: served in large metal jugs - probably a health hazard in themselves - it was sludge-thick, lumpy and didn't taste great either.

At home my mum was an avid fruit pie maker; a jug of custard was always on the table. I would substitute it with condensed milk or ice cream. Later in life I was persuaded to give Mum’s custard - made using Bird’s custard powder, milk and sugar - a go. It was delicious, I never looked back.

I still love custard, but, as we rarely eat puddings, I don’t often have it. Having recently tested sticky toffee puddings for this page, I thought it would be interesting to try out the various instant custards available in stores.

At this time of year, when the nights draw in and the chilly mists appear, a pudding topped with hot custard really does bring a smile to your face.

When I make custard I generally use a packet of instant, requiring the addition of boiling water, which is what I have selected here, as opposed to cartons or tins.

\*Tesco Instant Custard Powder, 76g, 80p

This one was a pale yellow in colour with an appetising smell. It dissolved well and had a nice smooth consistency. In taste, it had the right level of sweetness but overall, compared to some of the others, it was a little bland.

Pros: appearance, consistency.

Cons: taste.

Mark: 2/5

\*Birds Instant Custard, 3x75g, £2.50 (bought at Sainsbury’s)

This market leader is the brand my mother used throughout my childhood, and for which I had high expectations. It dissolved well, had an appetising yellow colour and was smooth in consistency. It didn’t have much of an aroma. The taste offered just the right level of sweetness but could have been stronger. It was pleasant enough but nothing to shout about, and it didn’t transport me back to childhood as I imagined it would. A handy pack of three, but it still works out dearer than the others.

Pros: appearance, taste.

Cons: none, although taste could be stronger.

Mark: 3/5

\*Sainsbury’s No Added Sugar Instant Custard, 72g, 85p

A slight custardy aroma and creamy yellow colour, this one dissolved easily; it was quite thin in consistency compared with some others (you could always use less water than the recommended amount) but had a smooth texture. I liked the taste because it was just right - not too sweet, which may be due to the ‘no added sugar’.

Pros: texture, taste.

Cons: consistency.

Mark: 4/5

\*Asda Just Essentials Instant Custard, 70g, 32p

This pale yellow custard had no smell but mixed to a reasonable consistency. The taste, however, wasn’t there - I couldn’t detect anything, which is a shame as it’s so cheap - I could buy three for the price of most others. People say you get what you pay for, and I suppose that’s the case with this basic offering.

Pros: consistency, price.

Cons: taste.

Mark: 2/5

\*Morrisons Instant Custard, 76g, 80p

This one had a pale yellow colour and had an odd cardboard-like aroma as I mixed it. The taste was not much better - it didn’t really have any. It also left me with a slightly unpleasant aftertaste. The only thing going for it was the consistency, which was suitably smooth. I really wasn’t keen on it at all.

Pros: consistency.

Cons: smell, taste.

Mark: 1/5

\*Pearce Duff’s Custard, 72g, 39p (B&M)

This was quite a dark golden shade, with a smell that carried a hint of marzipan. It dissolved well and had a reasonable consistency but had an odd, sweet taste which I couldn’t put my finger on. You can’t argue with the price though, so if you like it you’re quids in.

Pros: consistency, price.

Cons: taste.

Mark: 2/5

This is a second Asda, from the standard range.

A lovely custardy aroma drifted up from this one. With an appetising yellow colour, it was fairly thick, with a lovely smooth consistency and comforting taste; not too sweet, and an appetising yellow colour. Not a bad price either.

Pros: appearance, taste, price.

Cons: none.

Mark: 5/5

\*Aunt Bessies Instant Custard, 70g, 91p, Spar

With a sweet aroma, this turned into a thick mixture - far too thick for my liking. It was like a big dollop as opposed to a creamy liquid. The taste matched the smell - it was sweet, I would overly so. I didn’t like it.

Pros: none.

Cons: consistency, taste.

Mark: 1/5
