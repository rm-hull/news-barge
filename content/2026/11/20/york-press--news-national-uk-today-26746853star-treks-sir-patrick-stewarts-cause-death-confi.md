Sir Patrick Stewart’s cause of death has been confirmed.

It was announced the Star Trek actor had died at the age of 86 on October 28 by his representative Kelly Bush Novak.

The statement said he had died “following a brief and poignant journey with Alzheimer’s Disease and Lewy Body Dementia”.

## Sir Patrick Stewart’s cause of death

TMZ has reported that his death certificate listed cardiopulmonary arrest and a neurocognitive disorder with Lewy body as his cause of death.

At the time, his representative’s full statement read: “We announce with great sorrow the passing of Sir Patrick Stewart yesterday at his home in Los Angeles, in the loving care of his beloved wife Sunny, following a brief and poignant journey with Alzheimer’s Disease and Lewy Body Dementia.

“He has been held in the warm embrace of family and friends, surrounded by live music, prayers, tears, laughter and the kind of profound emotion that only he could inspire after a lifetime of devotion to all of us.

“We will forever cherish the peaceful ease and reflection on his face, an enduring reminder of his invincible grace.”

The actor is expected to appear in Marvel’s Avengers: Doomsday when it is released in cinemas next month.

He will reprise his role of Professor Charles Xavier, which he first played in 2000’s X-Men.

At the time of his death, Marvel released a tribute to him and said: “Sir Patrick Stewart leaves behind an indelible cultural footprint and legacy.

“Marvel Studios extends its deepest condolences to his family, friends, and fans around the world."

Sir Patrick Stewart’s close friend and co-star, Sir Ian McKellen, took to social media to share his own tribute.

He said: “Before everything else, Patrick was defined by being born and bred in Yorkshire, the proud county in the north of England which he loved to the end.

"You could hear it in his accented voice and in the stern honesty with which he told you what he thought and felt.

"Yorkshiremen have no truck with half-truths or avoidance of big issues.

"He wore his heart on his sleeve, which made him a man to rely on, to admire and in my case, love."
