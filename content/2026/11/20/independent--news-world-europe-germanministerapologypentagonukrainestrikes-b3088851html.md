Germany's defence minister has apologised for calling a US official's opposition to Ukrainian strikes inside Russia "stupid", but has insisted he stands by his criticism of the position.

Boris Pistorius said he had apologised to Pentagon policy chief Elbridge Colby over his choice of words following a heated exchange at Nato headquarters in Brussels earlier this week.

Speaking to Politico, Pistorius said he had used an inappropriate expression during a press conference and regretted the remark.

“In hindsight, I actually think the choice of words was inappropriate – and I didn't really think it was appropriate even shortly after,” he said.

“I shouldn't have done that,” he added, saying he had called Colby on Wednesday to apologise.

However, Pistorius said he had made clear during the conversation that the two men continued to disagree over Ukraine's military strategy.

“I apologised for my choice of words, but I also made it clear – and he sees it the same way – that we simply have different views on the matter,” he said.

![Pistorius said he had made clear during the conversation that the two men continued to disagree over Ukraine's military strategy.](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/20/16/2305068516..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The row erupted after Colby criticised Kyiv's long-range strikes inside Russia at a meeting of the Ramstein Group, a coalition of countries supporting Ukraine militarily.

The meeting discussed supplying further weapons to Kyiv as Russia intensifies attacks on civilian infrastructure ahead of winter.

Colby argued that Ukrainian deep strikes into Russia had failed to help bring the war to an end or slow Russian advances on the battlefield.

“While the public conversation focuses on the spectacle of deep strikes designed to incur pain on the Russian population, our assessment is that such strikes have not been effective in driving an end to the war, nor do they contribute to blunting Russia's advances on the front line,” he said.

He also suggested attacks on Russian infrastructure, including commercial warehouses belonging to Wildberries, the country's equivalent of Amazon, could strengthen public support for the war rather than weaken it.

Pistorius had sharply rejected the argument during Tuesday's press conference, saying it was misguided to criticise Ukrainian strikes on strategic military targets inside Russia.

![The disagreement highlights differences between US and European officials over how Ukraine should conduct its war](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/20/16/download..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“Instead of criticising that, we should rather criticise and talk about the terroristic strikes every night and every day on ... energy, water, and heating infrastructure in Ukraine,” he said.

The disagreement highlights differences between US and European officials over how Ukraine should conduct its war against Russia, particularly over the use of long-range weapons to strike targets beyond the front line.

There was no immediate comment from the Pentagon or Colby on Pistorius's apology.
