Most of the British public believe the UK is “going backwards” on race relations, with six in 10 saying racism has become more common over the past three years, new research reveals.

More than four in 10 (43 per cent) also feel the situation is worse than it was 20 years ago, according to a study by the Policy Institute at King’s College London (KCL).

Researchers warned the perception that progress on racism is reversing may reflect how heated debates over immigration and identity have become – alongside findings showing Britons vastly overestimate the size of the UK’s ethnic minority and Muslim populations.

The findings are based on a YouGov survey of 1,810 UK adults carried out between 23 and 27 March 2026 as part of the institute’s *Mistaken Britain* research series.

![Britons believe racism has increased in recent years while significantly overestimating ethnic minority populations, new research shows](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/01/12/01111850-9d765f66-d217-435a-8f72-d56887d59daf.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

While the belief that racism has worsened over the past three years is shared across all age groups – held by around six in 10 of every cohort – older generations were far more likely to say it is more widespread than two decades ago.

Half of baby boomers (50 per cent) and nearly half of Gen X (48 per cent) held that view, compared with 30 per cent of Gen Z and 36 per cent of millennials.

The polling comes alongside official Home Office figures showing race hate crimes again made up the majority of police-recorded hate offences in England and Wales, rising by 9 per cent to 107,926 in the year to March 2026.

Professor Bobby Duffy, director of the Policy Institute at KCL, said: “It is worrying that the public think we’re going backwards on race relations, with six in 10 feeling racism has increased over the last few years, and four in 10 saying the situation is worse than even 20 years ago.

“This may reflect how heated debates on immigration and identity have become, as much as people’s direct experience.”

Prof Duffy added that in this climate, there is “perhaps no surprise that there’s limited appetite for withdrawing from the ECHR or for implementing policies to restrict DEI programmes seen under President Trump in the US – although two-thirds of Reform UK supporters do support this.”

The study found Britons are twice as likely to oppose (53 per cent) as support (24 per cent) US-style restrictions on workplace diversity, equality and inclusion (DEI) programmes.

Similarly, 53 per cent want Britain to remain in the European Convention on Human Rights (ECHR), compared with 29 per cent who want to leave.

Alongside attitudes towards racism, the research uncovered widespread misperceptions about Britain’s demographics. On average, respondents guessed that 45 per cent of people in the UK belong to an ethnic minority – more than double the true Census figure of 17 per cent.

Perceptions varied sharply along party lines, with Conservative and Reform UK voters estimating that nearly half of the population is from an ethnic minority.

Reform UK supporters also expected 50 per cent of the UK to be Muslim by 2050, compared with demographic projections of between 10 and 17 per cent.

Conservative and Reform voters were also more likely to overestimate public hostility towards Muslims. While 32 per cent of respondents said they held negative views towards Muslims, Reform voters guessed the figure was as high as 50 per cent, and Conservative voters 40 per cent.

“We are also very wrong on which ethnic groups are most likely to have a degree-level qualification,” Prof Duffy said. “In particular, we rank White British people much higher than they actually come compared with other ethnic groups, reflecting a lack of awareness of the educational challenges of White working-class people.”

When asked to rank ethnic groups by their likelihood of holding a higher education qualification, 39 per cent put White British people first – the most common answer.

In reality, they rank sixth out of seven groups: 31 per cent of White Britons hold a degree or equivalent qualification, far below the rates for Chinese (56 per cent), Indian (52 per cent) and Black African (49 per cent) residents, and slightly below Black Caribbean (34 per cent) and Pakistani (32 per cent) residents.

Earlier research from the *Mistaken Britain* project found the public also misjudges crime statistics – guessing that foreign nationals account for 30 per cent of sexual offences and 25 per cent of the prison population, when official figures put both at 15 per cent.

Rates of mobile phone theft, domestic burglary, vehicle theft, and murder have all fallen, despite perceptions that all have risen, the study also found.
