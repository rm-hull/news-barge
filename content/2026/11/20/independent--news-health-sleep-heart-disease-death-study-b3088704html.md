Sleeping for nine or more hours a day could be a warning sign for adults with heart disease, according to a new 15-year study.

That amount of sleep is linked to a 50 percent higher risk of death from all causes compared to getting seven or eight hours of sleep, according to researchers in China and Georgia Southern University.

Longer sleep could be an indicator there’s an underlying health problem in these patients, the study suggests.

Such problems include depression, sleep-disordered breathing, inflammation, frailty, two or more chronic health conditions and strains related to taking medication.

“In individuals already living with cardiovascular disease, these conditions may be especially relevant because they can signal worsening health status not fully captured by conventional clinical risk factors,” the researchers wrote.

![Sleep is a key component of maintaining heart health. But too much could be the sign of a problem](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/20/14/29/GettyImages-1222078177.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

But asking adults about their sleep could help identify adults with heart disease who may need further evaluation, according to the study.

“Unlike many traditional cardiovascular risk markers, sleep duration can be assessed rapidly, noninvasively and at virtually no cost across clinical and community settings,” they wrote.

The research included data from more than 3,200 adults from the 2004 National Health Interview Survey. It asked people how much they slept in a 24-hour span.

Past research had linked long sleep to just a 30 percent increased risk of death from all causes in patients with and without heart disease, the researchers also pointed out.

The difference suggests sleeping longer may be a stronger indicator of health risk in people with heart disease, they said.

Cardiovascular disease is the leading cause of death in the U.S., according to the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, and one person dies from the illness every 34 seconds.

There are nearly 920,000 such deaths a year, the agency says.

![American adults should get between seven to nine hours of sleep each night for optimal health](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/20/14/24/GettyImages-2237732820.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The total number of deaths from heart disease and stroke has declined in recent years, according to the American Heart Association.

However, about half of all U.S. adults continue to have some form of cardiovascular disease, and the rate is higher than before the Covid-19 pandemic.

Getting the federally recommended seven to nine hours of sleep a night can help heart health by lowering blood pressure, harmful inflammation and stress.

Yet, oversleeping has been linked to a raised risk of chronic disease, including risk factors of heart disease, such as obesity and diabetes, Johns Hopkins Medicine says.

“We don’t exactly know the cause and effect,” Dr. Charlene Gamaldo, a neurologist and sleep specialist at Johns Hopkins Medicine, explained in a statement.
