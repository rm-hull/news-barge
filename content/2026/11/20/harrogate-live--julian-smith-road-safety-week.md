Sir Julian Smith asked the Government in the Commons to help North Yorkshire Council cut road deaths and injuries. Ministers are willing to meet him.

Sir Julian Smith, the Conservative MP for Skipton and Ripon, has asked the Government what more it can do to help North Yorkshire Council cut the number of people killed or seriously injured on the county’s roads. His seat takes in Ripon and Nidderdale.

He raised it at Business Questions in the House of Commons on Thursday 19 November, during Road Safety Week, and was told his concerns would go to Transport Ministers.

## Overview

* **What:** a question to the Leader of the House on road deaths and injuries in North Yorkshire.
* **When:** Business Questions, Thursday 19 November, during Road Safety Week.
* **His ask:** more Government help for the council with communications and strategies to reduce injuries and deaths.
* **The answer:** the Leader of the House said he would take the point to Transport Ministers, who he expected would be keen to meet Sir Julian.

## What he asked

According to the Hansard record, Sir Julian told MPs: “Road safety, which we heard about earlier, is a major issue in Skipton and Ripon and north Yorkshire. We have 5,000 miles of road, and there have been many deaths and many injured constituents.”

He went on: “What more can the Government do to help the council with communications and strategies to reduce injuries and deaths during this Road Safety Week?”

His office says he drew attention to the particular challenges facing North Yorkshire, which has one of the largest road networks in the country, and stressed the need for effective public awareness campaigns and practical measures to make the roads safer for everyone who uses them.

## What the Government said

Sir Alan Campbell, the Leader of the House, replied: “I agree with the right hon. Gentleman that this is a dark side to the absolutely beautiful constituency of Skipton and Ripon. The Government have brought forward a road safety strategy, but he makes a good point on communications, and I will take that to Transport Ministers.”

He added: “If he seeks a meeting with them to see what assistance local authorities can get, I am sure that those Ministers will be keen to meet him.”
