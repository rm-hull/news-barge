Businesses across Germany that rely on metalworkers and electricians were largely at a standstill on Friday as the result of a 24-hour warning strike by Germany's largest workers' union, IG Metall.

Affected sites include shipyards in the north, suppliers in Baden-Württemberg, and the BMW and Audi car factories in Bavaria.

The work stoppage marks the first 24-hour warning strike that IG Metall has called for in eight years.

Employees within the metal and electrical industry are demanding pay raises, but at the same time numerous employers are drawing up plans to thousands of jobs and reduce operating costs.

About 3.7 million employees in Germany fall under the collective agreement in the metal and electrical industry, with thousands of workers across the metal and electrical industry called to strike on Friday.

Warning strikes had begun in the first factories on Thursday. Further strike actions are expected next week.

During 24-hour warning strikes, union members can receive pay directly from IG Metall for participating. The pay rates for striking workers depend on how long they've been paying dues as a member of the union.

This warning strike comes after employers denied IG Metall's demands in a third round of negotiations.

For their part, employers have responded with sharp criticism, suggesting that affected companies suffer significant damages from the strikes at a time marked by an already tense economic situation.

**Volkswagen's 'catalogue of horrors'**

Meanwhile the metalworkers' union was gearing up for a bigger showdown with Volkswagen specifically, after Europe's biggest carmaker delivered its own list of demands ahead of talks for the next collective agreement there.

On Thursday, IG Metall vowed to resist Volkswagen's "catalogue of horrors" after auto giant pushed for workers to agree to drastic cost-cutting measures, from pay freezes to potentially longer working hours.

Europe's biggest carmaker is in crisis, battling fierce Chinese competition, high costs and US tariffs, and has announced plans to axe 120,000 jobs across its 10 brands by 2030.

The carmaker is demanding that, if needed, staff work longer than 35 hours a week, the limit under current agreements, without extra pay.

It also calling for salaries to be frozen next year and in 2028, and for the withdrawal of various other benefits, such as bonuses for long-serving staff.

"VW's management is stabbing its employees in the back," said senior IG Metall official Thorsten Groeger.

"This will be a tenacious, bitter, and exhausting conflict that will leave deep scars on the company."

Daniela Cavallo, chair of Volkswagen's works council, which represents staff interests within the company, said workers "felt" betrayed after having agreed to major job cuts two years ago.

"Management's promises are obviously worthless," she said.

The negotiations cover around 120,000 employees at the Volkswagen brand. The carmaker's other models range from premium Porsches and Audis to mass-market Seats.

Last month, VW announced a massive quarterly loss of 5.8 billion euros, and vowed to redouble its cost-cutting efforts.
