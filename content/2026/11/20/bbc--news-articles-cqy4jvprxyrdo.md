Joanne Young and her daughter have called for changes to be made to the law on consent

**The daughter of Joanne Young, who was drugged, raped and advertised for sex online by her husband, is among those calling for a change to the law on consent.**

Philip Young drugged and abused his wife over a 13-year period, using websites and apps to invite men to their home in Swindon without her knowledge. On Thursday, four men were found guilty of sexual offences against her.

The trial has sparked calls for the government to ensure defendants cannot use "third-party consent" as a defence in rape trials of this nature.

Daughter Alex Young said: "It has been 23 years since this was last updated. Society has progressed, crime has progressed. So why hasn't the law progressed with it?"

Philip Young advertised his wife for sex using websites such as Craigslist, Grindr and Fab Swingers without her knowledge.

During the trial, the prosecution stated that the four men, who were all strangers to each other, failed to seek consent from Joanne Young before they engaged in sexual activity with her.

The defendants claimed they had been duped by Philip Young and believed she was consenting.

In a post on LinkedIn, Alex Young said: "We live in a world where there is debate over whether third-party consent is enough to have sex with someone.

"We live in a world where someone's partner can consent for them, and that is enough to use it as an argument in a court of law.

"This is simply unacceptable."

Her calls echo those made by her mother, who spoke on the steps of Winchester Crown Court after the trial.

"No-one should be able to give it [consent] on your behalf," said Joanne Young, who has waived her right to anonymity.

"I urge the government to look at this as a priority so that women are properly protected in future."

The Sexual Offences Act 2003 states that "a person consents if he agrees by choice, and has the freedom and capacity to make that choice".

"If someone is drugged asleep and unconscious they can't give consent," Professor Miranda Horvath, director of the University of Suffolk's Institute for Social Justice and Crime, told the BBC.

"Someone can't give consent on your behalf.

"[Technology] is quickly evolving but we need to look at where improvements can be made in the legislation."

The guilty verdicts have prompted calls for a change to the laws of consent

Like Joanne Young, Laura Bishop from Bristol has also waived her right to anonymity after her ex-husband received a 14-year prison sentence for drugging and raping her while inviting other men to abuse her.

Speaking to BBC Radio 4's Today Programme, Bishop said the crimes will affect her for the rest of her life.

"It's something that unfortunately you can never get over," she said.

"You have to try and remain positive and optimistic but the betrayal, particularly by men, is very difficult to move on from."

Bishop has a PhD in cyber crime and uses her expertise and experience to work with MPs and charities to improve governance around digital safety and regulations.

"The sites my husband used to advertise me are still active and are known by the police to not provide evidence and forensics when asked.

"So why are they still accessible?" she said.

Bishop understands why many choose to maintain their anonymity but wants to encourage those who can speak out to do so.

"There's a million reasons why its not be appropriate to waive your anonymity and it might just be because it's something you can't actively do," she said.

"But for those of us that are able to, we have to stand strong for victims."

Debbie Beadle, chief executive officer at Wiltshire-based domestic abuse charity Fear Free, also hopes that Joanne Young's decision to speak out will encourage others to do so.

"Six in seven rapes against women are done by someone they know and this adds a barrier to people coming forward," she said.

"The fact that Joanne has come forward will help us others when this has happened to them."

Horvath said more needed to be done to tackle and "de-platform" perpetrators.

"The research being conducted and the evidence base we have is emerging [because] these kinds of offences are under-reported and under-detected," she said.

"We need to ensure proper implementation of the legislation we have through suspect-focused investigation, prosecutions and resourcing and training of the professionals working in these environments.

"[We also need to be] holding the platforms and tech firms to account that are continuing to proliferate this material and saying 'this is enough, we will not tolerate this in our society'."

Prosecutors said Thursday's trial was the first of its kind in England, but former minister for safeguarding and violence against women and girls, Jess Phillips, told LBC that she did not believe it would be the last unless changes were made.

"The ability of these men to find each other on websites that you and I could just log on to now just means that these cases are going to keep on coming," she said.

"The state needs to make sure that its online safety laws and regulations are strong enough to stop this from ever being able to happen," she said.

"I think that every single one of the websites listed in this case should be called on today to answer the questions, can you guarantee this isn't happening today?"

Phillips said there was "absolutely" a case for making it an offence to advertise another person for sex online and that exploitation of adults occurs "all across the internet" with "very little regulation on the safety or the certainty of consent".

The Domestic Abuse Commissioner, Dame Nicole Jacobs, has also called for the case to be a "turning point":

"Ultimately this case again highlights that we need to stop treating drug-facilitated sexual abuse as an isolated incident and recognise it for the form of horrific domestic abuse that it is," she said.

The men who committed crimes against Joanne Young are:

* Philip Young pleaded guilty at an earlier court hearing to 56 charges made up of 14 counts of voyeurism, 11 of rape, 11 of administering a substance with intent, seven of assault by penetration, four of sexual assault and one of publishing obscene articles in relation to his wife
* Norman Macksoni, 48, from Bedfordshire, and Richard Wilkins, 62, from Swindon, were each found guilty of one count of rape. Wilkins was also found guilty of one count of assault by penetration
* Connor Sanderson-Doyle, 32, from Swindon, was found guilty of one count of sexual assault by touching and one count of sexual assault by penetration
* Mohammed Hassan, 38, from Swindon, was found guilty of one count of sexual assault by touching
* A sixth defendant, Dean Hamilton, 47, of Street, in Somerset, admitted one charge of assault by penetration, two of sexual assault and one of rape at an earlier court hearing in February
