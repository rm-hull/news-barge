PLANS to squeeze four flats into the upper floors of a Scarborough town centre building have been thrown out.

Planners warned that the scheme on the upper floors above 79, Newborough would have created poor-quality homes and damaged the character of the conservation area.

The proposal would have seen the building converted into four flats, bringing new housing into the heart of the town centre.

But council officers said the scheme fell well short of the standards expected for future residents.

One of the proposed flats was just 29.8 square metres, well below nationally described space standards, while another failed minimum bedroom size and ceiling height requirements.

Planners also said all four flats lacked the required built-in storage space.

In an assessment, officers concluded the plans would create: “Cramped living accommodation resulting in a poor level of amenity for the future occupants.”

The council also raised concerns about where some residents would actually be living.

One of the flats would have looked directly onto buildings just 2.5 metres away across Waterhouse Lane, creating what planners described as a poor outlook, limited natural light and privacy concerns.

Another bedroom window would have faced directly onto an external staircase landing.

The scheme also ran into trouble because work had already been carried out to the building.

Planning officers said traditional Victorian timber sash windows had been removed and replaced with uPVC units, while a slate roof had been replaced with concrete tiles and alterations made to a dormer window.

The report stated: “The proposed development would result in the loss of historic fabric including Victorian windows, and slate roof.”

Officers said the replacement materials were unsympathetic to the building and would harm the character of the Scarborough Conservation Area.

A third issue was noise, and planners warned future residents would be located close to a late-opening sports bar, extraction systems and commercial plant equipment, but said insufficient information had been provided to show how noise disturbance would be addressed.

Despite supporting the principle of bringing more homes into the town centre, North Yorkshire Council concluded the benefits of creating four flats were outweighed by the harm to the building, and rejected the application.
