Three Chagossians have lost a bid to take their case against the Foreign Office over the Chagos Islands to the Court of Appeal.

Bertrice Pompe, Michel Mandarin and his son Misley Mandarin sought to challenge the government over alleged failings, including not consulting Chagossians about a “right to abode” on the archipelago.

The UK agreed in May 2025 to hand over sovereignty of the Chagos Islands to Mauritius.

The deal will involve leasing back a crucial military base on Diego Garcia, the largest of the Chagos Islands, for 99 years.

It was signed after a last-minute High Court challenge by Ms Pompe, which had delayed the signing by several hours, was dismissed.

![Bertrice Pompe, who was born on Diego Garcia, outside the High Court following an earlier hearing on the Chagos Islands deal](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/20/12/2216479523..?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&auto=webp)

In March, a judge ruled that the trio could not bring a full challenge against the Foreign Office over alleged “unlawful decisions or omissions” in relation to the islands.

At a hearing earlier this month, lawyers for the three Chagossians asked the Court of Appeal to allow them to challenge that decision.

However, in a judgment on Friday, judge Lady Justice Andrews dismissed their bid.

In her 21-page ruling, she said: “It is nonsensical to suggest that there is a duty to consult about maintaining the status quo, even if the claimant does not like the status quo.”

![Demonstrators opposed to the government's Chagos deal rally outside Parliament](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/12/12/2254465476..?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&auto=webp)

Lady Justice Andrews concluded: “I understand the claimants’ strongly held view that the agreement with Mauritius is insufficient to safeguard their interests, and that the UK government should have done more.

“Nevertheless, none of the grounds of appeal is arguable with a real prospect of success.”

US president Donald Trump criticised the deal over the islands as “terrible” at the United Nations General Assembly earlier this year.

Appearing alongside Prime Minister Andy Burnham, Mr Trump said that the move to transfer ownership of the British Indian Ocean Territory was “ridiculous”, describing the island as “a very important piece of real estate, militarily and strategically”.

Pushback from the president led to progress on legislation giving effect to the treaty being paused in April, with the UK government claiming that it could not proceed without American support.

A private members’ Bill regarding the deal is currently at the committee stage.
