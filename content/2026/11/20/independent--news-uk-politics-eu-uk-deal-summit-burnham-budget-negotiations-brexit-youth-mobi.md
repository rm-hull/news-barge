Andy Burnham’s deal with the EU is done and negotiations have concluded, Downing Street has said.

The prime minister is in Brussels to sign agreements covering food and agriculture exports, emissions trading and a youth mobility scheme which could open the doors to around 100,000 young Europeans to temporarily work in the UK.

The government said that “the deal is done” and both sides will work towards implementation, having agreed when they will come into force.

Those dates are set out in a joint statement, which will be published in due course, the government added, but talks will continue in some areas.

According to *The Guardian*, talks on tuition fees are scheduled to last another six months.

Before today’s summit, the prime minister said Britain and Europe are “stronger together” and that the deal he hopes to lock in will cut costs and create opportunities.

## Pro EU campaigners welcome deal but call for more

![The deal is signed, Downing Street said](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/20/11/2305443033..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Emma Knaggs, deputy chief executive of the European Movement UK, has welcomed Andy Burnham’s deal with the EU but insisted he must be “clear eyed” that much more needs to be done.

She told *The Independent*: “We welcome today’s deal. It starts to dismantle the trade barriers we put up ourselves when we left the single market. British exporters have paid for those barriers in lost sales, and families have paid for them at the supermarket checkout.

“The prime minister says the deal could add up to 0.5 per cent to GDP. We must though remain clear eyed, that full EU membership is the real prize on offer. Goldman Sachs estimates that joining the EU could make our economy up to 6 per cent larger in the long run. That’s billions extra every week.

“For our prosperity, our security and our influence in the world, Britain’s future lies in the European Union as a full member. We look forward to the prime minister’s national conversation about the options so that the British people can have a say in the future of the UK in Europe.”

## Senior Labour MP says deal is ‘first step in escaping madness of Brexit’

Senior backbench MP Stella Creasy, chair Labour Movement for Europe has welcomed the deal but insisted it must only be a “first step”.

She told *The Independent*: “This summit is a first step towards escaping the madness of hard Brexit - the idea that we should make it much harder to trade with our largest market, and cut our security links with a union of 450 million people on our doorstep.

“Nigel Farage and his gang have finally admitted Brexit is a mess but are still refusing to do the things needed to fix it - demanding the British people continue to suffer for their folly.

“What matters now is that Andy Burnham focuses on how to build on today’s progress and work with parliament and our allies in the EU to restore our role at the heart of Europe.”

## Nigel Farage calls Burnham ‘naive’ and claims he was ‘strong armed’ at the last minute

Reform UK leader Nigel Farage, who built a political career on pushing forward Brexit, has hit out again at the deal and claimed that Andy Burnham was ambushed at the last minute.

Mr Farage was a member of the European Parliament for almost 21 years for Ukip and the Brexit Party.

He posted on X: “Naive Andy Burnham has gone to Brussels and been hit by the stark reality I’ve warned about for decades. Flexibility is a fantasy.

“They’ve used this moment to strong-arm the British prime minister over tuition fee cuts for hundreds of thousands of EU students, and have absolutely no concern for the impact it will have on Britain.

“It’s the same old game, and he’s fallen for it out of sheer inexperience.”

## Analysis: Historic deal for Burnham but last minute panic shows it is not enough

Andy Burnham has managed to get his first big international deal as prime minister over the line on trying to unpick the harms of Brexit.

But the last minute nervous breakdown - a constant repeated pattern in all talks with the EU - shows that a much more radical rethink is needed.

While farmers and food and drink suppliers will be delighted about the end of border checks and young people will be helped by the new youth mobility scheme, there is far more to do.

The prime minister came into office this summer understanding this point and it is clear that he now wants to look forward to a national conversation about whether we as a country stick with Brexit or not.

It is obvious that his preferred option is to rejoin completely. That gives us full access to the EU trading bloc and a seat at the table to decide the rules.

While there will be much to welcome today, the panic again will show that the UK needs to stop tinkering on what is shaping up to be a historic mistake.

## 'Does it make life better for people in Britain'

Ahead of his talks with European Commission president Ursula von der Leyen and European Council president Antonio Costa, Andy Burnham said: “The test for this summit is simple: does it make life better for people in Britain?

“That is what I will deliver in Brussels today – cutting costs, keeping prices down in the supermarkets and creating opportunities for young people.

“Britain and Europe share values, interests and a global neighbourhood.

“In a more dangerous world, we are stronger together. A closer partnership with Europe is good for working people, good for Britain and good for Europe.”

## BREAKING: 'Deal is done', UK government says

A Government spokesperson said:

* The deal is done - negotiations have concluded.
* Now, both sides will work towards implementation of the youth mobility scheme, the SPS deal and the emissions trading scheme.
* Both sides have agreed when these will come into force. And those dates are set out in the joint statement, which will be published in due course.
* As with all international agreements, there is a legal process in each respective country which will now take place.
* And talks will continue on some areas - these will be set out in the joint statement later.

## What is ‘Made in Europe’? The scheme that Britain wants to be included in

The UK and the European Union are set to agree on measures easing food trade and youth mobility at a summit on Friday. While marking a reset in ties, the gathering is likely to expose lingering divisions and long-term uncertainties over their relationship.

A decade after Britain voted to leave the 27-nation bloc, initial rancour has faded. Shared global challenges — including Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine, US tariffs, Chinese control of critical minerals and war in Iran — have underlined the necessity of a closer alliance.
