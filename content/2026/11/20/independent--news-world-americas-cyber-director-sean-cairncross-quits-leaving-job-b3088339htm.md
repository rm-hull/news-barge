Sean Cairncross has been fired as White House national cyber director after a slew of technical outages, a source close to the White House has confirmed to *The Independent*.

Cairncross was the principal advisor to Donald Trump on cybersecurity policy and strategy. The president backed Cairncross’ firing due to his lack of strong cyber credentials, the source said.

Kirsten A. Davies, who currently serves as the Pentagon’s chief information officer, will take over as acting national cyber director and help strengthen Trump’s cybersecurity team, according to the source.

Stephen Miller — who replaced Trump’s chief of staff, Susie Wiles, after a massive Republican defeat in the midterms — pushed for the shakeup, a senior administration official told Axios, which first reported on Cairncross’ departure. But the publication’s sources say Cairncross’ departure was amicable.

![Sean Cairncross has been fired as White House national cyber director after a slew of technical outages, a source close to the White House has confirmed](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/20/0/41/GettyImages-2304986941.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Cairncross’ firing comes after a chaotic week for Americans with technical outages impacting airlines, banking, tech firms like Microsoft and Apple and even a national security system.

One of those outages, caused by a contractor replacing old copper wire at a radio facility in Arkansas, led the Federal Aviation Administration to ground all flights for more than four hours Friday.

Then on Tuesday, an internal network that the National Security Agency uses to communicate classified information went down during routine maintenance.

Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton tried to quell Americans’ anxiety over the outages at a House committee hearing Wednesday, saying, “Based on what we know today, after a great deal of diligence, these are separate service outages associated with maintenance and technical changes. They are not a coordinated or state-sponsored attack.”

![Cairncross’ firing comes after a chaotic week for Americans with technical outages impacting airlines, banking, tech firms like Microsoft and Apple and even a national security system](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/20/0/20/GettyImages-2304485779.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Michael LoSapio, the chief information security officer at software firm Palantir and Trump’s nominee for director of the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency, was asked about national security concerns after the rash of outages.

Senator Richard Blumenthal questioned LaSapio on whether the country is safe from cyberattacks at a Senate confirmation hearing Thursday.

“I feel like there’s more we can do,” he replied.

The Democrat from Connecticut then asked LaSapio to confirm that as the head of the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency, he’d have to replace workers ousted early in Trump’s second term as the administration sought to cut what it saw as government waste.

“I’m going to investigate and dig in and understand where our gaps are and, if needed, come back to this committee and request more resources,” LoSapio said.
