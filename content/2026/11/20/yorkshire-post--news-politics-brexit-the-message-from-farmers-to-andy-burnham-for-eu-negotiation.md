# Brexit: the message from farmers to Andy Burnham for EU negotiations

## The National Farmers’ Union has urged Andy Burnham to negotiate a transition period with any food and drink deal with the European Union, which it said should reduce “friction and costs at the border”.

The Prime Minister travels to Brussels today hoping to sign deals on food, energy and youth mobility at the UK-EU summit, having said the current Brexit deal is not good enough.

This includes a SPS (sanitary and phytosanitary) agreement that the Government expects will increase agricultural exports by 16 per cent by reducing red tape.

The Environment Department (Defra) previously said that £200 export health certificates will be scrapped, as well as sampling costs which can add £1,200 per beef load and £1,200 for a cheese load.

The NFU has been engaging with the Government around the negotiations.

President Tom Bradshaw said: “The EU is our largest trading partner for agri-food products, accounting for 68 per cent of agri-food exports last year, but trade volumes have fallen over a third since 2019.

“Future arrangements should have the ambition of reducing friction and costs at the border while supporting growth and safeguarding innovation.

![NFU President Tom Bradshaw](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/jpim-static/image/ugc/2026/10/30/10/Tom_Bradshaw_1793288187_fd8a076f-b7309eb09616308f.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


“This is about ensuring any policy framework between the UK and the EU supports our ability to produce safe, affordable food by improving farm business viability, profitability and resilience.

“We have made our priorities for a SPS agreement with the EU very clear to government from day one.

“British farmers and growers must retain access to precision breeding technologies that will help to strengthen food resilience to extreme weather and to science-based plant protection products that help protect food being grown for 29m households from pests and diseases.

“Any changes must also provide an appropriate transition period to minimise disruption for farm businesses and we will continue to make this case to government while we await the final agreement.”

A coalition of leading wildlife and environmental organisations and scientists has urged Mr Burnham to use the summit to tackle Britain's dependence on “harmful agricultural chemicals”.

In an open letter, the signatories, including Greenpeace and the Wildlife Trust, warned that aligning with EU pesticide restrictions must be a baseline not a ceiling for the UK to cut pesticide use.

Vicki Hird, strategic lead for agriculture at The Wildlife Trusts, said: “From bees and butterflies to farmland birds and hedgehogs, Britain’s wildlife has suffered devastating declines over recent decades. Intensive agriculture and widespread pesticide use fuelled by the demand for ever-cheaper food are major drivers of this loss.

“We cannot keep harming the pollinators and other invertebrates that underpin healthy ecosystems and food webs and expect nature – or our food system – to thrive.

“We need a wholesale shift away from chemical-intensive food production, with stronger protections against harmful pesticides and alignment with EU regulations.

“Farmers must be given the support they need from government and the food industry to build a food system that works with nature and can produce food for generations to come."

Mr Burnham is also expected to use the summit to progress talks to move the UK closer to the EU.

Reports suggested the parties are working to finalise a youth mobility deal which would grant those under 30 easier travel, work and study between the UK and EU as early as 2028.

The Prime Minister’s press secretary said: “The talks with the EU are going down to the wire.

“The status quo is not working. Brexit has done more harm than good.

“It has not fixed what was broken a decade ago and those problems have become even more severe.”
