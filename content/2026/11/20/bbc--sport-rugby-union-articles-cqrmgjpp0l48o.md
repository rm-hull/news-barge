England thumped Japan 78-15 last weekend

**There is a string of symbols tattooed on to Will Stuart's left foot.**

A circle, two triangles, an up arrow, another circle, R1, L2, an up arrow, a triangle, and, finally L1 three times.

If you know, in this week of all weeks, you know.

England and British and Irish Lions star Stuart is indelibly, if jokingly, stamped with a Grand Theft Auto cheat code,, external which gives your character maximum in-game sex appeal.

In the absence of the injured Stuart and his cheat code at the start of this year, England certainly lost their mojo.

They had a terrible Six Nations. They were comfortably held at bay by South Africa. Five straight defeats ramped up pressure on the squad and their staff.

Stuart has now returned from a ruptured Achilles tendon and, with him, so has England's swagger.

Against Australia and Japan, England scored 20 tries.

They are ranked top of the Nations Championship for points scored and entries into the opposition 22m. Their attack has shown dexterity of a card sharp, with the sophistication of a socialite.

Those are the sort of intricacies needed to befuddle the best defences.

"We want England to support us. We want to bring joy to them," said head coach Steve Borthwick this week.

So far, this autumn, that objective has been achieved. Twickenham has been seduced by two charismatic displays which have been high on entertainment, if low on jeopardy.

Saturday will undoubtedly be different.

New Zealand are sore. Last weekend's defeat by Wales - their first in 73 years - stung. Coach Dave Rennie's public accusation that the players had "let the country down" will have salted wounds.

He may well have said the same of the insipid performance the team put up in a 33-19 defeat by England last year, under his predecessor Scott Robertson.

The Rennie-ssance overseen by their new boss, with wins over France, Ireland and South Africa in his first few months in charge, has given way to talk of looming crisis.

![New Zealand look dejected after defeat by Wales](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/6111/live/d806bb00-e4fb-11f1-a8e4-b1e12a49db15.jpg)

New Zealand have lost four of their past seven matches after going down 3-1 in the 'Greatest Rivalry' series against South Africa and losing to Wales last weekend

All Black backs are up hard against a brick wall. And big names - Will Jordan, Jordie Barrett, Cam Roigard and Damian McKenzie - are back to blast their way out of a hole.

England have had their fun over the past couple of weeks.

One of the benefits of being in the northern half of the Nations Championship is a steadier supply of fodder.

Fiji and Japan - the two teams invited to fill out the southern half of the new competition - have won only one of 10 games between them and have a combined points difference of minus 257.

Despite last week's reverse, New Zealand - along with world champions South Africa - are where southern-hemisphere quality is concentrated. And where England's revival will be truly stress-tested.

The comeback of Stuart, and others, has been key for England.

England made a clean sweep of their last autumn campaign, with a high-calibre set of replacements key.

Any lingering final-quarter hopes for the visitors were flushed out by England's depth on the bench.

Stuart is playing the front-row super-sub once again. Fin Baxter - another long-time injury absentee - starts at loosehead, to allow England to rotate between two top-class front rows.

George Martin - an old-school second row enforcer who is recently back from his own 14-month lay-off - is on a bench, also including Henry Pollock and Marcus Smith, loaded with stardust and points to prove.

George Furbank, whose running threat and playmaking ability from deep adds an extra dimension to attack, starts at full-back after a rotten run of knocks and set-backs.

Captain Maro Itoje seems rejuvenated by a summer off. Ollie Chessum is perhaps in the best form of his life. Seb Atkinson - still only eight caps into his Test career - has brought a mix of hard running and soft skills to 12.

Will Joseph's pace, power, good form and ability to play in midfield, as well as on the wing, has thrust him right into Rugby World Cup reckoning.

England aren't at full strength. Immanuel Feyi-Waboso, Tom Curry and Alex Mitchell are big misses. Sam Underhill and Guy Pepper too.

But injuries are inevitable. And England, at the moment, are relatively fully loaded.

"The last time we had this depth was a year ago," Borthwick said, drawing an implicit link back to England's purple patch of 12 straight wins through 2025 and the very start of 2026.

The mood music, like the performances, has been good.

Itoje suggested there was a renewed sense of purpose and ownership in camp. Borthwick credited the players with leading a successful drive to improve discipline.

Attack coach Lee Blackett's fingerprints are all over some of the attacking shapes thrown over the past fortnight.

The momentum is real.

Victory over New Zealand, of any kind, would stomp the accelerator to the floor.

If it comes with eye candy, all the better.
