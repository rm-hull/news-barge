President Donald Trump faced fierce mockery after appearing to fall asleep during a meeting with world leaders in China.

Livestream footage of the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation session Thursday captured Trump seemingly getting some shut-eye as Chinese President Xi Jinping spoke about the importance of free and open trade.

As Xi spoke, the camera showed the 80-year-old president with his eyes closed for at least 10 seconds. In contrast, the camera then cut to Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney paying attention and diligently taking notes.

While jet lag may be at play, as the catnap came just two days into Trump’s trip to Asia, it’s not the first time the president has been caught dozing off during important events. White House officials and Trump have previously claimed that he is simply “blinking” when he is caught with his eyes closed for extended periods of time.

However, critics of the president don’t seem to buy it, and called him out on social media for “embarrassing” the nation by falling asleep during an important gathering of world leaders.

![Trump closes his eyes while Xi speaks at crunch China summit](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/19/12/54/Trump-closes-his-eyes-while-Xi-speaks-at-crunch-China-summit.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“EMBARRASSING on the world stage: Donald Trump falls asleep during the APEC summit in China,” the Republicans Against Trump account wrote on X.

Above that video, frequent Trump critic George Conway noted, “Embarrassing, yes. But the world is safer this way.”

The press office of California Gov. Gavin Newsom also seized on the moment to criticize Trump, sharing the clip and writing, “The world is laughing at him.”

Actor and comedian John Cleese brought the jokes, noting, “Maybe it wasn’t a very important meeting?”

Liberal social media influencer and Trump critic Brian Krassenstein declared, “there is no way that Trump is not asleep here,” while sharing the video.

“This is so embarrassing for every American,” he added.

![Trump has faced accusations of falling asleep during meetings, such as when he was pictured with his eyes closed at an event in the Oval Office](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/04/17/16/56/GettyImages-2244878578.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Sarah Longwell, the CEO and publisher of *The Bulwark*, an independent political media company, joked: “Sorry that affairs of state are interrupting nap time, buddy.”

White House Communications Director Steven Cheung responded to news reports Thursday about the incident, calling claims that Trump appeared to be sleeping “Fake News!”

“Fake News! President Trump was looking at papers in front of him while the speaker made remarks. You can clearly see his eyes move back and forth,” he claimed. “This is why the people desmite the media so much. They constantly lie.”

Trump’s Asian tour began with an Association of Southeast Asian Nations summit Tuesday in the Philippines. He then headed to Shenzhen, China, on Wednesday for a two-day visit that included meetings with both Xi and Russian President Vladimir Putin.

![Trump was even accused of falling asleep during a New York Knicks NBA Finals game](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/06/09/9/59/Trump-caught-sleeping-during-NBA-finals-game.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The 80-year-old president has frequently been accused of sleeping on the job — something he and the White House have denied in the past.

Over a handful of incidents have occurred just this year of Trump seemingly getting some shut eye during important meetings or events. In January, he was pictured with his eyes closed during an Oval Office event celebrating the Whole Milk for Healthy Kids Act, as two little girls watched.

A month later, Trump attended a meeting of his newly-created "Board of Peace" where he was spotted with his eyes closed as the speakers he invited gave their addresses.

Then in May, he appeared to fall asleep during a Memorial Day celebration, as well as in front of frustrated New York Knicks fans during the NBA Finals Game 3.

Trump was also accused of falling asleep at his UFC 250 birthday party and the White House Correspondents’ Dinner — as well as at the funeral for Senator Lindsey Graham in July.

He was also accused of nodding off during a reading of the names of the victims killed during the 9/11 attacks, though the White House insisted he was “solemnly listening.”

In an interview with the *Wall Street Journal* published on New Year’s Day this year, Trump shrugged off accusations he had been falling asleep in public.

He said: “I’ll just close. It’s very relaxing to. Sometimes they’ll take a picture of me blinking, blinking, and they’ll catch me with the blink.”
