A citywide campaign in Berlin aims to encourage men to take action against violence against women.

"Women can be subjected to violence anywhere: at home, at work, online and on the street. Remaining silent about this protects the perpetrators," said Cansel Kiziltepe, Senator for Labor, Social Affairs, Gender Equality, Integration, Diversity and Anti-Discrimination. That is why the citywide campaign #BrichDasSchweigen (English: #BreakTheSilence) is launching on Monday. Wednesday (November 25) is the International Day for the Elimination of Violence Against Women.

Violence against women is often kept quiet, according to the Senate Administration - due to uncertainty, fear and shame. However, the campaign emphasizes that violence against women does not begin with physical assaults. The website offers specific recommendations for action in various situations, such as when a friend constantly posts sexist comments in a chat, or when you witness physical violence. In addition to information in multiple languages, men are encouraged to become allies in the fight against violence against women and not to remain silent and do nothing.

The campaign, featuring 400 posters and more than 500 screens throughout the city - including in gyms and shopping centers - is designed to raise awareness of the issue. In addition, 30,000 flyers and postcards will be distributed in high-traffic areas. Creators such as Jonas Ems and Aurel Mertz, who are serving as partners in the campaign, will discuss violence against women on social media.
