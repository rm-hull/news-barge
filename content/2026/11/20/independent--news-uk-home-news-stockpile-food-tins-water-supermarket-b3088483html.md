Shoppers stocking up on tinned food and other long-life products helped lift supermarket sales last month, official figures show, after the government urged households to prepare for potential disruption.

The Office for National Statistics (ONS) said retail sales volumes rose by 0.7 per cent in October.

That was stronger than expected, with economists having predicted a marginal decline of 0.1 per cent for the month.

The growth marks a recovery after a fall of 0.6 per cent in September, which was revised down from a previous monthly estimate of 0.4 per cent.

Food stores drove part of the rebound, with sales up 0.8 per cent month on month – the strongest increase for six months.

Supermarkets told statisticians that they witnessed increased sales of “long-life items such as tinned goods and UHT milk” following government advice.

In October, defence secretary Wes Streeting warned that households and businesses should prepare for “increasingly sophisticated cyber attacks” and potential disruption to essential supplies by stocking key supplies.

The government encouraged households to ensure they have around three days worth of bottled water and non-perishable food.

Across the wider retail sector, sales volumes were 0.4 per cent higher for the three months to October compared with the previous three months.

Recent figures from Worldpanel by Numerator showed supermarket prices are now 2.6 per cent higher than a year ago, an increase from 2.5 per cent in September.

The latest data shows hand sanitiser sales climbed 83 per cent in the 10 days after its launch compared with the same period last year.

Bottled water sales rose 67 per cent over the same window, while tinned and jarred goods were up 37 per cent.

The number of people buying tinned meat each day after the launch rose by 43 per cent, equivalent to an extra 400,000 shoppers a day. The typical amount bought remained at about 500g, suggesting households are gradually stocking their cupboards.

Meanwhile, tuna maker Princes said demand for goods including tinned fish, meat and vegetables had risen since the Be Ready campaign began in October.
