Andy Burnham tucked into a Brussels waffle as he rounded off a Friday morning spent signing new pacts with the EU in the Belgian capital.

The prime minister finalised arrangements covering food and agriculture exports, emissions trading and a youth mobility scheme, opening the doors for 100,000 young Europeans to work temporarily in the UK.

Not long after reaching these agreements, Mr Burnham was hosted in Brussels for a three-course lunch that featured a well-known British favourite dish.

A creamy pea and mint soup served as the starter, which was followed by a main course of battered North Sea sole with chips.

The meal was rounded off with a dessert of Brussels waffle accompanied by fresh fruit.

The prime minister said it was a “good day for Britain and a good day for Europe”, as he met EU chiefs in Brussels to announce a package covering food and agriculture exports, emissions trading, and a youth experience scheme which could open the doors for 100,000 young Europeans to temporarily work in the UK, with Britons able to do the same on the continent.

![Andy Burnham with European Parliament president Roberta Metsola](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/20/16/01M7WS6WJJYDP71YC4TS4H8AV5.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Nigel Farage, the leader of Reform UK, made a joke about the menu as he held a press conference in Brussels after the summit.

He hit out at the food and farming deal signed by Mr Burnham, claiming that while it “might remove red tape from 20,000 people, but you’ll put red tape on many, many millions of people”.

Mr Farage added: “No-one ever talks about fishing, but the fishing sell-out of our six to 12-mile line now continues until 2038.

“How ironic that the leaders tonight will dine on battered North Sea Dover sole, undoubtedly caught within what ought to be our exclusive territorial fishing waters.

“That of course is ahead of the pud that’s being served tonight, which is Brussels waffle. Well, we heard plenty of that today from the British Prime Minister.”

Mr Burnham has claimed the deal will add £17 billion a year to the British economy, and also paves the way for closer ties to the EU in future.

The next 12 months will be spent “brainstorming” ideas with the EU for the future relationship between the UK and the bloc, the Prime Minister also said.
