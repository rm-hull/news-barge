A 67-million-year-old tyrannosaurus rex skeleton the length of a double decker bus is set to go on display in the UK for the first time.

The skeleton, which has been named Trinity, consists of 293 fossil bones from three T-Rex specimens all discovered in the United States between 1995 and 2013. It will now go on show in Portsmouth from February.

Trinity was sold at auction in 2023 for more than $6 million (around £4.5 million) to The Phoebus Foundation, a public-benefit art foundation based in Antwerp, Belgium.

Possessing art collections ranging from Flemish old masters to Latin American art, the foundation aims to preserve artworks and encourage scholarly research and public interaction.

The exhibition at Portsmouth Historic Dockyard is being co-ordinated with the University of Portsmouth to provide educational sessions for the public and to enable further academic learning from the skeleton.

The skeleton, which is 11.6m long and 4.57m tall, will be on display at the dockyard’s grade-II listed Boathouse 5.

![The skeleton of a 67-million-year-old tyrannosaurus rex the length of a double decker bus](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/19/10/01M7SA3HAX71VDA16K6D3YYPSD.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Hannah Prowse, chief executive of Portsmouth Historic Quarter, said: “These are genuine fossils from tyrannosaurus rex, and visitors will be able to experience their scale while discovering what scientists are still learning from them today.

“Bringing Trinity to Britain for the first time is an enormous privilege. At a time when fossils of this significance can disappear into private collections, we are proud to be creating an experience that puts authenticity, discovery and public access at its heart.”

Dr Nizar Ibrahim, scientific director of the Dinosaur Museum Bavaria, who has overseen the exhibition, said: “Tyrannosaurus rex may be the most recognisable dinosaur on Earth, but there is still an enormous amount we can learn about these extraordinary animals.

“Trinity gives us the opportunity to investigate evidence of growth, movement, injury, disease and survival and to move beyond the image of T-Rex simply as a giant predator.

“Visitors will be encountering genuine fossils while that investigation is still continuing.

“The bones are the beginning of the story, not the end, they can help us understand what these animals experienced and what their lives were really like 67 million years ago, in the age of the dinosaur.”

![Dr Nizar Ibrahim, Scientific Director of the Dinosaur Museum Bavaria looking at the skeleton of a tyrannosaurus](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/19/10/01M7SA22XDV4DY9KP6QHZ4SEJJ.jpg?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&auto=webp)

University of Portsmouth vice chancellor Graham Galbraith said: “If there’s one city in Britain where Trinity belongs, it’s Portsmouth.

“We look straight out across the Solent to the Isle of Wight, one of Europe’s richest dinosaur fossil sites, and our students and researchers have been at the heart of some significant discoveries made there.

“We’re working to connect local schools with the exhibition and offering public talks and events with our experts, so that people of all ages can get up close to science and perhaps discover a passion that stays with them for life.”

The Trinity T-Rex exhibition opens to the public at Portsmouth Historic Dockyard on February 12 2027 with tickets on sale from November 20 at trexportsmouth.co.uk.
