## Major update on gull issues at Yorkshire landmark

Grand Hotel, Copyright Chris Heaton. Seagull: Canva *(Image: Chris Heaton)*

A CONTROVERSIAL plan linked to Scarborough’s Grand Hotel has been dropped, despite experts previously identifying it as the town’s most promising solution to a decades-old nuisance.

North Yorkshire Council has confirmed it will not proceed with proposals to create an artificial nesting structure for kittiwakes at the town’s Grand Hotel as part of efforts to manage Scarborough’s growing urban gull population.

The decision marks a significant change of direction from recommendations made last year, when a council-commissioned study identified the Grand Hotel as the single most suitable location for a purpose-built nesting structure capable of accommodating more than 1,000 nesting spaces.

At the time, an ornithologist commissioned by the authority said the hotel’s terrace offered a “maximum design opportunity” and could potentially support 500 to 600 pairs of kittiwakes once fully colonised.

The proposal formed part of wider efforts to address longstanding complaints about noise and bird droppings while supporting the conservation of the nationally important seabird population.

However, a new report due before North Yorkshire Council’s Scarborough and Whitby Area Committee next week reveals the idea has now been abandoned following further expert advice.

According to the report, the council’s ecologist and Professor Helen Wilson concluded that a nesting structure located on the foreshore below the Grand Hotel would be unlikely to succeed because it would not be sufficiently visible to attract birds away from established nesting sites.

Officers warned that proceeding with a scheme considered unlikely to work could expose the authority to criticism for wasting public funds.

The report states that the proposal is therefore “not being progressed at the present time”.

Instead, the council is investigating alternative ways of living alongside the protected birds.

The update forms part of North Yorkshire Council’s wider Urban Gull Strategy, approved earlier this year.

Work is already underway on a pilot scheme using gull-proof refuse sacks in Scarborough’s Eastborough area, while researchers from Hull University’s Coexist Project are beginning a year-long study examining human-gull interactions and public attitudes towards the birds.

A total of £119,500 has been allocated for the first phase of the gull strategy, including funding for gull-proof sacks, seasonal cleaning, education campaigns and new litter bins.
