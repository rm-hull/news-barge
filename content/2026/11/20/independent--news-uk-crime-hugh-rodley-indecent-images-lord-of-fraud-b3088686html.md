A criminal dubbed the “Lord of Fraud”, who was caught with hundreds of child sex abuse images, has avoided prison.

Convicted fraudster Hugh Rodley, 79, admitted six counts of making indecent images of children and one of possessing indecent images of children.

At Cheltenham Magistrates’ Court on Friday, he was sentenced to 12 months in prison for each charge, to run concurrently, suspended for 18 months.

Rodley’s sentence was reduced from 18 months for each charge, on account of his guilty plea.

The court heard National Crime Agency (NCA) officers arrested Rodley at his home in Stoke Orchard, near Cheltenham, Gloucestershire, in December 2025 for unrelated alleged offences.

Investigators seized several digital devices from his home, including a laptop and mobile phone.

Police later discovered another phone under the bed in Rodley’s master bedroom, after he denied having any more devices.

![Hugh Rodley was made the subject of a 10-year sexual harm prevention order, and a deprivation order to cover his laptop and phones was issued for the same period](https://static.independent.co.uk/s3fs-public/thumbnails/image/2009/01/21/22/116159.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Prosecuting, Steve Homer said that hundreds of indecent images of children were found on Rodley’s devices, including several anime cartoons of children in “sexual poses”.

He said Rodley had made multiple online searches for sexualised terms involving children.

Mr Homer highlighted Rodley’s attempt to conceal the phone from police as an aggravating factor, along with the “young age of some of the children detected”.

Mitigating, Andy Lee told the court: “It’s fair to say that he is deeply ashamed and remorseful for his actions, having now found himself before the court yet again for a criminal offence.”

He said Rodley “panicked” when he told the police he did not have any other devices, and argued officers would have found it anyway during searches of the property.

Mr Lee said: “He hadn’t set out to search for any images of children, his sexual interest was in adult pornographic material.

“He fully accepts that then led on to these images being found on his device… he accepts responsibility.

“He is deeply ashamed and remorseful for doing that.”

![Rodley ‘panicked’ when he told the police he did not have any other devices, and argued officers would have found it anyway during searches of the property](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/22/13/46/PA-6760629-07b74ff9.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

He argued Rodley’s offending was “out of character”, adding these crimes are different in nature to his previous convictions for fraud.

Sentencing Rodley, chair of the bench Dr David Rockey said the offences were so serious that a custodial sentence was the only suitable option.

He added that Rodley had a “very large number” of images, including some which fell into Class A, the most serious category.

Dr Rockey said Rodley will be on probation for 18 months and ordered him to pay costs of £85 as well as a victim surcharge of £187.

Rodley was made the subject of a 10-year sexual harm prevention order, and a deprivation order to cover his laptop and phones was issued for the same period.

In 2009, Rodley was sentenced to eight years in jail for trying to steal £229 million from a Japanese bank.

He was given an additional seven years behind bars in 2012 after being convicted of conning more than 700 pensioners out of £6 million.

In June 2016, he was sentenced to a further seven years in prison after failing to repay more than £1.2 million.
