A man has been jailed for life for murdering a former Microsoft executive by hitting her with a weight-lifting bar and drowning her in the River Thames at a private members’ club.

Nelio Gouveia, 45, was sentenced at Oxford Crown Court to life imprisonment, with a minimum term of 22 years and six months, for the murder of Jennifer Symonds, 44, in Henley-on-Thames, Oxfordshire, in March.

Gouveia, who was working as a kitchen porter at Phyllis Court private members’ club, attacked Ms Symonds after the pair had spent an evening drinking alcohol and taking drugs.

The court heard they had arranged to meet on 5 March after discussing smoking cannabis and drinking. They visited pubs before going to Gouveia’s room at the club, where a member later reported hearing a man shouting “I love you” and a woman screaming.

After leaving Ms Symonds by a pavilion, Gouveia accessed a pornographic website before returning to his room to retrieve a metal weight-lifting bar. He tucked it into the back of his trousers and approached Ms Symonds, who was waiting for him by the riverside.

He struck her over the head three or four times, causing them to fall against a moored boat and into the river, the court heard.

![Mr Justice Jay said Gouveia’s actions were rooted in a propensity for violence towards women](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/06/24/13/2198159695..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Prosecutor Neil Moore said Gouveia then made a deliberate effort to drown her, using his hands to push her head underwater and his feet to keep her submerged. The attack lasted around 12 minutes.

“Jennifer Symonds fought desperately to cling on to her life,” Mr Moore said.

“The more she fought, the more determined the defendant became to ensure she was kept under the water.”

Gouveia admitted murder in court last month. The killing was captured on CCTV footage, which was not shown in court. The judge, Mr Justice Jay, described it as “deeply disturbing and upsetting”.

The court heard Gouveia was serving a suspended prison sentence for an earlier assault on another woman when he murdered Ms Symonds. He had punched the woman in the face and hit her with a wine bottle at a gathering in June 2022.

Sentencing him, Mr Justice Jay said the similarities between the two attacks were “disturbing”. He said Gouveia’s actions were rooted in a propensity for violence towards women, fuelled on this occasion by excessive drug and alcohol use.

The judge also referred to Gouveia’s “appetite for violent pornography” and said the murder was an “exercise of power and desire to dominate”. He said the removal of some of Ms Symonds’s clothing was an additional act of “humiliation and degradation”.

![Ms Symonds brother described her as a kind and caring person who had touched many lives](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/20/17/01M7WY3HFP2BRPWTKEBMY2NZXX.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Prosecutors suggested Gouveia may have attacked Ms Symonds after she rejected a sexual advance, although the judge said this explanation was possible but had not been established to the criminal standard.

Ms Symonds, who was born in Zambia and moved to the UK aged three, studied at the University of Gloucestershire and had worked for Microsoft until she was made redundant the previous year. The court heard she had enjoyed a successful career in IT and received several awards.

Her brother, Andrew Symonds, said she was “deeply missed every day”, describing her as a kind and caring person who had touched many lives.

Her father, Peter Symonds, said the family's trauma was “devastating” and that he was haunted by what his daughter had endured in her final moments. He asked the judge to “please protect other women for as long as possible”.

Gouveia was arrested on the day Ms Symonds’s body was found in the Thames. After the killing, he took a taxi to Heathrow Airport intending to fly to Latvia, but later returned to Henley and went to a police station.

He told officers: “I think I hurt someone. I think I may have killed someone and I need to speak to someone quickly.”

In mitigation, Clare Wade KC said Gouveia had experienced domestic violence and sexual abuse as a child, had attempted suicide at 14 and had served in the Portuguese army before being discharged because of depression. She described him as isolated and lonely, and said he was deeply remorseful.

Thames Valley Police said its thoughts remained with Ms Symonds’s family and friends.
