A new scheme to allow 100,000 young EU citizens to live and work in the UK every year has been negotiated by the prime minister with counterparts in Brussels.

Under its terms, it is understood that the same number of UK citizens will be able to reside in EU countries in the near future.

British diplomats entered talks seeking a cap of between 50,000 and 80,000 places for the new youth mobility scheme, which has reportedly been negotiated higher by the bloc.

It forms part of a major new UK-EU deal that will also cover food and agriculture exports, and emissions trading, marking a major reset in relations after the 2016 Brexit referendum.

Andy Burnham has previously indicated his ambition to forge closer ties with Europe, saying in the past that Britain’s decision to leave it has caused “more harm than good”.

Speaking before the summit, the prime minister said: “Britain and Europe share values, interests and a global neighbourhood.”

“In a more dangerous world, we are stronger together. A closer partnership with Europe is good for working people, good for Britain and good for Europe.”

Here’s how the new youth mobility scheme could work in Britain:

## Who can apply for the youth mobility scheme?

The new scheme will be “modelled on the UK’s existing youth mobility arrangements,” guidance released by the Cabinet Office before the EU summit stated.

There are currently 13 such arrangements in effect between the UK and other nations. While each has its own rules and caps, there are some key similarities between them all.

In most cases, the visa applicant must be between 18 and 30. However, Australia, Canada, New Zealand, and South Korea have negotiated expanded age limits to 35.

The first three of these nations are also the only whose applicants are able to stay for up to three years on the scheme, as they can apply for a one year extension while residing in the UK.

For all others, the limit is two years.

Under current rules, EU citizens without settled status can only visit the UK for up to six months at a time, while Britons are restricted to 90 days in any 180-day period across the Schengen area, with neither permitted to work.

Reports from Brussels suggest that the UK-EU deal will lean toward the three-year stay offered to Commonwealth partners, and cap the upper age limit at 31.

Applicants under existing schemes must also have no children under 18 living with them or depending on them financially, and cannot have previously entered Britain on the same route.

For high-demand countries such as Hong Kong, India, and Taiwan, places are allocated via a random ballot rather than first-come, first-served applications.

## What are the work requirements and costs?

Unlike standard UK work visas, current youth mobility applicants do not need a job offer or an employer sponsor before arriving.

However, under current Home Office rules, applicants must prove they can support themselves by holding at least £2,530 in personal savings for 28 consecutive days before applying.

On top of those savings, incoming participants currently pay a £340 visa application fee alongside the Immigration Health Surcharge – set at a reduced rate of £776 per year – to access the NHS.

That brings upfront fees to £1,892 for a two year stay.

It has not been specified whether EU applicants will pay the full surcharge or be able to benefit from reciprocal healthcare arrangements.

## What can participants do while in the UK?

If the new scheme follows the standard rules, young Europeans will be free to work in almost any job – full-time, part-time, or temporary – with only narrow exclusions such as professional sport and coaching.

They are also permitted to study and be self-employed, provided they rent their premises, have no employees, and use equipment worth no more than £5,000.

While participants can also use their visa to study in the UK, most opt for a student visa to do this instead as it guarantees sponsorship for the full length of their course. In any case, the tuition fees international students must pay are much higher, ranging from £11,400 to as much as £38,000.

It is understood that how the new deal will work for European students has been a sticking point for negotiators, with the EU deferring further talks on its demand that they should pay home tuition fees – currently £9,790 a year – to attend British universities.

Crucially, the youth mobility route is strictly temporary.

Holders cannot bring spouses or family members as dependants, have no access to welfare, and cannot count their time on the visa directly toward permanent settlement unless they switch onto another route such as a Skilled Worker visa.

This makes it very different from full freedom of movement, largely in place between the UK and EU before Brexit, which had no limit on numbers, age, or length of residence.
