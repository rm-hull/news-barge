# Changing places make Yorkshire's attractive landscapes more welcoming: George Jabbour

## Introducing more changing places facilities at key locations around our stunning landscapes would encourage a wider range of people to benefit from the UK’s invigorating countryside. This, in turn, would bring many advantages, not only to those spending more time outdoors, but also to society as a whole.

The manifest gain for individuals is improved physical and mental well-being. On the macro level, there are major socioeconomic rewards to be seized.

This is one of the reasons why the National Parks and Access to the Countryside Act 1949 made it clear that the purposes of Britain’s National Parks are not limited only to conserving and enhancing their natural beauty, wildlife and cultural heritage.

National Parks also exist to promote opportunities for the public’s understanding and enjoyment of their special qualities.

Therefore, making our splendid scenery more accessible is a duty that members of National Park Authorities across the country take seriously.

Accessibility should be taken in the broadest sense of the term. The concept in this context encompasses public transport, parking for Blue Badge holders and well-maintained footpaths, amongst others.

An article in The Telegraph recently concluded that the walk at Sutton Bank is the “best” in North Yorkshire, citing its accessibility.

Moreover, accessibility covers raising awareness about our protected landscapes, preaching about what they can offer and instilling the confidence necessary to explore rural countryside for the first time.

As well as bringing visitors to National Parks, ensuring that their experience is agreeable would increase the likelihood of them returning and telling others about it.

This is where changing places are essential. These facilities, which are equipped with particular features such as an accessible toilet, a hoist, an adjustable bench and additional space, meet the special requirements of individuals and families who need to use them, without compromising their ability to discover breathtaking destinations.

As a member of the North York Moors National Park Authority and the Chair of the Howardian Hills National Landscape Partnership until a few months ago, I am a firm believer in the value that changing places create.

As a result, I was genuinely excited when a suggestion was mooted last year about building a changing places toilet in Helmsley, the appealing rural market town that I represent on North Yorkshire Council.

Making this idea a reality was not straightforward. There were many obstacles, including raising funds, securing planning and finding an organisation to lead the project - perhaps the crucial component.

The Howardian Hills National Landscape team stepped up to manage the initiative. While it was an intense period of time, it was a pleasure to work closely with its Manager, Ellie Hook, and other stakeholders, all collaborating with each other to accomplish this mission.

A number of entities contributed to the cost, together with the Locality Budget scheme of North Yorkshire Council, to which each Councillor can make recommendations.

We could not have chosen a better home for this facility. Its strategic position at the car park at the start of the Cleveland Way in the heart of Helmsley means that this new community asset will serve a National Park, a National Landscape and a National Trail, besides the town itself.

As it is the first restroom of its kind in Helmsley, one can truly say that our thriving town and surrounding picturesque countryside have become more welcoming. It is not enough for us to champion a cause; we must also successfully deliver on it – this is what the public expects.

Councillor George Jabbour is a Conservative member of North Yorkshire Council, representing the Helmsley and Sinnington division
