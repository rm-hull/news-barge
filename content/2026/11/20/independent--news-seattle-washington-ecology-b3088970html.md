The Washington state pulp mill where a chemical tank ruptured in May, killing 11, had an extensive track record of egregious and willful safety violations, including failure to inspect or repair more than a dozen other tanks at the facility, state investigators said Friday.

Nippon Dynawave Packaging Co. faces more than $10 million in state fines, including the largest employer safety fine in state history, and investigators plan to refer their findings to prosecutors for possible criminal charges.

“Without repair or replacement, the tank walls were a ticking clock,” Dan Pitts, state Labor and Industries compliance manager, said during a press conference in Longview to announce the findings. "It was a question of time, and time ran out."

Nippon did not immediately respond to an email seeking comment about the state’s findings.

Besides the 11 who were killed, 12 were injured on May 26 when the tank holding more than 500,000 gallons (1.9 million liters) of “white liquor,” a highly corrosive chemical used in paper manufacturing, ruptured. The flood of caustic material was powerful enough to blow out building walls and overturn pickup trucks at the site.

“Nippon’s decision to keep G-10 operating when they knew it was not safe contributed to the death of 11 workers," said Labor and Industries Director Joel Sacks. "Because of that, we will be referring this case to prosecutors to consider criminal charges.”

The state Ecology department also announced that it has issued 34 violations that impacted air and water, and is seeking $961,000 in penalties and costs for responding to the emergency.

The state investigation mirrored an August report by the U.S. Chemical Safety Board that revealed inspectors hired by the company in 2025 found the tank that ruptured was “not fit for continued service” due to deteriorating thickness in the tank walls.

State investigators said Friday the company failed to take appropriate action to address the issue in violation of state law, and similarly failed to inspect or address deficiencies in 13 other tanks at the site.

Records reveal a history of violations at the paper mill

An AP review of inspections and safety complaints at the plant prior to the tank collapse found that Labor and Industries had investigated more than a dozen safety complaints at the plant since 2018 and issued almost $7,000 in fines for violations. Ecology had issued 54 environmental violation notices against the company since 2016 and fined them $41,500 for eight serious offenses.

More than $2.4 million in workers’ compensation claims have been paid for 169 employees injured on the job since 2015, the agency reported, including for injuries related to exposure to caustic substances.

After the May tank collapse, the state’s Department of Ecology filed a violation notice against the company listing 35 citations.

Nippon Dynawave said in an email Wednesday that they have remained engaged with the government agencies that are investigating the tank collapse. They said the mill has hosted hundreds of visits by regulators and investigators.

“The full deconstruction, forensic examination and technical analysis needed to determine the root cause have not yet been completed,” the company said. “The tank is currently being deconstructed, with great care being taken to log and retain portions of the tank to determine the root cause.”

Among workers killed in the rupture were brothers Tyler and Brad Covington. Their mother, Sheri Lynn King, has filed a wrongful-death lawsuit against Nippon Dynawave, saying her sons were burned and crushed by the white liquor and debris. The lawsuit claims the company failed to maintain and inspect equipment, failed to properly train and supervise employees and failed to implement safety policies and emergency response procedures.
