If data centers are the engines of the AI boom, then full-fiber networks are the highways that allow these engines to move from one place to another. Without these routes in place, even the most powerful engine cannot deliver value at scale.

This point is too often overlooked in the current debate around AI infrastructure. Much of the rhetoric around the UK’s AI expansion has centered around data centers, energy capacity and access to advanced chips.

While these are all vital components of the AI economy, they are not sufficient on their own. AI will only become transformative when it can be embedded into everyday services and adopted by businesses across every region.

That requires connectivity infrastructure capable of supporting a very different type of digital demand from the one the UK’s original broadband networks were originally built to serve. AI is not simply another wave of internet traffic, nor is it just about downloading more content more quickly.

It depends on constant, two-way exchanges of data between users, devices, cloud computing platforms, edge infrastructure and intelligent systems that need to respond in real time. This is why full fiber connectivity should be viewed as a critical foundation for the UK’s AI future.

## Data centers alone will not deliver AI at scale

Previous generations of broadband infrastructure played an important role in connecting the UK. For over one hundred years, the UK has relied on traditional copper-based networks. However, the demands being placed on networks today are far greater than they were even a decade ago, let alone a century ago. Much of the internet was historically shaped around one-way consumption, with users downloading information, streaming content or accessing centralized services. AI changes the nature of that demand because intelligent applications increasingly rely on continuous upstream and downstream data flows.

If the UK wants AI to support economic growth beyond a handful of major technology hubs, the underlying networks need to be capable of connecting millions of intelligent endpoints, not just linking a small number of data centers to existing centers of demand. Over 170 data center construction projects are currently in the works in the UK, attracting billions in investment and becoming the focal point of the national AI conversation. But while data centers may power AI, they cannot deliver its benefits on their own to public services, businesses and consumers.

## AI infrastructure must be built ahead of demand

The UK’s current fiber infrastructure is uneven. Some regions have benefited from significant investment, while others are still reliant on aging networks that were not designed for the demands of a large-scale AI adoption. At the same time, regions such as Manchester and parts of Scotland are becoming more attractive to data center operators, supported by improving connectivity, regional investment and operational advantages such as cooler climates.

This shift should be welcomed, but it also highlights why digital infrastructure cannot be concentrated only where demand is already strongest. If the UK is serious about using AI to drive national productivity, improve public services and support regional growth, then full-fiber networks must be implemented throughout the country, not just in certain areas. Otherwise, we risk becoming a digitally exclusive country where access to the latest technology becomes a postcode lottery.

As one example, AI combined with full fiber could help connect centralized health systems with homes, wearable devices and remote monitoring tools, enabling earlier interventions and more efficient public health services. In education, AI-powered adaptive learning platforms could support students with different needs by creating more personalized learning paths or support the necessary upskilling of the UK workforce.

However, these services will only work effectively if the underlying connectivity is reliable enough to support constant interaction, fast enough to enable real-time responses and resilient enough to be trusted in high-value public services. This is why investment in full fiber should be seen not as a response to today’s broadband demand, but as preparation for the services the UK will need over the next decade.

## XGS-PON provides the foundation for AI-enabled connectivity

Launched in 2010, XGS-PON is an advanced fiber-optic broadband technology significantly improving the capabilities of the previous GPON technology. The reason XGS-PON matters is that AI changes not only the volume of network demand, but also its character. Future networks will need to support more symmetrical traffic, greater upstream capacity, lower latency and higher resilience as more devices and services continuously send and receive data.

XGS-PON is well suited to this next phase because it provides symmetrical multi-gigabit capabilities, creating the headroom needed for future applications that are more data-intensive or require lower latencies. This makes it a stronger foundation for an AI-powered country than legacy broadband technologies designed around older patterns of usage.

## Building for the future

The UK’s AI future will not be determined by data centers alone. They are vital engines of compute, but engines need highways if they are to move goods, connect markets and boost connectivity across the UK.

Full fiber, and specifically XGS-PON, is the infrastructure that can carry the next generation of AI services from data centers into homes, schools, hospitals, businesses and public services. Building that infrastructure ahead of demand is vital for the UK’s technological growth story. If we fail to do so, the benefits of AI risk being slowed by bottlenecks and concentrated in too few places.

The AI economy will depend on many technologies, but its success will ultimately rest on whether people and organizations can access intelligence when and where they need it. That means building the digital highways now, before the traffic arrives, creating the right environment for innovation to happen and generate growth.
