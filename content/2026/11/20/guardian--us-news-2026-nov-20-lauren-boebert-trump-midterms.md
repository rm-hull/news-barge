Lauren Boebert says Donald Trump “did some things that hurt” her constituents before they voted the pugnacious Colorado Republican out of her US House seat during midterm elections, which saw her and the president’s party lose control of Congress.

“This could be a referendum on President Trump,” Boebert said to the Associated Press in an interview published on Friday. “It could be the referendum on Congress or the Republican party. I think it’s all of it.

“I’m not mad at the president. [But] the president did some things that hurt my voters.”

Boebert expressed those thoughts during some of her first comments to the news media after Democratic challenger Eileen Laubacher defeated her by less than a 1% margin in a race called 10 days after the polls closed.

The conservative firebrand who first joined Congress in 2021 had widely been favored to win a fourth consecutive term. But low approval ratings for Trump after he started the war in Iran, pursuing an aggressive mass deportation campaign and other unpopular initiatives created an environment in which Democrats flipped nine seats in the US Senate and about four dozen in the House – including Boebert’s.

In Friday’s interview, Boebert pinned some of the blame for that outcome on the agenda that Trump set for the first half of his second presidency. But she also said, “it’s all of the Republican party,” which had majorities in both congressional chambers dating back to the latter part of Joe Biden’s lone Oval Office term.

“I think it’s more all of us,” Boebert told the AP, alluding to high fuel prices after the Iran war, Republican infighting and unfulfilled promises. “We had solutions, and we didn’t take action.”

Boebert navigated various controversies throughout her time in Congress, which began with her supporting Trump’s lies that the 2020 election that he lost to Biden was unduly rigged by fraudsters.

A 2023 scandal saw her and a man with whom she was on a date get kicked out of a performance of the stage production Beetlejuice in Denver for inappropriate behavior, including vaping and groping each other. She later apologized for the conduct, which was recorded on surveillance video, saying: “I simply fell short of my values.”

Just weeks before her contest against Laubacher, an ethics complaint filed against her accused her of having sexual relationships with three staffers, including one who was paid to stay silent. She denied the allegations cited in the complaint, which she dismissed as “politically motivated”.

Boebert’s interview with the AP on Friday was not the first time she has spoken out against Trump. Notably, she was among the Congress members who most loudly called for the full disclosure of files related to the prosecution of Jeffrey Epstein, the late accused sex trafficker whose social circle encompassed the US and global elite – even after the Trump administration tried to convince her to drop her support for a measure that would compel such a release.

Trump responded to Boebert by calling her “dumb”. He also kicked off his second presidency by vetoing a Boebert-sponsored bill that would have funded a drinking water project in her district after the legislation unanimously cleared the House and Senate.

That prompted a critical statement from Boebert saying: “If this administration wants to make its legacy blocking projects that deliver water to rural Americans, that’s on them.”

Boebert struck a more conciliatory tone in Friday’s interview with the AP, remarking, “I respect President Trump, I still work with him, I want to continue to work with him. Not for him, with him.”

She only referred to her future plans in vague terms, telling the outlet: “Too many people come to Congress and get rich. I am not one of those. I’m gonna go make some money now.”
