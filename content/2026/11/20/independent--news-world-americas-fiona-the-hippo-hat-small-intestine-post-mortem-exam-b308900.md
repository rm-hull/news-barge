A post-mortem examination on Fiona the hippo confirmed that a swallowed hat became lodged deep within her small intestine, causing her death this week, officials at the Cincinnati Zoo announced Friday.

The nine-year-old animal, whose premature birth and fight for life captured global attention nearly a decade ago, died Thursday.

According to a statement from the facility, the hat entered her enclosure accidentally before being consumed, reaching a point in her digestive system where no supportive care could have saved her.

Since her passing, grieving visitors have covered a hippo statue at the zoo with flowers and handwritten notes. Others left watermelons and pumpkins, which were among her favorite treats.

"She brought so much love in her lifetime," read one tribute.

Delivered six weeks prematurely almost ten years ago, Fiona was assigned slim odds of survival. She ultimately transformed into a local source of pride and an international symbol of hope for parents, teachers, and children.

![Tributes were paid to Fiona the hippo following her death](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/20/22/download..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Visiting the zoo on Friday morning, Jessica Grieshop, who frequently brought her daughters to see the hippo, described her as an inspiration for "anyone that was going through hard times."

Leadership at the zoo stated that she would be remembered for decades to come.

"The support that we’re feeling from all over the world reminds me of the time when everyone pulled together to root for her to survive," said Cincinnati Zoo director Dave Jenike. "She did, and she gave us almost a decade of smiles."

Fiona was not the first hippo to perish at the facility after ingesting a foreign item.

In 1923, a hippo suffered an untimely death when a young boy tossed a rubber ball into its mouth, according to historical records at Chicago's Field Museum, which confirmed Friday that it had received the animal's remains at the time. A taxidermist crafted a life-sized specimen mold, though it is no longer part of the museum's active collection.

Unlike the watermelons and pumpkins she routinely ate, a hippo's body cannot process synthetic fibers, specialists noted. Hippos rely on a multi-chambered stomach to digest plant matter, alongside an exceptionally large gastrointestinal tract that ingested items must traverse.

Veterinary staff attempted to reach the object using a scope, said Mike Wenninger, the zoo’s director of animal health. However, surgical removal was deemed impossible given its location and the hippo’s physical structure.

![Handwritten notes were left for Fiona the hippo at Cincinnati Zoo](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/20/22/download.-1..jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The necropsy determined the item was positioned 25 feet (7.6 meters) past her final stomach compartment and 50 feet (15.2 meters) prior to the end of her intestines.

"No treatment aside from supportive care had any chance for success, and the necropsy confirmed that no amount of supportive care would have worked," Wenninger said.

Jenike praised the animal care staff as heroic in their attempts to rescue her.

Initially, Fiona exhibited no signs of illness after swallowing the hat 11 days prior to her death. The item was dropped by a visitor before a ceiling fan struck it and sent it flying into the habitat.

Her appetite began to decline several days later, leading to intensive veterinary care by Monday. Officials had remained cautiously optimistic that the obstruction might pass naturally.

The care team delivered IV fluids, conducted a colonoscopy, and utilized a vibration table borrowed from a nearby horse farm to try to loosen the blockage.

Fiona's story united millions during her life and final days, bridging political and social divides.

![Fiona the hippo was born prematurely almost a decade ago](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/20/06/2026-11-19T221650Z_879513361_RC2AROAQZILZ_RTRMADP_3_USA-FIONA.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Usha Vance, the wife of Vice President JD Vance, shared a photograph showing one of their children standing face-to-face with Fiona through her enclosure wall.

Chelsea Clinton also shared photos from a personal encounter, writing that she would treasure "the smiles she gave me."

Fiona was born weighing just 29 pounds (13.2 kilograms), far below the lowest recorded birth weight for her species. Caretakers undertook extraordinary measures to save her, hand-raising the calf and hand-milking her mother because she was unable to nurse.

She remained a beloved figure throughout her nine years. Captive hippos typically live between 40 and 50 years.

Her survival originally offered an uplifting chapter for the Cincinnati Zoo following widespread criticism over the 2016 fatal shooting of Harambe, a gorilla killed after a child entered his enclosure.
