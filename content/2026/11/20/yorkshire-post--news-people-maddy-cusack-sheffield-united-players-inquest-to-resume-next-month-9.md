# Maddy Cusack: Sheffield United player’s inquest to resume next month

## An inquest into the death of former Sheffield United midfielder Maddy Cusack will resume next month after there was a delay to obtain more information about her time at the club.

Miss Cusack’s family sent a written complaint to United within a week after she died, outlining issues they alleged stemmed from her time working under her coach Jonathan Morgan.

The 27-year-old player was found unresponsive at her home in Horsley, Derbyshire, on September 20 2023, after Mr Morgan made comments about her weight and relationship with another player, Chesterfield Coroner’s Court heard.

Assistant Coroner Sophie Cartwright KC heard eight days of evidence from Mr Morgan, Miss Cusack’s parents, her former teammates, and members of staff from United, before the inquest was adjourned in July to get more evidence from the club.

United was asked by the coroner to look at its Microsoft Teams folders and Google Drive records for any information related to Miss Cusack, and complete an audit of her health records on the club’s system in the months before she died.

Ms Cartwright told a pre-inquest hearing on Friday: “The inquest remains listed to recommence on the 7th of December, with a five-day listing.”

The court heard the final evidence will be heard in Derby, where 11 witnesses are expected to be called.

The inquest was meant to begin in January but was postponed just before Christmas last year because of delays in disclosure of information to Miss Cusack’s family.

The coroner asked the player’s relatives on Friday to provide any more text messages that may be relevant to the inquest, acknowledging the “emotional upset” they might experience by accessing her phone again.

Miss Cusack’s mother Deborah Cusack is expected to give more evidence next month, after she said Mr Morgan was her daughter’s “nemesis” who was referred to as a “prison warden” in messages between teammates.

Mrs Cusack told the former United coach earlier this year: “There’s a direct line – if you hadn’t been employed, Maddy would be here today.”

The inquest has previously heard that Mr Morgan called Miss Cusack “bottom heavy”, which he said was referencing her muscular legs, and said “here come the girlfriends” when Miss Cusack was walking with her partner and United teammate Grace Riglar.

He denied calling Miss Cusack a “psycho” from the sidelines during a match before he joined United.

Asked about a comment he made calling her “generally a liar” in an account to the Football Association (FA), Mr Morgan had said she “omitted information” to her family about how he had supported her.
