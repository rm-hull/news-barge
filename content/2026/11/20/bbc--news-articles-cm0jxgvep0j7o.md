Alice Webb was given seven times the recommended amount of filler before she collapsed and had a cardiac arrest in 2024

**A mother-of-five's death has been ruled an accident after she suffered fatal injuries from being given seven times the recommended filler during a cosmetic procedure.**

Alice Webb, 33, collapsed at a friend's house in Gloucester on 23 September 2024 and died the following day after a liquid Brazilian butt lift (BBL).

At Gloucestershire Coroner's Court earlier, a jury concluded Webb's death was an accident. But the coroner ruled there were "significant risks" of future deaths and plans to send a report to the government.

Jordan Parke, known as the "Lip King", was filmed by Webb laughing while carrying out the procedure before she collapsed and suffered five cardiac arrests. He died in February.

Speaking outside the court, Webb's sister April Palmer said the outcome was "disappointing" and the coroner's recommendations will "lead to meaningful change".

Ben Kingscote and April Palmer gave statements outside the court after the inquest

She said: "We want to see urgent change in the law, stronger regulation and better protection for the public. We cannot change what happened to Alice but we can fight for change."

Ben Kingscote, Webb's ex-husband, said: "We want Alice to be remembered for who she was - warm, generous and endlessly kind. Her five girls were her world. She was a special person."

The inquest jury returned a conclusion of accidental death following a three-day hearing and said Webb died from "complications arising from BBL procedure".

Gloucestershire Police Det Insp Adam Stacey, who attended the three-day inquest, praised Webb's family for their "dignity" throughout the "complex and challenging" investigation.

"Alice's loved ones do not want another family to endure what they have, and hopefully the Prevention of Future Deaths report which is due to be released by the coroner will be an important step in that direction."

## More from Gloucestershire

Palmer previously told the inquest that Webb had been trying to "find elusive perfection".

Webb visited Parke for a non-surgical BBL, a procedure which involves injecting dermal filler into the buttocks to provide more volume and shape.

The procedure was carried out at the studio of another beautician, Jemma Pawlyszyn, who told the inquest she was doing Parke a "favour" in letting him use her premises - then called Studio 23.

The intention was for Ms Webb, who also ran an aesthetics business, to undergo training along with Pawlyszyn before receiving the BBL procedure herself.

![Several blue bowls full of syringes on a wooden table.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/320/cpsprodpb/bf9d/live/a44d58e0-e4fc-11f1-a8e4-b1e12a49db15.png)

The syringes at Webb's house on the night of the procedure

Parke gave Webb seven times the recommended amount of filler before she collapsed and went into cardiac arrest.

The post-mortem examination found hyaluronic acid - which is used in a non-surgical BBL - in the fatty tissue and muscle of Webb's buttock, along her sciatic nerve, in her lungs, brain, heart, thyroid gland and kidney.

Home Office forensic pathologist Dr Stephen Leadbetter gave her cause of death as "embolisation of hyaluronic acid from introduction into the buttocks".

In October last year, a BBC investigation found that Parke was under investigation for gross negligence manslaughter, but was not charged.

The 34-year-old, who lived in Manchester, had built up a large social media following for his aesthetics work.

He had previously been issued with prohibition notices preventing him from performing the BBL procedure in Dudley and Salford over concerns about his practice from environmental officers.

Ashton Collins is the director of safer cosmetics charity Save Face

Following her death, Webb's family and safer cosmetics charity Save Face launched a campaign to call for a ban on liquid BBLs from high-street beauty clinics.

Ashton Collins, director of the charity, said she was "beyond disappointed" in the outcome of the inquest.

"This sends a dangerous and alarming precedent. It sends a message that even if you cause the most severe circumstances that you can get away with it and it will be deemed accidental.

"Alice's family have been robbed. It makes a mockery of their experiences.

"Now, more than ever, it's important to recognise these treatments are life-threatening."

The government announced a crackdown on "cosmetic cowboys" performing BBLs last month.

## Stronger fines proposed

Under the new rules, treatments that involve injecting fillers anywhere other than the face would be legally defined as cosmetic surgical procedures, meaning that can only be performed by qualified medics.

Other high-risk treatments like deep chemical peels and vampire facials - which use micro needling to inject platelets drawn from a patient's own blood back into the face - would be restricted to doctors, dentists, some nurses, midwives and pharmacists.

Those who break the rules could be hit with unlimited fines or fixed penalties of up to £4,000.

A consultation will be launched by the government to ask for input from doctors, regulators and businesses to draw up draft regulations.

The blueprint will then go through the parliamentary process, which is currently planned for spring.
