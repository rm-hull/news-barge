* **Major US banks have changed their terms of service regarding AI agents, telling customers they are at fault if something goes wrong**
* **If a customer has given an AI agents access to their account, the banks will treat any action taken by the agent as authorized by the customer — even if its a mistake**
* **AI companies are introducing "read only" tools to allow users to analyze financials without giving agents purchasing power**

Citibank has followed in the footsteps of JPMorgan and Bank of America in changing their terms of service this week to leave customers fully at fault if an AI agent with access to their bank account makes a mistake — or even if it gets hacked.

Banking giants Citizens and PNC have mirrored Citibank’s changes, refusing to accept responsibility for the actions of third-party AI agents given access to accounts by customers.

It was only a matter of time really, given how many people not only consult their AI before making financial decisions, but actually let their AI assistants run rampant with their bank details to analyse their spending and make purchases on their behalf.

## You’re on your own, Citibank says

“As third-party AI tools become more widely available, we want customers to understand the risks associated with sharing banking credentials or authorizing those tools to access their accounts,” a Citibank spokesperson said (via *TechCrunch*).

“We recently updated our Online User Agreement to provide greater clarity about those risks and to reinforce that customers who choose to grant account access to third-party AI agents are responsible for activities conducted through those agents.”

The issue stems from violations of the number one cybersecurity rule: don’t give anyone your login details. For some people, that apparently doesn’t include an AI agent. Typically a bank’s terms of service require a customer to log in and perform any financial transactions themselves, and AI agents have introduced a new liability that is tricky to navigate — until now.

Basically, if a customer gives a third-party AI agent permission to access a bank account, the bank treats it as an authorized action by the customer. The ‘third-party’ part of that statement is important because Citibank has deployed a range of AI tools for customers to use. If something goes wrong while using one of those, customers won’t face any extra liability.

Given the level of risk involved in providing an AI agent full access to your finances, some AI giants have begun pulling back on the levels of access customers can provide. Gemini for example has introduced a “read only” feature via Google Wallet for analysing spending and tracking payments. Anthropic launched a similar tool earlier in November.

The problem with giving an AI agent access to your finances isn't just that it might make a mistake. It's that, increasingly, your bank is making it very clear that you'll be the one paying for it.
