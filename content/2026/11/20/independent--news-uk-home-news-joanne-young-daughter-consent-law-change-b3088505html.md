The daughter of a woman who was drugged and raped by her husband and sexually abused by other men he met online over a 13-year period has called for consent laws to be updated in the wake of their convictions.

Former Tory councillor Philip Young abused his wife Joanne while she was unconscious at their home in Swindon, Wiltshire, and used sites such as Craigslist, Grindr and Fab Swingers to advertise her for sex without her knowledge.

The prosecution stated that the four men, who were all strangers to each other, failed to seek consent from Ms Young before they engaged in sexual activity with her, while the defendants claimed they had been duped by Young and believed Ms Young was consenting.

But on Thursday, a jury found four men guilty of sexually abusing Ms Young, who has bravely waived her right to anonymity and called for the government to look at consent laws “as a priority”.

Speaking outside Winchester Crown Court after the verdicts, Mrs Young said that “no one should be able to give it (consent) on your behalf”.

In a social media post later, Ms Young’s daughter, Alex Young, echoed her mother’s calls and said the law must be updated in order to protect women and girls.

“We live in a world where there is debate over whether third-party consent is enough to have sex with someone,” she wrote. “We live in a world where someone’s partner can consent for them, and that is enough to use it as an argument in a court of law.

“This is simply unacceptable. It has been 23 years since this was last updated. Society has progressed, crime has progressed. So why hasn’t the law progressed with it?”

She added Mrs Young had been “incredibly strong” throughout the ordeal and that she is “so proud of her, and every decision she has had to make to get here”.

“They tried to hide, but she made sure they were seen,” she continued. “If you are experiencing domestic violence, there is help out there for you. Violence against Women and Girls must end.”

The Sexual Offences Act 2003 states that “a person consents if he agrees by choice, and has the freedom and capacity to make that choice”.

Speaking on LBC on Thursday, former minister for safeguarding and violence against women and girls Jess Phillips said the Young case was not isolated.

She said: “The ability of these men to find each other on websites that you and I could just log on to now – it just means that these cases are going to keep on coming.”

Asked what the government could do to tackle the kind of offending where a perpetrator uses drugs to incapacitate a victim, and online platforms to recruit others for sexual abuse or post images of the abuse, Ms Phillips said online safety laws must be reinforced.

“The state needs to make sure that its online safety laws and regulations are strong enough to stop this from ever being able to happen,” she told LBC.

“I think that every single one of the websites listed in this case should be called on today to answer the question: can you guarantee this isn’t happening today?

“And can the pornographic websites where these things get posted, can you guarantee that in every video, that consent was completely and utterly sought?”

James Foster, specialist CPS prosecutor, said after the verdicts Young “could not consent on his wife’s behalf.”

“None of these defendants was entitled to simply assume that she was willing – consent is personal, it cannot be given by somebody else, it cannot simply be assumed,” Mr Foster added.
