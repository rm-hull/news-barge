New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani will spend the coming weeks in a medical boot after tearing his Achilles tendon in a pickup soccer match against the City Hall press corps on Thursday night.

Arriving at a Friday news conference on crutches, the 35-year-old leader explained how the injury occurred minutes into Thursday’s game when he was questioned by a reporter.

As for what caused the injury, the millennial leader of America's biggest city pointed to a neglected pre-game warmup, admitting: "I did not stretch."

Nevertheless, the avid soccer fan was pleased to share that he scored a goal "soon after getting on the pitch" before noting that he "forgot that I was 35."

He was also quick to point out that his team ultimately beat the journalists' squad.

![Mamdani is a huge Arsenal fan](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/20/10/2305768937..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The mayor, known for keeping a busy schedule, stated he will wear the medical boot for six to eight weeks and expects to be back on the pitch in about nine months.

Mamdani has made no secret of the fact that he is a huge Arsenal fan, the current English Premier League champions.

Mamdani has displayed his affection for the Gunners, who pipped City for the title last season to break a 22-year drought, in recent months, including an appearance at Eid al-Adha prayers in the Bronx while wearing a club-themed kurta.

Mamdani recalled supporting Arsenal from the age of nine thanks to his uncle and idolised players such as Tony Adams and, particularly, Nwankwo Kanu, given the Nigerian “had been born in Africa but now lived somewhere else, just like me.”

Mamdani told the New York Times about how Arsenal had become an escape for him and recalled the intense battles with City in recent seasons: “Over these past two years, no matter how chaotic life became, Arsenal remained the constant.

“And throughout every moment, when a Manchester City resurgence felt inevitable, when the socialist project felt fragile, when both football and politics appeared determined to reward only wealth and cynicism, I have returned to the words of a man with no enemies: Bukayo Saka. ‘It’s a process.’ And we should trust it.”
