The Harrogate Advertiser has been treated to an exclusive behind the scenes visit to a film set when a pioneering indie filmmaker returned to his home town to shoot his latest DIY low budget movie.

Writer, director, actor and producer Miles Watts has been making films and writing scripts since he was ten years old.

This talented cinematic jack of all trade made his first short film as schoolboy in Harrogate in 1984 - Indiana Jones and the Sacred Treasure, a three-minute adventure shot on a Super 8 camera.

This was at a time before digital cameras, a time before, even, the invention of “guerrilla filmmaking”, an independent production approach that uses ultra-low budgets, small crews, and minimal gear to shoot quickly in real locations.

But, as he got more serious about movie-making in those analogue days, taking a film course at Harrogate College, nothing was going to stop him, Miles tells the Harrogate Advertiser.

“I was just very frustrated at the way you have to go about raising money to make films, and quite frankly bored at the idea of sending off forms and applying for grants and justifying ourselves,” he said.

“I just wanted to make this film, simply, but to a high quality, and I thought I could do it, so I did it."

After doing an English degree at the University of York, Miles's breakthrough came with his official cinematic debut in 2010 , the vigilante action comedy CrimeFighters set in York which received theatrical distribution around the UK in Picturehouse Cinemas.

Miles also found more recent national acclaim with Zomblogalypse, a horror-comedy film he co-directed with Hannah Bungard and Tony Hipwell, which began as a web series and was so popular it celebrated its 15th anniversary with a series of public screenings in York.

Nothing seems to phase the irrepressibly inventive and positive Miles.

It would be wrong, however, to say that his passion isn’t without its challenges.

The film industry and the life of an independent film maker has changed a lot since his early days.

Miles’s return to filmmaking in Harrogate is for the 80 minute horror mystery Drop Dead which already boasts a nifty tagline - "tonight we're going to die like it's 1999".

A film within a film, it’s set in the late 1990s when a group of friends are doing their own Blair Witch Project film.

But that surprise indie hit, which premiered at the Sundance Film Festival and went on to take $248.6 million at the box office globally, did, at least, have a budget, if a small one, at approximately $50,000.

As Miles gathers his small cast and crew around him at Harrogate Sports and Fitness Club to work on Drop Dead at Harrogate locations including Hookstone Woods and parts of Wetherby Road, there is no evidence of any cameras whatsoever.

Or a budget, for that matter.

For Drop Dead is the second of two movies that Miles has shot this year entirely on iPhone.

He does admit, quietly, that he did have to buy a steadicam and lapel mics for this one.

The shoot lasts only six days with a crew of scarcely a dozen, mostly students from Harrogate, York and Manchester, plus a couple of school pals from those early film shoots in Harrogate, including Derek Boyes, who was on the same film course as Miles at Harrogate College back in 1991.

Miles may specialise in ‘genre films’ but he does feel the emotion of his own homecoming on this shoot.

"It's been amazing to be filming in Hookstone Woods with a childhood friend.

"It's a special place. I used to ride my bike in the woods as a child.

"I've filmed so many times in the York area, I just wanted to come home

"My folks moved back to Harrogate about ten years ago so I still visit occasionally.

"But it feels good to return to my own filmmaking roots."

Miles isn’t alone in his dogged commitment to filmmaking.

As well as boasting the traditional indie moviemaker’s command of the art of ‘begging, stealing and borrowing’ and a strong sense of humour, he also has the support of his own film production company.

Outward Films is a creative filmmaking partnership dreamt up by Miles with fellow York-based writer, actor, and filmmaker Oliver Semple and actor and writer Maria Gray.

From low budget to micro-budget to no budget, the arc of DIY filmmaking’s narrative over the last 20 years has, in financial terms, been its own horror story.

But the costs of making indie movies have also fallen dramatically at the same time.

Rather than bleating about problems, Miles’s focus is on the cinematic DIY freedom the situation affords true cineasts like himself.

"The reason I’ve been making a couple of movies this year for no money is because I just wanted to get them done.

"I've been really impressed by the young crew; they’re so talented and have an amazing work ethic.

“Some weren't even born in 1999!”
