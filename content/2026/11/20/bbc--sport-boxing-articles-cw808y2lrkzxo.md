Lauren Price hopes to fight on bigger stages as her career continues

**Lauren Price dominated Michaela Kotaskova to seal her place in history by becoming undisputed world welterweight champion.**

Price, 32, took an unanimous, shut-out decision and the acclaim of the crowd to add the WBO title to her collection and become the first Welsh fighter to claim the accolade of holding all four major world titles.

Already an Olympic champion and world champion from her amateur days, it further underlined her claim as being among the very best in boxing.

But the way in which she did it, extending a perfect professional record to 11 wins from her 11 fights, suggested there could yet be more to come.

Price won every round from every judge in front of a partisan Cardiff crowd, producing an unrelenting pace that punished her opponent at every opportunity.

Although Price could not force a stoppage, there was never any doubt she had done enough against an opponent full of stamina and stubbornness.

Kotaskova, beaten only once, but never stopped in her career, showed real heart but had no answer to Price's expertise.

The Tokyo gold medal winner could celebrate even before the final bell sounded, climbing the corner ropes and into the record books.

It was lapped up by a sell-out Utilita Arena crowd who excitedly acknowledged the achievement and the moment.

Wales has produced 14 world champions, some of them genuine greats.

They include, of course, Joe Calzaghe who so often graced this venue with successful defence after successful defence of his super-middleweight crown.

He never reached undisputed status, though he was able to cross the capital city to go from arena to stadium champion.

That is the next challenge for Price, claiming before she left the ring that more is to come while talk immediately turned to five-weight world champion Claressa Shields as a stadium fight next summer.

## Champions recognise champions

The moment Lauren Price became undisputed

That is for the future, but this was a night for history - with the calibre of some of the former boxers in the stands a nod to that.

Champions know champions. Five Welsh world title winners were in the capital to watch their compatriot do something never been done before.

From Cinderella Man Steve Robinson and Barry Jones, to the more recent Joe Cordina, who may still be eyeing a stadium return of his own in the coming weeks.

All have been proud to fly the flag, but perhaps none have worn it quite as much on their sleeve - or shorts - as Price has done.

Here, entering to the strains of a Welsh male voice choir, the atmosphere moved from Friday night in a city centre to full-blooded patriotism.

The anthem stirred so much Kotaskova couldn't help but raise an eyebrow before stepping out of her corner.

From then, she had no time to savour the moment let alone consider become an opportunity the Czech Republic's first world champion.

She felt flurries of punches from the first moments and struggled with the unrelenting pace set by the champion.

Steely-eyed, Price refused to allow a breath and, while Kotaskova continued to try and come onto the front foot, she was met by angry, but calculated attacks.

Kotaskova's problems only increased when she was cut in the fifth round, the hooks from the home fighter providing no let up.

At one point in the seventh, Kotaskova took a shot so fierce she almost turned her back to say no more, but still she came and still Price went hunting with hooks.

She could not find the decisive blow, finally letting the tempo slip, and even enjoying the final seconds as she accepted she had done more than enough to win and win a place in boxing history.

## Edwards impresses in significant undercard win

![Rhys Edwards in blue shorts throws a punch at a coverin Lee McGregor in white shorts](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/de83/live/20db8300-e52a-11f1-964d-a1d5fe40573e.jpg)

Featherweight Rhys Edwards secured an 18th career win as he took the decision on all three scorecards

Price was not the only Welsh winner on the night as Rhys Edwards secured a significant victory over Lee McGregor, taking an unanimous decision to claim the WBA Continental featherweight championship.

McGregor, 29, had stepped back down to featherweight after two defeats in his last three bouts and had made it clear he knew his chances of returning to world-challenging level was on the line.

His determination to keep coming forward showed what it meant, soaking up several clean shots, but Edwards proved too quick and too sharp.

The 26-year-old from Penygraig also had something to prove after a disappointing draw with Gully Powar last time out.

Then he faded in the latter round, but here Edwards managed to take McGregor's punches and maintain his own pace. It ensured what is perhaps the biggest win of his career, and to leave McGregor contemplating his options.

Elsewhere on the undercard, Willy Gilheaney's unbeaten record was taken away by the experience of Argentinean Alberto Igancio Palmetta, the former Olympic boxer showing plenty of know-how to overcome the Swansea fighter's guts and early control to take an unanimous points win.

Earlier in the night, Doncaster's Jake Goodwin took the decision in a game middleweight bout with Liverpool fighter Sean Arkwright. Goodwin – who had former world featherweight champion Josh Warrington in his corner – had only accepted the fight on two weeks' notice, accepting before he was even granted time off work.

And there were wins for Welsh fighters Brandon Scott and Kane Shepherd, while Cardigan's Josh Mellor and Newport's Lewis Howells fought to a draw.
