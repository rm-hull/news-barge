Relatives of a mentally-ill British woman lured to America by a sadistic offender who then killed her have said their "lives will never be the same again".

The siblings of 32-year-old Sonia Exelby, from Portsmouth, expressed hope that the tragedy would highlight the dangers posed online, particularly to vulnerable individuals.

They described their youngest sister as "kind, gentle and full of warmth" and someone who "touched so many lives".

Their remarks were delivered to a court in Ocala, Florida, after 54-year-old Dwain Hall was convicted of kidnapping and first degree murder.

He was subsequently given a life sentence.

During the trial, evidence revealed Hall had spent years grooming and manipulating Ms Exelby on a now-closed fetish website using the username Alpha Sadist.

Shortly before her arrival in Florida last October, Hall bought a shovel and rope. Prosecutors said he beat Ms Exelby and stabbed her four times, keeping the knife as a "prized possession" to relive the crime "over and over again for his own sexual, sadist pleasure".

Ms Exelby’s semi-naked body was found by investigators in a shallow grave on October 17 2025.

A later search of Hall’s phone found he had “numerous images of women being raped, tortured and murdered”.

Addressing the court on Friday, Ms Exelby’s brother Luke Wilton said: “Coming all this way to the courtroom in Ocala has been terrifying for our family. We didn’t know what to expect when we got here, but what we found, however, has moved us beyond words.

“From the moment we arrived, the warmth, decency and care shown to us has made us feel genuinely welcomed.”

Praising the authorities, he said the state of Florida had “fought in our corner” with a prosecution team “who have left nothing to chance, no stone unturned”.

He also thanked the jury for its “patience, attentiveness and dedication throughout this trial”.

Mr Wilton added: “This has been an exhausting, emotionally draining rollercoaster.

“Nothing can bring Sonia back. This tragedy has changed us forever, but because of the truth and the accountability reached in this courtroom today, we can finally begin to heal and start looking forward with our lives.”

Speaking through tears, Ms Exelby’s sister Gemma Isaj said: “Without Sonia, our lives will never be the same again. We must learn a new way of being.”

She added: “There are so many people to thank and we are truly, wholeheartedly humbled. It’s been remarkable how many people have come together to ensure that this cog of justice turns.

“We will forever be so grateful for the outstanding and dedicated professionals. Each and every one has been so kind.”

The victim’s other sister Sara Exelby said: “Today, our family wants to remember Sonia for the person she was – kind, gentle and full of warmth.

“She had a way of making people feel welcome and cared for, and she touched so many lives.”

She added: “We hope Sonia’s story encourages people to take mental health seriously. Mental health does not discriminate and can affect anyone.

“People deserve compassion, understanding and support. We also hope her story reminds everyone to be thoughtful about the harm that can happen online, especially when someone is vulnerable.”
