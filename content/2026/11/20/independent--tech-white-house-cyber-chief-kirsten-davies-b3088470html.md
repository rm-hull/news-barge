The White House has appointed a new national cyber director after Sean Cairncross stepped down from the role in the wake of widespread outages that disrupted air travel, banking and other critical services.

Kirsten Davies, chief information officer at the Defense Department, was named as the top cyber security official for the administration, according to posts to social media from various members of Donald Trump’s cabinet.

"Our nation stands in the crossroad of analog, digital, and quantum technologies, our American way of living increasingly interconnected," Ms Davies wrote in a post on X.

"Our opportunity is to dominate the cyber landscape for generations to come… But only if we modernize and drive resilience into the foundations our great nation runs on, as well as build cyber talent for our future."

Her new role will see her oversee cyber security policy ​and strategy ⁠across the federal government. The appointment will need to be confirmed by the US Senate, which will be controlled by Democrats beginning in January.

![Sean Cairncross stepped down as White House national cyber director and is to be replaced by Kirsten A. Davies, who currently serves as the Pentagon’s chief information officer](https://static.the-independent.com/2026/11/20/0/41/GettyImages-2304986941.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth congratulated Ms Davies on X, writing: "She has proven herself to be a dedicated patriot and professional, and we are confident she will advance President Trump’s America First agenda across the cyber security landscape."

President Trump faced widespread criticism as Americans contended with a cascade of outages that halted travel, severed work networks, restricted access to banks, and disrupted classified communications.

Even staunch Trump allies accused the administration of lying to the public or concealing details about the true origin of the disruptions. Over the past week, Microsoft, Chase Bank, air traffic control, and the National Security Agency have all been affected.

On Tuesday, Mr Rubio sought to reassure Americans, saying there is "zero" information suggesting the technical failures are linked or caused by a malign actor.

The White House made the change as Trump returned from a three-day trip to Asia, during which he downplayed the incidents, declaring that "things like that happen."
