From the shadow of **Rome’s Colosseum** to major squares in**Milan**,** Turin**,** Naples**and** Palermo**,** tens of thousands of workers and students took to the streets across more than 100 Italian cities**as a** 24-hour general strike**disrupted schools, hospitals and public transport. Called by** Italy’s**largest trade union,** CGIL**, alongside grassroots unions such as** Cobas**, the mobilisation coincided with an international student protest day in** over 45 cities** and a wider European day of action over living costs, military spending and the climate crisis.

Carrying banners declaring ***"Education builds, war destroys"*and*"Funds for schools, not for war"*** through **Rome’s** historic centre, demonstrators demanded higher wages, stronger social protections and public investment instead of defence outlays. Protester**Giorgia Giordani** faulted the government’s handling of surging energy bills and everyday household expenses, while a student outside**La Sapienza University** said crowds wanted answers, and that some were demanding the government’s resignation, echoing**CGIL** Secretary-General**Maurizio Landini’s** call for sweeping reform of Italy’s social and economic model.
