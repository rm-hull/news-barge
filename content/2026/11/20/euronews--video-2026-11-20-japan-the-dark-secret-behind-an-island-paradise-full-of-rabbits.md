**Known today as “Rabbit Island”, Okunoshima drew roughly 200,000 visitors last year**, most arriving to photograph the tame animals roaming its coastal paths. Yet behind its idyllic reputation lies a stark wartime secret: between** 1929 and 1945**, the Imperial Japanese Army operated a covert chemical weapons factory on the island, erasing** Okunoshima** from official maps to conceal production.

Today, the skeletal remains of a wartime power plant and former chemical storage depots punctuate the landscape, while the **Okunoshima Poison Gas Museum** documents the factory’s operations through workers’ gas masks, period photographs and production apparatus.

Museum director **Kazuhito Takahashi** notes that many domestic visitors arrive knowing little of what transpired on the island, stumbling upon its dark history only after making the trip for the rabbits. Aided by guided tours for students and travellers alike, the striking contrast between the island’s gentle wildlife and its haunting military ruins has turned**Okunoshima** into an unlikely crossroads of leisure and historical reflection.
