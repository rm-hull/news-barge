Two police officers have been dismissed for gross misconduct over their response to a road collision in which three people died, the police watchdog has announced.

Eve Smith, 21, Darcy Ross, 21, and Rafel Jeanne, 24, were discovered dead at the location of the crash on the A48 in the St Mellons area of Cardiff, South Wales, on 6 March 2023, roughly two days after their last known sighting.

Two other passengers, Sophie Russon, then aged 20, and Shane Loughlin, who was then 32, suffered severe injuries and were found at the scene with the Volkswagen Tiguan in which the group had been travelling.

The fatal incident is thought to have taken place at around 2am on 4 March 2023, with Gwent Police receiving the initial missing persons notification concerning the group at approximately 7.30pm that evening, according to the Independent Office for Police Conduct (IOPC) watchdog.

PC Richard Hardwick and PC Lauren Livingstone faced misconduct proceedings regarding their response to the crash amid allegations that they failed to inspect the residential addresses of two missing women.

![Floral tributes were paid at the time to those who died in the 2023 crash](https://static.independent.co.uk/2023/03/06/16/POLICE%20Cardiff%20%2013562391.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The disciplinary panel concluded on Friday that PC Hardwick breached professional standards of behaviour by failing to carry out home searches for two of the young women reported missing by their relatives, the watchdog said.

He proceeded to lie to his supervisor and IOPC investigators, asserting that he had already conducted the checks, while recording false information within a police log.

PC Livingstone was also determined to have committed gross misconduct after similarly failing to search the properties during her visit alongside PC Hardwick.

She intentionally submitted vague details to the police log suggesting that property checks had occurred despite knowing they had not, the IOPC stated.

PC Livingstone was further found to have acted dishonestly after telling her supervisor that the searches were complete.

Both officers have been dismissed from the force and will be added to the College of Policing’s barred list, banning either officer from working in policing in future.

Derrick Campbell, IOPC director of engagement, said: "Our thoughts and sympathies continue to be with the families and friends of the young people who sadly lost their lives, those who were seriously injured and everyone affected by this incident.

"The actions of PC Hardwick and PC Livingstone were inexcusable with both failing in the fundamental basics of policing; to act honestly and with integrity.

"Conducting property searches is a basic but essential part in the early stages of a missing person investigation.

![People attend a vigil in the St Mellons area of Cardiff, in memory of Eve Smith, 21, Darcy Ross, 21, and Rafel Jeanne, 24 (Ben Birchall/PA)](https://static.independent.co.uk/2023/03/17/14/174805860d1191a6964dee06eb3adc2aY29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNjc5MTQyMTgw-2.71282176.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

"However, PC Hardwick deliberately lied during the course of the missing person investigation, claiming on police records and to his supervisor that searches had been completed.

"To add to this, when given an opportunity to provide a truthful account, he lied to IOPC investigators. PC Hardwick would have known he was providing false information but he chose to do so any way.

"PC Livingstone had ample opportunity to tell the IOPC the required searches had not been conducted. Instead, she did not give an account and stated she could not remember the specifics of her visits to the homes of two of the young women who had been reported missing during her interview with the IOPC."

A gross misconduct hearing has yet to take place for a further two Gwent Police officers concerning their handling of the initial missing persons reports.
