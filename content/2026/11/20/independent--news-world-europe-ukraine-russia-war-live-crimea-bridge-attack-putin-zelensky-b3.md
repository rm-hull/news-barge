The overnight strikes, the largest on routes connecting the left and right banks of Kyiv since the start of the war, came hours after Vladimir Putin told Ukraine to expect a "painful" retaliation for its successful strikes this week on his flagship £3bn bridge to occupied Crimea.

Putin made the threat after an unprecedented trilateral summit with Xi Jinping and Donald Trump which ended without any breakthroughs on the wars in either Ukraine or Iran.

While Trump opened the gathering by declaring the trio were “really making history,” the talks concluded with a four-point “peace declaration” that made no mention of either Moscow’s invasion of Ukraine or Washington’s conflict with Iran.

European leaders, who were excluded from the Shenzhen talks, warned that the warm reception afforded to Putin would only embolden the Kremlin to escalate its campaign against Ukraine.

## Rival summit on Ukraine pushes for European role in talks to end war

European leaders have expressed their readiness to negotiate with Russia to end the war in Ukraine, insisting that Europe must have a seat at the table.

Speaking at the Coalition of the Willing summit in Berlin, British prime minister Andy Burnham said Europe had a “united negotiating position”, while German chancellor Friedrich Merz argued Russia’s “maximalist demands” were unrealistic.

“The US understands that there can be no peace without us. We, in turn, know that with the United States by our side, the prospects for peace increase,” Mr Merz added.

US envoy Jared Kushner said Donald Trump wanted to reach a deal with Europe’s support, adding that the president believed “peace is achievable” following his meeting with Russian president Vladimir Putin and Chinese president Xi Jinping at the Apec summit in Shenzhen.

Mr Kushner and fellow envoy Steve Witkoff travelled to Berlin to brief some 40 European leaders on Mr Trump’s efforts to end the nearly five-year war following his historic trilateral talks with Mr Putin and Mr Xi this week.

## Trump signals support for security guarantees for Ukraine

The summit between European leaders in Berlin on Thursday was called to discuss postwar security arrangements for Ukraine. President Volodymyr Zelensky wants to ensure that Russia doesn’t invade again, while Moscow wants to keep the Nato military alliance away from its borders.

“I think we’ve gotten the Russians comfortable with that concept where any security guarantee for Ukraine would be based on Article 5,” US envoy Jared Kushner said. “And I think that that comes with at least what the Russians are saying, which is their intention is when this war is over to not have another war in the future.”

Kushner said Trump is “open” to an Article 5-like security guarantee for Ukraine, although “obviously it has to be in America’s interest to do that. And we also have to make sure that it’s part of a broader security package.”

He stressed that Trump wants to make a deal with Europe “by his side,” and is not looking to impose a unilateral agreement.

"Any deal we do will be in close consultation with Ukraine and with Europe. And that’s something that he also asked us to come here today and reassure people in that regard,” said Kushner, who is Trump's son-in-law.
