# UK households planting veg now will 'make real difference' come spring

## November is the ideal time to plant broad beans, garlic and overwintering onions in the UK

As temperatures drop and gardens enter dormancy, it's easy to feel inclined to postpone vegetable cultivation entirely until spring returns. Yet November actually represents a vital period for sowing particular crops, with certain vegetables needing the cooler climate to establish themselves properly.

Horticulturists are encouraging people to capitalise on the winter planting opportunity, with broad beans, garlic and overwintering onions among the crops that can be planted immediately. Jo McGarry, luxury garden design specialist at Caragh Nurseries, observed that modest preparation during the colder period could yield a "real difference" when spring emerges, reports the Express.

She added: "People often think there's not much to do in the garden during winter, but it's actually a good time to get certain vegetables started. You can get them established now so they're ready to grow when the weather starts warming up again."

Three vegetables to plant in November.

### Broad beans

Broad beans represent an outstanding option for November planting, especially across the milder areas of the UK. They can be sown straight into the earth and will start developing their root networks throughout winter, providing them with a significant advantage when spring arrives.

"Broad beans are one of the easiest crops to get started now," Jo explained. "If you're in a milder part of the country, you can sow them straight into the ground and they'll start establishing before the worst of the winter weather arrives. Just make sure they're planted somewhere with good drainage, as they won't appreciate sitting in waterlogged soil."

### Garlic

November is equally an ideal opportunity to plant garlic, as the cloves need a period of cold temperatures to develop properly.

Jo said: "Garlic is one I'd definitely recommend getting in now. It needs a spell of cold weather to develop properly, so winter is a good time to plant it. Separate the bulb into individual cloves and plant them with the pointed end facing upwards, leaving enough space between each one so they have room to grow."

### Onion sets and shallots

Overwintering onion sets and shallots can similarly be put into the soil at this stage. Getting them into the ground during November gives them ample opportunity to become firmly established throughout the colder months, ready to spring back into growth once temperatures start to rise.

"Onion sets and shallots are another good option if you want to make use of the garden over winter," said Jo. "Choose varieties specifically suited to overwintering and plant them in a sunny spot with well-drained soil. They don't need much attention once they're in, but it's worth checking on them during particularly wet or cold spells to make sure they're not sitting in water."

### How to protect vegetables from frost

While certain crops thrive when planted during winter, younger plants and those exposed to particularly severe conditions may need extra protection to endure the colder season. "The main thing I'd recommend is keeping an eye on the weather and protecting anything that's likely to struggle with frost," Jo said.

"Horticultural fleece is really handy for this, as you can simply lay it over your crops when temperatures are expected to drop. Cloches and cold frames are also worth having, particularly if you're growing younger plants."

For those growing vegetables in containers, it's sensible to move them to a more sheltered location, such as against a wall or inside a greenhouse. Jo added: "Pots get much colder than the ground, so moving them somewhere protected can make a big difference."

Jo further suggested cutting back on watering throughout the winter period, as most vegetables grow considerably more slowly and demand less moisture than they would during the warmer seasons.

She said: "You want to avoid the soil becoming waterlogged, as roots can end up sitting in cold, wet conditions and start to rot."
