Police are preparing to lay a baby girl to rest, two years after she was found dead beneath a tree in the Salford suburb of Little Hulton.

The baby, named by police as Ava, was found by a dog walker in Ashtons Field on 20 November 2024.

Detectives said at the time that Ava was believed to have been born at 38 to 39 weeks’ gestation.

She was believed to have been buried in a shallow grave, before animals disturbed her remains and left her on top of a layer of snow.

Two years on, Ava’s identity – and that of her parents – remains unknown.

![Detective Inspector Louise Kelly](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/20/17/24/Louise-Kelly.jpg?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&trim=10%2C2%2C7%2C0&auto=webp)

Greater Manchester Police have renewed their appeal for any information about the baby girl and the events that led up to her body being discovered in the park. A £50,000 reward is being offered.

“Two years have now passed since Ava was found in Ashtons Field, and throughout that time our determination to find answers for her has never wavered,” Detective Inspector Louise Kelly, the senior investigating officer in the case, said.

“Most importantly, we want to secure the justice that Ava deserves.

“We know that the 20th November will be particularly significant for Ava’s parents, family members, and anyone who may have known her. I want to appeal to them to say we would like to speak to you to understand how Ava came to be found in Ashtons Field in November 2024.

“Behind every line of enquiry, every review of evidence, and every piece of information we assess is a baby girl who will never be forgotten.”

![Flowers left near Ashtons Field, where baby Ava’s remains were found](https://static.independent.co.uk/2025/10/15/12/cbf192b17547ee72b0bef32058b22c69Y29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzYwNjEyNDI5-2.78263346.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

DI Kelly said that police are working with local authorities to organise a funeral for Ava.

“Last year, we were able to share details of a distinctive item recovered with Ava – a pair of ladies’ size large underwear featuring what we believed to be cartoon donkeys,” she said.

“Despite the unique nature of this item, and our appeals to identify its origin or owner, we have received no new viable leads.

“We firmly believe that someone may hold information that could help us understand the full circumstances surrounding Ava's death. It may be something that seems insignificant, something seen or heard at the time, or information that has never before been shared with police.

“That one piece of information could prove crucial in helping us uncover the truth and provide the answers that Ava deserves.

“We will not give up on Ava. We simply ask anyone who knows something, no matter how small they believe it to be, to find the courage to come forward and speak to us.”

Kate Green, the deputy mayor of Greater Manchester for Safer and Stronger Communities, said her team is committed to supporting the police on the case.

“This reward remains available because we believe someone may have information that could help investigators. I would urge anyone who knows something to come forward and speak to police."

Anyone with information is asked to contact police on 101 quoting log 1319 of 20/11/24.

Information can also be shared via the Major Incident Public Portal.
