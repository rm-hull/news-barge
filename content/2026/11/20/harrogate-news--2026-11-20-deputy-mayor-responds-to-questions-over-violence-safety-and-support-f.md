**Violence, abuse and personal safety are wider societal issues, and tackling them requires an approach that recognises the experiences of different groups, according to York and North Yorkshire’s Deputy Mayor for Policing, Fire and Crime.**

The comments from Jo Coles follow the announcement of a student safety event in York focused specifically on violence against women and girls, and questions from The Harrogate Informer about how the Combined Authority addresses violence and abuse affecting men and boys.

The event, called **One York: It Takes All of Us**, will take place on Wednesday 25 November at York St John University’s Creative Centre. It is being organised by York and North Yorkshire Combined Authority in partnership with York St John University and the University of York.

Students aged 18 and over studying in York are being invited to share their experiences, discuss concerns and learn about support services available to women and girls. The evening will include discussions on women’s safety, opportunities to meet support organisations and the chance to contribute views on how York can become safer.

## Recognising different experiences

In response to questions about what attention is being given to violence against men and boys, male victims of domestic abuse and sexual violence, and whether comparable opportunities exist for male students, Jo Coles said the focus on violence against women and girls reflected the scale and prevalence of those offences.

> **Jo Coles, Deputy Mayor for Policing, Fire and Crime, said:**
>
> Everyone has the right to feel safe. The UK Government has made halving violence against women and girls one of its central missions, reflecting the scale, prevalence and disproportionate impact of these crimes on women and girls. In our county around 80% of victims of crimes including rape, sexual assault, harassment and domestic abuse are women and girls.

She said this did not diminish the experiences of male victims, adding that services commissioned by the Combined Authority support all victims.

> **Jo Coles, Deputy Mayor for Policing, Fire and Crime, said:**
>
> That does not diminish the experiences of male victims, and our commissioned services support all victims. However, it does help to explain why there is a particular focus on action to address violence against women and girls which has been left unchecked and unchallenged for far too long. It is also why our police and crime plan reflects that commitment.

Ms Coles also acknowledged that different groups can face different risks and barriers to reporting abuse, saying support services need to be responsive to those experiences.

She said the Combined Authority’s Gender-Based Violence Survey would assess experiences across the county, helping it determine whether services are being commissioned appropriately to support all victims.

## A wider societal issue

The questions put to the Combined Authority were not intended to challenge the importance of tackling violence against women and girls, but to ask how this work sits within the wider problem of violence and abuse across society.

Violence, domestic abuse, sexual exploitation and harassment can affect people of different ages and backgrounds. Male victims also face barriers to speaking out and seeking help, while wider concerns about men’s mental health and suicide remain part of the societal picture.

The challenge is therefore not simply about identifying which group experiences the greatest level of a particular crime. It is about ensuring that prevention, public awareness, reporting routes and support services recognise the different circumstances people face.

In her response, Ms Coles pointed to the Mayor’s Men’s Mental Health Investment Programme and the Youth Commission’s Anyone Can Be a Victim campaign as examples of wider work to support people.

> **Jo Coles, Deputy Mayor for Policing, Fire and Crime, said:**
>
> Through the Mayor’s Men’s Mental Health Investment Programme and the Youth Commission’s Anyone Can Be a Victim campaign, we will always work with all our partners to ensure we’re doing everything we can to support all victims. Our violence against women and girls strategy will also be published early next year.

She also referred to recent high-profile court cases involving national figures, including Russell Brand, which she said had brought issues of sexual violence, consent and abuse of power back into public debate.

> **Jo Coles, Deputy Mayor for Policing, Fire and Crime, said:**
>
> The Mayor and I are committed to ensuring anyone who is a victim is able to access help and support to enable them to cope and recover regardless of their gender, age or background.

## Changing attitudes and improving safety

The original announcement for the York event stressed that making communities safer requires collective responsibility. In her statement, Ms Coles said men and boys had an important role to play in improving women’s safety and challenging harmful behaviour.

That wider principle also raises the question of how communities, public services and institutions can create an environment in which everyone feels able to report abuse and access help, without their experiences being overlooked.

Addressing violence against women and girls is an important part of that work. So too is ensuring that male victims and other groups affected by violence and abuse are recognised within the wider response.

Ultimately, improving safety requires more than individual initiatives. It involves changing attitudes, challenging harmful behaviour, improving confidence in reporting and ensuring that support is available to people who need it.
