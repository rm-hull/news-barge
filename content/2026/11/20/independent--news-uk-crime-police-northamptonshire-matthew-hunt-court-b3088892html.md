A former police officer who sent sex-related messages to a vulnerable 14-year-old girl he met while on duty has been jailed for 14 months.

Former Northamptonshire Pc Matthew Hunt, who was sacked in 2024, was handed the sentence on Friday at Leicester Crown Court.

A judge described the messages – which discussed sexual experiences and urges – as “completely and totally inappropriate”, given the girl’s age and Hunt’s police role.

Leicester Crown Court heard on Friday that Hunt, now 27, did not send any inappropriate images to the teen and had made it plain during online contact that she could not have sex with him.

Hunt’s barrister said that the newly qualified officer had acted out of “naivety rather than malice” when he sent the messages in June 2024.

The defendant, of Earl Shilton, near Hinckley, Leicestershire, pleaded guilty at previous hearings to offences of sexual communication with a child and misconduct in a public office.

![The messages were ‘completely and totally inappropriate’ given Hunt’s police role, a judge said](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/09/29/14/69ac5d9b79bb6902c20ecd04767dccb7Y29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzkwNzczMzcz-2.21314942.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Judge Timothy Spencer KC told Hunt: “You were 25 at the relevant time and you were a serving police officer.

“You were young in life experience and your maturity was somewhat below your chronological age.”

The judge said that Hunt’s messages to the victim “turned to explicitly sexual conduct and sexual matters”.

He told Hunt: “From the beginning you knew perfectly well that you should not have been in contact with a 14-year-old.”

The judge said that it appeared Hunt had initially tried to act a friend and mentor to the teenager, but had then sent messages discussing previous sexual activity and pornography.

Imposing a prison term and ordering Hunt to sign the sex offenders register, the judge said the ex-officer had known his conduct was “completely and totally inappropriate” but had continued sending “pretty well daily”.

Judge Spencer added: “There is obvious and serious aggravation here. There was a high degree of public trust placed in you.”

Heather Oliver, offering mitigation on behalf of Hunt, asked the court to consider a suspended prison term.

She told the court the disgraced officer, who has recently been working in a factory, had recently completed a policing “tutorship” when the offending took place.

“He presents as exceptionally immature and naive,” the barrister said.

Ms Oliver said the messages – including one telling the teen she should not be having sex – had failed utterly to maintain boundaries, but were not the actions of a “furtive predator”.

Commenting after the case, the lead investigator, Detective Constable Donna Fleming, said: “No one is above the law and those who abuse the trust placed in them by their colleagues and the public will be held to account.

“I would like to thank the victim and her family for their support during our investigation, and I hope this case shows that Northamptonshire Police is determined to bring anyone who has committed a crime, regardless of who they are, to justice.”
