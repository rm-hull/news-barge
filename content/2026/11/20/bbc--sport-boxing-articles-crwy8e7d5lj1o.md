Lauren Price on making Welsh boxing history

**It won't be the biggest fight that comes to Cardiff over the next few weeks.**

But that doesn't mean Lauren Price's latest ringwalk into history shouldn't be seen as a main event in its own right.

The Welsh capital is about to become boxing's first city, the eyes of the world preparing to focus on the Principality Stadium and the heavyweight showdown between Tyson Fury and Anthony Joshua.

Less than half a mile down the road, and three weeks before the circus comes to town, Price takes on Czech challenger Michaela Kotaskova at the 5,000 capacity Utilita Arena.

There will be fewer fans, less fuss and frenzy, but more than just pride at stake.

Price, in only 10 fights, already has three of the four major world titles to her name in the welterweight division, after a perfect start to her professional career.

The remaining belt is on offer on Friday, and thus the opportunity to become the first Welsh fighter to achieve undisputed status.

Not even the iconic Joe Calzaghe managed that - the last great fighting pride of Wales who himself went from headlining the arena where Price fights to selling out the stadium down the road.

That is the aim for Price.

"People think I'm off my head wanting to fight at the Principality," the 32-year-old said after weighing in for her fight with Czech-born, Vienna-based Kotaskova.

"But I feel I've got a whole nation behind me."

The journey, says Price, keeps on getting bigger. Victory on Friday may see another arena bout in February, but stadia are in her thoughts for the summer.

Cardiff City Stadium has been mentioned, as has Cardiff Arms Park the venue where, in 1995, Naseem Hamed was crowned world champion at the expense of Cardiff Cinderella Man Steve Robinson.

It is part of the plan for promoter Ben Shalom.

"We want to bring the big nights here, can we get the big stadiums and can we create history with Lauren," Shalom detailed.

"Not just as a one-off, but we want to bring stadium fights to Cardiff regularly.

"That's what the building blocks have all been about.

"I was worried initially that having the fight on the Friday we wouldn't have the same number of supporters. It's been bigger. I've never seen the level of sales for someone at this stage of their journey."

## Emulating Taylor's Croke moment a 'realistic' goal

![Katie Taylor in a ring robe walks down a ramp with a packed stand of fans behind her and green strobe lights ](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/48c7/live/5f3a4480-e465-11f1-80e9-fd4d95b40178.jpg)

More than 80,000 fans witnessed Katie Taylor bow out from boxing at Dublin's Croke Park in September

Yet while Price's journey to tonight's fight is likely to pass the alluring outline of the Principality Stadium, a venue that could arguably provide greater inspiration is a few hundred miles west.

Settling into newly married life – something she says has added to her happiness at home and hunger at work - Price watched from the sofa as the trail-blazing Katie Taylor sold out Dublin's 83,000 capacity Croke Park for her final fight two months ago.

Is Price able to become that level of main event?

"She has the opportunity to go and prove that," argued Shalom.

"Don't forget, Katie is 10 years Lauren's senior, but the journey has been similar with the Olympic gold and having a nation behind her.

"Look at where Lauren is now, headlining already. We can see from the trajectory that's the goal - and it's a realistic goal."

Indeed, it's hard to ignore some of the shared stories between the two. A talented amateur having played international football for their country before putting on the gloves, flying the flag of a smaller nation, taking on all comers.

Partly because of the path forged by Taylor, Price has headlined earlier than Ireland's 2012 gold winner and scooped up more titles at this stage of her career.

And Price's visibility on terrestrial broadcast – this fight her third screened by the BBC – is very much part of a plan to build the brand into something bigger. After all, how better to become a household name than to be available to watch in every household?

Hence there is no envy at not being an undercard fighter on that Fury-Joshua bill, not when she has her own fans to consider and cultivate. An outstanding boxer with a standalone support, a main eventer in her own right.

But what she needs, as Taylor found with A-list adversaries such as Amanda Serrano, is a dance partner.

As a glance at the grudge match due to be held at the Principality Stadium will remind, big-stage boxing needs its rivalries.

Because of her quality, Price has dismissed all opponents without time to build one.

She goes into her 11th fight having only dropped a handful of rounds, a badly cut lip in her last defence against Stephanie Pineiro in April providing Price the opportunity to dig deep and "learn lessons".

She believes she is getting better and seems more confident, certainly looking more at home in the professional world of ticket-selling and soundbites than she did when she first hung up the amateur gloves after her 2021 Olympic success.

But the step up in class – and in stardust – lies elsewhere.

Five-weight champion Claressa Shields looked a possibility but talks broke down when Cardiff was not an option in a two-fight proposal. Chantelle Cameron and Mikaela Mayer fight each other at light-middleweight next month, Mayer giving up the WBO welterweight belt Price now eyes in the process.

## 'Price is not perfect - it makes me hopeful'

![Michaela Kotaskova poses on the scales at a weigh-in, wearing a black sports bra](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/dc67/live/e5bb0ea0-e464-11f1-80e9-fd4d95b40178.jpg)

Michaela Kotaskova has only been beaten once in 15 professional fights, losing on points to Chantelle Cameron in April

Even Kotaskova admits she was surprised to receive the call to face Price, believing the four are the elite out on their own – although not without warning the home fighter that "nobody's perfect".

Kotaskova believes she saw weaknesses in the Pineiro fight that can be exploited to cause a shock.

The 35-year-old should not be taken lightly, her only professional defeat coming at the hands of Cameron after 10 three-minute rounds at light-middleweight in April.

But she is not surprised Price is looking to the future given her class - and Price is comfortable with being ambitious.

"It comes from belief," said Price.

"I think I've showed a level in boxing to justify that, and you have to have plans.

"I believe in myself and I believe I get that win on Friday - and then I want to know when I'm boxing next."

And who?

A question for Shalom to answer, and he is not afraid to state his own belief that Price has the capability to hit heights never seen before in women's boxing, saying the vision at the beginning was to lay foundations for Price to become the greatest.

"I think people will naturally want to see Lauren Price kick on and do what we say she's going to do - beat everyone in the sport. Win every belt. Win at every weight division," Shalom said.

"Claressa Sheilds, for me, is the greatest female currently in world boxing in what she has done and her legacy," he added.

"I think Lauren is the only one that can challenge her. She has a background and a fan base to be respected and we really believe can take the throne."

Price was among Welsh sporting royalty at Thursday's weigh-in when Wales rugby great Sir Gareth Edwards turned up to show his support.

Likewise, she has lent her support to other Welsh boxers who gain exposure and a big-show opportunity on Friday's undercard.

But, make no mistake about who the main event in Cardiff is right now.

In fact, Price has the chance to make sure it's undisputed.
