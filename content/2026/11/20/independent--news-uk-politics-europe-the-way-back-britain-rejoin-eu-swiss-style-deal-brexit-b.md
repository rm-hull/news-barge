Rejoining the EU in full would make Britain twice as well off as a “halfway house” Swiss-style membership, a new study has found.

The economy would get a £112 billion a year boost compared to £54 billion, according to a report on the economic benefits of the various options for rebuilding the UK's links with Europe being considered by Andy Burnham.

The findings, reported by the *Financial Times*, came as the Prime Minister arrived in Brussels to try to finalise moves to “reset” Britain's relations with the EU.

The report, by the Productivity Institute think tank, says investment would flood back into the UK if it restored full membership of the EU.

The £112 billion boost would be equivalent to the entire yearly budget of the Department for Education.

Mr Burnham, who has said Brexit has “not worked” for Britain, is considering a range of proposals to renew ties with Brussels.

They include what he has described as “going all the way” and rejoining in full, or so called “halfway houses” such as rejoining the Customs Union or Single Market.

Under the Single Market option, which already applies to Switzerland and Norway, UK trade regulations would be brought back into line with the EU, allowing the free movement of goods, services and capital.

A more limited Customs Union agreement would remove tariffs on goods traded between members and set common tariffs for outside nations.

However the Productivity Institute study, led by Manchester University economic trends expert Jun Du, says restoring full membership would deliver double the quantity of economic fruits than either “halfway house”.

He says it would boost the UK's gross domestic product by up to 3.7 per cent per year in the long term - equal to £112 billion at 2025 prices.

This compares with a 1.79 per cent increase - £54 billion - from a Swiss style Single Market deal.

A Customs Union agreement would produce a 1.26 per cent rise in GDP worth £38 billion a year.

According to the *Financial Times*, the report, co produced with the Centre for Business Prosperity at Aston University, says that if the UK agreed to move closer to the EU by 2035, 80 per cent of the potential boost to the economy would be delivered within 15 years.

The full advantages would not be felt until 2050.

It states that reduced trade barriers, including EU red tape on UK exports, and an increase in investment hit by Brexit uncertainty are among the main reasons the UK would be better off economically by a complete reversal of Brexit.

The report’s findings were based on the possible impact of Mr Burnham's Brexit “reset” scenarios on the assumption that he unveils his plans in the next general election, due to take place by 2029, and gets a mandate from the electorate to negotiate with the EU.
