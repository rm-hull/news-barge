Xi Jinping met Anthony Albanese on the sidelines of the Apec summit in Shenzhen and declared a “full turnaround” in China’s relations with Australia since the Labor government took office in 2022.

“We have jointly promoted the full turnaround of China-Australia relations and exchanges and cooperation in various fields between our two countries have continued to deepen,” Mr Xi said.

“I will work with you in the spirit of equality, seeking common ground despite differences and mutually beneficial cooperation to take forward the China-Australia Comprehensive Strategic Partnership,” the Chinese president told the Australian prime minister.

Mr Xi also agreed to designate 2027 as the “Australia-China Year of Tourism”.

Mr Albanese said the relationship “delivers prosperity for both of our countries”, but stressed the importance of “frank” conversations.

“And the relationship that we are building is positive, is calm, is not defined by our differences, but one where we can have clear and frank exchanges for our mutual interest,” he said.

It was Mr Albanese’s fifth meeting with Mr Xi. When he took over as prime minister in 2022, bilateral relations were at a low ebb following Chinese trade restrictions on Australian exports and a freeze in high-level talks. Mr Albanese said his government “worked hard to stabilise the relationship” with the country’s “major trading partner”.

![Anthony Albanese, left, meets with Xi Jinping in Shenzhen, China, on 20 November 2026](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/20/06/download..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Mr Albanese said he discussed a range of issues with Mr Xi. “It is important that Australia continue to engage in a constructive way with China, manage our differences, but not be defined by them,” he said.

“We have different political systems, different histories,” he said, “but that doesn’t mean that you can’t have constructive dialogue, and that’s what Australia seeks.”

His meeting with Mr Xi came amid growing geopolitical tensions caused by Australia’s recent defence cooperation deal with the Philippines, its participation in a Quad summit with the US, India and Japan, and its signing of a US-led agreement on advanced AI technology and supply chains – all moves unlikely to be welcomed by Beijing.

From Shenzhen, Mr Albanese is going to Beijing for further talks with Chinese premier Li Qiang and to attend an Australia-China CEO roundtable, where business leaders are expected to seek greater trade certainty and improved market access.

Meanwhile, Mr Xi held separate meetings with Indonesia’s Prabowo Subianto, Singapore’s Lawrence Wong, Malaysia’s Anwar Ibrahim, and Thailand’s Anutin Charnvirakul. The meetings came a day after the Apec summit in Shenzhen, attended by leaders and representatives from 21 member economies.

Mr Xi had hosted an unprecedented trilateral meeting on Thursday with Russian leader Vladimir Putin and US president Donald Trump. Afterwards, the leaders of the world’s three dominant military powers issued a joint statement, titled “Declaration Towards Peace”, reaffirming that a nuclear war couldn’t be won and must never be fought.

Asked about the trilateral engagement, the Australian prime minister described it as “positive”. “It is always better to have dialogue than to have conflict,” he said.
