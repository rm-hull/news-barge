# “Clear the landslide now” - Residents of road in Halifax closed for three years send out clear message

## Fed-up residents say they are united in their demands for action to be taken on their road in Halifax, which has been closed for almost three years.

Prospect Street, off the A58, near the town centre, was closed on December 13, 2023 after a landslide struck on November 29 that year.

Since then, the road has been closed, leaving residents to grow increasingly frustrated over a lack of action to re-open it.

A public meeting was held on Saturday, organised by Halifax MP Kate Dearden and attended by Derek Benn, a council representative from the community safety partnership team.

Council leader Daniel Sutherland was invited to the meeting, but did not attend.

"Three years on, residents and the local school are still living with the disruption," one resident, who did not want to be named, told the Courier.

"Emergency services also continue to struggle to reach incidents by approaching the A58 end of the street where the landslide is.

"The residents were told that geotechnical surveys have been completed, but that the landslide is still moving. Officials said the long silence was due to extensive legal consultations.

"Residents were united in their demands: clear the landslide now, carry out construction work to stop it happening again, and ensure the council pursues whoever is responsible."

Halifax MP Kate Dearden said: "After such a long and difficult ordeal for residents, I would hope that the priority would be to work together, share information openly and ensure the community is properly informed, rather than creating separate meetings or withholding information that could help residents understand what is happening next.

"Residents deserve to see all of us in positions of influence working together, putting their needs first and focusing on securing a solution.

"Issues such as this one are above party politics, and I genuinely want to work together so that visible action to clear the road can begin."

Kate says the geotechnical survey has been concluded and that it is now up to the political leadership at Calderdale Council to decide how to proceed.

The Halifax MP also said the Environment Agency will undertake a further assessment of what regulatory powers they can use based on all the investigatory work Calderdale Council have carried out, and that will continue to inspect and regulate the landslip site, as well as work with the council to get the road cleared.

"I have received assurances that any action the council proposes to restore the landslipped area, and that needs support from the Environment Agency, will be prioritised," she added.

"I believe there are two potential options on the table and I understand the council are appointing contractors in advance of their next steps.

"Option one would mean the council could escalate its legal case to compel the party it believes to be responsible to undertake the work and cover the costs of reopening the road and clearing the landslip.

"Whilst this provides greater protection of the public purse and public accountability, it would require a further legal process, which could take several months to conclude. There is also no guarantee that the party identified as responsible would comply.

"Option two would mean Calderdale Council begins undertaking the work to clear the road, now that it has a clearer understanding of what it is dealing with as a result of the geotechnical survey.

"Whilst this would be an expensive option, and the impact on the public purse must be seriously considered, it could hypothetically mean the council could be in a position to commence work from early next year.

"However, I understand that with this option, the council may not be able to recoup these costs, and it may set a precedent for future incidents across Calderdale where responsibility is disputed.

"The council leadership need to make a decision on this as soon as possible, and be honest about the challenges that come with whichever option is put forward.

"I have written to the council asking them to give serious consideration to option two. However, residents and I also want to understand the legal assessments and cost implications in full before a decision is made. That is precisely the information the council should be sharing with residents."

Calderdale Council have been approached for comment by the Courier.
