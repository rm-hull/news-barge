A painting by Frida Kahlo has been withdrawn from an auction in New York at the request of Mexican authorities.

The painting, “Congreso de los pueblos por la paz,” or “Congress of the Peoples for Peace,” which depicts a white dove perched on a fruit-bearing tree alongside a smiling sun, the moon, and two slices of watermelon, had been scheduled for auction by Christie’s on Tuesday.

The auction house confirmed it had withdrawn the painting from this week's auction after Mexican authorities requested the sale be held until questions about the painting's provenance could be clarified, citing a declaration that recognizes Frida Kahlo’s work as an artistic monument of Mexico.

“In collaboration with the seller, we decided to withdraw the lot at this time for further research,” a Christie’s spokesperson said Thursday.

“We look forward to selling this work at a later date."

![Frida Kahlo's Congreso de los Pueblos por la Paz is exhibited in 2020](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/20/10/1250792563..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The same painting was auctioned in 2020 by Sotheby’s without any objections.

In Mexico, Kahlo’s works are considered artistic monuments and are protected under the country's federal law on monuments and archaeological, artistic, and historical sites.

Mexican authorities said they were working with the Diego Rivera and Frida Kahlo Museums Trust, administered by the Bank of Mexico.

Kahlo painted “Congreso de los pueblos por la paz” in October 1952 and donated it to the World Peace Council's peace congress, held in Vienna that year.

Her health was seriously deteriorating at the time, and she sent the painting to the congress through her husband, the painter Diego Rivera.

She died in Mexico City two years after creating it.

![Frida Kahlo](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/19/21/Frida_Kahlo_53144.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The trip to Vienna was recorded in the memoirs of Emma Hurtado, Rivera’s last wife, and has also been mentioned by historians such as Raquel Tibol.

The painting had left Mexico more than three decades before the 1984 decree declared Kahlo’s work an artistic monument. The decree prohibits the permanent export of her works that were in Mexico when it was published.

Last year, Kahlo’s 1940 self-portrait “El sueño (La cama)," or The Dream (The Bed), sold for $54.7 million.

The price set an auction record for a work by a female artist.
