Vice President JD Vance is no longer the frontrunner to be the Republican Party’s nominee for the 2028 presidential race, according to a surprise new poll.

Vance was quick to acknowledge the blue wave that wiped out the GOP’s candidates at the midterms earlier this month, handing the majorities in both chambers of Congress to rival Democrats, and took to X to state plainly that the Iran war was the reason for his side’s loss.

However, in a new post-election survey from Emerson Polling, the VP appears to be struggling to persuade voters he is the man to lead their party once President Donald Trump departs the scene, finishing second and picking up the support of just 20 percent of respondents.

Way out in front was Secretary of State Marco Rubio, who scored 36 percent of the vote – a major concern for Vance given that he led Rubio by 54 percent to six percent in the same poll just a year ago.

Although Vance is known to have opposed the Operation Epic Fury strikes against Tehran behind the scenes, he vowed to support Trump and Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth in prosecuting the war and has not managed to escape association with it as one of the administration’s leading personalities.

![Vice President JD Vance is no longer the frontrunner to be the Republican Party’s 2028 presidential nominee, according to a new poll](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/17/20/2026-11-13T003912Z_845318231_RC2NMOAI6JLT_RTRMADP_3_USA-VANCE.JPG?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&auto=webp)

Rubio, by contrast, has kept himself above the fray and busy with diplomatic missions around the world, cutting a serious and statesmanlike figure compared to some of his Cabinet colleagues.

The secretary joined Trump on his tour to Asia this week and delivered a no-nonsense press conference in Manila at which he confidently dismissed intelligence sector warnings about the prospect of Iran detonating a dirty bomb in Washington, D.C., and struck a soothing note on the recent spate of tech outages that have alarmed the public.

By contrast, Vance joked on Fox News that it was not yet time for Americans to stockpile food and water or “build a bunker” and Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin raised eyebrows by declaring “a guy that’s very smart with tech” had told him AI could be behind the outages.

Rubio has previously said he would not challenge for the GOP nomination if Vance were also running out of loyalty to his friend – and got angry when he was asked about it recently – but the VP has yet to make any commitment and Trump has enjoyed asking aides which of the two men they prefer.

Other candidates pitched in the Emerson poll drew a much less favorable response, with Florida Gov. Ron DeSantis scoring seven percent, political novice Donald Trump Jr six percent and right-wing broadcaster Tucker Carlson five percent.

![Secretary of State Marco Rubio’s stock is rising among conservatives dismayed by this month’s midterms results](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/18/09/download.-6..jpg?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&auto=webp)

Vance’s potential candidacy received a further knock this week when another conservative influencer, Ben Shapiro, baldly stated that he should not be their party’s nominee in two years’ time.

“I think he’s extraordinarily online,” Shapiro said of the VP. “I think the vice president spends an inordinate amount of time worrying what the podcast bros think, like the Tucker Carlsons, and I think that he steers directly into that.

“I think his entire affect has been militantly ‘anti-establishment’ in ways that are designed to appeal to 21-year-old Joe Rogan listeners. And I think that is not how you win a broad-scale national election.”

Despite Emerson’s disappointing findings for Vance, he is still ahead in the estimation of the prediction markets Kalshi and Polymarket.

On the Democratic side, Emerson placed Georgia Sen. Jon Ossoff out in front on 17 percent, 2024 nominee Kamala Harris on 16 percent, New York Rep. Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez on 15 percent, ex-transportation secretary Pete Buttigieg on 14 percent, California Gov. Gavin Newsom on 11 percent and Kentucky Gov. Andy Beshear on seven percent.

Arizona Sen. Mark Kelly, Illinois Gov. JB Pritzker and Pennsylvania Gov. Josh Shapiro were all on five percent each.
