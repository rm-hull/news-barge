## The EU and UK agreed on a youth scheme letting 100,000 young people live in each other’s territory, but a deal on how much to lower university fees was delayed by six months.

Brussels and London have agreed to let 100,000 young Europeans live in the UK, but a deal on university fees has been postponed by six months, at a major summit on Friday.

The EU and the UK have been seeking a broader reset in relations at their second bilateral summit since Brexit, and a youth mobility scheme to live and study abroad is one of its main elements.

“This is something which is important in terms of people-to-people contacts on both sides of the channel,” an EU official said.

The Youth Experience Scheme (YES) will allow 100,000 young people from the EU and 100,000 from the UK to live in each other’s territory for up to three years. It is expected to be up and running by January 2030 at the latest.

However, the two sides failed to settle the contentious issue of university fees, committing instead to hash out a deal within six months.

The UK, which hosts far more EU students than it sends to the bloc, wants to simply lower the fees.

The EU, by contrast, wants its students to be treated equally to British ones at UK universities, as they did before Brexit, when EU students benefited from equal treatment under EU rules prohibiting discrimination on grounds of nationality.

The post-Brexit rise in fees has driven EU enrolment down sharply. The number of EU students starting a course at UK universities fell by 57% between 2020/21 and 2023/24, according to the University of Oxford’s Migration Observatory.

“Since Brexit, the level of students from both sides studying on the other side has decreased, notably EU students in the UK. We think that this is something that makes our relationship poorer, and we want to address this,” the EU official added.

The UK has already agreed to rejoin Erasmus+, the EU exchange programme for education and vocational training, with participation starting on 1 January 2027.

Besides the youth scheme, the EU and the UK also agreed several other deals, including aligning agrifood standards and linking their carbon markets.

The question is whether member states will make final approval of these agreements conditional on a satisfactory deal on tuition fees.
