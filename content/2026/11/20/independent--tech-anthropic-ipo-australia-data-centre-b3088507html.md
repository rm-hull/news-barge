More than 99 per cent of public submissions have opposed plans for a A$32 billion (£16.2 billion) data centre in regional Australia with Anthropic as its anchor tenant, a filing for the project showed.

Subject to approval, the Western Downs Digital Park will be built by Singaporean developer Zerra DC in Kogan, about 250km (155 miles) west of Brisbane, which had a population of 355 in the last census.

Of nearly 600 public submissions on the plans, just two expressed support for the project, one was neutral, and the remaining 595 opposed it or asked for a delay until developers cleared certain environmental hurdles, according to the filing.

"A significant number of submissions raise reasonable matters regarding water, power, noise, roads, housing and the scale of the project," the filing, lodged on behalf of developer Zerra DC, said.

A Zerra spokesperson said the project took the submissions seriously, which "reinforces that people want a greater say in how the project develops".

![Anthropic will be the anchor tenant of a data centre planned in regional Australia](https://static.the-independent.com/2026/11/20/9/44/Anthropic.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The developer was expanding its community engagement and planned to report changes demanded by the public.

Anthropic declined to comment.

The initial response, though far from binding, gives a sense of the souring relationship between communities and the so-called AI factories being built at pace around the world in a boom McKinsey has valued at $7 trillion by 2030.

Already in Australia, Goodman Group scrapped a roughly A$1.2 billion data centre planned for Sydney in September after a public backlash, while NextDC delayed a project in Melbourne amid resident concerns about energy use, triggering a share sell-off.

![Anthropic declined to comment](https://static.the-independent.com/2026/11/18/22/2026-11-18T220921Z_121400764_RC2MQOAD3QB0_RTRMADP_3_ANTHROPIC-IPO.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The Anthropic project is on farmland and further from the suburbs, but with a planned computing load of 1.44 gigawatts, it would be one of the country's biggest.

About one-third of the submissions were from people who lived within 200 km of the site, the filing said.

Objectors mostly cited concerns about water use, electricity demand and prices, noise, road damage from construction traffic and pressure on housing.

Before the data centre project named Anthropic as its tenant on September 15, it had received five objections in a month, a log of public comments showed, with the rest arriving between then and November 11, when the local government stopped accepting submissions.

The filing about Australian data centre submissions was published the same week Anthropic made its stock market debut on Wall St, pricing its initial public offering on November 18 in a deal that valued the company at about $1.5 trillion.
