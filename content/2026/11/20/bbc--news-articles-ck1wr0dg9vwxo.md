![The headline on the front page of the Daily Mail reads: The bravest woman in Britain](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/41e0/live/7f7cc0d0-e468-11f1-99b6-a35a62fa4264.jpg)

Joanne Young is pictured on a number of Friday's front pages. On Thursday, four men were found guilty of sexually abusing Young after her husband drugged and advertised her for sex online. The Daily Mail calls her "the British Pelicot" and "the bravest woman in Britain" after she waived her right to anonymity to "condemn the 'horrific betrayal'". Her ex-husband - 50-year-old former Conservative councillor Philip Young - has already pleaded guilty to a number of rape charges, and another man pleaded guilty to rape and sexual assault at a previous hearing.

![The headline on the front page of the Daily Mirror reads: You are not alone](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/2d1a/live/aac44cd0-e464-11f1-99b6-a35a62fa4264.jpg)

"You are not alone" is the Daily Mirror's front page headline - words spoken by Young as she appealed to other survivors outside court after the verdicts were delivered. "Joanne Young faced the whole world as she described the impact 13 years of rape and abuse had on her life," the paper writes. It also pictures US President Donald Trump and Russia's Vladimir Putin, who held trilateral talks with China's Xi Jinping on Thursday, and describes their relationship as a "bromance".

![The headline on the front page of the Daily Express reads: Brave beyond all measure](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/8fc0/live/eb2132c0-e464-11f1-99b6-a35a62fa4264.jpg)

"Bravery beyond all measure" is how the Daily Express describes Young, whom it reports has "called for a change to the law on consent". Separately, the paper reports that Reform UK leader Nigel Farage says "our PM does not believe in Britain like me". Andy Burnham will attend a UK-EU summit in Brussels on Friday, where he has said a "win-win" deal can be struck with the bloc.

![The headline on the front page of the Guardian reads: Summit deal close as UK agrees to let in 100,000 young EU citizens](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/32d6/live/3db5f530-e469-11f1-99b6-a35a62fa4264.png)

Negotiators have "provisionally agreed to allow 100,000 EU citizens to come to the Britain every year under a new mobility scheme" ahead of the summit, according to the Guardian. The paper describes the number as a "concession" from the UK side, who are said to have previously sought a lower cap of 50,000-80,000. Downing Street has not commented on the details, which would also allow young Britons to live in Europe. Also on its front page are Joanne Young's remarks, and the International Court of Justice (ICJ) ruling that Myanmar was guilty of committing genocide against its Rohingya minority.

![The headline on the front page of the Times reads: Bravery in face of betrayal](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/5698/live/8e9d0470-e469-11f1-a094-5fdc692f3e33.jpg)

Young has said her case raises "serious questions" about how the law deals with consent, writes the Times, "after the defendants claimed they had been 'duped' by Young into believing his wife was a willing participant". The Times also covers reaction from European leaders to the historic talks between Trump, Xi and Putin in Shenzen on Thursday - "dismissing it as a 'photo-op'".

![The headline on the front page of the Daily Telegraph reads: Trump tells Europe: Stop funding war in Ukraine](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/2dfd/live/09e28d50-e468-11f1-a094-5fdc692f3e33.jpg)

Trump told European leaders to withhold funding for Ukraine in a "phone call 'tirade'" after the talks, the Daily Telegraph reports. It also writes that the Speaker of the House of Lords has warned peers that the "soaring cost of refurbishing Parliament risks drawing cash away from priorities like defence", and that pubs "could be allowed to serve pints before 06:00 under plans to relax drinking laws for the Rugby World Cup", which will be held in Australia in 2027.

![The headline on the front page of the Financial Times reads: Anthropic surges on Wall St debut after raising $81bn in giant listing](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/bdb2/live/ccbe6b90-e464-11f1-a094-5fdc692f3e33.jpg)

"Anthropic surges on Wall St debut" is the Financial Times' Friday headline, after the AI firm became one of the most valuable public companies in the world as it began trading on the stock market on Thursday. The "blockbuster listing caps a stunning ascent" for Anthropic, the paper reports. In other news, the FT covers Trump, Xi and Putin's meeting - where it writes nuclear weapons were the main talking point - and reports that the UK Treasury is resisting Alan Milburn's proposal to tackle youth unemployment by cutting employer National Insurance costs. The department declined to comment but pointed to Burnham's previous remarks that tax decisions would wait until next year's Budget.

![The headline on the front page of the Metro reads: Metro genius earns £15bn (in just one day)](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/3bcf/live/dcca6430-e464-11f1-99b6-a35a62fa4264.jpg)

Anthropic's "stock market bonanza" hits close to home for the Metro, which dedicates its front page to co-founder Jack Clark - a former journalist who "worked weekends as a sub-editor on our website". "Clark says much of his success is due to his career in news," the Metro reports.

![The headline on the front page of the Daily Star reads: Andrew dossier sent to police](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/e1a8/live/410033a0-e468-11f1-a094-5fdc692f3e33.jpg)

"Andrew dossier sent to police" is the Daily Star's headline, as it reports 80 pages of files "said to concern misconduct in public office" have been submitted "amid calls for a new probe" into Andrew Mountbatten-Windsor. The evidence has been handed over by the campaign group Republic, which said details had not been made public following legal advice. The former Prince Andrew was arrested in February on suspicion of misconduct in public office, in the wake of information that emerged from the Epstein files in the US. Mountbatten-Windsor has consistently denied any wrongdoing linked to his association with Epstein.

![The headline on the front page of the Sun reads: Grandular fever](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/99d2/live/df0eb210-e468-11f1-a094-5fdc692f3e33.jpg)

The Sun meanwhile splashes on Grand Theft Auto VI's UK release. "Grandular fever" is its headline, as it reports that a "sickie frenzy" saw 1m gamers skip school or work to play the highly anticipated game. "Some schools offered pupils rewards just for coming in," according to the Sun.

![The headline on the front page of the i Paper reads: Alzheimer's test that gives diagnosis in days will be available next year in the UK](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/4435/live/bc04cdd0-e464-11f1-a094-5fdc692f3e33.jpg)

The i Paper reports that an alzheimer's test that "gives diagnosis in days" will be made available in the UK next year. The paper reports that this could cut diagnosis time "from years to days after first visit to a memory clinic".
