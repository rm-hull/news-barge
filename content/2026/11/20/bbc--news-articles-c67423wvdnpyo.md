**A man has been jailed for life with a minimum of 22 years and six months after he murdered a woman, who was found drowned in the River Thames, while serving a suspended prison sentence.**

The body of Jennifer Symonds, 44, was recovered from the river at Phyllis Court Club in Henley-on-Thames on the morning of 6 March 2026.

Last month, Nelio Gouveia, 45, of Phyllis Court Drive, Henley-on-Thames, pleaded guilty to her murder during an appearance at Oxford Crown Court.

During a hearing at the same court on Friday, Gouveia was handed the mandatory life sentence for murder and ordered to serve at least 22 years and six months behind bars.

It can now also be reported that Gouveia had been serving a two-year suspended prison sentence for a violent attack on a woman at the time of the murder.

Jailing him on Friday, Judge Jay said Gouveia's actions "stemmed from your propensity in certain circumstances to violence against women".

Reacting to the sentence, Gouveia, who appeared in the dock wearing a grey tracksuit and clutching white rosary beads, looked down at the floor.

His defence had earlier told the hearing that "he doesn't feel he is worthy of forgiveness and is deeply remorseful of what he has done".

Friday's hearing was told that Gouveia and Symonds had likely met on Red Lion Lawn in Henley and had been exchanging infrequent text messages.

On 5 March, the pair agreed to meet up at the theatre in the town, before spending the night together.

Gouveia then left Symonds at a pavilion by the water at Phyllis Court in the early hours, before he returned with a metal weight lifting dumbbell and attacked her.

CCTV of the attack was not played in court, with the judge describing it as "deeply disturbing and upsetting".

Thames Valley Police launched a murder investigation after being called to Phyllis Court Drive at about 07:50 GMT on 6 March.

Gouveia was arrested the same day, and charged on 8 March. A post-mortem examination showed Symonds had died from drowning.

Portugal-born Gouveia worked and lived as a kitchen porter at the Phyllis Court private members' club on the banks of the River Thames.

The court heard a fellow employee at the members' club had reported that Gouveia said: "If girls are annoying and aren't behaving, you just hit them."

The 45-year-old was sentenced in 2024 to a two year suspended prison sentence for assault occasioning actual bodily harm after he punched a woman in the face in 2022.

Gouveia also poured red wine over the victim, before hitting her over the head with a bottle.

"I genuinely believed that he was going to kill me or cause me some serious harm," the victim said afterwards.

Symonds was born in Zambia and moved to the UK aged three. She had worked for Microsoft until being made redundant in 2025.

Before her death, the court was told she had been seeking to rejoin the army cadets as a volunteer.

At Friday's hearing, Symonds' older brother Andrew emotionally described her as "vivacious" and "outgoing" with a "quiet sense of rebellion wrapped in imagination and warmth".

In a statement read out by the prosecution, her parents said she had been "loving kind, generous and caring" and "touched so many lives in a really positive way".

"She was an integral part of our lives. The grief and trauma is devastating," they added.

The court was also told that Symonds had pursued many outdoor activities and enjoyed being out in nature and by the river in Henley.

Her friend, Gary Hamilton, told the BBC ahead of the hearing that she loved spending time on his boat, and would "try any new adventure".

"Her laugh was very infectious", he said. "She was really, very, very bubbly."

"She'd always be looking out for people. So if anybody was wanting a conversation or down or anything, I know she'd go to them."

"The whole community has been mourning for her."

Symonds' friend Cameron Finlay-Cooper, 32, described the moment he found out about her death as a "sucker punch".

"It's just abhorrently awful, there's no other word to describe it. It really is just a wound that a lot of people are trying to recover from," he told the BBC.

"She really was loved by everyone in this town," he said, adding that his friend would be remembered for being a "really incredible person".

Jonathan Barter wants to install a memorial to his friend near to her favourite spot along the River Thames.

"It just occurred to me that perhaps the town council would consider allowing us to put a plaque in her memory."

Suggesting the Red Lion Lawn in Henley as its location, he said: "She lived just up the road here.. and I think was really her quiet place."

"I thought we mustn't let Jen be forgotten."
