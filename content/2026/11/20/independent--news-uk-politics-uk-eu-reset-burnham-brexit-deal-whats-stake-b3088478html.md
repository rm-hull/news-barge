Andy Burnham hopes his crunch summit in Brussels will undo some of the harms of Brexit and herald the start of a new UK-EU relationship in more ways than one.

The prime minister will meet EU leaders on Friday having slammed the current arrangements – negotiated by the Conservatives when they were in power – as not “good enough for Britain”.

He has said the planned new agreement, expected to include deals on youth mobility as well as food and emissions trading, could “bring growth to the British economy”.

Mr Burnham has said he wants to start a conversation on the UK-EU relationship after the summit, that could even include trying to rejoin the bloc. But he needs Friday’s meeting to be a success first.

Here, we look at what is contained in a leaked draft of the deal to be agreed – and assess the stakes for the PM and his hopes of much closer ties to the UK in the future.

## Youth mobility

The draft agreement, seen by *The Guardian*, says it will “allow people from the EU and the UK to reside for up to three years to engage in activities such as work, studies, au pairing, volunteering, or simply travelling”.

However, under 30s may have to wait until 2030, because work permits are actually under the control of the 27 national governments that make up the EU, not Brussels.

How many will benefit is also still to be decided. The UK wants a cap of between 50,000 and 80,000 places, amid fears of claims of betrayal and a return to freedom of movement from Brexiteers, while the EU wants at least 100,000.

But some EU states, including Germany, are happy to allow unlimited numbers of British young people to work, live or study in their countries, even if the UK does not reciprocate.

## Cheaper university fees for EU students

This is still a bone of contention. There are two options in the draft text – “significantly reduced undergraduate” or “equal treatment” with UK nationals on tuition fees.

A draft seen by *The Telegraph* says: “The EU and UK will therefore work towards a supplementing agreement to the Trade and Cooperation Agreement providing for [significantly reduced undergraduate/equal treatment with nationals as regards] tuition fees for EU students in the UK and for UK students in the EU.”

## Food and drink exports – but not in time for next Christmas

The proposed agreement includes removing border checks on food and drink imports in exchange for the UK aligning with EU sanitary and phytosanitary regulations.

It should mean exports of fresh sausages, burgers, shellfish from domestic waters and seed potatoes to the EU can resume.

However, the draft agreement says it will not be implemented until 1 March 2028, in part because the UK needs to get legislation through parliament to dynamically align with EU standards.

## Travelling musicians and artists

Touring artists do feature in the draft text. This would be seen as a win for the UK, as the EU has long resisted reforming the system, which also depends on work permits.

Although no definitive agreement has been reached, it has been agreed that “the EU and the UK should explore further mutually beneficial solutions” for “touring artists”.

## Energy

The UK wants to join the EU’s electricity market by 2027, which will mean taxpayers will have to help fund projects to build roads, airports and other infrastructure across poorer EU states, according to the *Telegraph*.

## Goodwill and trust – with British voters and the EU

The PM hopes this agreement will be just a launchpad for his plans for much closer ties with the EU, including potentially going “all the way” and rejoining the bloc.

But he will have to land a number of the agreement's more sticky elements carefully, including the youth mobility scheme, which opponents already claim is free movement by the backdoor, and the elements of the deal which require food and drink exporters to align with EU rules.

He will also have to ensure he leaves with the trust and goodwill of EU partners, which will be vital for any new arrangements going forward.

The potential prize, however, is huge. Mr Burnham has not ruled out that the next Labour manifesto could include a pledge to rejoin the EU – a move that could write him into the history books.
