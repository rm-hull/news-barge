Spanish police used excessive force against protesters and journalists during a nationwide general strike over the country’s housing crisis, Amnesty International has said, reporting 36 injuries and calling for an investigation into officers’ conduct.

The human rights organisation said officers deployed pepper spray, batons, tear gas and rubber bullets against demonstrators during last week’s 24-hour stoppage. Five people were taken to hospital.

Amnesty said it had documented incidents in Barcelona, Malaga, Valencia, Alicante and Granada, as well as Madrid, where it reported that officers charged at demonstrators without warning.

The group also raised concerns about the treatment of journalists covering the protests, saying reporters had been pushed and struck with batons while documenting police activity.

Its findings were based on interviews with 24 people. Amnesty called on Spanish authorities to carry out an “in-depth review of the protocols and rules that regulate police action at demonstrations”.

Asked for comment by Reuters, Spain’s Interior Ministry pointed to an announcement made the day after the strike that the use of pepper spray during demonstrations would be curbed.

Madrid police authorities opened an internal investigation on Thursday to assess officers’ actions in the capital.

## **Spain's first general strike in 14 years**

The strike, held on Wednesday 11 November, was Spain’s first nationwide general stoppage in 14 years. It was initially called by tenants’ associations before receiving the backing of the country’s two largest trade unions, UGT and CCOO, alongside other workers’ organisations.

Millions of people were reported to have taken part, with picket lines set up at factories, ports, markets and transport hubs. The Confederation of Tenants’ Association said around four million people had participated by midday, while unions reported high turnout in several industries.

Barcelona experienced significant disruption, with roadblocks preventing access to some workplaces and major routes. Public transport was also heavily affected. In Madrid, thousands of demonstrators gathered along the city’s main boulevard, while clashes between police and pickets were reported near the capital’s wholesale market.

![The demonstrations reflected growing anger over the cost of housing in Spain](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/20/13/2304798608..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Spanish media reported that at least 25 people were injured and more than 50 detained during incidents across the country. The Interior Ministry said police and the Civil Guard had recorded 919 incidents by 5pm local time.

## **Housing crisis behind nationwide protests**

The demonstrations reflected growing anger over the cost of housing in Spain, where rising rents and property prices have made it increasingly difficult for people to find affordable places to live.

The Bank of Spain has estimated the country's housing shortage at around 750,000 homes, with the deficit expected to exceed one million by 2028.

The rental market has come under particular pressure as fewer people can afford to buy property. Campaigners have also criticised the use of homes as tourist accommodation, short-term rentals and investment assets.

Housing campaigners have called for tougher action against property speculation, restrictions on tourist lets and stronger protections for tenants.
