## York 19-year-old dies after being struck by vehicles on A64 - family seeks answers

Flowers laid in memory of Cashel Mulgrew, 19, from Stamford Bridge, York, who died on the A64 near Grimston Bar were relocated due to safety concerns.

A grieving family is seeking answers following the death of a much-loved young man who was fatally struck by two vehicles on the A64 near Grimston Bar in York.

Cashel Craig Edward Mulgrew stepped over a barrier onto the eastbound carriageway and into the path of traffic on the evening of March 21, 2026, an inquest at Northallerton Magistrates’ Court was told.

The 19-year-old was struck by the side of a Volvo car travelling at about 65mph. He went back over the barrier before going onto the carriageway again and, following several near-misses, was hit by a Ford Transit van travelling at 70mph as he lay in the road.

Mr Mulgrew, of Stamford Bridge, was pronounced dead at the scene at 10.05pm.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21739612.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Police at the scene on the A64 between Grimston Bar and the Hopgrove roundabout which was closed on the evening of March 21, 2026 (Image Supplied)

Senior coroner for York and North Yorkshire Jon Heath said a post-mortem examination found he died from a traumatic brain injury caused by vehicle impact.

Toxicology tests found 476mg of alcohol per 100ml of blood, about six times the legal drink-drive limit, as well as cocaine and its breakdown products.

Mr Heath said the combined effects would have caused "severe intoxication significantly impairing cognitive function and judgement".

Witnesses described seeing Mr Mulgrew go onto the road, with one saying a figure "leapt over the barrier". A driver recalled "a man running in the carriageway" while another motorist said: "It was so quick. If I hadn't swerved I am sure I would have collided with him."

The inquest heard Mr Mulgrew had previously struggled with his mental health and attempted to take his own life in 2023, but later said he regretted it and felt he had put it behind him. His GP said there had been "no immediate concerns".

His stepfather Ben Barber said he left home at about 6.30pm, carrying a heavy-looking rucksack which Mr Mulgrew said contained his boxing gloves and boots.

Mr Barber said he didn't "look or smell any different to when he usually goes out".

He later used a phone locator app, and saw he was on Hull Road near B&Q. A later check located him in an area of waste ground next to the A64 at Murton which was "somewhere that he might go".

Police forensic investigator Mark Jakeman said both the Volvo and Ford van were in good working order and conditions that night were dry and clear.

That stretch of the A64 was dark and unlit with no pedestrian access, meaning drivers would not have expected somebody in the road.

He concluded both collisions were "unavoidable in the circumstances".

The Volvo driver would have had no prior warning to be able to avoid him, while Mr Mulgrew was 'laid in the road' and only came into the view of the van headlights a second before impact, he said.

Mr Mulgrew's uncle, Eddie Mulgrew, said: “My heart goes out to the driver, it really does.”

But he questioned whether the van collision was "completely unavoidable" as the accident report stated.

He raised concerns that the van was travelling at 70mph when its legal speed limit was 60mph, arguing the driver might have had more time to react at a lower speed.

"My nephew was still alive when he was struck by that vehicle. We are talking a hair's length and my nephew would have still been alive."

Mr Jakeman said any additional reaction time would have been "fractional".

Mr Mulgrew also raised questions about a phone conversation involving the van occupants, which he said would've been a distraction. He asked why the van driver's witness statement was taken 51 days later, what his rest period had been before starting two night shifts, and noted the low tyre pressure of 21PSI recorded following the collision.

He said it was "a tragedy that within three hours and 12 minutes of appearing, to people who saw him, stone cold sober, and enjoying life" to being "inebriated to mind-altering substances" when "there's no way sensible decisions were being made".

The family had asked for his nephew's phone to be analysed to help "seek the truth in his memory" and explain the period before his death, he said.

Mr Heath adjourned the inquest to seek a response from the senior investigating officer on several issues raised by the family.

Offering his condolences, he said: "Cashel was clearly much loved by the family".

Mr Barber said: "Not a day goes by when we don't think about him. We will miss him for the rest of our lives."
