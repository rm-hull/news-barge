**Euronews Persian**

## More than 14 tankers have been assigned crews and Iranian flags at the terminal that handles up to 90% of the country’s crude exports, according to satellite imagery and ship-tracking data, with a convoy expected to head toward the Sea of Oman in the coming days.

Iran has positioned more than 14 oil tankers at Kharg Island in a convoy poised to attempt to break the US naval blockade of its oil exports, satellite imagery and ship-tracking data show.

Crews have been assigned to the tankers, Iranian flags have been raised over them, and Tehran plans to send them in convoy toward the Sea of Oman exit in the coming days, the reports said.

Ship-tracking firm TankerTrackers said available data confirmed Iran was resuming oil exports.

International media outlets reported on Thursday that an Iranian crude cargo had already been transferred between two tankers in a ship-to-ship operation in the Persian Gulf as the blockade tightened.

Satellite imagery and shipping data showed the transfers took place near Kharg Island with automatic identification systems (AIS) switched off to conceal the oil's origin. Washington had previously warned it would target vessels involved in such operations.

Israel's Channel 14 and Sabereen News, an outlet close to Iran-backed armed groups, reported that dozens of Iranian tankers were preparing to head towards China and were moving closer to the blockade lines.

A journalist with ties to Iran's IRGC threatened publicly that any attack on the tankers would lead to strikes on oil facilities across the Gulf.

Mohammad Enayati Najafabadi, in a post on X resharing a piece by Iran's state-affiliated Fars news agency, warned that disrupting an Iranian tanker's route "will lead to explosions at the oil facilities of the United Arab Emirates, Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Kuwait, Oman and Bahrain," which he described as a personal analysis.

The Fars piece used an image from the Shahnameh, the Persian epic, depicting the mythical hero Fereydoun defending Iranian ships at sea — a reference its authors explicitly drew to the US blockade.

## Kharg Island remains crucial

Kharg Island, in the northern Persian Gulf, accounts for around 85% to 90% of Iran's crude oil exports.

Its terminal can store around 30 million barrels and has waters deep enough to berth supertankers. The halt to loading operations since the naval blockade was imposed has placed mounting financial pressure on Tehran.

Alternative terminals including the port of Jask lack the infrastructure to replace it.

Tehran's moves come as Trump, during a recent visit to China where he met Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin, signalled no urgency over Iran and indicated the issue would not be addressed until next year.

Iranian authorities appear to be using that window to restore oil exports to Asian markets and ease pressure on state finances.

US Energy Secretary Chris Wright, speaking on Fox News on Thursday, said energy flows through the Strait of Hormuz were increasing despite the tensions.

Asked why petrol prices in the US had fallen even as the Iran standoff appeared unresolved, Wright said conditions had in fact changed but were not yet visible to the public.

The US had been moving more oil through the Strait of Hormuz and exporting more refined products — petrol, diesel and jet fuel — for several weeks, he said, claiming supply had actually risen.
