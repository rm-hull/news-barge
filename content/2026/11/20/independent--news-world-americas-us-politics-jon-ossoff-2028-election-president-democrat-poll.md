There is a new favorite on the Democratic side for 2028 - months before campaigning to be the next president begins.

Georgia Senator Jon Ossoff has emerged as the early frontrunner among potential Democratic candidates for the 2028 presidential election, following a decisive midterm re-election victory and a new national survey showing him ahead of several prominent party figures.

At 39, Ossoff is the youngest member of the U.S. Senate. Political observers view his age as a potential asset for the Democratic Party following two consecutive presidential cycles heavily focused on the age and health of Donald Trump and Joe Biden, as reported by *The Daily Beast*.

Ossoff recently secured another Senate term in Georgia — a state Trump won in 2024 — by defeating Republican challenger Mike Collins. Throughout that campaign, Ossoff frequently criticized Trump and his allies over rising inflation, the war in Iran and allegations of corruption, drawing frequent comparisons to former President Barack Obama for his speaking style.

Despite his current polling position, Ossoff has stated that he does not intend to run for the White House in 2028, though political commentators have noted that Obama made similar denials before launching his 2008 campaign.

![Georgia Senator Jon Ossoff leads a new national poll of potential 2028 Democratic presidential contenders, following his midterm re-election victory](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/04/03/2026-11-04T032625Z_115780188_RC2QGOA46PWQ_RTRMADP_3_USA-ELECTION-GEORGIA.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Few officials candidates for 2028 have announced their intentions, but several prominent names are being used for both sides of the political aisle.

For Democrats, Representative Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez, California Governor Gavin Newsom and former Transportation Secretary Pete Buttigieg have all said they will make decisions soon, while former Vice President Kamala Harris has indicated she is considering another bid.

Data from the Emerson College national poll illustrates a shifting landscape among Democratic voters as the party prepares to take control of both chambers of Congress after the midterms. When respondents were asked without prompts whom they would like to see as the 2028 Democratic nominee, 16 percent named Ossoff. Ocasio-Cortez placed second at 12 percent, followed by Newsom at 8 percent, while Harris and Buttigieg tied at 6 percent.

When presented with a list of specific candidates, Ossoff maintained a narrower lead with 17 percent support. Harris followed closely at 16 percent, Ocasio-Cortez received 15 percent and Buttigieg garnered 14 percent.

![Despite topping both open-ended and prompted surveys for the White House race, the Georgia Democrat says he does not plan to run, even as support drops for established party figures](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/04/09/2302767518..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

While support for Ossoff and Ocasio-Cortez has grown, polling numbers for other established figures have declined, with Newsom’s support dropping by 16 percentage points over the last year and Harris’s backing falling by 31 percentage points since her 2024 presidential election loss to Trump.

Following their midterm gains, congressional Democrats are preparing to launch oversight investigations into Trump and his administration regarding the Iran conflict and alleged corruption as they look ahead to the next presidential race.

On the Republican side, the 2028 field also remains unsettled. Although Vice President JD Vance has widely been viewed as the natural successor to Trump, the same Emerson College open-ended survey found Secretary of State Marco Rubio leading Vance among respondents, 36 percent to 20 percent.
