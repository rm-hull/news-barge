Fiona, the hippo who won hearts across the globe after surviving a premature birth, has died at the Cincinnati Zoo at the age of nine after swallowing a hat belonging to a visitor.

Her death comes a decade after the same zoo fatally shot Harambe, an endangered gorilla, after a little boy fell into his exhibit in 2016.

That decision drew months of international backlash, which cast a shadow over the Cincinnati Zoo.

That outrage faded with the premature birth of an adorable hippo named Fiona, this time bringing worldwide positive attention to the reeling zoo.

The zoo shared her miraculous survival in videos and blog posts. They showed her taking a bottle, splashing in a pool and learning to run. She helped restore the zoo’s image.

![Fiona the Hippo in Hippo Cove at the Cincinnati Zoo](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/20/06/2026-11-19T221650Z_879513361_RC2AROAQZILZ_RTRMADP_3_USA-FIONA.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

But a decade later, the zoo has found itself in a similarly tragic situation. This time, with Fiona, who died Thursday after eating a visitor’s hat.

“Despite multiple attempts to move the hat out of her system, it was ultimately too much to overcome,” the zoo said.

Mourning for Fiona spread quickly, and comparisons between the two beloved animals followed nearly as fast.

Both captured the world's attention, and both died in incidents involving visitors.

AI images of the 17-year-old great ape ushering nine-year-old Fiona across the rainbow bridge, or the pair surrounded by clouds next to the gates of heaven, were plastered across social media.

Some posters argued the two deaths show the zoo needs to do better at keeping animals safe.

![A tribute outside the Gorilla World exhibit at Cincinnati Zoo in 2016](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/20/05/Fiona_the_Hippo_Harambe_91219.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Others called for compassion for the facility.

“As devastated as we are, we cannot begin to fathom the grief that Fiona’s keepers, veterinary team, and the entire Cincinnati Zoo family are experiencing today,” Dino Buddies, a local wildlife outreach company, said in a statement.

“We hope that everyone extends these incredible individuals the compassion, kindness, and grace they deserve as they mourn the loss of an animal they loved so deeply.”

Michelle Curley, a zoo spokesperson, said the hat landed in her enclosure by accident. It hit a ceiling fan and then got deflected through a small opening above the area’s viewing windows.

But that hasn't quelled the despair.

“The two saddest days in all my time here in Cincinnati come from the Zoo,” Marianne King posted on Facebook.

The first, she explained, was Harambe's death and the second was Fiona.

![After Harambe was killed, the zoo reopened its gorilla exhibit with a higher, reinforced barrier](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/05/28/9/19/Screenshot-2026-05-28-at-10-22-10.png?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Joanne Prater, who lives in the Cincinnati suburb of Loveland, was attending a high school fundraiser Thursday evening and said every person she spoke with talked about how sad they were about Fiona's death and compared the situation to Harambe.

“They are tokens of Cincinnati. You can’t say Fiona or Harambe without everybody knowing who that is here in Cincinnati," she said. "I guess both of their deaths could have been prevented, but unfortunately accidents do happen.”

After Harambe was killed, the zoo reopened its gorilla exhibit with a higher, reinforced barrier.

Meanwhile, tongue-in-cheek online petitions sought to add his face to Mount Rushmore or the Lincoln Memorial, and to put him on the dollar bill. There was also a more somber “Justice for Harambe” petition seeking to hold the boy’s mother responsible for his death. The county prosecutor determined there was no cause for charges.
