**A driver who was using his mobile phone shortly before crashing into a stationary car on the A1(M) has been jailed for nine years. The collision, near junction 57 at Darlington, resulted in the death of a woman in her 80s.**

Joshua Stephen Jones, 30, was driving a Volkswagen Tiguan northbound on the A1(M) shortly after 8.30pm on Christmas Day 2024.

He collided with the rear of a Ford Fiesta carrying a married couple in their 80s, who were travelling home after spending Christmas with their family.

Earlier, a separate collision further north on the motorway had brought traffic to a standstill across all lanes. The couple, along with other motorists, had managed to stop safely after seeing hazard lights, temporary speed limits and blue lights ahead.

Jones was approaching the stationary traffic minutes later. He had his cruise control set at 75mph but did not brake, slow down or appear to react to the vehicles ahead before striking the Fiesta.

The female passenger in the Fiesta died as a result of the collision. Her husband, who was driving, suffered numerous serious injuries, requiring hospital treatment and a lengthy recovery.

North Yorkshire Police officers attended the scene.

## Mobile phone use before the collision

An examination of Jones’ mobile phone showed that he had been using the device while driving. It had been unlocked five times in the ten minutes before the collision, with applications used including Instagram, WhatsApp and Signal.

In the minute before the crash, multiple key presses were recorded on the phone keyboard. The Signal app contained a partly typed, unsent message saying Merry Christmas.

The North Yorkshire Police Forensic Collision Investigation Unit concluded that the collision was most likely caused by Jones being distracted by his phone.

Jones, of Catterick Garrison, was charged with causing death by dangerous driving and causing serious injury by dangerous driving. He pleaded guilty at court on 24 September 2026.

At Teesside Crown Court on 18 November 2026, he was sentenced to nine years in prison and disqualified from driving for ten years.

## Family statement

The family of the victims described the devastating impact of the collision. In a personal statement, the daughter of the woman who died said:

> **Daughter of the woman who died:** Our hearts are totally broken. We have been subjected to a living nightmare that we will never wake up from. And all because of one person’s selfish and reckless actions.
>
> We have lost a loving Wife, Mam, Grandma and Great Grandma, who was the centre of our world, as we were hers.
>
> Our entire lives and Christmas-times will never be the same.

Following the sentencing, investigating officer DC Stephanie Palmer, of North Yorkshire Police, said:

> **DC Stephanie Palmer, North Yorkshire Police:** This tragic case serves as a stark reminder of the potentially devastating consequences of using a phone while driving.
>
> Even a momentary distraction can have life-changing results, reducing a driver’s ability to react to hazards, and putting everyone on the road at risk.
>
> No phone call, text, or notification is worth risking a life for. We urge drivers to remain focused on the road at all times, and make safety their top priority.
