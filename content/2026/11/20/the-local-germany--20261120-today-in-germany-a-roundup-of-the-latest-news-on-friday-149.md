**Friday's top story: Gen Z protests set for 73 cities in Germany**

Three weeks after the first major youth protests, thousands of young people in Germany are set to demonstrate again today in many German cities.

The new group "Gen Z rules" (*Gen Z Regelt*) has called for nationwide protests again – with demonstrations organised in 73 cities this time around.

Among the main demands are better education, an affordable life and climate protection.

Organisers from *Gen Z Regelt*had previously issued a statement aimed specifically at German Chancellor Friedrich Merz: "We've had enough. Your austerity policy robs us of all hope."

Other organisers, including the Federal Student Conference, have arranged a separate rally in Berlin.

Ahead of demonstrations on Friday, Federal Youth Minister Karin Prien promised an open ear for the legitimate interests of young people.

"It is important to me not only to acknowledge the concerns of young people, but to involve them in political decisions," said the CDU politician to the German Press Agency.

Prien's tone was notably different from others in her party, such as Interior Minister Alexander Dobrindt, who had previously warned against violence even before the last protests, and then quickly blamed "violent left-wing groups" and "troublemakers from the immigrant community" for incidents of property damage in Frankfurt.

**Man steals car with 10-year-old girl in back seat in Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania**

A man stole a car with a 10-year-old girl inside in Zarrentin am Schaalsee earlier this week. The child was unharmed, police in Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania said.

A 38-year-old woman had parked outside a doctor's office and left her daughter in the back seat. She planned to be inside only briefly, so she left the engine running. An unknown man seized the chance and drove off.

He stopped after about 150 metres, apparently when he noticed the girl, and got out. The child also climbed out of the car and watched from a distance as the man got back in and drove away.

Meanwhile, her mother had realised that the car was gone and called the police. While she was still on the emergency line, her daughter walked back to her. The car has not been found.

Rostock police are appealing for witnesses who saw the incident or know where the car is.

**Germany's metalworkers' union launches 24-hour warning strikes**

For the first time in eight years, the IG Metall union has resorted to 24-hour warning strikes.

On Friday, businesses across Germany are largely at a standstill as a result. These include shipyards in the north, suppliers in Baden-Württemberg, and the BMW and Audi car factories in Bavaria.

About 3.7 million employees fall under the collective agreement in the metal and electrical industry, with thousands of workers across the metal and electrical industry called to strike.

Further strikes could be expected next week.

During 24-hour strikes, union members can receive pay directly from IG Metall for striking.

The union is demanding pay increases for its members and workers who belong to the industry's collective bargaining agreement. This comes as big players in the industry are set to lay off tens of thousands of workers in the coming years.

Bayern Munich coach Vincent Kompany has pledged to "look forward" after a month away with meningeal irritation.

Speaking on Thursday with his arm in a sling, in his first official media appearance since returning, the 40-year-old said he had made a full recovery: "I feel very good and am happy to be back with the team.”

Kompany collapsed on the training pitch two days after Bayern's Champions League match against Arsenal on October 21st. He was hospitalised with meningeal irritation caused by a viral infection and also dislocated his shoulder.

He will be back in the dugout on Friday, when Bayern host strugglers Cologne. German media have speculated that his long working hours played a part. Club powerbroker Uli Hoeness said Kompany "works 12 hours a day, six days a week – it's simply too much".

Kompany declined to address the speculation but admitted: "I simply carried on when I was at a point when I should have taken some time to rest."

Bayern top the Bundesliga on goal difference ahead of Borussia Dortmund and are the only side in Europe's top five leagues unbeaten in all competitions.

Many train stations will become "read-aloud stations" for the day. In Potsdam, for example, Tram Line 96 will become a moving reading room. The "Reading Aloud Express" will leave Platz der Einheit/West at 11.07am and runs until 2.04pm between Marie-Juchacz-Straße and Campus Jungfernsee.

Readers on the “Reading Aloud Express” include Babelsberg 03 captain Laurin von Piechowski, "Hasenscheisse" singer Christian Näthe and Deputy Mayor Torsten Wiegel.

Libraries are also a good place to catch some well-known figures reading aloud. In Mönchengladbach, for example, the City Library will host football team Borussia’s mascot, Jünter, for a reading. Doors open at 4pm.
