Danish police have thwarted an attempt by Russian intelligence services to assassinate an American-Ukrainian rapper in September ahead of a planned performance, police and security officials announced.

Foreign minister Lars Lokke Rasmussen called the alleged plot to kill rapper Kyivstoner an unacceptable transgression, as Denmark said it was expelling Russia's defence attache in the country in response.

"We don't want to break off diplomatic relations with Russia in this particular situation that Denmark and Europe are in... But on the other hand, we want to send a very, very strong signal," Mr Rasmussen said.

The Russian embassy in Copenhagen did not immediately reply to ⁠a request for comment.

Moscow has regularly denied accusations of stepping up hybrid atacks – incidents ​of aggression ⁠that fall short of acts of ‌war – in the region following its full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022.

Police thwarted the attempt when they arrested three men with links to ‌the Danish criminal underworld shortly before the rapper Kyivstoner was ‌due to give a concert north of Copenhagen on September 25, police and security officials told a press conference.

"It is our clear assessment that the assassination attempt was planned by the Russian intelligence services," Rasmus Kieffer-Kristensen, head of Denmark's national security and intelligence service, PET, said.

The police and security services made their announcements as two Danish men appeared in court near Copenhagen on Friday, charged under Section 108 of Denmark's Criminal Code, which covers assisting a foreign intelligence service, as well as attempted murder, court documents showed.

Both men denied wrongdoing, broadcaster DR reported. In the court documents, prosecutors said the men were driving towards the venue with two loaded pistols and two knives when police arrested them at ‌around 9.55 p.m., five minutes before the concert was due to begin.

One of the suspects had bought a ticket to the concert, according to the charges. The concert was cancelled.

"A Russian ⁠assassination attempt is not something we've seen in Denmark before, and that gives me ⁠an impression of an increasingly aggressive and risk-taking Russia," Kieffer-Kristensen told the press conference.

"We believe that the case is important for understanding the threat that Denmark faces from Russia." PET said in September ‌Russia was recruiting Danish citizens to help plan ​sabotage against Danish defence firms with ties to Ukraine.
