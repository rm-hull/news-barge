A diamond ring worn by one of the most famous survivors of the Titanic sinking is expected to sell for up to £80,000 at auction.

Lucy, Lady Duff Gordon, was wearing the solitaire diamond ring when the passenger liner sank on 15 April 1912, killing around 1,500 people.

The ring is believed to have been her engagement ring and was the only piece of jewellery of significant value she owned to survive the disaster.

Known within the Duff Gordon family as the “Titanic Ring”, the piece has remained in the family for generations and is now being offered for sale through a direct descendant.

![Lucy, Lady Duff Gordon, was wearing the solitaire diamond ring when the Titanic sank](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/20/10/40/01M7W8DRKZCFJRQPDZ119QWSRW.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

It was passed to Lady Duff Gordon's grandson, John, the 3rd Earl of Halsbury, a British peer and scientist. Following his death, the ring remained with the family and has been treasured ever since.

The ring is due to go under the hammer in Devizes, Wiltshire, on 21 November, with auctioneers expecting it to fetch £80,000.

Andrew Aldridge, of Henry Aldridge & Son, said it was exceptionally unusual for jewellery connected to a Titanic survivor to come to market, particularly an item with such a direct family provenance.

“It’s exceptionally rare for jewellery related to a Titanic survivor to come to market but almost unprecedented for such a personal item from such a high-profile passenger to be sold at auction via direct descent,” he said.

![Photo issued by Henry Aldridge and Son Ltd of the back of a postcard written by Jack Phillips, the Titanic’s senior wireless operator when the ill-fated liner was docked at Southampton](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/05/14/01M6P1C7MA3PK4P90R36Q6NJQK.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The ring is accompanied by a signed letter of provenance and official diamond certification from the Birmingham Assay Office.

Mr Aldridge described it as a “truly unique item owned and worn by one of the most famous and most written about Titanic passengers”.

Lady Duff Gordon travelled to the United States with her husband, Sir Cosmo Duff Gordon, as first-class passengers aboard the Titanic in 1912.

The couple, alongside their secretary Laura Mabel Francatelli, escaped the sinking in Lifeboat No 1.

![The front of a postcard written by Jack Phillips, the Titanic’s senior wireless operator when the ill-fated liner was docked at Southampton](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/19/10/3756a2b8b1c25f7bfb30f56aadb1a306Y29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzk1MTY5ODY0-2.86373014.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The boat was designed to carry 40 people but was lowered into the water with just 12 aboard, seven of whom were crew members.

The Duff Gordons later faced controversy over their escape. While aboard the RMS Carpathia, the ship which rescued Titanic survivors, Sir Cosmo gave each of the men from Lifeboat No 1 a £5 cheque.

He said the payments were intended to help the men after the White Star Line stopped paying their wages following the sinking.

The gesture fuelled rumours that the Duff Gordons had bribed the lifeboat's crew not to return to rescue people in the water, amid fears that the boat could be swamped.
