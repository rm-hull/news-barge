**A driver who struck an 81-year-old pedestrian in Northallerton and fled the scene has been jailed, while a passenger who helped conceal evidence has received a suspended prison sentence.**

Luke Scott, 34, of Brickside Way, Northallerton, was sentenced to 26 months in prison at Teesside Crown Court on 19 November. He was also disqualified from driving for three years and eight months.

Scott pleaded guilty to causing serious injury by careless driving and perverting the course of justice.

Hayley Richardson, 34, of Berry Close, Great Yarmouth, who also pleaded guilty to perverting the course of justice, received a nine-month prison sentence suspended for 18 months and was ordered to complete 80 hours of unpaid work.

## Pedestrian suffered life-changing injuries

The collision happened shortly before 8.14pm on Tuesday 12 March 2024, when the victim was crossing Northallerton High Street after going out for the evening with his wife.

He was struck by a black Ford Fiesta driven by Scott. The force of the impact propelled him into the air, leaving him unconscious and suffering a brain haemorrhage, multiple skull and facial fractures, and fractures to his pelvis and legs.

Scott and Richardson, who was travelling as a passenger, left the scene.

CCTV enquiries led officers to footage of the vehicle immediately after the collision.

The following day, police received reports of a partially burned Ford Fiesta which had apparently been abandoned in Northallerton Rigg.

Officers linked the vehicle’s registration and insurance to Richardson. Further enquiries established that the pair had visited The Buck Inn before the collision, where they were seen drinking alcohol.

Both were subsequently arrested, interviewed and charged.

## Victim’s family describe devastating impact

In a victim personal statement, the man’s wife described how the collision had completely changed their lives and taken away the activities they had previously enjoyed together.

> **The victim’s wife said:**
>
> Since that day, our lives have changed completely and bears no resemblance to what it was before. We are no longer able to do any of the things that we previously enjoyed as a couple or as individuals and the disruption that has been caused to us cannot be measured.
>
> This is not way that either of us anticipated spending our retirement. He is not capable of making his own statement about how his life has been ruined by the callous actions of others. He no longer has the mental ability to do so. I have tried to speak on his behalf as best as I can but I don’t believe I could ever do it justice.

She said the consequences had extended across their family, affecting their children and grandchildren.

> **The victim’s wife said:**
>
> All I can say that is that his life, my life, the lives of our children and our grandchildren have been ruined. Two complete strangers have taken away any enjoyment of life that he might have once had, taken away his hobbies, his friends, his ability to think. Through doing this to him they have also destroyed my life and my enjoyment of it.

## Police investigation

Detective Sergeant Ed Drake, of North Yorkshire Police’s Serious Collision Investigation Unit, said the actions of the defendants had caused lasting damage to the victim and those close to him.

> **Detective Sergeant Ed Drake said:**
>
> The selfish and callous actions of these two individuals has caused irreparable damage to not only the victim but to his family and all those who knew and loved him and their lives will never be the same again.
>
> This case demonstrates our determination to pursue every available line of enquiry to establish the truth and ensure those responsible are held accountable. We will continue to do everything in our power to seek justice for victims and their families, and to bring offenders before the courts, no matter how long it takes.

North Yorkshire Police is asking anyone who witnesses dangerous driving to report it. Incidents presenting an immediate risk should be reported on 999, while non-emergency reports can be made through 101.

Information can also be passed anonymously to Crimestoppers on 0800 555 111 or through its online reporting service.
