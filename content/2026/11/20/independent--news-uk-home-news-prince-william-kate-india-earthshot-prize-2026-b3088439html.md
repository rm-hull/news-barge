William’s private secretary says the Prince and Princess of Wales are “very much a team” after their three-day trip to India to stage the Earthshot Prize.

William and Kate travelled to Mumbai to celebrate Anglo-Indian links, young people and William’s environmental awards, which fulfilled their belief that royal tours should have “purpose, meaning and impact”.

The visit was also significant for Kate, marking her first long-haul overseas trip since she announced her cancer diagnosis in March 2024.

William’s private secretary Ian Patrick said the prince had been particularly pleased to have Kate alongside him.

“The prince has really enjoyed having the princess alongside him for Earthshot, and for her to see first-hand how much it has grown in scale since she last attended in Boston,” he said.

“She’s enormously proud of what he’s achieved and it has been particularly special for them to experience this moment together.

“They are very much a team.”

![The Prince and Princess of Wales at the 2026 Earthshot Prize Awards (Aaron Chown/PA)](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/18/17/ed577344ca90975c18ac7dbe1b8486eaY29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzk1MTA4NjQz-2.86519621.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Earthshot CEO Jason Knauf described Kate as a key figure in the background, and when debating whether to go ahead with the initiative, the princess was the “loudest voice saying yes”.

During the visit, the future King announced the launch of a five-year personal accident insurance scheme for temporary rangers on the “front line” protecting “precious wildlife”.

The scheme, developed and launched by William’s United for Wildlife initiative, aims to address the problem of India’s temporary rangers not being covered by policies that protect permanent staff, potentially leaving their families with no income and compensation if they were killed or injured on duty.

It will provide protection for 10,000 temporary rangers across seven states from December.

The states covered are Madhya Pradesh, Karnataka, Assam, Uttarakhand, Maharashtra, Kerala and Rajasthan.

![William highlighted the risks faced by conservation workers, who often patrol remote areas and face difficult conditions while protecting wildlife.](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/18/09/01M7PZ3AK5KGHB0S12A0G33MGR.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

William announced the scheme as his India visit ended and highlighted the efforts of the workers: “Day and night, India’s wildlife rangers stand on the front line of protecting this precious wildlife. They patrol remote forests, endure difficult conditions and face significant dangers, all in service of safeguarding nature for future generations.”

The prince had announced similar initiatives following Earthshot Prize events in Rio de Janeiro in 2025 and Cape Town in 2026, extending support for wildlife rangers in several regions.

The royal couple also held bilateral talks with India’s Prime Minister Narendra Modi in New Delhi on Wednesday evening, the last day of the tour, to strengthen diplomatic ties between the two countries.

![Prince William and Kate with India's prime minister Narendra Modi](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/18/17/2026-11-18T170906Z_1986412341_RC2HQOAEVSW7_RTRMADP_3_BRITAIN-ROYALS-WILLIAM-INDIA-MODI.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

William’s aide said the couple want their international engagements to deliver meaningful outcomes, rather than simply serve as ceremonial visits.

“The prince and princess want their overseas travel to have purpose, meaning and impact,” Mr Patrick added.

“It’s about building stronger relationships, creating opportunities for collaboration and supporting projects that deliver real results.”

The couple attended the inaugural Children’s Earthshot during their visit and described the youngsters who had created solutions to tackle environmental challenges in their local communities as “incredible”.

Mr Patrick said: “One of the strongest themes of this visit has been young people. They believe passionately that the next generation must have a voice in shaping their own future.

“They don’t want young people to feel unheard or that the challenges facing the planet and society are beyond their control. They want them to feel optimistic, empowered and part of the solutions.”
