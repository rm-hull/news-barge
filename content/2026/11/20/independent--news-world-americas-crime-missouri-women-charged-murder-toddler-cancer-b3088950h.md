Two women in southwestern Missouri are facing second-degree murder charges after a 3-year-old boy died of a treatable form of cancer, more than two years after he last saw a doctor, according to court records.

The boy’s mother, Destiny Marie Schliefert, and a caregiver known to the children as “Grammy,” Debbie Sue Potter, were charged on Thursday in Christian County, according to Ozarks First, a local television station.

In addition to second-degree murder and an alternative count of felony murder, both women were charged with child abuse or neglect resulting in death, four counts of first-degree child endangerment and one count of animal neglect.

Emergency responders were called to the family’s home in Ozark, Missouri, just south of Springfield, on July 31 after Schliefert reported that her son was unresponsive, according to court documents cited by Ozarks First and the NBC affiliate KY3. First responders found the boy dead in an upstairs bedroom. Weighing 24 pounds, he appeared severely malnourished, officers wrote in court records.

An autopsy conducted on August 3 classified the boy’s death as a homicide caused by metastatic neuroblastoma in the setting of medical negligence. Medical examiners found tumors throughout his skull, rib cage, chest cavity and right leg, as well as a large mass surrounding his left kidney and adrenal gland, and a fracture in his right femur that may have been linked to a bone-attached tumor.

![Destiny Marie Schliefert faces charges of second-degree murder and child abuse after her 3-year-old son died of untreated cancer in Ozark, Missouri](https://static.the-independent.com/2026/11/20/20/52/Destiny-Schliefert.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&trim=330%2C208%2C2%2C290&auto=webp)

The boy had been diagnosed with cancer at Children’s Mercy Hospital in Kansas City in March 2024, according to medical records. Schliefert was informed of the diagnosis and given instructions for follow-up testing, investigators said, but the child’s last recorded visit to the hospital was on May 14, 2024.

No subsequent appointments were scheduled or attended, and the boy received no medical care after the family moved to Ozark in 2025. A physician involved in the child’s care told investigators that the cancer was survivable with proper treatment.

During police interviews on Wednesday, both women told detectives that they had not sought medical care because the boy lacked health insurance and Schliefert was waiting on paperwork, Ozarks First reported. Schliefert also acknowledged that she feared losing custody of her children if authorities saw the state of the home.

Another adult living at the residence told investigators that Schliefert and Potter drove the boy to a hospital in early July, when his health had visibly deteriorated, but turned back without going inside because Schliefert was afraid of being reported to the Missouri Division of Social Services. Asked by detectives whether taking her son to a hospital might have helped him, Schliefert replied, “probably.”

![Debbie Sue Potter, a caregiver known as ‘Grammy,’ also faces charges of second-degree murder and child abuse following the 3-year-old’s death](https://static.the-independent.com/2026/11/20/20/40/Debbie-Potter.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&trim=413%2C418%2C2%2C204&auto=webp)

Digital evidence recovered from Schliefert’s phone documented the toddler’s physical decline beginning in May, according to a probable cause statement.

Another resident told investigators that by mid-May, the boy could take only two or three steps before stopping and spent much of his time in bed or being carried. In a June text message, Potter told Schliefert that the child needed to start walking again.

Photographs from June and July showed rapid weight loss and emerging tumors along the boy’s rib cage and forehead, court documents said. On July 2, Schliefert searched online for the “fastest way to get a toddler to gain weight fast.” She later told the police that she believed his lack of appetite stemmed from his grandfather’s death in September 2025 and said she had given him an appetite stimulant.

Inside the house, investigators found piles of trash, rotting food, mold, animal waste and a cockroach infestation, according to court records. Detectives wrote that cockroaches were crawling on the boy’s body and that his designated sleeping area in the basement was covered with belongings.

The additional child endangerment charges stem from the living conditions of two other children at the residence, ages 7 and 15, while the animal neglect counts relate to an emaciated dog found confined to a kennel without food or water, according to Ozarks First.

Both women are being held without bond at the Christian County Jail.
