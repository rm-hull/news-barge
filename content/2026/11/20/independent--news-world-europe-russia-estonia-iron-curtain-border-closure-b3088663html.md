Russia has accused Estonia of trying to create a new “Iron Curtain” along its border after Tallinn announced plans to close road crossings with Russia for at least six months.

Russian Foreign Ministry spokesperson Maria Zakharova warned on Friday that Moscow would take political and diplomatic measures in response to the planned restrictions, which Estonia says are necessary on security grounds.

“Should Tallinn proceed with these plans, our country will take the necessary political and diplomatic measures in response to safeguard its national interests,” Ms Zakharova told a press briefing in Moscow.

She accused Estonia of attempting to erect a “new Iron Curtain” under what she described as the false pretence of a threat from the East.

Estonia's government has said the road crossings will close from 1 December, with a decision on whether to extend the suspension to be made after six months.

The move follows a suspected arson attack on Estonian defence company Threod Systems, which Tallinn has blamed on Russia. Moscow has denied involvement.

Estonia said the incident was part of a wider pattern of hostile activity against the country and other European states. Its government has previously accused Russian special services of involvement in attacks on defence industry companies, allegations the Kremlin has rejected.

The border restrictions could also affect people with family ties on both sides of the frontier, many of which date back to the Soviet era.

Estonia and its Baltic neighbours Latvia and Lithuania are the only European Union countries that still operate overland border crossings with Russia.

The crossing at Narva, a predominantly Russian-speaking city on Estonia's eastern border, is an important route for people travelling between the two countries, including those visiting relatives in St Petersburg.

Estonia, a member of Nato and the EU, has been a staunch supporter of Ukraine since Russia launched its full-scale invasion in February 2022.

The Kremlin has repeatedly denied accusations from European governments that it is behind sabotage operations and other hostile activity across the continent.

Ms Zakharova's latest comments mark a further escalation in the diplomatic dispute, although she did not specify what measures Russia might take in response to Estonia's plans.
