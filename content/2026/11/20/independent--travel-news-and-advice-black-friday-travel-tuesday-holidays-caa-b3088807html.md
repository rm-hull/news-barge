Holidaymakers planning breaks are being urged to guard against fraudulent online listings and AI-generated advertisements ahead of the Black Friday sales.

In a warning to consumers, the Civil Aviation Authority (CAA) cautioned against "false holiday deals that often appear too good to be true" in the run-up to the promotional period, which falls on 27 November.

Historical trading patterns indicate that UK holidaymakers could spend roughly £500 million on Travel Tuesday alone—taking place on 1 December —with industry estimates indicating booking numbers will be 3.5 times higher than on a typical day.

The aviation regulator noted that total UK consumer expenditure reached approximately £6.4 billion throughout the Black Friday sales window last year.

Data from the City of London Police shows that holiday fraud cost British consumers a total of £11 million in 2024.

![Based on previous sales figures, UK travellers are expected to spend around half a billion pounds on so-called Travel Tuesday alone, with the industry estimating 3.5 times more bookings are made that day compared with an average day](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/08/16/17/01M04PFY1S6X3ETCHZCHYNS1ER.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Chief Superintendent Amanda Wolf, head of report fraud operations, said criminals were using “sophisticated websites, adverts and social media posts” that are sometimes enhanced by AI to “make fake holiday offers appear genuine”.

“Victims can end up paying for flights, accommodation or package holidays that simply do not exist,” she said.

“Before booking, take a few minutes to research the company, check whether the holiday is Atol protected and, wherever possible, pay by credit card rather than bank transfer.”

Tim Johnson, consumer director at the CAA, said: “We are urging people to make sure they do everything they can to protect themselves, including checking that your travel provider is an Atol holder and that your flight-inclusive package holiday is Atol protected.

“Using a credit card and taking out travel insurance can also provide extra peace of mind.”

Travel companies selling air package holidays in the UK must hold an Atol (Air Travel Organiser’s Licence), the CAA said.

Using a credit card when booking may also add extra protection, while travel insurance can cover cancellations, missed transport, medical emergencies and lost or stolen items, the regulator added.

![Last year, UK consumers spent around £6.4 billion in total during the Black Friday period, the CAA said](https://static.independent.co.uk/2025/07/20/11/20110807-1748fd9c-04a1-478b-a8af-873d7179f22c.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Other tips include checking website addresses for misspellings or unusual domains, not to assume a professional-looking website or padlock symbol means the company is genuine and to be cautious if asked to pay by bank transfer, into a personal account or feel under pressure to act quickly, according to the CAA.

Unusually cheap deals may also be a warning sign, the CAA said, advising consumers to compare prices with other providers for similar trips.

A lower price does not necessarily mean a website is fake, but if the cost difference seems “unusually large”, the regulator advised holidaymakers not to let the pressure of a limited-time deal rush them into paying.

Buyers should also avoid clicking links in unexpected emails, texts or social media adverts and read the terms and conditions, including cancellation policies and booking details, before paying, the CAA said.

Ms Wolf added: “If you believe you have been a victim of holiday fraud, report it to Report Fraud as soon as possible at reportfraud.police.uk or by calling 0300 123 2040.

“If you live in Scotland, contact Police Scotland on 101.”

Anyone who thinks they have been a victim of fraud or cybercrime should contact their bank immediately.
