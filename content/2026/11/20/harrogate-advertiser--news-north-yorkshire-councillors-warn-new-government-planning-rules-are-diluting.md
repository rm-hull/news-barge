Council chiefs are to write to the Government expressing concerns that new planning rules are “diluting local democracy”.

North Yorkshire Council leader Councillor Carl Les agreed to raise the authority’s concerns as members voted to amend the council’s constitution to comply with the changes.

The reforms were introduced through the National Scheme of Delegation of Planning Functions in England, which came into effect on October 31.

The changes restrict which planning applications can be considered by elected councillors, with more decisions instead being made by council planning officers.

The letter was proposed by High Harrogate and Kingsley councillor Chris Aldred, who warned that the reforms would reduce democratic accountability and public involvement in planning decisions.

He said: “I think we need to make it clear that we don’t believe this is a good idea and that it is actually diluting local democracy.”

Speaking at this week’s full council meeting at County Hall in Northallerton, Councillor Aldred pointed out that several councils, including Fenland, Central Bedfordshire, Tameside and Bolton, had refused to amend their constitutions to reflect the new arrangements.

He said the council’s letter should highlight three main concerns - reduced oversight by elected members, less community involvement in significant planning decisions and the potential for greater officer-led decision-making to undermine transparency and accountability.

He added: “The new scheme diminishes the role of local planning committees, with more planning applications being decided by officers without sufficient input from elected representatives.”

Councillor Aldred also highlighted concerns raised by the Local Government Association, which has warned that the reforms risk eroding the democratic role of planning committees.

The organisation has called for the new arrangements to be reviewed in 2027, rather than 2028, and warned that a one-size-fits-all approach may not adequately reflect the needs of local planning authorities, particularly those covering large geographical areas.

At the meeting, councillors rejected a proposal by Hipswell and Colburn councillor Kevin Foster that the local division councillor, rather than the planning committee chair, should be the nominated member to work with officers when a planning application was called in for further consideration.

The proposed amendment was instead referred to the council’s constitution working group for further consideration.

Speaking after the meeting, Councillor Foster said: “I am very disappointed that members have rejected the opportunity for local councillors to have a say in planning matters at an early stage, on behalf of the residents they were elected to represent.”
