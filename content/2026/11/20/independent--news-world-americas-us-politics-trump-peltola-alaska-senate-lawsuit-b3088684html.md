The Trump administration has effectively dropped its lawsuit seeking to block certification of Democrat Mary Peltola's victory in Alaska's U.S. Senate race.

The move on Thursday ended a much-criticized gambit by the president to prevent a political rival from taking office. The legal challenge centered on the presence on the ballot of a second candidate with the same name as the incumbent, Republican Sen. Dan Sullivan.

The Alaska Supreme Court had already ruled that the second Sullivan's candidacy is legal and that he could be on the ballot. Attorneys for the state, which is currently controlled by Republicans, also objected to the administration's move, calling it legally “meritless” and filing papers to help get the initial case dismissed.

![President Donald Trump leaves after speaking at a campaign rally at the Menard Memorial Sports Center on October 13, 2026 in Wasilla, Alaska](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/14/09/2300090995..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The administration moved Thursday to have an appeals court declare its case moot, two days after the race was decided. In their filing, Justice Department attorneys urged the 9th U.S. Circuit Court of Appeals to overturn the lower court's dismissal of the case to erase the loss, but then dismiss the case anyway.

They said the court “should hold that this case is moot, vacate the underlying order, and remand for dismissal on that ground.” That decision came after Peltola won an outright majority in the race to become Democrats' 56th senator, the party's ninth Senate pickup in its midterm blowout.

Peltola won by more votes than the other candidate, Dan J. Sullivan.

In a statement responding to the filing, Peltola said it was time to move on: "Now that the election is over and the voters sent a very clear message about the direction they want our state to go in, I’m eager to move forward and get to work on behalf of all Alaskans.”

![Alaska Democratic Senate candidate, former Rep. Mary Peltola (D-AK) speaks during at an election-night watch party at Sheraton Anchorage Hotel on November 03](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/19/15/2303429811..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Messages seeking comment were also left with Sen. Sullivan's campaign and the Alaska Department of Law. The Department of Justice filed the lawsuit at President Donald Trump's direction, saying that a Peltola win should not be certified until an investigation could determine if voters were confused.

The administration never explained how such an investigation could take place given the ballot's secrecy.

The case was filed before *The New York Times* reported on texts and other documents that appeared to show Peltola's campaign recruited the retired schoolteacher from the tiny fishing community of Petersburg.

He was listed as Daniel J. Sullivan Jr. on the ballot, with no party preference, to distinguish him from the senator, who was identified as a Republican and the incumbent.

![Peltola defeated the incumbent Senator Dan S. Sullivan (pictured)](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/04/18/2303390870..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Dan J. Sullivan has long insisted he ran on his own and Peltola has denied any involvement.

Trump has also demanded that Peltola be prosecuted for the scheme, and federal investigators swarmed into Alaska before Election Day. Tricks involving decoy candidates, such as the second Sullivan, have been a staple of U.S. politics for decades, and Trump's allies have also used them, though the schemes occasionally lead to criminal charges.

It was at least the second time since the Nov. 3 election that the Department of Justice has moved to dismiss one of its high-profile lawsuits interfering with states' election administration.
