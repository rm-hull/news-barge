Leaders across Europe have roundly condemned Russia after a cruise missile landed in Poland while Vladimir Putin’s forces carried out a major bombardment of neighbouring Ukraine, fuelling fears that the war in Ukraine is spilling further into Europe.

Polish prime minister Donald Tusk said the incursions appeared to be an “intentional provocation” from Russia, coming as Poland is set to host “Coalition of the Willing” military exercises with Ukraine’s allies.

Warsaw said it had scrambled jets and appealed to its Nato allies for help after detecting 11 foreign objects threatening its airspace, including three that it said had made it through.

Poland said the missile hit a rural area at Nowy Lipowiec in the southeast of the country, about 30 miles from the Ukrainian border. The crash location is also about 47 miles from where thousands of European troops were taking part in the military exercises to prepare for a potential post-war mission in Ukraine.

Mr Tusk said there was no doubt the objects that landed in Poland were Russian, adding: “It seems we are dealing with an intentional provocation.”

“Today, it is clearer than ever before that Poland must participate in decisions and discussions regarding the future of Ukraine and security in Europe,” he said.

British prime minister Andy Burnham condemned the violation as “utterly unacceptable”, while his defence minister, Wes Streeting, said Europe would not be divided or deterred from its support for Ukraine.

![A flash of an explosion is seen near Chmielek, in Bilgoraj County in eastern Poland, some 300km from Warsaw](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/20/15/2305451051..?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&auto=webp)

Nato secretary general Mark Rutte said he had spoken to Mr Tusk about the “grave violations of Polish airspace overnight”. Mr Rutte said: “We stand in solidarity with Poland and will continue to strengthen our ability to deter and defend.

“This incident is clearly dangerous – a consequence of Russia’s ongoing war against Ukraine.”

EU chief Ursula von der Leyen also offered her “full solidarity” to Poland. “I also want to strongly condemn this violation of the Polish airspace. We stand in full solidarity with the Polish people,” Ms von der Leyen said during the press conference in Brussels.

Drones have repeatedly strayed into the airspace of countries on Nato’s eastern flank during Russia’s war in Ukraine, fuelling tension with the Western military alliance.

Polish defence minister Wladyslaw Kosiniak-Kamysz said the attack early on Friday involved a Russian Kh-101 air-launched cruise missile, another missile he did not identify, and a drone.

He said that the incursions were unlikely to have been accidental, having occurred just ahead of Poland hosting defence ministers from Britain, France and the Baltic states.

Poland would ask the Nato military alliance for more support securing the alliance’s eastern flank, he added.

Investigations were meanwhile opened into the incidents in Nowy Lipowiec, Molodycz and Rudnik nad Sanem, interior minister Marcin Kierwiński said.

Footage shared on social media from what appeared to be a security camera at a property in the village of Chmielek, near Nowy Lipowiec, showed an explosion in the distance.

“I was making coffee, getting the kids ready for school, and then I heard a whistling sound. And then a thud – a dull, muffled kind of impact,” said resident Natalia Szponar. “I was a little panicked. I felt a bit scared.”

Andrzej Borowiec, mayor of neighbouring Aleksandrow county, said an object around seven metres long had fallen at 5.40am GMT (6.40am local time) on Friday.

“Smoke was rising – black smoke... something that looked like one large, distinct mushroom cloud,” he said.

![Vladimir Putin met with Donald Trump and Xi Jinping in China this week – an unprecedented summit compared to Yalta](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/19/17/2305282287..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

European countries previously have expressed alarm at what they deem to be Russia’s attempts to destabilise the continent.

US President Donald Trump discussed the war at a summit in China on Thursday with Putin and Chinese leader Xi Jinping, according to his envoy Steve Witkoff. Another envoy, Trump’s son-in-law Jared Kushner, told European leaders that the United States believes Russia is “comfortable” with Nato-like security guarantees for postwar Ukraine, in what could be a step towards compromise.

Russia’s nighttime attack on Ukraine came hours after Russian president Vladimir Putin vowed to retaliate for Ukrainian strikes this week on a bridge linking occupied Crimea with the Russian mainland. The bridge is vital to Moscow’s war effort.

Overnight strikes killed at least eight Ukrainian civilians and wounded more than two dozen, Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky said, calling it “one of the largest” attacks Russia has undertaken in recent months. Russia fired more than 400 drones and over 110 missiles, 27 of them ballistic and some carrying cluster warheads that scatter dozens of smaller bomblets across a wide area, he said.

“The Russians spread this attack across many cities and regions of Ukraine. Absolutely all the targets were ordinary civilian infrastructure – things that literally sustain people’s lives,” Mr Zelensky said in a post on social media platform X.

![Defence Secretary Wes Streeting meeting British solders while attending the Exercise KOALICJA demonstration in Poland](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/20/15/01M7WSNF8A2FFH92JXVF9JSTW2.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Europe has long been vying for inclusion in the US-brokered talks between Ukraine and Russia, but is yet to find a seat at the table. Ukraine has been supportive of Europe’s inclusion.

Speaking at an EU-UK summit press conference in Brussels, Britain’s Mr Burnham said: “The incursion into Polish airspace is utterly unacceptable. I condemn it unreservedly.

“And from a British point of view, [we] send a strong message of solidarity to Prime Minister Tusk and the Polish people, who we will support always in any way that we can.”

The prime minister continued: “As I said at the [recent] Coalition of the Willing [meeting], everyone is losing in this war. Everybody, and Russia is losing the most – when you see the number of young people, Russian young people, their deaths at the front line.

“Nobody is winning; this is senseless. No ground is being taken, minimal gains.”

Mr Streeting, speaking at a joint press conference in Nowa Deba, Poland, said it was another example of a Russian provocation aimed at dividing Europe.

“Europe will not be deterred from our support for Ukraine,” he said.

“We will not allow Putin to divide our continent, and we recognise that the frontline in Ukraine is the frontline for freedom and democracy everywhere.”
