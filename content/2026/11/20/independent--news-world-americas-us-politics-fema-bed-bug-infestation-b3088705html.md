Employees at the Federal Emergency Management Agency’s Washington, D.C., headquarters have reportedly been ordered to stay home due to a bed bug infestation.

More than 1,000 staffers were forced to work from home while some of the agency’s offices were fumigated Thursday and received a warning Wednesday night not to come in, *The Washington Post* and CNN report.

The infestation was found across multiple floors of the agency’s offices in the 500 C Street building.

It tops off a turbulent week for the Department of Homeland Security, which oversees FEMA, following the potential cybersecurity concerns of last week’s air traffic control outage.

“This closure is necessary to perform a comprehensive, building-wide pest control treatment to address an active pest issue and ensure a safe, healthy workspace for all personnel,” an internal memo to employees reportedly said, adding that staff would be updated once the situation had been “successfully resolved.”

![Employees at the Federal Emergency Management Agency’s Washington, DC, site have reportedly been ordered to stay home due to a bed bug infestation](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/17/08/2198369476..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

*The Independent* has contacted FEMA for comment.

Staff expressed frustration after Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin mobilized the agency after the nationwide ground stop on November 13.

FEMA was instructed to coordinate with airports to help get medication and other supplies to stranded passengers across the country, but after 24 hours, the assignment ended.

Staffers were reportedly “mostly sat around” with little to do.

“It was a giant waste of time and resources,” a FEMA official told CNN.

“Pull people from their actual important work to pretend they are doing work... and [potentially] infect them with bed bugs while doing it,” another staffer complained to the *Post*.

Bed bug infestations can lead to people facing bites on their skin and unsanitary stains on their linens, plus time-consuming and costly efforts to find and eradicate the bugs.

![Bed bug infestations can lead to people facing bites on their skin and unsanitary stains on their linens](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/20/13/41/GettyImages-2247775631.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

FEMA’s reported infestation is not the first time a federal building has been plagued with bed bugs, which are notoriously difficult to eliminate.

In June, employees at the U.S. Department of Agriculture - ironically, the agency charged with protecting the country against invasive pests - were sent home after the offices were repeatedly infested with bed bugs.

Employees reporting to the George Washington Carver Center in Beltsville, Maryland, were warned of the infestation in mid-May.

The irony of the outbreak happening in the offices of the Animal and Plant Health Inspection Service “was lost on no one,” a USDA employee told NOTUS at the time.
