After a four-day trip to multiple international events in Asia, the first thing President Donald Trump wanted to address when he spoke to reporters on Friday was his plane.

The president just had to gush about how the former Qatari luxury jet he has repurposed as Air Force One had flown home from Shenzhen, China, to Joint Base Andrews outside of Washington without stopping for fuel.

It’s unclear why that was supposed to be so impressive. The 30-year-old Boeing 747-200 he wanted to replace can remain in the air indefinitely by way of in-flight refuelling — a feature his ex-Qatari flying palace lacks.

Still, Trump insisted that the new “record” for Air Force One had proved “new equipment is better than old equipment” and called the flight “one last event for the history books.”

He spent the next 80 minutes taking what he thought was a victory lap but in reality was more of a lame duck quacking without consequence.

![The president did not say why his Vice President and Secretary of State needed to be present for a news conference when both men have their own responsibilities](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/20/19/2306099705..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Trump said that in four days he had met “more than 20 world leaders” and “secured two new trade deals, and about 10 others coming very soon,” including an agreement under which Thailand would drop its tariffs on American goods and buy $2.6 billion a year in U.S. farm products, a figure Trump himself called “a preliminary number.”

He also boasted of what he said was more than $17 billion in commercial deals from the Manila leg of the trip, including a Philippine Airlines order for 20 Boeing 787 aircraft. He also claimed that the Philippines would soon buy “100 percent” of its farm products and energy from the United States.

There was just one problem — they weren’t actual trade agreements with long-term benefits. Just short-term “deals.”

Most of the other agreements he bragged about aren’t real yet, either.

The trip’s three-way summit with Xi and Putin, the first of its kind, produced a pledge from China to “conduct trilateral discussions on reducing nuclear risks.” Trump then said he doubted the arsenals themselves could be cut. “It’s very hard for me to say let’s cut them in half,” he said. “So I’m not sure that can ever happen.”

Vice President JD Vance and Secretary of State Marco Rubio flanked him on stage like a pair of chaperones, while Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent and White House Chief of Staff Stephen Miller watched from stage right. Those three appeared to be there mainly for moral support.

Trump then gave the podium to Vance, who likened staying behind in Washington “a little bit to Home Alone, but with better food and more gold.” Vance spent much of his time on a permitting bill the Senate had passed while the president was away, and he ended by telling Trump: “So congratulations on a good trip, sir.”

Rubio explained what the trip was for, starting with a question of his own: “Why does this matter to the American people?” Of the trilateral summit, he said: “The better these three leaders get along, the easier and more conducive the environment will be to actually agree to things that are concrete down the road.”

The meeting, in his words, had “created a foundation upon which, hopefully, the hard work of diplomacy and trade negotiations can occur.”
