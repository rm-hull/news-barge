An Indian ⁠man was sentenced to more ⁠than 19 ​years in US prison on Friday after admitting to orchestrating a failed Indian government-backed plot to kill a Sikh separatist in New York ⁠City, in connection with what US and Canadian authorities have called a broader effort to target Indian dissidents.

Nikhil Gupta, 55, pleaded guilty in February to murder-for-hire, conspiracy ⁠to commit murder-for-hire and conspiracy to commit money laundering.

The US district judge Victor Marrero handed down the ​235-month sentence at a hearing in a Manhattan federal ‌court.

The discovery of alleged ‌assassination plots against Sikh separatists in the US and Canada has tested relations with India, which has ‌denied involvement in such plots.

Prosecutors accused Gupta of plotting with an Indian government official to kill Gurpatwant Singh Pannun, a dual US-Canadian citizen and lawyer at Sikhs for Justice in New York who has advocated for a sovereign Sikh state in northern India.

Gupta and the Indian government official, Vikash Yadav, arranged to have $15,000 in cash delivered to a purported hitman who ‌was actually an undercover US law enforcement officer, court papers show.

Yadav is also charged in the case but is not in US custody. It is unclear ​whether he has hired a defense lawyer.

India’s government has dissociated itself from any plot against Pannun, saying it was against government policy.

Prosecutors with the Manhattan US attorney’s office had recommended a sentence of about 24 years in prison.

“This case cuts to the very core of American values,” prosecutor Ashley ⁠Nicolas said in court. “We don’t condone the use of violence to silence voices ​in this country, ever.”

Addressing the ​court on Friday, Gupta said ​he regretted straying from the non-violent example set by the Indian independence leader Mahatma Gandhi.

“I’m ​sorry to Mr ‌Pannun, I’m sorry ​to the communities I hurt, ​I’m sorry to my own family,” Gupta said.

Pannun told the court that he was grateful to the US for prosecuting Gupta and said he would keep advocating for Sikh sovereignty.

“Self-determination for my people and my homeland, Punjab, is my lifetime goal,” he said.
