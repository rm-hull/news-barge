The parent company of Pontins holiday parks has been fined £1 million after a guest died following a ceiling collapse.

Wendy Jones, 68, was one of 16 people injured during the incident, in the Fun Factory part of the Brean Sands site in Somerset, in February 2019.

She was taken to hospital, along with five others, and spent six months there before her death in August 2019.

Britannia Jinky Jersey Ltd, the parent company of Pontins, later admitted a charge under the Health and Safety at Work Act along with Stephen Bennison, 60, who carried out work on the ceiling.

On Friday, Mr Justice Moody fined Britannia Jinky Jersey Ltd a total of £1 million plus prosecution costs of £72,345 and handed Bennison a 10-month prison sentence, suspended for 18 months. Bennison must pay £1,000 of prosecution costs.

![Wendy Jones was at the Fun Factory part of the Brean Sands holiday park](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/19/16/26/0c17a120-193d-11f0-8a1e-3ff815141b98-copy-306e59e5.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&auto=webp)

Describing the ceiling collapse, the judge told the court: “It was an accident waiting to happen.

“Mrs Jones was on holiday at Brean Sands holiday park with her daughter, Hayley Kendall, Hayley’s partner, and her grandson.

“At about 6pm, Hayley and her mother went to the Fun Factory to play bingo.”

The judge described how the Fun Factory, which could host up to 1,000 guests, had a ventilation duct on the ceiling which was boxed in.

Ms Kendall heard a “loud bang” while queuing at the bar before turning to see wood, metal and yellow foam insulation had come down from the ceiling.

“It must have been a terrifying experience for those people who were underneath it or nearby,” the judge said.

The judge told how the ventilation duct had come down “in a domino fashion”, injuring Mrs Jones and 15 others including a baby, children and adults.

He said it was “only a matter of luck” that nobody else had sustained life threatening injuries in the incident.

Mr Justice Moody told the court that the ventilation duct had been installed at the site before Britannia Jinky Jersey Ltd took over in 2011.

After becoming the new owner, Britannia Jinky Jersey “invested very high sums in the business”, the judge said.

In 2015, it was decided that sound proofing work would be carried out in the Fun Factory following complaints from neighbours.

![Wendy Jones, 68, was one of 16 people injured during the incident in the Fun Factory part of the Brean Sands site in Somerset in February 2019](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/19/16/15/7d6e3780-193d-11f0-8a1e-3ff815141b98-copy-7de8b7c3.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Bennison, who ran a sound and lighting company, had previously carried out work for Pontins and was contracted to install sound insulation slabs – despite having no prior experience in doing so.

“I find that Mr Bennison was engaged because he was cheaper,” the judge said.

“Britannia Jinky Jersey did have health and safety policies. Unfortunately, the process was not followed in this case.”

Bennison failed to produce a method statement or risk assessment and subcontracted the work out to “ad hoc tradesmen” who also had no experience with soundproofing, the judge added.

After the installation, there were leftover insulation slabs and it was agreed that Bennison would put these on top of the ventilation ducting.

Concerns were raised about the soundproofing work by the general manager a short time later and a chartered surveyor reported it was not “adequately installed” and “could result in a collapse of the ceiling”.

However, Bennison, of Farington, Lancashire, “essentially rejected” the report, the judge said.

He described Bennison as having a “causal disregard for health and safety obligations” and a “wilful blindness” to the risk of offending.

![The incident was described as ‘entirely preventable’](https://static.independent.co.uk/2025/04/15/08/251afc166167f9b2eed597954b46d367Y29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzQ0Nzg1MzM0-2.9771920.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

In the days before the incident, guests noticed a bulge in the ceiling and reported it to staff but their concerns were not escalated before the collapse, the judge added.

James Havard-Jones, the son of Mrs Jones, told the court her “life was cut short by the negligence” of others.

“An accident is something unforeseeable, this was not unforeseeable,” he said.

“This was preventable. The risk existed and nobody did what should have been done to stop it.”

Speaking after the case, Detective Superintendent Roger Doxsey of Avon and Somerset Police said it was a “particularly complex investigation”.

“Wendy’s family are absolutely right when they say this incident was entirely preventable,” he added.

“Errors were made during the installation of the soundproofing, and they were compounded by the warning signs not being heeded.

“Britannia Jinky Jersey’s use of a not suitably trained subcontractor to conduct the soundproofing work, at notably less cost, had severe consequences that Wendy’s family continue to suffer.”

Det Supt Doxsey continued: “It is a minor miracle, given the weight of the ducting, the nature of its collapse, and the volume of other people present in the Fun Factory at the time of the incident, that more people were not seriously injured and killed.”
