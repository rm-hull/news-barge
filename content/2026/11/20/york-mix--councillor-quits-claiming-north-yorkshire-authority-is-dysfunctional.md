A long-serving North Yorkshire councillor has announced he will not stand for re-election in May, saying he is exhausted by the job and believes the council has become too big and bureaucratic.

Richmond’s Stuart Parsons was first elected to North Yorkshire County Council in 2005 and has served on that authority and its successor unitary council ever since.

The former chair of Richmondshire District Council, who served on that authority from 2003, said he hoped someone younger would take his place when elections are held next year.

He said: “I’m 65, going on 66, and perhaps somebody younger could do a better job than me.

“The truth is, I’m exhausted by it.

“The change to a unitary authority still isn’t working. It hasn’t brought the solutions we were promised, and the system is dysfunctional.”

Cllr Parsons, who is the leader of the North Yorkshire Independents political group on the authority, believes the creation of the unitary authority had failed to deliver the improvements promised and had instead made it harder for councillors to help residents.

He said the council had some “absolutely fantastic” frontline staff but claimed they were often prevented from helping residents by bureaucracy and decisions made by senior managers.

He said: “You have people bending over backwards to help residents, only to be stopped by somebody two levels above them because something doesn’t meet policy.

“It becomes incredibly frustrating.”

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/north-yorkshire-council-county-hall-hq-sign.webp)

Asked whether North Yorkshire Council, which replaced the county’s former district and borough councils in April 2023, was simply too big, he replied: “Oh God, yes. It’s far too big.

“They talk about being a local council, but they’re not.”

He said routine jobs which had previously been dealt with locally now required referrals to teams elsewhere in the county.

As an example, he said tree maintenance issues in Richmond had to be referred to staff in Harrogate, whereas the former Richmondshire District Council’s grounds maintenance team would often deal with fallen trees and branches without even being asked.

He also criticised what he described as excessive paperwork, claiming community organisation Heart of Richmond, which installs defibrillators, had been faced with a 97-page document to obtain permission.

Similarly, he said local charities wishing to put banners on railings at Friary Gardens were now being required to complete lengthy applications, despite a longstanding arrangement under the former district council.

“The council isn’t helping or respecting local traditions, and if it can’t do that, then it needs to change,” he said.

Cllr Parsons also claimed that local knowledge had been lost following the reorganisation, with confusion over responsibility for some areas of Richmond previously maintained by the district council.

## More than full time

He said the workload of being a councillor had increased significantly, describing the role as more than full-time for anyone wanting to do it properly.

“For our last council meeting, we had 589 pages of reports to read, and that was a relatively small agenda.

“When the budget comes around in February, there will probably be 1,400 or 1,500 pages.”

The councillor said that while the larger political groups could rely on one or two councillors reading the report and “telling them which way to vote”, he didn’t have the luxury.

“If I’m fighting for Richmond on my own, I have to read it all, understand it and work out what I want to challenge.”

He said council budget cuts since 2010 had made the job increasingly difficult, but added that the recent controversy over changes to home-to-school transport had been particularly damaging.

“The final thing that really finished me off was home-to-school transport,” he said.

“The political shenanigans, and I include both sides in that, became absolutely unbearable.

“I believe we have damaged young people, schools and their futures by adopting that policy.”

Despite his frustrations, Cllr Parsons said the most rewarding part of his time in local government had been working alongside dedicated council employees.

“I will miss the frontline staff enormously, and I really hope somebody recognises them properly for what they’re trying to do.”

He said he intended to take a short break after May but would continue supporting local organisations, including Richmondshire Museum and other attractions in the town.

“I’m not going to disappear from view,” he added.

“If the community still wants me to be involved, that’s fine. If they don’t, that’s fine too.

“But I think I can do more off the council than I can actually do on it.”
