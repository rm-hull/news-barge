## Condoms and drinking fears raised in Conservative Club booze row

Empty Cans. Photo Is For Illustrative Purpose. Courtesy John Cameron On Unsplash.

USED condoms, underage drinking, and anti-social behaviour have all been cited in objection to controversial plans for 6am alcohol sales next door to a Conservative Club.

Whitby Conservative Club has warned that Penny Petroleum (Scarborough) Ltd’s plan to sell alcohol from 6am could fuel nuisance, disorder and public safety concerns.

The business has applied for a premises licence to sell alcohol for off-premises consumption at Whitby Service Station, Flowergate Cross, between 6am and 11pm every day.

North Yorkshire Council’s statutory licensing sub-committee is due to consider the application next week.

In an objection submitted on behalf of the Whitby Conservative Club’s committee, representing around 400 members, the club said it was concerned about the impact alcohol sales could have on its neighbouring premises.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21743273.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Whitby Conservative Club And Penny Petroleum, Whitby. Google Maps

The garage shop is attached to the club building, with its entrance located next to the access point to the club’s gardens.

The committee said the club had already been affected by anti-social behaviour, with teenagers allegedly entering the gardens outside opening hours after buying alcohol elsewhere.

In its submission, the club said: “At these times they are purchasing alcohol from local off-licences and supermarkets, drinking it in the garden and leaving the empty cans and bottles for the club staff to clear up.

“On occasions, items of a sexual nature have also been left.”

The club said this included used condoms.

The club’s committee added that “the sale of alcohol so close to the garden will unduly add to the issue that the club is attempting to eradicate”.

Committee members also raised concerns about underage drinking and anti-social behaviour in nearby Pannett Park, claiming youths had previously gathered there to consume alcohol and had been linked to vandalism.

Among the objections lodged with the council are concerns that alcohol sales from 6am to 11pm could increase opportunities for anti-social behaviour, littering and vandalism, as well as create public safety risks linked to the petrol station forecourt and surrounding road network.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21556599.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The Penny Petroleum Whitby Service Station That Has Applied For A 6am Premises Licence From North Yorkshire Council. Google Maps

The licensing report notes that no representations have been made by responsible authorities, including North Yorkshire Police.

According to the applicant, alcohol would account for only around 15 per cent of the products available within the convenience store and would form part of a wider retail offering including groceries, household goods and toiletries.

Several objectors have also argued that the premises may be prohibited from selling alcohol under Section 176 of the Licensing Act 2003, which prevents premises used primarily as a petrol filling station from being granted a licence to sell alcohol.

One objector questioned the need for early-morning sales, stating: “Who buys alcohol at 6am?

“Is it going to attract criminal activity?”

Another resident expressed concern that late-night sales could encourage people to continue drinking after pubs have closed, stating: ” Buying alcohol at 11pm would suggest people wanting to continue drinking after pubs have closed, so are they likely to be already intoxicated?

“Are they going to drive drunk or walk across a potentially dangerous forecourt in an intoxicated condition?”

Council officers said members of the licensing sub-committee would need to consider whether the premises were primarily used as a garage before determining the application.

If councillors conclude that the site falls within the definition of an excluded premises under Section 176, the sale of alcohol could not be authorised.

The sub-committee can grant the licence as applied for, grant it with additional conditions, or refuse the application.
