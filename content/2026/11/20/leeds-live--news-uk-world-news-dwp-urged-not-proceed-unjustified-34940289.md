# DWP urged not to proceed with 'unjustified' and 'wrong' PIP cuts plan

## DWP has been urged not to proceed with 'unjustified' and 'wrong' plans for Personal Independence Payment (PIP) and Universal Credit health element recipients

The Department for Work and Pensions (DWP) has faced calls to abandon what critics are describing as "unjustified" and "wrong" proposals affecting Personal Independence Payment (PIP) and Universal Credit health element claimants.

The Milburn Review's final report recommends that the Labour government and the DWP introduce an "additional cost allowance" to replace personal independence payment for young people, when their disability-related extra costs "are significant, established and ongoing".

The report states that eligibility "would be more closely targeted at those with demonstrable extra costs for functional needs that are long-term and enduring", with the goal of fostering independence and participation. Disability Rights UK responded: "Alan Milburn rightly highlights the ways in which successive governments have turned their back on young people including young Disabled people.

"The Review has spotlighted major systemic failures across education, health and employment support. However, the Review's analysis that social security needs to be cut for young people, particularly young Disabled people, is unjustified and wrong.", reports Birmingham Live.

"To characterise young people as being happy to stay on benefits, when the Review has outlined all the ways in which they have been let down, makes absolutely no sense. To compare spending on social security with spending on employment support, might be a good soundbite, but it ignores the reality that it is successive governments that have underinvested in employment support.

"Removing social security protection from large numbers of young Disabled people will result in increases in poverty, destitution and homelessness, compounding precarity and worsening health and wellbeing outcomes."

Beth Light, Policy and Parliamentary Coordinator at ALLFIE, comments: "The government refers to NEET rates as a "moral failing" that they have an obligation to solve.

"But it is a moral failing to force young Disabled people further into poverty and away from support because of the harmful systemic and structural barriers that shut us out of education, employment and training. Until these barriers are recognised and removed, punishing Disabled people by throwing us into deeper poverty is the true moral failing."

Inclusion London stated: "This approach will make it harder, not easier, for young Disabled people to get and stay in work. No one should live in poverty because they cannot work, or because they cannot find an accessible job. That's why the government must not use cuts against individuals as a solution for a system problem.

"Much of the new support for employment that Milburn proposes would be welcome, and some of his ideas could genuinely help Disabled people work. But the cuts are indefensible. Additional employment support is an investment that would eventually pay for itself, through reduced benefits claims and increased tax receipts. It does not have to be tied to cuts."
