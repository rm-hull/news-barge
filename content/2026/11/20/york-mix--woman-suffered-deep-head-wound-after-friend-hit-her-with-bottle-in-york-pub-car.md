A man has been spared prison after hurling a glass beer bottle across a pub car park which struck his friend on the head.

Oliver Reed, 22, known as “Ollie”, threw the glass projectile in a fit of pique after nearly getting into a fight with a man at The Beagle pub, York Crown Court heard.

The victim suffered a deep, 3cm-long wound which “went through all the layers of the skin”.

Prosecutor Jennifer Gatland said it appeared that Reed – a high-flier who earned a “handsome wage” – had aimed the bottle at the other man with whom he had a heated argument, but it hit the victim instead.

As blood poured from the her head wound, Reed got in his car and drove off from the pub in Foxwood Lane.

He was later arrested and charged with wounding. He ultimately admitted the offence and appeared for sentence yesterday (Thursday).

Ms Gatland said the incident occurred in January when the victim and her female friend had gone to meet others for a pub quiz at The Beagle.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/york-crown-court-july-2024.webp)

Reed, from Huntington, was also there with a friend but was sitting apart from the two women, who had been chatting to a man who exchanged Instagram details with the victim.

Later in the night, the two women and this man went outside into the car park and were followed by Reed, who had a beer bottle in his hand.

Immediately, the atmosphere between Reed and the other man turned “hostile” and there was a heated exchange during which they “squared up” and Reed told the named man: “I’ll run you over.”

The victim was standing between the two of them to keep them apart and told them to stop.

## High-pitched scream

According to the victim’s female friend, the other man made an “off comment” to Reed and challenged him to a fight, but Reed said he “wasn’t interested”.

But then Reed walked over to the edge of the car park and put his beer bottle down on a fencepost as if a fight was about to ensue.

Instead, he picked up the bottle and hurled it towards the spot where the other man was standing next to the victim who was “the peacemaker throughout”.

The glass bottle struck the victim on the side of her head next to her ear. The blow struck her so hard that she began staggering as she let out a “high-pitched scream”. Blood was gushing from the head wound.

Ms Gatland said it was suspected that Reed was aiming the bottle at the other man, but it hit the victim instead.

She said it was uncertain whether the glass bottle smashed on impact with the victim’s head or whether it shattered when it fell to the ground.

“(Reed) ran and got in his car,” said Ms Gatland.

“He drove over to them and stopped the car. He reversed the car away and drove out of the car park.”

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/york-hospital-ambulance-accident-emergency.webp)

The named victim was treated at York Hospital for a deep gash to her head behind the hairline. The wound was stitched and she was discharged.

Reed, of Oak Glade, Huntington, was arrested later that same day, 24 January, but declined to comment in police interview.

He was initially charged with wounding with intent which he denied, but offered a guilty plea to wounding without intent, or unlawful wounding, which was accepted by the prosecution.

The more-serious charge of wounding with intent was withdrawn and no evidence offered.

## ‘Frequent nightmares’

Reed appeared for sentence today when the prosecution read out a statement from the victim.

She said the attack had left her with a “permanent scar and a noticeable dent on my head”.

“I continue to suffer from headaches and painful twinges in my head,” she added.

She had since suffered from “frequent nightmares” and struggled to sleep which left her feeling “exhausted and emotionally drained”. She had since sought therapy for the ongoing stress and anxiety caused.

Reed’s solicitor Neil Cutte said his client had “done something very stupid, very foolish, in the heat of the moment”.

He said that Reed was a hard-working man who had never been in trouble before and “earned a handsome wage”.

He said that Reed didn’t intend to strike his female friend with the bottle and that up until then they had been close friends.

Recorder Simon Jackson KC said that despite the seriousness of the offence, the “nasty” wound to the victim and the evident physical and psychological harm caused, he could spare Reed an immediate jail sentence.

That was because of his “strong personal mitigation” including his remorse, lack of previous convictions and the fact that he had a good job.

Reed was given a 15-month jail sentence, but this was suspended for two years. During that period, he will have to complete 250 hours of unpaid work and a mental-health-treatment programme.

He was ordered to pay £6,000 compensation to the victim.
