An inquest jury has concluded that a mother-of-five died from an accident after undergoing a Brazilian butt lift cosmetic procedure.

Alice Webb, 33, became unwell after receiving the BBL injection by beauty influencer Jordan Parke, known as the Lip King.

Ms Webb, from Wotton-under-Edge, Gloucestershire, suffered five cardiac arrests after undergoing the procedure in Gloucester in September 2024 and died later at the city’s hospital.

The inquest jury returned a conclusion of accidental death following a three-day inquest at Gloucestershire Coroner’s Court.

The jury said in their findings that Ms Webb died from “complications arising from BBL procedure”.

Senior coroner Katy Skerrett said she would be writing a regulation 28 preventing future deaths report with her concerns about the lack of regulation in the aesthetics industry.

The coroner told the court: “I have already indicated in terms of preventing future deaths, I am satisfied that my duty to issue a preventing future deaths report has been triggered.”

She said wanted to “reflect” on the evidence before issuing it next week.

“I am very satisfied there’s significant risk of future deaths arising in similar circumstances, and that’s why I will be issuing a preventing future deaths report,” she added.

The inquest has heard how Mr Parke was filmed by Ms Webb carrying out the procedure on her and could be heard laughing and complaining: “I’m exhausted.”

He gave her seven times the recommended amount of filler before she collapsed and went into cardiac arrest.

Mr Parke, 34, from Manchester, was under investigation for gross negligence manslaughter in relation to Ms Webb’s death, before he died earlier this year.

He had previously been issued with prohibition notices preventing him from performing the *BBL* procedure in Dudley and Salford over concerns about his practice from environmental officers.

The procedure was carried out at the studio of another beautician, Jemma Pawlyszyn, who told the court she was doing Mr Parke a “favour” in letting him use her Studio 23 premises.

The intention was for Ms Webb, who also ran a business, to undergo training along with Ms Pawlyszyn before receiving the *BBL* procedure herself.

Ms Webb messaged her sister-in-law Morgan Townsend to say the procedure “kills” and was “painful”.

The emergency services were called after Ms Webb collapsed and she was taken to Gloucestershire Royal Hospital where she later died.

A post-mortem examination found a “gel-like material” – hyaluronic acid – was present in the fatty tissue and muscle of Ms Webb’s buttock, below the fatty tissue and muscle and along her sciatic nerve.

Hyaluronic acid was identified in Ms Webb’s lungs, brain, heart, thyroid gland and kidney.

Home Office forensic pathologist Dr Stephen Leadbetter gave her cause of death as “embolisation of hyaluronic acid from introduction into buttocks”.

During the inquest, Ms Webb’s sister April Palmer said she had developed an interest in beauty and aesthetics when training to be a hairdresser after leaving school.

“Behind her bright and caring nature, Alice quietly struggled with low self-worth,” Ms Palmer said.

“Despite being a naturally beautiful woman and hearing that often from family and friends, she found it hard to see what everyone else saw, and negative remarks weighed heavily on her.

“Hoping to feel better in herself, she underwent her first cosmetic procedure, which gave her a small lift in confidence, although, sadly, she continued trying to find that elusive perfection.

“She never needed to. She was truly beautiful inside and out.”

Last month, the Government announced a crackdown on “cosmetic cowboys” performing *BBLs*.

It also plans on bringing in a licensing scheme to ensure “lower risk” treatments such as Botox and lip fillers remain safe.

Under the new rules, treatments that involve injecting fillers anywhere other than the face, such as *BBLs*, would be legally defined as cosmetic surgical procedures – meaning they can only be performing by qualified medics.

Other high-risk treatments such as deep chemical peels and vampire facials – which use microneedling to inject platelets drawn from a patient’s own blood back into the face – would be restricted to doctors, dentists, some nurses, midwives and pharmacists.

Those who flout the rules could be hit with unlimited fines or fixed penalties of up to £4,000.

The Government will launch a consultation asking for input from doctors, regulators and businesses to draw up draft regulations.

The blueprint will then go through the parliamentary process, which is currently planned for spring 2027.
