Vladimir Putin was still grinning as he emerged from a meeting with Donald Trump focused on the war in Ukraine on Thursday, as the leaders gathered for an unprecedented summit in Shenzhen with Chinese leader Xi Jinping.

The Russian president left the tête-à-tête in an “excellent” mood, the Kremlin said, after around 40 minutes of talks on the ongoing conflict in Europe and the warming ties with Washington, which have benefitted from a recent détente.

But more than five thousands miles West, an uneasy mood hung over a meeting in Berlin, where Ukraine’s European backers had gathered in anticipation of any ground breaking disturbances from Shenzhen.

The war in Ukraine turns five in February but negotiators are still searching for a diplomatic breakthrough, with Russia vying to redraw Europe’s security architecture and absorb swathes of its neighbour to the west - a concession Ukraine categorically rejects.

![Russia's President Vladimir Putin was warmly welcomed by his “friend” US President Donald Trump in Shenzhen](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/19/8/51/bfeff330c7434a519bd3cf7322ba4555-ap.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

While Moscow’s advance has all but ground to a halt, Kyiv has developed a homegrown arsenal of weapons capable of dealing catastrophic damage to Russia on and far behind the frontlines.

A series of effectual strikes on the Kerch Bridge connecting Russian with occupied Crimea this week shows how far the Ukrainian resistance has come since the illegal invasion and annexation of the peninsula in 2014.

But both sides continue to exchange heavy strikes and this year has seen a “dramatically” higher toll of civilian casualties in Ukraine than in any year since the full-scale invasion in 2022, according to the UN.

## ‘More than 113,000’ civilian casualties since 2022

New data shows that in October 2026, the number of civilians killed - 372 - and injured - 1981 - was 95 per cent higher than in October last year, according to the UN Human Rights Monitoring Mission in Ukraine (HRMMU).

Almost 18,000 civilians have been killed and almost 58,000 have been injured in Ukraine alone since the invasion, the UN said. And the figure continues to climb as Russia ramps up its attacks on areas housing civilians.

On November 2, a school in Komyshuvakha, southeastern Ukraine, was hit by several bombs, killing at least 72 people and leaving four missing, according to local authorities.

The UNHCR said Russian long-range drone and missile strikes remain the leading cause of all civilian casualties in Ukraine, accounting for 55 per cent of the tally for October. Moscow has denied deliberately targeting civilian targets in Ukraine.

There is no comprehensive, verified figure for the number of Russian civilians killed during the war in Ukraine. But Russia’s permanent representative to the UN, Vassily Nebenzia, claimed this month that Ukrainian attacks had caused “about 37,000 civilian casualties” including 9,200 deaths and nearly 28,000 injured between February 2022 and late September 2026.

“The third quarter of 2026 was particularly bloody,” he acknowledged. “Almost 102,000 munitions were recorded to have been fired at civilian facilities in Russia, an increase of 85 per cent compared to the previous period.”

Between July and September, Nebenzia said, 631 people were killed and another 4,400 were injured, averaging 54 casualties every day.

Nebenzia did not provide evidence for his claims and it was unclear whether he was referring to Russian territory or territory in occupied Ukraine. Ukraine also denies deliberately targeting civilian targets.

Russia and Ukraine last month finally agreed to a humanitarian ceasefire to evacuate the devastated Ukrainian town of Oleshky, where some 6,000 residents had been trapped without regular access to food, water or electricity. Many were said to have resorted to cooking garden weeds and pigeons to survive.

## War dead and injured ‘swells past two million’

Russia is believed to have lost as many as 1,630,980 troops in the war since February 2022, according to the latest assessment from Ukraine’s military, emerging on Thursday. That figure includes soldiers both killed and injured in the conflict.

According to the report, Russian forces suffered 1,970 losses over the past day alone. By comparison, Moscow was taking a mean average 415 casualties each day in October 2022; 709 in October 2023; 1,354 in October 2024; and 1,008 in October 2025, based on estimates from the Center for Strategic and International Studies (CSIS).

![Russian drone strikes continue to devastate Ukraine, as pictured here in Chornomorsk, Odesa](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/15/17/2304732003..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Ukraine said in September that Russia had taken 300,000 casualties in 2026 alone - 18% of the casualties in 16% of the time since the invasion.

That toll is becoming unmanageable, according to Western officials, who in August estimated that the Kremlin was losing six thousand more troops per month than it was able to enlist. That trend has only worsened with October 2026 becoming the deadliest month for Russian combat losses since 2022, according to Ukraine’s general staff.

British intelligence estimated in September that around 500,000 Russian soldiers had been killed since the start of the invasion, while Russia’s state statistics agency Rosstat put the figure at 450,000 up to August, according to leaked data. That figure included 4,000 foreign citizens believed to have been killed while fighting for Russia.

Ukraine, like Russia, does not report on its own casualties, citing operational secrecy. But Ukrainian president Volodymyr Zelensky said late in July that Kyiv had suffered around 450,000 casualties, including 50,000 deaths and 400,000 wounded: a ninth of the deaths tallied by Rosstat a month later.

The CSIS estimated in January that Russia was then taking casualties at a ratio of around 2:1, or “2.5:1”. Modern militaries hold as a rule of thumb that an attacking ground force should outnumber defenders by around 3:1 to have a good chance of overwhelming a prepared opponent.

Putin can still draw on a much larger population to prosecute his war, even five years in. In September, he authorised another expansion of the military - the fourth this year - allowing Moscow to stand 2,441,630 units, including 1,550,500 active duty military personnel. Up to 640,000 Russian troops are currently deployed against Ukraine, the European Parliament estimated in February.

Ukraine had a comparative force of around 677,000 active duty personnel available last year, up from 209,000 in 2021, although estimates vary.
