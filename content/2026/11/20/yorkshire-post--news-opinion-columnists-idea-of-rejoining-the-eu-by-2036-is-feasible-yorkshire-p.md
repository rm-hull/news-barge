# Idea of rejoining the EU by 2036 is feasible: Yorkshire Post Letters

## James Bovington, Church Grove, Horsforth, Leeds.

I disagree with Peter Auty (The Yorkshire Post, November 11) that Andy Burnham has been deceitful about the UK’s future relationship with the EU.

I welcome the Prime Minister’s more positive view of the UK eventually rejoining the EU but would like him to agree publicly with Scotland’s new Labour leader that the UK make definite plans for Breturn. There is a UK-EU summit imminent, and Mr Burnham is simply discussing options.

I hope the UK rejoins in my lifetime, but in negotiations potential opt-outs won’t be the UK’s to surrender because no country can’t surrender what it doesn’t have. Mr Auty speaks about the UK being the loser in negotiations ‘and the worse for it.’

He can’t bring himself to acknowledge the life-enhancing benefits that younger people in particular gain through freedom of movement as they enjoy the benefits coming from linguistic, cultural and social opportunities offered by extended stays elsewhere in Europe and which the insular parties of the right casually threw away. With tacit Labour support sadly.

Hence, it was heart-warming to learn that thousands of pupils in English schools want a GCSE qualification in Romanian just as their Polish speaking friends have. Does Mr Auty remember the regimes in power in Warsaw and Bucharest some 40 years ago? Moderate even slightly progressive in Poland admittedly but authoritarian and cruel in Romania.

The freedoms now enjoyed in the former Soviet bloc countries and especially since accession to the EU in 2004 and 2007 are a wonder to behold. For reasons I’ll never grasp many English people were keen to give them up. Now’s the time to reclaim our freedom of movement and plan to rejoin and make leading in Europe the UK’s national ambition.

I hope a progressive Labour-led coalition emerges at the next general election making manifesto commitments to return to the single market and the customs union without the necessity for a referendum.

A referendum would be needed were the same coalition to propose at a later general election that the UK rejoin. The European Movement’s campaign to rejoin by 2036 is ambitious but hopefully feasible.

**William Loneskie, Justice Park, Oxton, Lauder, Berwickshire.**

Now that Prime Minister Andy Burnham has revealed his true colours and backs us re-joining the EU I think it's important to highlight some differences between the EU now and the EU in 2016.

Germany and France are both in economic difficulty - Germany because of the soaring cost of energy after the EU decided to stop its member states from importing energy from Russia, and the Nordstream pipelines were blown up; and France because of soaring national debt caused by the mismatch between tax receipts and government expenditure particularly in pensions. A UK re-entry would undoubtedly require a massive contribution from the Treasury meaning more borrowing at a time when the UK is one trillion in debt.

France is nearly ungovernable and like Italy and Spain is seeing a breakdown of law and order with rioting and looting and massive anti-government demonstrations on account of the cost of living. European police forces have become paramilitary organisations armed with tear gas and water cannon.

It is also possible that the AfD in Germany and the National Rally in France, both Eurosceptic parties, will take power and undermine the whole EU governance by its unelected Commission. They may even lead to the collapse of the whole EU project to make a "country called Europe".

In Poland its Europhile Prime Minister Donald Tusk is being challenged by President Nawrocki over the former's intention to scrap the zloty and take Poland, whose economy has been booming over the last decade, into the Euro. Poland has been successful outside the Euro while countries within it struggle.

The personnel in charge of the EU project do not inspire confidence. Macron and Merz are very unpopular leaders while the EU's top diplomat, Kaja Kallas, has gone on record saying that Russia must be broken up into small states. How does she think Putin will respond to threats like that?

Make no mistake UK re-entry would include Britain ditching the pound and adopting a foreign currency. A country which does not control its currency is not really a sovereign country. Burnham seems more concerned about Europe the Ukraine and the ridiculously named "fight against climate change" than he is with domestic policy.

**Jarvis Browning, Fadmoor, York.**

Of course our PM would say Brexit has not worked, he doesn’t want to see the other side of the coin. We’ve had more trade outside the EU and at the moment we’ve a very good strong pound against the dollar and the euro; that must be telling us something.

Why change it while the going is good? We are lions, we don’t need to chase a sick dog of Europe.

If they really want us to rejoin, then bend the rules and make it more favourable. I doubt they will as they don’t want to be seen as weak.
