Donald Trump, Vladimir Putin and Xi Jinping took aim at the United Nations and the International Criminal Court as they met for an unprecedented trilateral summit in China on Thursday.

The leaders of the United States, Russia and China emerged from 90 minutes of closed-door talks with a four-point ‘peace declaration’ relishing in their shared distrust of the international bodies.

They pledged to advance reforms of the UN, to increase supplies of fuel products for all countries, to uphold the immunity enjoyed by state officials in international courts, and to avoid nuclear war.

But the leaders left Shenzhen with no tangible plans on how to achieve their objectives, and the Kremlin played down the importance of Ukraine during their trilateral talks.

Putin also admitted afterwards that the summit did not set ambitious goals and that nobody would be forced to comply with its decisions, as he vowed fresh strikes on Ukraine’s energy infrastructure.

![The leaders issued a four-point peace declaration in Shenzhen on Thursday (L to R: Putin, Xi and Trump)](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/19/15/2305279486..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

In their joint declaration, the leaders firstly committed to reforming the UN so that it could “respond more effectively to global challenges” and help maintain international peace and security”.

But there was no detail on what those reforms would look like, or how they would be agreed. Trump has attacked the UN for failing, in his view, to help him broker lasting peace agreements in foreign conflicts.

But his alternative Board of Peace, overseeing the end of the war in Gaza, has also struggled in its main objective, with Israel only this week carrying out strikes and a ground operation in the strip.

Secondly, the leaders said they would increase the production and supply of refined petroleum products “for the benefit of all nations”, stressing the importance of stable energy flows.

Trump’s war in Iran has contributed to record high energy prices and forced nations around the world to rebalance delicate supply chains.

Strikes on energy infrastructure in Russia and Ukraine have also contributed to rising energy prices, but analysts have said the impact was “minimal” until the war in the Middle East broke out in February.

![Returning from China, Donald Trump disembarks Air Force One at Joint Base Andrews, Maryland on November 19](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/20/07/2305377948..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Thirdly, the leaders reaffirmed the importance of immunity for state officials and said that the International Criminal Court and other similar bodies must not abuse their jurisdiction.

President Trump said the ICC, which has an arrest warrant out for his “friend” Putin for alleged war crimes, was “out of control”, condemning what he cast as excessive overreach.

The ICC also has a warrant out for the arrest of Israeli prime minister Benjamin Netanyahu for alleged war crimes in Gaza. Netanyahu is a close ally of Washington and went to war with Trump in Iran.

Fourthly, the leaders “reaffirmed that a nuclear war cannot be won and must never be fought” and said they would “conduct a dialogue on reducing nuclear risks and preventing proliferation of nuclear weapons”.

But no time frame was given for the talks, and the leaders announced no plans for arms control talks long sought by Trump.

A nuclear arms reduction treaty between the US and Russia expired in February, ending the last legally binding limits between the world’s two largest nuclear powers. Putin had warned recently that he could use nuclear weapons if the West tried to cut off Kaliningrad from the rest of Russia.

![Trump made the comments while at an APEC summit in Shenzhen, China, which saw him meeting Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/19/11/download.-2..jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The summit drew comparisons to the 1945 Yalta Conference between the UK, US and USSR that defined the global order after WWII and was seen to ratify Soviet control of occupied countries.

Indeed, Trump said the summit would be “bigger than Yalta” as Europe’s liberal democracies watched on anxiously from a parallel meeting in Berlin held in solidarity with Ukraine.

Some analysts said the outcome of the summit may actually diminish Trump’s stature on the world stage as he enters the lame-duck phase of his presidency, while bolstering two of Washington’s adversaries.

“Trump has always loved the pageantry and the sense that he's sitting down with the most important people in the world, and he got that moment,” said Christopher Preble, a senior fellow at the Stimson Center think tank in Washington.

“But as in other summits, it’s hard to see any substantive progress made on any major issue.”

And while Yalta lasted eight days and still resonates eight decades later, Shenzhen was over in less than two hours, with a five-paragraph outlook whose lasting historical significance has yet to be seen.

![British Prime Minister Andy Burnham and Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky in Berlin on Thursday](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/19/18/2305348465..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Still, in comments that would ring alarm bells in Taiwan, President Xi urged his peers against interference in “domestic affairs”, saying the others should avoid red lines on “sensitive issues”.

And Putin - who insisted explicitly that there had been no “dividing the world into spheres of influence” - revived his rhetoric of “[coming] to terms with the realities on the ground” - a phrase frequently used to press his demands for peace terms that Ukraine says would amount to its capitulation.

Trump and Putin discussed the war at length during a bilateral summit, the Kremlin said, but the issue was barely mentioned with Xi, who has cast the intractable conflict as a European problem.

And in spite of overtures to peace, Putin emerged from the day of talks threatening harsh retaliation on Kyiv for a series of paralysing strikes on the bridge connecting Russia with Crimea this week.
