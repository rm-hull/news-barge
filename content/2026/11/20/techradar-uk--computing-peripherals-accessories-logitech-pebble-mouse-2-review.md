### TechRadar Verdict

The Logitech Pebble Mouse 2 makes for the perfect companion when you’re on the go thanks to its compact size, silent click technology and seemingly never-ending battery life. While I couldn’t see it becoming my primary device as it doesn’t offer much in the way of additional features, but, at this price point, I can’t argue.

The Logitech Pebble Mouse 2’s is a mouse that's primary purpose is to be portable. It’s the perfect device to take on the go with you but this does come at a slight detriment in the form of its ergonomics and any other flashy features.

It’s slimline at just over 0.98 inches / 25mm tall when sat on your desk but is relatively flat when it comes to overall shape. There’s a small incline, making it slightly taller towards the back end that sits in your palm, but this is in no way comparable to a normal curved mouse shape, which sits snugly and comfortably in your hand, particularly if you opt for a claw grip.

![Side profile of a hand resting in a fingertip grip on the slim, pale pink Logitech Pebble Mouse 2 M350s on a wooden desk.](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/i3BMu4oAiYg3v2SZpPCFK3-1200-80.jpg)

When holding and using the device, the shape is something you have to get used to with time but I barely noticed the difference after a while. Its diminutive size is something that's likely only suited to smaller hands, as it can become uncomfortable using the device for long periods of time for those with larger ones. However, in turn, the size of the mouse is absolutely perfect for those who need a reliable mouse on the go — it's so small it can fit in your pocket without any issue.

You can pick up the Logitech Pebble Mouse 2 in Pink, Black or White. There are also currently two limited designs on offer: the Study Buddy and the Coffee Cat, both created by designer EdgyKatrina. While already adorable in form factor, these limited edition designs take the mouse to the next level in terms of style — as a cat person, the Coffee Cat edition really tugged on my heart strings.

You don’t get any extra buttons on this mouse, featuring just the right click, left click and the scroll wheel, which can also be pressed as a third button. You can reprogram the middle click using the Logi Options+ app that's available on both Windows and Mac.

![Pale pink Logitech Pebble Mouse 2 M350s with cat and red mug artwork sitting on a blue-lit desk mat beside a white mechanical keyboard.](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/qamXpHbDtuNPKxDvgQczt-1200-80.jpg)

Contributing to the portability is the silent touch technology, which makes both buttons and the scroll wheel significantly quieter than a normal mouse button. This makes it great to use when you’re commuting or in a situation where you don’t want to disturb those around you. Another benefit is that they feel satisfying to press — not in a mechanical clicky way but more of a haptic touch style.

The mouse is powered by a single AA battery that can be replaced by removing the faceplate of the mouse, which is attached via magnets and can simply be pulled off and placed back on. Logitech claims that the mouse can provide up to 24 months of battery life based on computing conditions — something that's pretty much impossible for me to verify right now. I’ll get back to you in a couple of years and confirm.

When it comes to connectivity, the mouse offers Bluetooth as an option but it can be connected to three devices at once and switched between all three using a button on the base. I found this particularly useful when I was traveling with both my laptop and iPad, as it allowed me to pretty seamlessly control both with one device. It does work with the Logi Bolt USB receiver, but this needs to be bought separately.

The feet of the mouse don’t offer as smooth of a glide as my usual mouse — the Razer Cobra Pro — or even any of the other gaming mice I have tested. It’s not something that is off-putting in any way but is still worth noting considering it definitely feels like it has the smallest bit of resistance when using it.

Outside of this, using the mouse felt pretty flawless for its seemingly intended purpose: being used on the go. I was able to stuff it in my bag, grab it when needed and put it to use across a range of devices whenever I needed. Despite being small, it felt comfortable to use once I got used to the shape and size.

![Low-profile side view of the pink Logitech Pebble Mouse 2 resting on a wooden desk next to the Razer Cobra Pro gaming mouse.](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/2igw2zrnGTG9c2EZEgAJG3-1200-80.jpg)

It’s fantastic to not have to worry about the battery. The Logitech Pebble 2 can sit for — at least what feels like — an eternity at the bottom of my backpack, ready for whenever I next need it without me having to charge it between work days or trips.

While I enjoy using the mouse and will continue to rely on it when I am away from home, I can’t see it becoming a staple on my desk, especially considering how much I game and require a device that stacks up. Unfortunately, the lack of extra buttons and ergonomic design, as well as being limited to Bluetooth connectivity without an additional purchase, lets it down. However, at the asking price of £14.99 / $19.99 / AU$54.00, it’s cheap enough to be considered an additional accessory that you can reserve specifically for on the go usage – which is what I will be doing.

## Logitech Pebble Mouse 2 review: price and availability

* **$19.99 / £14.99 / AU$29**
* **Available now**
* **Offers limited edition designs which are only held while stocks last**

The Logitech Pebble Mouse 2 is a budget-friendly device, coming in at $19.99 / £14.99 / AU$29. While it originally sold at £24.99 / $29.99 / AU$54 on release, the price has since come down and this also applies to the limited edition designs. This makes it cheap enough to pick up as a secondary device specifically for portability.

If you’re looking to pick up the standard edition, it’s available in the majority of regions through the Logitech store or official partner retailers like Amazon, BestBuy and JB Hi-Fi. The limited edition design is limited to the UK and US as well as other select markets, but can only be purchased while stocks last.

Similar lines include the Logitech Pop Mouse, which currently comes in at a slightly higher price point of £19.99 / $29.99, making the Pebble Mouse 2 a better priced alternative if you’re hoping to save some cash.
