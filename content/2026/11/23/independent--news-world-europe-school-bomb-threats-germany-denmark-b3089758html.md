Police in Denmark and Germany are investigating a raft of bomb threats directed at schools across the two countries.

They are the latest in a string of threats levelled at schools, airlines and airports, and news organisations across Europe in recent months.

Cyberattacks have also hit supermarkets and blood banks.

Danish police say that there is currently no reason to be concerned.

“It is currently not the police's view that the threats are credible, but they understandably create insecurity locally at the schools,” they said.

![People gather outside a Naples school on 11 November following reports of bomb threats across Italy](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/23/10/2026-11-17T120436Z_1666661583_RC2OLOAVI3RA_RTRMADP_3_EUROPE-SECURITY-SCHOOLS-ITALY.JPG?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&auto=webp)

There was currently no reason to evacuate any schools, or to implement any special safety measures, they added.

The bomb threats were received via email, and police said that they were in contact with several schools and municipalities.

German news organisation *Bild* reported that hundreds of schools across the country had received the bomb threats.

“At present, there is no evidence to suggest that the schools contacted so far are facing any specific threat,” police in the southwestern German city of Freiburg said in a statement, adding that 450 schools were affected across the country.

Berlin police gave similar advice, while police in the state of Thuringia said separately that they told schools to continue as normal because the perpetrators' apparent intention was to scare without taking action.

![Passengers were hit with delays after threats against flights leaving from and arriving at Prague Airport](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/23/11/2026-11-06T133339Z_411554756_RC2DIOAED1ZM_RTRMADP_3_EUROPE-FLIGHTS-CZECH.JPG?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&auto=webp)

Last week, about a dozen schools in Spain were evacuated after receiving bomb threats by email.

A police spokesperson said that the messages were sent straight to the schools. Specialised police units inspected the sites and found no suspicious devices.

Hundreds of schools in Scotland and Northern Ireland were also targeted by hoax bomb threats earlier this month.

Police Scotland said threatening messages were sent to distribution lists, but nothing suggested they posed a credible threat or any danger to the public.

The hoax threats, which originated from outside of the UK, caused dozens of schools to evacuate their students and staff, according to reports.

Police Service of Northern Ireland (PSNI) said more than 300 schools in Northern Ireland had reported a “concerning email” to police.

Some 6,300 schools in Italy also received bomb threats via email on the same day and hundreds were evacuated by authorities.
