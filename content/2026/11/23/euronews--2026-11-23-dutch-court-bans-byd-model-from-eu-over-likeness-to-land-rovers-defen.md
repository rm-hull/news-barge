## The Chinese carmaker plans to appeal, and the ruling doesn't stop the car going on sale in Britain in January.

Jaguar Land Rover (JLR) on Monday welcomed a ruling by a court in The Hague, the Netherlands, that bars Chinese carmaker BYD from selling its Ti7 SUV in the European Union because it copies the Land Rover Defender too closely.

On Friday, the district court found that the Ti7 shares distinctive and protected elements of the Defender L663's design. These include the curved "shoulders" of the bodywork, the rounded corners and wheel arches, and the overall silhouette.

The ruling bans BYD from importing, manufacturing, stocking or selling the Ti7 anywhere in the EU. When products are sold across the bloc, a national court in Europe can issue intellectual property rulings that apply throughout the EU.

However, the ban does not cover the United Kingdom. The court said it had no jurisdiction over BYD's British arm, and it rejected JLR's claims based on its UK design rights.

The Ti7 went on sale in China last year. It is not yet available in the EU, but deliveries to Britain are expected to begin in January, according to the court.

"JLR continues to invest in creativity and innovation. We protect our intellectual property and take legal action when necessary," a JLR spokesperson told Euronews. "Legal proceedings are ongoing and we won't be commenting further at this time."

BYD said it would appeal.

"BYD strongly disagrees with today's judgment and will appeal it. The BYD Ti7 is the result of BYD's own independent design work and infringes none of JLR's rights," the company said in a statement.

BYD's shares fell 4 percent in Hong Kong earlier on Monday and closed the session 1.9 percent lower.
