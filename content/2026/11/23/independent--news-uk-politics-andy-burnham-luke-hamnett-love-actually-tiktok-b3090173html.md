Andy Burnham has been labelled "unserious" after an influencer was invited into Downing Street to recreate Hugh Grant’s famous prime ministerial dance scene from the film *Love, Actually*.

In a video shared online recently, Luke Hamnett is shown dancing through No 10 and down the stairs to the Pointer Sisters’ *Jump (For My Love)*.

He halts upon suddenly spotting the Prime Minister seated and ready to conduct an interview with him.

Kemi Badenoch has criticised the clip, describing the content as "jarring".

She said: “When I was a new MP, we had to hand in our phones as we stepped into 10 Downing St, so it’s jarring to see a Labour PM turn it into a bit of a joke.

“Hugh Grant could dance through No 10 in Love Actually because he was pretending to be Prime Minister. Andy Burnham actually IS the Prime Minister.

“At a time when Trump, Xi and Putin are projecting power on the world stage, and people across Britain are anxious about the future, this looks extremely unserious.

“Being relatable doesn’t mean treating the public as though they are children who need entertaining.”

![Andy Burnham interviewed was interviewed by TikTok and Instagram creator Luke Hamnett](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/23/10/59/Screenshot-2026-11-23-at-10-13-20.png?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Conservative former minister Esther McVey told the Commons: “Perhaps the Prime Minister’s time would be better spent polishing up his negotiating skills rather than concentrating on the choreography of a dance routine of a TikTok influencer.”

Ms McVey was referring to a youth mobility scheme, which will allow 100,000 Europeans aged 18 to 31 to live in the UK for up to three years as part of the agreements struck between Mr Burnham and EU leaders last week.

The Prime Minister’s official spokesperson said: “The Prime Minister has been very open about his desire to communicate with the public in a variety of different ways and on the channels that they use and you’ve seen that reflected in (the) Prime Minister’s comms since coming into office.

“And I think that particular piece of content was part of an interview, as you say, but the Prime Minister… engages with communications on a variety of different channels, which is a reflection of how the public gets this information these days.”

Another video emerged later of influencer Max Balegde filming himself outside the door of No 10, mouthing along “bomboclaat p\*\*\*\* wagon chicken nuggets” to what appeared to be a viral meme song.
