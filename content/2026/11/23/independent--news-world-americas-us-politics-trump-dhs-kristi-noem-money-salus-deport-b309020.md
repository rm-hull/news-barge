Donald Trump’s administration appeared to have cut off a government contractor from running its “self-deportation” program after blowing through tens of millions of dollars as part of a contract worth nearly $1 billion.

But the Department of Homeland Security handed the company another $150 million this month, according to government procurement records.

Salus Worldwide Solutions — founded by a donor to ousted DHS Secretary Kristi Noem, whose handling of contracts at the agency came under bipartisan scrutiny leading to Trump firing her — was awarded a no-bid aviation contract last year despite no prior federal contracting experience .

The company was tapped to run the administration’s Project Homecoming, which offered $1,000 and a flight out of the country to immigrants who sign an agreement to “self-deport.”

In September, reportedly unimpressed with the company’s performance, DHS under Noem’s successor Markwayne Mullin awarded a separate contract — potentially worth more than $2 billion — to rival firm Response AI Solutions, *The Daily Beast*’s PunchUp first reported.

![A government contractor with ties to Kristi Noem was awarded another $150M despite DHS cutting ties with the firm for the administration’s self-deportation efforts](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/23/20/2296072924..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Salus filed a challenge to that decision with the Government Accountability Office last month.

But on November 10, 11 days before its existing agreement was supposed to come to an end, DHS approved another $150 million for Salus, PunchUp first reported.

That new injection of cash brings the government’s total commitment to the company to at least $898 million, according to federal spending records.

Salus was founded by William Walters III, a former State Department official and a political donor to the president and Noem. Walters donated $10,000 to the Noem-backing American Resolve super PAC in 2024.

Last year, the company received a contract worth up to $915 million to support the administration’s self-deportation plan, intended to boost the numbers of immigrant removals as federal agencies pump enormous resources into deporting tens of thousands of people from the country.

![DHS had previously awarded a Noem-linked firm a contract worth up to $915M but the agency under new Secretary Markwayne Mullin has tapped a rival firm to support the government’s mass deportation efforts](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/04/22/2026-10-29T002633Z_699032352_RC2EANAXZXR7_RTRMADP_3_USA-CYBER-DHS-LEAK.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Daedalus Aviation Corp. received a separate $464 million no-bid contract to buy 10 airplanes that were reportedly kept out of service or have been used to ferry around Trump administration officials instead.

Noem has also disputed reporting that her agency vastly overpaid one Walters’ company for five airplanes at a substantial markup just hours before she left office.

In a furious social media post last month, Noem demanded that *The New York Times* “entirely” retract claims that the agency spent $108 million for a luxuriously appointed Boeing 737 that the company bought months earlier from a seller who asked for less than $90 million.

Noem also said that characterizing Walters a “donor” was “materially misleading” and "conveys a false implication of a direct financial relationship obviously intended to influence official action."

Democrats on the Senate Appropriations Committee, which is reviewing the agency’s “wasteful use” of taxpayer dollars, said earlier this year that DHS appears to be “running an in-house private jet service.”

Previous reporting has alleged that Salus had approached subcontracting firms with proposals to perform work for DHS only if it would then direct lucrative kickbacks to Noem’s chief aide Corey Lewandowski or consulting firms connected to him.

The Trump administration has cited “emergency” conditions hundreds of times to rush through no-bid contracts, dozens of which are under congressional scrutiny and the subject of federal lawsuits.

The agency has failed to use its massive pile of taxpayer funding to “actually improve the lives of Americans by requiring basic compliance with the Fourth Amendment, acquiring and deploying body-worn cameras to every law enforcement officer in the field, mandating minimum officer identification, or properly training new hires,” according to a group of Democratic senators demanding investigations.

Instead, “this administration has lit taxpayer dollars on fire by acquiring new and unnecessary planes to jet Trump officials around in extreme luxury that the vast majority of Americans will never experience,” they said.

![Democrats in Congress are calling on Trump’s anti-immigration chief Stephen Miller and other top administration officials, as well as Noem, to preserve records ahead of investigations next year](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/23/20/2306101178..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Last week, incoming House Oversight Chairman Robert Garcia demanded White House chief of staff Stephen Miller, Noem and Lewandowski preserve records ahead of Democratic investigations into DHS spending and alleged misconduct.

More than two-thirds of the more than 980,000 deportations in the first 18 months of the second Trump term involved immigrants initially intercepted at the U.S-Mexico border, but deportations resulting from arrests in the country’s interior have accounted for a rising share of all removals, according to a recent analysis from the Migration Policy Institute.

But the overall impact of the government’s “self-deportation” campaign has been difficult to measure independently under Homeland Security’s reduced immigration enforcement reporting.

Last year, DHS declared that 1.6 million people self-deported, a figure that the Center for Migration Studies has called a “self-serving fantasy.”

That figure would account for 15 percent of the estimated total population of unauthorized immigrants in the U.S., or about 200,000 people per month voluntarily leaving the country, according to the nonpartisan think tank.
