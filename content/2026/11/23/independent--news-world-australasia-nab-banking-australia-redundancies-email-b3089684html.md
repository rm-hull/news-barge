National Australia Bank has been forced to apologise to staff after some learned they had been laid off through an automated email.

The bank, which last month announced plans to slash 2,500 jobs in the next year, said the emails were "mistakenly sent" to some employees who had requested a voluntary redundancy package as part of the restructure, days before they were due to be formally notified by their managers.

The emails were not meant to be sent before staff had received an outcome of the process directly.

The bank said it would bring forward those conversations following the error.

“These emails were not intended to be sent before any colleagues had received their outcome of the process directly," NAB said in a statement that was posted to its internal company platform on Monday and shared with Reuters.

"We are very sorry this happened, and that receiving an automated email this way would be upsetting and unsettling to many.

![A National Australia Bank (NAB) branch in Sydney](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/23/09/2026-11-23T065345Z_2131057265_RC2OBEAT0Y2T_RTRMADP_3_NAB-LAYOFFS.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“This is not the experience we wanted any colleague to have, and we sincerely apologise for the distress this error has caused to any who received these emails.”

The bank is said to be investigating how the automated emails were sent.

The cuts at Australia's biggest business lender and third-largest home lender represent about six per cent of its workforce and are aimed at simplifying operations and lowering costs.

NAB has said up to 1,500 jobs would ​go by the end of the year, with the cuts mainly focused on corporate roles.

The bank is one of many major companies in Australia, including airline Qantas and tech firms Canva, WiseTech and Atlassian, which have announced job cuts this year.

Australian Treasurer Jim Chalmers warned on Monday that the nation's jobless rate would rise above five per cent in the coming months after hitting a five-year high of 4.9 per cent, while economic growth was expected to slow to below two per cent as rate hikes rippled through the economy.
