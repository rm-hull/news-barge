# Rejoining the EU is no solution to our problems: Yorkshire Post Letters

## Thomas W Jefferson, Batty Lane, Howden, Goole.

Some further candid commentary from Gareth Robson (The Yorkshire Post, November 17).

It’s more than we get from the handful of Rejoiners who regularly pepper these columns, too defensive to admit what they really want.

Apparently it’s a “psycho-drama of national delusion” to believe in national sovereignty.

The democratic nation, which has underwritten peace and prosperity in the free-world, is no longer up to the job so we have to huddle into the EU and if only we had shown less “strutting national ego”, Europe would by now have been a “better-integrated power bloc” able to face-down the others.

Isn’t the truth that the EU’s failings have contributed to causing the security problems we face?

Failed economic policy brought low growth which, together with weak political leadership, caused its members to underfund NATO by up to a trillion euros.

That emboldened Putin’s aggression and left Europe unable to fulfil its traditional roll of keeping essential sea lanes open, not to mention causing a rift with America.

There is no point in Rejoiners coming into these columns pretending that the EU is the answer to any of the problems we face.

Its failings are part of the problem.

**Jas Olak, Vice Chair, Leeds for Europe, Roundhay, Leeds.**

JA King tries to justify his opposition to freedom of movement and rejoining the European Union by claiming “unemployment is rapidly rising” (The Yorkshire Post, November 16). It isn’t. UK unemployment fell to 4.7 per cent in the three months to August.

That wasn’t a blip, either (Jobless at lowest rate for over a year, The Yorkshire Post, October 21). Even if Mr King’s claim were true, rising unemployment would deter overseas jobseekers even under freedom of movement.

He forgets freedom of movement works both ways. Defenders of Brexit love nostalgia, so they should remember the TV programme "Auf Wiedersehen, Pet". When domestic jobs dried up, British workers had the freedom to travel to Europe to find work and support their families.

Without it, UK musicians today find it almost impossible to tour Europe and young Brits are excluded from seasonal work where an EU passport is a minimum requirement.

It is ironic that Mr King talks about EU membership “opening the floodgates” when migration only peaked after Brexit. We largely swapped single, young Europeans wanting to work here temporarily for people willing to migrate much further to get here, understandably on a more settled basis and often with dependents.

Mr King accuses Leeds for Europe members of “bombarding” you with letters. The same is true of Mr King and other regular pro-Brexit correspondents, of course. But Prime Minister Andy Burnham wants a wide debate on what our future European relationship should look like.

Time then for other groups and individuals who have never written before, or at least not recently, to get in touch?
