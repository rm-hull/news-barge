Australian entertainment icon Molly Meldrum died “peacefully” on Monday in a Melbourne hospital after a long health battle. He was 83.

Best known for presenting the ABC music programme *Countdown* between 1974 and 1987, Meldrum helped introduce Australian audiences to international artists like Abba, Blondie and Madonna.

“We have lost a true Australian icon, a man who opened doors for so many and entertained millions of people here and around the world,” Meldrum’s longtime friend and manager Mark Klemens said.

“He was unconventional and wonderfully unpredictable, but always caring, always funny and always down to earth, with a passion the rest of us could only envy,” he added. “He made the most of his life, and boy, was it a full one. Rest in peace, Molly.”

![Molly Meldrum](https://static.the-independent.com/2026/11/23/07/1469640682..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

His partner Yan Wongngam and family described Meldrum as the “heartbeat, the champion and the spirit of Australian music”, saying he had influenced generations of musicians and music fans.

“Affectionately dubbed ‘the Guru’, he shaped not only the sound of a nation but also the dreams of generations who grew up with his voice guiding them,” they said in a statement.

“Yan cared for Molly with great love and devotion, especially through his final years,” they added. “The family is deeply grateful to him, to Molly’s many loyal friends, and to the staff at Epworth Richmond for their wonderful care.”

The music journalist, TV presenter and producer will be recognised with a state funeral.

Victoria premier Ben Carroll said Meldrum’s family had accepted a state funeral for him.

“All of our thoughts as Victorians and indeed Australians are with Molly Meldrum’s family and his friends, and we know how wide that network was,” Mr Carroll said.

Gavin Wood, who narrated *Countdown* and occasionally filled in as presenter, recalled his time working with Meldrum as “the wildest ride I ever had”.

He described Meldrum as “a life force” and one of Australia’s “greatest characters”.

“There was not one other person in this country that could have done what Molly did and you would have to have been slightly mad to do it,” he said.

“Molly had the passion and he had the love, he was just an all-round great guy and everybody loved him.”

Meldrum was also a passionate advocate for homegrown talent, giving emerging Australian performers a national platform. Artists including INXS, Kylie Minogue, Skyhooks and Mental As Anything benefited from exposure on the programme.

Australian prime minister Anthony Albanese praised Meldrum for championing local musicians and paid tribute to him on X.

“With an interview style that was inimitable and an enthusiasm that was uncontainable, Molly Meldrum loved music almost as much as we loved him,” he said.

“On Countdown and beyond, he championed Australian artists and brought international acts to our shores. He gave new artists a leg up and established artists a welcoming embrace. And over the years as he implored us to do ourselves a favour, he became a central part of the Australian soundtrack. We were lucky to have him. May Ian ‘Molly’ Meldrum rest in peace.”

![Molly Meldrum](https://static.the-independent.com/2026/11/23/07/2026-11-23T041037Z_456610248_RC2FTOAUNQRM_RTRMADP_3_PEOPLE-MOLLY-MELDRUM.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

British singer Elton John once described him as the “single most important influence in Australian pop music”.

“I don’t think anyone has done more for music in Australia than Molly Meldrum,” he said in 2019.

Meldrum leaves behind his partner Yan Wongngam, an adopted son, and two younger brothers.

Meldrum was one of the first openly gay figures on Australian television and became known for his unconventional interviewing style and unpredictable on-screen antics.

Recalling a chaotic interview with him in 1977, then Prince Charles joked during the 40th anniversary celebrations of *Countdown* in 2014: “There’s an old show business saying which warns never to work with animals or children, but nobody prepared me for Molly Meldrum.”

Meldrum received the Order of Australia in 1986 and was inducted into the Logie Hall of Fame in 2012 and the Aria Hall of Fame in 2014.
