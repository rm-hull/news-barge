**A 20ft-long Cold War era missile perched on the back of a truck in a field has become a hit on TikTok - and revealed a love story behind it.**

Photos and videos of the striking sight near Llangollen, Denbighshire, spread across social media, prompting speculation, theories and curious comments from people wondering how it got there and why.

But for the man who owns it, the story has nothing to do with secret military operations or preparations for war - it is about him using an unusual hobby to honour his late wife.

Adrian Hyde, 59, admits it has now "all just snowballed and I've got carried away" and has spent about £200,000 on numerous vehicles.

Military enthusiasts Adrian and his wife Janette spent years talking about owning a military vehicle and planned to buy a tank as a joint 50th birthday present.

"We had all the area and ground ready, the driveway ready. We knew exactly where we were going to buy it from. We were going to buy a Russian one from Poland," said Adrian.

But this dream was put on hold when Janette was diagnosed with cancer in 2016.

Despite having cancer three times, Adrian said she never lost her determination and the couple "kept saying 'we'll eventually get that tank'. I think that's what kept us going".

Janette died in January 2025 at the age of 57, and Adrian found himself returning to their shared dream and bought the tank three months later.

"The first week after she passed, I said 'I'm going to get that tank she wanted'."

Adrian bought a CVRT scimitar, external and had it restored by "some military guys in Rugby" who made it road legal.

He named it Janbabes, after his wife.

Then, after getting a call from a military vehicles company telling him they had an armoured Land Rover, he bought that as well.

Adrian said word got round about his collection, leading to a call from a friend saying he had a 1957 AFS Bedford RL missile launcher and a Thunderbird missile, external for sale.

But because the driveway was full, he asked his friend John if he could put the missile launcher in his field, about 100 yards from his house and he said yes.

Adrian said the bug then got him and he bought a Special Forces Polaris 6 MRZR 4, external, which is a project for next summer, with the collecting and restoration helping him cope with his grief.

"I'll be in the front room and look at the tank and think: 'That's part of you dear. That's Janbabes sitting there'."

The unusual sight quickly attracted attention locally before exploding online after a video posted, external by a hiker and content creator went "absolutely viral" on TikTok and Instagram.

Adrian said: "I've had people ringing me about it. I've got loads of people trying to get friendly on Facebook. It's gone crazy.

"To me it's natural, I have always grown up with military kit, but I suppose it is different."

He said many people have commented saying the missile was live, but that was "ludicrous".

"It's all inactive. It's from the 1950s but in mint condition," he said.

The Thunderbird was withdrawn from service in the 1980s, according to the Imperial War Museum.

"The missile is a Thunderbird missile from the Cold War era, but everything has been stripped off it.

"The inside is hollow. It's got the original outside shell but the inside all the electronics, explosives, jets, they've all been taken off and decommissioned."

A friend made the frame for it and bolted it on the back of a 1957 Bedford truck.

While many may see his military collection as odd, Adrian said it represented a dream he and Janette talked about for years, but never had the chance to realise together.

"It's quite emotional because it was our dream for 10 years. Janette would have loved it."

But the military equipment is not the only way Adrian has honoured his wife.

Adrian said he hopes to sell the missile and truck, raising money for a cat charity, which is "what Janette would have wanted as she absolutely loved cats"

Following her death, he bought and donated an ambulance to cat charity Candy Tibby Trust in Wrexham, filling it with cat food and raising £3,000 in Janette's memory.

He hopes the sale of the missile and truck can do something similar as his wife was a cat lover.

As for what's next on the list, Adrian said he "doesn't know what yet".

"There are always plans to go buy something. It all depends on what crops up.

Whatever arrives next, its origins can be traced back to a husband keeping alive a dream he once shared with his wife.
