Business leaders are urging City of York Council to offer fresh parking incentives for residents, warning that steep charges are driving local shoppers away.

Since hourly parking rates at council car parks were hiked in April 2025, independent shops, cafes, and pubs in York city centre have reported a noticeable drop in footfall from residents.

Following a review, Minster Badge holders now receive a 50% discount at community car parks outside the city centre.

The York BID (Business Improvement District) wants the council to consider how similar incentives could support trade in the city’s retail heart.

Andrew Lowson, York BID’s executive director, said: “There’s so much happening in the city centre.

“We’re seeing new businesses open alongside the shops, restaurants and pubs that have become much loved parts of our high street.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/Andrew-Lowson-at-York-BID-AGM-2026.webp)

“So many traders are investing in their offer and working hard to bring customers through the door.

“However, we’re hearing from local traders that higher parking charges are putting local people off, especially when they only want to come in for an hour or two.

“Those visits matter. They’re people popping into a shop, meeting a friend for coffee or staying for lunch.

“We understand the financial pressures the council faces, and we support better sustainable transport.

“We also need to look at what can be done now to encourage local people into the city centre. The changes at community car parks give us a starting point for that discussion.”

## ‘Businesses need progress’

Council representatives were at the BID board meeting. BID Directors called for further consideration of resident incentives for city centre car parks, alongside clearer information on how council car parks are being used.

With a reduction in parking charges appearing unlikely at present, the BID is seeking commitments on other ways to improve access, including the proposed extension of Park & Ride operating hours.

It has also asked for car park occupancy figures and Minster Badge uptake to be published, to help establish where incentives could make a difference and assess their impact.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/12/marygate-car-park-york.jpg)

City of York Council’s executive member for transport Cllr Kate Ravilious said: “We appreciate the BID’s constructive approach to this issue and have listened to its concerns.

“There are different measures that could help, and we need to consider each carefully, including as part of current budget setting discussions, before making any decision.

“We share the BID’s commitment to wanting to get as many residents and visitors into York to spend their money and support local businesses as possible”.

Michael Hjort, chair of York BID, added: “York has a huge amount to offer, and we want residents to be able to enjoy it regularly. For businesses, local customers who come back week after week make a real difference.

“We’d really like to work with the council to agree which of the proposed measures can be taken forward and when. Businesses need to see progress, and we’ll keep bringing their experiences to the table as those plans develop.”
