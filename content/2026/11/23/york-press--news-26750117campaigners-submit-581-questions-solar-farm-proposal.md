## Campaigners submit 580 questions over 1,000-acre solar farm

Campaigners fighting plans for a large solar farm near Thirsk have submitted more than 580 questions to the developer amid concerns that the countryside could become a “tinfoil vista”.

Protect Hambleton Association (PHA) has sent a 163-page response to the company behind the proposed 350MW Sedgeby Solar Farm, which would cover around 1,000 acres of farmland near the villages of Sessay, Little Sessay and Thormanby.

The group says its questions are based on more than 270 issues identified by the Planning Inspectorate in its scoping opinion on the development earlier this year.

Campaigners say the proposed solar farm, covering approximately 420 hectares, would occupy almost a third of Sessay parish.

The application will be determined by the Secretary of State rather than North Yorkshire Council due to its size.

The application has been submitted by Sedgeby Solar Limited, a subsidiary of renewable energy company BayWa r.e. UK Ltd.

Campaigners say they are particularly concerned about the loss of agricultural land, the visual impact of solar panels across the Vale of Mowbray, construction traffic and potential effects on wildlife and flooding.

According to the group, the developer's soil survey found that almost three-quarters of the surveyed site, around 288 hectares, consists of Grade 2 and 3a farmland, classified as Best and Most Versatile agricultural land.

PHA argues that taking the land out of conventional food production for up to 40 years would have significant consequences for local agriculture.

The group is also concerned about the cumulative impact of other solar developments proposed or approved nearby, including schemes at Husthwaite, Raskelf, Boscar and Pilmoor.

Mike Willis, chairman of Protect Hambleton Association, said: “Our community has the same deep connection to what James Herriot called ‘the finest view in England’ as every other village along the Hambleton corridor.

“If schemes at Husthwaite and Raskelf go ahead, to add to those at Boscar and Pilmoor, and then Sedgeby, solar panels on the ground will gradually take over.

“Our heritage landscape will become a tinfoil vista, one field at a time.”

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21746344.jpg?type=mds-article-620)More than 70 residents attended a public meeting organised by the group at Sessay Village Hall. *(Image: Protect Hambleton Association)*

The campaign group has also raised concerns about heavy construction vehicles using narrow rural roads near Sessay School, St Cuthbert’s Church and the Grade II-listed bridge at Little Sessay.

Other issues include possible effects on groundwater, drainage, the nearby Pilmoor Site of Special Scientific Interest and wildlife habitats.

PHA says it submitted its detailed response to the developer in September and has shared sections with North Yorkshire Council, Natural England, Historic England, the Environment Agency and other public bodies.

It claims it has yet to receive detailed responses.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21746342.jpg?type=mds-article-620)A map of the proposed Sedgeby Solar Farm. *(Image: Sedgeby Solar Limited.)*

The group also says the developer has indicated that battery energy storage will not form part of the initial application.

Campaigners have questioned the scheme's likely electricity output, arguing that its performance would be significantly below its stated 350MW generating capacity when seasonal conditions and other factors are considered.

Mr Willis also pointed to a recent High Court ruling concerning the proposed Woolpots solar farm at Husthwaite, which resulted in the planning appeal decision being sent back for reconsideration.

He said: “Woolpots shows what we've been saying all along. You can't judge these schemes field by field.

“Take Sedgeby, Woolpots, and the other schemes along the corridor together and you're looking at thousands of acres in front of the national park.

“That's a decision about the landscape, not just one planning application.”

PHA says it supports renewable energy in principle but believes solar developments should be directed towards rooftops, brownfield sites and other less sensitive locations.

More than 70 residents attended a public meeting organised by the group at Sessay Village Hall on November 12.

The association is now encouraging residents to register properties, businesses and public footpaths which could be affected by the development ahead of the formal consultation process.

Further information is available at www.protecthambleton.org.uk

In response to the concerns, a spokesperson for Sedgeby Solar Limited said: “We recognise there are different views on Sedgeby Solar Farm and will continue to work with the community as the proposals develop.

"The matters referenced arise from the scoping process, which defines the scope of the environmental assessment.

"Surveys and technical work are therefore still underway, and we will provide further information as that evidence becomes available.”
