Band D rises of £17 to £36 a year for police, fire and a new bus precept are on the table. The Mayor's survey closes at 10am on 4 January 2027.

Households in the Harrogate district are being asked how much more council tax they would pay next year for policing, the fire service and, for the first time, local buses.

The survey comes from David Skaith, the Mayor for York and North Yorkshire. The precepts are the parts of the council tax bill that go to North Yorkshire Police and North Yorkshire Fire and Rescue Service. He will propose next year’s amounts early in 2027. This year he is also proposing a transport precept towards bus services, the York and North Yorkshire Combined Authority says.

## Overview

* **What:** a survey on the police, fire and new bus precepts for 2027/28.
* **The options for a Band D home:** police £12 to £21 more a year, fire £3 to £9, buses £2 to £6. Together that is £17 to £36 a year.
* **The levels that keep services as they are:** police £15, fire £5. The bus option the Mayor describes in most detail is £3.
* **Deadline:** 10am on Monday 4 January 2027.
* **How:** the online survey takes about ten minutes. A print version is also published.

## Policing: £15 to stand still

North Yorkshire Police has a budget of £243 million. Around £111 million of it, 46 per cent, comes from council tax.

The combined authority says the force needs an extra £12 million next year to cover inflation and other costs. About £5 million of that would come from council tax, and the rest from savings and government funding.

A Band D home pays £335.86 a year for policing now. The survey offers four levels:

| Band D rise a year | Raises | What the survey says it means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Up to £12 | about £4 million | Below inflationary pressures; likely service cuts unless more savings are found |
| Up to £15 | about £5 million | Keeps current levels of service |
| Up to £18 | about £6 million | Further investment, such as neighbourhood policing and crime prevention |
| Up to £21 | about £7 million | More significant further investment in the same areas |

The government is expected to cap the policing rise at £15 for an average Band D home, the combined authority says.

## Fire: £5 to stand still

North Yorkshire Fire and Rescue Service has a budget of £54 million, and around £38 million of it, 67 per cent, comes from council tax.

The service needs about £1 million more next year after savings. Government funding is expected to fall by £0.6 million, so it would need around £1.6 million from council tax to avoid cuts.

A Band D home pays £116.62 a year for the fire service now. The options are:

| Band D rise a year | Raises | What the survey says it means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Up to £3 | about £1 million | Below inflationary pressures; likely service cuts unless more savings are found |
| Up to £5 | about £1.6 million | Expected to keep current service levels |
| Up to £7 | about £2.3 million | Recruitment of additional firefighters |
| Up to £9 | about £3 million | More firefighters, plus wildfire response and specialist support vehicles |

## Buses: a new charge on the bill

There is no bus precept on bills now. The survey sets out four options:

| Band D a year | Raises | What it would pay for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Up to £2 | about £650,000 | Keep reduced bus fares for under-19s |
| Up to £3 | about £1 million | As above, plus free bus travel for care leavers and better services in some rural areas |
| Up to £4 | about £1.3 million | As above, plus extra evening and Sunday journeys on key routes |
| Up to £6 | about £2 million | As above, plus more journeys on key routes through the week and services to new areas |

The combined authority says a £3 precept would pay for £1 bus fares for younger people.

## What it means for you

The figures above are for a Band D home. You can check which band your home is in before answering.

At the levels the survey says keep services as they are, plus the £3 bus option, a Band D bill would rise by £23 a year:

| Band | Police rise | Fire rise | Bus precept | Total a year |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | £10.00 | £3.33 | £2.00 | £15.33 |
| B | £11.67 | £3.89 | £2.33 | £17.89 |
| C | £13.33 | £4.44 | £2.67 | £20.44 |
| D | £15.00 | £5.00 | £3.00 | £23.00 |
| E | £18.33 | £6.11 | £3.67 | £28.11 |
| F | £21.67 | £7.22 | £4.33 | £33.22 |
| G | £25.00 | £8.33 | £5.00 | £38.33 |
| H | £30.00 | £10.00 | £6.00 | £46.00 |

The band figures for each precept come from the combined authority’s own tables. The totals are our addition of them.

These precepts are only part of the bill. North Yorkshire Council and any town or parish council set their own shares separately.

## Drop-ins near Harrogate

The combined authority is holding drop-in sessions for questions about the precepts. The ones in this area are:

* **Ripon Library:** Monday 23 November, 10am to 1pm
* **Ripon Bus Station:** 26 November, 10.30am to 11.30am
* **Knaresborough Bus Station:** 2 December, 12pm to 1pm

The published list gives the Ripon Bus Station session as Friday 26 November and the Knaresborough one as Tuesday 2 December. In 2026, 26 November is a Thursday and 2 December is a Wednesday. It is worth checking the day with the Policing, Fire and Crime Team on 01423 569 562 before you go.

The team also provides the survey in other languages and formats on request, by phone or at [info@northyorkshire-pfcc.gov.uk.](mailto:info@northyorkshire-pfcc.gov.uk.)

## What happens next

The survey closes at 10am on Monday 4 January 2027. The Mayor will then propose the precepts early next year, the combined authority says.
