Drivers in Germany should be aware that roads may be slippery this week as temperatures drop to freezing or below in many parts of the country.

Light snowfall in the southwest was forecast on Monday, and isolated rain or snow showers are possible elsewhere, especially in the east and northeast, according to the German Weather Service (DWD).

In the country's northern regions police were warning drivers to exercise caution – several accidents have been reported already since Sunday.

Precipitation in the southwest and the Alpine foothills around the Black Forest had turned to snow in the early hours of Monday morning, reaching lower elevations.

In places in the southern Black Forest like Bernau, winter services were already underway, according to a report by local newspaper Stuttgarter Nachrichten.

More light snow was expected to fall in the higher elevations throughout Monday. So far, Feldberg mountain has seen the most snow with up to 20 centimetres near the peak. But surrounding regions at lower elevations saw only a very light dusting.

By Monday afternoon to evening, the DWD expected five to ten centimetres of snow to accumulate over the region from the Black Forest to the southern Alpine foothills, and from the western Allgäu to the Ammergau Alps.

Areas above 1,000 meters in this zone could see snowfall up to 20 centimetres, or even up to 40 centimetres in the higher areas on the northern side of the Allgäu Alps.

Elsewhere in Germany temperatures dropped below freezing on Sunday night to Monday morning, with isolated showers in eastern areas also leading to snowfall.

Temperatures dropped as low as -5C on Monday morning, with local temperatures largely dependent on cloud cover.

The amount of snowfall seen in eastern Germany, however, was significantly less than that in the Black Forest. There was up to three centimetres of snow in the Ore Mountains.

In other regions without precipitation, there was frost and potentially icy conditions on roads, especially in the early morning.

**The start of winter service**

When freezing conditions affect roads in Germany, winter maintenance services (called *Winterdienst* in German), step in to clear out the snow or ice.

Winter service for Germany's 13,200 kilometres of highways has been available since November 1st. Around 6,300 road wardens are deployed nationwide, Autobahn GmbH had announced.

Last year, which saw a particularly long spell of icy conditions in January, about 368,000 tons of salt were spread nationwide — significantly more than in the previous winter.

Schleswig-Holstein's Minister of Transport, Claus Ruhe Madsen (CDU), had called on all road users to be cautious. This included attentive driving, sufficient safety distance, and winter tyres.

Driver's in Germany are legally mandated to have winter- or 'all weather' tyres on their cars when driving in near freezing temperatures.

Effectively this means that drivers should ensure their tyres are suitable from around October until April each year, or simply use all weather tyres all the time. You can check if your tyres qualify by looking for an alpine symbol (a small mountain with a snowflake in it) on the tyre wall.
