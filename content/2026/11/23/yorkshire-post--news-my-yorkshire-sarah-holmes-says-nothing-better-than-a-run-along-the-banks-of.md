# My Yorkshire: Sarah Holmes says 'nothing better than a run along the banks of the River Wharfe'

## What’s your first Yorkshire memory?

We lived on the other side of the Pennines when I was a child, in Blackpool, but we were sent over to my lovely Barnsley grandparents for some wonderful holidays, and while I’m sure we were taken out to see so many wonderful places, the occasions that really stick in my memory are excursions to New Miller Dam, where we’d walk for miles, and, at the end of the trek, end up at a little local pie shop which sold delicious pork pies that were warm, with the jelly dripping succulently from them.

What’s your favourite part of the county – and why?

I love so much of it, you are out and about, you turn a gentle corner, and the view is completely different. But I’m particularly fond of the area around Janet’s Foss, there are so many lovely paths with stunning views. It’s hard to feel stressed up there, it’s like a giant weight being lifted from your shoulders.

What’s your idea of a perfect day out in Yorkshire?

For me, there’s nothing better than a run along the banks of the River Wharfe, then having a walk, an ice cream, and then an afternoon tea at The Old School House café in Poole-in-Wharfedale. Activity, and then some “down time.”

Do you have a favourite walk – or view?

You really cannot beat that area up around the old ruined medieval tower house, that is Barden Tower. And the walk along the river, away from Bolton Abbey – the wildlife is always wonderful (I’ve spotted an otter up there) and the colours are always changing.

Which Yorkshire sportsperson, past or present, would you like to take for lunch?

The Brownlee brothers would probably be great lads to talk to over a good meal. I’ve often seen then out running along local tracks going in the opposite direction, but, of course, it would be very wrong and impolite to stop then and to talk, This would be my great opportunity.

Which Yorkshire stage or screen star, or past or present, would you like to take for dinner?

Parkie. Sir Michael Parkinson. I’d love to talk to him about all the people he interviewed over the years, the ones that he warmed to and (like Rod Hull and his Emu) the ones that he didn’t. I didn’t always know who these talented stars were – I was only a child at the time – but I knew that he could engage in a conversation, and that he listened. Listening is a great skill. He was in a time before social media altered everything, and he was a conversationalist – how many people have proper conversations these days?

If you had to name your Yorkshire ‘hidden gem,’ what would it be?

The rocky outcrop that is Almscliffe Crag, the millstone grit outcrop near North Rigton. It’s a wonderful, magical place, and a whole group of us have been known to go up there on the longest day of the year, just to watch the sun rise over the horizon. We just sit there, and it’s really incredibly special.

If you could own one thing in Yorkshire for a day, what would it be?

Ripley Castle. My husband Andrew and I were married there, so of course we have a great many wonderful memories. It’s a magnificent place, but it still feels like a family home. The lake, the deer park, the woodland. Quite lovely.

What do you think gives Yorkshire its unique identity?

The people. They are that unique blend of warmly welcoming and straight-to-the-point blunt. No nonsense. They tell it how it is. I remember that some of the family were travelling North, and decided to stop off and see my grandparent’s old home in Barnsley. They knocked at the door, and they hoped that the new owners of the place would say ‘hello,’ and that would be it. Not a bit of it, they were welcomed in, shown around, and given a cup of tea. Tell me where else that happens.

Do you follow sport in the county, and if so, what?

There was cricket on the television all the time when I was growing up, so I still have a soft spot for Yorkshire, but both our daughters – Sophie, 18, and Millie, 17, are exceptional competitive swimmers, and really worth watching out for. Me? In the water, I’m hopeless.

Do you have a favourite restaurant, or pub?

We go over to The Smith’s Arms, in Beckwithshaw, a lovely old-school pub, with a warm welcome, good traditional food and a very pleasant beer garden for the better weather. Well run, nice people.

Do you have a favourite food shop?

Wheaton’s, in Harrogate, but I’m also a great fan of good farmshops, where-ever they may be. If they also have a good café, then all the better

How do you think that Yorkshire has changed, for better or for worse, in the time that you’ve known it?

There was that cataclysmic change in industries, where many towns were devastated when so many long-established places closed almost overnight, but I think that we are, in fact, pulling ourselves up again in so many other ways. The sporting achievements in the Olympics, the arts, with Bradford being a City of Culture (and Sheffield as a contender this year) small businesses and creative people coming to the fore. It’s a time to be optimistic, I believe.

Who is the Yorkshire person that you most admire?

As a collective, all the people who work in the voluntary sector, who give time, effort and commitment without question, and, as an individual, Alan Bennett, who combines laughter with a deep concern for humanity. A wonderful storyteller.

Has Yorkshire influenced your work?

The environment where I live sustains me, and enables me. I am so privileged to be in a sector which helps look after people - it’s sometimes very tough, but strangely, it is what keeps me going.

Name your favourite Yorkshire book/author/artist/CD/performer.

David Hockney, because we used to have prints of his work on the walls at school, and I fell in love with them then – I often go over to see his work at Salt’s Mill, and Julia Chapman, the crime writer, who books are all about the Dales Detective.

If a stranger to Yorkshire only had time to visit one place, it would be?

I’d take them on a walk around Bolton Abbey and Ilkley Moor, but I think we’d spend most of our time on the banks of the Wharfe.
