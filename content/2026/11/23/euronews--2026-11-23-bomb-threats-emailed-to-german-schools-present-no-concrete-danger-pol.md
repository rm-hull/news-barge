## It comes after more than 300 schools across Italy were evacuated earlier this month after receiving emailed bomb threats, which also claimed that an explosive device had been planted in Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni’s car.

Bomb threats were emailed to schools in Germany but there appeared to be no "concrete danger" at present, police said on Monday, after a wave of hoax bomb warnings at schools across Europe in recent weeks.

More than 450 schools in the southwestern state of Baden-Wuerttemberg were sent the threats, police said.

Police in the eastern state of Thuringia also said "numerous" schools there received bomb threats, which were not considered to be genuine.

Meanwhile, police in Denmark also said they were investigating bomb threats that had been sent to several schools across the country but maintained there was no cause for concern.

It comes after more than 300 schools across Italy were evacuated earlier this month after receiving emailed bomb threats, which also claimed that an explosive device had been planted in Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni’s car.

"At present, based on the checks carried out, no element has emerged that would suggest there is a concrete danger," Meloni wrote in a Facebook post.

Italian media reported that 312 schools had received threats.

Education Minister Giuseppe Valditara said around 300 alerts had been recorded across the country, adding that "it is clear that there is a strategy".

The emails began: "We have come to annihilate you. We will carry out genocide against the Italians." They claimed a "powerful explosive device" with a "remote trigger" had been placed in each building.

"We have placed a bomb in the engine of your prime minister’s car," the emails continued. They were signed "Mikhail Andropov, leader of the Vilayat of Misoginistan".

Bomb threats sent to **media outlets in Ireland** and Bulgaria in the past week were signed with the same name.

Similar hoax threats were also reported at schools in the UK and Ireland on 11 November, prompting some evacuations.

Almost a week after that scare, at least 100 other schools in Italy received email and poison threats, triggering a new security protocol introduced by the government.

Speaking in Brussels, Italian Foreign Minister Antonio Tajani said there was a "shared vision and desire to be increasingly united in responding to attacks and preventing different types of threats by email, cyber or hacking."
