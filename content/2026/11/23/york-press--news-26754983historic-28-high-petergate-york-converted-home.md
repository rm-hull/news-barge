A historic York building near the Minster is set to become a home again more than 50 years after it was converted for business use.

The Grade II-listed 28 High Petergate has been given the go-ahead to return to residential use after spending 17 months on the market without attracting an office tenant.

The property, which stands within York’s Central Historic Core Conservation Area, was originally a home before being converted into consultation rooms in 1973 and later used as offices.

Planning guidance would usually expect office premises to be marketed for 18 months before a change of use is considered, but City of York Council accepted the 17-month period in this case.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21754145.jpg?type=mds-article-620)28 High Petergate, York, which is to become a house again after 50 years. *(Image: Rightmove)*

Savills, which marketed the property, provided evidence that it had been actively advertised but no tenants had come forward.

The building’s listed status was also considered to limit its appeal for modern office use.

Plans will not alter the exterior of the property or increase its floorspace, with the conversion designed to preserve the character of the historic building.

Internal changes will include the removal of modern stud walls to simplify the floor plan and allow the property to function as a home again.

These works will be dealt with through a separate listed building consent application.

A council conservation officer said the proposals would allow the vacant building to be brought back into use with “minimal harm”.

No vehicle or cycle parking will be provided as part of the scheme.

Planning officers concluded that, despite the loss of a small amount of office space, returning the property to residential use would help secure its long-term occupation and maintenance.

They also found the plans would preserve the listed building and the character of the surrounding conservation area.

Appealing Properties Ltd and CSE Financial Services Limited are behind the proposal, which was approved on November 19.
