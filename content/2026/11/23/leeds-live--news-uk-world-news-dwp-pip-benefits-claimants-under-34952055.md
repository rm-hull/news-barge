# DWP PIP benefits claimants under 25 could have payments protected in shake-up

## A Personal Independence Payment (PIP) review under the Government is reportedly set to recommend an overhaul

A DWP Personal Independence Payment (PIP) review under the Labour government - due to be published this week - will recommend a major overhaul rather than abolishing the benefit for under-25s.

The Timms Review, led by Labour minister Sir Stephen Timms, will examine ways to make the benefit, which supports 4 million people, more equitable for both claimants and taxpayers.

The review will maintain the system for all age groups, according to the Guardian. The findings are understood to closely mirror a draft set of proposals that surfaced in September, which advocated for a range of modifications, particularly to the assessment process, to better accommodate conditions that fluctuate over time.

Preliminary recommendations released earlier this year include establishing a fast-track procedure to enable new claimants with terminal illnesses to access PIP more quickly – and cutting the number of benefit reassessments for individuals with lifelong or degenerative conditions, reports Birmingham Live.

Additional proposals include examining rigid and "unnecessarily stressful" regulations that automatically suspend someone's PIP award after they've spent more than 28 consecutive days in hospital. A Department for Work and Pensions spokesperson said: "These are not the final recommendations. The review will report to the secretary of state this autumn and he will carefully consider the final proposals within it.

"The Timms review interim report, co-produced with disabled people, made clear that Pip is no longer fit for purpose. Our goal is to deliver a system that supports disabled people and those with long-term conditions to live independent lives, while remaining fair, sustainable and fit for the future."

The interim report, published this summer, concluded that PIP was "not working" and committed to making bold and radical proposals to overhaul the benefit, ensuring it continued to support disabled people "both now and into the future".

"People describe Pip as something that 'breaks' them, rather than a support that enables them to live independently and participate in society," it stated, referencing testimony given to the review by an individual. "Pip cannot be everything to everybody so as the review looks at the role and purpose of Pip, the steering group will have some challenging discussions," the report said.
