## Ursula von der Leyen and Jared Kushner cannot agree on when and how the issue of European security archicture should be discussed in the peace talks to end Russia's war on Ukraine.

A friction point has emerged between the European Union and the United States over the sequence in the peace talks to end Russia's war on Ukraine, particularly when and how the question of European security architecture should be addressed.

European leaders see the topic as a Russian trap and insist a ceasefire in Ukraine must come first. But in a social media post, US special envoy Jared Kushner, who is brokering the negotiations, said it should be tackled head-on, without waiting.

"Security guarantees for Ukraine cannot be finalised in isolation from post-war European security architecture. The two must be considered together," Kushner wrote.

Among these potential guarantees, he said, is a provision fashioned after NATO's Article 5 of collective defence to aid Ukraine in the event of a new Russian attack.

US President Donald Trump would sign off on an **Article 5-like** clause only if "the broader security arrangements made it highly unlikely that the guarantee would ever be invoked", Kushner warned.

This, he argued, makes it necessary for the matter to be on the table from the get-go as part of a bigger deal.

"Nothing is agreed until it is agreed," he said.

Kushner's assertion clashed with the position of European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen and **other European leaders**.

They all agree that it is up to Russia to restore the security architecture that European nations established after World War II in multiple treaties and conventions and that Moscow broke when it invaded Ukraine.

Von der Leyen says discussions on security architecture are contingent on Russia establishing a ceasefire in Ukraine and demonstrating a genuine commitment to peace.

Asked about Kushner's latest post, the Commission held its ground.

"Our position has not changed since last week," Paula Pinho, the Commission's chief spokesperson, said in reply to a Euronews question.

"So once there is a ceasefire, then discussions can take place on Europe's security architecture. Those discussions need to take place in the appropriate forum, meaning with NATO allies and with the EU member states fully involved," she added.

"So we stick to our position."

Chancellor Friedrich Merz echoed von der Leyen's view last Thursday while hosting allies, including Kushner, for a meeting of the Coalition of the Willing in Berlin.

The meeting saw a **strong pushback** against any attempts to divide the continent into spheres of influence.

"What's needed now is the right sequence, the right order: first peace in Ukraine, then agreements on Euro-Atlantic security," Merz said in the closing press conference.

"Moscow knows: Its maximalist demands from December 2021 are unrealistic."

These demands resurfaced in a paper tabled by Russia at the last round of talks in Abu Dhabi. According to the New York Times, the wish list asks NATO to stop its expansion, withdraw its troops to 1997 borders and halt military support for Ukraine.

Europeans have rejected the requests outright, saying they are a ploy to divert attention from Ukraine and gain veto powers to which Russia is not entitled.

"What cannot happen is that we are forced to roll back NATO to the 1997 borders or that we should have others to decide our posture other than allies themselves," NATO Secretary General Mark Rutte said on Monday.

But the prominence that both Moscow and Washington have attached to the security architecture makes it increasingly difficult for Europeans to ignore. Privately, officials admit the subject will have to be discussed one way or another.

The real test will happen when and if Russia agrees to hold multilateral talks with the US, Ukraine and Europe at the same time, something it has so far refused to do.

In his post, Kushner said the US intended to reach a "common understanding" on security architecture "over the coming weeks".
