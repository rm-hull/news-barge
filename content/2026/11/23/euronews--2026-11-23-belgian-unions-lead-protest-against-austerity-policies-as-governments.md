## Prime Minister Bart De Wever offered his resignation to the king on 2 November over the budget impasse, but the monarch refused to accept it and gave him more time to find a solution.

Belgium is facing a day of political turmoil on Monday as a national strike against planned austerity measures causes widespread disruption, with the government's future hanging in the balance.

Prime Minister Bart De Wever is to report to King Philippe, the third time in a month, on progress in agreeing a new cost-cutting budget with his coalition partners, as thousands march in Brussels against the same spending plans.

A national strike called by the country's main unions has grounded all flights, after air traffic controller Skeyes said it was unable to provide regular services at Belgian airports due to staff walking out.

Public transport, schools and other public services were also facing disruptions.

Belgium has seen repeated rounds of strikes and cost-of-living protests in recent months.

Much of the anger has been directed at De Wever's efforts to save €10 billion over the next few years in a bid to reduce the country's high deficit.

The issue has caused controversy also within the Flemish conservative's ruling coalition, which has been locked in budget talks for weeks.

De Wever offered his resignation to the king on 2 November over the budget impasse, but the monarch refused to accept it and gave him more time to find a solution.

To bring down the deficit, which exceeds 5% of GDP, the government is looking at spending cuts notably in healthcare and a VAT increase.`

Under the current fiscal framework, EU governments must keep their budget deficits below 3% of GDP and public debt under 60% of GDP.

But divisions within the coalition remain with the only leftist party in the mix, as well as unions pushing for taxes on high earners to offset some of the cuts.
