## Spain's Maritime Rescue Service and Civil Guard have escorted five boats to the port of La Restinga and located a sixth. The island is now housing around 900 migrants after a record-breaking November.

Migration pressure on El Hierro, in Spain's Canary Islands, intensified again on Sunday as more than 350 migrants arrived aboard six small boats. Another boat carrying 64 people reached the island, home to just over 12,000 people, in the early hours of Monday.

Spain's Maritime Rescue Service and Civil Guard carried out the rescues and transferred the migrants to the port of La Restinga.

According to Spanish public broadcaster RTVE, the five boats that have arrived so far were carrying 74, 82, 22, 35 and 79 people respectively, among them around 20 minors. The Civil Guard patrol boat Río Tajo and the rescue vessel Diphda took part in the operations.

With these latest arrivals, around 900 migrants are now being housed in facilities on the island, whose reception capacity is under enormous pressure. Some of them are expected to be transferred to other islands in the Canary archipelago between Monday and Tuesday.

## **A historic November for the Canary route**

The surge follows several days of mass arrivals that have overwhelmed the resources available on El Hierro, an island of barely 12,000 inhabitants. More than 2,500 migrants reached the Canary Islands between Saturday and Sunday. That is the highest number of rescues recorded in a single day in the 30-year history of the Canary migration route.

So far this November, more than 100 cayucos have reached the island, carrying more than 10,000 migrants. The island's Temporary Foreigners Reception Centre (CATE), which can hold 500 people, has been completely overwhelmed.

Last Tuesday, the Spanish government announced an immediate €5 million aid package to reinforce healthcare and tackle the crisis's socio-economic impact on El Hierro. This comes on top of €15.18 million previously announced for reception facilities, assistance on the coast and transfers.

Fernando Clavijo, president of the Canary Islands regional government, has criticised the lack of forward planning. Humanitarian organisations, meanwhile, warn that available resources are stretched to their limits and are calling for long-term measures to address the root causes of migration.
