Snow could fall on northern high ground this week, a Met Office meteorologist said in a forecast reported on Monday.

Tom Morgan said winter could make an early appearance for some, despite another week of meteorological autumn remaining.

He said high pressure over Scandinavia was bringing a colder easterly flow, while milder systems were trying to move in from the Atlantic.

Mr Morgan said: “Generally though, for most of us, it will be staying on the cold side and there’s even the potential for some snow, mainly over the high grounds in the north.”

The forecast said most areas were expected to stay dry on Monday before rain returned to South West England and parts of South Wales on Tuesday.

Rain could also be heavy further north, with snow likely on higher ground in Scotland.

Wednesday and Thursday were expected to be drier but colder.

Before Sunday night, temperatures had been forecast to fall below freezing widely, with rural parts of Scotland potentially reaching minus 9C.

Freezing fog was also possible in parts of the Midlands.

A yellow cold-health alert was reported to be in force for the North West, North East and Yorkshire and the Humber until 9am on Tuesday.

The UK Health Security Agency said the conditions could increase demand for healthcare and pose a greater risk to the lives of vulnerable people.

The cold forecast follows heavy rain in the South West on Sunday, with a yellow rain warning reported to have been lifted.

The report said 24 flood alerts remained in England, while a flood warning for the south Devon coast at Beesands, Torcross, Slapton and Dawlish had been removed.

On Friday night, the UK recorded its coldest night since January 9, with a low of minus 10.6C at Tulloch Bridge in the Highlands.
