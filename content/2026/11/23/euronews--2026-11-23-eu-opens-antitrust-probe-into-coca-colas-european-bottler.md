## Discounts that depended on drinks staying inside national borders, and a refusal to sign supermarket deals covering more than one country, are among the practices under scrutiny.

The European Commission on Monday opened a formal antitrust investigation into Coca-Cola Europacific Partners (CCEP), the largest supplier of fizzy drinks in eight EU countries. It suspects the company abused its dominant position to stop retailers buying across borders.

"People across Europe enjoy soft drinks every day. But they should not have to pay more simply because they live in one EU country rather than another," said Teresa Ribera, the Commission's executive vice-president for clean, just and competitive transition, who oversees competition policy.

"We are investigating whether Coca-Cola has been putting barriers between national markets, making it harder for retailers to buy products across borders and secure better deals," she said.

CCEP is headquartered in the UK and is one of the two largest European bottlers of Coca-Cola brands. It sells those drinks, along with other brands, in Belgium, France, Germany, Luxembourg, the Netherlands, Portugal, Spain and Sweden.

The Commission is concerned that CCEP may have abused its dominant position in the wholesale supply of carbonated soft drinks and sports drinks to supermarkets and other retailers. Restaurants, hotels and cafés are not part of the case.

The investigation will look at whether CCEP:

* made wholesale price cuts conditional on drinks not being exported to other EU countries
* sold different packaging volumes in different countries to restrict cross-border trade
* gave retailers incentives to limit imports of fizzy drinks into Spain
* refused to let retailers negotiate deals covering several countries at once, insisting instead on separate national negotiations

"This could undermine the Single Market, limit competition and ultimately mean higher prices and less choice for consumers," Ribera said.

If proven, the practices could breach Article 102 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the EU, which bans companies from abusing a dominant position. Here the alleged abuse is dividing the Single Market along national borders.

The investigation comes three days after an EU-UK summit in Brussels, Belgium, where UK Prime Minister Andy Burnham sought to show he is delivering the closer post-Brexit ties promised by his predecessor, Keir Starmer.

The case follows unannounced inspections at CCEP's premises in several EU countries in March 2025. The Commission said it would treat the investigation as a priority. It added that opening a probe does not prejudge the outcome, and that there is no legal deadline for completing it.

Because the Commission has opened proceedings, national competition authorities can no longer apply EU competition rules to the same practices.

CCEP said it would continue to "engage fully" with the Commission's inquiries. It said it took competition law very seriously and was "firmly committed to complying with all applicable laws and regulations".
