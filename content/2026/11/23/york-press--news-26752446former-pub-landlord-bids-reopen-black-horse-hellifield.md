## Former landlord bids to reopen pub after two year closure

Gary Smith outside the Black Horse *(Image: Lesley Tate)*

The last landlord of Hellifield's Black Horse before it closed more than two years ago says he could have it reopened in a matter of weeks and believes it could once again be a thriving pub at the centre of the village.

Gary Smith, who ran the North Yorkshire pub for more than 10 years from 2013, has offered to lease the building from the Gargrave charity that owns it, and in the summer instructed solicitors to put in a formal offer.

His offer of a 21-year lease included a full restoration of the Main Road pub, and the creation of a bunk barn, together estimated to cost £400,000.

The offer, which was rejected, would have provided the charity with a 'steady and reliable rental income', and with an active tenant in residence made sure the property was secure, maintained and brought back into regular use, said Mr Smith.

He says when he took on the pub in 2013 it was semi-derelict and a 'huge gamble' but it turned out to be a success, employing 17 people.

![](https://www.cravenherald.co.uk/resources/images/21730324.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The Black Horse, Hellifield *(Image: Lesley Tate)*

He claims his plans for the pub ended following a change of trustees at the charity, but he still hopes his bid to reopen the pub will eventually be successful.

He said: “It was a huge gamble when I took in on in 2013, but it became a huge success so much that it needed extending and further investment, we were turning people away, and I believe it still could be a success.”

Owners, the Gargrave Lands Charity, which provides financial assistance to residents of Gargrave and surrounding areas, but not Hellifield, says it is no longer interested in leasing the pub, which closed in September 2024, and wants to sell it instead.

Together with the pub, its main asset, the charity owns pieces of land that it rents out.

The pub has been marketed for two years, originally to lease or buy, and is currently on the market with leisure specialists Fleurets for a reduced price of £350,000.

In March last year, the freehold price of the property was £495,000.

Last year, villagers backed a proposal to buy the Black Horse and run it as a community pub, but despite pledges of £215,000 towards buying the pub and restoring it, it was not enough for the charity, that has said it would welcome another offer from the community, or from Mr Smith.

Meanwhile, the pub, the only one in the village, although there is also a social club, is looking increasingly dilapidated and has recently been vandalised.

![](https://www.cravenherald.co.uk/resources/images/21730325.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The Black Horse, Hellifield *(Image: Lesley Tate)*

Both Hellifield Parish Council and North Yorkshire Council's member for Mid Craven, Councillor Simon Myers, have written to the charity about the condition of the pub, which is in a highly prominent position in the centre of the village.

Cllr Myers said: “I do have every sympathy with the trust, but on the other hand, the pub is in the centre of Hellifield and is increasingly derelict and has become vandalised. I have asked them to do something about it.”

A spokesperson for Hellifield Parish Council, said: "The parish council considers that the Black Horse Public House, which has been vacant for what is now a considerable period, constitutes an eyesore in the centre of Hellifield village. In addition the assorted disused planters, ironwork and old outside furniture are used as a 'play area' by children and youths in the village and present a significant hazard to them.

"The parish council has written to the owners of the Black Horse - Gargrave Poors Land Charity Trustees - requesting that they remove the assorted items and general detritus, tidy up the outside of the property, and render it more secure so it does not present a safety hazard."

A spokesperson for Fleurets said: “For a number of reasons the charity no longer considers that letting the property is an option. We are trying very hard to find a buyer in a challenging market. If Mr Smith or the community can demonstrate they have the funding to buy the property we would be delighted to hear from them.”
