The AfD has gained greater influence in Treptow-Köpenick for the 2026–2031 electoral term. Compared with the previous term, it now has 14 district assembly members instead of eight. Its parliamentary group in the 55-member District Assembly (BVV) has therefore grown considerably. It also has the right to nominate candidates for two of the six positions in the district executive (Bezirksamt). The AfD has nominated long-serving district executive councillor Bernd Geschanowski and district assembly member Christian Krüger for these positions.

Whether the two will actually be able to take office, however, depends on whether they are elected by the BVV. For that to happen, the AfD must convince the other groups that its candidates are suitable. The AfD will also need support from other parties to get its own motions passed in the BVV over the next five years. As expected, the other parties have expressed strong opposition to the AfD and its candidates in comments to our editorial team. But how does the AfD itself assess the situation? The Berliner Morgenpost put the question to the party.

### Treptow-Köpenick: AfD Parliamentary Group Leader Expresses Optimism

“Yes, we expect that to happen in some cases,” replies AfD parliamentary group leader Michael Gleichmann when asked whether he expects other groups to support some of his party’s motions. “At the inaugural meeting, for example, we received support from other parliamentary groups for our motion to refer the major proposed amendments to the rules of procedure – some of which were only introduced during the meeting itself – to the Rules of Procedure Committee for proper consideration. This shows that support across party lines is possible,” he explains.

> The firewall is undemocratic. Anyone who continues to uphold it disregards the voters’ decision. Moreover, it has demonstrably failed as a strategy against the AfD.
>
> Michael Gleichmann, AfD parliamentary group leader in Treptow-Köpenick

“This is exactly the approach we would like to see in the BVV going forward. The AfD assesses every motion on its merits, whether in the Bundestag or the BVV. We expect the same from the other parliamentary groups,” Gleichmann says. He describes the so-called firewall as undemocratic. “Anyone who continues to uphold it disregards the voters’ decision. Moreover, it has demonstrably failed as a strategy against the AfD,” he explains.

### How the AfD Intends to Find Majorities for Its Motions

Securing majorities for its own motions in the Treptow-Köpenick BVV is likely to prove extremely difficult for the AfD. Nevertheless, Gleichmann publicly expresses a willingness to engage in dialogue. “We want to win people over with proposals to solve specific problems in our district. That is the mandate voters have given us,” he explains. “We are prepared to seek talks with the other parliamentary groups early on. If suggested changes would improve a motion, we are open to them. Here, too, we are offering the other groups constructive cooperation,” the parliamentary group leader says. “Our focus is on the issues themselves. We have communicated this to the other groups from day one.”

Gleichmann is also optimistic about the prospects of the two candidates his parliamentary group has nominated for the district executive. “For now, we assume that both candidates will be elected,” he says. “Bernd Geschanowski brings many years of experience as a district executive councillor. Christian Krüger has extensive knowledge of law, administration and local politics. So far, we have not heard any substantive objections to either candidate,” Gleichmann adds.

Gleichmann says the AfD has reason to be sceptical, however, because only representatives of the Left Party, SPD and Greens were elected to the BVV’s presiding board at the inaugural meeting on 29 October. “Nevertheless, we are approaching the election with an open mind and expect both candidates to be assessed fairly on their merits,” he explains. He says this is a matter of “written and unwritten democratic rules of the game”.

### Michael Gleichmann Warns Against “Serious Foul Play”

“Under Section 35(2) of Berlin’s District Administration Act, the district executive should be formed on the basis of nominations from the parliamentary groups in proportion to their strength in the BVV. This gives our parliamentary group the right to nominate candidates for two district executive councillor positions,” Gleichmann explains. If the other parliamentary groups were to reject both candidates solely because of their party affiliation and leave the positions permanently vacant, the AfD politician argues, this would amount to “serious foul play against our almost 38,000 voters. The consequences would be borne by the district’s residents. The other parliamentary groups would have to explain that to them.”

Gleichmann also points out that majorities in the assembly can change. “That is why, at the first meeting, I asked all district assembly members to consider one question: Would you still think your actions were right if you yourself were in the minority? For me, this question is fundamental. Fair rules of the game benefit everyone.”
