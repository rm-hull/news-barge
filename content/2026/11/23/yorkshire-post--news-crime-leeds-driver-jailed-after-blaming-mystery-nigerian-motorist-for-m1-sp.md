# Leeds driver jailed after blaming mystery Nigerian motorist for M1 speeding offence

## A Leeds man who tried to avoid a speeding offence by falsely claiming a Nigerian driver was behind the wheel of his Mercedes has been jailed for 16 months.

Syed Miah, 48, of St Lukes Road, Beeston, was sentenced at Leeds Crown Court on Monday (Nov 23) after pleading guilty to perverting the course of justice.

On Thursday (Jun 6), 2024, Miah's Mercedes was caught travelling at 79mph on the northbound M1 near Wakefield.

When questioned by police, Miah initially nominated another man as the driver, but officers were unable to trace him.

After being given the opportunity to reconsider his account, Miah provided a printout of a Nigerian driving licence, an international driving permit and an international phone number.

![Syed Miah](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/jpim-static/image/2026/11/23/17/46/syed_miah.jpg?trim=71,0,269,0&width=640&quality=65)


He claimed a friend had borrowed his car to travel to London following a bereavement on the day of the speeding offence.

However, investigators became suspicious when a simple internet search revealed images of Nigerian driving licences and permits that closely resembled those supplied by Miah.

Parts of the online images matched the documents he had provided, while the driving licence number was identical apart from several digits that appeared to have been altered.

Police carried out further enquiries with Border Force and food delivery companies that Miah claimed the nominated driver had worked for.

Despite these checks, officers could find no trace of the man and no evidence that he existed.

Investigators also discovered that Miah was the only person insured to drive the Mercedes.

Further examination of his mobile phone location data placed him close to the scene of the speeding offence at the time it occurred.

Miah pleaded guilty to perverting the course of justice at a hearing in October.

Appearing at Leeds Crown Court on Monday (Nov 23), he was sentenced to 16 months in prison and ordered to pay a £187 surcharge.

Rachel Wainwright, of West Yorkshire Police's Digital Submissions and Investigations team, said: “Miah’s attempts to pull the wool over the eyes of our investigation team landed him in a lot more trouble than if he had been upfront and honest in his dealings with us.

“At no point did we find evidence that another driver had been behind the wheel of his car. And his dishonestly has resulted in him being sentenced for a much more serious offence than a speeding offence.”
