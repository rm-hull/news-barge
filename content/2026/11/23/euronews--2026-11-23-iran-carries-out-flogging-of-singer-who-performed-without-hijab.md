**Euronews Persian**

## Ahmadi and four members of her concert team received lashes in Qom on Monday, her lawyer said, over a December 2024 performance, streamed live without an audience. A court sentenced nine team members to 74 lashes each, a two-year travel ban, and a two-year ban on artistic activity.

Iran flogged pop singer Parastoo Ahmadi and four members of her concert team on Monday following a December 2024 performance near Qom in which she sang without a hijab, her lawyer said.

Lawyer Mostafa Nili told Emtedad news channel that the Qom prosecutor's office enforcement team carried out the sentence against Ahmadi in the early hours of Monday.

The flogging sentences against band members Sohail Faghih-Nasiri, Amin Taheri and Amirali Pirnia, and concert organiser Mohsen Lashkari, were carried out on the same day, Nili said.

A criminal court in Qom province had sentenced nine members of the concert team to 74 lashes each, a two-year ban on leaving the country and a two-year ban on artistic activity.

The judiciary also sealed off the Deyr Gachin Caravanserai, the historic venue where the concert took place.

## 'Hypothetical concert'

Ahmadi performed at the caravanserai near the historic city of Qom on the evening of 11 December 2024 without hijab. No audience was present.

The concert was broadcast live on YouTube and drew around 3 million views in the days that followed.

Ahmadi described the performance as a "hypothetical concert." Writing on her YouTube channel, she said, "I want to sing for the people I love. This is a right that I could not give up, that is, singing for the land that I love passionately."

The criminal court of Qom charged the group with "offending public decency by producing and disseminating vulgar and immoral content online."

Amnesty International called in June for the sentences to be quashed, saying flogging was "a cruel, inhuman and degrading punishment" that violated the absolute prohibition on torture and other ill-treatment.

The organisation called on Tehran to abolish compulsory veiling and corporal punishment and to end the criminalisation of freedom of expression.

## Tehran regime contradictions

Under Islamic Republic law, women are banned from singing in public in spaces where men are present.

Although criminal law does not explicitly criminalise women's singing as such, female performers typically face charges of encouraging corruption and prostitution, failing to observe hijab, or offending public decency.

Authorities have used the charge in recent years to close many Instagram pages belonging to Iranian female singers. Appearing in public without hijab is punishable by between 10 days and two months in prison, up to 74 lashes or a fine.

The sentences against Ahmadi and her collaborators stand in contrast to scenes from pro-government rallies held during the US and Israeli military strikes on Iran earlier in 2026, when images circulated widely showing women at the gatherings without mandatory hijab.

A performance by Lebanese singer Julia Boutros was also broadcast at a pro-government gathering in Tehran, where Boutros also did not wear hijab.

Earlier, during the Woman, Life, Freedom protest movement in 2022, Ahmadi had performed the protest song "Az khun-e javanan-e vatan" or "From the blood of the nation's youths".

After it was widely shared on social media, her home was searched by security forces and she was ordered to report to the security prosecutor's office in Tehran.

The Woman, Life, Freedom movement emerged in Iran in September 2022 following the death of Mahsa Amini, 21, in the custody of Tehran's morality police. Amini had been detained over alleged issues with her hijab.

Human rights organisations say the subsequent crackdown left more than 500 people dead.
