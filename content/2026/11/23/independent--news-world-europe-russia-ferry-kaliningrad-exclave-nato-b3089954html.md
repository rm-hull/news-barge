Russia has unveiled plans to spend 47 billion roubles (approximately £440m) to build two giant rail ferries to ensure uninterrupted supplies to its tiny exclave of Kaliningrad.

The highly militarised region is sandwiched between Poland and Lithuania and has been the centre of rising tensions between Russia and Nato in recent months.

Kaliningrad provides Russia with a platform for air defence, missile and naval forces that can project power deep ⁠into northern and central Europe while also restricting Nato forces’ ability to reach the Baltic member states overland.

But at the ​same time, it is ⁠boxed in between two Nato states, meaning it is difficult for Moscow to resupply or defend in the event of a war.

Kaliningrad has become increasingly reliant on the sea route since 2022, after Lithuania banned transit of many goods, including construction materials through its territory in compliance with the EU sanctions against Russia.

![A map showing Kaliningrad](https://static.independent.co.uk/2024/04/04/16/newFile-1.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“Kaliningrad region has a special geography, and due to the unlawful actions of certain countries, disruptions to logistics chains and attempts at a transport blockade are complicating the operations of enterprises located there,” prime minister Mikhail Mishustin said on Monday.

The two ferries are ‌expected to go into operation in 2030 and operate between the port of Ust-Luga in Russia’s Leningrad region and Baltiysk in Kaliningrad. ‌

Most of the money will come from Russia’s ‌rainy-day fund, according to Mishustin.

The announcement comes after Sparta II, a Russian cargo ship with links to the military that transported cargo to Kaliningrad, was hit by drones and caught fire in the Baltic Sea on Saturday, in what Finland’s military said was likely an attack by Ukraine, highlighting growing security risks in the region.

![Two ships try to extinguish a fire in the Russian cargo ship Sparta II after a drone strike in the Gulf of Finland](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/23/08/download.-8..jpg?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&auto=webp)

Kaliningrad Governor Alexei Besprozvannykh said on 21 November that a drone alert went off in a district bordering Lithuania after the appearance of a “suspicious object”.

In a document sent to Nato back in September, Russia warned the alliance it would be ready to resort to nuclear weapons if the Western alliance attempted to cut off Kaliningrad.

Maritime shipping now accounts for over half of supplies ⁠to Kaliningrad, compared with just 8 per cent in 2021, before the war in Ukraine started. ‌

About 30 ships, ​including four rail ferries, now transport ‌goods between the exclave and ​Russian ports on the Baltic Sea.
