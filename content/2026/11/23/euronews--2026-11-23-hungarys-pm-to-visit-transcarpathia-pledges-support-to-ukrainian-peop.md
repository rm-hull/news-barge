## Ties soured sharply under Hungary's previous PM Viktor Orbán, whose government repeatedly clashed with Kyiv over Ukraine and blocked its progress towards EU membership.

Hungary's Prime Minister Péter Magyar said on Monday that he planned to visit the southwestern Ukrainian region of Transcarpathia and pledged his support for Ukrainians there under attack from Russia and ethnic Hungarians living in the border region.

"The vast majority of Hungarians, including ourselves, stand by our Hungarian compatriots in Transcarpathia, and we also stand by the Ukrainian people who have been attacked, even if someone in Moscow takes a dim view of this," Magyar said in parliament.

He said the government in Budapest is also raising the issue of Hungarian schools, the use of the Hungarian language and Hungarian education, and is calling Ukraine to account over the fulfilment of the commitments made to the Hungarian community.

Magyar said that no elected Hungarian prime minister had visited Transcarpathia and the Hungarians living there since 2015.

"This will change shortly...I will return to Transcarpathia as an elected Hungarian prime minister and personally take the message of support from the mother country to our Hungarian brothers and sisters" he said.

Last month, Hungary and Ukraine came close to ending a long-running dispute over the rights of ethnic Hungarians in Ukraine, potentially clearing a major obstacle in relations between Budapest and Kyiv.

Ties soured sharply under Hungary's previous PM Viktor Orbán, whose government repeatedly clashed with Kyiv over Ukraine and blocked its progress towards EU membership.

But Magyar has sought to repair relations since taking office in May, but the rights of ethnic Hungarians in Ukraine's Transcarpathia region remain a source of tension.

In June, the two countries agreed an 11-point plan to strengthen minority rights. Hungary responded by allowing two negotiating clusters to open in Ukraine's EU accession talks.

But Budapest has blocked further progress, insisting that Ukraine's parliament, the Verkhovna Rada, first pass the legislation required under the deal. The laws are due to be adopted by the end of the year.

## Deal could ease tensions

The breakthrough follows another flare-up between the two governments earlier in October.

Magyar and Ukrainian Foreign Minister Andrii Sybiha clashed over Volodymyr Zelenskyy's C8 regional development initiative, with Sybiha accusing the Hungarian leader of following in Orbán's footsteps after Magyar said Hungary had been included against its will.

A bill covering language and cultural rights is due to go before the Rada later in October. Kyiv has not officially commented on the latest developments.

Ukrainian outlet LIGA.net reported that experts from both countries held intensive talks this week and agreed which parts of the 11-point plan Kyiv can deliver.

Dániel Hegedűs, deputy director of the Institut für Europäische Politik, told Euronews that the move could help reverse recent negative trends in bilateral relations, but that the legislation could still run into obstacles in the Rada.

"Given the absence of stable majorities in the Verkhovna Rada, passing the legislation in October is an ambitious goal and a real test of whether the Ukrainian administration can regain political control over issues of strategic importance to the country's EU integration," Hegedűs said.
