Benjamin Netanyahu has urged Donald Trump to launch new military strikes against Iran “as soon as possible”, according to reports, even as senior Israeli defence officials warn that such action could reignite war across the region.

Citing an unnamed source familiar with the matter, Israel’s public broadcaster Kan reported that the Israeli prime minister has been pressing his US counterpart to initiate fresh military action against Tehran.

Iran’s deepening economic crisis – caused primarily by US-led sanctions – has strengthened voices inside the White House arguing that financial pressure should be given more time to take effect.

The reports come amid heightened speculation over whether Trump and Netanyahu – who have both recently suffered major election defeats – will launch further attacks on Iran.

![The Israeli prime minister has pressed Donald Trump to strike Iran ‘as soon as possible,’ according to Israeli broadcaster Kan](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/07/28/15/35/GettyImages-2209045059-(2).jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Netanyahu lost his parliamentary majority in Israel’s 27 October election –creating a deadlock that has left him governing as a caretaker prime minister while opposition rivals attempt to assemble a coalition to oust him.

The US president has sent mixed signals over restarting the conflict.

On Friday, Trump issued a sharp warning to Tehran on Friday over unsubstantiated reports of an Iranian plot to detonate a "dirty bomb" in Washington. “There is a rumour that they are building a dirty bomb. If they do, we're going to fight,” the president told reporters at the White House, despite senior administration officials saying there was no credible or imminent threat to the US homeland.

But while speaking to reporters after meeting Qatar’s prime minister last week, Trump suggested it was highly unlikely he would need to order new major strikes, declaring that the war “effectively over”.

![The US president has appeared reluctant to resume military action again Iran following bruising midterm results](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/21/22/08/GettyImages-2220313126.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The disastrous Republican results at the midterms prompted vice president JD Vance to publicly break with Trump over the conflict, writing on social media that "the reason we lost is the war".

The prospect of renewed military action has also widened a rift between Netanyahu and the Israel Defence Forces (IDF), according to Channel 12.

Military intelligence officials reportedly warned the prime minister that a unilateral Israeli strike on Iran could trigger a multi-front war involving Lebanon, Syria and Gaza – directly contradicting assessments by Netanyahu and the Mossad intelligence agency that an operation could be contained to between three and five days.

At home, critics have accused Netanyahu of exaggerating the immediate Iranian threat to rally domestic support and block the formation of an alternative government.

Opposition leader Avigdor Liberman warned last week that the prime minister was weighing a unilateral strike without US involvement or approval, calling any attack at this stage “unjustified” and “completely motivated by politics”.
