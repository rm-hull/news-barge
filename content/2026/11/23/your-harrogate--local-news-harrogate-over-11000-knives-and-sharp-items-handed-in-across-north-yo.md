More than 11,000 knives and other sharp items have been handed in across York and North Yorkshire since knife surrender bins were introduced last year.

Harrogate, Knaresborough, Ripon and Boroughbridge are among the places where people can anonymously dispose of unwanted knives and blades.

The figures were released during Operation Sceptre, a national week of action on knife crime which ended on Sunday 22nd November.

North Yorkshire Police officers visited schools across the county to talk to pupils about the risks of carrying a knife.

Earlier this year, young people at The Barn youth club in Tadcaster used virtual reality headsets to explore how they might respond to situations involving knives.

Cllr Kirsty Poskitt, a lead youth support worker at The Barn, said:

> “The experience is a powerful and thought-provoking tool that helps young people see the potential consequences of carrying a knife in a way that feels real and relatable.
>
> "Several of those who took part needed time to reflect afterwards, and our youth workers were on hand to support them and talk through what they had experienced.”

North Yorkshire Council, City of York Council and North Yorkshire Police jointly run the surrender scheme, which received more than £15,000 from the region’s Serious Violence Duty Prevention and Early Intervention Fund.

Detective Chief Inspector Matthew Wilkinson, of North Yorkshire Police, said:

> “While incidents involving knives remain relatively low in North Yorkshire, one incident is one too many.”
