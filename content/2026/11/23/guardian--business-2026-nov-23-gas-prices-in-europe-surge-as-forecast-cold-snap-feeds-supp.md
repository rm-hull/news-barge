European gas prices surged on Monday as a forecast cold snap in the coming weeks fed fears across the continent’s over depleted supply this winter and costly household bills.

The Dutch month-ahead contract – the European benchmark for gas prices over the next four weeks – rose by as much as 8.6% to €96.08, before settling up 4% at €92.50.

The jump in gas prices comes as much of north-west Europe braces for a tough winter, when energy demand is at its highest.

Europe’s gas supplies have been hit by a slowdown in exports from Qatar due to the war in the Middle East, as well as an increase in consumption during the summer to power refrigeration and cooling during the prolonged heatwave.

Storage levels on the continent are now well below the seasonal average, at about 69% full against a typical level of 80%.

In the UK, energy system operators have said the country will have enough gas and electricity to meet demand during the coldest months, partly because of paying higher market prices than neighbours to secure imports.

However, it means British households will probably pay some of the highest energy bills in Europe this winter.

This week the energy regulator Ofgem is expected to announce a 26% rise in its quarterly price cap from January, adding an extra £443 to an average annual bill for a dual-fuel household. It could be one of the biggest quarterly price rises on record.

The charity Citizens Advice has said it expects a wave of more people seeking emergency help. Its chief executive, Clare Moriarty, said every day its “advisers see people whose budgets have been completely wiped out by years of compounding crises.”

“Millions of households are entering the coldest months of the year with nothing left,” she said.

Last month, the chancellor, John Healey, used his first budget to announce a giveaway of £100 to 20m households in council tax bands A to D to help with energy bills. But Moriarty said that, while the top-up was welcome, it “simply won’t stretch far enough for those who need the most support”.

Chris O’Shea, the chief executive of British Gas owner Centrica, has also said he is “hugely concerned” about the forecast jump in energy bills, warning that the number of people unable or unwilling to pay their bills would rise in the new year.

He told the BBC on Sunday that there was a “massive problem” with people not paying their bills in the UK, with some without the means to do so and an “increasing number of people that are choosing not to pay their bills”.
