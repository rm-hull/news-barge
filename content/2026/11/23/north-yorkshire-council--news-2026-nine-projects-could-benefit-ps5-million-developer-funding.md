More than £5 million generated from developers could be invested in nine projects across North Yorkshire as part of proposals to be considered by councillors.

The priority schemes, which would improve transport, healthcare, leisure and community facilities, have been identified to receive funding from the Community Infrastructure Levy (CIL).

Projects proposed to benefit from this year's funding include a new neighbourhood healthcare facility in Northallerton, repairs to Bedale Hall, the Selby Station Gateway scheme, and the refurbishment of Sheriff Hutton Bridge Cricket Club’s pavilion.

![Galtres Centre](https://www.northyorks.gov.uk/sites/default/files/2026-11/GaltresCentre.jpg)

Funding could also be used to install 24-hour prescription dispensers at Thirsk Surgery and Sherburn and Rillington Surgery, deliver a new padel tennis facility at The Galtres Centre in Easingwold, upgrade The Glebe playground in Killinghall, and create a new car park at Park Street Surgery in Ripon.

While Section 106 agreements require developers to fund improvements directly related to a specific site, such as roads and schools, Community Infrastructure Levy gives councils the chance to pool funds from multiple projects for a wider range of infrastructure.

The proposals will be considered by members of our executive committee when they meet on Tuesday next week (1 December).

Deputy leader and executive member for open to business, Cllr Mark Crane, whose responsibilities include planning policy, said: “We know that CIL funding is making a real difference in our communities.

“A wide range of projects have already benefited from this investment, and one of the key advantages of CIL is that it allows us to pool contributions from multiple developments and direct them towards infrastructure projects that are not site-specific and can benefit more residents.

“The projects identified for this year’s funding programme cover a range of priorities, from transport and healthcare improvements to leisure and community facilities.

“The proposals will be considered by members of the executive next week as we continue to make sure investment is targeted where it is needed most and where it can deliver the greatest long-term value.”

In 2025/26, more than £3.7 million was collected by the council through CIL. More than £3.1 million will help fund infrastructure projects supporting development across the county, with a further £593,000 distributed to town and parish councils for smaller, community-level schemes.

An additional £7.2 million was secured from developers through Section 106 agreements to fund site-specific improvements for communities.

CIL money is generated in four of the former district and borough council areas of Hambleton, Harrogate, Ryedale and Selby, and must be spent in the area where it is collected. CIL does not operate in the former Craven, Richmondshire or Scarborough areas.

Among the projects identified to receive CIL funding this year is the redevelopment of a building to provide a new neighbourhood healthcare facility, in Northallerton.

A total of £2 million has been earmarked to reconfigure the building, which would be bought and operated by a Community Interest Company, helping to deliver flexible healthcare services including clinical appointments, community nursing, physiotherapy, occupational therapy and specialist clinics.

Another project that could benefit from this year’s funding pot is the Selby Station Gateway initiative. The investment would help deliver elements of the wider Transforming Cities Fund scheme, which aims to encourage the use of sustainable transport by improving access to the station, as well as walking and cycling routes.

A total of £400,000 has been earmarked for repairs to Bedale Hall, which accommodates a range of uses including the library, museum and community group spaces. The works include work to the lobby ceiling, which would restore full access to the main entrance and ballroom, helping to protect the building’s future.

Elsewhere, surgeries in Thirsk and Sherburn and Rillington are both set to benefit from a free-standing, 24-hour prescription dispenser unit, allowing patients to collect prescriptions at any time and easing demand on staff.

Further funding has been proposed to help deliver a new car park at Park Street Surgery in Ripon, refurbish the pavilion at Sheriff Hutton Bridge Cricket Club to modernise facilities and improve accessibility, develop a new padel tennis facility at The Galtres Centre in Easingwold, and create a safer, more inclusive playground at The Glebe in Killinghall.

Previously approved CIL allocations will also be retained, including the Barlby Road Gateway scheme to create a more attractive entrance to Selby, the proposed expansion of Easingwold Community Primary School, and the creation of a new 3G pitch in Bedale.
