A Lego and chocolate thief and a man who strangled a woman were among defendants sentenced recently at Scarborough Magistrates Court.

Rodney Bignall, 46, of Pavilion Square, Scarborough, denied harassing a woman and was convicted at trial. He was given a 12-month community order with probation supervision and made subject to a 12-month restraining order. He must pay £186 prosecution costs.

Aaron Cook, 45, of Barrowcliff Road, Scarborough, pleaded guilty to theft of chocolate worth £140.80 from the Co-op, theft of Lego worth £300 from TG Jones and two charges of breaching a criminal behaviour order by going into TG Jones and a Co-op store. All the offences happened in Scarborough. He was given a 16-week prison sentence and ordered to pay £440.80 compensation.

Deborah Madden-Broome, 66, of The Crescent, Filey, pleaded guilty to breaching a restraining order. She was given a 12-month community order, fined £80 and ordered to pay £85 prosecution costs.

James Berry, 44, of Prince of Wales Terrace, Scarborough, pleaded guilty to strangulation of a woman. He was given 12 months’ imprisonment suspended for 18 months with a probation requirement on condition he wears an alcohol abstinence tag for 90 days.

Daniel Wright, 39, of Grey Fold, Byram, pleaded guilty to drink driving, driving without insurance and driving without a licence in Scarborough. He was banned from driving for 40 weeks, fined £750 and ordered to pay £85 prosecution costs and a £300 statutory surcharge.

Lauren Shimells, 31, of Newby, Scarborough, pleaded guilty to stealing alcohol worth £26 from Sainsbury’s in Scarborough. She was jailed for six weeks, plus six months previously suspended.

Luke Bourne, 40, of Gladstone Road, Scarborough, pleaded guilty to assaulting a police officer and assaulting a man in Scarborough. He was given a 12-month community order with a probation requirement and 135 hours’ unpaid work. He must pay each victim £100 compensation.

Adam Bellow, 20, of Penton Road, Cayton, denied a charge of assaulting a man in Scarborough and was convicted at trial. He was given an 18-month community order with a probation requirement and 200 hours’ unpaid work and ordered to pay £300 compensation and £325 prosecution costs.
