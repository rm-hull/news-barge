A trial of legal action against a Turkish businessman, in which Andrew Mountbatten-Windsor will give evidence, is set to start on Monday.

Turkish national Nebahat Isbilen is suing her former adviser, businessman Selman Turk, alleging he misappropriated tens of millions of her funds and did not explain where the money went.

She also claims Mr Turk misled her about a £750,000 payment made to the former Duke of York in 2019, which was later paid back.

Mr Turk denies dishonesty and is defending the claim, arguing that Mrs Isbilen made the £750,000 payment of her own accord as she thought Andrew could help with her husband, whom she claims has been the victim of political persecution in Turkey.

The 11-week trial is due to begin at 10.30am on Monday at the Rolls Building in London.

In October, Mr Justice Adam Johnson ruled that Mr Turk could summon Mr Mountbatten-Windsor to give evidence.

The judge continued that it was “highly unlikely” Andrew would “wish voluntarily to provide a witness statement and give evidence” at a trial, meaning a witness summons would “likely” be needed.

It is understood that the former prince is due to give evidence on December 14.

A summons requires a witness to attend court to give evidence, although Andrew could appear by video-link.

A witness can also be forced to hand over documents, with court rules stating a witness “must be offered or paid a sum reasonably sufficient to cover his expenses in travelling to and from the court”.

It is unknown how long Andrew’s evidence is expected to take, but Mr Justice Adam Johnson said in October that the evidence is “limited in scope”.
