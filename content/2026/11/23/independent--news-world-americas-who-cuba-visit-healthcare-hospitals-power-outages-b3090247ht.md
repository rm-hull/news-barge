The head of the World Health Organization is currently in Cuba to see firsthand a medical system severely impacted by deep economic troubles and persistent power outages across the Caribbean island.

WHO Director-General Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus, undertaking his second official trip to Cuba as director-general, toured a dark pediatric cardiology facility on Monday. He was accompanied by Cuban Public Health Minister Dr. José Ángel Portal Miranda during the walkthrough.

Tedros landed on Sunday for a three-day itinerary following an invitation extended by the island's health authorities.

Welcoming the global health leader, Portal Miranda described the visit as a vital opportunity for Tedros to observe the immediate reality and pressing obstacles facing Cuban healthcare.

According to the state newspaper Granma, Tedros indicated his eagerness to listen, gain insight into the island's plight, and find ways to deliver international aid.

"I want to use this visit to better understand the challenges you face and would be happy to mobilize the international community to support you," he stated.

Cuba’s historically celebrated system of free universal care has suffered severe decline. Observers note that energy sector sanctions imposed by the U.S. have intensified fuel scarcity, further destabilizing an economy already burdened by years of stagnation.

Hospitals throughout the country are operating without basic medical provisions, including vaccines, gauze, anesthetics, and syringes. Furthermore, facilities are unable to acquire replacement parts required to fix key diagnostic tools, such as CT scan machines and hemodialysis units.

Mario Cruz Peñate, representing both the Pan American Health Organization and World Health Organization on the island, called the decline shocking.

He noted that Cuba historically maintained health metrics equivalent to wealthy nations, boasting low mortality rates, extended life expectancies, thorough prenatal care, and high vaccination rates.

A government report issued in June highlighted the toll, showing that pediatric cancer survival rates fell to 65% from 85% prior to the start of the energy blockade in January.

Tedros' schedule includes visits to biotechnology and health facilities, meetings with top officials, and a news conference.
