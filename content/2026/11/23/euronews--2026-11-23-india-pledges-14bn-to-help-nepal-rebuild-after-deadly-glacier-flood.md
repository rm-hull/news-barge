## Balendra Shah's first visit to a neighbouring country since becoming prime minister also brought agreements on a railway, a hydropower plant and fuel infrastructure.

India has offered Nepal a development cooperation package worth 240 billion Nepali rupees (€1.4 billion) to help it recover from the catastrophic floods that tore through the Himalayan country in August.

Indian Prime Minister Narendra Modi made the announcement in New Delhi, India, on Monday, standing alongside his Nepali counterpart Balendra Shah.

"We express our deep condolences for the loss of lives caused by the devastating floods in Nepal in August," Modi said.

"To support Nepal's reconstruction, and give fresh momentum to its long-term development, we announce a development cooperation package worth 240 billion Nepali rupees (€1.4 billion)," he added.

New Delhi did not immediately say what the package would include. Nepal estimates the total cost of reconstruction at €4.35 billion.

His call came a day after the COP31 climate summit in Antalya, Türkiye, collapsed without a deal, when the EU and other countries rejected a final text that made no mention of fossil fuels.

## **A disaster on a vast scale**

The 26 August disaster began with a chain of events high in the Himalayas. A glacial collapse sent large amounts of rock, ice and meltwater into valleys below, triggering powerful floods downstream.

Scientists studying satellite imagery said the collapse appears to have involved the failure of bedrock beneath a glacier in the Himalayan region. The rockfall was powerful enough to register as a magnitude 5.2 seismic event.

The resulting surge of water and debris entered rivers that flow through valleys in Tibet and Nepal, causing them to rise rapidly.

Floodwaters swept away homes, buildings, roads and bridges and carried mud and rocks downstream.

More than 1,400 people were killed and 5,000 left missing. In the days after the disaster, at least 900 workers were reported missing from 12 hydropower projects damaged by the floods.

The scale of the death toll overwhelmed morgues and forensic teams.

## **'Deal with future disasters'**

Nepal, a largely rural nation of 30 million people, produces only a tiny share of global greenhouse gas emissions. Shah said the disaster showed the "imperative to address the challenges of climate change through collective action".

In October, the United Nations' climate loss and damage fund approved $20 million (€18.2m) for Nepal. That is the most a country can request in a single application, and it was the first money the fund had set aside for any nation.

"Our commitment will be to help affected families rebuild their lives, create new opportunities and further strengthen Nepal's capacity to deal with future disasters," Modi said.
