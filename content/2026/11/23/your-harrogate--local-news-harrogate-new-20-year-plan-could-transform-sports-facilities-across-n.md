Plans to improve sports facilities across North Yorkshire over the next 20 years have been unveiled by the county council.

North Yorkshire Council has developed its first county-wide playing pitch strategy and built sports facilities strategy, identifying where investment is needed in leisure centres, swimming pools, sports halls and outdoor pitches.

The plans aim to improve access to sport, modernise ageing facilities and ensure communities have the infrastructure needed to meet growing demand.

They will be presented to councillors on Tuesday 1st December and could help shape future investment in sports facilities across Skipton and the wider county.

The strategies highlight several challenges, including ageing leisure centres, increasing demand for swimming, gymnastics, padel and athletics, and the need for additional sports pitches.

The council has also identified opportunities to improve the quality of existing football, rugby and cricket pitches, develop more artificial grass and 3G pitches, and upgrade changing facilities.

Greater use of school sports facilities and community halls is also being considered to help more residents access activities closer to home.

North Yorkshire Council's executive member for leisure, Cllr Simon Myers, said:

> “Sport and physical activity play a vital role in helping people to stay healthy and active.
>
> “We want residents across North Yorkshire to have access to good-quality sports facilities and the opportunity to take part in sport and physical activity close to where they live.
>
> “These strategies give us the clearest picture we have ever had of sports facilities across the county.
>
> "They show where facilities are meeting local needs, where there are gaps in provision, and where improvements may be needed in the future.”

The council says the strategies will also help address the needs of an ageing population, with more residents aged over 65 expected to require accessible opportunities to stay active.

Cllr Myers added:

> “Having a strong evidence base will put us in a much better position to plan for the future, and attract external funding so facilities can continue to meet the needs of residents as our communities grow.”

The plans have been developed alongside Sport England, national sporting bodies, local clubs, schools and community organisations.

They will also support North Yorkshire's first county-wide local plan, which will guide development until 2045.

If adopted, the strategies will help the council prioritise improvements, secure funding and negotiate contributions towards sports facilities from developers building new homes.

Councillors will consider the proposals at their executive meeting on 1st December.
