## Hungarian Prime Minister Péter Magyar said his government did not consider the proposal an acceptable starting point for negotiations.

The prime ministers of the Visegrád Four (V4) countries voiced concerns over the European Union's next seven-year budget proposal at a meeting in Bratislava on Monday, questioning spending priorities and plans to include funding for Ukraine.

The leaders were meeting with the Austrian chancellor in Slovakia to discuss economic cooperation, migration and the draft of the budget.

Hungarian Prime Minister Péter Magyar said his government did not consider the proposal an acceptable starting point for negotiations.

He said he had therefore written to European Council President António Costa to explain that he saw little point in holding a special council meeting scheduled for later in the week as "there is no point spending two days in Brussels not agreeing on anything."

Czech Prime Minister Andrej Babiš also signalled his concerns over the proposed budget, saying he had expected the European Commission to show at least some sign that it wanted to save money.

He argued that maintaining a European Parliament in three locations was unnecessary and costly, while also voicing concerns over member state contributions.

Slovak Prime Minister Robert Fico, who hosted the meeting, said the leaders had not adopted a joint position on the budget during Monday's meeting, as this had not been the purpose of their discussions, although he noted that they were broadly aligned on the subject.

## Funding for Ukraine

For his part, Polish Prime Minister Donald Tusk spoke mainly about security, saying that Poland was committed to peace but that recent events showed the impact of Russia's invasion of Ukraine on Poland.

Tusk underlined the importance of continuing support for Kyiv, arguing that the country's ability to defend itself was of strategic significance for Europe's security.

However, his Hungarian counterpart, Magyar, warned that "support for Ukraine should not be included in the EU budget."

Magyar said there was no question that Ukraine deserved solidarity and noted that Hungary was already providing various forms of assistance to the Ukrainian people and government.

But he argued that financial support for Ukraine should not be incorporated into the bloc's budget in a way that would make contributions mandatory for all members.

On the same issue, Tusk said he did not think it was a good idea for the EU to finance support for Ukraine through joint borrowing - something the Hungarian government has already made clear that it would not take part in.

At a press conference after the meeting, the leaders stressed that regional cooperation was not only important but also achievable, given their shared positions on a number of key issues.

Fico said the V4 countries and Austria broadly agreed on key issues affecting the region, including cohesion policy, migration policy and agricultural strategies.

One of the main topics was how to deal with illegal migration. All the leaders called for strict rules and their consistent enforcement, while Fico also praised a plan for some EU member states to establish immigration return hubs in Rwanda.

Austrian Chancellor Christian Stocker said it was in Austria's interest to cooperate with its neighbours and that he wanted the country to be an ally of the V4.

He added that closer cooperation would also help ensure that the rest of the EU better understood developments in central and eastern Europe and the strategic priorities shaping the region.
