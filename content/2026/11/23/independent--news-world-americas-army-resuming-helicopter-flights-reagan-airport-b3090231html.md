The Army is pushing to resume helicopter training flights in the airspace near Reagan National Airport in Washington, D.C., almost two years after a midair collision killed 67 people, according to a report.

An Army Black Hawk helicopter collided with an American Airlines passenger jet that had been cleared to land at the airport on January 29, 2025, in what was the deadliest plane crash on U.S. soil in more than two decades.

Ever since the devastating crash, the Federal Aviation Administration has taken action to restrict mixed traffic around the airport.

Now the Army intends to resume the helicopter flights, a spokesperson told *Politico*, adding that they could return as soon as the week of December 6.

The FAA needs to approve the plans, and the agency told *Politico* that it had not received a formal request from the Army regarding the resumption of the flights.

![The Army is reportedly pushing to resume helicopter training flights in the airspace near Reagan National Airport in Washington, DC, nearly two years after a midair collision killed 67 people](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/23/21/2196927019..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“Our intent is to make sure all the stakeholders are aware and have proper coordination with FAA, and make sure Congress is aware as well,” the Army spokesperson told the outlet.

*The Independent* has contacted the Army for comment.

“Approval from the agency is required before any training in the area can restart,” the FAA said in a statement.

Three months after the deadly collision, Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy was furious after two commercial planes were instructed to perform “go-arounds” to avoid an Army Black Hawk helicopter.

“Unacceptable. Our helicopter restrictions around DCA are crystal clear. In addition to investigations from @NTSB and @FAANews, I’ll be talking to the @DeptofDefense to ask why the hell our rules were disregarded,” Duffy said in a statement at the time.

“Safety must ALWAYS come first. We just lost 67 souls! No more helicopter rides for VIPs or unnecessary training in a congested DCA airspace full of civilians,” he added. “Take a taxi or Uber - besides most VIPs have black car service.”

![Ever since the devastating crash, the Federal Aviation Administration has taken action to restrict mixed traffic around the airport, but now the Army reportedly intends to resume the training flights in the airspace](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/23/21/2219484905..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

In August, President Donald Trump was aboard Marine One when it was involved in an “air-traffic safety incident” above Washington, D.C.

Communications between Marine One and air-traffic controllers were investigated by the National Transportation Safety Board after the president’s helicopter took off from the Ellipse, near the White House, before commercial flights at Reagan National Airport were halted, as required under safety protocols brought in after the fatal collision.

The incident did not amount to a serious close call, and the White House said that “at no point was the President in harm’s way.”

Congress last week agreed to require airliners and other civilian aircraft to carry systems that help pilots locate nearby aircraft after families of the victims of the crash have called for aviation safety reforms.

The victims included 28 members of the figure skating community.
