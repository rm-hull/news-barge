Tom Jackson, the former Denver Broncos linebacker who became one of ESPN’s best-known NFL analysts, has died aged 75.

Jackson died on Friday, his family confirmed to ESPN, but asked that the news not be made public until it was announced by his longtime friend and broadcasting partner Chris Berman on *Sunday NFL Countdown*. No cause of death has been disclosed.

“Tom was so much more than an accomplished NFL analyst. There was nobody kinder, more thoughtful, more loyal or more engaging than him. And when it came to laughter, Tom’s was frequent and unmistakable,” Berman said. “Tom was my best friend professionally and personally. If you ever met him or just knew him from watching ESPN, he was your best friend, too.”

Berman said Jackson made a surprise visit a couple of weeks ago to ESPN, where he worked for 32 years over two stints after playing 14 seasons for the Broncos, and that the two talked on Tuesday.

A fourth-round draft pick out of Louisville in 1973, Jackson was an inspirational leader of Denver’s first Super Bowl team in 1977. He started 177 games for the Broncos between 1973-86, retiring after the team’s second Super Bowl appearance. He was a first-team All-Pro in 1977 and a second-team All-Pro the following season; he also was a three-time Pro Bowler.

![Tom Jackson presents Randy Gradishar into the Pro Football Hall of Fame class of 2024](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/22/16/TOM_JACKSON-DECESO_66756.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The Broncos held a moment of silence and showed a video tribute to Jackson before their game against the Las Vegas Raiders on Sunday. After the Broncos won 38-24, coach Sean Payton said the team would give a game ball to Jackson’s family.

Following his playing days, Jackson had a second career at ESPN from 1987-2016 and was featured on *Sunday NFL Countdown*and* NFL Primetime.*Before the internet, and before NBC took over the Sunday night package in 2006,* NFL Primetime* was must-see viewing for fans and players because it showed highlights from all the games, as well as the interaction between Berman and Jackson, who often finished each other’s sentences.

“I can remember coming from games and waiting in a rush to get home, to watch *Primetime* because all I wanted to know was, what were you and Tom Jackson gonna say about one, the team?” ESPN analyst and Hall of Fame wide receiver Randy Moss said to Berman.

![Jackson started 177 games for the Denver Broncos between 1973-86](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/22/16/Obit_Tom_Jackson_91344.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

NBC’s *Football Night in America*paid tribute to Jackson Sunday night. Host Maria Taylor said: “Before there was a* Football Night in America*, there was* NFL Primetime*, where TJ and Chris Berman set the standard for an NFL highlight show. It’s a standard that we look to every single Sunday.”

NFL Commissioner Roger Goodell, who was in Mexico City for Sunday night’s game between the San Francisco 49ers and Minnesota Vikings, said Jackson was “an outstanding player whose toughness and leadership helped define an era of Broncos football. He brought credibility, passion and humor to everything he did, and generations of fans came to know him through his work at ESPN.”

Jackson briefly returned to ESPN in 2019 and reunited with Berman to do an online version of *NFL Primetime*. He was awarded the Pete Rozelle Radio-Television Award from the Pro Football Hall of Fame in 2015.

“In my lifetime there was Howard Cosell doing *Monday Night Football*halftime highlights and then there were Boomer and TJ doing* Sunday NFL Countdown*. I am not here as a colleague but as one of million who watched, learned from and idolized the two of you,” ESPN host Mike Greenberg said during Sunday’s show.

ESPN’s studio crew wore orange ties in honor of Jackson during the three-hour show.

Berman said Jackson was the last of seven people interviewed for the studio analyst job and that the last person rarely gets the job. But Jackson nailed the interview.

“Tom Jackson was one of the most influential and beloved figures in ESPN’s history. He brought the credibility of an All-Pro NFL player, the insight of a gifted analyst and a warmth and authenticity that connected with generations of football fans. Alongside Chris Berman, he helped make ‘NFL Primetime’ an iconic part of our football weekends,” ESPN chairman Jimmy Pitaro said in a statement. “Away from the set, Tom was a trusted colleague, generous mentor and dear friend to many. ... His legacy at ESPN, throughout the NFL and among the countless fans who admired his incredible work will endure.”

ESPN’s NFL studio in Bristol, Connecticut, is named the Chris Berman-Tom Jackson Studio. It is the only named studio on ESPN’s campus.

“The Denver Broncos are heartbroken by the passing of Ring of Fame linebacker Tom Jackson, a cornerstone of the legendary Orange Crush defense and one of the most beloved players in franchise history,” the Broncos said in a statement.

“An inspirational leader and fierce competitor, TJ was a proud member of the Broncos’ first Super Bowl team that helped establish a winning tradition in Denver. Off the field, Tom carried himself with class, warmth and humility, forging deep connections and lifelong friendships with teammates and throughout Broncos Country.”

Jackson is survived by his wife, Jennifer, and daughters Taylor and Morgan.
