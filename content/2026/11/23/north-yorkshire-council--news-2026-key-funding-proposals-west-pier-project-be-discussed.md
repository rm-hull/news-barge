Proposals to transform Scarborough’s historic West Pier are progressing as councillors prepare to consider key funding proposals for the project.

The regeneration scheme would deliver major investment in the pier, including the restoration of key heritage buildings, improved facilities for the fishing industry, new commercial opportunities and enhanced public spaces.

Significant long-term investment is required to protect the pier's historic listed buildings and improve its structural condition and safety after decades of exposure to harsh marine conditions.

Final proposals are now being drawn up ahead of a planning application being submitted, representing the next stage in efforts to safeguard the pier’s historic character while strengthening its role as both a thriving working port and a popular visitor destination.

The scheme forms part of our wider plans to revitalise Scarborough Harbour and support the regeneration of the wider town.

The final funding package for the project is due to be presented to members of the council’s executive on Tuesday next week (1 December), where councillors will be asked to consider approving the additional investment required to deliver the revised scheme.

Additional funding of £2.4 million is required to progress the project, alongside previously identified investment and money from the ringfenced Scarborough Harbour Reserve.

Deputy leader and executive member for open to business, Cllr Mark Crane, said: “This project has the potential to secure the long-term future and regeneration of Scarborough’s West Pier and we are now reaching a pivotal stage in its development.

“The pier is one of the town’s most important assets, supporting the fishing industry, local businesses, visitors and the wider economy. The proposals have been re-scoped and re-costed following extensive engagement as we look to protect the unique heritage while providing modern facilities that meet future needs.

“The final funding package for the scheme will now be carefully considered by councillors next week as we continue to work towards securing the long-term future of this important harbour asset.”

The regeneration programme aims to address the impact of decades of harsh marine conditions on the pier's ageing buildings and infrastructure.

This includes repairs and upgrades to existing historic buildings, the creation of modern flood-resilient facilities, improved public toilets, upgraded pedestrian routes, new retail kiosks and an enhanced public realm that can accommodate events and activities throughout the year.

The scheme has been shaped through engagement with harbour users, businesses and stakeholders, as well as a public survey undertaken earlier this year.

The consultation demonstrated strong support for the project, with more than 80 per cent of respondents backing the restoration and improvement of buildings on the pier. Proposals to enhance the public realm and create a more welcoming space also received support from more than 75 per cent of those surveyed.

Feedback from stakeholders has also helped refine the plans. Earlier proposals for a restaurant within Building 1 have been withdrawn following engagement, while the revised scheme will also include enhanced fisheries facilities, new bait sheds and welfare facilities.

It also retains flexibility for future harbour improvements, including potential provision of a boat hoist.

Subject to agreement by members of the council’s executive and planning approval, the scheme could represent one of the most significant investments in Scarborough Harbour in recent decades.

Funding is proposed to come from a portion of the £20.2 million grant awarded to Scarborough as part of the Government's Local Regeneration Fund, formerly known as the Towns Fund, alongside investment from the council’s own capital budgets.

The Local Regeneration Fund has already supported several local projects, including enhancements to the West Stand at Scarborough Cricket Club, new teaching facilities at the Construction Skills Village, and the year-round Scarborough Fair programme of arts, heritage and sporting events.
