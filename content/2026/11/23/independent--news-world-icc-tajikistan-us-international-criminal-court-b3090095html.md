Tajikistan has taken steps toward withdrawing from the Rome Statute of the International Criminal Court (ICC), the presidency of the court's governing body said on Monday.

The country of 10.3 million people is the fourth country – joining Chad, Venezuela and Naoero – to initiate the one-year process of withdrawing from the ICC since the United States launched what it called a diplomatic push to "dismantle the court" in July.

In March this year, the court criticised Tajikistan for failing to arrest Russian President Vladimir Putin, wanted by the ICC for alleged war crimes in Ukraine, when he visited Tajikistan in 2025.

Burkina Faso, Mali and Niger ​announced their exits the previous year, denouncing the court as an instrument of "neocolonial repression".

The ICC is the world's permanent war crimes tribunal, which prosecutes individuals accused of genocide, crimes against humanity, war crimes and the ​crime of aggression when national courts are unwilling or ​unable to act.

![Russian President Vladimir Putin speaks with Tajik President Emomali Rahmon](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/23/17/download.-2..jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

In a statement, the presidency of ‌the ICC's governing body, the Assembly of States Parties, said it had “taken note of the situation with concern”.

“Decisions by States Parties to disengage from the Rome Statute risk undermining the collective pursuit of justice and weakening global efforts to end impunity,” it said.

“The Presidency calls on Tajikistan to remain committed as State Party to the Rome Statute and to continue engaging constructively within the framework of the Assembly of States Parties. The Presidency reiterates that all States Parties are entitled to raise their concerns within the Assembly and encourages Tajikistan to take part in a meaningful exchange on this matter.”

It added that the decision to withdraw does not release a state from obligations incurred while it was still ​a party to the treaty.

The Council of the European Union said it too “regrets” Tajikistan’s decision.

“Tajikistan’s membership reflected its commitment to the rules-based international order, multilateralism, and international justice. These are core values shared by the European Union and Tajikistan, and they lie at the heart of our relations,” it said in a statement.

“The EU strongly encourages Tajikistan to reconsider this decision and to reverse the withdrawal.

”The European Union will continue to engage with Tajikistan with a view to strengthening our shared commitment to international law, accountability and multilateral cooperation. The EU and its Member States remain steadfast in their support for international criminal justice and in their determination to fight impunity.”

Currently, the ICC has 125 member states, but that still includes the seven states that have formally started the withdrawal process, which takes a year to complete.
