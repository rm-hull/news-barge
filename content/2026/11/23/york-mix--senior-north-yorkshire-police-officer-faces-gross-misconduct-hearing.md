A senior North Yorkshire Police officer is to face a gross misconduct hearing over allegations he failed to declare a conflict of interest while handling a colleague’s grievance.

Superintendent Andrew Berriman will appear before a disciplinary panel next week.

The five-day hearing is scheduled to take place from Monday, 30 November to Friday, 4 December.

It will be held at North Yorkshire Police Headquarters in Northallerton, and will be chaired by Assistant Chief Officer Sarah Jackson.

According to the hearing notice published by the force, Supt Berriman is accused of failing to declare a conflict of interest when he agreed to act as the decision-maker for a grievance submitted by a Detective Chief Inspector (DCI).

It is further alleged that the Superintendent told the DCI that he “had no allegiances with anyone in North Yorkshire Police”.

The notice says this statement “was untrue if the Superintendent had a close personal association with an officer related to the grievance, a former senior officer”.

Before the main allegations are heard, the panel will consider a preliminary application from Supt Berriman requesting that the case against him be struck out due to alleged failures in the investigation by the Independent Office for Police Conduct (IOPC) and the “appropriate authority”.

Supt Berriman is alleged to have breached the Standards of Professional Behaviour relating to:

* Honesty and integrity
* Authority, respect and courtesy
* Duties and responsibilities
* Discreditable conduct.
