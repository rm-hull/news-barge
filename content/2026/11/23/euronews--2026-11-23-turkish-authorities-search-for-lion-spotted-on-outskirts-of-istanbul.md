## The National Parks Directorate said specialist veterinarians and teams equipped with thermal imaging cameras were assisting the search, which was focused on a wooded area.

Police and animal welfare teams in Istanbul are searching for a female lion seen prowling the city’s outskirts, Turkish officials said on Monday.

A resident in the Uskumruköy neighborhood spotted the animal on Sunday evening, and it was later seen on security camera footage.

Uskumruköy is on the northern edge of Istanbul’s European side, an area surrounded by forests.

“The location of the lion has been identified and the necessary care is being taken to capture it alive,” the Istanbul governor’s office said in a statement posted on social media.

Grainy images published by the DHA news agency showed the lion walking through a grassy area.

It was unclear where the lion came from, but some media reports suggested it had escaped from a nearby home.

The National Parks Directorate said specialist veterinarians and teams equipped with thermal imaging cameras were assisting the search, which was focused on a wooded area.

Local residents have been warned of the dangers.

Sarıyer Mayor Mustafa Oktay Aksu warned local residents to take precautions, saying: "We kindly ask residents not to leave their homes, both so that they are not harmed and so that they do not hinder the operation."

Aksu said it was not yet known where the lion had come from, and that they were considering the possibility that it may have been raised by a private individual.

Uskumruköy neighbourhood head Şeyda Üst also said in a statement that she had been living in the area for about 22 years, that she had previously dealt with reports of monkeys and snakes, but that this was the first time a lion had been seen.

Üst said teams were continuing their work in the area, that the lion had been detected using heat-sensitive drones, that the necessary warnings had been issued to residents and that they were waiting for the animal to be captured.

In Turkey, individuals are not allowed to keep aggressive and predatory wild animals such as lions as a hobby or as pets. Housing such animals is subject to regulations for facilities licensed under the relevant legislation, such as zoos and wildlife parks.
