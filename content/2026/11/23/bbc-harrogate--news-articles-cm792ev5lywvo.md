Hospital staff hope to give patients in remote areas the option to have rehabilitation sessions online

**Second-hand digital devices could help deliver rehabilitation sessions to stroke and falls patients in rural parts of North Yorkshire, hospital staff have said.**

Staff at the Friarage Hospital in Northallerton said many patients lived a significant distance from healthcare services, and those without smartphones, tablets or laptops were unable to access online services.

The hospital said donated devices could help bridge that gap and give patients access to remote therapy sessions.

Clinical lead Tina Hutchinson said: "Being able to deliver rehabilitation sessions online allows us to be more efficient and may enable us to offer additional support where needed due to the time and cost savings."

Hutchinson added: "Many of our patients live in rural communities and travelling to these areas can be time-consuming, with some patients living more than an hour away.

"It also allows patients to access us through another route if something in their home environment changes."

The stroke early supported discharge (ESD) team and the falls team are set to host a donation day at the hospital's Cafe Hub on Tuesday.

Any devices donated would be refurbished and given to stroke or falls patients who do not have access to digital devices, the hospital said.

The falls team works to reduce the risk of falls of patients who are vulnerable to them, while the stroke ESD team provides rehabilitation to stroke survivors following their discharge from hospital.

Therapy assistant Danielle White said while virtual therapy sessions were not a replacement for face-to-face care, they "provide another way for patients to access support from the comfort of their own homes".
