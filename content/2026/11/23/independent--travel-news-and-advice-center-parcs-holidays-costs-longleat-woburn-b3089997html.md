Center Parcs has reached a "tipping point where you can’t be passing costs" on to consumers, according to a senior executive at the holiday company.

Speaking on Monday at the Confederation of British Industry’s (CBI) annual conference, Raj Singh-Dehal, chief corporate officer at Center Parcs, stated he is "awaiting the bonfire of regulation" supported by several other industry executives.

Addressing the pressures stemming from regulatory burdens, Mr Singh-Dehal cited delays within the planning framework as "one of the biggest issues we face".

"The cost of compliance monitoring, reporting, form filling – it just grows and compounds every single year," he told attendees.

Earlier at the conference, Louise Haigh, the First Secretary of State and effectively Andy Burnham’s deputy prime minister, argued that greater public control would diminish uncertainty and reduce costs by enabling regulators to make decisions faster.

Mr Singh-Dehal said that the company is focusing further on cost efficiencies in order to avoid passing cost inflation onto customers, amid pressure from higher electricity and water costs.

![Raj Singh-Dehal, chief corporate office of Center Parcs, he is ‘awaiting the bonfire of regulation’ other industry figures have also backed](https://static.independent.co.uk/2024/11/05/12/05112205-5c8daf6b-a50a-4d6d-aa5e-0c140a8c010b.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

He added: “In terms of what we do as a business, I think we’ve reached the tipping point where you can’t be passing costs over to the consumer.

“You kind of reach the point of saturation of what can just be passed straight through to the market.

“But we’re having to look increasingly inwards to make sure that we look at our cost base.”

Ahead of the Autumn Budget, Center Parcs was among the signatories of a letter by hospitality businesses that wrote to the prime minister urging him to cut the hospitality VAT rate from 20 per cent to 10 per cent. Chancellor John Healey did not cut the VAT rate at the key announcement in October.

UKHospitality said the “vast majority of the signatories are small and medium-sized businesses that operate venues at the heart of their communities”.

In the letter, the signatories told Mr Burnham: “It’s time for a fairer tax burden for hospitality. Without action, closures will only continue to accelerate, jobs will continue to be lost and opportunities for young people reduced.

“More than 370,000 people have signed the #VATsTheProblem petition calling for a 10 per cent rate of VAT for hospitality, bringing the UK in line with the European average.

“A lower rate of VAT for hospitality can turn closures into openings, lost jobs into new jobs, and boarded up high streets into thriving centres of community.

“Alongside your policy to reform business rates for the high street, we urge you to bring forward your plans to deliver a lower rate of VAT for hospitality.”
