Jimmy Gomez will resign from Congress after a House ethics report released on Monday found “substantial evidence” that the California Democrat had “engaged in sexual misconduct”, including by “making unwelcome sexual advances and sexual contact toward a House staffer”.

The 19-page report from the House ethics committee found that Gomez “made unwanted sexual advances” toward a House staffer on two occasions, “including an incident in which he made sexual contact with her and another in which he was ‘forceful’ with her in his congressional office”.

Gomez, who represents parts of Los Angeles, lost his re-election bid earlier this month to the democratic socialist Angela Gonzales-Torres and was set to leave Congress in January.

Following the publication of the report, Gomez announced he would resign on 1 December.

The congressman said that he had “strong disagreements with the committee’s report and its conclusions” but said he took full responsibility for the “personal mistakes I committed outside of my marriage”.

Gomez said he cooperated with the investigation and responded voluntarily to requests for information.

In a joint statement, House Democratic leaders called the report “serious and deeply troubling”.

“It is appropriate that he is stepping down. Decisive action to set a new standard for reform is an urgent priority for House Democrats,” Representatives Hakeem Jeffries, Katherine Clark and Pete Aguilar, who are expected to serve as House speaker, majority leader and majority whip in January, said.

“We strongly support the effort led by Teresa Leger Fernández and the Democratic women’s caucus to reform the House rules in order to protect members and staff from instances of sexual harassment and misconduct. It’s time to rebuild trust in the institution.”

The report stated that “Gomez’s conduct also reflects a broader environment of sexual misconduct and toxic working conditions in some Capitol Hill offices”.

The allegations against Gomez are the latest in a series of ethics investigations that have seen a slew of representatives resign from Congress or lose their bids for re-election this year.

In April, Eric Swalwell, also a Democratic representative from California, resigned from Congress following multiple allegations of sexual assault and misconduct that ended his bid for governor and saw a dramatic reshuffling in the race. Gomez was a co-chair of Swalwell’s gubernatorial campaign and stepped down after the allegations against Swalwell were made public.

That same month, Tony Gonzales, a Texas Republican, stepped down after admitting that he had an affair with an aide who later died by suicide.

In August, the Republican congressman Chuck Edwards, of North Carolina, announced he would not run for re-election after an ethics committee report determined that he “fostered a hostile work environment and engaged in sexual harassment” by giving gifts and lavishing attention on two young female staffers.

Cory Mills, a Florida Republican who is accused of a variety of ethical lapses, was defeated in the Republican primary. In October 2025, a Florida judge granted a protective order against Mills at the request of his former girlfriend.

In November’s midterm elections, Max Miller, a Republican from Ohio, lost his bid for re-election amid a House ethics investigation into drug use and domestic abuse allegations surrounding his divorce to Emily Moreno, the daughter Bernie Moreno, a Republican US senator.

The House ethics report on Gomez did not make any specific recommendations.

It noted: “The constitution charges the House with policing the behavior of its own members, and the House should take whatever action it deems appropriate in light of the committee’s findings.”
