# Linton-on-Ouse: Labour MP and mayor rail against asylum camp plan

## Mayor David Skaith is hoping to use his mayoral connections with Prime Minister Andy Burnham to put across the local view.

Cross-party politicians from North Yorkshire are urging Shabana Mahmood to reconsider her plans to turn former RAF Linton-on-Ouse into a migrant camp.

A group of MPs - including Labour’s Rachael Maskell, Liberal Democrat Tom Gordon and former prime minister Rishi Sunak - have signed a letter to the Home Secretary, saying it “is the wrong location for a large-scale asylum accommodation centre”.

The text, written by North Yorkshire Council leader Carl Les, states: “Such a concentration of residents would be unprecedented in a rural community of this size and would inevitably place significant pressure on local infrastructure, services and communities.”

While York and North Yorkshire’s Labour mayor David Skaith has said that Linton-on-Ouse is “simply the wrong place” and said it should be used for a “housing-led redevelopment” instead.

Mr Burnham has pledged to raise the mayor’s “significant concerns” with the Home Office.

As well as the impact on housing, these also include public safety concerns with the plan.

Mr Skaith is calling on the Home Office to fund additional policing resources should it go ahead.

In a letter to Ms Mahmood, seen by The Yorkshire Post, he said: “We have particular concerns regarding policing demand, which is currently negligible in this part of the county due to the village’s rural setting.

“Public safety resources would inevitably need to be deployed if these proposals progress.

“Can the Home Office guarantee that any additional policing costs would be centrally funded?

“Strategic policing requirements at this site would inevitably put considerable pressure on the resources of North Yorkshire Police elsewhere in the county.

“Taxpayers in North Yorkshire already contribute almost half of the police budget each year and resources are always stretched due to North Yorkshire’s extensive geography.

“We remain extremely concerned about the security of this site and what measures would be put in place to ensure those accommodated at the site, as well as residents, are kept safe.

“We would welcome clarity on what measures the Home Office are considering to ensure anyone with a criminal record is not housed at the site?”

The intervention has come after North Yorkshire Police raised funding and resource concerns with the deputy mayor for policing, Jo Coles.

In a letter, Deputy Chief Constable Catherine Clarke said “a key challenge remains funding and cost recovery,” before adding that “significant uncertainty remains regarding the extent to which additional policing costs will be recoverable should the site generate sustained demand”.

The force has already raised concerns about its capacity to police protests at the site with Wetherby and Easingwold MP Sir Alec Shelbrooke.

Earlier in the year, far-right protesters clad head to toe in black surrounded a police investigation centre in Basingstoke after the group’s leader Danny Thomas was arrested.

A Home Office spokesperson said that it would continue to engage with police forces about the migrant centre plans.
