# How we can make North's industry rise again: Simon Dalley

## I run a digital agency with staff in Lancashire and South Yorkshire. But I want to explain why I have started a new research site on reindustrialisation given the experiences of the places where my business has its locations.

My working life has been spent online. I’ve run GrowTraffic, an SEO agency, since it started in 2009.

Most days I’m looking at how people search, what Google shows them and, more and more, what AI tools tell them.

So people are sometimes surprised when I say I think the UK needs to make more things.

![Simon Dalley shares his expert insight](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOjhjMjgzYTliLTQ5YTEtNDk1Mi1iNTI4LTM1ZWEwYTE1YWM1ZTo1ZTZkZTdkOS1kNjczLTQxYTgtYmJhOC1kYTc2NTFmYTZmYWY=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


I live in Bacup, at the top of the Rossendale valley, a town that grew up around its mills.

In both places you can still see what industry built: the terraces, the chapels, town centres laid out for a working population that isn’t there in the same numbers now.

The digital economy has been good to me and to the people I employ.

It hasn’t replaced what those towns lost.

This year I went down a bit of a rabbit hole on reindustrialisation and ended up building a website about it, ReindustrialiseUK.

I fund it myself.

I should also say that I have a stake in a small manufacturing business, so I’m not a neutral observer.

What struck me early on was how much of the debate runs on conviction.

Some people are certain that subsidies and state investment are the answer.

Others are just as certain they waste money.

Very few of the arguments come with the evidence laid out so that you can check it for yourself.

So that’s what we’ve tried to do.

On the site you can put in a postcode and see which industries employ people in your area, with the source and date of every figure.

Every claim in our research links to its source.

And when new evidence changes our view, we’ll keep a record of what we said before.

Policy should be able to admit when it was wrong, and so should the people who write about it.

I’ll be honest about where I start from.

I don’t believe central planning can work.

Nothing and nobody understands an economy well enough to run it from the centre.

But I don’t think the market on its own is going to bring good jobs back to the places that lost them, and I’m not content to wait and see.

Between those two positions sit a lot of practical decisions about energy prices, skills, investment and where things get built.

I want research good enough to turn into real policy recommendations on them.

Yorkshire has plenty of firms that still make things the world buys.

They know better than any think tank what stops them hiring another ten people.

If you run one of them, I’d like to hear from you so please do visit the ReindustrialiseUK website to make contact.

That’s the evidence I’m shortest of as we move this important project forward.

*Simon Dalley is the founder of GrowTraffic, which was named Small Business of the Year 2025 by the Barnsley and Rotherham Chamber of Commerce, and the owner of ReindustrialiseUK.*
