South East Water has plunged into a half-year loss following regulatory settlements over persistent service outages and a £29 million hit from the summer heatwaves that left customers without running water.

The company, supplying roughly 2.4 million customers in the south east of England, posted a £22.9 million pre-tax loss for the six months to 30 September, swinging from an £18.2 million profit in the same period a year earlier.

Industry regulator Ofwat ordered the supplier to pay £30.5 million in July following three probes into supply failures between 2020 and 2023 that impacted upwards of 286,000 people.

The business confirmed it allocated a £14.6 million provision in its interim accounts to fund the required compensation package.

Further supply breakdowns throughout areas of Kent and Sussex during hot spells this summer forced the supplier to spend another £29 million on emergency interventions, including stations providing bottled water alongside customer payouts.

The firm subsequently raised £50 million from shareholders in October, having issued a warning during the summer that fresh capital financing was necessary to ensure its ongoing financial survival.

The group insisted it was seeing the “early signs of improvement” after a management overhaul, with plans for a “significant transformation”.

It appointed John Halsall as chief executive earlier this year after predecessor David Hinton and former chairman Chris Train stepped down following a scathing report into the outages by MPs, who said they had “no confidence” in the company’s leadership.

Richard Morse has also been named as its incoming new chairman, to take over from interim chairwoman Lisa Clement on 3 December.

South East Water said: “We know that customers will not judge us on the number of initiatives we launch or the promises we make.

“They will judge us on whether their water supply is more reliable, whether we communicate better and whether we provide the right support when they need it – we look forward to providing the evidence in future results.”

It added: “The early evidence is encouraging and, while the full benefits will take time, our promise is to make those improvements increasingly visible in the service our customers receive.”
