President Donald Trump took to Truth Social Sunday to post a video of himself praying in a restaurant, following it with praise from the Trump War Room account calling him: “A President who isn’t ashamed to pray.”

The clip shows the president, wrapped up in a black winter coat, with his hand on the shoulder of a man as he bows his head in prayer while members of the news media and other diners watch on.

The footage is set to the 2020 song “Graves into Gardens” by the Christian band Elevation Worship and Brandon Lake and, according to the religious news website *ChurchLeaders*, was originally captured at the Machine Shed restaurant in Urbandale, Iowa, on January 27.

The site explains that Trump stopped en route to Des Moines – where he was due to give a speech, days after anti-ICE protester Alex Pretti had been killed by his federal immigration agents in Minnesota – when the customer at the table asked if he could pray for him.

“Absolutely! Come on. Let’s go,” the president answered.

![President Donald Trump prays with a diner at a restaurant in Urbandale, Iowa, in January this year, a moment he has revisited on Truth Social](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/23/15/47/Trump-Prayer.png?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&trim=28%2C5%2C184%2C0&auto=webp)

The patron closed his eyes and recited: “Lord, we give you thanks for this president. Lord, thank you for your hand of protection. Pray for continued wisdom. Pray for discernment.

“I pray for health. I pray for more peace, Lord. And I just pray for protection against the world, the devils, the flesh and all principalities, Lord. Thanks for the Spirit. In your name we pray, Jesus. Amen.”

​​“Great people here,” Trump said when he had concluded. “Great people in Iowa. They are amazing people.”

Unfortunately, the prayer for peace was not answered as, a month later, the president launched his Operation Epic Fury airstrikes against Iran, plunging the U.S. into a fresh conflict in the Middle East after he had explicitly promised to stay out of “forever wars” during the 2024 presidential campaign.

It also brought Trump no luck on the domestic political front, as the Republican he endorsed in that state’s midterms Senate race, Ashley Hinson, lost to Democrat Josh Turek on November 3, scoring just 46.4 percent of the vote to his 50.6 percent.

As for presidents being ashamed to pray, Trump’s predecessor in the White House, Democrat Joe Biden, was a devout Catholic and other recent occupants of the Oval Office such as Barack Obama, George W Bush and Bill Clinton were also practizing Christians, so it is not clear at whom the line was directed.

Despite picking up considerable evangelical support, Trump has not always convinced on religious questions.

He could not quote a favorite verse from scripture when invited to do so by Bloomberg in 2015, cursed at the National Prayer Breakfast, raised eyebrows by selling branded “God Bless the U.S.A.” Bibles and even posted memes of himself as Jesus Christ, upsetting the Pope.

The afterlife appears to be on his mind, however, as he recently told CBS News journalist Ross Douthat that he “hopes” he will go to heaven when he dies, adding: “I don’t want to go to hell.”
