Almost 40,000 children and young people across North Yorkshire have now benefited from a holiday activity programme as Christmas bookings open to provide vital support for families affected by rising living costs.

Since its launch five years ago, the FEAST holiday scheme has supported 39,794 individual children and young people through a wide range of activities, all of which include a healthy meal. More than 200,000 free meals have been served to children who might otherwise have missed having a lunch due to their family’s financial constraints.

More than 40 activity providers in North Yorkshire are now set to deliver about 80 sessions across North Yorkshire this festive season from Monday, 21 December, offering a range of events from sports and outdoor adventures to creative arts, science and technology activities.

Funded by the Department for Education, FEAST is the Holiday Activities and Food (HAF) programme for North Yorkshire, which gives children and young people the chance to take part in fun activities and to learn new skills in a safe and supportive environment during the school holidays.

Children from families facing financial pressures across the county who qualify will also be given access to free nutritious meals.

And parents and carers are being advised that bookings for this Christmas’ activities go live today (Monday, 23 November), with providers confirming bookings from Wednesday, 25 November.

Executive member for corporate services, Heather Phillips, whose responsibilities include stronger communities, said: “We know that many families are still feeling the impact of rising household costs, particularly during the winter months when fuel is so expensive and the prices of groceries too.

“FEAST helps by providing free activities, nutritious meals and opportunities for children to spend time with friends, learn new skills and try new experiences. Bookings are now open so please take advantage of this vital service.”

Over the summer holidays, children across North Yorkshire explored activities ranging from filmmaking and 3D printing to coding and digital creativity, helping them build confidence and discover new interests.

At Art Camp, Jaxon Pitt, from Harrogate, enjoyed everything from Lego challenges and 3D printing to smoothie-making and film production.

The nine-year-old said: “I just love everything about Art Camp. We do fun things that I wouldn't get to do at home.”

Without FEAST, Jaxon said he would likely have spent more time at home watching YouTube or playing video games. Instead, he has developed new skills and made friends.

Art Camp manager Dante Thomas said introducing children to new experiences can have a lasting impact.

He said: “It gives children the chance to discover things they may never have tried before and helps build their confidence.”

The strategic director of North Yorkshire Together, Laura Ward, added: “This Christmas, more than 40 local providers are putting on FEAST activities across North Yorkshire, and for families who might struggle to afford a holiday club, a fully funded place means their child can go along and have a go too. We want to reach every family who needs that support this Christmas, so if you know a parent or carer whose child could benefit, please tell them about FEAST.”

Executive member for children and families, Cllr Janet Sanderson, said: “Jaxon’s experiences showcase what programmes like FEAST mean to children and families in North Yorkshire.

“The feedback we hear from children, young people and parents shows just how much these opportunities mean and the positive difference they can make during the school holidays.”

The FEAST holiday programme is open to children aged five to 16, with free places available for those who receive benefits‑related free school meals.
