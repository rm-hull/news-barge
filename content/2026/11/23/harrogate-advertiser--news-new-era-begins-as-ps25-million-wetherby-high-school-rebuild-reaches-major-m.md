Pupils and dignitaries last week made their mark during an exciting milestone on the long-awaited new £25m Wetherby High School.

As the framework for the front of the new building rises up, messages were written on the steels by head students Eve and George, senior leadership team, project supporters Cindy Bentley and Alan Lamb, and Department of Education Project Director Laura Murray.

The new build was given the go-ahead last June following a near 20-year campaign to replace the ageing structures, some dating back to World War II, with state-of-the-art teaching facilities and improved sports provision.

Wetherby ward councillor Alan Lamb, who signed the first steel beam last Wednesday, said: “After nearly 20 years of campaigning for a new building, it was quite a feeling to stand there and see the steel frame going up.

“There were times when we came closer than most people realise to losing our high school altogether but it makes moments like Wednesday all the more special.”

The work is being funded by the DfE Schools Re-building Programme and delivered by Constructors BAM, with Bond Bryan as Architects.

Coun Lamb added of the ceremony: “It was particularly good to share the moment with Cindy Bentley, our chair of governors..

“Cindy, her fellow governors and the staff deserve huge credit for improving the education our young people receive, year after year.

“Now they’ll have a building to match. That’s a huge boost for pupils, staff and the whole town.

“I wrote three words on the steel - never give up.”

Back in 2021 a report by planning officers, to Leeds City Council Executive, stated that Wetherby High School’s condition was “extremely poor” and was beyond economic repair which put it at risk of closure.

Chair of Governors Cindy Bentley said: “It was a very emotional day to see the new build rising from the ground - something for which we have been campaigning for 20 years.”

A Wetherby High spokesperson told the *Wetherby News* that an exciting milestone had been reached.

“The rebuilding of Wetherby High School represents a significant investment in the future of the students and the local community,” they said.

“Both Coun Lamb and Cindy Bentley have been fantastic supporters of the school’s ambition to realise new facilities to enable the physical learning environment to match the fantastic learning and opportunities staff at the school provide.”

The school spokesperson added that the project offered more.

“This is an enormously exciting opportunity for Wetherby High School and working in partnership with BAM construction will create opportunities for students to learn more about work in the construction industry and provide support for the local community,” they added.

“This partnership has already benefited the Wetherby District Food Bank with BAM construction’s donation to the school’s one tin challenge.”

Construction on the new school building started last summer and is planned to be ready for occupation from February 2028.

Work will then continue to complete the wider site and reinstate playing fields.
