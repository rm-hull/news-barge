An outbreak of the highly pathogenic H5N1 strain of bird flu has been identified in the northern part of France, the World Organisation for Animal Health announced on Monday, as Europe faces a seasonal surge in cases of the deadly disease.

The spread of avian influenza, commonly known as bird flu, has alarmed governments and the poultry industry after destroying flocks across the world in recent years, disrupting supplies, driving up food prices, and increasing the risk of human transmission.

Citing a report by French authorities, the Paris-based WOAH said the virus was detected on a poultry farm in the Pas-de-Calais region, killing 80 out of 4,300 birds. The remaining birds were subsequently culled.

The outbreak was identified on Thursday, a day before France implemented a "high" alert level for bird flu, introducing mandatory restrictions such as the confinement of poultry flocks.

![The outbreak was identified on a poultry farm](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/04/16/15/73225724.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Earlier this month, Scotland declared a bird flu prevention zone following a rise in detections of the virus among both captive and wild bird populations.

The measure, which came into force at midday on 10 November, means that all bird keepers in Scotland are now legally required to observe strict biosecurity protocols to protect their flocks.

Mandatory steps include fencing off free-ranging birds, thoroughly cleansing and disinfecting footwear and concrete areas, and keeping domestic ducks and geese separate from all other poultry.

The AIPZ is set to stay in place until further notice and will be subjected to regular review as part of broader efforts to track and manage disease risks.

Surveillance data reveals that the highly pathogenic avian influenza strain is currently circulating in wild birds across the UK, with the risk to poultry and other captive birds rated as very high.

Between October and April, six cases of the disease were identified in kept birds across Great Britain.
