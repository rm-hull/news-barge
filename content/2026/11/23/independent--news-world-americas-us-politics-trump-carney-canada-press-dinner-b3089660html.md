President Trump was ruthlessly mocked by an impersonator during a speech delivered by one of his fellow world leaders at a gala event.

​The Trump impersonator burst onto the stage at the Parliamentary Press Gallery Dinner in Ottawa on Saturday after Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney jokingly pressed an “emergency comedy button.”

The impersonator, who wore orange makeup and sported a distinctive blonde hairstyle similar to the one favored by Trump, followed a man parodying Carney.

​Carney and Trump have been locked in a bitter feud amid a trade war between their two countries, which has seen the pair publicly trading insults and Canadians shunning U.S. products.

​“We’ve got two Carneys here, it’s a disaster. Isn’t this incredible?” the impersonator asked. “Two Carneys, he’s so popular. Almost as popular as me.”

![President Trump was mocked by an impersonator during Prime Minister Mark Carney's speech at the Parliamentary Press Gallery Dinner in Ottawa](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/23/08/download.-24..jpg?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&auto=webp)

​Then, the mock Trump began to reference the U.S. president’s repeated comments about parts of a man’s physique - notably the “a\*\*.” That joke was combined with a nod to the hit show* Heated Rivalry* - a romance between two male hockey players, one Canadian and one Russian.

​“It’s just like *Heated Rivalry*if the hockey players were straight and all goalies,” the impersonator said. “But he’s got a great a\*\*, doesn’t he? What a beautiful a\*\*.”

​Throughout his second term, Trump has repeatedly called for Canada to become the U.S.’s 51st state despite the idea being deeply unpopular in the Great White North. Trump nicknamed Carney’s predecessor, former Prime Minister Justin Trudeau, “Governor Trudeau” and has used a similar nickname - “Governor Carney” - for the current Canadian leader.

Tensions between the two countries soared in August following the collapse of trade talks, with the U.S. imposing tariffs on Canadian imports and the latter country retaliating with its own reciprocal levies.

![The impersonator mocked Trump’s repeated references to men’s physiques by referencing the romance series Heated Rivalry, much of which is set in Canada](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/04/20/08/2248296283.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

​Trump later signed an executive order declaring that Lake Ontario be renamed Lake America.

On Saturday, the Trump impersonator made a barbed reference to the ongoing dispute between Canada and the U.S.

​“Mark, we’re going to have an incredible deal, like all of them,” he said. “I call him Mark; I don’t call him ‘the governor’ to his face because I’m a coward.

​“But we’re gonna have a great deal. There’s only one thing standing in the way: me, because I’m crazy.”

​The impersonator also took a swipe at the ongoing search for a press secretary at the White House. Katie Zacharia, the most recent press secretary, left the role after serving for just five days and holding one widely panned press conference.

![Carney and Trump have publicly criticized each other amid a trade war between Canada and the US](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/06/16/13/2281267274..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

​“What’s incredible is I’m here because I need a new press secretary, and this is where the press is,” the impersonator said. “And I’m here to get a beautiful press secretary.”

​The Parliamentary Press Gallery Dinner is held each year and often involves party leaders poking fun at themselves and their political opponents.

The Trump impersonator at the event was Mark Critch - the star of CBC’s hugely popular *This Hour Has 22 Minutes.* The Carney impersonator was Canadian actor and comedian Mark McKinney.

​The speech delivered by the real Carney was also peppered with jokes and jabs at himself and other politicians, including Trump.

​“This is a tough room,” he began. “And I’ve been in the Oval Office. Alone. With its new interior decorator.”
