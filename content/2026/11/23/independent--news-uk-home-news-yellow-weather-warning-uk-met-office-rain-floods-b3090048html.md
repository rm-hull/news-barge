A yellow weather warning has been issued across Cornwall and parts of the South West, with heavy rain and flooding expected.

The Met Office has warned heavy rain could cause flooding and travel disruption due to difficult driving conditions and some road closures.

Approximately 30 to 40mm of rain is expected quite widely, but this could reach 60 to 90mm in areas where the rain persists.

“There is a small chance that some communities will become cut off by flooded roads,” read the warning issued by the Met Office on Monday. “There is a slight chance of power cuts and loss of other services to some homes and businesses.”

Areas affected have been advised to consider preparing a flood plan and an emergency flood kit. Those at risk of power cuts are advised to “consider gathering torches and batteries, a mobile phone power pack and other essential items”.

The warning is forecast to be in place from 10am until 11.59pm on Tuesday.

It comes after parts of the UK are bracing for an “early arrival” of winter with snow, frost and sub-zero temperatures forecast for the coming days.

Below-average temperatures are expected for most areas of the UK over the next few days, though cloud and rain will keep the far South and West a little milder, according to the Met Office.

A yellow cold health alert is also in force for the North West, North East and Yorkshire and the Humber until 9am on Tuesday.

This means that weather conditions could lead to an increased use of healthcare services and a greater risk to life for vulnerable people, the UK Health Security Agency said on Monday.

The UK saw its coldest night since 9 January overnight on Friday as a low of minus 10.6C was recorded at Tulloch Bridge in the Highlands.

Around 24 flood alerts are still in place in England, but the more severe flood warnings for the south Devon coast at Beesands, Torcross, Slapton and Dawlish were removed.

Met Office meteorologist Tom Morgan said: “We’ve got one more week of meteorological autumn to go but winter will be making somewhat of an early arrival for some of us through the week ahead.

“It’s all courtesy of a large area of high pressure sitting to the north-east of the UK across Scandinavia, trying to bring in this much colder easterly flow.

“But out towards the West we’ve got much milder systems trying to push in from the Atlantic and it’s going to be a case of which of those two air masses win through the week ahead.

“Generally though, for most of us, it will be staying on the cold side and there’s even the potential for some snow, mainly over the high grounds in the North.”

## Met Office five-day forecast

### Monday

Morning fog slow to clear but once it does, a fine chilly day follows with long sunny spells for most. Cloudier in Northern Ireland and south-west England, where occasionally heavy rain and strong winds will push in by afternoon.

A band of heavy rain pushes in from the West overnight, reaching Wales and western areas of England and Scotland by dawn. Dry and chilly further east with clear spells.

### Tuesday

A cloudy but dry day away from the band of rain which stalls in a line from south-west England to western Scotland. Remaining chilly for most with a noticeable breeze.

### Wednesday to Friday

Rain and hill snow lingers over Scotland on Wednesday, but mostly dry and fine elsewhere. Further rain arrives in Northern Ireland on Thursday afternoon and pushes eastwards through Friday.
