The Metropolitan Police have arrested eight people after almost a tonne of cocaine was found hidden in boxes of oranges.

In one of the biggest drugs operations in the Met’s history, detectives also seized £450,000 in cash, as they targeted an organised crime group that was allegedly smuggling class A drugs into the UK.

The Met said that five men were arrested at New Spitalfields Market in Sherrin Road, Leyton, on the evening of Sunday 22 November. The five men, aged 27, 33, 41, 43 and 58 were arrested on suspicion of conspiracy to supply class A drugs after a large quantity of suspected cocaine was found hidden in boxes of oranges.

A sixth man, aged 44, was arrested nearby with 100 kilogrammes of suspected class A drugs.

![Around 450,000 in cash was also seized](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/23/19/10/Oranges-2-MPS.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

A 48-year-old man was arrested on suspicion of the same offence after police conducted a warrant on a property in Cheshunt, Hertfordshire. Officers seized approximately £450,000 in cash during their search of the property.

An eighth arrest was made when a lorry was stopped near Maidstone and taken to Dover to be searched, with police seizing around 250kg of suspected cocaine. The driver, a 50-year-old man, was subsequently arrested on suspicion of conspiracy to supply class A drugs.

In total, 893kg of suspected cocaine has been seized in connection with the investigation, which is ongoing. All eight men remain in custody following their arrests.

Detective Superintendent Lindsey Billany, who is leading the Metropolitan Police investigation, said: “The seizure of these drugs, which would have been worth millions of pounds, represents a significant loss to this criminal enterprise and is one of the largest drug seizures we’ve seen.

“This policing operation demonstrates our focus on bringing down the drug traffickers whose trade fuels violence on London’s streets – and should serve as a warning to organised crime groups importing class A drugs.”
