## The EU investigation could lead to sweeping tariffs or quotas on materials used in defence, renewable energy and transport, as Brussels steps up efforts to protect European industry from cheap Chinese imports.

The European Commission launched an investigation on Monday into a surge in cheap imports of composite resins and glass fibre materials, primarily from China, amid growing concerns about the impact on European industry.

The materials are widely used in renewable energy, electricity infrastructure, transport, aerospace and defence.

The probe comes as the EU grapples with a flood of low-cost Chinese imports, which have risen by 25% over the past year, and seeks to rebalance its trade relationship with Beijing. The bloc's trade deficit with China has reached a record €1 billion a day.

The investigation is expected to last between nine and 11 months and could lead to quotas or tariffs on composite materials entering the EU.

Although China is the main target, the so-called "safeguard" measures would apply to imports from all the EU's trading partners.

The Commission opted for the broader investigation after imposing additional duties on resins and glass fibre reinforcements in recent years. Chinese producers were found to be circumventing these measures by shifting production to other countries, prompting European manufacturers to demand tougher protection.

“The Commission’s decision to launch this investigation confirms the seriousness of the situation facing Europe’s composite input materials industries,” said Ludovic Piraux, president of Glass Fibre Europe, which represents the EU's glass fibre industry.

“This case is about safeguarding the industrial capabilities we need to reduce our energy dependencies, strengthen our defence, transform mobility and remain a force in advanced manufacturing.”

## EU countries back car deal

The investigation had been delayed for several months as the Commission pursued bilateral negotiations with Beijing to curb Chinese imports.

Those talks produced an **agreement** in October, although it covers only hybrid cars. Under the deal, the EU will introduce country-specific quotas allowing 350,000 Chinese hybrid vehicles onto the European market annually over four years, with a fixed annual adjustment rate of 6%.

EU officials expect the measures to more than halve current Chinese hybrid car exports to the bloc.

EU countries backed the agreement on Friday, clearing the way for it to take effect on December 1.

But the deal has done little to ease wider concerns about the impact of cheap Chinese imports on European industry, with growing calls for a more comprehensive approach to trade relations with Beijing.

The Commission hopes to extend negotiations to other sectors. EU Trade Commissioner Maroš Šefčovič is expected to hold a video call with his Chinese counterpart, Wang Wentao, in January, ahead of a meeting in March 2027.
