Residents have been welcomed into Richmond Library’s new-look home, offering a fit-for-purpose space with cost-saving benefits.

Over the last few weeks, staff and volunteers have been busy moving resources from the former base on Queens Road to the council-owned Mercury House on Station Road.

The new base offers a more modern, fit-for-purpose space, which has improved public access and provides customer and registration services.

It will provide taxpayers with better value for money by making use of a building we already owned, reducing costs and making it easier to look after.

![Book shelves in Richmond Library](https://www.northyorks.gov.uk/sites/default/files/2026-11/3Bookcases.jpg)

Executive member for culture and arts, Cllr Simon Myers, whose responsibilities include libraries, said: “We are delighted to welcome users into Richmond Library’s new home after a smooth transition over the last few weeks which has been months in the planning.

“The building on Queens Road was partly leased from an external landlord, so utilising a building that we already own helps to secure its future. It also means that the library is now under the same roof as other important council services.

“Staff and volunteers are looking forward to welcoming existing users, and we hope the new base will encourage more people to join. We are also hoping to inspire more local people to come forward and volunteer to help with the daily running.”

Since 2017, the library has been run in partnership with the community organisation CRACCL, which manages Colburn, Catterick and Richmond libraries.

The organisation relies on volunteers for delivering the day-to-day services, with support from council staff.

In recent years, the building on Queens Road has suffered from maintenance issues and has resulted in parts of the building being closed off. Mercury House offers a viable alternative which is fit-for-purpose.

The new library includes an improved children’s section with the aim of welcoming more school groups. There is a dedicated IT area and ground floor space for the local studies collection.

There is disabled parking and bus stops outside of the building, as well as an accessible entrance.

Library user and volunteer Caroline Stott has been impressed by the new space in Mercury House.

“I walked into the building and was immediately impressed with how modern, light and airy it is,” she said.

“It is a much-improved space than the previous library building, with welcoming surroundings for existing and new customers.”
