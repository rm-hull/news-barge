**When TV personality Laura Anderson announced she was going on holiday with her ex-partner's ex-wife Natasha Gray and their children, some friendswere sceptical.**

"They'd say to be wary of that relationship and not see it as genuine," she says.

While there is no formal title for the mums and dads of your child's half-siblings, Laura says she thinks of Natasha as her friend.

Laura, a radio host and former Love Island contestant, has a three-year-old daughter, Bonnie, with her ex, actor Gary Lucy.

Natasha, a nurse, was with Gary for 14 years prior to Laura's relationship with him and they have four children.

Laura tells CBeebies Parenting Download how she and Natasha built a friendship to enable their children to spend time with their half-siblings.

Also on the podcast, co-parenting expert Aaron Dale shares his experience of getting to know his son's stepfather and gives tips to other parents in a similar situation.

Laura was 14 when she found out she had a half-sister from her father's previous relationship and now the two are "very close".

She felt a responsibility to make sure her own daughter didn't "miss out on that sibling bond" and the idea of spending time with Natasha and her children developed naturally.

"Maybe there was a point that I wasn't fully ready when Bonnie was younger and I was still navigating my break-up," she says.

"But I became more comfortable with the idea and I thought, 'No, she's right. We need to make an effort'... It felt like a chance to give [Bonnie] more than just me and her."

Aaron - author of The Co-Parent: A Toolkit for Parenting Together, Apart - grew up on the same estate as his half-siblings but says his father never introduced them. His two sons have different mums.

"I made a promise to myself that my sons would never feel like they were 'half' anything. My sons are brothers, full stop."

Aaron co-parents with his ex-partners and says he has a "great relationship" with the stepdad to one of his sons. He says a blended family creates "a wider circle of love" for children.

"I've seen this first hand with my son's stepfather. Not only does he have an amazing relationship with my son, but so does his daughter and their wider family."

He acknowledges it's not always straightforward but says it's the responsibility of parents to "put differences aside".

"Whatever the dynamic looks like between the adults involved, your children's relationships with their siblings deserve to be a priority."

## Respect existing bonds

Aaron says it's important to take time to get to know the other adults in your child's family including their values, personality, interests and what they want from life.

"Don't become so consumed with how this person might fit into your child's world that you forget to consider whether they fit into yours," he says.

He advises having a conversation about the roles you would like each other to play, what you're comfortable with and where boundaries need to be established.

You should also respect "existing bonds", he adds. "You are becoming an extension of a child's existing family, not a replacement for somebody else.

"Don't enter a child's life feeling that you have to take somebody else's place, and don't make them feel that loving you somehow means being disloyal to another parent."

Laura's advice for getting to know your blended family is to "make it about the children".

"Be as selfless as possible and remove any negative feelings you might have towards your ex," she says.

Even if you initially feel rejected when trying to establish a blended family, she urges you not to "give up at the first hurdle".

## 'Love all round'

Despite scepticism from friends, Laura says she and Natasha have proven their blended family works.

"I think they definitely changed their minds once they saw pictures of us all together. They'd say, "You look so comfortable and so happy.'"

Laura says the trip she had in the summer with Natasha and their children was "one of the best holidays" she had been on.

"I felt really comfortable and it was just love all round."

Laura and Natasha have booked a staycation in the Cotswolds to celebrate Christmas with their children.

"I've got matching PJs and someone's going to come in and cook for us - and we've got a Santa coming in."

She adds: "We want to have those memories, we just want to soak it all up."
