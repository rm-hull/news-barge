With artificial intelligence boosting a bulk of the U.S. economy, Donald Trump’s pressure campaign to ensure his ousted ally Jensen Huang was returned to Nvidia appears to have worked.

Roughly 11 days after his exit from the AI giant, the company’s board of board of directors reinstated Huang as CEO — despite ongoing investigations into whether his affair with a woman linked to Chinese intelligence has endangered national security.

Nvidia’s news release was published moments before the stock market opened Monday morning. Shares of Nvidia then skyrocketed more than 30 percent following the announcement, effectively recovering nearly all its losses since Huang disclosed the affair two weeks ago.

Members of Congress and watchdogs are outraged, warning that the president appears to have supported an illicit scheme to sell computer chips to an adversary while juicing volatile tech stocks bolstering America’s economy to enrich shareholders.

“Jensen Huang was compromised and exploited by a Chinese spy. Nvidia should have moved on without Huang and ignored the Administration’s inexplicable desire to save Huang,” said Republican Rep. Don Bacon, chair of the House Armed Services Committee’s subcommittee on cybersecurity.

![Donald Trump pressured Nvidia’s board of directors to reinstate Jensen Huang as CEO following lagging stocks for the AI chipmaker, despite ongoing national security investigations into whether the company violated trade rules involving the advanced tech and China](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/15/21/2026-11-14T002154Z_1599363360_RC27ZNA5MQUU_RTRMADP_3_NVIDIA-CEO-RELATIONSHIPS.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“Congress needs to get to the bottom of who approved high end computer chips sales to China,” he added. “We cannot say we want to win the AI race and then give China our best technology. We need sunlight on who is responsible.”

Nvidia’s press release claimed that the decision to bring Huang back to the fold was based on “input from senior levels of the United States government.”

Trump also dialed into the meeting, telling CNBC Monday that he spoke to the board directly about Huang’s return. “He’s done nothing wrong” and is “exonerated,” Trump told the network.

“We gave him assurance, at least with everything we know, and we know a lot,” Trump told CNBC.

Nvidia’s announcement said that “based on that input, the Board is confident that Mr. Huang’s reinstatement poses no national security risk.”

Outgoing Texas Republican Sen. John Cornyn isn’t convinced.

“No national security risk? Based on what?” he wrote.

“You gotta be kidding me!” he told MSNOW.

With Democrats poised to take control of both chambers of Congress in January, the scandal is likely to consume congressional hearings and oversight investigations for the final years of Trump’s presidency.

Huang also is scheduled to testify during a closed-door, transcribed interview before three House committees on December 1, and has also agreed to testify publicly before the Senate Banking Committee on December 3.

“This reckless decision to bring back Jensen Huang does not change the fact that Huang and Nvidia still owe Congress answers,” Democratic Senator Elizabeth Warren, who is poised to lead the Senate Banking Committee next year.

“The Trump administration needs to explain what ‘input’ was given and by which senior officials to support Huang’s reinstatement,” she added.

“This is nuts,” wrote Reuters national security correspondent Erin Banco. “You can’t just wave a magic wand and declare ‘there’s no national security risk’. We’re not in *The Office*.”

![Huang resigned less than two weeks ago after admitting to an affair with a woman suspected of having ties to Chinese intelligence agencies](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/17/15/download.-4..jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Tech stocks were dragging in the aftermath of Huang’s resignation on November 12. Nvidia, which has been the world’s most valuable company for roughly two years, lost roughly $1.5 trillion of its market value in that time.

The president — who held between $13 million and $62 million in Nvidia stock, according to financial records — announced on Truth Social that “the country needs Jensen’s leadership at Nvidia in order to ensure the success and prosperity of not only the company, but the American economy.”

Democratic Rep. Ruben Gallego said “we need to investigate what stock positions took place before the announcement.”

“This White House is the most corrupt in history,” he said.

Rep. Hakeem Jeffries, expected to serve as House Speaker next year, said “Congress will not be deterred from conducting a swift, thorough and wide-ranging investigation into business malfeasance” at Nvidia, as well as “corrupt abuse of power” at the Department of Commerce under Secretary Howard Lutnick.

![Trump has now declared that Huang is ‘exonerated’ despite ongoing congressional and federal investigations into Nvidia and whether his affair with an alleged spy has endangered national security by putting advanced AI chips into the hands of an adversary](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/23/14/2026-11-23T142223Z_1619699420_RC2RKEAJXLRU_RTRMADP_3_NVIDIA-CEO.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Huang’s return marks a dramatic reversal for the company, which last week had unanimously accepted his resignation and indicated that he would not be welcome back.

Trump, however, has repeatedly urged the company to put Huang back in his position, arguing that Huang was an essential driver for Nvidia’s success.

Less than 24 hours before Nvidia’s announcement, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent told CBS *Face the Nation* that the administration had not received the answers it needed about the “culture of noncompliance” at the company and whether Huang knew about the transfer of chips to China in violation of U.S. policy.

He indicated that officials may be investigating whether other executives may or may not have known about the scheme, and referred to Jensen's affair with the alleged spy as “a canard.”

![Democrats are poised to lead congressional investigations into Huang and Big Tech’s relationship to the Trump administration, with the president and his allies holding millions of dollars in shares with companies with close ties to the government](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/13/20/2026-11-13T192547Z_1156151811_RC27TNARL3S0_RTRMADP_3_NVIDIA-CEO.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

*The New York Times* also reported Sunday night that Alice Huang — the woman at the center of the scandal — was in contact with China’s spy agency for more than a year while engaging in an affair with the Nvidia CEO.

Huang’s resignation followed news of his affair with Alice Huang, an executive at Megaspeed, a Singapore-based Nvidia customer that the Commerce Department had been investigating over allegations that it breached U.S. export restrictions.

*The New York Times* previously reported that U.S. intelligence determined Alice Huang had been recruited to influence Jensen Huang to push Nvidia toward selling Beijing more of its advanced chips.

Pressed on whether the U.S. government even knows where Alice Huang is now, National Intelligence Director Jay Clayton told ABC’s This Week “I can’t tell you that” and “I do not.”

When host Jonathan Karl said there are reports she is in China, Clayton replied: “Oh.”

Clayton confirmed, however, that there is still an ongoing federal investigation into both her and her firm but declined to discuss Department of Justice findings until the probe concludes.

Nvidia’s board now says Jensen Huang “is the right person” to lead the company.

“I am honored to return to NVIDIA and lead one of the most consequential technology companies in the world at the beginning of a new industrial revolution,” Jensen Huang said in a statement Monday.

“I take this responsibility with the greatest humility and renewed commitment,” he added.
