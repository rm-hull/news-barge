Carrying placards warning that ***"Rent is eating into our wages"*** and ***"The rich earn more, the workers pay more"***, demonstrators rallied in **Brussels** to oppose**around €10 billion** in proposed**spending cuts** before**clashes broke out with riot police**, who responded with tear gas, water cannons and charges as a car burned and officers made arrests amid plumes of smoke. For protesters already squeezed by high energy costs, inflation and shrinking purchasing power, the** planned savings threaten to place an unfair burden on the lowest-paid** while undermining social security, employment support and help for families.

The street anger spilled over on the very day **Prime Minister Bart De Wever** met**King Philippe** to update him on strained talks over**Belgium’s 2027 budget**. Having set a year-end deadline for coalition partners to sign off on the cuts, and warned that the government could collapse if negotiations fail,** De Wever** now faces sharpening resistance both inside parliament and on the streets as he seeks to reconcile fiscal discipline with demands to protect living standards.
