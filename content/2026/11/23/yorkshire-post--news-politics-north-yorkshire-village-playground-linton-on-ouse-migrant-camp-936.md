# Linton-on-Ouse: Labour’s plan to put a migrant camp next to this North Yorkshire village’s playground

## The Home Office wants to turn the former RAF Linton-on-Ouse into an asylum camp.

When RAF Linton-on-Ouse was opened in 1937, it was seen as a new model for military bases.

Instead of having some fenced off site in the middle of nowhere, it was built into the historic North Yorkshire village.

The playground is next to the RAF base, which allowed the children of servicemen and women to play with locals.

The pub and village hall are within spitting distance of the site, which helped Britain win the Second World War through bombing raids and propaganda drops.

However, now that integration is filling the villagers with fear over the prospect of the former RAF base being turned into an asylum camp.

After it closed in 2020, two years later the Conservative government broached plans to convert it to hold migrants.

This caused consternation amongst residents, as the 1,300 men who would be moved in would roughly triple the size of the village.

These plans eventually failed on technical grounds, over issues with utility supplies, however in June this year, Labour announced that it was resurrecting the plans as part of its push to close asylum hotels.

The reaction from local campaigners was as you would expect. Prof Olga Matthias, who leads the Linton-on-Ouse Action Group, was asked for her reaction to the new plan: “I can scream. Is that good enough?”

She added: “Who knew that stupidity has such a big, long shelf-life?

“We’re utterly and completely gobsmacked. Why are they doing this?”

The chief contention from the locals is that this small village is not equipped to host more than 1,000 asylum seekers.

There’s no village shop, that’s closed, and there’s no Post Office either. The transport connections, unsurprisingly, for a small North Yorkshire village are not great.

There is one bus service which goes to York and runs five times a day.

Local MP Sir Alec Shelbrooke, who represents Wetherby and Easingwold, has been trying to make this point to the Government.

He said the village would be “overwhelmed” by the arrival of more than a thousand people.

“It simply would not able to handle that many people who can’t drive,” he added.

![Wetherby and Easingwold MP Sir Alec Shelbrooke. Credit: Sir Alec Shelbrooke](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/jpim-static/image/2026/08/28/13/06/Sir-Alec-Shelbrooke.jpg?trim=0,0,0,0&width=640&quality=65)


“The thing is, they’re not held within the base; they’re allowed to come and go, and the whole community just wouldn’t be able to cope.”

Then there is the issue of the integration with the former RAF site and the village.

Unlike the other two MoD bases that the Government wants to turn into migrant camps - in Piddington, in Oxfordshire, and Barnham, in Suffolk - Linton-on-Ouse is not separate from the village.

Sir Alec has invited the Asylum Minister Anna Turley along to make this case in point. So far, she has not accepted.

“I want to show her - there’s the playground - this is how close it is to the RAF base,” he told The Yorkshire Post.

“It’s only by coming here you can appreciate how connected everything is.”

So far, no Government minister has visited Linton-on-Ouse. Earlier this week, Ms Turley was in Hull to announce that the Royal Hotel will no longer be used for asylum accommodation.

She said it was “being handed back to the people of Hull – back to the community”.

![RAF Linton-On-Ouse in North Yorkshire. PIC: Owen Humphreys/PA Wire](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOjZhYmI1MjYyLWVhNGMtNDk2OC05YzU2LWIxY2QzNTExMzNhNjphYjJiNDZlMi1hZDBkLTRhNjctYjZmOC1hNGQyYTRhYmQwOGY=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


Ms Turley said these asylum seekers would not return to Hull, but would be spread out across the country.

“We are looking at former military bases which are more contained and more secured and have less of an impact on local communities. I know Hull has done its bit,” she added.

So could these people end up in Linton-on-Ouse?

The question locals are asking is - is this small village of 650 people more appropriate for 1,300 asylum seekers than bigger cities such as Hull?

The impact on the village is already palpable. On a recent walk down the High Street, The Yorkshire Post noticed eight “for sale” or “to let” boards along one road alone.

Children are being pulled out of the village school, while there are fears houses will be pushed into negative equity.

![Immigration Minister Anna Turley major asylum seeker announcement outside Blackpool Metropole Hotel.](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/jpim-static/image/2026/08/12/3/29/Immigration-Minister-Anna-Turley.jpeg?trim=0,0,0,0&width=640&quality=65)


Sir Alec’s office has surveyed the majority of households in Linton-on-Ouse to find out their opinion on the plans.

Staff asked the 320 houses to respond and of that some 211 did. Of those, 96 per cent said they would change the way they lived should the former RAF base be turned into an asylum camp.

A similar figure said they thought the plans would have a “very negative” impact on the village.

In particular, the respondents raised public safety and village character as problems, and 99 per cent disagreed with the Home Office’s proposal for it to be an open camp.

In the House of Commons earlier this week, Sir Alec raised concerns that the Government has already made its mind up on Linton.

When responding to York Central MP Rachael Maskell’s question around infrastructure in the village, Ms Turley said: “We are confident that the move towards this kind of accommodation not only protects against the impact on local communities that many people have seen across the country, but provides a fairer deal for the taxpayer.”

Sir Alec said: “Some of the language that the Minister has used in the last few answers is quite disturbing.

“It will seem as though some of the decisions have already been made on Linton-on-Ouse, because the Minister just said in response to the Member for York Central that these are the most appropriate places to house people.”

Sir Alec once again reiterated his invitation to the minister to visit: “The difference with Linton-on-Ouse is that the village is inside the site.

“The Minister said that sites would be self-contained. That would suggest that they are secure, but they are not.

“The children’s playground is but yards from where potential accommodation would be, and the school and the nursery are a five-minute walk away.

“The Home Secretary said that she would not open hotels or asylum centres near primary schools. Is that still the policy, which would rule out Linton-on-Ouse?

“The children’s playground is, basically, within the site. Is that a factor? Please will the minister come to Linton-on-Ouse to see it for herself?”

Ms Turley again declined to say whether she would visit, responding: “There has been no decision on Linton-on-Ouse; that is under consideration. I want to be absolutely clear on that.

“As we have made clear, we will take everything into consideration, whether it is local schools, local amenities or local play areas. All of that is taken into account when we make any application.”

A Home Office spokesperson said: “We are working to fairly house asylum seekers across the country, engaging with local authorities to reduce the impact on communities.

"This includes closing every asylum hotel and moving to alternative accommodation, including former military sites, saving taxpayers over £1bn on asylum support since the election.”
