Andy Burnham has thrown his weight behind *The Independent*’s “brilliant” Christmas appeal which will help lift young people out of homelessness by transforming some of Britain’s empty buildings into safe and comfortable homes.

In an impassioned article for *The Independent*, the prime minister urged the country to “put hope in the hearts” of Britain’s disadvantaged young people “and give them the best possible present this Christmas: the chance to build the future they deserve.”* The Independent*has joined forces with charity* EveryYouth* to launch Project Nest – a groundbreaking appeal which will give young homeless youngsters the best opportunity to succeed by giving them a secure place to call home.

From the moment he entered Downing Street in July, Mr Burnham has made it clear that one of his main objectives is to end rough sleeping in the UK, a cause close to his heart.

As well as the PM, the campaign has attracted support from private sector companies such as IKEA, Mitie, EE, Octopus energy, Specsavers and Barratt Redrow, which have pledged to give to EveryYouth as part of this publication’s campaign.

![Prime minister Andy Burnham has thrown his weight behind the campaign](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/21/00/e6e23dc730eab299f4f038b6232296e9Y29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzk1MzAzMDAy-2.86549763.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Urging readers to support the cause, he wrote: “Let’s rally to the call and show what this country can achieve when we come together.

“For too long, we’ve been living in a time where people feel that nothing can be fixed and everything is too difficult. But ending rough sleeping is an issue that can be fixed - if the will to do it is there.”

The project will simultaneously put a roof over young people’s heads while breathing life into dilapidated buildings that are blighting Britain’s city centres.

With the help of generous readers and supporters, *The Independent* is aiming to raise £200,000 to fund the renovation of three properties in Sheffield, Fife, and Basingstoke. Every single penny raised during the appeal will go to fund these homes to change young people’s lives.

Mr Burnham warned that most young people have “never had a fair chance in life”, describing stories of homelessness as “heartbreaking”.

EveryYouth has helped people like Ayat, now 25, who became homeless when she was 17 after running away from home. She had come out to her mother, who responded by trying to force her into an arranged marriage with her cousin in Egypt.

![Ayat was helped by EveryYouth](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/12/12/42/Ayat-Project-Nest.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The PM said: “Young people who should be full of hope for their future, moving from place to place, carrying their worldly belongings in a bag because they have nowhere safe to call to home.

“Some have been left traumatised by abuse from a parent or guardian - the one person they thought they could trust. And even for those who have avoided that horror, most have never known what it feels like to be truly loved by anyone.”

He insisted: “That’s not something we can just sit back and accept as a fact of life.”

The prime minister noted that changing the life chances of young people who have been left homeless “needs a sustained effort to help them get the training, education and ultimately long-term employment where they can save for their future and move into independent living.”

It means “rewiring the whole of the state to put care for people at the heart of everything we do – an approach that sees people as individuals, as names not numbers”, he wrote.

Last week, he gave his full backing to Alan Milburn’s review which aims to tackle the crisis of Britain’s so-called Neets - young people with no employment, education or training.

And, days later, when he unveiled his Brexit reset deal with the EU which included a youth mobility scheme for 100,000 young people to live and work for up to three years in the EU he said to them: “This deal is for you.”

He continued: “Last week’s report from Alan Milburn is a watershed moment. We are going to deliver a once-in-a-generation reform where we prioritise young people and invest in their success.

“And that includes getting things right for those facing the biggest challenges of all. So I could not be more supportive of this brilliant Christmas campaign from The Independent to help young people who are homeless.”

![Project Nest will give young homeless youngsters the best opportunity to succeed by giving them a secure place to call home](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/12/17/51/Project-Nest-bc2bd140.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

He underlined that his government wants to lead on this rough sleeping issue and has recently given councils a further £900 million to deal with the crisis on Britain’s streets.

Mr Burnham pointed out: “Last month we brought together people from all these different parts of society for the first ever rough sleeping summit in Downing Street. We had fantastic commitments to help them access not just the safe places to stay but thousands of homes, employment opportunities and the wider wrap-around support that each individual needs to build a more secure future. And that’s just the start of this society-wide movement to end rough sleeping in this country.

“Today, following on from that summit, some of those partners are now joining us in backing Project Nest. IKEA are offering to help with the furnishing of the properties, Mitie with refurbishment, EE and Octopus are offering help with the utilities, and Specsavers will offer free sight and hearing care to the young people EveryYouth supports. Barratt Redrow Foundation has agreed to match the first £100k the appeal raises.”

He added: “By backing Project Nest, we can put hope in the hearts of some of the most disadvantaged young people in our country and give them the best possible present this Christmas: the chance to build the future they deserve.”

*EveryYouth* currently runs more than 60 bed spaces across 21 homelessness hubs around the UK, most of which are in houses rented from local councils at a peppercorn rate. If successful, Project Nest will allow the charity to purchase its first property.

In Sheffield, the money will be used to buy and renovate a derelict three-bed house while in Fife and Basingstoke it will fund the renovation of empty, dilapidated one-bed properties.

![Mr Burnham gave his full backing to Alan Milburn’s review last week, which aims to tackle the crisis of Britain’s so-called Neets](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/23/16/2026-11-23T125407Z_1786316868_RC2MTOAHXTUN_RTRMADP_3_BRITAIN-EU-BURNHAM.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The accommodation will specifically serve young people aged between 16 and 25 who are in work. With no savings or family to fall back on, moving into work can bring its own challenges: young people in supported housing can face changes to their support, while those earning a salary can still struggle to afford private rents.

Project Nest breaks that cycle, with ultra-affordable, no-deposit homes where young people can live for up to two years, keep a job, save and get support to move into independent living.

Crucially, young people in these homes can also access support offered by youth homelessness charities to address the childhood trauma that leads to homelessness. The young people *EveryYouth*supports have been failed long before they lose their home. Virtually all grew up in absolute poverty, and 90 per cent experienced traumatic childhoods. Approximately half were physically abused by a parent or guardian, and 70 per cent say they never felt loved.* The Independent*’s editor-in-chief Geordie Greig said: “This is campaign by* The Independent* is a page turner for hope and decency as Every young person deserves to feel seen, heard, supported and given the opportunity to have a roof over their head. Backing Every Youth charity with our Project Nest campaign.

“It means backing every young person’s basic needs. The support and donations we have had so far has been very moving and motivating.”

Nick Connolly, *EveryYouth’s*Chief Executive Officer, added: “* EveryYouth* helps homeless young people build lives they are proud of. Today, homeless young people must choose between supported housing, which is vital to help them navigate the early stages of adult life, and employment, which is the key that unlocks their future.

“Through the Project Nest appeal, *The Independent*and* EveryYouth* are changing the game. Homeless young people can now have a job, a home and reliable help to build their new lives.”
