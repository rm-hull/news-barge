The boss of one of the UK’s largest business groups is set to call on the government to pursue a “closer relationship” with the European Union days after prime minister Andy Burnham agreed a new Brexit reset deal.

Addressing the group's annual conference in London, CBI chief executive Rain Newton-Smith will argue that unlocking investment is the sole route out of a "financial straitjacket" marked by low growth and heavy taxation.

Prominent figures from commerce regularly attend the gathering, where both government ministers and opposition politicians are scheduled to speak.

The event comes shortly after last month's autumn Budget, the first delivered by chancellor John Healey. Ms Newton-Smith will suggest the country needs to move past this "steady as she goes" package, which she believes "left too many question marks over our economy".

![CBI chief executive Rain Newton-Smith will tell the organisation’s annual conference in London that unlocking investment is the only way to escape the “financial straitjacket” of high tax and low growth](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/23/08/01M83JRQV1NPNWYD95KZR1SQG5.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

In her keynote speech, she will say: “In the last few years, we in the UK have been too ready to talk ourselves down. To put on our gloom-tinted glasses. They don’t sit well on me.

“The prime minister has brought a dose of hope to our politics. And just as pessimism can be self-fulfilling, confidence can be contagious.

“It’s time we gave a shot of confidence to our economy.

“The biggest contribution business can make to this country is not paying more towards our debts but investing in the growth that gets us out of them.”

The business group leader will also further encourage the government to pursue a “closer relationship” with European Union, arguing that Brexit delivered greater sovereignty but that “sovereignty without prosperity is not enough”.

She will also detail how the next phase of the relationship should become a “national opportunity” for trade, investment and growth.

It comes after the UK and EU struck a “landmark” deal on Friday, with agreements over issues including food and agriculture exports, emissions trading, and a youth experience scheme allowing more young Europeans to take temporary work in the UK.

![At Friday’s summit in Brussels, Mr Burnham made three significant agreements with the EU, including allowing 100,000 young Europeans to live and work temporarily in the UK each year](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/23/08/2306064105..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

A senior ally said the prime minister “deeply” believes the UK should rejoin the bloc after a summit on Friday saw Brussels open the door for a year of negotiations over the UK’s relationship to the EU.

Ms Newton-Smith will say: “This is no time to re-fight the referendum. But we must come back to the table with bolder ambition.

“Last time, the CBI and TUC called the negotiations a national emergency… this must now be a moment of national opportunity. For trade, investment and growth.”
