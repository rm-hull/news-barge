Good morning. The world’s nations have failed to reach agreement at the Cop31 talks in Turkey. In a dramatic conclusion to the summit, major powers rejected what they called an unacceptable deal on the future of the climate, which failed even to mention fossil fuels. The proposed deal “failed to meet the moment”, said Ireland’s environment minister, Darragh O’Brien.

* **What was in the proposed agreement?** The Antalya pact invited nations to electrify their economies, called on wealthy countries to fund climate adaptation for poorer ones, and urged signatories to minimise any “overshoot” of the Paris agreement’s 1.5C global heating threshold. However, it failed to endorse the “transition away from fossil fuels” demanded by more than 130 countries, instead including controversial provisions on carbon capture and storage technology that critics argued would perpetuate fossil fuel use.
* **What precipitated the collapse of the talks?** In this analysis piece, the Guardian’s environment editor, who was at Cop, explains that after a marathon negotiating session ran more than 40 hours past its deadline, a small group led by Saudi Arabia refused to accept any mention of a “transition away from fossil fuels”. While such holdouts would normally force through a watered-down compromise, nearly 40 nations – led by the EU, the UK, small islands and least developed countries – broke with precedent and openly rejected the weakened text, refusing to salvage the usual face-saving semblance of unity.

![Palestinians stand amid rubble.](https://i.guim.co.uk/img/media/3f84cee4d3a31ab236a50a7d425bc2b6fc6b6fe8/237_0_2475_1980/master/2475.jpg?width=445&dpr=1&s=none&crop=none)

Israel’s destruction of Gaza has left a devastating environmental legacy with poisoned water, contaminated soil and toxic air threatening the life-support systems that the population depends on for survival, according to a report.
