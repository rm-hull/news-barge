While most videogames got out of the way of Grand Theft Auto 6, on the day of its release The Binding of Isaac: Rebirth's free online DLC Repentance+ released an update. It included an easter egg daily challenge called Crime Mode (the seed is V1CE BSMT) with references to GTA like a wanted rating, guns, cops, and cars. Well, driveable minecarts.

What should have been an easy PR win for publisher Nicalis quickly turned sour as players pointed out that the music in Crime Mode sure sounds AI-generated. "It has that 'tinniness' that something like Suno generated music has", user ShweetStuff noted before looking at the music files and finding that, yep, there was Suno metadata in the file comments. Yet there was no AI disclosure on the DLC's Steam page.

Players were not thrilled by this development. Steam user reviews on The Binding of Isaac: Repentance+ dropped to Overwhelmingly Negative, with only 8% positive recent reviews. Players put the blame on Nicalis, who licence Binding of Isaac from its original creator Edmund McMillen. "Edmund please take back control of the game", one said.

"Woke up to a shit show," McMillen wrote on the hatesite formerly known as Twitter when he found out what had happened, "obviously I had no idea it featured generated music in the daily run, looking into how that happened and getting it removed, stay tuned."

As of the latest update, the AI-generated music is gone. "It's a little sad given Joey Kuras (game's sound designer) did some excellent sounds for Mewgenics", ShweetStuff wrote, "but I suppose this is the industry nowadays!"
