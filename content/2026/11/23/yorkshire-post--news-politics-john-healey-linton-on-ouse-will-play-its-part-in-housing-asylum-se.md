# John Healey: Linton-on-Ouse will ‘play its part’ in housing asylum seekers

## In the Budget, the Chancellor allocated £150m for the next financial year to turn a “handful of army sites” into asylum camps.

John Healey has appeared to confirm that Linton-on-Ouse will be used as a migrant camp, saying the village “will play a part” in closing asylum hotels.

In the Budget, the Chancellor allocated £150m for the next financial year to turn a “handful of army sites” into asylum camps.

The Home Office declined to say which former military bases the funds would be used on, however it appears the Government’s ambition is to use it to transform the former RAF base in the small North Yorkshire village of Linton-on-Ouse.

Earlier in the year, it was listed as one of three former Ministry of Defence sites, where the Government was applying for planning permission to turn them into asylum centres.

Speaking to The Yorkshire Post, Mr Healey appeared to confirm this. When asked what his message was for the residents of Linton -on -Ouse who are concerned about the plans for the centre, Mr Healey said : "I understand the local concerns.

“Any developments there will be well managed and will be secure but this is part of a commitment at the election that the public wanted us to make and backed us to do and that is closing all the asylum hotels around the country.”

Speaking while attending AES Engineering in Rotherham to help the company launch a new environmental innovation, Mr Healey added: “We're already closing some, numbers are down, we're also cutting to a six year low the number of small boat crossings but developing alternatives for those asylum seekers that do arrive here; processing those claims more quickly and, as we are, removing those who have no right to stay means that we have to develop alternatives.

“Linton on Ouse will play a part in that and we will close the asylum hotels across the country."

The Rawmarsh and Conisbrough MP previously told GB News that this would make “a big difference to many communities at the moment that have got hotels used for asylum seekers”.

“I know what it's like in our part of Rotherham. Our one hotel in the constituency was an asylum hotel.

“People wanted it back for their friends and families. It's now returned to a regular hotel, and people are using it for weekend events. They're visiting their relatives."

A number of hotels in Mr Healey’s constituency have stopped housing asylum seekers, including the Holiday Inn in Manvers which was caught up in the 2024 riots.

It is unclear which hotel the Chancellor was referring to.

The Government has consistently denied it has made a decision on Linton-on-Ouse, saying it will take into account residents’ views and a feasibility study that has been commissioned.

Planning permission was recently granted to house asylum seekers at a former Ministry of Defence site near Piddington, in Oxfordshire.

The local villagers held a symbolic referendum over the issue to leave the UK and become the “Principality of Piddington”.
