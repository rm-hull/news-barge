1,040 people in Skipton and Ripon claimed unemployment-related benefits in October, 155 more than a year before. Sir Julian Smith MP called the rise worrying.

1,040 people in the Skipton and Ripon constituency were claiming unemployment-related benefits in October 2026, according to the latest claimant count figures. That is 155 more than a year earlier and 70 more than in September. The seat takes in Ripon and Nidderdale.

Sir Julian Smith, the Conservative MP for Skipton and Ripon, said the rise was “deeply concerning” and pointed to the increase in employers’ National Insurance contributions.

## Overview

* **October 2026:** 1,040 people claiming unemployment-related benefits in Skipton and Ripon.
* **Change:** up 155 on a year earlier, and up 70 on the previous month.
* **Young people:** 160 claimants aged 18 to 24, up 10 on the month and up 5 on the year.
* **In context:** his office says the constituency’s claimant rate is still well below the national average.

## What the figures show

The claimant count, published by the Office for National Statistics, counts people claiming Jobseeker’s Allowance and people on Universal Credit who are required to look for work.

It is not the same as unemployment. Some people out of work do not claim a benefit, and some claimants are in work but earn below the threshold that makes them eligible. Reliable unemployment figures are not available for single constituencies, so the claimant count is the closest guide. The figures are rounded to the nearest five and are not adjusted for the seasons.

## What Sir Julian said

“The sharp rise in the claimant count across Skipton and Ripon is deeply concerning,” he said. “Behind every statistic is a person worried about their future, a family facing financial uncertainty, or a young person trying to get their first step on the employment ladder.

“Local businesses across our towns and villages have repeatedly warned about the impact of the increase in Employers’ National Insurance contributions. At a time when businesses are already facing rising costs, these additional burdens make it harder to recruit, invest and create new jobs.

“Skipton and Ripon has a strong local economy and hardworking businesses, but these figures suggest Labour’s policies are having real-world consequences. Ministers need to listen to employers and change course before more jobs are put at risk.

“I will continue to press the Government to take action to support growth, investment and employment.”

Earlier this month Sir Julian backed the Conservative plan to reform welfare.
