Another two people have been arrested as part of an investigation into a fire that killed five family members in Waterford.

Phillip and Kim English, and their sons, 12-year-old Callum and nine-year-old Jayden, died in the fire at their home in the Birchwood area of Waterford on 13 October.

A third son, six-year-old Elliot English, sustained critical injuries in the fire and died at Children's Health Ireland Crumlin on 6 November.

Another child was injured in the fire and is still being treated in hospital.

The men were detained under Section 50 of the Criminal Justice Act 2006.

![Flowers are laid outside the home of Philip and Kim English](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/16/13/01M52AN08YKHRZVHSWMHB8ZE18.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The arrests were made on Monday by the Garda team based in Waterford, with assistance from colleagues at the National Bureau of Criminal Investigation.

The two men were arrested on suspicion of alleged activities contrary to a law relating to “participating in or contributing to criminal organisation activities” between 1 May 2024 and 23 November 2026.

The two men are the 7th and 8th people to be arrested as part of the investigation, Gardaí said.

Investigations remain ongoing, Gardaí added.

## ‘Act of total evil’

During the family’s funeral in October, Parish priest Father Chestnutt said that an “act of total evil” left a family and community devastated and heartbroken.

![Four coffins of Philip and Kim English and their sons, Jayden and Callum, are carried into Sacred Heart Church Parish Centre, The Folly, Waterford, ahead of their funeral in October](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/18/14/aef5cb4da20237e793603737f5622a35Y29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzkyNDE3MDcy-2.86226582.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“I acknowledge, on behalf of all, our gratitude that the first responders did everything possible to save the lives of the family,” he said.

“On Tuesday morning, a force of darkness organised, came in the dark and devastated a family, taking away four innocent lives.

“Heartbroken, shocked, devastated, and yes, anger. What comforting words can find this day? Maybe no words, but your presence here says so much. For the families, you have received in so many ways an outpouring of love and support, which you can be assured will continue this day on.”

Bishop for Waterford and Lismore Alphonsus Cullinan said it was “heart-wrenching” to see four coffins on the altar.

“Four beautiful, innocent lives have been taken. Two loving, devoted parents who did their best for their children,” he added.

Kim’s sister also spoke during the mass and said that Kim and Philip loved each other unconditionally.
