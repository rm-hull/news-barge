A range of initiatives, from anonymous knife surrender bins to virtual reality experiences in youth clubs and visits to schools, are helping to raise awareness of the risks associated with carrying bladed weapons and keep North Yorkshire communities safe.

More than 11,000 knives and other sharp items have been surrendered since specialist bins were introduced across North Yorkshire and York in 2025.

The milestone has been highlighted during Operation Sceptre, a national week of action focused on knife crime awareness, prevention and education.

As part of the campaign, North Yorkshire Police officers are visiting schools across the county to raise awareness among pupils.

Separately, young people in Tadcaster have used virtual reality technology to explore the consequences of carrying a knife.

Knife bins are located in Harrogate, Knaresborough, Ripon, Tadcaster, Boroughbridge, Colburn, Eastfield, Malton, Northallerton, Pickering, Richmond, Scarborough, Selby, Settle, Skipton, Stokesley, Thirsk, Whitby and York.

The scheme, which was rolled out in 2025, is operated jointly by North Yorkshire Council, City of York Council and North Yorkshire Police.

It was made possible thanks to more than £15,000 from the York and North Yorkshire Office for Policing, Fire, Crime and Commissioning, part of the York and North Yorkshire Combined Authority, through its North Yorkshire and York Serious Violence Duty (SVD) Prevention and Early Intervention Fund.

North Yorkshire Council’s executive member for corporate services, Councillor Heather Phillips, whose responsibilities include community safety, said: “The response from our communities shows a growing recognition of the importance of this work.

“While North Yorkshire remains a safe place to live, it is important that we continue to take proactive steps to raise awareness of the risks associated with carrying knives and help prevent harm before it occurs.

“The knife surrender bins provide a safe and anonymous way for people to dispose of unwanted bladed items, while the work with young people in schools and community settings is helping to raise awareness of the potentially life-changing consequences of carrying a knife.

“By combining practical initiatives with education and early intervention, ourselves and our partners can continue to make our communities safer for everyone.”

As part of Operation Sceptre, North Yorkshire Police officers are working with young people to raise awareness of the impact knife crime can have on individuals, families and communities.

Detective Chief Inspector Matthew Wilkinson, of North Yorkshire Police, said: “While incidents involving knives remain relatively low in North Yorkshire, one incident is one too many.

“This provides an opportunity to work closely with our communities, particularly young people, to highlight the serious risks and life-changing consequences associated with carrying a knife.

“By encouraging open conversations, providing opportunities to safely surrender unwanted knives and helping people make informed choices, we can all play a part in preventing harm and keeping our communities safe.”

In Tadcaster, North Yorkshire Police helped the council’s community safety officers deliver an immersive virtual reality experience to young people exploring the consequences of carrying a knife.

The VR headsets place users in realistic scenarios involving knives and allow them to consider their responses in a safe environment.

Councillor Kirsty Poskitt represents the Tadcaster division as an Independent councillor on North Yorkshire Council.

She is also a lead youth support worker at The Barn, a popular community hub.

She said: “The virtual reality headsets were brought to one of our youth clubs earlier this year, and we identified a number of young people who we felt would benefit from taking part in the experience.

“We know young people can be particularly affected by issues relating to knives and violence, so it is important that we continue to have open and honest conversations about the risks.

“The experience is a powerful and thought-provoking tool that helps young people see the potential consequences of carrying a knife in a way that feels real and relatable.

“Several of those who took part needed time to reflect afterwards, and our youth workers were on hand to support them and talk through what they had experienced.”

To report knife crime or if you have concerns about weapons, you should call 999 in an emergency or 101 for non-emergencies.
