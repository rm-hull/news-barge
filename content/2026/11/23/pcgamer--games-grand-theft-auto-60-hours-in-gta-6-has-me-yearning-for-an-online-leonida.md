I honestly can't recall the last time I took such delight in existing within a virtual world as the way I have with Grand Theft Auto 6's Leonida. Rockstar has delivered on its promise of both breadth and depth—the map is *huge,* but utterly stuffed with detail. Large, open highways shrink into single lanes surrounded by construction that force me to slam the brakes. Sidewalks sparse with pedestrians eventually lead to densely packed retail areas, restaurants, and clubs.

Pockets of culture erupt all over: the sheer redneckery of Port Gellhorn, the Latin culture that permeates Little Cuba, and the affluent snootiness of Capri that has cops flinging microaggressions in every direction. You don't need to undertake any missions in these areas to understand the people that live here—Rockstar has nailed so much of the environmental storytelling and the conversations between NPCs you'll stumble on as you roam.

![Lucia Caminos holding a cup of coffee in Grand Theft Auto 6.](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/Nh9DUzrb9bvja9HYzSTmvM-1200-80.png)

It's a world that, when I first ventured into it, I had no interest in exploring in a multiplayer setting. I spent an ungodly amount of time in GTA 5's Online mode when it first released. I was there on the ground for its incredibly shaky launch (I made a character and then got stuck in that birdseye view loading screen for hours, incredible stuff) and continued to sink time into it over the years, particularly during Covid.

And I like it well enough, but its novelty has waned over the last decade. Its core experience is inherently different from what singleplayer brings, and I had yearned to return to a world less about blowing other players up with flying motorbikes and more about, well, blowing non-players up with regular motorbikes.

But after 60 hours roaming around Leonida and witnessing what it has to offer? I now want nothing more than for Rockstar to spin this map into the next generation of Online. Even outside of missions and driving around, there are so many little things that I would kill to do with pals.

Grab a pack of brewskis from the convenience store, hop in a boat, and go fishing. Throw on our best all-American attire and get down and dirty in some mud racing. Seek out other players meticulously planning a jewellery store hold-up, only to swoop in and steal the goods for ourselves. Hit up the gym for a little workout before nipping to a nearby restaurant for a bite to eat. Leonida is a place that is so rife with things to do, and I can imagine doing almost all of them with some buddies by my side.

![A car driving along the highway in GTA 6.](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/yRnewaDLb6YSuKR6wNQ6t7-1200-80.png)

It's also the perfect opportunity for Rockstar to introduce things that are sorely missing for me right now. A lack of safehouses makes sense with GTA 6's offline story, but I yearn to buy highrise apartments and suburban houses. Maybe even buy a business or two, for a little side cash. There's also the matter of *that gigantic casino* and the go-kart track that are currently inaccessible but the latter of which is donning a large "COMING SOON" sign. It feels like I'm being taunted. Surely you wouldn't put these things in the game with no intention of opening them at some point… right?

And even as someone who was never interested in nor dabbled with GTA Online's flourishing roleplaying scene, I've encountered so many instances where I've thought roleplaying in Leonida actually be pretty darn neat. I could become a RydeMe driver, or a Snapmatic influencer, or a backwater recluse who lives off the land. And when the game comes to PC? The potential for bending the world to account for even more professions becomes all the greater.

Rockstar hasn't actually said a damn thing about GTA 6 Online right now, but it feels inevitable. Leonida is the biggest playground the developer has given players to date, and unleashing players across its sprawling map is Online's natural evolution. And, y'know, considering how much money GTAO has made over the last 13 years, I'm almost certain that Rockstar won't leave that kind of opportunity on the table.
