# Drax says biomass an ‘important but smaller’ part of its group as it reveals plans for three solar schemes

## North Yorkshire power station operator Drax has said biomass remains an “important but smaller part” of its group as the company announced plans for three new solar projects in the north of England.

Drax said in a trading update that it had made a final decision in October to invest around £120m in three new solar farms, totalling around 130MW of power and set to enter service in 2028.

It comes after Drax earlier this year acquired Bluefield Solar Income Fund, later revealing plans to launch a dedicated solar and wind division.

The firm said in its trading update: “The group aims to grow its megawatt (MW) under management and create value by using its broad and deep portfolio of asset-backed generation with differentiated trading and optimisation capabilities.

![A general view of Drax Power Station  (Photo by Christopher Furlong/Getty Images)](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOjFjM2FkNzkzLWU3MGQtNDg5Ni1iNWY0LWE3YjFhMWQyMmM0ZTpmYTBmZjU1NS0yYjAwLTRhOGEtOGE5Zi1kMmM4NjQ5NDc0YWU=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


“Drax has a broad portfolio of UK-focused flexible and renewable generation assets, of which biomass remains an important but smaller part of the group.”

The company added that between 2026 and 2029, it plans to increase its MW under management by over 20 per cent, from 6.2 gigawatts (GW) to 7.6GW.

November saw Drax announce a partnership with Brookfield to develop one of the UK’s largest data centres at its Selby location, with Brookfield estimating that total investment in the development could potentially exceed £20bn, including an initial £2.5bn as part of the first phase.

It is planned that the first phase of the scheme will be operational from the first quarter of 2028.

Speaking on the Selby site, Drax added in its trading statement: “Drax believes that the size, flexibility, and location of the site make it an important long-term part of the UK energy system and the group is focused on options to maximise value from the site. In addition to an AI data centre, Drax is exploring long-term options to utilise multiple generation technologies, including dispatchable power, in addition to its existing biomass generation, which can support UK energy security.”

Drax’s North Yorkshire power plant has been the subject of much controversy in recent years. The site burns biomass wood pellets, a method of energy production which has had its green credentials brought into question by multiple scientists and campaign groups. Drax maintains that the use of biomass plays a key role in reducing the UK’s emissions.

Drax Group CEO, Will Gardiner, said: “The UK needs energy security and routes for decarbonisation which are affordable, whilst at the same time encouraging investment to support economic growth. Demand for power is expected to double by 2040 and as the share of gas generation reduces, there is an increased need for more generation across the board, including solar and wind, and the dispatchable flexible generation to support those and help keep the lights on when the wind isn’t blowing and the sun isn’t shining.”

He added that the group is “broadening its generation portfolio”.

Drax said it expected its earnings to land “significantly above” market expectations in the full year.
