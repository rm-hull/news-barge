The cash-strapped NHS trust that runs York hospital has ruled out cutting the opening hours of urgent treatment centres to save money, as the organisation admits it faces "significant financial pressures".

Reducing the hours of the treatment centres, which help prevent overcrowding of A&E units, had been suggested in York, Scarborough and Malton during a consultation to save £200,000.

But the York and Scarborough Teaching Hospitals NHS Foundation Trust confirmed the proposals have since been ruled out.

“There are currently no plans to change the opening hours of our urgent treatment centres,” a trust spokesperson told The Press.

“Like many NHS trusts nationally, we are facing significant financial pressures and have been asked to identify further savings.

“As part of this, we worked with colleagues to suggest potential opportunities for efficiencies, with changes to opening hours being one of the ideas put forward for consideration.

“These were initial suggestions to help inform discussions, rather than formal proposals for change.”

![York hospital](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21249609.jpg?type=mds-article-620)York Hospital *(Image: Newsquest)*

The York and Scarborough trust is seeking an £8 million support package from NHS England as it battles a growing deficit and attempts to deliver tens of millions of pounds of planned savings.

A finance report said the trust had an adjusted deficit of £18.4 million at the halfway point of the financial year, around £9.2 million behind its planned position.

The trust is currently forecasting a deficit of £30.5 million under its mid-range scenario and £45.5 million under its worst-case scenario.

Andrew Bertram, the trust’s finance director, has said continued shortfalls in the trust’s financial recovery programme remained one of the biggest pressures on the organisation’s finances.

He told a board meeting in October that the trust’s position had deteriorated further during the first six months of the year.

“We’ve delivered £24.7 million against the £31.8 million year-to-date position,” he said.

Meanwhile in August, the trust announced that the care unit at Bridlington Hospital is to shut and move to a community-based model of care for patients who no longer need acute hospital treatment.

The unit was established during the Covid pandemic as a temporary step-down facility to provide additional capacity for people who were medically ready to be discharged from hospital but were delayed in accessing the rehabilitation or social care support they needed to return home.

Clare Smith, the trust’s chief executive, said: “Most patients are already discharged from our hospitals with the appropriate support in place, and we will continue to work closely with community and social care providers to ensure that those who may have been admitted to the BCU [Bridlington Care Unit] whilst waiting for these arrangements to be made can be safely discharged with the support they need.”

She added that the closure of the unit “does not impact the future of the wider [Bridlington] hospital site”.
