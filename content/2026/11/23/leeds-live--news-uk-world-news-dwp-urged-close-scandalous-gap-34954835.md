# DWP urged to close 'scandalous' gap between full and basic state pension rates

## The Department for Work and Pensions has been urged to 'plug the gap' between the basic old state pension (£192 a week) and the new state pension (£251 a week from April)

The Department for Work and Pensions has been urged to "plug the gap" between the two state pension rates. The DWP currently pays the state pension at the basic rate for those who retired before 2016, and the full new rate for men born after 1951 and women born after 1953.

A petition, already attracting thousands of signatures, has been launched by Silver Voices, a prominent charity representing older people. The petition, hosted on the Change.org website, highlights the case of pensioner Susan as a compelling example of why the two state pension rates need to be unified, reports Birmingham Live.

It states that the retiree "is exasperated and angry every time the amount of the state pension is quoted in the media as it is invariably the top of the new state pension mentioned, which very few older people receive."

The petition further states: "Two thirds of pensioners, like Susan, rely on the basic old state pension which is much lower. Next April, the new state pension will be £251 per week (£13036 per year), whereas the old state pension will be just £192 per week (£9989 per year). Quite a scandalous gap, which is growing year by year."

The petition continues: "Susan, who is 78, qualifies for the old state pension because she retired before April 2016. Her state pension entitlement is £182.47 which forces her to claim pension credit and housing benefit in order to survive.

"Susan, who had a responsible job as a bilingual personal assistant and translator, was forced out of the rented accommodation she had lived in, in Somerset, for many years, under a no-fault eviction notice.

"She struggled to find affordable accommodation in the south and has now rented accommodation near friends in Wakefield. She pays £625 rent per month, but housing benefit only covers £460 of that."

Susan says: "I struggle to heat my home in the winter and to afford food and household essentials, even with the benefits. But as I paid taxes all my working life, why should I have to apply for benefits at all in order to survive? I should be able to have a dignified retirement on the state pension alone."

The petition further states: "The gap between the old and new state pensions was created in 2016 and the Government claims it represents the different contribution records of individuals and the fact that many older retirees also have an earnings-related addition to their state pensions.

"Many older pensioners, particularly women, do not have significant additional state pensions, however, and must live on the much lower rate. Those with small additional state pensions are also infuriated that the additional element is only updated by CPI rather than the Triple Lock formula.

"Whatever the initial complex reasons for the gap, the biggest scandal of all is that the cash gap is growing year by year as percentage increases are applied by the Triple Lock formula. The gap has grown by £1157 since 2016, about £22 per week.

"There is no case for the cash gap to keep growing and measures should be taken to consolidate the two state pensions into a genuine 'living pension' over a period of time. Silver Voices argues that the living pension should be set at the level of half average earnings (about £350 per week currently).

"This petition is initially aimed at the Pensions Commission, due to report in spring 2027, which is looking at the future of pensions in the UK. If the Commission does not recommend plugging the gap, we will directly lobby the Government to do so."
