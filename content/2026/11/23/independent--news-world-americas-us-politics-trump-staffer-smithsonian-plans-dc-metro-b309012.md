A Trump administration staffer was pictured working on plans for an overhaul of the Smithsonian Institution while on a busy Washington, D.C. commuter train, according to a report.

The plans focused on apparent changes at the National Museum of American History, and follow a damning White House report released in July that branded the leadership of the museum as radical activists.

The policy staffer was snapped working on the plans by a fellow commuter on the Orange Line of Washington, D.C.’s Metro transit system at the end of October and shared the pictures with *ProPublica.*

During the busy commute, the staffer was working on a document titled “Smithsonian Institution Renewal Plan” consisting of 25 items, according to the outlet.

Firing the museum’s director, Anthea Hartig, was reportedly one of the items on the list, along with a dozen other administrators and curators, and financial grants awarded to “woke third parties” would also be reviewed.

![A Trump administration staffer was pictured working on plans for an overhaul of the Smithsonian Institution while on a busy Washington, DC, commuter train, according to a report](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/23/17/49/GettyImages-2207404061.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Other plans the staffer was working on included “announce exciting cool exhibit on D-Day” and “no X rated exhibitions policy,” which potentially referred to a gay history exhibit.

New exhibits were listed on the plan, including one called “The Riches of Our Western Civilization” and one on “Great Families of American History.”

The plans also called for a new “Colossus Statue” of George Washington to sit at the museum’s entrance, according to the outlet.

*The Independent* has contacted the White House for comment.

![The plans follow a damning White House report released in July that branded the leadership of the institution, particularly the National Museum of American History, as radical activists](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/23/17/21/GettyImages-2289386960.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The Smithsonian Institution, established by Congress in 1846, oversees 20 museums and is run by a board of 17, led by the chief justice of the Supreme Court.

In March, President Donald Trump had already indicated his intent to enforce changes at the Smithsonian through an executive order, which aimed to cut funding for programs promoting “divisive narratives” and “improper ideology,” as part of his ongoing critique of what he perceives as overly liberal culture.

“The Smithsonian Institution, and the National Museum of American History in particular, under its current leadership and current interpretive ideology, cannot be trusted to tell America’s story honestly and in a way that is inspiring, unifying, and worthy of our great republic,” the report said.

Trump has sought changes at other Smithsonian institutions. In January, the Smithsonian’s National Portrait Gallery swapped out Trump’s portrait and removed accompanying text that referenced his impeachments and the January 6, 2021, Capitol riots.

The White House shared photos of the new portrait at the time in a post on X. The original text accompanying the portrait was removed and replaced by a new placard that only lists the president’s years in office.

The change came after a Trump administration official complained about the accompanying text.
