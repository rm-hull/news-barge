President Donald Trump was watching CNBC on Monday morning, waiting to see how the market would take the news that Nvidia’s board had put Jensen Huang back in charge, when he picked up the phone to claim the credit.

“I watched the screen and I said, boy, that thing’s like a rocket ship going in the wrong direction,” he told the hosts. “Now it’s a rocket ship going in the right direction.”

But the rocket ship was the smaller part of the call. For the rest of it, Trump described how he got a private company’s board to take back the CEO it had just lost, and what he told the directors about national security when they asked.

Huang resigned on Nov. 12 amid reports of affairs, one of them with Alice Huang (no relation), a director of Megaspeed, an Nvidia customer, accused of unlawfully sending American chips to China.

U.S. officials reportedly identified her as a Chinese intelligence asset, another term for an alleged spy. Days before Monday, the board said it wasn’t considering a return for Huang — until this past weekend, when Trump and his allies spent days browbeating board members into reversing themselves.

![In October, Trump awarded Huang the National Medal of Science. Now he’s awarded the disgraced CEO his old job back](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/09/12/2298607332..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“I spoke to ’em. Let me just put it that way, and I convinced ’em,” Trump said Monday. It was “a lot easier,” he said, once “the stock starts dropping 40, 50 percent,” a decline The Independent reported at more than 20 percent after the company’s earnings call.

He wouldn’t reveal to whom he spoke beyond saying, “Basically, I was represented at the board,” and said he “called ’em a little bit through representatives.” Asked whether the board would have acted without him, he said: “The answer is no.”

He was just as direct about the alternative. “Some other board would have fought it, and if they would have fought it, it would have been a disaster, in my opinion,” Trump said. This board, he said, “decided to do what *we* wanted to do.”

He said he worked on the coercion effort with Vice President JD Vance, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent and Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick.

Trump’s own timeline puts the stock first. Asked whether the previous week’s tech sell-off figured in his thinking, he said, “Probably so,” adding that it would have been “a lot tougher if I made the call a week and a half ago,” when “everybody was riding high.”

Asked whether the sell-off had him worried about an AI bubble, he answered: “I’m not concerned now because I think we have one of our great stars back in play.” Huang, as he described it, was like a star on a baseball team: lose one, and “the team isn’t going to do as well,” and “you saw that it affected the market.” The sequence he described looks less like a security judgment than a reaction to a falling stock price.

![Huang is one of the technology executives who is closest to Trump and other members of his administration](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/23/19/2026-11-23T151536Z_862976933_RC27TNAMYK5I_RTRMADP_3_NVIDIA-CEO-TRUMP.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

He tied it to his own record by complaining how he’d “watched our country lose $5 trillion because of a bad election,” referring to the drop in share prices since the Democrats took the House and Senate in resounding fashion, at which point he decided: “I better get involved.”

He then said he made the deal without knowing how investors would take it: “It’s possible the market could have gone down.”

Asked where he would stop, with Microsoft, Alphabet, OpenAI and Anthropic mentioned, Trump said he could “only say it’s touch, it’s feel” and that “there’s not going to be a formula.” He added: “I run the country. I don’t run Nvidia. But Nvidia is a big part of it.”

The board said input from “senior levels of the United States government” left it “confident” that Huang is no national security risk. It didn’t say whose input that was. Trump did.

“They did ask for assurance on the national security threat, and we gave them assurance, at least with everything we know, and we know a lot,” the president said. Asked whether that was his own view or his national security team’s, Trump said: “Yeah, it’s both.”

Trump opened the call by saying, “We’ve done a very thorough investigation.” Later, he said “a couple of weeks ago, we hadn’t done thorough investigations” and that the effort “really heated up over the last fairly short period of time because we wanted to get an answer.” That overlaps with the days Nvidia’s shares were falling, not with any quelling of concerns over either Huang being compromised or over the damn-the-torpedoes push to advance the technology itself, which Trump has insisted all along could be regulate by the companies themselves.

Asked what the CIA told him, he said: “I don’t want to get into that.” He added: “I have clearance, and if I didn’t have clearance, I wouldn’t have done the deal.”

![Trump reportedly enlisted Vice President JD Vance to convince tech executives to lobby for Huang's return](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/23/20/2306100795..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

On what Nvidia sold to the company where Alice Huang worked, Trump said: “I don’t even know what they sold, if they sold, and which chip they sold, because that’s a big thing.” If chips later reached China, he said, “that wouldn’t be Jensen’s fault,” and Jensen Huang “certainly did not know about it, or at least that’s been our very powerful impression.”

Asked then about reports that Alice Huang was in contact with an officer of China’s Ministry of State Security, he said: “Well, who isn’t in contact? ... every time you have a Chinese woman that’s good-looking or beautiful, they say she’s a spy.” Still, he said, he didn’t ask Xi Jinping whether she was one when he visited the Chinese leader last week: “I didn’t discuss that, no.”

Trump said the bigger risk, bigger than potential China spying being allowed by one of the US’s biggest tech leaders, was losing Huang. “The bigger threat would be losing the brain power of somebody like Jensen,” he said, adding that Huang “could take the next plane to China, and I guarantee you, they would give him whatever he wanted.” The board’s investigation into whether Huang was targeted by a “foreign intelligence effort” was still open when it reinstated him.

The attorney general’s statement, which Trump cited in his Truth Social post, says the government “does not possess evidence that he has engaged in any unlawful conduct,” which addresses crimes and says nothing on whether Huang was compromised.

Republican leaders of three House committees have asked Huang for closed-door testimony, and the SEC and Justice Department are investigating the company over matters that include alleged chip smuggling, The Independent has reported. Asked on Monday what he would tell the China hawks in his own party, Trump said: “They’re going to be great.”
