Beneath a record-breaking **141-metre rainbow flag** unfurled along**Avenida Atlântica**, revellers turned** Rio de Janeiro’s Copacabana**seafront into a roaring celebration of music, ornate drag costumes and pulsing sound trucks for the** 31st Rio Pride Parade**, one of** Brazil’s**oldest. Starting from** Posto 5**on** Copacabana beach**and featuring** more than 200 artists**staged by** Grupo Arco-Íris**under the banner***"We recognise every form of love and existence as legitimate"*,**marked** 15 years**since** 10 judges**on** Brazil’s Supreme Court**unanimously ruled on** 5 May 2011** to recognise same-sex stable unions as family units with equal rights and duties.

Yet the beachfront euphoria unfolded against a sobering backdrop of persistent hostility: parade coordinator **Cláudio Nascimento** noted that hard-won judicial milestones, from civil unions and marriage equality to transgender name and gender recognition and the criminalisation of LGBTphobia, have not stemmed day-to-day abuse, with**Brazil’s 2026 Atlas da Violência** logging**10,250 reports** of violence against gay and bisexual people in**2024**(** up 5.5%**on** 2023**) alongside a** 2.5% rise** in cases involving transgender and *travesti*people. Urging**Congress** to enshrine court victories into robust legislation and strengthen protections for transgender citizens, organisers made the**Copacabana march** both a tribute to past breakthroughs and an urgent demand for safety and equality.
