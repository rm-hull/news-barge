Israeli professors, students and rights activists are calling for the immediate release of Mahdi Tounsi, a sociology student at Jerusalem’s Hebrew University who has been held without charge for more than two weeks after he was seized from his dorm room.

The detention comes amid a racist campaign of delegitimisation against Palestinian citizens of Israel, whose votes were critical to stripping Benjamin Netanyahu’s coalition of its majority in elections last month.

Police took Tounsi, 26, a criminology and sociology student from East Jerusalem who is active in Jewish-Palestinian campaign groups, from his dorm room on 8 November.

When he appeared by video link at a court hearing on 19 November he had visible bruises on his face, did not have the glasses he needs to see properly, and his hair and beard had been shaved.

Tounsi told his lawyer that police beat him in the dorm, in the patrol car and at the police station, Israeli media reported. Interrogators questioned him about his activism with Israeli-Palestinian organisations Standing Together and Ir Amim.

Both groups called for his immediate release and warned against using security agencies to silence political activity and critical speech.

“[This] could happen to any student and any one of us, especially in a month when the government is persecuting Arab citizens, from [journalist] Abed Abu Shehadeh to the leaders of Arab political parties,” Standing Together said in a statement, calling on the university to intervene to ensure a fair judicial process.

Ir Amim described Tounsi as thoughtful, gentle and a valued partner “in movements bringing Palestinians and Israelis together”, in a statement that also warned of “serious concerns for his safety, wellbeing, and right to due process”.

After losing the election, Netanyahu and his allies weaponised false accusations of mass fraud – Israel’s election commission validated the results – in an attempt to hold on to power by delegitimising the votes of Palestinian citizens of Israel.

The prime minister has also been accused of politicising security institutions in his campaign against Palestinian-led parties, and fostering a rise in racist rhetoric.

When police powers to hold Tounsi under the original arrest warrant expired last week, they submitted an order to hold him without trial in administrative detention, signed by the defence minister, Israel Katz.

The administrative detention law allows Israel to hold individuals for up to six months without charging them or allowing them to see evidence against them, although it has to be presented in a closed court.

Because the Shin Bet security force did not send a representative to the hearing on Tounsi’s detention last week, it was postponed until Tuesday.

Ahead of the court hearing, Jewish and Palestinian students demonstrated at universities across the country, including Jerusalem and Tel Aviv, with signs including “Free Mahdi” and “Kidnapping Palestinians will not silence justice”.

The university authorities did not inform his family, and his detention only came to light after concerned friends reported him missing.

Hebrew University said in a statement that it was notified about the arrest shortly before police seized Tounsi, that no university representatives were present when he was detained and police afterwards said the arrest took place “without unusual incidents”.

It did not directly address the allegations of police violence, his visible bruising, the shaving of his head and beard, removal of his glasses or the shifting legal framework.

“The university is not a party to the legal proceedings involving the student, and we do not have information that would allow us to comment on the circumstances leading to the arrest or on the ongoing legal proceedings,” the statement said.

“We are concerned for his wellbeing,” the statement added, saying the university hoped “that his rights will be fully respected throughout the process”.

Israeli police did not respond to requests for comment about Tounsi’s detention.

Tounsi is a resident of East Jerusalem, not a Palestinian citizen of Israel. Palestinians holding that status can move freely inside Israel and Jerusalem but are stateless and can not vote in Israeli national elections.
