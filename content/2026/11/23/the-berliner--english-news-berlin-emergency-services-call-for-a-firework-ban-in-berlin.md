The debate around banning fireworks is a topic of contention every year in Berlin. This year, the Berlin Hospital Association, the State Firefighters Association, and the State Police Union have come together to join the conversation, they too want to implement the ban. However, this requires a change in federal law. Each state could then decide on a ban on private fireworks for New Year’s Eve.

According to a joint statement obtained by the German Press Agency, the groups are calling for a Länderöffnungsklausel (a state opening clause) in explosives law, which would allow Berlin to decide on firework bans and zones for permitted fireworks on New Year’s Eve.

Last year, more than 2.2 million people signed a petition to implement a firework ban in Germany. Surveys have also shown that 75% of Berliners support a New Year’s Eve ban.

“Every year on New Year’s Eve, we see an increase in injuries caused by the improper or reckless handling of fireworks,” explained Marc Schreiner, Managing Director of the Berlin Hospital Association. Children are particularly at risk when they playfully set off explosives they find on the ground.

A ban on fireworks on New Year’s Eve would relieve the burden on hospital emergency rooms and create space for those who really need care. The BG Trauma Centre alone treated more than 30 seriously injured people on New Year’s Eve 2025/26, including 8 children. According to the Charité hospital, 41 people were injured by fireworks between 5 p.m. and 6 a.m.

The Berlin Fire Department responded to an average of 893 emergency medical service calls and between 643 and 825 fire calls over the past three New Year’s Eves. In 2025/26, 35 police officers were injured and 47 vehicles burned.

Designated firework-free zones have existed in Berlin for some time. On New Year’s Eve 2025/26, these zones included Alexanderplatz, the Steinmetzkiez neighbourhood in Schöneberg, parts of Sonnenallee in Neukölln and adjacent side streets, as well as the Admiralbrücke and adjacent streets in Kreuzberg.
