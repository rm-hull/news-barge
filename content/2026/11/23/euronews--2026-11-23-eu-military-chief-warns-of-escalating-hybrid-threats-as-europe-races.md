## General Seán Clancy says Europe is better prepared to defend itself but must speed up efforts to close critical military gaps, amid growing threats from Russia and uncertainty over US military commitments.

Europe faces an escalating wave of hybrid threats, stretching from the Baltic to the Black Sea, and must urgently accelerate its military preparedness to confront them, the EU's most senior military official has warned.

General Seán Clancy, chairman of the EU Military Committee, told Euronews on Monday that the proliferation of asymmetric attacks was his greatest security concern, pointing to incidents in Poland, Denmark and Germany.

"The main concern is the spread and escalation of hybrid threats," he said in an interview with Europe Today. "We've seen this particularly over the past six to twelve months, from the Baltics to the Black Sea."

His warning follows another weekend of Russian attacks on Ukraine and comes as European governments grapple with growing concerns about sabotage, drone incursions and other forms of hybrid warfare.

Clancy said the threats were designed to destabilise not just Ukraine but European democracies more broadly.

"That's why readiness and preparedness are at the centre of the EU's strategy," he said. "The sooner member states can act collectively in a crisis, the better our chances of containing it."

## Military chiefs meet

Europe's military chiefs will meet in Brussels on Wednesday to assess the continent's defence readiness, with Ukraine and the Coalition of the Willing high on the agenda.

Clancy acknowledged that enormous challenges remained, particularly in building up weapons stockpiles, industrial production capacity and the ability to sustain military operations over time.

"We're in a better position than we were. But we need to move faster and accelerate our preparedness," he said.

"Europe is stepping up. It's leaning in. We're closing the gaps."

However, Europe remains heavily dependent on the United States for critical military capabilities, including air defence, air-to-air refuelling and other strategic support.

Clancy dismissed fears that Washington's ongoing review of its military presence in Europe would undermine the continent's security, insisting that any transition would be managed jointly.

The transatlantic partnership, he said, was "without equivocation, our most important relationship, and it will remain so, now and into the future."

Asked whether Europe could defend itself against Russia without American support, Clancy expressed confidence in its armed forces.

"We are ready to defend and secure Europe," he said, while stressing that NATO remained ultimately responsible for the continent's collective defence.
