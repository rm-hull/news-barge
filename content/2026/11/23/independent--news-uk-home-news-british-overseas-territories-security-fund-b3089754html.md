The UK is stepping up security support for British Overseas Territories in the face of rising threats from organised crime and “hostile actors” across the globe.

On Monday, the Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office (FCDO) unveiled an £18 million will be allocated to tackling emerging security threats.

New support will focus on policing, criminal justice and prisons, maritime security, cyber and safeguarding capabilities.

The FCDO says that it will draw on UK expertise to enable “increased information sharing and cooperation” between the Territories.

The funding boost comes as the Foreign Secretary and Minister for the Overseas Territories this week host elected leaders and representatives from across the Territories in London (23–26 November).

The four-day annual event aims to explore closer collaboration on shared key priorities, including security, economic growth and climate.

Concern around cyber security has grown in recent weeks, following a slew of attacks affecting schools, blood banks and supermarket fridges across Europe.

![French blood banks have also been targeted by cyber attacks in recent weeks](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/05/13/download.-9..jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“The Overseas Territories are an essential part of the British family of nations, and in a more dangerous world our security is increasingly bound together,” said Foreign Secretary Ed Miliband.

“Through improved policing, justice and security, today’s funding will help to protect us all from malign actors that would do us harm, from organised crime to cyber hackers and hostile states.”

Minister for the Overseas Territories Uma Kumaran described the funding as an “important moment” for the UK and Overseas Territories to “come together and tackle head-on” the key issues of our time.

“The strength of our relationship lies in our ability to tackle challenges around security, growth and climate together, drawing on our respective expertise, experience and global connections,” she added.

“When the Territories prosper, Britain prospers too. By standing together, we are stronger, safer and better prepared for the future.”

There are 14 UK Overseas Territories across the globe, ten of which are permanently inhabited by British nationals.

This includes the Falkland Islands, Gibraltar, Anguilla, Bermuda, Pitcairn and the Cayman Islands.

All the Territories have historic links to the UK and, together with the UK and Crown Dependencies like Jersey and Guernsey, form one undivided realm where the King is sovereign.

This means they have no separate international representation and have no representatives in the UK Parliament.
