A long-term plan to shape the future of North Yorkshire’s sports facilities has been set out in the most comprehensive assessment ever undertaken across the county.

We have developed its first-ever playing pitch strategy and built sports facilities strategy, setting out how sports facilities across the county could be improved and developed over the next two decades.

Together, the strategies establish a single framework to help guide investment in playing pitches, leisure centres, sports halls, swimming pools and other community sports facilities, ensuring funding is used where it can make the biggest difference for residents.

The assessments identify opportunities to modernise facilities, improve access to sport and physical activity, and protect important sports infrastructure as new homes are built across the county.

As North Yorkshire continues to grow, the strategies will identify where investment in sports facilities is needed and support the development of the council's first county-wide local plan, which will guide how the county develops until 2045.

The plans will be presented to councillors on Tuesday next week (1 December).

![An indoor sports pitch](https://www.northyorks.gov.uk/sites/default/files/2026-11/PitchSportsFacilities3.jpg)

Executive member for leisure, Cllr Simon Myers, said: “Sport and physical activity play a vital role in helping people to stay healthy and active.

“We want residents across North Yorkshire to have access to good-quality sports facilities and the opportunity to take part in sport and physical activity close to where they live.

“These strategies give us the clearest picture we have ever had of sports facilities across the county. They show where facilities are meeting local needs, where there are gaps in provision, and where improvements may be needed in the future.

“They also highlight some significant challenges, including ageing leisure centres and sports facilities, growing demand for pitches, and the need to support an increased number of residents aged over 65 to stay active and independent.

“Having a strong evidence base will put us in a much better position to plan for the future, and attract external funding so facilities can continue to meet the needs of residents as our communities grow. I look forward to discussing the strategies with councillors when we meet next week.”

The strategies have been developed in partnership with Sport England and national governing bodies, as well as local sports clubs, schools, town and parish councils, and other stakeholders.

The built sports facilities strategy highlights a range of challenges including the need to modernise older leisure centres and meet increasing demand for activities such as gymnastics, swimming, padel and athletics. It also points to pressure on facilities in some of North Yorkshire's fastest-growing areas and the need to improve energy efficiency and sustainability.

The playing pitch strategy identifies current and future demand for youth football pitches, artificial grass pitches, cricket facilities and rugby pitches, while highlighting opportunities to improve pitch quality, invest in changing facilities, develop additional 3G pitches and make better use of existing sites.

The strategies also highlight opportunities to make greater use of schools, community halls and existing sports facilities, helping more people to access sport and physical activity in their local communities.

Together, they set out priorities to protect existing facilities, improve quality and access, increase capacity where demand is highest and help attract external investment into North Yorkshire's sports infrastructure.

Action plans have been developed to help guide future investment and support negotiations on developer contributions linked to new housing schemes.

If adopted, the strategies will help strengthen future funding bids and ensure investment is focused on projects that deliver the greatest benefits for residents, helping people to be more active while supporting healthier communities across North Yorkshire.
