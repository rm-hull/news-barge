A tonne of cocaine was found hidden in boxes of oranges as part of one of the biggest drug seizures in the history of the Metropolitan police.

Suspected cocaine weighing 893kg has been seized and eight arrests made as part of the investigation that targeted an organised crime group alleged to have been smuggling class A drugs into the UK. A Met spokesperson said the investigation was continuing.

Officers made arrests at London’s New Spitalfields Market on Sunday evening, the force revealed.

A large quantity of suspected cocaine was found hidden in boxes of oranges and five men, aged 27, 33, 41, 43 and 58, were arrested on suspicion of conspiracy to supply class A drugs.

Video footage shows officers rifling through the cardboard boxes full of oranges to find black packages, featuring a drawing of a four-leaf clover, buried underneath.

Pictures taken separately on Sunday evening show blocks of suspected class A drugs filling five empty cardboard boxes. Some blocks feature the number “1100” on their front.

![Boxes of plastic-wrapped cocaine packages sit beside police evidence bags in parking space](https://i.guim.co.uk/img/media/93b899d031213a4a8547a7a6f71d8bddf1e7e637/129_602_4565_3682/master/4565.jpg?width=445&dpr=1&s=none&crop=none)

The fruit and vegetable market, which relocated to Leyton in 1991, is the UK’s largest wholesale market, covering an area of about 13 hectares (31 acres).

A sixth man, aged 44, was also arrested nearby with 100kg of suspected class A drugs, the force spokesperson added.

Separately, police searched a property in Cheshunt, Hertfordshire, and arrested a 48-year-old man on suspicion of the same offence. Officers seized an estimated £450,000 in cash.

A lorry was also stopped near Maidstone and taken to Dover to be searched, with officers seizing around 250kg of suspected cocaine. The driver, a 50-year-old man, was also arrested on suspicion of conspiracy to supply class A drugs.

All eight men arrested remain in custody.

![Gloved hands reach into a crate of oranges hiding plastic-wrapped packages of cocaine](https://i.guim.co.uk/img/media/aa8bc0b5ed5b1db780d3357a820132988a9afc31/0_1639_4284_3427/master/4284.jpg?width=445&dpr=1&s=none&crop=none)

Det Spt Lindsey Billany, who is leading the Met’s investigation, said: “The seizure of these drugs, which would have been worth millions of pounds, represents a significant loss to this criminal enterprise and is one of the largest drug seizures we’ve seen.

“This policing operation demonstrates our focus on bringing down the drug traffickers whose trade fuels violence on London’s streets – and should serve as a warning to organised crime groups importing class A drugs.”
