Choosing the right iPhone for your needs used to be an easy process. Apple’s SE models catered to the affordability crowd, its standard models to those who wanted a well-rounded iPhone experience, and its Pro models to anyone who wanted a top camera phone with strong battery life.

Nowadays, the lines between iPhone options are a little more blurred — Apple currently sells seven different models, but the most expensive iPhones don’t necessarily offer the best features. In some cases, paying more gets you less.

Whenever I’m asked to recommend an iPhone, my default answer is the iPhone 17. Apple’s current standard model remains the best-value product in the iPhone lineup, and while it’s due to be replaced by the iPhone 18 in March or April next year, its chipset, display, and cameras will remain impressive for years to come.

But what if you want something a little more distinctive? In 2026, Apple offers three very different types of ‘special’ iPhone — the iPhone 18 Pro, iPhone Air, and iPhone Duo — with each catering to very different types of user.

I’ve spent considerable time with all three models, and in this guide, I’ve summarized my thoughts on each and highlighted the key, experience-changing differences between them, to help you decide which to buy if you’re currently on the fence. ### iPhone 18 Pro

![The iPhone 18 Pro being held in the hand](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/kVzMeK5QeccStN6ijSZjo9-1200-80.jpg)

The iPhone 18 Pro was crowned TechRadar’s Phone of the Year award for 2026, and for good reason: it’s the Swiss Army Knife of modern flagships, and the iPhone to choose if you want the very best cameras, the longest battery life, along with Apple's familiar and proven iOS software.

Granted, when compared to the iPhone Air and iPhone Duo, the iPhone 18 Pro isn’t all that exciting — it looks and feels largely the same as the iPhone 17 Pro, and by extension, the same as most Pro iPhones before it. But don’t mistake consistency for dullness; the iPhone 18 Pro is the only iPhone to do everything at an 8/10 level or above, and I guarantee that you’ll miss at least one key feature of this phone if you choose the Air or Duo instead.

## Pros versus the iPhone Air?

More cameras (telephoto, ultra-wide), longer battery life, *slightly*faster chipset, stereo speakers,* slightly* faster wireless charging (25W)

## Cons versus the iPhone Air?

More expensive, thicker, heavier, smaller screen (unless you choose a Pro Max)

## Pros versus the iPhone Duo?

Cheaper, more cameras (telephoto), Face ID, better selfie camera, *slightly* longer battery life, thinner, lighter, creaseless display

## Cons versus the iPhone Duo?

Single screen, no stylus support, no multitasking support

### iPhone Air

![The iPhone Air being held in the hand](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/M5XrvSAPWSSjDrxP8Ab9FJ-1200-80.jpg)

The iPhone Air holds a special place in my heart. Sure, it’s got fewer cameras than the iPhone 18 Pro and iPhone Duo. And shorter battery life. And weaker speakers. And a marginally weaker chipset. But after using this phone as my daily driver for the best part of a year, I’ve come to adore its unique combination of style and performance.

To hold and use, there is no other phone like the iPhone Air. It feels almost weightless in your hand and pocket, and despite being an older chipset than the A20 Pro in the iPhone 18 Pro and iPhone Duo, Apple’s A19 Pro remains blisteringly powerful.

I haven’t been too bothered by the Air’s one-day battery life, but it’s true that you’ll get almost two days from the iPhone 18 Pro, and a day-and-a-half from the iPhone Duo. The bigger compromise is the Air’s single-lens camera system, which simply can’t compete with the triple-lens system on the Pro and the dual-lens system on the Duo (though all three phones share largely the same 48MP main lens).

The good news is that, while the iPhone Air retails for a whopping $1,099 / £1,099 / AU$1,899, it’s routinely available for significantly less money. Do note, though, that the iPhone Air 2 is rumored to launch in March or April next year.

## Pros versus the iPhone 18 Pro?

Cheaper, thinner, lighter, larger screen (unless you choose a Pro Max)

## Cons versus the iPhone 18 Pro?

Fewer cameras (wide only), shorter battery life, *slightly*weaker chipset, mono speaker,* slightly* slower wireless charging (20W)

## Pros versus the iPhone Duo?

Cheaper, thinner, lighter, larger outer screen, Face ID, better selfie camera, creaseless display

## Cons versus the iPhone Duo?

Single screen, fewer cameras (wide only), shorter battery life, *slightly*weaker chipset, mono speaker,* slightly* slower wireless charging (20W), no stylus support, no multitasking support

### iPhone Duo

![The iPhone Duo being held in the hand](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/u5pohCxC2ELbLGErgLF8XM-1200-80.jpg)

The iPhone Duo is the most exciting iPhone to launch since the iPhone X in 2017 — but it’s also the most compromised (and expensive). Its USP is, of course, its folding form factor: you get a 5.4-inch cover display and a 7.6-inch foldable internal display, neither of which is anything like the screens on the iPhone 18 Pro and iPhone Air.

Being a foldable, the iPhone Duo comes with a significant learning curve (if you haven’t used a foldable before), but you’ll be rewarded for your patience. The phone’s internal nano-matte screen is an absolute joy to use, and while I’m still coming to terms with the outer display (I find it a little cramped), developers are continuing to update their apps to make both iPhone Duo screens more usable and familiar.

The Duo’s biggest weakness is its lack of Face ID. Since launch, Apple has deployed a Face ID-like system for its foldable phone, but you’ll still be forced to use Touch ID in low-light environments, and for authorizing payments and adding passwords. In this respect, the Duo feels like a step backwards — but if you’re willing to accept that compromise and learn the ropes of a foldable phone, there’s really nothing like its beautiful inner display.

## Pros versus the iPhone 18 Pro?

Dual screens, stylus support, multitasking support

## Cons versus the iPhone 18 Pro?

More expensive, fewer cameras (wide, ultra-wide), Touch ID, smaller outer screen, weaker selfie camera, *slightly* shorter battery life, thicker, heavier, inner display crease

## Pros versus the iPhone Air?

Dual screens, more cameras (wide, ultra-wide), longer battery life, *slightly*faster chipset, stereo speakers,* slightly* faster wireless charging (25W), stylus support, multitasking support

## Cons versus the iPhone Air?

More expensive, Touch ID, smaller outer screen, thicker, heavier, weaker selfie camera, crease
