A seven-year-old boy has been pulled alive from the rubble of a residential building in Ukraine’s Kyiv region, more than 14 hours after it was hit by a Russian strike.

The overnight attack on the town of Dymer killed eleven people, according to local officials. A 79-year-old woman was also found alive under the debris and taken to hospital for treatment.

The rescued boy was reported to be in a stable condition.

On Monday, the death toll from the Russian strike on the residential building in Dymer was revised to 11, according to regional ⁠governor Tymur Tkachenko.

Ukrainian president Volodymyr Zelensky confirmed the rescue of the nine-year-old boy.

“When the boy saw the rescuers, he calmly and without panic began to talk to them, constantly saying that he was okay,” interior minister Ivan Vyhivskyi said.

Authorities said emergency personnel were working to establish whether anyone else was still trapped under the collapsed building.

“The work of the rescuers is complicated by the constant threat of repeated Russian attacks and air raid alerts, which force them to suspend operations,” the state emergency service said.

The rescue operation involves more than 130 people, including police officers, explosive specialists, dog handlers, and medical teams.

![A rescue worker looks for survivors under the rubble of an apartment block that partially collapsed after it was struck during a night of Russian missile and drone attacks in Dymer, Kyiv region, on 22 November 2026](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/23/04/2026-11-22T141206Z_1111696061_RC22TOA9253T_RTRMADP_3_UKRAINE-CRISIS-ATTACK-KYIV.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Mr Zelensky said authorities would continue their efforts until they had “complete information about every single person”. He warned, however, that ongoing air raid alerts and fresh security threats were making the search increasingly difficult.

The president said the strike on the residential property was “practically blowing a section of it to smithereens”.

The building was struck at around 5am local time on Sunday, when residents were still asleep, interior minister Ivan Vyhivskyi said.

![People wait for news about residents believed to be trapped under the rubble of an apartment block that partially collapsed after it was struck during a night of Russian missile and drone attacks in Dymer on 22 November 2026](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/23/04/2026-11-22T120425Z_1071130366_RC2XSOASH1BO_RTRMADP_3_UKRAINE-CRISIS-ATTACK-KYIV.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Relatives of people still unaccounted for gathered near the remains of the apartment block, anxiously awaiting news about their loved ones.

Among them was Arkadii Kozenko, a retired man whose 18-year-old son was inside the building when the drone struck, *The Kyiv Independent* reported.

Mr Kozenko said he was alerted to the attack by a phone call, telling him that a Russian drone had hit the residential building, triggering an explosion.

His son, Roman Komarenko, had been due to attend an internship later in the day.

“We are waiting for the result,” he told the outlet, “whether he is alive or not”.

“We are hoping for the best,” Mr Kozenko added.

![Police officers keep watch at the site of an apartment block that was hit during a night of Russian missile and drone attacks in Dymer, Kyiv region, on 22 November 2026](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/23/04/2026-11-22T141154Z_1839522492_RC21TOALT1PI_RTRMADP_3_UKRAINE-CRISIS-ATTACK-KYIV.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Elsewhere in the Kyiv region, a Russian attack on a private residence in Bila Tserkva killed one person and sparked a fire.

Another strike on a grocery store in Vyshneve left three people dead and 13 injured, Mr Zelensky said.

The region will hold a day of mourning on Monday.

“Constant Russian terror, constant torment inflicted by these scumbags,” Mr Zelensky said in his nightly address. “And all this terror will be met with our fully justified responses.”

![Residents react at the site of a partially collapsed residential building following a Russian air strike in Dymer, Kyiv region, on 22 November 2026](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/23/04/2305877930..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Ukraine’s prime minister, Sergiy Koretsky, said Russian strikes had damaged a power substation in the Dnipropetrovsk region.

Mr Vyhivskyi said Ukraine had recorded more than 70 strikes across 10 regions over the weekend by Sunday evening.
