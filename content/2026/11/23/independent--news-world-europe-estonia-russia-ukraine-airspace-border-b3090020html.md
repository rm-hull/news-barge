Estonia has issued a blunt response after being accused of letting Ukraine use its air space in order to carry out attacks on Russia.

On Monday, Russia’s Foreign Ministry claimed Estonia had been letting Ukraine use its air space “on the quiet” – in what it called a provocative move that risked triggering an open war with the country.

The ministry set out the ‌allegation in a ‌statement after summoning Estonia's charge d'affaires in ⁠Moscow to issue a formal protest over Estonia's decision to close its shared border with Russia. The ministry also accused Estonia of attempting to obstruct the movement of Russian cargo ships in the Baltic Sea.

![Police and Border Guard service members stand on the bridge over the Narva River at the border crossing point with Russia in Narva, Estonia, November 17, 2026](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/20/12/2026-11-17T154428Z_356970175_RC2MPOAGCV7N_RTRMADP_3_UKRAINE-CRISIS-ESTONIA-BORDER.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Following the allegation, Estonia’s foreign ministry published a statement reading: “Estonia has never allowed, and will never allow, its territory to be used for military operations by another country.”

Estonia confirmed on Friday that it is closing its road border crossings with Russia on security grounds for at least six months after accusing Russian security services of staging an attempted arson attack on an Estonian defence company.

“Russian security services are intimidating and trying to recruit our citizens who are travelling through our border checkpoint,” Estonian Interior Minister Igor Taro told journalists at a press conference. “This is something we cannot tolerate anymore.”

The Kremlin has denied any involvement in the weekend arson attack.

![The impending border closure follows an arson attack](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/20/12/2026-11-17T154429Z_952512580_RC2MPOA8XXUW_RTRMADP_3_UKRAINE-CRISIS-ESTONIA-BORDER.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Foreign Ministry spokeswoman Maria Zakharova on Friday said that Russia would ​respond with political and diplomatic measures if Estonia went ahead with the closure.

“Such moves are tantamount to attempts to ‌erect ⁠a new Iron Curtain under the false pretence of some sort of threat from the East,” Zakharova said.

Estonia has five crossing points where travellers can enter or leave Russia. They are slated to close on December 1.

According to Estonian police data, of the 760,000 people who crossed the Estonia-Russia border this year, 307,000 were Russian nationals, ⁠175,000 were ​Estonian citizens and 54,000 held Finnish passports.

Finland closed its border with Russia in 2023 followings the full-scale invasion of Ukraine.

Rail freight is not affected by the road closure but may be subject to strict customs checks.
