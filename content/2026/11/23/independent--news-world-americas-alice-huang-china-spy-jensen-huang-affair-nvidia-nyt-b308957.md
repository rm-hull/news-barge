Alice Huang, the Chinese woman central to Nvidia's recent scandal, maintained contact with an official from Beijing’s primary spy agency for over a year during her affair with former CEO Jensen Huang, the *New York Times* reported on Sunday.

Alice Huang communicated with an officer from China's Ministry of State Security between the spring of 2025 and the autumn of this year, the report stated, citing people familiar with intelligence briefings.

A spokesperson for Jensen Huang declined to comment, while Nvidia’s representatives and the Chinese embassy in Washington did not immediately respond to requests for comment. Alice Huang couldn’t be reached for comment.

Alice Huang previously led Singapore-based Megaspeed International, a major Nvidia client accounting for billions in chip sales.

US authorities have investigated Megaspeed since 2024 over allegations it smuggled advanced Nvidia AI chips to China.

The report on Sunday followed growing scepticism among US officials regarding initial claims that Alice Huang was a spy, first reported by the newspaper on 29 October.

That article sparked a scandal leading to Jensen Huang's resignation two weeks later and a steep drop in Nvidia's share price.

US officials have repeatedly noted that they have not concluded Alice Huang spied for China. Attorney general Todd Blanche said earlier this month the Justice Department had not yet established whether she was a Chinese spy.

Jay Clayton, the director of national intelligence, repeated that point on Sunday. "Attorney general Blanche has been very clear that this is a matter under investigation, both Ms Huang and her company, for various matters," Clayton told CNN. "And I'm going to let that investigation play out."

The fallout has also prompted scrutiny of Washington's export controls on China, with lawmakers questioning whether the Donald Trump administration has been too lax regarding semiconductor regulations.

The affair itself is of less interest, Senator John Kennedy, a Louisiana Republican, told the NBC. "I hope he had a good time," Kennedy said on Sunday. "It was the most expensive sex since Helen of Troy."
