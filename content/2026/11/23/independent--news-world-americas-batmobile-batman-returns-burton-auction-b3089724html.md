A Batmobile from Tim Burton's 1992 blockbuster “Batman Returns” is up for sale, and bidding could require a fortune.

The car will cross the block on Monday in Gardena, near Los Angeles, as the centerpiece of a Julien's Auctions sale. Dozens of pieces of Batman and other superhero memorabilia also feature in the sale. The vehicle is one of three custom-made versions used in the film.

It was hijacked by the Penguin, careened through Gotham's streets and lived beneath Wayne Manor. Julien's has set a pre-auction estimate of $5 million to $7 million.

![The Batmobile from the Batman Returns movie is displayed at Marconi Automotive Museum in Tustin, California](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/23/05/Batmobile_Auction_73754.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

At that price, the car could break the Batmobile auction record set in 2013, when the vehicle driven by Adam West in the 1960s TV series sold for $4.6 million, including fees. The glossy black machine is known as the “Number One Hero car”.

It measures just under 20 feet (about 6 meters) long and has two sweeping tail fins. Julien's auction notes on its website say it appeared in most of the film's driving sequences. Since filming, the car has spent years on display at the Marconi Automotive Museum in Tustin, California.

![Julien's called the car “one of the most recognized and important cinematic vehicles in Hollywood history.”](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/23/05/BATIM%C3%93VIL-SUBASTA_62359.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

It still has its original paint, Chevrolet V-8 engine and radio-controlled flaming exhaust system. Michael Keaton's Batman used that exhaust to torch one of the Penguin's circus goons.

Citing the influence of Burton's “darker aesthetic” on modern superhero movies, Julien's called the car “one of the most recognized and important cinematic vehicles in Hollywood history.”

Whoever buys it should not expect to see it on the road. Julien's noted that the vehicle is “not street legal” and the engine and transmission have been tested a “limited number of times” since filming concluded.
