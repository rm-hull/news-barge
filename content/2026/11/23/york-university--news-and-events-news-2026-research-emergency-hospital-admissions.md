# York study reveals unexplained variations in emergency hospital admission rates across England

Posted on Monday 23 November 2026

![Image shows an ambulance speeding through a residential street.](https://www.york.ac.uk/media/news-and-events/pressreleases/2026/ambulance-1000.jpg)

Researchers analysed more than 15 million emergency department attendances in England between 2024 and 2025. They found that hospital admission rates ranged from under 10% to more than 40% across different regions.

Differences in patient health, local geography and general practice services explained only around a fifth (22%) of this variation. Even after adjusting for patient age, illness severity and local care access, substantial unexplained differences of between 2% and 42% remained.

The findings suggest that uniform national policies are unlikely to solve emergency department crowding. Instead, health leaders need locally tailored strategies that account for regional resources, urgent care facilities and transport links.

**Local resource**

Senior Research Fellow Rita Santos, from the Centre for Health Economics at the University of York, said: “A uniform, one-size-fits-all national approach to managing emergency admissions is unlikely to be effective due to underlying local factors. A tailored strategy must take regional factors into account, matching local resource availability with patient needs and hospital capabilities.”

The study, which was funded by the National Institute for Health and Care Research (NIHR), examined national anonymised data covering 15,201,063 emergency department visits. Around one in four attendances (26%) resulted in hospital admission. Three quarters of visits occurred during weekdays, and 26.5% of patients arrived by ambulance.

**Higher chances**

Older adults, men and people living in socioeconomically deprived areas had higher chances of admission. Patients arriving by ambulance or during hours when general practice surgeries were closed were also more likely to be admitted.

Admission rates remained highest in areas such as North East London, North West London and the South West. Areas including Yorkshire, Lincolnshire and the North East recorded average rates. The researchers note that differences in hospital culture and local service setups may explain some of the remaining variations.
