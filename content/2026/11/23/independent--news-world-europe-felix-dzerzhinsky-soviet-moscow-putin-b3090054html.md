A bronze memorial plaque honouring Felix Dzerzhinsky has been unveiled in Moscow, hailing the founder of the Soviet secret police as an "outstanding statesman" to the applause of hardline nationalists.

Mr Dzerzhinsky was a Polish noble-turned-revolutionary who laid the foundations of the repressive system over which Josef Stalin was to preside.

He is reviled by many for the tens of thousands of summary executions carried out by his Cheka to shore up Bolshevik power between 1918 and 1922.

Vladimir Medinsky, an aide to President Vladimir Putin who has led Russia's delegations to peace talks with Ukraine, was among the politicians who inaugurated the plaque on Friday.

The plaque was placed on a building not far from the Kremlin where Mr Dzerzhinsky worked from 1924 to 1926.

It depicts him in a suit, set against a background of factory chimneys, a steam train and an electricity pylon.

The plaque credits him as the initiator of the sweeping industrialisation that transformed the early Soviet economy.

Mr Medinsky, who has authored some of Russia's new history textbooks, hailed Mr Dzerzhinsky as a romantic idealist while appearing to acknowledge that he was also part of a darker chapter.

"And the worst thing we can do today is to try to tear things down. To tear down monuments to the jeers of the crowd," he said, according to the state news agency TASS.

"We must try to remember. To acknowledge both our successes and our mistakes. And to learn from them. Because if we do not remember our history, we are bound to repeat all our mistakes."

But within the security apparatus that holds huge sway in Putin's Russia, he is regarded as a hero and a powerful symbol of a state that relies heavily on the FSB, the successor organisation to the Soviet KGB, which Putin once ran.

The plaque is not the only focus for Mr Dzerzhinsky's admirers.

![Angry Soviet demonstrators using cranes pull down a statue of Felix Dzerzhinsky in August 1991](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/23/15/2026-11-23T153627Z_1806769791_RC2CD246517K_RTRMADP_3_RUSSIA-POLITICS-DZERZHINSKY.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

When the Soviet Union crumbled in 1991, a monument to Mr Dzerzhinsky outside the KGB's headquarters on Moscow's Lubyanka Square was torn down by jubilant demonstrators.

Some saw that as a moment of hope that would usher in a new, freer era.

In time, the giant statue was moved to a park near the River Moskva, along with other symbols of Soviet power.

Some of the nationalist war bloggers who have been championing Russia's war in Ukraine said the time had now come to put the statue back.

"A plaque is all well and good, but it’s not enough," wrote Blue Z Beard, a prominent war blogger with over 100,000 followers on Telegram.

"Bring Felix back to Lubyanka Square!"
