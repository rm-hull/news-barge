President Donald Trump and the relatives of several high-profile California murder victims condemned Gov. Gavin Newsom on Monday during an event at the Justice Department, attacking the Democrat’s decision to grant clemency to hundreds of death row inmates.

Speaking at the department with Attorney General Todd Blanche, Trump joined family members who lost loved ones in decades-old murder cases to criticize Newsom's move to commute 548 death sentences to life without parole.

Early in the event, several relatives spoke in graphic detail about their loved ones’ slayings in an effort to highlight how unjust they felt Newsom’s move was.

Trump accused the governor of siding with convicted killers over grieving relatives, telling the audience that Newsom “stole away the only closure those families might ever have” and that the administration is “working as hard as they can to get this travesty overturned in some form.”

The president invited several relatives to the podium to speak about their cases, beginning with Sandy Fern, the mother of eight-year-old Michael Lyons, who was abducted, tortured and murdered in Yuba City, California, in 1996. Fern said Newsom erased the death sentence of her son's killer, Robert Boyd Rhodes, “with a swipe of a pen, without notifying the families.”

![President Donald Trump joined the grieving relatives of several California murder victims at the Justice Department on Monday to denounce Governor Gavin Newsom’s decision to commute the death sentences of 548 inmates, calling the move a ‘shocking act of injustice’](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/23/23/2306567030..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Addressing the governor directly during her remarks, she added: “Face me, Governor Newsom. Tell me, Michael’s life doesn't matter.”

Mark Klaas also addressed the gathering regarding the 1993 abduction and murder of his 12-year-old daughter, Polly. He stated that for three decades his family believed the jury’s sentence would remain intact, and said he stood before the crowd “outraged that Governor Gavin Newsom unilaterally erased that sentence and 547 more and commuted them to life without parole, without the consent of victims' families or the people of California.”

“This decision was not made by a jury,” Klaas said. “It was not approved by the voters. It was made by one politician acting alone.”

He argued that the pursuit of justice “should not become a political exercise.”

![California Governor Gavin Newsom announced plans earlier this month to commute all 548 California death row sentences to life imprisonment without the possibility of parole, a move that still requires approval from the California Supreme Court before taking effect](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/18/14/2305572109..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Trump later recognized the relatives of Placer County Detective Michael Davis and Sacramento County Deputy Sheriff Danny Oliver, who were fatally shot in 2014 by Luis Bracamontes during a multi-county crime spree. Davis’s brother, Placer County Sheriff’s Lieutenant Jason Davis, told attendees that his family had begun to heal before Newsom “single-handedly ripped that band-aid off and opened that wound again.”

“And Gov. Newsom, under the cloak of darkness, unilaterally makes a decision to commute the sentences of the most heinous criminals in our society,” Davis said. “I am disgusted and frustrated. My brother did not die in vain.”

The president also heard from Gloria Vega, whose father, Palm Springs Police Officer Jose Gil Vega, was fatally shot in 2016 just months before his planned retirement, and acknowledged several other families whose relatives were killed by inmates on California’s death row.

Trump characterized the clemency grants as a “shocking act of injustice” and argued that Newsom and “the radical left look at these heinous acts, and their sympathy is not for the children and their parents, but for the monsters who extinguished innocent young lives.”

He pledged that his administration would “bring justice for American families.”

![Trump called the clemency grants a ‘shocking act of injustice’ and later pledged that his administration would ‘bring American families their justice’](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/23/23/2306567029..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“Law enforcement is not the enemy. The enemy are those who rape and murder our citizens, and they deserve no mercy,” Trump said, later adding, “And the ultimate crimes deserve the ultimate punishment.”

During the event, Trump announced a new presidential directive instructing the Justice Department to seek capital punishment for child murderers, as well as rapists and human traffickers who kill their victims, while ordering the attorney general to speed up federal executions.

“Once jurors have spoken, capital punishment will be swift and certain,” he said.

The president announced nearly $150 million in new Justice Department public safety grants to hire more than 1,000 law enforcement officers, directed housing officials to tighten tenant screenings in public housing and endorsed newly introduced legislation from Republican Congressman Brandon Gill that would mandate the death penalty for undocumented immigrants convicted of murder.

![The event follows heavy Republican losses in the recent midterm elections, which handed Democrats control of Congress and raised the prospect of new legislative oversight and investigations into the Justice Department’s operation](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/23/23/36/GettyImages-2262525091.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Monday's appearance marked Trump’s second speech at the Justice Department in less than two years. During his prior visit in March 2025, the president focused largely on grievances tied to past federal investigations into his conduct and vowed retribution, though several attempts by the administration to prosecute political adversaries have encountered legal setbacks in court.

The event also follows sweeping Republican losses in the recent midterm elections, which handed Democrats control of Congress and raised the prospect of new legislative oversight and investigations into the Justice Department’s operations.

Critics have argued that the department’s traditional independence has eroded during Trump’s second term, pointing to Blanche — Trump's former personal defense attorney, who succeeded Pam Bondi as attorney general in April and supports broad presidential authority over the agency — as well as symbolic changes such as a large banner bearing Trump’s portrait hung on the exterior of the Justice Department building.
