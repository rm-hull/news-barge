The Cincinnati Zoo reopened its Hippo Cove exhibit on Monday with new safety measures in place after the death of its beloved hippo Fiona.

Fiona died 11 days after swallowing a zoo visitor’s knit hat. The visitor had tossed the hat into the air, where it got caught in a ceiling fan that flung it directly into a narrow gap above the exhibit’s viewing windows, into Fiona’s enclosure.

After Fiona swallowed it, the hat got lodged in her small intestine. She was unable to pass it through her system, which led to her death on Thursday.

Hippo Cove was temporarily closed because of the emergency but it has now reopened with changes designed to "guard against something like this ever happening again," Cincinnati Zoo Director Dave Jenike said in a press release.

![Fiona swims in her enclosure at the Cincinnati Zoo in June 2018](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/20/15/Fiona_the_Hippo_59283.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

"We added a layer of netting above the viewing windows and, out of an abundance of caution, removed all ceiling fans in the area," Jenike said.

While mourning Fiona’s death, her fans were shocked to realize that she died at the same zoo where Harambe, an endangered gorilla, was fatally shot after a little boy fell into his exhibit in 2016.

Harambe’s death led to months of international backlash, which cast a shadow over the Cincinnati Zoo.

Addressing the facility's safety record, Dan Ashe, president of the Association of Zoos and Aquariums, defended the zoo’s standards.

![Flowers, handwritten notes, watermelons and other items at a memorial for Fiona the hippo at the Cincinnati Zoo in Cincinnati, Friday, Nov. 20, 2026](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/23/20/download.-1..jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

"The Cincinnati Zoo has been accredited since 1978 and was last accredited in 2025 after an exhaustive inspection, a standard practice for Zoos seeking or renewing accreditation," Ashe said.

"Nothing was of concern for the hippo habitat in the inspection report. It seems like it was a freak event that couldn’t have been foreseen."

Zoo officials are reminding visitors that they depend on patrons to keep personal belongings secure and to promptly inform staff members if an object enters an animal enclosure. Visitors can still see other hippos at the habitat, including Fiona’s four-year-old brother, Fritz.

Fiona rose to fame in January 2017 when she was born six weeks premature. At birth, she weighed just 29 pounds (13.2 kilograms), 25 pounds (11.3 kilograms) below the lowest birth weight ever recorded for her species.

![Fiona captured hearts across the globe after being born premature](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/20/18/2026-11-19T221328Z_462527325_RC2AROAF2QM6_RTRMADP_3_USA-FIONA.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Millions of people across the world monitored her progress online and kept up with her throughout the years.

In the days since her death, grief-stricken fans have covered a hippo statue at the zoo with flowers and notes written in crayon. Others brought watermelons and pumpkins, which were among her favorite treats.

"She brought so much love in her lifetime," one note read.

Fiona would’ve been 10 years old in January.
