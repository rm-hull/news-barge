Honda has issued a recall covering more than 810,000 vehicles over concerns that its airbag inflators could potentially rupture.

The National Highway Traffic Safety Administration reported that the safety action impacts 810,592 automobiles.

Specifically, the recall applies to 2012–2013 Honda Civic and CR-V models, 2013 Honda Accords, along with 2013 Acura ILX and RDX vehicles.

Regulators at the NHTSA explained that the affected cars feature certain Takata front driver and passenger airbag inflators.

Extended exposure to high absolute humidity combined with severe temperature changes can cause the internal propellant to degrade, creating a rupture hazard.

![The issue lies with a small batch of wheels on Honda Civics from 2016 to 2021](https://static.independent.co.uk/2025/11/07/22/49/Honda-Civic.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

A bursting inflator can launch sharp metal fragments into the cabin, threatening drivers and passengers with critical injuries or death.

The move contributes to the recall of tens of millions of Takata airbags, standing among the largest product recalls ever recorded.

Authorized dealerships will install replacement front driver or passenger airbag inflators at no cost to owners.

Notification letters advising drivers of the hazard are scheduled for dispatch on Dec. 18, with further correspondence to follow when repair parts are ready.

Drivers can contact Honda customer support at 1-888-234-2138, call the NHTSA hotline at 1-888-327-4236, or check www.nhtsa.gov.
