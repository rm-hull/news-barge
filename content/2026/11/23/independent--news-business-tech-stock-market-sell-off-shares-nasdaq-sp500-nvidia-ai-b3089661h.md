Stock markets are set to rise on Monday, following a huge sell-off last week led by AI concerns and the ongoing furore around Nvidia.

Former chief executive Jensen Huang’s exit from the chipmaker sparked a massive downturn in share price for what was – until six weeks ago – the biggest company in the world by market capitalisation value. Now it’s outside the top three.

Yahoo Finance showed a one-month decline of 42 per cent for Nvidia shares, while the wider technology sector also suffered hugely. The likes of Google’s parent company Alphabet (22 per cent), Instagram owner Meta (19 per cent), cloud and software firm Oracle (23 per cent) and chipmaker Micron (33 per cent) all endured massive downturns.

On Monday, however, the first signs of recovery could be seen, with US futures pointing to the Nasdaq index rising 3.4 per cent when trading hours start this afternoon.

The S&P 500 is also set to rise more than 2.6 per cent, while in Europe each of London’s FTSE 100, Germany’s DAX and France’s CAC 40 indices were up around 0.5 to 0.6 per cent and the wider Euro Stoxx 50 was up 0.8 per cent after two hours of trading.

Matt Orton, chief market strategist at investment management firm Raymond James, said on Yahoo Finance's Market Hang programme that investors were “finally getting details with respect to what's underneath the curtain” in AI firms.

![Wall Street analysts expect an upturn in the wider market - but it’s still TBD on AI firms](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/23/08/2306070241..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

He added that he hoped that “the market will kind of start to calm down and realise that there's a lot of really high-quality companies that are on fire sale right now," he added.

## Are these stocks really bargains at the moment or could they fall further?

But the upturn isn’t a guarantee, others are cautioning.

“There's nothing surprising in the overnight bounce after the waterfall moves of last week, but investors can't emerge from their shelters just yet,” said Chris Beauchamp, chief market analyst at IG.

“Such selloffs rarely turnaround in a day. Markets still seem to be in a mood to cut back risk, and it is far from clear that stocks have reached the kind of bargain valuations needed to tempt investors back in to the water.”

In individual stock terms, Nvidia is up 8 per cent in pre-market trading, Alphabet is 4.5 per cent higher and AI programme Claude’s maker, Anthropic, is up 4 per cent.

The latter went public last week and surged to $111.50 after a successful IPO - but was quickly caught up in the sell-off to drop more than 8 per cent on Friday to below $89.

Wall Street strategist Ed Yardeni believes the wider US market will not be pulled into a bear market, however. “We got more of a buying opportunity than we bargained for,” he said in an analyst note. “We expected a pullback. It has turned into a correction.”

Amish Patel, head of equity research at Raymond James, said investors need to see “greater clarity on the returns that investment is generating” before piling back into AI firms, but added that “valuations have already come back considerably during this sell-off, which is beginning to improve the risk-reward”.

“If the fundamentals continue to hold up and companies can provide greater confidence, those lower valuations could begin to tempt investors back into the sector,” he said.
