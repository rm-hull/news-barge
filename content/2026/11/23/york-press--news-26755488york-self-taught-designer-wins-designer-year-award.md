## Self-taught designer wins national fashion award from York guild

Designers and models travelled from across the UK to attend the event *(Image: MAREKS STRODS)*

A SELF-TAUGHT fashion designer from York has won Designer of the Year at a national award ceremony, involving the McQueen family and Anna, winner of The Great British Sewing Bee.

The Company of Merchant Taylors - a guild with roots tracing back to 1273 - has hosted the first Golden Thread Awards on Sunday (November 22) at the Merchant Taylors’ Hall in York.

As reported by The Press last week, the fashion show aimed celebrate a mix of established and emerging designers, reflecting the guild's longstanding heritage in tailoring and cloth.

Marina Rose, who runs York based House of White Rose, was presented with the Golden Thread Award by Gary James McQueen, chair of the jury, on November 22 at The Milner York.

Marina said: "Good things come when you least expect them. I am completely self-taught. I've taught myself everything. I have learned so much, I've made so many mistakes, and here I am."

Eighty designers from 11 regions across the UK took part in the competition with the thirteen finalists showcasing their collections earlier in the day at the historic Merchant Taylors' Hall in Aldwark.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21754981.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Marina Rose, House of White Rose, was chosen as Designer of the Year *(Image: MAREKS STRODS)*

Marina added: "I don't like to think about it as a competition. I have my friends here."

Among those in attendance was 2026 Great British Sewing Bee winner Anna Pownall, who presented the Emerging Designer Award to Daniel Zak Roberts of Birmingham City University.

Anna said: "What an incredible day. Such an inspiration to see all the designers, and how they've approached the fabrics differently. Each one is so unique."

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21754992.jpg?type=mds-article-620)A runway for the Textile Innovation Award *(Image: MAREKS STRODS)*

She wore an outfit made entirely from second-hand materials sourced from a St Leonard’s Hospice charity shop in York.

Anna added: "I think sustainability is a really big issue in fashion right now, so I think it's great to get inspired by what's already there.

"It's also lovely to see things like this happening in York - I think so often as a creative, it's all feels like everything has to be in London.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21754991.jpg?type=mds-article-620)All designers brought something new to the runway *(Image: MAREKS STRODS)*

"It's really nice to see things like this in the North and York giving opportunities to young designers."

Other winners included Liubov Brovarna from BROVARNA in Alton, who received the Textile Innovation Award, and Emilio Pugliese and his team who were awarded the Look of the Night.

Brigida Gilma Amos Vunge was named Model of the Night.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21754980.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Designer Liubov Brovarna *(Image: MAREKS STRODS)*

Gary James McQueen, chair of the jury, said: "Every one of the finalists was remarkably talented. Deciding the winners was genuinely difficult."

Sally Hobbs CBE, Master of the Company of Merchant Taylors in the City of York, said the awards continue a long tradition of craftsmanship and talent in the city.

She said: "Our Company has been part of York's making since the fourteenth century. On Sunday that tradition met the next generation, and it is in very good hands.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21754985.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Whilst the show mostly featured female models, Emilio Pugliese was commended for his menswear *(Image: MAREKS STRODS)*

"We are proud to have given these designers a stage in York, and we look forward to doing so again."

Entries for the 2027 Golden Thread Awards will open in the new year.
