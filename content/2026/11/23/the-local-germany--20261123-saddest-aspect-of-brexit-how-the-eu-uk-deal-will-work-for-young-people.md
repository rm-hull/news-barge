Two years after the European Commission launched the proposal, the EU and the UK have agreed to put in place a scheme that will make it easier for young people to move across the Channel for a defined period of time.

The deal is part of the ‘reset’ of the EU-UK relationship pursued by the Labour government. It was announced following the EU-UK summit on Friday in Brussels, with a joint statement saying that “people-to-people ties” are “at the core of the renewed EU-UK agenda, particularly for the younger generation.”

The ‘youth experience scheme’ – a name chosen to avoid the Commission’s term ‘youth mobility’ as it evoked free movement – will allow EU and UK citizens between the age of 18 and 31 to move across the Channel for up to three years “to work, study, au pair, volunteer or simply travel.”

“There will be no obligation to undertake particular activities under the scheme,” says a note by the British government. The age requirement will apply on the day the visa of the participant starts.

The scheme will be open every year to 100,000 young people from the UK and 100,000 from the EU, distributed between EU Member States “on the basis of the relative population”. This proportion will apply to both young people from Europe moving to the UK and vice versa.

**Visa and healthcare**

After free movement of people between the UK and the EU ended, standard visa rules apply to young people moving across the Channel for holidays, study or work.

Currently, EU citizens can travel to the UK for up to six months without a visa. UK citizens can stay in the Schengen area visa-free for up to 90 days in any 180-day period. For longer periods, both have to apply for a visa or residence permit associated with the purpose of their stay.

Under the youth experience scheme, the UK and EU Member States will issue visa or residence permits initially valid for two years, with the possibility to extend it for a third year.

Participants in the scheme will have to pay an application fee - but the fee has not been set. Applicants will also have to prove that they have sufficient funds to support themselves during their stay and indicate their intended primary activity.

EU nationals will have to pay the UK Immigration Health Surcharge at a discounted rate (75 percent of the full amount) and UK nationals will need to have “comprehensive healthcare coverage.”

**Three countries limit**

Part of discussion around these new arrangements concerned the UK demand to allow British nationals to move freely within EU countries during the three years, as they could do before Brexit.

In the end, it was agreed that they will be able to stay in a total of three EU countries over the three-year period. They will have to apply to one EU Member State and reside there for at least 12 months, after which they will be able to switch to a different EU state.

**Access to the labour market**

The approval of the scheme does not mean that there will be an automatic right to work in the host country, as the agreement allows to “restrict participants’ access to the labour market”.

The British government however said in a note: “We have had confirmation that the vast majority of participating Member States do not intend to impose any work restrictions on UK participants.”

The exact terms and restrictions “will be published before the agreement is signed,” the explainer adds.

Each person will be able to participate only once in the scheme. Participants will not have access to social welfare benefits in the host state, nor will they be able to bring dependants with them.

**Start date in 2030?**

These arrangements are for now agreed in principle. “We aim to apply this agreement no later than 2028 and endeavour to ensure that opportunities under the scheme will be available to our young people no later than January 2030,” the UK government said.

According to media reports, some EU Member States have requested that the agreement becomes applicable only after the EU and the UK will have reached a separate deal on university fees.

Since Brexit, EU students have to pay international fees to study in UK universities (and vice versa) and some EU countries are pressing to restore the much lower domestic fees for their nationals. Tuition fees in EU countries are lower than in the UK.

No further clarity was however provided on this point at the press conference following the summit.

**University fees**

The joint statement published after the meeting says: “The UK and the EU share the objective of further increasing opportunities for studying, as well as other activities under Erasmus+ across the UK and the EU”.

“The EU and UK will therefore work towards a supplementing agreement to the Trade and Cooperation Agreement on tuition fees for EU students in the UK and for UK students in the EU. The Agreement will apply to all students from the other Party irrespective of the immigration pathway. The aim is to increase opportunities for students to study in each other’s territories,” the statement continues.

The EU and the UK aim to conclude the negotiations in this area within six months, according to the document.

A statement by British in Europe, the coalition representing UK citizens living in the EU, welcomed the youth experience scheme but expressed disappointment at “the continuing confusion over home fees at UK universities for EU citizens.”

British nationals living in the EU, as well as EEA and EFTA countries, currently benefit of a grace period during which, if they decide to study in the UK, they pay domestic fees. This period ends on 31 December 2027, after which they will have to pay international fees.

The group called for an extension of the grace period until 2030 to allow the UK and EU reach a more permanent agreement “on this outstanding issue.”

“Millions of young UK citizens benefited from the right to live, work, and study in the EU prior to Brexit, as did millions of EU citizens in the UK," said British in Europe.

"The saddest aspect of Brexit for us has been the removal of this opportunity for young British and EU citizens.

This point was recognised explicitly in the press conference by Prime Minister Andy Burnham who said this past decade has been hardest for young people, whose horizons have been limited by Brexit and Covid. We welcome his words,” British in Europe said.
