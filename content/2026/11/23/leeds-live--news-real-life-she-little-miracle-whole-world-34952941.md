A couple who spent 16 years hoping to become parents are turning the heartbreak of losing their baby daughter into a mission to help other families facing the devastating loss of a child.

Scott Kennedy, 38, and his wife Lucy, 39, from Leeds, had endured years of infertility, including unsuccessful rounds of IVF, and had been told they would never conceive naturally.

But despite the odds being against them on July 17, 2026, they received the news they had dreamed of hearing for so long - they were expecting a little girl. After years of hoping, their dream of becoming Mummy and Daddy was finally coming true and like any like parents to be they began imagining their future with their daughter, from her first Christmas to family holidays and watching her grow.

They named her Edith Hazel Kennedy and it would be an understatement to say she was already loved beyond words. But just days after reaching the 20-week pregnancy milestone and enjoying a bump photoshoot while on holiday in Spain, Scott and Lucy's lives changed forever.

On October 25, Scott and Lucy were told the words no expectant parent should ever have to hear - Edith no longer had a heartbeat. Their beautiful daughter was born sleeping the following day, at just 21 weeks leaving them shattered.

Amazingly, rather than allowing their unimaginable loss to be the end of Edith’s story, the couple are determined to ensure her name lives on by helping other families facing baby loss. They have already helped send 33 memory boxes to a hospital in Edith’s memory and are now hoping to raise £3,000 towards a cold cot for St James’s Hospital in Leeds.

Scott has also written a children’s book, The Mystery Garden, in memory of his daughter, while he is preparing to take on a gruelling 70-mile sponsored walk from Leeds to Scarborough on what would have been Edith’s due date. Scott told Leeds Live: “We had spent 16 years hoping, wishing and dreaming of becoming parents. Sixteen years is a long time to carry that hope, and there were moments when we wondered whether it would ever happen for us.

“When we finally discovered we were expecting a little girl, it was difficult to put into words what that meant to us. It felt like everything we had waited for was finally happening.

“We weren’t just expecting a baby, we were finally going to become Mum and Dad. Edith was already so loved before we ever got to hold her.” Scott, originally from Leeds, works as a professional van driver travelling between England and Northern Ireland, while Lucy, originally from Tadcaster, works in social housing. The couple now live in the Leeds area.

They had been told Lucy would never conceive naturally, making the positive pregnancy test all the more extraordinary. Scott continued: “We discovered Lucy was pregnant on July 17. It was one of the happiest moments of our lives.

“After 16 years of hoping, seeing that positive result changed everything. Suddenly, the future we had imagined for so long was becoming real. We were going to become parents, and we couldn’t wait to meet our little girl. We imagined everything that comes with being parents, we looked forward to the simplest things that many people perhaps take for granted, like holding her hand, hearing her laugh and watching her take her first steps.

“We didn’t need her to be anything special in the eyes of the world. We just wanted to be her mum and dad and give her the happiest life we possibly could.” But tragically their dreams were shattered when they discovered their daughter’s heartbeat was no longer there on Sunday, October 25.

Scott said: “Sunday, October 25, is a day that will stay with us for the rest of our lives. There are no words that can properly describe what it feels like to be told that the little person you’ve spent so long dreaming about is no longer alive. Our world stopped. We went from planning a future with our daughter to facing something no parent should ever have to experience. It was a moment that changed our lives forever.”

The following day, Edith Hazel Kennedy was born sleeping at 21 weeks. Scott recalls that meeting their daughter was both heart-breaking and precious in equal measures.

He said: “Meeting our daughter was one of the most heart-breaking and precious moments of our lives. We had spent 16 years waiting to become parents, and although we finally got to meet our little girl, it wasn’t in the way we had imagined.

“She was absolutely beautiful. She was our daughter, our little girl, and we were finally able to see the person we had loved so much throughout the pregnancy.”

The couple had already chosen Edith’s name before she was born, with her middle name carrying a particularly special meaning. Hazel was chosen in honour of her 16-month-old cousin, who sadly died in August. Scott said: “It was important to us that there was a connection between the two little ones. Although we have experienced the heartbreak of losing both of them, we wanted a little part of her cousin to live on through Edith’s name.

### 'They will forever be connected through family, love and memories'

During their time in hospital, the couple received support from baby loss charity 4Louis, whose memory box and cold cot became incredibly important to them. A cold cot, sometimes known as a cuddle cot, helps keep a baby’s body cool after death, allowing parents additional precious time with their child.

That extra time meant that Scott and Luc could hold Edith, take photographs and create memories they will treasure for the rest of their lives.

Scott added: “The support we received from 4Louis was something we will never forget. The memory box gave us something physical to hold on to. It provided us with keepsakes and memories of Edith that we can look back on throughout our lives. The cold cot was equally important.

"It allowed us to spend additional precious time with our daughter, rather than feeling that we had to say goodbye so quickly. That time was priceless. We honestly don’t know how we would have managed without that support. It made such a difference to us."

It was the support they received that inspired the couple to begin fundraising in Edith’s memory, having experienced first-hand how much a memory box could mean to a grieving family, they wanted to make sure other parents could receive the same comfort. They have already helped send 33 memory boxes to a hospital, with each one intended to provide keepsakes and memories for families going through the loss of a baby.

Scott said: “We saw first-hand how much a memory box meant to us, and we started thinking about other families who might be going through the same heartbreaking experience. We wanted to do something in Edith’s memory that would make a genuine difference. Every single box represents a family who may be facing the worst day of their lives.

“If those boxes can provide even a little comfort, give parents something to treasure or help them create memories with their baby, then we know Edith’s name is helping other families. We will never be able to change what happened to us, but perhaps we can make things a little easier for someone else.”

Their next ambition is to raise £3,000 towards providing a cold cot for St James’s Hospital in Leeds.

The couple want to help ensure other parents have the opportunity to spend precious additional time with their babies, without having to worry about whether the equipment will be available. Scott said: “We want other parents to have the opportunity we had - to spend precious additional time with their baby, to hold them, take photographs, make memories and say goodbye in their own time.

“We know that no amount of fundraising can take away the pain of losing a child, but we believe that providing families with more time and support can make an enormous difference. Our target is £3,000, but our bigger ambition is to help make sure that no family misses out on this support because the equipment isn’t available.” Scott has also found a personal way to keep his daughter’s memory alive by writing a children’s book called The Mystery Garden.

The story follows two little girls who discover a mysterious, beautiful garden and explores themes of love, imagination, friendship and the special memories that stay with us. He said: “Writing it became a way for me to channel some of the love I have for my daughter into something positive.

“For me, the book is about creating something that will last. Something that carries a little piece of Edith’s story and allows her name to continue living on. It has been a very emotional project to put together, but I’m incredibly proud of what I’ve created.” The book is available as a PDF directly from Scott for £3, with plans to make it available on Amazon for £4.99. All proceeds, apart from any unavoidable selling or platform fees, will go towards Edith’s fundraising efforts.

And there is yet another huge challenge ahead, on March 11, 2027 – the date Edith was originally due to arrive – Scott is planning to walk approximately 70 miles from Leeds to Scarborough in her memory. He hopes to complete the challenge in around 22 hours, raising awareness and money to help provide cold cots and memory boxes for other families.

### 'Every step will be for her'

Scott said: “Instead of spending that day doing what we had imagined, I wanted to turn it into something meaningful that would honour her life. It will be physically and mentally demanding, but I feel strongly that I want to do this for Edith. Every step will be for her.

“I know there will be difficult moments, but when I think about why I’m doing it, I know I’ll find the strength to keep going. It is my way of making sure that Edith’s due date remains a day of love, remembrance and purpose.”

Despite everything they have been through, Scott and Lucy want their daughter’s story to encourage people to speak more openly about baby loss and remember the babies who are no longer here. They say people should never be afraid to mention a baby’s name or acknowledge the grief of parents who have experienced such a loss.

Scott said: “The biggest thing we want people to take away from Edith’s story is that every baby matters, no matter how far along a pregnancy is. A baby doesn’t have to take their first breath to become someone’s entire world. Edith was wanted, she was loved and she will always be our daughter.

“Don’t be afraid to say a baby’s name. Don’t avoid speaking to parents because you don’t know what to say. Sometimes simply acknowledging their baby and letting them know you remember can mean more than you realise.”

For the couple, Edith’s legacy is about making sure other families receive the compassion and support they need during the darkest moments of their lives. Scott added: “We spent 16 years waiting to become parents, and although our time with Edith was nowhere near long enough, she has already changed our lives in ways we could never have imagined.

### 'Forever our little girl. Forever our daughter. Forever our Edith.'

“We cannot bring our daughter home and we cannot change what happened on October 25 and 26, but we can make sure her life continues to make a difference. Our daughter Edith Hazel Kennedy will always be remembered. She will always be loved. And everything we do from this point forward will be for her.

The family is raising money through their GoFundMe campaign, with all money raised intended for 4Louis so Edith’s name can be added to memory boxes or cold blankets funded through the donations.
