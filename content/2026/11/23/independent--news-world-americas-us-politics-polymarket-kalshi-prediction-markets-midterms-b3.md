A handful of mostly professional, sophisticated accounts on Polymarket's overseas prediction market netted most of their winnings from bets on congressional races in the U.S. midterm elections, according to research to be published on Monday.

The Anti-Corruption Data Collective, a nonprofit research group, also found that Polymarket and rival Kalshi were only about as good as mainstream forecasting models at picking election winners.

Prediction market proponents often say they surface unbiased, accurate information by channeling the wisdom of crowds, unlike traditional election polls and forecasting models.

![Kalshi and Polymarket say they have developed advanced systems to detect and prevent misconduct and refer suspicious activity to federal authorities](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/23/11/2026-10-27T171834Z_1668764689_RC2MRMAZRU64_RTRMADP_3_USA-CFTC-POLYMARKET.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

But the finding that sophisticated accounts or "wallets" won at the expense of average traders, while markets did not meaningfully outperform forecasters, undercuts that claim, raising questions of whether election betting offers any net social value, according to the nonprofit research group. Polymarket winnings from U.S.congressional races total roughly $23 million.

"Prediction markets create real risks to election integrity that cannot be ignored, while concentrating the financial rewards on a select few," the report said.

## Prediction markets defend their value

Kalshi and Polymarket say they have developed advanced systems to detect and prevent misconduct and refer suspicious activity to federal authorities.

Polymarket said on social media that its markets accurately forecast outcomes in 98.4% of races, as of the day before Election Day.

"Prediction markets have become an important source of real-time election information as they bring together the knowledge and insights of informed participants to show the public how likely different outcomes are," a company spokesperson said.

![Democratic Senate candidate Abdul El-Sayed speaks at a victory rally following his resounding victory in Michigan](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/05/18/EEUU-ELECCIONES-EL-SAYED__4825.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

A Kalshi spokesperson referred to the company's post-election analysis, which said its results substantially outperformed most forecast models, according to a statistical measure known as the Brier score, which compares outcomes to forecast probabilities.

According to the research group's report, traders wagered more than $180 million on congressional races on Polymarket's blockchain-based offshore platform, which is publicly visible online.

Just 819 wallets, including many that are automated or controlled by professional traders, reaped 91% of the winnings, putting them in the top 1%, the research group said.

The report found that the top 0.1% of wallets, or 82, collected more than half of the winnings, underscoring that concentration further. Meanwhile, 99% of wallets that bet on the midterms collectively lost $15 million.

The report identified automated and professional accounts by observing round-the-clock activity, trading volume and breadth of market exposure.

![President Donald Trump attends the White House Crypto Summit at the White House in March 2025](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/23/07/2026-11-22T222811Z_2022032574_RC2ATOARB96Q_RTRMADP_3_TRUMP-CRYPTO-CLUB.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The Commodity Futures Trading Commission, which is fighting in court to shield prediction markets from state oversight, has said the industry's forecasts can generate "informational value" that amounts to a public benefit.

However, the research group's work showed that Kalshi, Polymarket, and Polymarket U.S. combined had an accuracy rate of 91% in U.S. Senate races and 95% in House of Representatives elections, roughly matching an equally weighted average of six election forecast models.

The findings broadly align with other assessments of prediction markets' accuracy in the midterms, including those by CNBC and the *New York Times*. The latter found prediction markets began to outperform forecasters only shortly before Election Day.

"There is little learned exclusively from prediction markets not already captured by one of the high-quality available forecast models," the report said.
