Treasury Scott Bessent has sought to reassure Americans about the recent run of worrying tech outages by comparing them to vaccinations.

Bessent was appearing on CBS News’s *Face the Nation* Sunday and was asked about the outages, which have hit Microsoft, air traffic control systems, banking services, the National Security Agency, Apple and Delta in recent weeks.

“In this age of AI, what should Americans be prepared for, and should they worry about their financial institutions?” the secretary was asked by anchor Margaret Brennan.

“It’s a good question, Margaret, and, no, they shouldn’t,” Bessent answered. “The banks have been at the forefront of cybersecurity for decades.

“They experience attacks every day, every week, every month, and they are by far the most resilient companies in the U.S. economy.”

![Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent likened the recent spate of tech outages to receiving a vaccination against future threats](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/23/11/26/Scott-Bessent-d69be03b.png?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&trim=27%2C1%2C104%2C0&auto=webp)

Using “SI” (super-intelligence), President Donald Trump’s preferred term for AI, he continued: “What we are seeing… Because of the SI, as they go in to patch their security systems, the patches may cause a glitch, which is what happened last week.

“You know, I think the American people should think of it as a vaccine. Your arm may hurt, you may feel a little bad the next day, but it increases your long-term immunity and resiliency.”

A clip of Bessent’s analogy shared on X was met with incredulity, particularly because the Trump administration has engaged in scaremongering over vaccines since allying with Health and Human Services Secretary Robert F Kennedy Jr’s Make America Healthy Again movement.

“Convince people vaccines are dangerous, then tell them to think of their personal and financial safety as a vaccine,” one person wrote. “These morons are even bad at lying.”

“MAGA spent years questioning vaccine safety,” said another. “Now they’re recommending a healthy dose of infrastructure collapse.”

![Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin was ridiculed last week for saying ‘a guy that’s very smart with tech’ had told him AI might be behind the outages](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/17/13/43/Screenshot-2026-11-17-at-8-21-37-AM.png?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&auto=webp)

“What an outrageous ridiculous uninformed irresponsible thing to say,” commented a third person. “Nothing about a cyber attack is like a vaccine. It is not voluntarily administered. It has the potential for great harm. And it does NOT increase our immunity or resiliency.”

Vice President JD Vance and Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin both attracted similar derision last week for their responses to the outages, with the latter hauled over the coals for saying “a guy that’s very smart with tech” had told him AI could be the problem.

Secretary of State Marco Rubio struck a more reassuring note at a press conference in the Philippines, at which he tried to find a positive in the chaos, as Bessent did.

Rubio sought to reassure the public that there was no evidence “nefarious actors” were behind the disasters and argued that their occurrence was “actually a positive development” because it demonstrated businesses’ willingness to come forward and report problems, rather than attempt to conceal them to safeguard their reputations.

“​​I think we’ve now reached a different era, which is companies – which is a good thing – are now immediately sharing vulnerabilities because they don’t want to be accused of covering up something,” he said.

His political stock has risen accordingly, at the expense of the hapless Vance, as Republicans look ahead to 2028.
