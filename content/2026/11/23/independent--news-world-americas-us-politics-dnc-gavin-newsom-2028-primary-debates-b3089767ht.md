White House contenders seeking the Democratic nomination must reject spending by undisclosed donors before they can appear on a primary debate stage, the Democratic National Committee announced Monday.

The party is preparing for a potentially huge field of contenders for the 2028 race. The DNC, which runs the presidential primary process, is planning 12 debates to help choose its 2028 White House standard-bearer.

So many candidates are expected to enter that provisions have been built in for some debates to run across two nights, with a maximum of 20 candidates taking part in total. The first two debates are scheduled for June and July 2027, while the final one is expected in March 2028, though the party said it could adjust the schedule if needed.

![California Gov. Gavin Newsom is considered among the frontrunners for the 2028 Democratic nomination](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/18/14/2305572109..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The DNC said it plans five presidential primary debates in 2027 and seven in 2028. It will announce locations for the early debates in January. To qualify for the debates, candidates must sign a pledge denouncing "dark money"—political spending by groups that do not disclose their donors to influence elections.

The requirement is part of a DNC effort to curb the influence of outside groups that can spend unlimited amounts to support or oppose candidates without revealing their funding sources. For the first time, candidates must pledge to disavow dark money as a condition of making the debate stage, something DNC Chairman Ken Martin has pushed for.

Corruption in government was a top concern for voters in the midterm elections. Candidates will also have to meet polling and grassroots fundraising requirements to demonstrate voter support before they can qualify for the debates.

The DNC said the criteria for making the debate stage will be similar to 2020, when there was also a big field and a dozen debates. The debates could play a key role in generating attention for candidates and winnowing the field of possible nominees.

![Popular Decmorat Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez has so far refused to rule out a run for the White House 2028](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/23/10/2305111237..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Anticipating many presidential hopefuls, the DNC said it will cap participation in each debate at 20 qualifying candidates over two nights, if needed. The announcement comes before major candidates have launched campaigns to succeed term-limited Republican President Donald Trump.

It follows the party's convincing midterm performance, when Democrats flipped both the House and Senate and secured their biggest majority in the new Congress in decades. After routing Republicans in the November 3 midterm elections, Democrats now face a potentially bruising presidential nominating battle, with no clear frontrunner yet.

At a gathering in Denver shortly after the elections, tensions between centrist Democrats and a restive progressive wing surfaced, underscoring long-standing ideological and generational divides as the party seeks a 2028 White House. In a statement, Martin said the party aims to stay united and help the eventual nominee win the White House.

“The DNC is keeping our foot on the gas as we head into the 2028 cycle,” he said in an emailed statement. The debate schedule is one of the DNC's most closely watched decisions, along with selecting the city for the 2028 convention.

Earlier this year, the DNC narrowed the list of cities to Boston, Denver and Philadelphia. The DNC said more details are set to be announced in January, including specific times and locations for the debates.
