Jensen Huang is returning to lead Nvidia after he left in the wake of a run of scandals, the company has said.

Mr Huang resigned from the company he helped found on 12 November, after reports of a number of affairs, including with one woman accused of being a Chinese spy.

In the time since, Donald Trump and many tech leaders called for his return and a petition supporting him gathered hundreds of thousands of signatures – leading Mr Huang to report that he would return to the company if its board asked him to.

Now, Nvidia says that its board has unanimously agreed that he should return. The decision followed “a series of discussions and meetings with Mr Huang”, which left it satisfied with his “candour and ability to lead”, the board said.

Nvidia’s announcement came after a pressure campaign from people including Donald Trump, who had said hours before the announcement that rehiring Mr Huang would be “the best thing for the company, and also, for the USA”. The two are close, and the president has repeatedly backed Mr Huang throughout the scandal.

In a Truth Social post immediately after Nvidia announced the decision to reappoint Mr Huang, Trump said it was “the correct and proper decision”. “The Country needs Jensen’s leadership at NVIDIA in order to ensure the Success and Prosperity of not only the Company, but the American Economy,” he wrote.

“The Country needs Jensen’s leadership at NVIDIA in order to ensure the Success and Prosperity of not only the Company, but the American Economy,” the post continued, citing attorney general Todd Blanche’s statements that the government “does not possess evidence that he has engaged in any unlawful conduct”.

“On a personal note, I have gotten to know Jensen, and he is a Great Patriot and National Treasure,” Trump added.

In its announcement, Nvidia’s board said it had received “input from senior levels of the United States government” that had led it to be “confident” that Mr Huang does not pose a national security risk, it said. Critics had argued that Mr Huang’s relationship with an accused Chinese spy could undermine the US’s work to keep its chips, which are central to today’s AI technology, could be acquired by China.

“The board believes Jensen is the right person to lead Nvidia, and that his reinstatement is in the best interests of our stockholders, employees, partners and customers,” said Harvey C Jones, its chair. “We have every confidence in Jensen and look forward to working with him as he resumes his leadership of the company.”

Mr Huang said he was “honoured” to return to Nvidia. “I take this responsibility with the greatest humility and renewed commitment,” he said in a statement.

Nvidia had previously announced that it had launched an independent investigation into accusations that Mr Huang had been successfully targeted by a “foreign intelligence effort”. That will continue to its conclusion and Mr Huang is committed to co-operate with it, Nvidia said.

It has also recently formed a new committee that will oversee Nvidia’s compliance with trade rules and national security risks. Nvidia will appoint a new chief compliance officer who will oversee that work, it said, and it committed to co-operate with ongoing government investigations.

Those investigations are examining a host of allegations that have dogged Nvidia for some time, and became more widely discussed in the wake of the scandal and Mr Huang’s departure. Multiple investigations of the company ​are ⁠underway, including by the US SEC and the Department of Justice, related to everything from potential accounting issues to alleged chip smuggling.

After Mr Huang resigned on 12 November, Nvidia appointed its chief financial officer, Colette Kress, to run the company. But that decision was met with immediate criticism, and reports that Mr Huang did not have confidence in her as his successor.

In the days that followed, pressure had mounted on Nvidia’s board to reappoint Mr Huang. Trump repeatedly backed his return, alongside other tech leaders such as Elon Musk, and a petition supporting him now has more than 200,000 signatures, including people claiming to be employees of and investors in Nvidia.

Last week, five days after his departure, Mr Huang posted his first public statement on the scandal. He said that he had seen “an outpouring of calls from NVIDIA’s employees, investors, business partners, and customers for me to return as CEO” and that he would be “honoured” to return if the board decided it was in the best interest of the company and the country.

In the days that followed, Nvidia repeatedly denied that it was looking to bring him back. In its quarterly earnings call on Tuesday, for instance, Ms Kress said that it was “business as usual” and Mr Jones, the chair of its board, confirmed that Nvidia was not considering bringing back Mr Huang.

But Nvidia’s share price continued to fall, and has dropped more than 20 per cent since those earnings were announced. That provoked fears that concern about Nvidia and its falling share price could undermine the investment in the US’s AI industry, which in turn could have dramatic effects on the US economy.

In the wake of Mr Huang’s reinstatement, however, the company’s share price jumped back up. Nvidia’s share price was up 31 per cent in the moments after the announcement.
