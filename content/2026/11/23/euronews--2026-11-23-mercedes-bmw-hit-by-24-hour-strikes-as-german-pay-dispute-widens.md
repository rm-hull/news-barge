## IG Metall, Germany’s largest trade union, called on more than 23,000 workers at 19 northern sites to join a 24-hour strike. The walkouts hit Mercedes-Benz, BMW, and Siemens Gamesa, with Meyer Werft workers also called out.

Workers in Germany’s metal and electrical industries walked out this week after three rounds of pay talks ended without a deal. IG Metall called on more than 23,000 workers at 19 sites in northern Germany to take part in a 24-hour strike.

Further walkouts are expected in Berlin and the union's central district, which covers Hesse, Rhineland-Palatinate, Saarland, and Thuringia.

The latest "warning strikes" follow nationwide walkouts on Friday that drew 151,000 workers, according to IG Metall. Audi’s main plant in Ingolstadt and three BMW plants in Bavaria were among the sites hit.

Three rounds of negotiations for around 130,000 employees in northern Germany have so far been unsuccessful.

IG Metall is seeking a 5% pay rise over one year for 3.7 million workers, including those in the automotive sector. Employers have offered a one-off payment in July 2027 worth 1% of gross annual pay, and a 1.3% pay rise from July 2028. The union said the offer would leave workers worse off after inflation.

Strikes began at several factories on Sunday evening as night shifts started, including at Mercedes-Benz plants in Bremen and Hamburg.

Tagesschau, the news service of German public broadcaster ARD, reported that production lines at Mercedes-Benz’s Hamburg plant have been idle since 10pm on Sunday.

"So far, the plant is completely sealed off and no one has been allowed in to work," an IG Metall representative told the news service on Monday morning. "Only plant security is present, and otherwise nothing is happening here."

Wind turbine maker Siemens Gamesa in Cuxhaven is also impacted.

The union also called on workers at the Meyer Werft shipyard in Papenburg to walk out on Monday, NDR reported.

Volkswagen, which owns Audi, is seeking a two-year pay freeze and more flexible working hours in separate talks with IG Metall covering about 120,000 employees, according to Reuters.

BMW previously sought to cut nearly 8,000 jobs through voluntary severance, according to Münchner Merkur.

Mercedes-Benz is seeking €800 million in annual savings in Germany, Handelsblatt reported. Longer working hours without extra pay and cuts to benefits were among the options under discussion, German public broadcaster SWR reported. Mercedes-Benz has also said it would suspend investment in Germany.

Euronews contacted BMW, Mercedes-Benz and Volkswagen for comment.
