Kremlin warns of Baltic escalation after Ukraine strike on cargo ship

Meanwhile, Moscow warned about worsening security in the Baltic Sea after a suspected Ukrainian drone strike on a separate Russian cargo ship at the weekend, AFP reported.

“It is indeed a very dangerous trend, the expansion of terrorist activity in the Baltic region... This is very dangerous. It is, of course, leading to an escalation of tensions in the region,” Kremlin spokesperson Dmitry Peskov told reporters.

On Saturday, Finland said it had detected a drone attack in international waters on a Russian cargo vessel that later began drifting towards Finnish waters.

Picture released by the Finnish Border Guard shows fire onboard the damaged Sparta 2 cargo ship in the Gulf of Finland. Photograph: Lehtikuva/Finnish Border Guard/AFP/Getty Images

The ship, identified as the Sparta II, was anchored about 90 kilometres (nearly 50 nautical miles) east of Helsinki while efforts to put out a fire on board were under way, but was later towed towards Russian waters.

Peskov said Russia “would like to hope that the Baltic states will somehow use their influence” on Ukraine to “warn it against continuing such dangerous terrorist practices”.

Lying at the easternmost end of the Baltic along the coasts of Finland, Estonia and Russia, the Gulf of Finland is a key maritime passage for Russia, leading to the former capital, St Petersburg.

And in the last few minutes, Russia’s RIA news agency reported that Russia has summoned the top Estonian diplomat in the country, so we may get more lines on this today…

Russia's calls for return to pre-1997 European security order 'unacceptable,' Rutte says

Nato’s Rutte got a question on Russia’s demands to rewrite the European security order as part of the Ukraine peace settlement.

“We are all totally clear that the proposals Russia put forward in 2021, basically going back to … for Nato to basically move backwards from where we are now and to the 1997, borders are unacceptable. And there’s nobody in Nato who thinks this is a good idea.”

Rutte also gets pushed a bit on the Polish incident last week, as he says:

“It is clear that this is a dangerous incident. It comes from Russia. Whether it is intentional or not has to be decided, of course, by authorities. I will never speculate. I can only comment on that when we have the evidence coming out of, of the system, where Poland and other Nato allies work together to assess what exactly happened.”

Over in Berlin, Rutte is still diplomatically answering questions from national delegations and avoiding many any news.

So far his most entertaining line was about making sure he never forgets to mention Italy as a key Nato ally, because he doesn’t want to fight Italy’s defence minister, Guido Crosetto.

“If I would ever forget about Italy, then the defence minister, Guido Crossetto, whom you don’t want to get into a fight with given his build, will remind me quite forcefully, I can assure you.”

Meanwhile, Rutte is still answering questions at Nato’s parliamentary assembly – on the Arctic security and the expected review of US force posture in Europe, among others – but he’s bravely avoiding saying anything remotely new so far.

Albania denies Ukrainian drones that sank Russian warship were launched from its territory

Meanwhile, Albania has strongly denied suggestions that the naval drone attack against the Russian warship in Libya originated from its territory.

“The reports in question are inaccurate and do not reflect the facts. Albania was not involved in this incident, and its territory was neither used nor made available for the launch or facilitation of such an attack,” Albania’s ministry of foreign affairs said.

It added:

“We categorically reject these allegations, which have been presented without verifiable evidence. We call on those responsible for disseminating them to correct the record and to exercise accuracy, due diligence and responsibility when reporting on matters of such sensitivity.”

'We have to show Putin that his attempts to weaken our support will fail,' Rutte says

Turning to Ukraine, Nato’s Rutte stresses the need to secure the flow of “crucial supplies” or air defence weapons and ammunition to Ukraine.

“It helps Ukraine to defend itself today, but it also strengthens Ukraine’s hands in any future negotiations with Russia. … But support needs to be concrete. We have to show Putin that his attempts to weaken our support will fail.”

He also stresses the need to offer broader support to Ukraine, as well as build the resilience of Nato societies against any potential acts of sabotage against critical infrastructure.

“Our citizens need to know that the lights will stay on, that their homes will stay warm, that hospitals, transport and communications will keep working.”

He then wraps up with a point about the value of democratic societies and why Nato rivals fear that freedom most.

'We are all targeted' and 'it won't go away anytime soon,' Nato's Rutte warns

Nato’s Rutte is speaking now, calling for “action and real leadership” in Nato’s response to growing threats from China, Iran, Russia and North Korea, and “intensifying malign activities targeting our nations.”

“One day it can be in the form of an arson attack against a factory or drones disrupt disrupting an airport. The next, it is sabotaged railways or cargo vessels targeted at sea and cyber-attacks.”

Rutte specificially references “the dangerous incident in Poland on Friday,” and “some serious stuff happening on the Black Sea” prompting Nato to look at the regional security more closely with Bulgaria, Romania and Turkey.

“It’s all happening now, and it won’t go away anytime soon. … The conclusion of all of this is that we are all targeted, that we are all affected – when it comes to our pipelines, our cables, our energy grids and our economies – and they are all interconnected. … That means that we are all in this for the long term, and therefore we must step up to the plate, to ensure that, we stay ahead of danger.”

He urges Nato leaders to “stay focused” on defence spending, building up to the 5% GDP defence spending pledge for 2035 made at the last summit in the Hague.

“But if we are serious about keeping our alliance strong and secure, then all allies must pull their weight not only in 2035, but starting now. At least, everybody needs to have the credible path to get there.”

Addressing Nato lawmarkers, he urges them “to build the political and public support for accelerating defence investment and for sustaining it over the longer term.”

“These investments are also a boon to prosperity. There are dividends to be gained from investing more in defence. This is about real jobs for real people.”

Work underway to set up fourway peace talks with Russia, US, Europe 'before Christmas', Zelenskyy says

Zelenskyy also said that “partners” – presumably the US – want the fourway talks between Ukraine, Russia, the US and Europe to take place before Christmas.

“We are ready to do this literally tomorrow. We have heard from our partners that they want to try to make it happen before Christmas. We hope it happens sooner, and we must come to this meeting with a clear agenda, prepared and agreed by all of us.”

He added:

“When the main question comes up, ‘what happens after this war?’, our common answer must be security. Real security. Security for Ukraine, security for all of Russia’s neighbours, security for Europe and the entire Euro-Atlantic area. This is only possible if we work together as closely as possible. I believe this is only possible when Russia has no temptation to test whether security guarantees really work with Nato.”

Ukraine 'keep finding ways to get back on our feet after every Russian attack,' Zelenskyy says

In the last half hour, Volodymyr Zelenskyy addressed the Nato parliamentary Assembly meeting in Berlin.

Ukraine's president Volodymyr Zelenskyy is seen on a screen as he speaks at the 72nd Annual Session of the Nato parliamentary Assembly in Berlin, Germany. Photograph: Liesa Johannssen/Reuters

He warned that “Russian aggression has shown that when Ukraine is left out, and when there is no clear understanding of how Europe should be protected, anything can happen.”

He also celebrated overnight strikes against Russia, saying Ukraine “keep finding ways to get back on our feet after every Russian attack.”

“Today, Russia can feel our response all the way from western Siberia, where its oil refiners are burning, to the coast of Libya, where its warships are sinking.”

Zelenskyy stressed that Ukraine has repeatedly offered “steps to reduce the fighting,” and “never been the ones to raise the stakes,” but Russia “still gives us no peaceful response.”

He urged Nato leaders to “invest in stronger security and greater resilience,” saying “does anyone here still believe that one day Russia will simply become peaceful and safe again?”

“Just look at the last, last 100 years of Russian history. … There is no reason to believe that Russia will become a different country in one year or five years. So we must act in a way that makes sure that no matter what kind of Russia we face and no matter what threats our countries face, we will always have enough to defend ourselves.”

He urged partners to support Ukraine ahead of winter, particularly helping with air defence systems.

“The stronger our defences are, the less chance Russia has to break through them and the more chance diplomacy has to work.”

The Ukrainian president also slightly pushed back against the suggested Article Five-like security guarantees for Ukraine in case of a peace deal with Russia, arguing that it should simply be allowed to join Nato instead.

“When we talk about Article Five-like guarantees, we need answers. We need answers to some very clear questions. Which countries will support Ukraine if Ukraine is attacked again, what exactly will our partners do in response? Which troops will be stationed on our territory in advance? How much will all these cost and will it really work? All of this is not easy and so far we have no clear, specific answers with respect to such ideas.”

Morning opening: Ukraine claims responsibility for sinking Russian warship in Libya

Jakub Krupa

Ukraine’s president Volodymyr Zelenskyy has claimed responsibility for sinking a Russian warship at a Libyan naval base on Sunday, saying it was part of Kyiv’s response to Russia’s continuing aggression.

Acombination of satellite images shows a Russian corvette and the northern port area before (above) and after (below) an explosion aboard a Russian ship docked at the Benghazi naval base, Libya. Photograph: VANTOR/Reuters

“An exemplary Ukrainian operation in response to Russia’s war,” he said in a post on X, with a video showing the moment the corvette was hit by Ukrainian drones.

“Our warriors deployed seven Ukrainian-made naval drones. Despite all the enemy’s efforts to counter the attack, the Russian corvette Merkury was sunk. It’s one of their newest ships, commissioned in 2023, during the full-scale war,” he said.

Zelenskyy added that a Russian dry cargo vessel MV Angara, which he said was used for transporting weapons, was also sunk.

Elsewhere, Nato and the UN will meet today to discuss the security situation in the region.

Nato’s North Atlantic Council (NAC) will meet to discuss the events of last Friday, when three Russian missiles crashed in Poland amid a broader assault on Ukraine. Poland is expected to ask for further military support from allies.
