President Donald Trump predicted Monday that the scandal that forced Jensen Huang out of Nvidia — an affair with a woman U.S. officials suspect of being a Chinese spy — would do the chief executive no lasting harm.

Huang was returned to the top job at Nvidia on Monday, days after resigning, after Trump called in to a meeting of the board to press for his reinstatement, When asked about the affair that triggered the whole episode during an interview with CNBC, Trump said Huang would emerge stronger for it.

“I think he’s going to start off at an almost higher level,” the president said. “He apologized for indiscretion, which was unfortunate. That was probably what brought it to such a level, because sex sells.

“I think it could be a net positive,” Trump added. “I don’t think he’s going to be diminished at all. I think people respect what he’s gone through. They respect it.”

The president went further still on the national security question at the center of the scandal, telling CNBC that Huang “did nothing wrong” and has been “exonerated” despite the company not having completed its internal probe.

![Trump browbeat Nvidia’s board into reinstating the disgraced CEO over the weekend](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/12/15/2026-11-12T135616Z_782149966_RC27TNA8A8AX_RTRMADP_3_NVIDIA-CEO.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Huang resigned earlier this month after acknowledging an extramarital affair with Alice Huang — no relation — a businesswoman that U.S. intelligence has assessed was in contact with China’s Ministry of State Security during the relationship and was working to steer him toward selling Beijing more of his company’s advanced chips. A second affair, with a subordinate, had moved Nvidia’s board to remove him from the company he co-founded three decades ago.

His ouster sparked demands for his reinstatement from top technology industry figures, many of whom have benefited from a controversial circular financing scheme under which Nvidia has loaned burgeoning AI companies cash used to buy Nvidia chips.

Trump had demanded over the weekend that Huang be returned to his former post, writing over the weekend that doing so would be “the best thing for the company, and also, for the USA.”

The chip giant’s board acquiesced to his demands, issuing a statement which said it had unanimously agreed to bring Huang back after “a series of discussions and meetings” which left the board satisfied with his “candor and ability to lead” the company.

![Jensen Huang with a woman confirmed by a Reuters source to be Alice Huang in Taipei, Taiwan released on June 7, 2024.](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/23/16/2026-11-23T014955Z_369227831_RC2DKOA9OV9C_RTRMADP_3_USA-CHINA-NVIDIA-SPY.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

In a Truth Social post immediately after Nvidia announced the decision to re-appoint Huang, Trump said it was “the correct and proper decision.”

“The Country needs Jensen’s leadership at NVIDIA in order to ensure the Success and Prosperity of not only the Company, but the American Economy,” he said.

Trump also claimed that Attorney General Todd Blanche had assured him that the Department of Justice “does not possess evidence that he has engaged in any unlawful conduct.”

Separately, Nvidia’s board said it had received “input from senior levels of the United States government” that had led it to be “confident” that Mr Huang does not pose a national security risk.

“The board believes Jensen is the right person to lead Nvidia, and that his reinstatement is in the best interests of our stockholders, employees, partners and customers,” said Harvey C Jones, its chair. “We have every confidence in Jensen and look forward to working with him as he resumes his leadership of the company.”
