A renowned British Asian surgeon has won over £100,000 for race discrimination after the NHS told him he had a '99 per cent' chance of landing a top job but then gave it to a white colleague.

Usman Butt has won the huge payout after he successfully sued the 'racist' NHS Trust for failing to give him the promotion, with a judge criticising 'systemic' failures.

Mr Butt was the only candidate to become Chair of Division of Surgery but was rejected after NHS chiefs claimed the division was too big for him - despite it being the smallest within the Trust.

He was told that he was '99 per cent likely' that he would get the role but after being unsuccessful the Trust hired a white colleague - who he believed was less experienced or qualified.

Mr Butt took the case to Manchester Employment Tribunal which has found that 'the procedural failings were systemic and numerous'.

A report into Northern Care Alliance NHS Foundation Trust found that Black, Asian and Minority Ethnic (BAME) staff were treated as 'second-class citizens' and their white colleagues were 1.86 times more likely to be appointed to roles.

The tribunal found that Mr Butt was discriminated against because of his race and awarded him £101,338.74 in compensation.

Mr Butt was a British Asian Consultant Orthopaedic Surgeon working for the Northern Care Alliance NHS Foundation Trust - which is based in Manchester - from August 2015.

He had a masters in business administration and a senior leader level seven degree, both awarded with distinction.

He also fulfilled the accolade of chartered manager awarded by the chartered management institute.

He applied and was interviewed in May 2022 for the role of Chair of Division of Surgery within the Salford care organisation but was not successful because he did not have the required experience.

![The Northern Care Alliance NHS Foundation Trust based in Manchester](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/05/22/9/37/Northern-Care-Alliance-NHS-Foundation-Trust.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Evidence from the NHS Trust's workforce race equality standard document showed that white individuals were 1.86 times more likely to be appointed from shortlisting across all posts compare to BAME individuals.

In November 2023 he was advised to apply for the role again and was the only candidate.

He was shortlisted, achieved a threshold score and was told that it is '99 per cent likely that he would be appointed'.

The tribunal heard: "It was not in dispute that [Mr Butt] met all of the essential requirements of the role. [Mr Butt] also met one of the two other desirable criteria, being management qualification."

He faced a stakeholder panel of nine members and he 'was considered appointable by that panel and there was no dispute about that part of the process'.

He 'delivered a well-structured presentation, was personable, and responded to questions effectively'.

Every member of the panel scored Mr Butt with at least the minimum score to be considered appointable.

There were concerns from some panel members about him only having experience in a small department, despite surgery being the smallest division with 1,033 employees.

However in February 2024 he received an automated email telling me that he would not get the position.

The previous holder of the position, who had resigned, was asked to remain and agreed to do so.

A few months later a white British doctor was appointed.

It was later found that she had been asked different interview questions and Mr Butt believed she was less qualified and experienced for the role than him.

Mr Butt claimed this amounted to race discrimination, yet the NHS Trust disputed his claim.

It was heard that the NHS Trust commissioned an independent investigation into themselves related to diversity.

The barrister who conducted the investigation, Carlo Breen, said in their report: "I was very troubled to hear allegations of racism: that most Black, Asian, and Minority Ethnic (“BAME”) staff felt and still do feel that they were and are treated as second-class citizens.

"During interviews with some individuals from a BAME background, I was extremely troubled to hear that they believed that the Trust was and remains racist.

"This issue cannot and must not be ignored. The Trust should engage with these colleagues to understand why they have come to this conclusion, and to ensure that any such behaviour, if apparent, is stamped out and never repeated.

"This perceived cronyism resulted in legitimate and real concerns not being raised due to a perceived lack of independence and accountability.

"Any measure afforded to the staff to ‘speak up’ was met with mistrust and suspicion."

A British Asian consultant spinal surgeon, who had previously been rejected for the role, told the tribunal that 'there existed a deeply embedded culture in [Northern Care Alliance NHS Foundation Trust] where senior leadership roles were frequently allocated through informal networks that tended to exclude BAME clinicians'.

Mr Butt moved to a different Trust in July 2024 after his notice period expired.

He later successfully took claims of race discrimination and indirect discrimination to the employment tribunal in Manchester.

He was awarded £101,338.74 in compensation for the injury to feelings and financial losses.

Employment Judge Phil Allen said: "The procedural failings were systemic and numerous.

"We considered that it should have been a relatively simple exercise, if the reasons had been accurate and genuine, for [Northern Care Alliance NHS Foundation Trust] to have proved why it did not appoint [Mr Butt] to the role for which he applied.

"We found that he had proved primary facts from which we could conclude that the difference in treatment was because of race.

"Similarly, and applying the same approach, we found that [Mr Butt] had shown facts which shifted the burden of proof and meant that we could conclude that a hypothetical comparator who was not a person of colour (or was not British/Asian) would have been treated differently.

"We have found that following the reversal of the burden of proof and particularly in the light of the unreliability and inconsistency of the evidence which we heard from [Northern Care Alliance NHS Foundation Trust's] witnesses, we found that [Northern Care Alliance NHS Foundation Trust] had not shown that the decision not to appoint [Mr Butt] was in no sense whatsoever because of race."
