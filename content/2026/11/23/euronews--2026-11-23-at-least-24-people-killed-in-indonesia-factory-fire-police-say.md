## Dozens of employees are still reported to be missing after a blaze broke out at a candy factory in Banten province at around 4:30 pm local time on Saturday.

At least 24 people were killed in a deadly fire that ripped through a factory in western Java over the weekend, police have said.

The blaze broke out at a candy factory in the Serang Regency of Banten province at around 4:30 pm local time on Saturday, trapping workers inside. It had been extinguished by 6:30 am the following day.

Dozens of employees are still reported to be missing.

Hendra Kurniawan, the chief inspector general of the Banten regional police force, said on Sunday that the bodies "were no longer physically identifiable" and that DNA testing would need to be used for identification purposes.

The presidential advisor for workers' welfare, Said Iqbal, said in a statement on Monday that the fire could have been caused by a short circuit in the building's AC system, but he stressed that further investigations were needed to determine how it began.

Indonesia's National Search and Rescue Agency (Basarnas) shared footage on social media on Sunday showing emergency crews looking through the charred remains of the building.

Other images from the scene show extensive damage to the factory's roof as well as burned-out vehicles.

The candy company, Fonusa Agung Mulia, describes itself online as a subsidiary of Indonesia's UNIFAM food company.
