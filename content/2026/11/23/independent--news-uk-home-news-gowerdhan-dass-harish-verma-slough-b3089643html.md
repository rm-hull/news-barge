A driving instructor accused of "poisoned" his dad’s mind against his older brother after the siblings fell out over seat allocation at a family wedding has been left with nothing after losing a bitter £400,000 inheritance fight.

Pensioner Gowerdhan Dass died, aged 89, in February 2024, passing his whole estate, including his home in Northmead Road, Slough, to youngest son, driving instructor Harish Verma, 57, and his wife, Paramjit.

That was in line with a will made in November 2023 - just three months before his sudden death - which left Harish's cab driver older brother Bani Verma, 67, and the rest of the family empty-handed.

Bani went on to sue, claiming his little brother had "unduly influenced" their ailing dad to rip up the earlier will and poisoned his mind against him and other family members in a bid to scoop all the inheritance.

Driving instructor Harish defended the will, disputing the allegations and claiming Bani had distanced himself from their dad in his latter days and even mocked his memory by wearing a garish tartan Tam-o-Shanter bonnet on the day of his funeral.

![Bani Verma in Scottish tam o shanter on day of father's funeral. Part of court exhibits](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/23/8/22/VermaHat-(1).jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

But after a week-long trial, Recorder Jonathan Cohen KC backed Bani’s case, finding on the balance of probabilities that Harish and Paramjit had made Gowerdhan believe the rest of his family were shunning him in order to pocket his fortune.

Ruling that both husband and wife committed “fraudulent calumny” in "poisoning" the pensioner’s mind, the judge reinstated an earlier will under which Bani, Harish and the families of their two now dead other siblings get about £100,000 each.

However, the judge went on to rule that Harish and his wife should pay the costs of the bitter dispute, with £115,000 up front towards Bani's fees, effectively wiping out Harish's share of the estate.

“Harish did make false representations to Mr Dass," said the judge.

“The false representations were made for the purpose of inducing Mr Dass to alter his testamentary disposition and Harish made such representations knowing them to be untrue.

“The fraudulent calumny induced the deceased to make the 2023 will."

He added that, in giving instructions for his final will, the dad had told his solicitor in his younger son's presence that he no longer had contact with his immediate family apart from him and his wife, which the judge said was “completely untrue”, and that Harish had done nothing to correct this false impression.

![Bani Verma outside Central London County Court after hearing in will row with brother Harish](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/23/8/45/BaniVerma-(1).jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

During the hearing of the case, Central London County Court heard Gowerdhan described as a kind and gentle character with “never a cross word for anyone” and a passion for making pickles.

He had worked as a land surveyor in India as a young man before coming to the UK in the 1970’s where he later worked as a security officer at Heathrow.

The court heard bad blood began to develop between Harish and Bani over 30 years ago, stretching back to a dispute about missing stock in a grocery store Bani was then running and a £5,000 loan from Bani to kick start the DJ career Harish dreamed of establishing, which was said to have not been repaid, explained the trial judge, Recorder Cohen KC.

The animosity between the brothers was then cemented at the wedding of Bani’s first daughter in 2011, said the judge, when Harish and Paramjit attended the post-nuptials family gathering but could find no seats.

“They tried to sit down in empty seats and were told by a family member - not Bani - that the seats were reserved,” he added.

“Harish appears to have regarded this reservation of seats as aimed at him and he did not feel welcome. He therefore left as he was upset.

“It also upset Bani, who regards Harish as 'walking out', which was perceived as an openly hostile gesture in front of both families on a celebratory occasion.

“When Harish was cross examined, he readily accepted not only that he does not like Bani but that he actually hates him,” noted the judge.

![Paramjit Kaur Verma outside Central London County Court after hearing in dispute over will of her father-in-law Gowerdhan Dass](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/23/8/03/ParamjitVerma.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The two brothers clashed after this incident, with Bani insisting he was “thrown out” of Harish's home, where their dad was staying, while Harish claimed he told his older brother to leave.

“The one conclusion which I can safely reach on this incident is that its consequence was that it became harder if not inappropriate for Bani to visit Mr Dass at Harish’s home rather than at Mr Dass’ own home less than 100 yards away,” the judge commented.

“My view is that Harish has been unable to show any insight into the difficulty caused by this incident.”

Mr Dass’ last will of 2024 included a specific clause which stated: “I declare that no provision is made in this Will for my son BANI PARSHAD VERMA as there has been little to no contact for many years and my son is not financially dependent upon me and has his own wealth and financial resources.”

Harish’s barrister, Nina Roberts, argued that by the time that will was made he and his wife had become the anchor of Gowerdhan’s life, caring for him and repeatedly visiting while also ensuring he could live as independent a life as possible.

Cabbie Bani, by contrast, was playing no part in his father’s life when he made his final will, she claimed.

On top of all this, she claimed Bani showed "disrespect" for his dad both during his life and even afterwards, pointing out that he donned inappropriate Scottish headgear on the day of his funeral, whilst he joked around “as if it were a wedding”.

In his evidence Harish insisted that he believed that Bani had lost contact with their father, and when Mr Dass made similar non-contact comments about the rest of his family to the solicitor drafting his will, “he did not think it was his place to correct him”.

![Harish Verma outside Central London County Court after hearing in will row with brother Bani.](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/23/8/24/HarishVerma-(1).jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The judge accepted that Harish and his wife had given Gowerdhan loving care in his final years, but found that he became increasingly vulnerable as he grew older and was “unduly influenced” by the couple.

Giving his ruling, the judge said: “Harish has accepted that he sat next to his father, who was making the non-contact statements to (the solicitor). Harish knew those statements were untrue but did not correct them or point that out to his father.”

The falsity of the non-contact statements was “truly startling” in the case of some family members who had done their utmost to keep in touch with Gowerdhan, said the judge, adding:

“In my judgment, it is overwhelmingly likely in the circumstances I have found in this lengthy judgment that Harish and Paramjit did poison Mr Dass’ mind so as to believe these untrue statements, which both knew were untrue and were for their own financial gain. There is no other sensible explanation. They were undoubtedly fraudulent.”

Paramjit had helped in the deception by keeping Gowerdhan away from his other daughter-in-law, Harjinder, after he made his final will, said Recorder Cohen, adding that neither she nor her husband had informed the rest of their family of plans to sell the pensioner’s home, pool some of the profits and buy a house together.

“In the circumstances with which I have already dealt, I am satisfied that the will of Mr Dass was overborne by the fraudulent actions of Harish and Paramjit,” the judge concluded.

The will of 2024 was overturned to be replaced by Mr Dass’ 2015 will, which divides the £400,000 estate four ways between the brothers and the families of their now dead other siblings.

But the judge went on to order Harish and his wife to pay Bani's costs of the trial - with £115,000 up front pending a full assessment of the bills - as well as their own significant costs of the case.
