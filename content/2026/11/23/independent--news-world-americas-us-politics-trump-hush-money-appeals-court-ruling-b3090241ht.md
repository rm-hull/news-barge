A federal appeals court will let Donald Trump try again to move his hush money case out of state criminal court and into a federal court, where he could try to have it thrown out entirely after he became the first president to be convicted of a crime.

The president’s legal team has been fighting in state and federal courts to erase his felony convictions more than two years after a jury in New York found him guilty on 32 counts of falsifying business records as part of a scheme to bury embarrassing stories of his affairs before the 2016 election.

Trump was convicted in state court in Manhattan, but his legal team has been trying, so far unsuccessfully, to remove the case into federal jurisdiction, where he hopes his Department of Justice could make it disappear on “immunity” grounds.

On Monday, a panel of three federal judges in Manhattan sent the question back to the federal district court presiding over whether the case can go there instead. The judge who previously reviewed the case, Alvin Hellerstein, died last month. Appeals court judges want the next judge on the case to let Trump make another attempt.

The decision is a rare legal victory for the president in the case, which surrounded Trump’s efforts to pay off an adult film star to prevent her from speaking publicly about having sex with him.

![A federal appeals court will let Donald Trump try, again, to move his hush money case out of state criminal court and into federal jurisdiction, where the president hopes his Department of Justice can make it disappear](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/07/07/17/2154737383..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Last week, appeals court judges spent more than an hour hearing from Trump’s legal team and state prosecutors about the president’s attempts to move the hush money case into federal court, marking the second time in 11 days that both sides appeared in court to rehash the legal saga.

Trump is also mounting an appeal of his conviction through the state courts, including arguments in the state’s mid-level appellate decision earlier this month. That court has not yet issued a ruling.

Monday’s appeals court decision could also bring those proceedings to a halt.

*The Independent* has requested comment from the office of Manhattan District Attorney Alvin Bragg, whose office tried the case.

Two months after a weeks-long trial in Manhattan and a jury’s unanimous guilty verdicts, the Supreme Court ruled that the president is effectively “immune” from criminal prosecution for actions tied to his “official” acts in office, a landmark decision that upended federal indictments against Trump and has been at the center of arguments over his already-decided hush money case.

But judges and prosecutors have routinely rejected the idea that the president is immune from prosecution for “private conduct” at the center of the hush money case, which involves the president’s scheme to boost his chances during the 2016 presidential election through a series of covered-up payments to adult film actress Stormy Daniels.

![Trump’s then-attorney Michael Cohen testified that Trump use him to pay off Stormy Daniels to suppress the release of damning stories of his alleged affair with the porn star](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/08/20/18/49/GettyImages-1464176343.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Trump’s then-attorney Michael Cohen paid Daniels $130,000 for the rights to her story about having sex with Trump, and Trump then reimbursed Cohen in a series of checks, some of which were cut from the White House in 2017 after his 2016 victory.

Those reimbursements were falsely recorded in accounting records as “legal expenses,” fulfilling a conspiracy to unlawfully influence the 2016 election, the jury found.

Trump’s legal team has also argued that Manhattan prosecutors improperly used testimony from White House aides and other evidence that he claims should have been shielded from criminal prosecution by the Supreme Court’s immunity decision.

The president and his allies have also argued that Cohen recanted his 2024 testimony, and Cohen has tried to patch up his relationship with his former boss following his election victory despite his tell-all books and hours of damning comments on his radio, TV and podcast appearances.

“Does the prosecution, as it was tried, relate to the president’s official acts? Yes, it does,” Trump’s attorney Robert Giuffra Jr. argued to appeals court judges last week.

“This is a case that Congress clearly wanted in federal court,” he argued. “The president is entitled to a federal forum to clear his name.”

Hellerstein, who died last month at age 92, had rejected Trump’s attempts to transfer the case to federal jurisdiction three other times already.

In August, he wrote that “paying hush money to an adult film star or engaging in a cover-up of an embarrassment are not subsumed in presidential immunity.”
