# Andy Burnham government confirms financial literacy lessons in curriculum shake-up

## The Labour government says children will get a stronger grounding in the financial literacy skills they need to manage money

Andy Burnham has confirmed that pupils are set to gain vital real-world money management skills as part of a significant curriculum overhaul. The Labour government has announced that children will receive a more comprehensive grounding in the financial literacy skills required to handle their finances effectively.

Following research from the Initiative for Financial Decision-Making, pupils will be taught about interest rates, payslips and mortgages as part of a wholesale restructuring of the curriculum, with full details expected to be unveiled in the coming weeks.

The government has stated that pupils will be introduced to the "big three" concepts of inflation, compound interest and diversification of risk, deliberately linking mathematics to real-world subjects such as interest, mortgages and savings within citizenship lessons.

The new financial literacy curriculum is currently being trialled in classrooms to ensure schools can deliver it as effectively as possible from 2028, reports Birmingham Live. Education Secretary Lucy Powell said: "Young people are growing up in a world that is changing faster than ever, where spending money can be as easy as tapping a screen.

"But too many are leaving school without the practical skills and confidence they need to navigate adult life. We need an education system that prepares young people not just to pass exams, but to seize the opportunities ahead of them.

"That means mastering the fundamentals of maths and English, while learning how to apply those skills in the real world, from managing money to navigating new technologies and preparing for work. When children understand why what they're learning matters, they are more engaged and motivated to succeed.

"That's why we are building an education system that connects what children learn in the classroom to the world beyond it, giving every young person, whatever their background, the foundations to succeed whatever their path in life."

Sophie Pender, Founder of The 93% Club, the UK's membership organisation for state-educated people, said: "A life skill as essential as financial literacy shouldn't be left to your family and network to teach you. Not every child benefits from knowing people who work in finance or who have lived experience of things like applying for a mortgage, investing, or making their money work for them, meaning many start adult life on the back foot.

"We also know that family attitudes to money can affect the choices you make. For example, when we surveyed state-educated people last year, 45% of working-class students said family or friends had questioned their choice to go to university because of concerns about debt, despite the likely increase in earning potential. Young people need to be empowered and educated to make that decision for themselves rather than relying on others' opinions."
