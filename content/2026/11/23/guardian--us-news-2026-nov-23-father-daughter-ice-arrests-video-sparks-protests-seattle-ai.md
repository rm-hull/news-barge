Protesters marched through the Seattle-Tacoma international airport on Sunday after widely shared footage captured masked immigration agents detaining a father and daughter on an airport jet bridge as they boarded their Alaska Airlines flight.

Video of the incident on Saturday – filmed by fellow passengers – quickly went viral on social media, sparking condemnation from lawmakers, including Washington Democratic governor Bob Ferguson and Seattle mayor Katie Wilson.

Footage and photographs showed protesters with bullhorns and signs that read “ICE out of Washington” and “Abolish ICE” at the Seattle airport. Protesters could be seen walking inside and around the airport chanting “ICE out now” and other rallying cries.

“I made my view very clear to Alaska Airlines: They need to call on ICE to get out of our airports,” Ferguson said in a briefing. “I’ll be making that request of every other airline that operates at SeaTac airport as well.”

On Sunday a lawyer representing Ricardo Larrondo Mendoza and his daughter, Sofia Hernandez Mejia, said they were heading to a family baptism ceremony in California when they were detained by masked agents.

![Protesters at the Seattle airport](https://i.guim.co.uk/img/media/823ab541fadcf3ff002b322456beb10cb76f2fee/0_0_5400_3600/master/5400.jpg?width=445&dpr=1&s=none&crop=none)

Other passengers boarding the Alaska Airlines flight at Seattle-Tacoma international airport used their cellphones to film the father and daughter being detained by the officers, who were wearing unmarked jackets and khaki pants.

The videos show the officers struggling with someone on the ground. The flight, bound for Los Angeles, was delayed.

ICE has stepped up operations at airports since the summer as the agency has shifted tactics from sweeping urban raids to more targeted operations in all corners of US, with arrests reaching record levels.

Ferguson called ICE’s tactics “deeply un-American” and called for the agency’s abolition.

“The video is horrific and it is chilling,” Ferguson said at a news conference on Saturday with labor leaders and local elected officials.

Wilson called ICE’s tactics “unacceptable and un-American”.

In a statement to the Guardian, ICE said its officers detained Mendoza, an immigrant from Mexico, who it said had overstayed his visa “in violation of our nation’s laws”.

According to the ICE statement, Mejia “hurled hot coffee on the officers”. ICE said she was in the US illegally from Mexico and had overstayed her visa. She could be charged with assaulting a federal officer.

The father and daughter were travelling to attend a baptism and were seeking asylum in the US, where they had lived since 2015, their lawyer, Olia Catala, told the Associated Press. They had no criminal record, Catala said.

Mejia, who is in her mid-20s, had married a US citizen and filed paperwork for adjustment of her status, the attorney said. Mejia’s husband – who was not detained -was accompanying Mejia and her father when they were detained on Saturday, Catala added.

“They’ve done everything correctly, and these are educated people,” Catala said of the father and daughter. “She went to college, to university, she worked for a nonprofit. No criminal history [for] any of them. So it’s just heartbreaking.”

![A man argues with a police officer during a protest at the Seattle-Tacoma international airport](https://i.guim.co.uk/img/media/35d066e1665ecbb70be1b4436e8fe1a09bd10249/0_0_5400_3600/master/5400.jpg?width=445&dpr=1&s=none&crop=none)

Both were being held at the Northwest ICE Processing Center in Tacoma, Catala said.

She said Mejia was carrying a cup of coffee when the confrontation occurred. “When somebody’s charging at you, you’re going to drop a cup of coffee,” Catala said.

“It was just a lot of commotion, and honestly, if somebody is running at you or charging at you and they’re not wearing a uniform, you don’t know who they are,” Catala said. “There was a lot of panic, chaos, not knowing what’s going on.”

Alaska Airlines said ICE officers gave no advance notice of the enforcement action, which occurred during a busy travel weekend ahead of the Thanksgiving holiday. The airline said it was working with the Port of Seattle, which manages the airport, to see if protocols were followed, and had raised concerns “directly with the appropriate authorities”.

The Port of Seattle said in a statement that the arrest was distressing not only for the family but for other passengers, and that local authorities would “engage federal officials to understand more about what occurred”.
