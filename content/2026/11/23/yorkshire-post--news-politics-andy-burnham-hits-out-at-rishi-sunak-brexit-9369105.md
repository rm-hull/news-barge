# ‘Stuck in the past’: Andy Burnham hits out at Rishi Sunak over ‘bad Brexit deal’

## Andy Burnham slammed Rishi Sunak and other Conservative prime ministers who warned him not to get involved in a “divisive” Brexit debate.

Andy Burnham will start setting out the options for the UK’s long-term relationship with the European Union next month, but maintained the “British people will be the boss”.

He said it would be “unthinkable” for Britain to rejoin the EU without a referendum.

Mr Burnham announced a fresh timeline on changes to the Brexit deal following agreements signed at last week’s summit in Brussels.

He confirmed that negotiations on tuition fees for European students - which have a six-month cut off - are separate from the other agreements.

Speaking in the House of Commons this afternoon, the Prime Minister revealed that he would start setting out plans for a closer relationship next month.

He said: “As part of the 10-year plan process, it is right we consider the range of options for our long-term relationship with our largest market.

“So, we will say more next month when we publish the plan for Britain about how we move forward.

“But the initial agreement we have reached with the (European) Commission is that it will be an informal exchange of ideas, with the results to be presented around the time of the next UK-EU summit.”

He added: “Always, the British people will be the boss and they will decide what comes next.”

Mr Burnham said that while he would honour the Brexit red lines in the 2024 manifesto, which include not rejoining the bloc, he would look to put forward options for the next election.

Responding to questions from MPs, he said it would be “unthinkable” for the UK to return to the EU without a referendum.

The Prime Minister’s official spokesman explained: “Now the summit is done we will begin discussions with our European partners about the realistic options for our longer-term relationship.

“We will present those options and our assessment of them to inform decisions in the next Parliament.

“The Prime Minister will say more alongside the plan for Britain next month - that will be the start of that process not the end of it.”

Mr Burnham hit out at former Tory prime ministers Rishi Sunak and Theresa May who urged him not to reignite the European debate.

Mr Sunak said Brexit could become an “all-consuming” distraction for the Government.

The Richmond and Northallerton MP told Sky News: “I certainly wouldn't reopen up this whole debate about rejoining the EU.

“Obviously, it's divisive. But we saw last time around, it was also all consuming. So it ends up being a huge distraction for government.”

His intervention came after Baroness May of Maidenhead urged Mr Burnham to “accept Brexit and move on”, and followed similar warnings from fellow ex-Tory leaders Boris Johnson and Lord David Cameron.

But the Labour leader said: “I’m not sure they’re the people I’ll be listening to here because they left Britain with a bad Brexit deal.

“They’re stuck in the past because they can’t face up to the bad deal that they did.

“I’m not a prime minister that’s going to be stuck in the past. I want to lead Britain forward to a more hopeful future, get the economy growing again, get cost of living down for people, lower energy bills.

“All of these things can be achieved by a more positive, constructive relationship with the European Union.”
