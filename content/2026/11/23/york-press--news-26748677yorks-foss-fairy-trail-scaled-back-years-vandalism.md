## Foss Fairy Trail founder steps back after more than 100 incidents of vandalism

Tracy Ostle at the Foss Fairy Trail *(Image: Dylan Connell)*

A much-loved York attraction that brought families together during lockdown is set to be scaled back after its founder said it has become "impossible to manage".

The Foss Fairy Trail, located beside the River Foss just beyond the bridge at Heworth Green, was created by Tracy Ostle during the Covid-19 lockdown.

Along the riverside path, visitors can discover fairy houses, tiny doors and other magical mementos – many of them handmade by Tracy from recycled materials and wood found along the riverbank.




Over the years, families have also added their own creations to the trail.

![Many of the fair houses were handmade by Tracy](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21744290.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Many of the fair houses were handmade by Tracy *(Image: Foss Fairy Trail)*

Tracy said: “In lockdown it was wonderful for families.

“We had lots of volunteers and it flourished.

“People would leave little houses and treasures for others to find, and it was always a lovely surprise.”

But Tracy admitted, in the last five years, volunteer numbers have gradually dwindled, with the site’s upkeep falling largely on Tracy.

Ongoing problems with vandalism and anti-social behaviour have also made it increasingly difficult to maintain the trail.

![The trail's beloved bug house was torched by vandals - one of 100s of reported incidents at the trail since it was established in lockdown](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/18388068.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The trail's beloved bug house was torched by vandals - one of 100s of reported incidents at the trail since it was established in lockdown *(Image: Foss Fairy Trail)*

Tracy said the area had suffered more than 100 incidents of vandalism since it was established.

In July, a bug hotel built with help from pupils at Haxby Road Academy had been badly damaged, just months after being repaired following an alleged arson attack.

Fairy houses were reported to have been set alight and thrown into the River Foss, causing £500 worth of damage.

At the time, Tracy said that this has left the beloved trail’s future in doubt.

![A mural will remain signposting families to the trail, which will remain in place, Tracy said](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21744292.jpg?type=mds-article-620)A mural will remain signposting families to the trail, which will remain in place, Tracy said *(Image: Foss Fairy Trail)*

North Yorkshire Police launched an appeal for information and said the incident had been “deeply disappointing”.

Despite the challenges, Tracy says the trail's greatest success has been the impact it has had on local families and children.

Primary school pupils have visited the site, and students have used the area for photography projects, with many residents adding their own fairy-themed creations to the site.

![The houses and tiny doors decorating the Foss riverside have delighted many of the area's children over the years](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21744291.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The houses and tiny doors decorating the Foss riverside have delighted many of the area's children over the years *(Image: Foss Fairy Trail)*

Tracy said: “Kids will remember it, like I remember Bluebell Wood as a child.

"The fairy houses are still there, and families can still enjoy them for as long as they last, but it's been impossible for me to keep on managing it alone.

Tracy hopes the area will be redeveloped by another community project in the future.

She has joined the River Foss Society and hopes any remaining funding from the trail can be ringfenced for projects in the Heworth Green area, such as a new community mural, planned for Huntington Road beside the cycle track in summer 2027.
