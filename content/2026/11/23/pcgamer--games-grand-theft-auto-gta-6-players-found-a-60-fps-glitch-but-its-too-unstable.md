Clever Grand Theft Auto 6 players have found a glitch with the Xbox version of the game that allows players to forcibly remove Rockstar's 30 fps cap. But even though people claim the game jumps up to 60 fps, the actual performance is nowhere near as smooth, according to Digital Foundry's tests.

The glitch is surprisingly simple to perform: You just need to set the console's output to 60 Hz, load GTA 6, swap the output to 120 Hz, load a different game, and then use the Quick Resume feature to go back to GTA 6. For whatever reason, this forces the game to run above 30 fps, but not by much.

In Digital Foundry's testing, GTA 6 doesn't suddenly become a buttery smooth 60 fps dream. Instead, it has an average frame rate of about 36 fps and still drops below that number during particularly intense scenes, like in city areas or when multiple cars are exploding due to rocket launcher hijinks.

The most notable improvement is lower input lag when it manages to climb up to around 40 fps, but even that has an asterisk because the frame rate varies too much for the benefits to be consistent. Unless you plan on spending your time in Leonida roleplaying as a fisherman, you're going to have a bad time.

While Rockstar technology production director Marc Chambers said an official 60 fps mode for GTA 6 is too much for the current generation of consoles to handle, including the PS5 Pro, Digital Foundry theorizes that a 40 fps mode might be possible in the future.

![Grand Theft Auto 6 - Xbox Series X|S Unlocked FPS Glitch Tested - How Fast Can It Really Run? - YouTube](https://img.youtube.com/vi/VzKA9ayxZMY/maxresdefault.jpg)

I'm hopeful for console players, but none of this has stopped me from wanting the game on the only platform where 60 fps won't be much of a challenge to hit: PC. Tweaking settings you don't care as much about to juice the frame rate is much preferable over having to deal with a set "performance" mode or a glitch that could be patched out any day. A good PC version should be able to nicely scale up or down in performance based on your hardware setup, and Rockstar has shown that it can pull that off in the past with games like Red Dead Redemption 2.

For a game as good as GTA 6—Chris says it's "(mostly) great" in his 100-hour impressions— you shouldn't have to deal with quirks that tarnish the experience. I'm not a Rockstar employee, I don't know what challenges a PC version introduces or why it wasn't prioritized for a simultaneous PC and console release, but I do know that a PC version likely won't have as many caveats when it comes to performance as it does on consoles. So, I'll deal with Rockstar's intended 30 fps cap until the moment I can boot up GTA 6 on my PC, and then forget that I ever had to endure the limits of the current console experience.
