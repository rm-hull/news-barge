## German Chancellor Friedrich Merz told reporters the Russian invasion of Ukraine would likely not have happened if allies had agreed to admit Kyiv in 2008 during a summit in Bucharest.

German Chancellor Friedrich Merz described the decision not to admit Ukraine into NATO in 2008, following a proposal by then-US President George W. Bush, as a “mistake.”

Speaking alongside NATO Secretary General Mark Rutte at a press conference in Berlin, Friedrich Merz told reporters: "I think we all see today, at least from the point of view of what a mistake it was, not to follow the American proposal in 2008 at the NATO summit in Bucharest."

He added that the full-scale invasion of Ukraine would have been 'prevented' had the alliance opened up membership back then.

"If Ukraine had become a member of NATO, this war would have been likely to be prevented," said Merz, in comments that were translated from German.

The meeting came amid ongoing US-led negotiations to end the war in Ukraine, with Rutte visiting the German capital for the NATO Parliamentary Assembly's annual meeting.

Rutte struck a cautious tone on the prospect of reviving discussions about Ukraine's NATO membership, stressing that successful peace negotiations would need to come first.

The remarks came in response to recent comments by French President Emmanuel Macron, who called for the debate over Ukraine's membership of the alliance to be reopened.

Kyiv's potential NATO membership has resurfaced as part of a potential final resolution to ending the war.

Russian President Vladimir Putin has reportedly indicated he would be willing to end the war in Ukraine, provided NATO agrees to return to its 1997 posture. This would effectively mean reversing the alliance's subsequent expansion by withdrawing membership from countries that joined after the collapse of the Soviet Union.

However, NATO membership for Ukraine remains a distant prospect, according to Rutte. He noted that while "it's a long-standing position that Ukraine should be a part of NATO," there is no unanimous agreement among alliance members on its accession.

Rutte also rebuffed the notion that the Kremlin could have any say in the current or future makeup of the NATO alliance. "What cannot happen is we are forced to roll back NATO to the 1997 borders," he said.

"It's only the 32 allies deciding who is in NATO and who is not in NATO".

Rutte was also addressing ongoing tensions in Europe over increasing Russian hybrid attacks, including last week's incursion into Poland, when two Russian cruise missiles and a drone landed on Polish territory, according to Polish officials.

He attributed Russia's increased aggression to mounting battlefield casualties, claiming that Russian forces are losing up to 50,000 soldiers each month in Ukraine. He contrasted this figure with Soviet losses in Afghanistan and US casualties during the Vietnam War.

"Ukraine is rolling back the frontline in the direction of Russia and striking deep" into Russian territory, Rutte told journalists.

Rutte reiterated NATO's position that the best solution to growing hybrid attacks is doubling down on support for Ukraine.

"Most importantly, we need to keep our military support for Ukraine going," said Rutte.

"For every incident in Europe - more support for Ukraine," he added.

Meanwhile, Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said Russian President Vladimir Putin had ordered the strikes following what he described as “weak meetings” with US President Donald Trump and Chinese President Xi Jinping.

“Russia is trying to compensate for its military and diplomatic impotence with precisely this kind of terror," Zelenskyy wrote in a post on "X" last Friday.

Separately, NATO ambassadors met in Brussels Monday to discuss the Russian incursion into Poland. The alliance is now considering deploying more assets to the region in response.

NATO's chief military officer, the Supreme Allied Commander (SACEUR) is now "examining the deployment of additional assets as he continues to assess our deterrence and defence posture in Poland and elsewhere," a NATO official confirmed to Euronews.
