When the Berlin Greens gather for their party conference in January, they will not only vote on the coalition agreement and nominate their team for the Berlin Senate – they will also elect a new party co-chair.

Nina Stahr, who represents the party’s pragmatic Realos wing on the state executive committee, will remain in office. But her current co-chair, Philmon Ghirmai, who belongs to the party’s left wing, must step down. Having recently taken his seat in the Berlin House of Representatives, Ghirmai must vacate his position on the executive committee because the Greens keep party leadership positions separate from parliamentary mandates.

The Greens have distinct internal factions, with the left wing and the Realos vying to shape the party’s political direction. Accordingly, the party’s dual leadership is also structured to maintain a balance between the two wings. Since Ghirmai, the departing co-chair, belongs to the left, his successor is also being sought from that wing. According to information obtained by the Berliner Morgenpost, the surprise frontrunner is a local politician from Reinickendorf who has so far remained largely unknown: Merieme Benali-Jockers.

### Berlin Greens’ Likely Next Co-Chair Comes from Reinickendorf

At an internal meeting of the party’s left wing last Friday, Benali-Jockers prevailed over Emily Büning, the Greens’ long-serving national executive director. This was confirmed to the Morgenpost by several sources within the party. Benali-Jockers is 37 years old and a spokesperson for the Greens’ district executive committee in Reinickendorf. Her political focus is on health and social policy. A fellow party member describes her as a “politician with an intrinsic commitment to social justice”.

“I don’t want to be the candidate of the left wing; I want to represent the entire party,” Benali-Jockers told this newspaper on Tuesday. She brings a perspective that is sometimes still underrepresented within the Greens: “My lived reality is that I face multiple forms of marginalisation,” said the politician from Reinickendorf, who grew up in Märkisches Viertel.

Her understanding of poverty and discrimination comes not only from politics but also from personal experience. “I grew up poor, have a migrant background, am a woman and am perceived as Muslim.” She says this perspective is common across the city but underrepresented in politics. “I want to represent people who are often overlooked in everyday life.”

### Benali-Jockers Focuses on Social and Health Policy

Benali-Jockers was born in Morocco. The granddaughter of a guest worker, she arrived in Reinickendorf at the age of three months. After leaving school, she initially trained as a physiotherapist. She then studied healthcare and nursing management. She works as a health protection coordinator for a federally owned company and is currently representing the Greens in coalition talks with the Left Party and Social Democrats on “Health and Care”.

“I want to ensure that everyone has access to good healthcare and that social inequalities are reduced,” she writes on her district association’s website. “Beyond that, promoting diversity in our society and ensuring that everyone in Reinickendorf feels welcome and valued are particularly important to me.”

As a woman with a migrant background, she knows from personal experience how important an open and inclusive society is. As the mother of a young boy, she also feels a strong personal commitment to leaving future generations a liveable environment. If elected, Benali-Jockers would take over the leadership of a party that could enter Berlin’s government but has itself suffered a disappointing election result.

### “We Need to Communicate More Clearly”

“14.3 per cent is a disappointment,” Benali-Jockers admits. “I see it as constructive criticism.” She is convinced that the Greens had the best election manifesto, but acknowledges that “we need to communicate more clearly”. The party had failed to get its own messages across clearly enough, according to Benali-Jockers.

In particular, she believes potential voters need to be reached more effectively through social media channels, with communication tailored more closely to specific target groups. If she becomes the new party co-chair, one of her first and most important tasks at the beginning of next year will be to draw lessons from the poor election result.

Between now and the party conference in January, Benali-Jockers intends to introduce herself to all district associations, internal party working groups and the Green Youth. Other candidates may enter the race before the conference and challenge Benali-Jockers for the vacant position on the state executive committee. However, with the backing of the party’s left wing, the previously little-known politician from Reinickendorf is the clear favourite to succeed Ghirmai.
