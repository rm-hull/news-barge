A pupil at a York school has opened its new library – based on his design.

The grand opening of Woodthorpe Primary School was marked by a ribbon-cutting ceremony led by Archie, the Woodthorpe pupil whose design won a national competition run by York modular building company Portakabin.

Archie’s design was chosen from 1,901 entries to the Inspiring Libraries competition, which invited pupils and teachers across the UK to imagine their dream reading space.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/woodthorpe-primary-school-york-library-opening-3.webp)

His “Eco Library” featured solar panels, a living wall, grass-effect flooring, cloud-shaped lights and softly shaped, natural bookshelves.

Judges praised its architectural thinking and its aim of bringing the outdoors in. After a few meetings with Archie, Portakabin went on to build a fully equipped library for the school, using his design as inspiration.

Archie said: “I was so excited when I found out I’d won. I really didn’t think I would. I’ve loved seeing my design being built, and it’s amazing to see my idea brought to life.”

The opening ceremony was attended by Woodthorpe staff and students, representatives from Portakabin and staff from Explore Libraries.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/woodthorpe-primary-school-york-library-opening-2.webp)

Once the ribbon had been cut, children were treated to a special reading by local author Catherine Jacob, whose picture book *Betsy Buglove Saves the Bees* follows a bug-loving girl determined to protect the insects in her garden.

Regional manager at Portakabin Wendy Jones said: “We ran this competition to inspire a love of reading among children and encourage them to spend more time enjoying books.

“We wanted to create a space that would spark their imagination, make reading an exciting experience and help foster a lifelong love of books.”

Helen Elsworth, vice principal of Woodthorpe Primary School said: “The opening of our new library is a significant milestone for our school, providing pupils with a calm, welcoming environment where they can develop a lifelong love of reading, nurture their curiosity and reach their full potential.

“We are excited to see the lasting impact this inspiring space will have on their learning and wellbeing for many years to come.”

The competition was launched to support the government’s pledge that every primary school should have access to a bespoke library space. Winners were announced in February 2026 during the National Year of Reading.

Portakabin noted when announcing the winners that around 1,700 primary schools in the UK still have no library, with the gap felt most keenly in disadvantaged areas.

For Woodthorpe, the new building means every child now has a dedicated place to find a book and a quiet corner in which to enjoy it.

The library is now open to pupils, giving them a new home for reading, learning and developing their imagination.
