Meteorologists are keeping a close eye on a storm that could ground balloons at the 100th Macy’s Thanksgiving Day Parade.

The parade is set to feature a record-breaking 100 inflatables from Bluey and Mickey Mouse to Hello Kitty. It kicks off at 8:30 a.m. on Thanksgiving in all time zones.

A storm system is taking aim at Manhattan and New York City officials could be forced to ground the parade’s giant balloons if wind gusts top 34 mph, or sustained winds exceed 23 mph.

The restrictions were put in place after strong gusts pushed the Cat in the Hat balloon into a light pole during the 1997 parade, causing debris to fall and injure a spectator.

![The SpongeBob SquarePants balloon during the 2024 Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/23/19/32/GettyImages-2187307614.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Macy’s Thanksgiving Day Parade made its debut in 1924 and balloons have only been grounded one time during its illustrious history, in 1971 because of high winds.

## ‘Need the storm to slow down’

Meteorologist Matt Makens, a broadcast veteran who shares forecasts on YouTube, has been keeping a close eye on the “dynamic” system and said the forecast will definitely change between now and Thanksgiving.

“We need the storm to slow down,” Makens said. “The timing of the system may spread very heavy rain and very gusty wind to the city during the parade, however, most recent data indicate a slow-down to that arrival which would put the biggest impact through the city after the parade is over.”

“Right now, data show the potential for 50 mph gusts, but again, if that storm slows down, that’ll put the peak gusts after the parade,” he added.

“Even if the storm slows and winds aren’t as strong, we will need to track this very carefully to ensure certain building-wind orientations don’t create a ‘wind tunnel’ effect and cause balloon issues at certain intersections.”

![This year’s balloons seen at the 100th Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade - Balloonfest at MetLife Stadium on Nov. 7, 2026](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/23/19/17/GettyImages-2304297666.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Jason Moreland, head of meteorology at AlertMedia, echoed Makens’ point about the uncertainty surrounding the forecast at this point in the week.

“Timing is now the main uncertainty. The storm is three days away, and the models agree it will come,” he said.

“A faster storm brings the afternoon’s stronger wind and steadier rain into the parade hours; a slower one keeps gusts in the mid-30s and holds the rain off until late in the parade.”

He warned: “A dry Thanksgiving Day is unlikely either way.”

## Parade attendees urged to prepare

Spectators are urged to be prepared for bad weather in case heavy rain and strong winds strike during the parade.

Moreland said that if unfavorable weather conditions develop on Thanksgiving morning, parade attendees are in for a chilly and “raw” morning with temperatures of 46 -49F that will feel like 38-42F in the wind.

That means anyone watching in person will want to consider rain gear and plenty of layers.

![Spider-Man by Marvel in the 99th Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade on November 27, 2025 in New York City](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/23/19/48/GettyImages-2248805018-(1).jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“Chilly rain with very strong wind won’t be pleasant unless people are dressed appropriately with lots of layers and can stay dry,” Makens added.

“This assumes the brunt of the storm hits during the parade, but recent data shows the storm slowing down, which will help spectators. For organizers, protecting the floats and members should be top of mind with preparation for cold, wet weather and strong wind.”

Those watching at home can expect to see new balloons and floats in addition to iconic characters from past parades. A tribute to Dolly Parton led by Megan Hilty is planned, along with special appearances by Melissa Joan Hart and others.

NBC and Peacock will broadcast the event with a Spanish-language simulcast on Telemundo.
