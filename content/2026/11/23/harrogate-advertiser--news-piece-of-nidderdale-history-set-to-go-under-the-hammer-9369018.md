A historic building in the heart of Nidderdale, once used by local brass bands for rehearsals and performances, is set to go under the hammer with a guide price of £20,000.

The stone-built property in Lofthouse, known as The Bandroom Gallery, has been part of village life for generations.

Originally built as a mission room for St Chad’s Church in nearby Middlesmoor, it became a home for local brass bands from the 1950s, providing a space for musicians to practise and perform.

In more recent years, it has been used as a music and community venue, as well as an exhibition space during the NiddArt Trail.

Now the building is being offered for sale through Dacre, Son & Hartley’s online auction service, with a guide price of £20,000.

The property comprises one main room, measuring approximately 6.7m by 4.5m, and a separate storage room.

It is within the Lofthouse Conservation Area, and any change of use would be subject to planning permission.

Alison Reilly, sales manager at Dacre, Son & Hartley’s Pateley Bridge office, said: “This is a distinctive building that’s full of character, at the heart of a beautiful village, within the Lofthouse Conservation Area, and surrounded by Upper Nidderdale’s dramatic countryside.

“The building is steeped in history and deeply rooted in the community.

“Most recently it has been used as a music, arts and exhibition space, and a buyer could continue building on this established identity, or seek an alternative use, subject to securing the relevant planning consents. All this means it now offers an exciting opportunity for a new owner to create its next chapter.”
