A restaurateur and his sister have been cleared of murdering their 100-year-old mother by administering morphine to “speed up” her death.

But Browns Brasseries founder Jeremy Mogford, 79, and Sarah Pickering, 72, remain on trial charged with the attempted murder of Pamela Mogford, who died in Bledlow Ridge, Buckinghamshire on 15 February, 2022 after she was given a dose of oral morphine solution by her children.

Mr Justice Jay directed the jury to return a not guilty verdict for both defendants on the charges of murder at Oxford Crown Court on Monday.

Closing the Crown’s case, prosecution barrister Julian Christopher KC said it would no longer seek a conviction on the murder charges.

Judge Jay told jurors: “You have heard what the prosecution said.

“It’s up to you to work it out, but it follows that on my direction the only proper verdict you can reach on count one is not guilty.”

![Mogford and Pickering (pictured) accept giving their mother morphine after the GP had left, but deny intending to quicken her death, rather maintaining they did so because she was in pain](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/23/14/01M84A3CZ2S4AJ09Z5K1DRRHQ0.jpg?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&trim=1%2C0%2C1%2C0&auto=webp)

The defence case is due to begin on Tuesday and both defendants are expected to give evidence in their trial.

The court previously heard experts cannot conclude that the level of morphine given to Mrs Mogford was enough to kill her.

It was said the defendants gave their mother the equivalent of “at least four times” as much morphine as her GP had given her the evening before she died in an effort to “speed up” her death.

The court previously heard that the GP, Dr Thomas Neale, had told Mrs Mogford’s children not to give her any further pain-killing medication.

Dr Neale said he left three further ampules of morphine in Mrs Mogford’s home in case of need, which could be administered by a medic called to her home.

Mogford and Pickering accept giving their mother morphine after the GP had left, but deny intending to quicken her death, rather maintaining they did so because she was in pain.

![The court previously heard the GP, Dr Thomas Neale, had told Mrs Mogford’s children not to give her any further pain-killing medication](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/23/14/01M84A3AFD7JF1F4QQFA1Q7070.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The defendants were arrested after texts were discovered from Pickering on the evening of her mother’s death which included “I am actually killing her” and “I shall wait until Foyle is over then I can go to my grave knowing I have topped someone. It’s certainly on my bucket list”.

In a statement to police on 16 October 2023, Mogford said he “emphatically refuted” the criminal allegations and found them “very hurtful”.

His barrister Trevor Burke KC told the court he and Pickering had “acted out of love” when they administered the morphine.

The trial continues.
