Compass, one of the biggest companies in the FTSE 100, is planning to switch its primary listing from London to the US in the latest blow to the struggling UK stock market.

The £43.6bn ($57bn) catering giant, which has been listed in London since 1988, could announce its intention to move its listing to New York as soon as Tuesday when it reports its annual results.

Compass is a big government contractor, providing meals in more than 2,000 schools, academies, colleges and universities across the UK and in some NHS hospitals, as well as offices in the private sector.

It also provides grab-and-go foods for fast-food chains and cafes such as Starbucks, Costa and Taco Bell. Last year, the company made about three-quarters of its $3bn profit in North America.

Compass, which switched its reporting currency from British sterling to US dollars in October 2023, also changed the trading currency of its London-listed shares to US dollars in April this year. Its planned move to the US was first reported by the Financial Times.

The company, which is headquartered in Surrey, England, and has more than 600,000 staff around the world, will consult with shareholders over the proposed move which would see it retain a secondary listing in London.

Its potential departure would be the latest in a string of exits by companies seeking deeper pools of capital and higher valuations in the US.

In recent years, the equipment rental company Ashtead, the building material supplier CRH, and the owner of Paddy Power, Flutter Entertainment, have all moved their primary listings to the US.

The UK’s biggest drugmaker, AstraZeneca, listed its shares directly on the New York Stock Exchange this year, putting its US shares on an even footing with its London-listed stock.

A string of companies have also left London’s stock market after agreeing to takeover deals. The flurry of deal-making this year includes warehouse landlord Segro’s £14bn takeover by its US rival Prologis, easyJet’s £5.7bn takeover by the US private equity company Apollo and the £9.9bn takeover of asset manager Schroders by a US investor.

Other deals include the £10bn takeover of the lab testing group Intertek by a consortium led by the Swedish private equity company EQT in June. Beazley, the specialist insurer with clients including Lloyds of London, agreed an £8bn deal to be acquired by its larger rival Zurich in February.

Meanwhile, fewer companies are joining London’s stock market, as the UK faces a dearth of flotations. There were just seven listings in the first half of this year, raising £577m in total, according to data from the professional services company EY.

Airtel Money, the mobile money arm of Airtel Africa, has been one of the few companies to join London’s market this year. The payments processor listed at £1.96 a share in October and traded at about £2.05 on Monday.

Compass was approached for comment.
