A 29-year-old man has been charged with murder in the fatal shooting of a Dallas mother who was killed after a highway collision on her 35th birthday, police said on Sunday.

The victim, Stephanie Gonzalez, had just finished a shift as a supervisor at Quality Sausage Company in West Dallas and was about 10 minutes from home on the evening of November 15 when she was involved in a crash on eastbound C.F. Hawn Freeway, according to the Dallas Police Department and relatives. She was shot at the scene around 9:15 p.m. and died after being taken to a hospital, police said.

The suspect, Juan Gonzalez-Orduna, was booked on Sunday into the Lew Sterrett Justice Center on a murder charge, according to police. Jail records show that Gonzalez-Orduna is also being held on an unrelated immigration detainer. It was not immediately clear whether he had retained a lawyer.

Before Gonzalez-Orduna’s arrest, relatives said they suspected Gonzalez had been targeted because she was killed on her birthday while driving home to her children.

“She was on her way home to see her babies,” her cousin, Vivian Britton, told NBC DFW. “We feel horrible for her children, and we just want to know what happened.”

![Stephanie Gonzalez, 35, was fatally shot on a southeast Dallas freeway on November 15 while driving home from work to her husband and four young children](https://static.the-independent.com/2026/11/23/18/44/Dallas-mom.png?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&auto=webp)

Another friend, who gave only her first name, Emily, told CBS News Texas that she was heartbroken and worried about Gonzalez’s young children.

“I’m confused, I’m hurt, and I’m worried about her — her babies at home,” she said. “I know how much she loved them.”

In an online obituary, relatives remembered Gonzalez as a devoted mother who loved music and caring for animals on her farm, calling her “a loving daughter, wife, mother, sister, aunt, and friend whose warm spirit touched the hearts of everyone who knew her.”

Gonzalez leaves behind her husband, Luis Enriquez Gonzalez, and four children, including an infant less than a year old.

“Whether she was spending time with family, enjoying music, or tending to her farm, she embraced life with warmth and appreciation,” the obituary read. She is also survived by her mother, a brother, a sister and a nephew.

A cousin, Julissa Clemente, organized an online fundraising campaign to help cover funeral and household expenses.

“Our family is heartbroken by this loss, and it is hard to put into words how deeply Stephanie will be missed,” Clemente wrote on the GoFundMe page, adding that Gonzalez’s husband and children “are now facing an unimaginable loss without her.”

Authorities have not disclosed a possible motive or detailed the events leading up to the crash and shooting, and the investigation remains open.

Dallas police are asking anyone with information about the shooting to contact Detective R. Kramer at 214-671-3608.
