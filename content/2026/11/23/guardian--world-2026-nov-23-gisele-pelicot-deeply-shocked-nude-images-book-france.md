Gisèle Pelicot has said she is “deeply shocked” that nude photographs of her had been used without her consent to promote a psychoanalyst’s book that suggests she was complicit in her abuse.

Pelicot, 73, became a global icon in the fight against sexual violence in 2024 during the trial that saw her former husband convicted of drugging and raping her, as well as recruiting 49 other men to rape her while unconscious.

“I am deeply shocked that images from the most intimate part of my private life are being displayed and exploited for commercial purposes,” Pelicot told broadcaster BFM TV. “I refuse to let my privacy become an advertising tool.”

She said she would pursue legal action against Tinbad publishing house and the author, Sabine Prokhoris, who has described her book as “a critical examination of the notion of psychological control and domination”. One chapter concerns the Pelicot case.

Tinbad sent some media nude photographs and graphic videos of Pelicot, which her husband had taken, to promote the book, according to French weekly le Canard enchaîné.

“Today I find myself once again humiliated, exposed and hurt,” Pelicot told the broadcaster. “As if the ordeal of the trial had not been enough.”

Tinbad has apologised, describing the episode as an “unfortunate communication error”.

The publisher maintained that it had not sent “any images of rape or any pornographic videos” to the press, while not denying that nude photographs had been distributed.

Prokhoris said she placed “full responsibility” for the “inappropriate” promotional campaign on her publisher.

According to her book’s back-cover blurb, it calls for readers to “think freely” about the notion of coercive control, described as “a cornerstone of #MeToo orthodoxy”.

Tinbad said that no further print runs of the book would be produced beyond the 280 copies already in circulation.
