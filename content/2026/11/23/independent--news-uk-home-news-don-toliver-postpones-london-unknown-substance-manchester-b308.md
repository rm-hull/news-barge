Rapper Don Toliver’s show at the O2 in London on Monday will be rescheduled following an incident with an “unknown substance” at his Co-op Live Arena concert in Manchester.

The Manchester venue was evacuated and a major incident declared on Sunday night, with concertgoers describing a “horrible smell”, which police initially believed to be tear gas.

According to Greater Manchester Police, 89 people displayed symptoms believed to be linked to the exposure.

Monday’s show at the London venue has been postponed following production delays as a result, promoters Live Nation said.

A representative for the promoters said in a statement: “Due to production delays following last night’s show at Co-op Live in Manchester, tonight’s Don Toliver concert at The O2 is rescheduled to December 15. All tickets for tonight’s show at The O2 will be valid for December 15.

“We are sorry to everyone affected by last night’s events in Manchester and full refunds for that show will be automatically processed to ticket holders.”

Co-op Live said in a statement that its bosses were working with police to identify the cause of Sunday’s incident.

![A police cordon and forensic investigations at the Co-op Live](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/23/10/2306466590..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“Following an incident in the crowd at last night’s show, we are working closely with Greater Manchester Police to identify the cause and action appropriately,” the statement said.

“Our priority remains the safety and security of all visitors to Co-op Live, and we will provide more information once we have an update from GMP.”

Attendees were safely evacuated, Greater Manchester Police said, and major incident status was lifted at 4am on Monday.

One woman was taken to Manchester Royal Infirmary for further assessment and treatment.

The remaining 88 people underwent dry decontamination at the scene and were subsequently accommodated within the Etihad Stadium while the incident was managed.

Enquiries to determine what the substance was are ongoing, the force added.

No one has been arrested in relation to this incident.

Assistant Chief Constable Tom Harding said: “Enquiries remain ongoing to establish the nature of the substance, where it came from and any associated risk. Testing will take place throughout the day as the investigation into the cause of this incident progresses.

“We are trying to establish whether any criminality has occurred in relation to this incident.

“Anyone at last night's event who has since noticed side-effects or become ill should attend their local hospital, contact their GP or call 101.

“I would like to thank the public who attended for their patience whilst we responded to this incident. There were over 12,000 people in attendance during this concert who were safely escorted from the arena and made their way home.

“Our coordinated response meant colleagues from the emergency services and partner agencies could tackle this incident swiftly and safely.”
