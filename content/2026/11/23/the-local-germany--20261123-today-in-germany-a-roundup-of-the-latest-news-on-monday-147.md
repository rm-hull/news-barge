**Monday's top story:Opposition attacks Reiche's fuel price cap over motorway exemption**

Opposition politicians have sharply criticised Economy Minister Katherina Reiche's new draft law on a fuel price cap, which would exempt motorway petrol stations.

"It would be hard to design a more absurd fuel price cap: of all places, it is supposed to be exempt precisely where filling up is particularly expensive," Green Party leader Felix Banaszak told the Kölner Stadt-Anzeiger.

According to Handelsblatt, the draft went to an internal government vote on Friday evening. Banaszak called instead for a windfall profit tax on crisis profits, with the money returned directly to the public.

Janine Wissler, deputy leader of the Left Party's parliamentary group, said the cap had been designed to be as ineffective as possible and accused Reiche of representing corporations rather than drivers.

The CDU/SPD coalition agreed a price cap in September, but Reiche's first proposal drew heavy criticism, particularly from within the SPD. It would only have applied if German fuel prices rose five percentage points more than the EU average for three consecutive weeks.

That has never happened, even since the Iran war pushed prices up. A cap would not have been possible before mid-January, and the law would have expired after three months.

**Germanwatch says no COP31 deal better than a bad one**

Environmental group Germanwatch has sharply criticised the UN climate conference in Antalya, Turkey (COP31), after talks ended on Sunday without an agreed final declaration.

"At this COP, no final declaration was better than a bad one," said Jennifer Tollmann, the group's director of policy. She warned that a text shaped mainly by the wishes of the fossil fuel lobby "could have set international climate diplomacy back by years".

Germanwatch said the summit had been marked by a "massively obstructive fossil fuel lobby centred around the Arab states and Russia". It added that the problem was made worse by a weak joint presidency, held by Australia and Turkey, which often managed to agree only on the lowest common denominator.

Despite lengthy negotiations, delegates failed to agree on a joint resolution. The EU, Panama and other countries rejected the draft because it contained no explicit commitment to phasing out fossil fuels. As a result, oil-producing nations such as Saudi Arabia prevailed in the talks.

Germanwatch is calling for faster emissions cuts and genuinely adequate support for poorer countries to adapt to climate change and to deal with loss and damage. "We must start down this path right now," said Laura Schäfer, head of its international climate policy division, who also expects Germany and the EU to take a leading role.

**Germany fourth in world for non-native English skills**

Germany has ranked fourth in the world for English proficiency among non-native speakers, according to the EF English Proficiency Index 2026.

Sweden topped the table with 615 points, followed by Austria (613) and Denmark (612). Germany scored 611, four points fewer than in 2025. The index draws on English test results from 2.3 million adults across 117 countries and regions. A score of 600 to 700 points equates to C1 level.

Germans did best in reading (640 points), then listening (611), writing (581) and speaking (549). Men and women performed similarly, but test takers aged 21 to 30 scored highest.

Bavaria, North Rhine-Westphalia and Baden-Württemberg had the strongest results among the federal states. Karlsruhe came top among cities, followed by Aachen and Heidelberg, which may reflect their large student populations. Students scored 651 points, second only to strategy and project managers on 684.

Berlin fared comparatively badly. Twenty German cities, including Cologne, Stuttgart, Munich and Frankfurt, beat the capital's 619 points, which is only just C1 level.

**Berlin CDU wants constitution amended to protect Jewish life**

The Berlin CDU has asked the state parliament to add an article to the Berlin constitution protecting Jewish life. The motion could be considered at Thursday's plenary session.

The proposed Article 29a would read: "The state combats antisemitism and protects and promotes Jewish life and Jewish culture."

The Left Party, Greens and SPD are in coalition talks to form a new state government. They have already agreed a joint statement aiming to amend the constitution, following Brandenburg's example, to cover antisemitism, antiziganism, racism and other group-based hostility.

The SPD's new parliamentary secretaries, Maja Lasic and Marcel Hopp, told the *Berliner Morgenpost* they want "broad, cross-party consensus". They said the Brandenburg-style text could serve as a basis for agreement among all democratic groups.

The three parties hold a majority since the September 20th election, but not the two-thirds needed to change the constitution. Working with the CDU looks obvious, but a quick deal does not.

The Left Party had invited the other group leaders to a meeting last Friday, but CDU leader Stefan Evers declined, citing a Bundesrat commitment.

**Fifteen injured as fire forces evacuation of Berlin hospital**

A fire at a hospital in Berlin's Lichtenberg district on Sunday evening left 15 people injured and forced the evacuation of the entire building, the fire department said.

The blaze reportedly began in a patient room on the first floor of one wing. Crews put it out, but smoke spread to other parts of the hospital. Because it was initially unclear how many people were at risk, a mass casualty alert (MANV) was triggered.

One person suffered life-threatening injuries and another was seriously hurt. Both were taken to other hospitals under emergency medical supervision. The remaining 13 had minor injuries.

Once the fire was out and the building had been extensively ventilated, firefighters handed the scene to police, who are now investigating the cause.

It was the second serious incident in the district in as many days. At about 10pm on Saturday, furniture and clutter caught fire in a third-floor flat in a high-rise block. Two people were initially reported missing, so 46 firefighters attended.

The situation proved less serious than feared, with no full-scale fire. Both residents were treated at the scene and did not need hospital care. The fire was out within about an hour, though the flat is uninhabitable because of smoke damage.
