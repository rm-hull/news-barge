A large animal “resembling a lion” was roaming loose near Istanbul on Monday, authorities warned as residents were told to stay home and thermal drones searched overhead.

The big cat was picked up by surveillance cameras on Sunday night in the Zekeriyakoy area, north of the European side of Istanbul, the country’s national parks authority said.

Surveillance and thermal camera footage broadcast by Turkish television showed what appeared to be a lioness walking through undergrowth and teams searching the woodland in Istanbul’s Sariyer district.

Resident Arda Koç told the İhlas News Agency that he ran into the lion while driving and promptly notified the authorities.

“I took my car out for a drive hoping to see it. There are a lot of people living in the area, families with children walking around,” he said.

“While I was driving, it suddenly jumped out in front of me. At first, I was scared. Then I immediately called the gendarmerie,” he said.

![Footage circulated in Turkish media appeared to show a female lion on the loose near Istanbul](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/23/11/18/Screenshot-2026-11-23-at-11-08-59.png?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The search party lost the animal’s trail in the thick vegetation overnight before it reappeared near a football pitch in Uskumruköy, Sarıyer, according to Turkish outlet Yeni Şafak.

Conservation teams were expected to sedate the cat, which is believed to have escaped from a private property in the region, with a tranquiliser dart, local media reported.

The Istanbul Governorship said efforts were underway to capture the lion alive. The gendarmerie, police forces, and Sarıyer Municipality Enforcement Directorate teams had all been drawn in to help, it said.

The parks authority said: “Following operations in the wooded area, the animal’s location was identified. Efforts to capture it are continuing, with a team that includes specialist veterinarians on site.”

![Turkish gendarmerie officers block a road as police and animal welfare teams search for a lioness spotted roaming in Sariyer district in Istanbul](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/23/11/download.-1..jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

But Oktay Aksu, the mayor of Sariyer, warned that the animal seen on camera could still be another wild animal and urged residents to remain in their homes, “both to avoid harm and to prevent hindering the work”.

The governor’s office said that the animal was first seen on security cameras at around 6pm on Sunday evening. Regional prosecutors have since launched an investigation to determine where the lion escaped from and who its owner is.

Turkey has become a hub for the illegal trade in wild animals as traffickers exploit its air links to Africa and Asia.

In December 2024, a young gorilla was rescued after being discovered at Istanbul Airport on a flight from Nigeria to Thailand.
