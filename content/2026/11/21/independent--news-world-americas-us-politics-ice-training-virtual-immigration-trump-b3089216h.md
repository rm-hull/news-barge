Immigration and Customs Enforcement reportedly allowed hundreds of new recruits to complete their training virtually, with some finishing in less than a day.

The findings appear in a 19-page preliminary report by the Department of Homeland Security’s inspector general obtained by *The New York Times*. The report documents relaxed standards at DHS under former Secretary Kristi Noem, who pushed to quickly hire 10,000 new officers as Congress approved billions of dollars in new funding to accelerate Donald Trump’s demands for mass deportations.

Previously obtained ICE records showed that the agency had slashed training periods for new recruits. A former agency lawyer turned whistleblower has also described the training as “deficient, defective and broken.”

ICE has faced intense scrutiny over a series of fatal shootings by its agents and viral videos showing officers using rough tactics amid the president’s deportation campaign. Public opinion has increasingly soured on the agency. An October Pew Research Center survey found that 56 percent of Americans viewed ICE unfavorably, while just 35 percent viewed it favorably.

Markwayne Mullin, who took charge at DHS after Noem was fired in March, has signaled a shift from his predecessor’s policies. Last week, he told lawmakers: “There were some decisions that were made before I was secretary that we are having to undo.”

![ICE reportedly allowed hundreds of new recruits to complete their training virtually, with some finishing in less than a day](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/14/18/37/GettyImages-2278429002.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The draft inspector general report found that, under Noem’s leadership, more than 3,500 recruits with law enforcement backgrounds were permitted to complete their training online instead of attending a month of in-person instruction.

The in-person program had included de-escalation techniques, firearms instruction and lessons on the use of force, the *Times* reported.

Recruits finished the online training far more quickly: The vast majority finished within 10 days, while more than 10 percent finished in a day or less.

“Some students clicked through the learning modules without absorbing content,” the report said.

One ICE Enforcement and Removal Operations official reported that the “virtual training appeared ineffective for many recruits, and they would have preferred that those recruits (even with prior law-enforcement experience) take in-person training instead,” according to the report.

![Public opinion has increasingly soured on ICE. An October Pew Research Center survey found that 56 percent of Americans viewed the agency unfavorably, while just 35 percent viewed it favorably](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/14/20/2298656446..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Beginning last November, the training time for new recruits without backgrounds in law enforcement was reduced to about 300 hours from 543 hours. In total, close to 6,000 officers went through a “sharply curtailed training model” between the summer of 2025 and 2026, according to the draft report.

The report also criticized ICE’s testing policy.

Recruits previously had just two opportunities to pass a training test, but under Noem, the agency allowed unlimited attempts. One recruit took the test more than 30 times.

In the draft, one unnamed instructor described the changes in training as a “catastrophic failure.”

DHS officials reportedly offered no explanation for the truncated training, according to the draft.

When reached by the *Times*, a DHS spokesperson said that Mullin, a former Oklahoma senator, had ensured officers were obtaining “extra training.” An ICE representative added: “A significant portion of officers brought on during the hiring surge have law enforcement experience and have already successfully completed a law enforcement academy.”

![The slimmed-down ICE training came while Kristi Noem was in charge of DHS and as the Trump administration sought to accelerate deportations](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/01/01/KRISTI_NOEM-DIVORCIO_33543.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

DHS Inspector General Joseph Cuffari denounced the newspaper’s coverage of the draft report.

“We strongly condemn the premature release of draft reports before the department has had the opportunity to complete its review and provide formal comments,” Cuffari’s office told the *Times*. “Such disclosures undermine the integrity of the oversight process, risk presenting incomplete or misleading information, and compromise the fairness and accuracy essential to credible oversight.”

His office has also opened investigations into a range of other issues at ICE, including its use of force, detention practices and arrests of U.S. citizens.

A spokesperson for Noem didn’t respond to the outlet’s request for comment. The former secretary reportedly felt scapegoated and “thrown under the bus” following her firing.

When facing controversy earlier this year over the killing of a U.S. citizen in Minnesota, she told a source: “Everything I've done, I've done at the direction of the president and Stephen,” *Axios*reported. She was referring to Stephen Miller, now the White House chief of staff.* The Independent* has contacted DHS for comment.
