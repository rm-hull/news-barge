# Major tram power likely to be handed to Tracy Brabin by Government

## The Department for Transport has begun consulting on devolving Transport and Works Act Orders, which is the method by which the Government approves tram or rail projects.

The Government has taken a step closer to scrapping an archaic law and handing down Whitehall powers to West Yorkshire Mayor Tracy Brabin, which could speed up delivery of mass transit by around a year.

As reported by The Yorkshire Post ahead of the Budget, the Department for Transport has begun consulting on devolving Transport and Works Act Orders (TWAO), which is the method by which the Government approves tram or rail projects.

Instead, Ms Brabin will be able to sign these off herself, which should cut up to a year off the mass transit timetable, currently slated to open in the late 2030s.

The moves are part of Prime Minister Andy Burnham’s devolution agenda, which seeks to move powers from Whitehall to local communities.

Bus and Local Transport Minister Simon Lightwood, who is also the MP for Wakefield, said: “Public transport plays a crucial role in helping people get around West Yorkshire, connecting communities, supporting jobs and driving economic growth.

"The best transport schemes are shaped by the people who know their area's needs best. That's why we're putting more power in local hands, so mayors like Tracy Brabin can play a greater role in delivering the transport schemes for the region.

"Tracy Brabin has ambitious plans for West Yorkshire's transport network, and we want to help local leaders get on with delivering the infrastructure that will improve connectivity, support growth and make everyday journeys better."

Kane Emmerson, from the Centre for British Progress, added: “This could make a real difference for West Yorkshire. The region has drawn up three plans for mass transit in Leeds in more than 30 years, and Whitehall killed off the first two.

“Today only 38 per cent of Leeds residents can reach the city centre within 30 minutes, compared with 87 per cent in Marseille.
