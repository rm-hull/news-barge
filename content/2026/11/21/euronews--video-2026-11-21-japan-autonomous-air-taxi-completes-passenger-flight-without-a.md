With **Mount Fuji** rising in the distance, a futuristic two-seat aircraft lifted vertically into the air above**Yamanashi Prefecture**, propelled by** 16 spinning rotors**along a pre-programmed route without a pilot on board. Inside the cabin, a lone passenger waved through the glass as visitors and journalists crowded around to photograph** Yamanashi’s first passenger demonstration**of the** EH216-S,**an all-electric vertical take-off and landing craft built by Chinese firm** EHang,**during a** 380-metre** flight along the academy’s runway.

A second, uncrewed sortie later stretched **1,800 metres** above the nearby**Kamanashi River**, showcasing an aircraft engineered to bypass conventional runways altogether, reach speeds of** up to 120 km/h**and cover roughly** 30 kilometres**on a single charge. Staged to introduce the public to urban air mobility and test the potential for short-hop flights across** Japan**, the showcase underscored both the promise of an aerial alternative to congested roads and the regulatory, safety and infrastructure hurdles still standing in the way of commercial rollout.
