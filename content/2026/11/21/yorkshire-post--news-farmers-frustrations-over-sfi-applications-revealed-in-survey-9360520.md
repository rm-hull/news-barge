# Farmers’ frustrations over SFI applications revealed in survey

## When a new window for SFI applications worth more than £230m was announced for September it was welcomed by the farming industry - which was left dismayed just six hours later when the window closed unexpectedly and without warning.

It left industry leaders calling for clarity and answers after they had previously warned Defra there was going to be high demand. Nearly double the amount of applications were submitted for this second window, compared to the first window in Spring. Now, farmers from across Yorkshire have made their views known to Defra, telling of concerns, frustrations and the financial and personal impacts on farming families and businesses. The Yorkshire Agricultural Society (YAS) surveyed its farming members to understand their experiences of the recent SFI application process, which opened and closed on September 22 this year. It sent the responses of 38 farmers, tenant farmers, advisers and landowners from across its farming networks to Defra’s Senior Team and the EFRA Committee – to help ensure farmers’ experiences and perspectives are represented in discussions about the future of agri-environment schemes and any future iterations of the SFI. Thirteen of the respondents to the survey from our region managed to submit their application, while 23 said they wanted to apply but didn’t manage to submit because they either started and couldn’t proceed or were unable to apply. Key themes which came from responses included: strong concerns about the first-come, first-served allocation process; frustration with the short application window and the speed at which available funding was allocated; technical issues within the application system, including land parcel and eligibility errors; concerns about the adequacy of the budget relative to demand; and financial and personal impacts on farm businesses, including stress and uncertainty. A breakdown by YAS of the results show most respondents found understanding the scheme (cited by 63 per cent) and finding information (54 per cent) easy, suggesting guidance was not the main barrier. However, completing the application (for 62 per cent) and using the online system (for 68 per cent) were rated difficult by around two-thirds. The most common issues were technical problems, timescales, deadlines, using the online system and getting help or advice. One farmer who started an application but couldn’t submit it said: “Frankly I am sickened and it has significantly affected morale, profitability and mental health on my small family farm.” A land agent added the window was a mockery as they had to apply for what the system would accept, rather than the actions they wanted to take. The YAS submission concluded by saying: “Difficulties arose mainly on the day rather than from understanding the scheme: the speed at which the budget was allocated, technical and data issues, limited access to help and a lack of advance information. “Based on what respondents reported, areas that may merit attention in future rounds include the allocation method when demand exceeds the budget, the length and timing of windows relative to the farming calendar, advance communication of opening times and remaining funding, the accuracy of land and agreement data, Rural Payments Agency support capacity at peak demand, and early clarity on SFI in 2027.” However, there were some positives taken from the window. Several said the portal was straightforward when no errors occurred, with one agent finding it easier than the old paper system. Two noted the system did not crash and performed better than in Window 1. Others valued the information on action compatibility, the new legume option, the ability to apply before an existing agreement ended and the support available. Defra said it received around 6,700 applications in Window 1 and around 12,200 applications in Window 2. Changes are expected to be made prior to the opening of SFI windows for 2027 and the NFU has said a budget of around £1bn will be needed to meet demand, and make sure farmers can continue work supporting the government’s environmental targets. NFU analysis indicates 48,000 farmers are eligible to apply. This includes farmers whose SFI23, expanded SFI, Countryside Stewardship and Higher Level Stewardship agreements are due to expire by and in 2027, as well as those who were unsuccessful in securing an SFI26 agreement. NFU Vice-president Robyn Munt said: “Farmers have shown in their droves that they want to take part in agri-environment schemes. The experience of SFI26 left no doubt about the strength of that demand.” At the Agricultural Industries Confederation (AIC) in Portsmouth last week, Defra Minister for Minister for Food, Fisheries and Farming, Stephen Morgan, said: “Defra will scrap the first come, first served model from the Sustainable Farming Incentive (SFI27) after the issues seen with this year’s iteration.” Other changes and improvements are also being considered.
