The lawyer for a Black man who was beaten up by three French police officers hailed a “historic ruling” on Saturday after a court acknowledged the racist nature of the violence and sentenced them to between one and two years in prison.

The assault on Michel Zecler, a music producer, took place six years ago during the coronavirus pandemic and was caught on camera. It showed the officers repeatedly punching him, using a truncheon and tear gas against him for no apparent reason.

Zecler told The Associated Press after the incident that the officers hurled repeated insults at him, including a very strong racist epithet.

“What is very important is that the racist nature of the violence he suffered has been recognized," Zecler's lawyer, Serge Money, told France Info. “As a legal professional, one can only be satisfied. This is a first.”

Money said that from the very beginning of the proceedings, Zecler maintained that he had been racially insulted, whereas the defendants’ accounts were somewhat inconsistent.

"Ultimately, they were convicted of lying, as the falsification of written records was established,” he said. The verdict was issued late Friday in Paris.

Videos published showed the violent arrest of Zecler in the 17th arrondissement of the French capital. The three officers intended to stop him for not wearing a face mask during the restrictions imposed because of the pandemic.

The video images, obtained by the AP, both from a security camera inside the studio and filmed by neighbors outside, showed officers following Zecler inside his music studio, where they could be seen repeatedly punching him and beating him with a truncheon.

“I want to understand why I have been assaulted by people who were wearing a police uniform," Zecler told AP at the time. "I want justice actually, because I believe in the justice of my country."

Zecler suffered injuries to his head, forearms and legs.

French law enforcement agencies have long faced complaints of excessive force. After Zecler was taken into custody for violence against public authority, then-Justice Minister Eric Dupond-Moretti said in a television interview that he was scandalized by the images. He admitted that some officers were racists, but denied systemic racism within the force.

Money said that without the video, Zecler would have ended up in prison. He added that the officers apologized.

“This ruling should set a precedent for all those who have not been believed," he said. “There has to be a first, a starting point. It is now up to the justice system to make use of this ruling. I have no doubt that everyone will recognize its significance. This is no small matter, particularly given that the investigating judges had initially dismissed the racist dimension of the case."

Rap artist Stomy Bugsy said “this is a victory for all victims. If it can help curb, reduce and ultimately put an end to this kind of police misconduct and abuse, then it is a victory.”
