Age UK York is calling on local people to help tackle loneliness among older residents through its Stamp Out Loneliness campaign.

The campaign encourages simple acts of kindness and connection that can make a lasting difference.

Loneliness remains a significant challenge for many older people in York, particularly during the winter months when opportunities for social interaction can be limited.

Through the Stamp Out Loneliness campaign, Age UK York is encouraging individuals, families, community groups and businesses to play their part in creating a more connected city.

The campaign highlights the importance of reaching out to older neighbours, friends and relatives, whether through a friendly phone call, a visit, an invitation for a cup of tea, or simply taking the time to stop and chat.

Simon Holmes, chief executive, Age UK York said: “Loneliness can affect anyone, but it is particularly challenging for older people who may be living alone or have limited opportunities for social contact.

“Through our Stamp Out Loneliness campaign, we’re encouraging everyone in York to take a few moments to check in on an older neighbour, friend or family member. A simple conversation can make a world of difference and help someone feel seen, valued and connected.

“It was estimated that in Yorkshire last year, 120,000 older people ate their Christmas dinner alone and almost a million older people aged 65+ in the UK say they’re often or always lonely.

“We’re hoping, with the help of the local community, to provide connection to those who find the festive period challenging.”

The charity are encouraging people across the community to send a Christmas card to older residents who may be spending the festive season alone.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/Christmas-Cards-.webp)

Following the success of last year’s campaign, Age UK York is hoping to once again inspire local schoolchildren to create their own handmade cards.

This year, members of the public are also being encouraged to get involved, with special post boxes being placed in all four Age UK York charity shops, making it easy for people to donate cards and share festive goodwill with older residents who may otherwise feel isolated during the Christmas season.

Caroline Usher Cox, communications and marketing Manager, Age UK York, said: “The idea for the Stamp Out Loneliness campaign came directly from conversations with older people in our community, many of whom told us they could no longer afford to send cards through the post. For others, mobility issues can make reaching a post box difficult.

“Through this campaign, we hope to spread joy and connection by reaching people who may be spending days without seeing or speaking to anyone.

“The response from recipients last year was truly overwhelming. Some older residents were moved to tears when they received a unique, hand-written card from a local child, reminding them that they are valued, remembered and part of their community.”
