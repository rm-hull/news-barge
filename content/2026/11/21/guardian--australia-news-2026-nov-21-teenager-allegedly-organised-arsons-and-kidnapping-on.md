Victorian police allege a 17-year-old boy tasked others to carry out a series of arsons, home invasions and a mistaken identity kidnapping that resulted in the partial amputation of a victim’s finger during Melbourne’s hospitality wars.

The boy, who was arrested on Friday afternoon, allegedly communicated with the offenders under the username “Onepercent” on the encrypted communications app Signal.

He was charged on Saturday with extortion, making threats to kill, three offences of criminal damage by fire, and home invasion. He was remanded to appear in court later on Saturday.

In a statement, police said it would be alleged that Onepercent was involved in tasking an arson at a York Street, South Melbourne licensed premises on 15 April; two home invasions and a kidnapping four days apart in Springvale later that month; and two arson attacks during March at a Williamstown ice-cream store and a car in Moonee Ponds.

The boy was arrested at a house in the Moonee Ponds region, in Melbourne’s north-west, police said.

It is alleged a trafficable quantity of cocaine, three phones and cash were seized during the arrest.

Police allege that when the mistaken identity kidnapping occurred at the Springvale property on 22 April, a 34-year-old victim received serious injuries to his lower body and the partial amputation of one of his fingers.

It is alleged the fire at Capriccio Gelateria in Williamstown on 22 March caused $600,000 worth of damage and endangered the lives of residents at the property upstairs.

On 16 November, police appealed for more information about the person behind the Onepercent user name.

“We need the public’s assistance to identify the user of this handle,” Det Insp Graham Banks said at the time.

“There will be people in the wider organised crime network and in our community who will have intimate knowledge of who this person is.

“We are hopeful that those people may now be in a position to come forward and speak to police.”
