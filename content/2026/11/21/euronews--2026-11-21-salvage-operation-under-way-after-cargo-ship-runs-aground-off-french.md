## French authorities are racing to prevent a stranded cargo ship from capsizing after it ran aground off the south-western coast. The Maltese-flagged vessel, which is carrying more than 100 tonnes of fuel, poses a potential pollution threat as salvage teams prepare to refloat it.

A Maltese-flagged cargo ship remains stranded on the south-western coast of France as authorities work to prevent it from capsizing and causing potential environmental damage.

The 169-metre vessel, named MIRO G, ran aground near Cap de l’Homy, in the Landes department, close to the village of Lit-et-Mixe, shortly before 1am on Friday, 20 November.

The ship had encountered difficulties in severe weather, with wind gusts reaching around 90km/h. Despite dropping anchor in an attempt to stabilise its position, the vessel drifted towards the shore and became stuck on a sandbank.

While 19 crew members had already been rescued, a further 11 people – including the captain and two other crew members, as well as salvage experts and company representatives – were evacuated by helicopter on Saturday ahead of worsening weather conditions. No injuries were reported.

French authorities said the ship was carrying no cargo but remained a potential pollution risk because of the fuel on board. Aerial surveillance carried out by the French Navy found no signs of pollution in the surrounding waters.

Salvage experts have been assessing how to refloat the vessel, but the operation has been complicated by technical problems and worsening weather conditions.

## **Ballast tanks filled to stabilise stranded vessel**

The ship's ballast tanks have been filled with seawater to increase its stability and reduce the risk of it capsizing or shifting further towards the beach. However, the water will eventually need to be pumped out to make the vessel lighter and allow it to be pulled free.

The operation has been hampered by the failure of the ship's two main generators, which are needed to power the pumps. Engineers are working to restore an alternative electricity supply.

The French salvage tug *Abeille Bretagne* has also arrived in the area, carrying a 1,000-metre towline. However, shallow waters mean it cannot approach closer than around 900 metres to the stranded vessel, making the towing operation particularly challenging.

Strong winds and rough seas are expected to continue, with officials warning that the operation to refloat the ship could take several days or longer.

Access to the beach at Cap de l’Homy has been restricted while the emergency response continues.

The French maritime authorities said they were working to take advantage of the first suitable weather window, but stressed that there was no guarantee the operation would succeed.
