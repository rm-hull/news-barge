# NFU warning on poor connectivity in rural areas

## The family of a farmer, whose legacy has enabled funding for the next generation of farmers in the region, visited the Yorkshire Agricultural Society to see first-hand how it has helped students.

The late Mary Goodall bequeathed a legacy to the Yorkshire Agricultural Society (YAS) to further its charitable aims to support young people in the agricultural industry. And, out of that came the Goodall Agri-Development Pathway, which launched in 2023. Already the course has had 22 alumni, 12 current pathfinders and is set to take up to 12 students again for the 2027 cohort. The pathway is a fully funded training programme, designed to help the next generation of farming professionals build skills, broaden their experience and prepare for greater responsibility. The opportunity is open to people aged 23 to 40, who work in farming and allied industries in the North of England. Several of Mary’s family visited the YAS headquarters, on the Great Yorkshire Showground at Harrogate, to share more about her life and to hear about her great legacy. They said: “We are delighted that her legacy is supporting young people in agriculture through a programme that reflects her lifelong commitment to farming. The Pathway is an impressive example of how practical development and shared experience can help the sector’s future leaders to thrive.” Her family added Mary was, “very forward thinking, she was determined and she had lots of friends in the farming community - she was very welcoming. She was a farmer all her life and that’s why this legacy is so special.” Yorkshire Agricultural Society Manager David Tite added: “We are incredibly grateful to Mary for her gift which has enabled us to roll out a programme that develops the next generation of farmers through modules and a study trip in Europe. “It’s a unique and life changing programme and wonderful to see that her legacy lives on in the industry she loved so much.” The programme comprises eight face-to-face modules featuring farm visits, conferences, expert speakers and a European study trip. Topics include teamwork, leadership, media training, business growth and direct sales, sustainable farming and diversification, farm administration and HR, and lean management. Participants, known as Pathfinders, will gain practical knowledge, confidence and a wider industry perspective to support their career ambitions. Those completing the programme in 2026 will be recognised at a celebratory awards presentation at the Great Yorkshire Show in 2027. There are a limited number of places available on the Pathway each year. Applicants are interviewed by a selection panel before being informed if their application is successful. Whilst places are fully-funded, a £250 deposit refundable upon completion of the Pathway is required to secure a place if your application is approved. Applications for 2027 closed yesterday.
