England beat New Zealand by record margin

**England (19) 37Tries:** Slade, Van Poortvliet, Earl, Chessum, Atkinson**Cons:** F Smith (2), M Smith**Pens:** M Smith (2)**New Zealand (5) 12Tries:** De Groot, B Barrett**Cons:** McKenzie**England's revival roared up a gear as they beat New Zealand by a record margin after former captain Lewis Moody led the hosts' response to the haka.**

Moody, who was diagnosed with motor neurone disease last year, stood at the centre of a flying V formation that England had adopted for their victories over the same opponents last year and in the 2019 Rugby World Cup semi-final.

In terms of margin, this win trumped both - and the other seven England victories in the teams' 121-year rivalry.

And the hosts were full value for it. They dominated the All Blacks from the start.

Henry Slade crossed for the opening try in the fourth minute and only England's own mistakes prevented them taking a bigger lead than their 19-5 advantage into the break.

Beauden Barrett marked his 150th Test appearance by rounding off a coast-to-coast Kiwi try to cut England's advantage to 24-12. But his team never really threatened to reel in Steve Borthwick's men.

England introduced five forwards off the bench in the immediate aftermath of that 55th-minute score and, with Ellis Genge in fearsome carrying form, they had more than enough to see out victory in comfort.

Seb Atkinson took a delayed pop pass from Marcus Smith to stroll in for England's fifth try just after the hour.

The hosts finished the game camped down in the New Zealand 22m in search of a sixth score while choruses of Swing Low rolled around the stands.

For New Zealand, who suffered a shock defeat by Wales last weekend, it is a result that suggests a long Test season, which included four Tests against South Africa, may be catching up with them.

By contrast England, who could still end up in next weekend's Nations Championship grand final depending on results, are riding a wave of confidence.

## Moody sets the mood

![Lewis Moody faces the haka](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/800/cpsprodpb/17b9/live/509e99e0-e5c7-11f1-a738-4787e1a18cce.jpg)

Moody, who captained England at the 2011 Rugby World Cup, alongside current skipper Maro Itoje (left) and Itoje's vice-captain Ellis Genge

Moody's involvement in the pre-match challenge ensured New Zealand were met by a wall of noise and relentless pressure from kick-off.

Fast starts have become a feature of England's autumn campaign so far, with Australia and Japan both blitzed in the opening minutes.

New Zealand are a class above those teams, but fared little better against the revved-up hosts.

The game was only four minutes old when Slade, with roars from the stands at his back, won the race to a Fin Smith grubber.

After that initial strike, England maintained a stranglehold on the match.

A brilliant 50:22 from George Furbank bought more territory and England, hitting hard in defence, penned New Zealand back.

Their dominance did not immediately translate into points.

Individual errors - Jack van Poortvliet's fumble at the back of a breakdown, Fin Smith's over-cooked cross-field kick, Alex Coles being held up over the line - meant chances went begging.

After half an hour, England had enjoyed 74% of possession, 82% of territory and made 183 metres to New Zealand's 34. A sense the hosts may ultimately regret those mistakes nagged at fans in the stands.

It proved misplaced. Van Poortvliet plunged over from short range after a Cadan Murley break and then, four minutes later, the excellent Ben Earl thundered off the back of a scrum, trampled through Cam Roigard and went under the posts.

New Zealand prop Ethan de Groot burrowed over to repair some of the damage, but at 19-5 down at the break, the visitors were both reeling and somewhat fortunate to still be in touch.

England started the second half at the same breathless pace that marked their approach to the first. Ollie Chessum, lurking out wide, ploughed through the unfortunate Will Jordan for a try inside two minutes of the restart.

Beauden Barrett's score briefly had the embers glowing for New Zealand, but England's bench stomped out any hopes of a comeback.

Genge, Will Stuart, Luke Cowan-Dickie, George Martin and Greg Fisilau gave the forward brought crunching physicality and line speed.

The starters were not far behind, with Chessum levelling former All Black captain Scott Barrett with a superb tackle.

With victory long since secured, an 81,953-strong crowd could relish those individual duels, reflect on a scoreline that exceeded the famous 2012 win and look forward to next weekend, and a possible rematch with New Zealand or meeting with South Africa, at leisure.
