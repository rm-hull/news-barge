The FBI is investigating an apparent hack of a national law enforcement database that it maintains, a person familiar with the matter said Saturday.

The National Crime Information Center, known as the NCIC, is a computerized database of criminal justice information that is available to local, state and federal law enforcement agencies and includes records on fugitives, missing persons, criminal history data, stolen property and other topics.

The FBI did not immediately respond to messages about the apparent hack, which was first reported by CNN and confirmed to The Associated Press by a person familiar with the matter who was not authorized to discuss it by name and spoke on the condition of anonymity.

The scope of the problem was not immediately clear.

Asked about the subject on Saturday, President Donald Trump said in a Fox News Channel interview: “We just heard about it and I will say that it’s not good.”

News that the NCIC was targeted comes as the country has encountered a series of digital outages in the government and private sector that Trump administration officials have said were caused by routine efforts to upgrade computer systems and fix vulnerabilities, rather than a cyberattack. There was no immediate indication that the FBI incident was related to those outages.

The bureau has historically been a frequent target for hackers.

In September, for instance, the FBI said it was investigating a hack of its job portal website after a criminal hacking group known as ShinyHunters claimed it had obtained a massive trove of data on bureau employees and applicants.

And in March, the FBI disclosed that it was investigating “suspicious activities” on an internal system that contains sensitive information related to surveillance operations and investigations.
