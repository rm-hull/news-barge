# Ryedale Festival recognition shows importance of saluting nation's volunteers: Ed Anderson

## Last Saturday, some 250 organisations across the UK were announced as winners of the King’s Award for Voluntary Service (KAVS). This is the highest recognition that a voluntary group can attain and is described as being equivalent to an MBE.

The successful groups will be presented with an impressive solid glass memento by the Lord-Lieutenant of their County, together with a certificate signed by The King.

Two members of the group may be invited to a Royal Garden Party and the group can use the KAVS emblem on its stationery and marketing materials for as long as it continues to exist.

As Ryedale Festival Chair, I am very proud that the Ryedale Festival is among many Yorkshire-based organisations that were recognised in the list. Since its beginnings in 1981, the Ryedale Festival has grown into an event with a national and increasingly international reputation.

Under the inspirational leadership of our Artistic Director Christopher Glynn, it has grown considerably in its scope and impact in recent years. Last July it included some 60 events over 17 days at 35 venues right across North Yorkshire and in parts of the East Riding.

It is estimated to bring some £2 million of economic benefit to the area and is enjoyed by listeners nationally through broadcasts on BBC Radio 3.

The Festival is renowned for combining four distinct characteristics that I believe mark it out as being among the very best of UK classical music festivals.

Firstly, there is the quality of the music itself which is always of the very highest standard. Many of its artists are internationally recognised household names such as Roderick Williams, Tenebrae, the Royal Liverpool Philharmonic, John Wilson and the Sinfonia of London, the Kanneh-Mason family and many others.

Secondly there is the unique beauty of our Yorkshire venues, ranging from Castle Howard, Duncombe Park, Ripon Cathedral, Sledmere House and Selby Abbey at one end of the scale through to numerous quite small but wonderful Churches in towns and villages across our County. The Festival’s mission is to bring music of the highest quality to places that would not otherwise have the opportunity to experience it, while continuing to respect our core geographic area of Helmsley and the surrounding towns and villages.

This year we included nine new venues, including St Mary’s Church in Richmond, Howden Minster and Bridlington Priory.

Thirdly, it is renowned for engaging with young people including up and coming artists who often go on to become household names. We partner with the BBC’s New Generation Artists scheme but also create career-changing opportunities for our own Waverley Young Artists.

We hold special events for young people, starting with our ‘Concerteenies’ for children aged 3-7, but also run a work-experience scheme with local secondary schools that gives young people a way into classical music and opens their eyes to what working in the arts can involve.

Our fourth distinct characteristic, and the one that has led to this Award, is the remarkable contribution of our volunteers. Each year around 100 people give their time in a whole host of ways, from transporting artists, hosting performers in their houses, stewarding and providing qualified first aid facilities at our concerts, serving refreshments, assisting with marketing and many other roles without which the festival simply couldn’t take place.

The Ryedale Festival volunteers, in common with everyone who volunteers their time and skills, would be the first to say that they volunteer because they believe in the organisation that they support; but equally that they themselves derive benefit from the companionship and sense of worth that go with volunteering.

In my former role as Lord-Lieutenant of West Yorkshire, I had the privilege of presenting dozens of KAVS awards to groups across the county. The recognised groups ranged from volunteers at addiction centres, organisers of community luncheon clubs, groups that had restored and maintained disused historic cemeteries, numerous groups working with disadvantaged young people, groups combating loneliness in older people and many, many others.

In every case, what struck me so forcibly was firstly that those who volunteered, often over many decades, never sought or expected any recognition for their work and, secondly, I would witness the absolute joy they experienced when they received their award from the Lord-Lieutenant on behalf of His Majesty.

It is estimated that across Yorkshire there are in excess of 20,000 charities, voluntary groups, community associations and other third-sector organisations, many operating under the radar. What is certain is that, as a sector, they provide the glue that holds our society together.

Let us therefore salute all those who give their time and skills for the benefit of others and celebrate the power of volunteering.

Ed Anderson is Ryedale Festival Chair and a former Lord-Lieutenant of West Yorkshire
