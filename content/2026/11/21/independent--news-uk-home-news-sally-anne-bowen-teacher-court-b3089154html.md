A chemistry teacher who engaged in sexual relationships with two pupils at an all-boys school in the late 1980s will be sentenced on Monday, following her arrest for failing to attend her court hearing.

Sally-Anne Bowen, 65, was convicted earlier this year of seven counts of indecent assault and had been scheduled to be sentenced over the offences at Harrow Crown Court on Friday.

The former teacher was in her 20s when she had sex with one pupil up to 30 times and kissed a second boy, with both victims aged around 14 to 15 at the time.

Her trial heard she flirted in class while wearing short, tight skirts, with one former pupil likening her behaviour to "a Sabrina Carpenter video".

The defendant, who was on bail, failed to appear at court on Friday morning, resulting in a warrant being issued for her arrest.

She could not be found at her home in Hemel Hempstead, Hertfordshire, before police eventually located and arrested her in Hampshire.

She was remanded into custody and will appear at the same court for sentencing on Monday.

A Metropolitan Police spokesperson said: “On Friday, November 20, officers were made aware that a 65-year-old woman had failed to appear at Harrow Crown Court for sentencing.

“A warrant was issued for her arrest and officers promptly launched inquiries to locate her.

“At around 15:00 hrs, officers from a neighbouring force found the woman at a location in Hampshire and arrested her on suspicion of failing to appear at court. She was taken into police custody.

“On Saturday, November 21 she appeared before Uxbridge Magistrates’ Court where she was remanded to appear at Harrow Crown Court on Monday, November 23.”

![Sally-Anne Bowen arriving in court](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/08/24/10/01M0SGGHZZ5FTKN97ZB2ZT84FW.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Her trial heard that Bowen was in her mid-20s when she taught at Christ’s College in Finchley, north London.

One victim told jurors that he had sex with her “about 20 or 30 times” over several months, regularly visited her home and considered her his girlfriend.

He had fallen in love but kept their relationship secret because Bowen had “drummed it” into him that he must not tell anyone, the court heard.

The victim said Bowen was able to teach for years “because I remained silent”.

In a police interview played to jurors, the victim said Bowen dressed in a “sexually provocative” way.

He said his “introduction to the world was completely governed by Sally-Anne Bowen”.

The man said he enjoyed the sex, “games” and “secrets” at the time, but it led to “the destruction of my education”.

He added: “I had no respect for authority, no self-respect. My mother never forgave me.”

The second victim described spending lunchtimes with Bowen in a cafe, where he was “goaded” into kissing her by other boys and recalled touching her breasts.

Both victims cannot be identified for legal reasons.

Former pupil Tobias Oliver told jurors Bowen flirted and openly discussed sex in class.

He said she once sat on a desk and slowly opened her legs, revealing her underwear.

Mr Oliver also recalled her discussing sexual positions from the Kama Sutra during a lesson.

“The topics of conversation in class often strayed away from chemistry and became quite sexual,” he said.

Prosecutor Madeleine Wolfe said during the trial that while the boys may have appeared willing, Bowen had “exploited” her position as a teacher “for her own sexual gratification”.

The court heard the first victim’s mother became suspicious and raised concerns with the school.

She was later told Bowen had left, without an explanation, Ms Wolfe said.

Bowen told the boy she was moving to a kibbutz in Israel, leaving him “completely lost” and “heartbroken”, the prosecutor added.

Former head of science Richard Luker recalled to the court the mother’s concern that her son planned to travel abroad with Bowen.

The victim reported the allegations to police in 2014.

Bowen denied the allegations as “total fabrication” and claimed a medical condition made sex difficult and painful.

She denied flirting, saying: “I didn’t flirt with students, I was just friendly.”

Defending her clothes, she said she “wasn’t going to dress like Margaret Thatcher”.
