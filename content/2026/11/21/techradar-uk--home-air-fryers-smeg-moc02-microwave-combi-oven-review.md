### Smeg MOC02: 30-second review

The Smeg MOC02 looks every bit a Smeg appliance, with rounded corners, a band of color around the door and an aluminum control panel. I tested the Storm Blue version and it looked great on my countertop.

Of course, at £449.95, it needs to do more than look good and for the most part, it succeeds. There's a microwave, grill, fan oven and air fryer inside, a series of combination modes that let you double up the functions, plus defrost and keep warm settings. And that's before the 25 automatic presets.

Getting started is relatively easy, even if some of the icons aren't obvious and there’s also a little more waiting around than I expected. After preheating, the Smeg waits for a press of the dial to start cooking, and my microwave-to-air-fry sequence needed another press between stages. The presets and multi-step options help with choosing settings, but you’ll still need to stay nearby and check the food.

Performance-wise, the air fryer made a good first impression. My eight-year-old declared the fish fingers the "best he’d ever had", and breaded chicken bites came out evenly browned with juicy meat inside. The grill was another highlight, making quick work of melting cheese and browning toppings, although some breadcrumbs on my mac and cheese burned before others. I preferred the microwave and oven combination for a jacket potato, while the cake preset left a runny middle that needed another five minutes on the oven setting.

The compact size suited my kitchen but it did mean turning food on the rack or air-fry plate was fiddly, and the ventilation clearances mean it needs more space than its dimensions suggest. If you’ll regularly use its range of cooking modes, there’s plenty here to justify giving the MOC02 a spot on your counter. If you mainly reheat leftovers, much of what you’re paying for will go unused, but at least it will look the part.

### Smeg MOC02: price and availability

* **Price: £449.95 (about $590 / AU$880)**
* **Available in blue, white, black and green**
* **The Smeg MOC02 isn't yet sold in the US or Australia**

The Smeg MOC02 costs £449.95 in the UK. It's sold in White, Black, Emerald Green and Storm Blue and is available from Smeg directly, AO and Amazon. That works out at about $590 / AU$880, thought it's not currently sold in the US or Australia.

At first glance, the Smeg looks expensive for a combi microwave. It’s about £90 more than the £359.99 Ninja Crispi Microwave, £50 more than the Sage Combi Wave 3 in 1, and roughly three times the price of the £149 IKEA GÅTEBO.

That said, when you consider what you get for that extra money, the Smeg is better value. For starters, the Ninja can microwave, air fry and bake but its FusionCrisp mode follows a sequence, whereas the Smeg gives you more choice over which modes you use together.

Ninja lists 14 functions, but that figure includes presets within its three modes. Smeg, instead, has six distinct functions, three combined modes for each recipe, plus 25 automatic programs, making the headline numbers a tad misleading.

Compared to the Sage, the Smeg offers more automatic cooking programs. Compared to IKEA's model, the Smeg lets you queue up tasks and has a dedicated Keep Warm setting. All of which expands the Smeg's capabilities considerably.

Of course, if you mostly reheat leftovers or defrost food, a basic microwave will do those jobs for less but if you'll regularly switch between reheating, grilling, baking and air frying and want a machine to handle it all, the Smeg stands out.

**Value for money: 4/5**

### Smeg MOC02: design

* **Storm Blue finish, brushed aluminum controls and a soft-close door**
* **Compact enough to fit on my worktop**
* **Easy to set up, although some cooking icons and modes take getting used to**

If you've ever owned, or seen a Smeg appliance in real life, you'll know they have a distinctive design, and the MOC02 is no exception. Even if the Smeg logo wasn't emblazoned across the front, I’d still recognize the rounded corners, icon design and the band of color around the door.

I tested the Storm Blue version, which has a dark glass window and a strip of brushed aluminum down one side. That strip holds the colored LCD display above three buttons and a round dial. These buttons include:

* **Chef’s hat:** This button opens the automatic programs menu. You then use the dial to cycle between meat and poultry, fish and shellfish, vegetables, cakes and sweets, and bread, pizza and cereals
* **Plus:** This is used to line up multiple cooking modes
* **Curved arrow:** This works like a back button on a browser.

Across the five categories are 25 presets, numbered P01 to P25, covering everything from roast chicken and frozen fish fingers to jacket potatoes, mug cakes and reheating pizza.

![The Storm Blue door and silver control dial on the front of the Smeg MOC02](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/FpbiKL6wKwvzpScj3mNGQ5-1200-80.jpg)

The whole unit is surprisingly compact given the range of features it offers. I found a spot for it in the corner of my worktop which kept it out of the way, but also gave me the 11.8-inch clearance needed overhead, and the 7.9-inch gap between the oven and my walls. The door opens to the left, so you'll also need to leave yourself enough room to swing it open and lift a dish straight out.

There's then a glass turntable in the bottom and Smeg includes a raised grill rack, crisp plate and round, shallow air-fry plate in the box. The air-fry plate has holes in it to let hot air reach the food, and the rack lifts your dishes closer to the grill.

In terms of space, I could easily fit a small chicken inside. There's also enough room, height-wise, for a dish on the raised rack. How much room you have to work with though, depends on where the food sits. With the rack or air-fry plate, I found it a bit fiddly to reach in and turn food halfway through cooking. With a pull-out air fryer, I can put the basket down and get at the food from above. Here I was reaching through into a fairly snug — not to mention hot — space.

If you tend to cook in a wide rectangular dish that won't fit as the turntable rotates, you can turn the rotation off in the settings.

![Front view of the open Smeg MOC02, showing the glass turntable and rear fan cover](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/okMrJazEQCfb2Jr6wyEQA6-1200-80.jpg)

Getting started was easy and I soon got the hang of the controls. I turned the dial to pick a mode, used the back button if I went past what I wanted, and pressed the little chef’s hat to open the automatic program.

When I used the cake preset, I could see the program number, weight and time left on the display. Air frying took me longer to find. The icon isn't instantly recognizable and although I could work it out, by process of elimination, I ended up looking it up in the manual to make sure.

![Close-up of the circular perforated cover over the Smeg MOC02&amp;rsquo;s rear convection fan](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/j6yrxbBAPimMDk3c8KXvA6-1200-80.jpg)

The plus button then does three things, depending on when you press it. Before you start cooking, it lets you line up a second mode with its own settings, so you can set both stages in advance. This has its pros and cons, which I detail more in the Performance section.

Once the Smeg is running, pressing the button adds 30 seconds to the time. And if the display is showing the clock, the same plus button starts the microwave at full power, which is handy when you just want to warm something up quickly.

While trying to figure out what each of the icons and presets were, I tried scanning the QR code inside the door, which is a shortcut to the My Smeg Assistant. This is Smeg's AI chatbot. However, when I asked it cooking questions, it often didn’t understand what I was asking. It was better at helping me troubleshoot general appliance problems but not that useful all things considered.

![A round air-fry plate rests on the raised rack above the glass turntable inside the Smeg MOC02](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/ZVLRBL5JiijCyBQrgJUpi5-1200-80.jpg)

Smeg recommends washing the grill rack, air-fry plate and crisp plate by hand in warm soapy water with a soft sponge or cloth. If you’re using the air fryer a lot, that means washing up the plate after cooking and checking the glass turntable for any crumbs or drips that have fallen through. Smeg also recommends wiping out the cavity once it’s cool, before splashes have a chance to dry on.

### Smeg MOC02: Performance

* **The grill was quick, although mac and cheese browned unevenly**
* **Air fry worked well for frozen food but I preferred the oven for a jacket potato**
* **The cake preset needed extra time**

For an appliance with such a wide range of features, it can be overwhelming knowing where to start, but as fish fingers are my eight-year-old's favorite food – much to my dismay — the air fryer function was first up.

I spread them out in a single layer in the shallow plate and selected the P10 — frozen fish fingers — preset. They cooked well, with a crispy outer shell and a fluffy inside. Frozen fish fingers have a habit of falling apart easily or being soggy when cooked, but my son exclaimed they were the "best fish fingers" he'd ever had. High praise indeed for Smeg. This is the sort of quick freezer dinner I’d use the MOC02 for regularly, especially when I only need a small batch.

![After](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/4LrZPTFz6MUYP4NPDCdjA6-1200-80.jpg)
![Before](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/mFeMEgTNYXKf8zpSge9LA6-1200-80.jpg)

I also used the air fryer for some breaded chicken bites. They cooked evenly across the basket, with a browned coating and juicy meat inside. I was pleased with the balance, because getting enough color on breaded chicken can mean leaving it in long enough for the meat to dry out.

As I mentioned in the Design section, turning both the fish fingers and chicken bites was fiddly. I had to work through the door, with less space to angle my tongs around the plate or remove the plate completely. This isn't enough to put me off, but it was noticeable.

![The Smeg MOC02 display shows five minutes at 200&amp;deg;C](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/7dADBM4LTnpTyXh5ARbmG5-1200-80.jpg)

Next up was a jacket potato, which let me try the microwave and air fryer together using the plus button feature. I set the microwave stage first, then lined up air fry to finish and crisp up the skin. Entering both stages before starting was simp[le and quick, and I assumed that would be the last input it needed from me.

Instead, the Smeg beeped when the microwave stage finished and didn't start the air fryer stage until I'd pressed the dial. On the plus side, this gave me a chance to get the potato ready for the next part — adding oil, salt and wrapping it in tin foil — but it also meant I needed to stay within earshot.

![A foil-wrapped jacket potato sits upright in the air-fry basket inside the Smeg MOC02](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/SwVLAAo7SDRhTJSoCdwTA6-1200-80.jpg)

If you’ve set it going while you do something in another room, it’s easy to imagine coming back to find it still waiting to start the second stage. The finished potato was not as crispy as I'd have liked and was more squishy than fluffy on the inside.

I also tried the microwave + oven setting for a jacket potato and much preferred how it turned out. The skin was crispier and browned in places and the potato held its shape more. This is the combination I’d choose to cook a potato next time.

![A cooked jacket potato with browned skin sits in opened foil in the air-fry basket](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/bebJEfzruz674iEcsZ32B6-1200-80.jpg)

The grill was my favorite of the modes I tried and it feels like the feature that really sets the Smeg model apart from the pack. After using the oven setting to cook a lasagna on the raised rack, I could set the grill to melt the cheese on top with a press of a button. I also used the setting for grilled cheese, and for browning the breadcrumb topping on a mac and cheese.

Every time it worked quickly and with the rotating turntable, it cooked relatively evenly other than on the mac and cheese. Some breadcrumbs were golden while others burned. This is likely because of the differences in height across the toppings rather than a fault with the grill, but in such a compact space, there isn't a lot of room to play with and solve this.

Keep Warm was the function I used least, but it came in handy for keeping the fish fingers warm while I waited for my son's rice to finish cooking. It also proved useful when we ordered a takeaway and my eldest son was still finishing his Xbox game.

Across all settings, the Smeg combi microwave heats up and beeps to let you know it's reached the correct temperature, after which you have to press the dial to start the actual cooking process. This makes timings easier to follow and gives a more even finish, but it does mean you have to hang around until it's ready. You can't just put the food in and leave the room.

### Buy it if

**You want to bring several appliances together** The MOC02 can microwave, grill, bake and air fry, with combination modes for dishes that benefit from more than one type of heat.**You often cook smaller meals** Fish fingers and breaded chicken bites worked well, making the Smeg MOC02 useful for quick dinners. The size made it more suited to smaller portions too.**You want something you’ll happily leave on display**

The rounded corners, colored door surround and aluminum panel make the MOC02 easy to recognize as a Smeg. It comes in White, Black, Emerald Green and Storm Blue, so there’s a choice beyond the usual black or silver too.

### Don’t buy it if

**You mainly reheat leftovers** At £449.95, much of the price goes towards cooking functions you may rarely use. A basic microwave will cover reheating and defrosting for less.**You need room for large dishes and batches** A small chicken fitted inside, but turning food on the raised rack or in the air-fry basket was fiddly. Check your favorite cookware fits comfortably, with enough room to lift it out when hot.**You expect cooking to be completely hands-off**

The Smeg needs a button press after preheating and between some cooking stages, so you’ll need to stay nearby. Automatic programs may also need extra cooking time.

I used the Smeg MOC02 in my kitchen and tried the microwave, grill, oven and air-fry functions with everyday food. I cooked a jacket potato using different modes, queued a microwave-to-air-fry sequence, air-fried frozen fish fingers and made mac and cheese.
