Denmark has urged fellow European Union members to take concrete ⁠steps to stand up to Russia after saying it had prevented an assassination against a Ukrainian rapper allegedly planned by Russian intelligence, in a note sent to EU governments that was confirmed by two diplomats.

Police thwarted the attempt when they arrested three men with links to the Danish criminal underworld shortly before the rapper Kyivstoner was due to give a concert north of Copenhagen on September 25, police and security officials said.

"It is ⁠our clear assessment that the assassination attempt was planned by the Russian intelligence services," Rasmus Kieffer-Kristensen, head of Denmark's national security and intelligence service, PET, said.

Two Danish men appeared in court near Copenhagen on Friday, charged under Section 108 of Denmark's Criminal Code, which covers assisting a foreign intelligence service, as well as attempted murder, court documents showed.

Both men denied wrongdoing, broadcaster DR reported.

![Rapper Kyivstoner](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/20/13/50/kyivstoner.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Foreign Minister Lars Lokke Rasmussen called it an unacceptable transgression, and Denmark announced it was expelling ⁠Russia's defence attache.

The message circulated on Friday evening to ​senior ⁠European diplomats proposed that the ‌bloc should impose further restrictions on Russian nationals' access to the 29-nation Schengen free travel zone.

It suggested halting tourist visas for Russian citizens and ceasing recognition ‌of Russian non-biometric passports throughout the zone.

Denmark would ‌present its proposals at a meeting of EU ambassadors in Brussels on Monday.

"We must take concrete actions that project European strength and decisiveness," the message said.

"There is clear momentum to ⁠act decisively now, and we should seize this moment to replace our current patchwork of national measures with a truly unified EU-wide approach."

The note also urged member states to make more use of limited visas that do not permit travel throughout the rest of the Schengen zone, and to set tougher entry restrictions for current and former Russian combatants.

The Russian embassy in Copenhagen said ‌on Friday that Denmark's accusations were not backed with proof.

European Commission ​President Ursula von der Leyen said the EU stood in ‌full solidarity with Denmark and with ⁠all member states targeted by Russian hybrid attacks.

She said the bloc ⁠would soon propose a "new European Security Strategy" to reinforce preparedness and deterrence.

The European Commission also plans ‌shortly to present ​its 22nd sanctions package against Russia over ‌Moscow's full-scale invasion of Ukraine nearly five ​years ago. Visa restrictions are among the measures being discussed.
