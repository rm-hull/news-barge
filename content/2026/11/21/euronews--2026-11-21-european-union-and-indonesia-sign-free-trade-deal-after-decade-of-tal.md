## Trade between the EU and Indonesia was valued at €28.9 billion in 2025, with EU exports worth €10.2 billion and EU imports worth €18.7 billion.

The European Union and Indonesia on Saturday signed off on trade and investment agreements that will lift 98.5% of tariffs on EU goods following a decade of negotiations.

"The agreements will create new opportunities for businesses on both sides and deliver significant economic benefits," the European Commission said in a press release, adding that exports to the Southeast Asian country were projected to increase by nearly 40% within 15 years.

It added that the deal would help save EU exporters more than €600 million a year in customs duties.

The deal is expected to be particularly beneficial for sectors including machinery and motor vehicles.

It is also slated to open up new investment opportunities into Indonesia in areas such as electric vehicles, electronics and pharmaceuticals, the Commission said.

The European Parliament will need to approve the agreements, while Indonesia will also undertake its own ratification procedures. If confirmed, the agreements could apply from early next year.

Trade between the EU and Indonesia was valued at €28.9 billion in 2025, with EU exports worth €10.2 billion and EU imports worth €18.7 billion.

Speaking ahead of the signing ceremony, European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen called it a "momentous day" for the EU and Indonesia.

"This is one of the most ambitious deals the European Union has concluded in Asia. It connects our Union of 450 million people with the largest economy in Southeast Asia, and the fourth most populous country in the world," she said.

"The agreement will remove duties on almost all trade between us. This opens new opportunities for European and Indonesian companies, farmers, workers, and especially for small businesses," von der Leyen added.
