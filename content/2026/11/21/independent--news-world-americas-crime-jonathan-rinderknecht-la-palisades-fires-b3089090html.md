A man accused of starting last year's deadly Palisades Fire has been found guilty on federal arson charges on Friday, after an earlier trial this year ended in a mistrial.

Jurors convicted 30-year-old Jonathan Rinderknecht of lighting a blaze on 1 January 2025 that smouldered undetected deep within root systems before flaring back up on 7 January, becoming one of the most destructive wildfires in California history. The Palisades Fire killed 12 people as it incinerated entire neighbourhoods across Los Angeles and Malibu.

Rinderknecht had pleaded not guilty to federal charges of destruction of property by means of fire and arson. The scope of the initial charge was narrowed to cover only the 1 January blaze, known as the Lachman Fire, whereas the second charge addressed the Palisades Fire. He was convicted on both counts.

Prosecutors pursued the retrial after Rinderknecht's first jury deadlocked 10 to 2 in favour of acquittal following a two-week trial in June. They dropped a third charge of timber set afire upon filing a fresh indictment for the new case.

For the second trial, prosecutors mounted a more tightly focused case featuring digital forensic data, expert witness testimony, and evidence of Rinderknecht’s state of mind in the hours prior to the fire. They placed less emphasis on his resentment towards the rich and powerful, which they had previously framed as arson driven by "societal revenge".

Focusing heavily on evidence placing him at the location where the fire originated, prosecutors presented photographs showing his car parked at the base of a trail leading up to the site alongside mobile phone mast data tracking his movements on the hill. Images from fire detection cameras established the ignition window, while expert witnesses excluded all other potential sources of ignition, including fireworks.

They established that Rinderknecht was acting erratic and angry in the hours leading up to the fire through the testimony of Uber passengers and texts sent to his girlfriend at the time.

“He was mad at everything, and frustrated,” said Assistant U.S. Attorney Mark Williams.

After the fire began, Rinderknecht called 911 more than a dozen times as he walked down the hill. Prosecutors presented the video he screen-recorded on his phone while calling. As he was calling, he asked ChatGPT: “Are you at fault if a fire is lit because of your cigarettes?”

These were part of an alibi Rinderknecht was trying to create for himself, prosecutors said.

They also presented extensive testimony on how fire investigators determined the Palisades Fire was a holdover fire from the initial blaze Rinderknecht sparked. Jurors were instructed not to consider the Los Angeles Fire Department’s role in putting out the fire.

Rinderknecht’s defense attorney Steve Haney casted doubt on the government’s case and said that their evidence proves nothing.

Haney said the fire scene could have been compromised because the area was accessible to the public until the Palisades Fire started Jan. 7.

“Six days of anyone and everyone walking across the spot the government now calls a crime scene,” he said.

The defense called their own expert witnesses, including a retired fire investigator for the U.S. Forest Service who argued the fire origin area was different from what the government concluded.

Haney argued the government could not conclusively say there was no one else by the fire ignition point that night because of missing cell data records, and pointed to a neighbor’s testimony that he saw a group of teenagers running away from the direction of the trailhead.

He told jurors to disregard any evidence related to Rinderknecht’s character, such as his angry text messages, because they were “evidence of the man, not evidence of the act.”

Finally, Haney presented an alternate explanation for what ignited the fire — fireworks. Multiple witnesses, including two neighbors and a local firefighter, all said they heard a loud bang or explosion from the direction of the hill the fire purportedly started on.

In the thousands of images of the evening collected from cameras in the area, none showed fireworks in the sky above the hill where the fire started, prosecutors said.
