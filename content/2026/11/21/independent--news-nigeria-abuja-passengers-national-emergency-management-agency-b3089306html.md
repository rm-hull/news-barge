A boat carrying people to a funeral has capsized in southern Nigeria, killing at least 24 people, emergency services said on Saturday.

The boat was taking passengers from one community to another for a burial ceremony when it sank on Friday in Bayelsa State in the country’s South South region, Nigeria’s National Emergency Management Agency spokesman Manzo Ezeki said in a statement.

He said that search and rescue operations were ongoing without specifying how many people were missing.

Police spokesman Musa Mohammed said on Friday that another 24 people had been rescued and sought medical assistance.

Authorities haven't said how many passengers were believed to have been on board.

Mohammed said that preliminary reports suggested that the boat suffered a mechanical problem.

Passengers asked the boat operator to steer toward land, but he allegedly declined, according to Mohammed. The boat operator then reportedly fled the scene, while the manager of the boat was taken into custody.

The Bayelsa state government responded by urging boat operators and passengers to adhere to safety regulations.

“Overloading, speeding, recklessness and traveling without life jackets must stop. No journey is worth a life,” Bayelsa Commissioner for Information Ebiuwou Koku-Obiyai said in a statement on Saturday.

Boat tragedies are common in Nigeria. A lack of enforcement of safety regulations means faulty boats without life jackets sometimes operate in the country.
