## The “no war, no peace” US-Iran confrontation appears to be moving towards a possible breaking point as Iran increases its military actions in the Gulf and the Strait of Hormuz, while its critical attempt to break the US military naval blockade in the Persian Gulf appears to be imminent.

Iran launched two waves of drone and ballistic missile attacks on Saturday night against four US bases in Bahrain, home to the US’ Fifth Fleet headquarters, the Iranian media reported, while Bahrain announced the attacks, warning the population to shelter.

Iran’s semi-official Fars news agency claimed that the Iranian military targeted four US bases in Bahrain, with two of the named as Sheikh Isa and Juffair, in what it called a response to continued US incursions in Iran’s airspace.

Iran’s Revolutionary Guards Corps (IRGC) claimed that it struck 2 Patriot defence batteries and a radar system. Bahrain and the US military did not confirm the claims.

Bahrain said that its air defence intercepted several Iranian attacks, calling them a “heinous Iranian aggression”.

Local sources in Bahrain reported loud explosions and Bahrain’s Interior ministry announced that a fire broke out at a civilian industrial compound, with no injuries reported, while local videos showed smoke rising from a fire in an unidentified location.

Right after the attack on Bahrain, reports emerged from southern Iran of explosions in Sirik. The US military has retaliated regularly against Iranian military facilities in recent weeks.

Earlier on Saturday, US Central Command CENTCOM denied Iranian claims that a surface-to-air missile damaged a US F-16 fighter jet on Friday evening.

Meanwhile, at least three oil tankers were attacked in or near the Strait of Hormuz on Saturday in what appeared to be the latest Iranian strikes on shipping through the strategic chokepoint.

In a sharp increase of incidents in the Strait of Hormuz over the last week, the UK military’s maritime security center (UKMTO) said two tankers were struck separately by projectiles early Saturday while exiting the Persian Gulf through the strait. There were no reports of casualties.

The Iranian navy said in a statement carried on state media that a tanker hit a naval mine east of the strait and caught fire. State media also said that the military carried out its most “intense and varied” operations yet in the strait overnight to stop vessels.

Iran’s army spokesman Brigadier General Mohammad Akraminia warned the US again on Saturday that any attack on Iran will be met with “a decisive and immediate response” as “we are much stronger than in the past,” Iranian TV IRIB report.

“Any new aggression will be met with a decisive, immediate and effective response from the armed forces, and the defence of the country will not be limited to a specific geographical area,” Akraminia said during a visit to Iranian troops in the south of the country.

The Iranian TV showed for the first time the ground troops deployed into defensive positions in the coastal areas of southern Iran on Saturday.

Meanwhile on Saturday, as more than 14 Iranian tankers are reported to be prepared off Kharg Island to break out of the US military blockade in the coming days, Iran’s deputy minister of foreign affairs Kazem Gharibabadi told Iranian media that the US blockade of Iranian ports “is an act of war and we will take any necessary measures to break this economic siege.”

Iran warned of a “fierce military response” if the convoy was engaged by the US military, threatening with widespread attacks on energy infrastructures across the Gulf region.

Iran’s president Masoud Pezeshkian also said that Iran is subject to a “silent economic war” which is “tougher than a war with missiles,” adding that Iran has the capability to withstand it.

As Gharibabadi announced that Tehran has warned the United States that the Iranian convoy will sail soon to China, the Washington-based Institute for the Study of War said that “Iranian attempts to run the blockade impose a series of dilemmas on the United States by either collapsing the blockade or returning to a war that imposes unideal political costs on the United States by straining its relationships with Gulf allies.”

Against the backdrop of the intensifying crisis, the diplomatic negotiations between the US and Iran spearheaded by Qatar continue.

Iran’s deputy foreign minister Gharibabadi announced on Saturday that a Qatari delegation held talks with Iranian officials on Friday evening for some two hours, before returning to Doha.

Iranian Foreign minister Abbas Araghchi suggested that a deal is possible when he declared that reopening the Strait of Hormuz would be “not difficult” if the US accepted Iran’s “reasonable conditions,” especially by ending the blockade which he called “completely illegal,” lifting sanctions and Washington acknowledging “past mistakes.”

In parallel, US president Donald Trump told US network Fox News on Saturday that he planned to make a “permanent” deal with Iran to prevent it from acquiring a nuclear weapon and that he was never interested in a regime change in Iran.

“I intend to reach a permanent deal with Iran that prevents them from obtaining nuclear weapons, even if another president takes my place in the future and is unfit for the office,” Trump said.
