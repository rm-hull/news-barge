Italy has given provisional approval to Tesla's Assisted Driving System (TAD), Transport Minister Matteo Salvini said on Friday, ⁠making it the first major European Union country to embrace the technology developed by the U.S. electric vehicle maker.

In a post on X, Salvini ⁠said the decision ​was "another ⁠step forward toward innovation, technology and the mobility of the future".

The green light ⁠followed "a thorough technical feasibility assessment", the minister said, ​and ⁠came ahead of ‌an ongoing European Union approval process.

Tesla's CEO Elon Musk wrote back on X: "Thank you, much appreciated!"

TAD, ‌formerly known as Full ‌Self-Driving (FSD), is a so-called 'hands-off, eyes-on' system, that allows a vehicle to drive autonomously while requiring continuous supervision from ⁠a behind the steering-wheel.

Several smaller EU countries have already taken the same step as Italy.

After receiving initial approval in the Netherlands in April, the system was subsequently recognised in countries including Lithuania, Estonia, Denmark, and Belgium, allowing Tesla ‌to expand its footprint ahead of ​a broader EU vote.

![A Tesla electric car drives near the up market Via del Corso shopping area on March 12, 2025 in Rome, Italy](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/21/14/2204717928..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Tesla ‌said on X ⁠on Friday that TAD had been ⁠approved in Italy, posting a video from inside one of ‌its cars ​navigating the streets of ‌Rome in autonomous mode.

The news comes as Tesla will wind down Cybertruck production at its Texas gigafactory around mid-2027, Musk said, after years of persistently weak sales for a futuristic model once touted as a rival to legacy pickup trucks.

The move is a major retreat from what has been one of Musk's pet ⁠projects. In an X post, Musk said the wind-down would let Tesla make more of the Cybercab, a robotaxi he has made central to the automaker's autonomous-vehicle ambitions, but one that has yet to prove itself commercially.

The announcement comes at the end of a bruising week for Musk, as his record political spending of nearly half a billion dollars yielded little in Tuesday's US midterm elections, with the vast majority of the Republican candidates he backed losing their races.

Combined, his companies were on track to lose more than $180 billion in market capitalization for the week.

While it was not immediately clear whether Cybertruck production would be moved to another facility or phased ⁠out entirely, the automaker's Austin plant is the only known Tesla facility equipped to manufacture the specialized components ​for the heavy, ⁠angular model.

In response to an X user who said it was the "end of an era" and described the Cybertruck as "the best vehicle Tesla has ever made," Musk replied "True, but the Cybertruck fleet will be around for a long time."

Tesla has been shifting its focus to robotics and autonomy, a transition analysts say comes at the expense ⁠of the electric-vehicle business it helped pioneer as Chinese rivals gain ground.

![The Tesla Cybercab electric vehicle is exhibited during the press preview of the 2026 Los Angeles Auto Show in Los Angeles, on November 19, 2026](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/21/14/2305374924..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The Cybercab, which has butterfly doors and no steering wheel, pedals or mirrors, is one of Musk's key bets in ​his push to turn Tesla into an AI and robotics company rather than just an automaker.

Musk has said he sees it as Tesla's main autonomous model supplying a global ‌network of robotaxis.

After years of delays to Musk's self-driving targets, Tesla began offering paid ⁠rides in a small fleet of Cybercabs in Austin in September. However, the automaker still has to answer the US auto safety agency's questions on whether it has ⁠properly self-certified Cybercab to demonstrate compliance with federal motor vehicle safety standards.

The National Highway Traffic Safety Administration opened an audit in September to review the basis for that certification.

"Cybertruck was an incredible technology development platform and lives on through Cybercab," Cybertruck lead engineer ‌Wes Morrill said on X.

However, the Cybercab is unlikely ​to make up for lost Cybertruck volumes soon. The two-seater robotaxi is still a small program, with ‌production beginning only earlier this year. Tesla has listed an installed capacity of ​more than 125,000 units.

About 570 of the more than 1,200 Tesla robotaxis operating in Texas are Cybercabs, with the rest mainly Model Y vehicles, according to the state's Department of Motor Vehicles.
