**EVER wondered how long it takes to walk around York Christmas Markets on a weekend? Our reporter put it to the test today.**

Despite the bitterly cold weather, tens of thousands crammed into the city centre today (November 21) as they visited York’s Christmas Market for its second weekend.

Starting last Thursday (November 12, 2026), the five-and-a-half weeklong event will run until the Monday before Christmas (December 21) and attracts people from across the world who visit York to enjoy the city’s festive charms.

Located on Parliament Street, close to 70 vendors in alpine style chalets sell hot food, drinks, festive items, and more with little room to move for the throngs of people waiting in line to get their hands on a hot chocolate or bratwurst.

On social media, people were quick to complaint about the obstructions in place from the crowds with some saying that it takes them up to half an hour to cross the area on during the busiest days of the Christmas Market.

The Press decided to put this to the test and film how long it takes to make a loop of the stalls – starting and ending at Browns Department Store on the edge of St Sampson’s Square.

All in all, the journey took around eight and a half minutes with the side of Parliament Street containing Wendy’s, the former Make It York shop, and Barclays Bank taking almost exactly three minutes to walk down.

The narrower side of Parliament Street – past M&S, Pret, Church Street – took over five minutes to pass, with most of the crowds bunching up around key junctions for Shambles Market and Church Street.

Whilst this journey was not fast, it was not as slow as expected for the time of day (around 2pm) with heavier crowds spotted in other areas of the city, such as Stonegate and Low Petergate.

Outside of York city centre, shoppers seeking more room at Monks Cross and Vangarde Shopping Park had to jostle with thousands of football fans as York City FC are currently playing at home against Walsall.

Traffic was also busy on the roads with traffic lining up down Piccadilly and many other city centre streets gridlocked with cars.
