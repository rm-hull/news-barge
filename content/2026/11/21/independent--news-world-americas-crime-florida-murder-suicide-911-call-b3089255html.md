Before a Florida father allegedly killed his wife and three children and himself, a horrifying 911 call captured one of the children pleading for their life.

The Hillsborough County Sheriff's Office identified the family as Isaac Luechtefeld, 34, his wife, Raquel Luechtefeld, 34, and their children, Enoch, 5, Hannah, 3, and Job, 1. Investigators believe Isaac shot his family inside their Plant City home before killing himself on Thursday.

Dispatchers received a 911 call from inside the home on Sparkman Road, about 25 miles east of Tampa, around 3:30 p.m. Thursday and heard what sounded like gunshots.

“It’s an open line, and we can hear a male’s voice say, ‘Sorry, buddy.’ And we hear a loud bang,” Hillsborough County Sheriff Chad Chronister said during a news conference Thursday. An open-line call connects to 911 without the caller speaking, allowing dispatchers to hear background sounds.

"In the distance, we can hear a small child begging for his life," Chronister continued.

![Hillsborough County Sheriff Chad Chronister detailed a chilling 911 call made Thursday from the Florida home before a father allegedly killed his wife and three children, then himself](https://static.the-independent.com/2026/11/21/19/35/Work-900-px-x-600-px-(11).png?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The child could then be heard pleading, “Daddy, please don't shoot me. Daddy, please don't kill me,” the sheriff said. The child repeated the pleas three or four times before dispatchers heard another loud bang, Chronister said.

Officials have not released the actual audio of the 911 call despite sharing these details.

Deputies arrived at the home around 3:40 p.m., just 11 minutes after the 911 came through.

As they approached the scene, officers discovered one of the children lying lifeless on the floor and then forced their way inside, Chronister said.

They found all five family members dead from gunshot wounds. Investigators believe Isaac shot the three children before killing his wife and then turning the gun on himself.

“When you become a deputy sheriff, you expect to see awful things, but what the deputies encountered here today is nothing short of pure evil,” Chronister said.

Investigators have not established a motive and are still trying to piece together what led to the killings.

![The family had lived in their Plant City home for about a year, and deputies had never been called there before](https://static.the-independent.com/2026/11/21/19/09/Screenshot-2026-11-21-at-2-50-18-PM.png?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Chronister said the family had lived at the home for about a year, and deputies had never been called there during that time. Neither parent had a criminal history, he added.

The tragedy also left first responders and the 911 dispatcher shaken. They will be required to “take a time out” and will be connected with the department’s peer support team and other resources to help them process what they experienced, Chronister said.

“There are no words that can capture the depth of this tragedy,” Chronister said in a statement released by the sheriff's office.

“A family has been lost, and our hearts are with their loved ones, friends, and everyone in our community who knew them,” he said.

The sheriff also urged anyone struggling emotionally to reach out for help, particularly with the holiday season approaching.

“I’m making the plea to all of you. If you need help, simply have the courage and strength to ask for it,” he said.
