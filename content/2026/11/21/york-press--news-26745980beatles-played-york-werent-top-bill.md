## The night The Beatles played York - but weren't top of the bill

A young Paul McCartney signing autographs outside The Rialto in York in 1963. >(Image: The Press)</i>

We look back at the night The Beatles played York but weren't top of the bill.

The idea of The Beatles appearing near the bottom of a concert poster seems almost unthinkable today.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21740842.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The Beatles having a sneak peak through a window at The Rialto in York in1963. *(Image: The Press)*

But on March 13, 1963, John Lennon, Paul McCartney, George Harrison and Ringo Starr arrived at York’s Rialto as part of a packed bill topped by American singers Chris Montez and Tommy Roe.

Our photo of the surviving poster is a fascinating snapshot of a moment just before Beatlemania exploded.

It advertises “one night only” at the Fishergate venue, with two performances, at 6.40pm and 8.45pm. Chris Montez and Tommy Roe take pride of place, while Britain’s “dynamic” Beatles appear towards the bottom, alongside acts including Debbie Lee and The Viscounts.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21740761.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Poster from the Beatles' March 1963 gig at the Rialto in York. *(Image: (Image Supplied))*

Tickets started at just 4/6.

Yet within months, the pecking order would look very different.

The Beatles visited the Rialto four times during 1963, first hitting the York stage on February 27 as a support act to Helen Shapiro.

![The Rialto in 1961 (Image Archive image)](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21740760.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The Rialto in 1961 - just two years before welcoming The Beatles. *(Image: The Pres)*

Shapiro was already an established star, but illness meant she had to pull out of the York performance. The Beatles didn't.

The following day, The Evening Press described how “screams of delight” had greeted the Liverpool quartet as they performed songs including Love Me Do and Please Please Me.

Among those watching was 13-year-old Lynn Martin, who years later told The Press: “You couldn’t hear anything because of all the screaming but we could see them on the stage.

“You just couldn’t believe that there they were in front of you.”

The Beatles returned on March 13 and May 29 before making their fourth and final Rialto appearance on November 27 - with that date this year marking the 63rd anniversary at their visit.

By then everything had changed.

From Me To You and She Loves You had both reached number one and the group who had appeared below other stars on York bills earlier that year were now the main attraction.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21740757.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Louis Armstrong appeared at the Rialto in York in 1932. Jack Prendergast, the Rialto promoter, is on the left (Image Archive image) *(Image: The Press)*

The Rialto itself had already built an extraordinary musical pedigree. Under proprietor Jack Prendergast, father of composer John Barry, the venue had previously welcomed performers including Louis Armstrong, Lonnie Donegan and Cliff Richard, as well as Count Basie, Duke Ellington and Sarah Vaughan.

And the stars kept coming. In 1964, the venue was visited by a young Mick Jagger, pictured reading York music magazine Ouse Beat.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/12689402.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Mick Jagger reading York music magazine Ouse Beat at the Rialto in 1964. *(Image: The Press)*

The Rialto has long since disappeared, but its place in York’s musical history remains.

The Rialto itself is long gone. The former cinema and concert hall was replaced by a purpose-built Mecca Bingo hall, which was later demolished as the Fishergate site entered another chapter in its long history.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21740759.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Rialto in Fishergate in 1929. *(Image: The Press)*

Today, the site looks very different again. A £50 million, four-storey development called Rialto House was completed on the former Rialto Cinema and Mecca Bingo site, providing 275 student rooms.

Developed by Olympian Homes, funded by Cain International and built by GMI Construction, its name at least preserves a link with the entertainment venue where York audiences once watched music history being made.

And that old March 1963 poster captures something particularly special: a fleeting moment when The Beatles could still arrive in York without being the biggest name on the bill.
