Homes, commercial sites and farmland bought to make way for a now-scrapped HS2 route are finally being sold back to the public, *The Independent* can reveal.

A total of 562 properties were acquired for £169m by the Department for Transport for the Birmingham to Leeds leg of the project called Phase 2b eastern leg.

Many have since been rented out – and others simply left empty – as the government assessed their future after the section of 123-mile line was axed more than three years ago.

But now the government has confirmed that five empty homes – four in Derbyshire and one in Nottinghamshire – are being placed on the market as part of a pilot to a strategy to offload the no-longer-required estate.

Past owners who were forced to sell due to statutory blight from the then-planned HS2 line will get the first opportunity to buy back their former homes.

However, the homes will be sold at market rate – meaning the price of properties are likely to be higher than when they were purchased by the government after the planned route was announced in 2017.

At a recent Public Accounts Committee (PAC) meeting, transport bosses faced a grilling over the time being taken to sell off the properties.

MP Rupert Lowe, a committee member who is also the leader of Restore Britain, said: “The decision was made in October 2023 that we were not going to go ahead, so a lot of this land is now surplus to requirement. Why has it taken so long for it to be returned.... I think, having messed up people’s lives, it is incumbent on the state to put right as quickly as possible.”

Dean Creamer, DfT’s director general major rail project, said the government had needed to draw up a strategy after the lifting of safeguarding restrictions on the eastern leg route last year.

He said: “The challenge we will have, and the balanced judgment we have to make, is that we want to get properties disposed of, but we do not want to flood the market and undermine the local housing markets for local residents, and we want to protect the taxpayer.”

Along with the eastern leg of HS2, the Crewe to Manchester stretch known as the Phase 2b western leg was also cancelled three years ago. But unlike the eastern leg, where properties will be sold, safeguarding restrictions remain after the government outlined in January plans for rail link between Manchester and Birmingham.

!['Stop HS2' posters in homes at the Shimmer housing development in Mexborough, Doncaster, where many homes were purchased for the cancelled HS2 line](https://static.independent.co.uk/s3fs-public/thumbnails/image/2017/07/17/18/hs2-houses-demolished.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

On the western leg, some 210 properties were bought by the government at a cost of £228m.

Around 60 per cent of properties bought up for HS2 are rented out, Mr Creamer told the PAC meeting in September.

He said: “The government does not want to get into the business of being a property developer or manager here, but where we are holding assets, we need to do it in a sensible and well-balanced way where we rent out as much as possible. We will look to maximise that at market rates as far as possible, without fundamentally spending more taxpayer money than we absolutely need to.”

Meanwhile, work continues to build the HS2 line from London to Birmingham, with opening not expected until at least 2036. The expected cost of completing the high-speed railway is now between £87.7bn and £102.7bn,

Darwin Friend, research director at the TaxPayers' Alliance, said: "It's absolutely right that the Department for Transport now looks to sell these properties but this is another damning indictment of the HS2 white elephant. The government splashed vast sums of taxpayers' cash buying up land and homes for track that will never be laid, bringing huge disruption and uncertainty to families and communities.

"Ministers must move quickly to offload these properties.”

A DfT spokesperson said: “Following cancellation of the eastern leg of HS2 Phase 2b by the previous government we are beginning to sell land and property no longer required for the scheme on a pilot basis from October, helping bring years of uncertainty to an end for local communities.

“Some previous owners of properties acquired along the cancelled line will start to be given the opportunity to buy back their former property at its current market value. Ahead of this, a small number of vacant properties are now being placed on the market.”
