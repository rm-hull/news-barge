As reported by The Shanghai Observer and Automaton, authorities in Shanghai have arrested 13 people and seized an untold number of unauthorized model kits and figures based on pop culture properties like Final Fantasy. The cops were reportedly put on the trail by a suspect Tifa Lockheart figurine.

Police in Fengxian, a district of Shanghai, began receiving complaints in June. The Shanghai Observer showed pictures comparing a Play Arts Kai (Square Enix's premium figure line) Tifa with a seemingly less articulated counterpart in the same outfit. Reading through machine translation, it sounds like customers in the area bought these figures online expecting the genuine article.

The leader of the operation reportedly commissioned a Guangzhou factory to produce the false Tifa and her ilk. They even sold some figures at higher prices than the genuine article, offering added accessories or features not available in the official product.

I'm of two minds about this, personally: I'm generally pro-kitbashing, selling custom figures, and using proxies in trading card games. In the face of some seriously inflated pricing from official merch lines, bootlegs can often be more enticing than the real deal, particularly if they offer features you can't get from official products⁠—the added customization options from this arrested vendor actually sound weirdly pro-consumer.

But it certainly appears, at least from The Shanghai Observer's reporting, that these suspect figures were being presented as the genuine article to customers. Paired with the increased price vs. official merch, and I find myself soured on the whole operation. It may seem small and silly, but as we endure the Golden Age of Scams, it's weirdly nice to see scammers face *some*consequences* somewhere*.
