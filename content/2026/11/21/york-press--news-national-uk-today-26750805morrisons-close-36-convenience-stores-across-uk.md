Morrisons has announced plans to close 36 convenience stores across the UK.

The latest closures affect former McColl’s sites and follow a review of the retailer’s convenience estate.

Consultations are now underway with affected employees.

These closures represent around two per cent of the supermarket chain’s network of 1,700 convenience stores.

A Morrisons spokesperson said: "The performance of our company-owned Morrisons Daily convenience stores continues to improve as they benefit from the changes to range, customer service and logistics we have implemented this year."

They added that the latest closures were necessary because a small number of stores have continued to underperform despite efforts to improve their performance.

The announcement comes just six months after Morrisons revealed plans to close 100 convenience stores, a move that put hundreds of jobs at risk.

Many of the affected stores were part of Morrisons’ £190 million rescue of McColl’s in 2022 and have struggled to return to profitability.

## Morrisons launches new £1 bakery item in 400 stores

Morrisons has launched a new limited-edition item in its bakery, which will be available until just after Christmas.

The supermarket has added a £1 Bear Paw Cob to the selection, which is a giant, paw-shaped loaf of bread.

It is inspired by Morrisons' Christmas advert this year, which was a festive reimagining of Goldilocks and the Three Bears.

The supermarket added: "Each doughy paw is handcrafted from scratch by Morrisons expert bakers and baked fresh daily at its Market Street bakery counters, ensuring every bite is just right.

"Priced at just £1, the novelty bake is designed for tear and share fun, and is paw-fect for pairing alongside festive cheeses, hearty dips, or Christmas buffet spreads."

The Morrisons Bear Paw Cob is available in over 400 bakeries until Sunday, December 27.

The closure programme began in July with stores in Woodley, Berkshire, and Stokesley, North Yorkshire.

Morrisons had previously cited rising employment costs, including increases to the national living wage and employer National Insurance contributions, as key factors hindering the turnaround of underperforming locations.

Despite the closures, Morrisons reported improving sales figures, with like-for-like revenue up by 3.2 per cent in the 13 weeks to July 26.

The company said it would seek to redeploy affected employees into alternative roles within the wider business where possible.
