A historic house in York is celebrating the festive season with a charming display.

Fairfax House is once again filled with festive mice for its annual Christmas event, this year themed A Christmas Tail: The Town Mouse and The Country Mouse.

Inspired by Aesop’s fable, the display follows a family of country mice visiting their city cousins for a season of Georgian feasting, parlour games and merriment.

Many mice are dressed in hand-crafted and exquisitely miniature Georgian finery, custom-made by Fairfax House’s volunteers.

Sarah Burnage, curator at Fairfax House, said: “We drew inspiration from our amazing stuccowork ceilings in the House, which includes in the Library a portrait of the famous eighteenth-century author Alexander Pope.

"Being represented on the ceiling suggests Pope was one of the Fairfaxes' favourite literary figures.

"Pope wrote an interpretation of Aesop's classic fable 'The Town Mouse and the County Mouse" so it seems a fitting nod to the House's former residents and their tastes."

On a wander through the House, there are all sorts of Georgian amusements brought to life, with feasts, fashion, bargains at the Frost Fair and a lavish ball. There are stunning examples of Georgian fashion to discover, offering a wonderful glimpse into 18th-century style.

Dr Burnage said: “We absolutely love this time of year when the house is dressed for a Georgian Christmas and overrun with our mischievous mice. We really hope visitors enjoy what we’ve created this year - we’ve certainly enjoyed making it.”

The exhibition runs until January 3, with special candlelit evenings on December 16, 18 and 21.

During these evenings, the house will be illuminated by candlelight to evoke the atmosphere of an intimate Georgian gathering.

Tickets are priced at £9.50 for adults and £8.50 for concessions, with free entry for children under 16.

To book tickets, visit the Fairfax House website.
