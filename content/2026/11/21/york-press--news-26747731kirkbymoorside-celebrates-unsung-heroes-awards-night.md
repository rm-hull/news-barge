## Town honours 'unsung heroes' at annual community awards night

The winners were presented with an ironwork silhouette of the Kirkbymoorside skyline, mounted on sycamore. This bespoke award was made by local metalwork artist, Mike Bennett of MUST Forge, Lockton.
The event was sponsored by Rack Systems Engineering *(Image: Martin Sheppard)*

A North Yorkshire town has celebrated the achievements of its unsung heroes at a community awards night.

The 2026 Kirkbymoorside Community Awards, organised by the town council, concluded with a ceremony at the Memorial Hall on Saturday, October 31, bringing together around 120 guests to honour the people who help shape the community.

Joe Coughlan, mayor of Kirkbymoorside, said: "We are thrilled to have organised this now annual event recognising the kindness of our residents making a positive difference to the fabric of the Kirkbymoorside community.

"We look forward to building on the success of this again next year."

Guests were welcomed with live jazz music from John Lane and Richard Hayes.

The evening also included the unveiling of a painting by Lewis Creighton, donated by Chris Feather, former proprietor of The Feathers in Helmsley, and a celebration of Kirkbymoorside in Bloom’s recent Royal Horticultural Society commendation of five Golden Roses.

Angus Ashworth of Ryedale Auctioneers hosted the awards ceremony, beginning with the Window Display of the Year category.

Lesley Temple, marking 60 years at Kitching Walker Solicitors, presented the Lex Cup to Children in Distress for their creative window displays, most recently with a Hallowe’en theme.

The 10K Committee was named Voluntary/Charity Organisation of the Year.

The group organises the annual May race, which draws runners from across the region while promoting fitness and raising funds for Kirkbymoorside and Gillamoor Primary Schools.

William (Bill) Breakell was named Individual/Quiet Contributor of the Year for his long-standing efforts to improve public transport.

Mr Breakell is the driving force behind the Moorsbus service, applying his professional experience and networks to manage fundraising, planning, and day-to-day operations.

Young Person of the Year went to Jess Ackroyd of Kirkbymoorside Primary School.

Jess regularly attended the school’s lunchtime running club in preparation for the 10K event and showed such dedication that she also joined additional evening training sessions.

Despite running with an ankle injury, she completed the 10K in her best time.

The council’s highest honour, the Overall Community Achievement Award, was given to Sheila Ridley.

Ms Ridley managed the Memorial Hall for 30 years until her retirement in 2025, and the council praised her dedication to keeping the hall open to the community and running the weekly markets.

All winners received a bespoke ironwork silhouette of the Kirkbymoorside skyline, crafted by Lockton artist Mike Bennett of MUST Forge.

The event was sponsored by Rack Systems Engineering Ltd and Smailes Goldie.

The evening ended with the unveiling of the Honours Board, which lists all past and current award recipients.

The board was made by cabinet maker Tom Trimmins, with lettering by Paul Gamble, and will be permanently displayed in the Memorial Hall.

Kirkbymoorside Town Council represents the most local tier of government and is responsible for reflecting the interests and needs of the community.
