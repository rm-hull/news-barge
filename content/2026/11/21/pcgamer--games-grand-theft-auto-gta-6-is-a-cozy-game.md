GTA 6 has dragged us out of our comfort zone here at PC Gamer, forcing us to play a game on a console. A tragedy! But it's OK because GTA 6, as you may have heard, is rather good. And it's especially good when the shooting stops and you just get to hang out with your weird crime pals.

This seems to be the consensus among my colleagues in crime, who I've gathered to share their favourite parts of GTA 6's early game—fishing, interior decorating and chilling with some problematic faves.

![Relaxing in GTA 6](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/GcwjmKnoBRXuShvZqnoCVD-1200-80.jpg)

![PC Gamer headshot - Fraser Brown](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/o5oYVAFnHjesaVgoJsQD9E-140-80.png)

I've been playing GTA since the top-down days, but with every new incarnation of Rockstar's criminal caper I've been drifting away from the action and learning to appreciate life in the slow lane. I have two wolves inside me, and in GTA 6, with its potent life sim DNA, the wolf who just wants to knock back beers with my virtual buddies is soundly beating the one who keeps screaming, "Do more crime!"

Guys, GTA 6 is a cosy game. It's a restorative holiday. I wake up and meditate with my partner before she goes to work. I do a bit of lifting. Watch some TV. Then it's time for a drive. Maybe I'll buy a new shirt or get a tasteful and definitely not monstrously-huge slab of ink to honour my son at the grotty tattoo parlour. Then the ocean beckons me—it's fishing time! I take some snaps of my catch so I can brag about it to my best friend. Oh, Brian is calling? He wants me to do more crimes? Sorry, Bri! I'm going treasure hunting in my boat and sending my partner thirst traps.

It's all just so pleasantly wholesome. Playing videogames with Cal, fixing up the house with Lucia, doing errands for my eccentric neighbour. Sure, that time I agreed to steal her best friend's mobility scooter definitely falls into the crime caper category, but only slightly. That's been my favourite chase so far—inching down the road while an old lady slowly jogs after me.

GTA's combat is serviceable and sometimes a whole lotta fun (I beat up some dudes with a basketball last night), and I've had a hoot getting stuck into Leonida's coke smuggling scene, but it feels like GTA's most impressive innovations are found in the quieter parts—the way it's constantly getting you to build relationships on your off hours, the wee nods to roleplaying, the believability and vibrancy of Leonida. This has been a crucial part of GTA since the San Andreas days, but in GTA 6 it feels like it's become the focus. I'm extremely happy about this.

![Relaxing in GTA 6](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/5ZZCuTXRbrbjPX2BNa4bBD-1200-80.jpg)

![PC Gamer headshot - Mollie Taylor](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/fJx5QFMzkRdRzZWYkj5h2E-140-80.png)

GTA 6 has so many fun, small ways to make standing around and listening to dialogue more immersive that it almost makes the parts where you actually have to pick up a gun and shoot feel weirdly stagnant. I *love* the missions where you're just shooting the shit—building furniture, buying beers for a pal going through it, playing videogames. I love that I can do things like pour myself a coffee or light a cigarette while I'm having a conversation with another character. I love that I can construct a whole morning routine where I work out, go for a shower, and pick my outfit for the day.

The 'quieter' moments are where Leonida and its inhabitants really shine. I've lost track of how many times I've forgone a mission in favour of stopping to engage in all of the little random encounters—like a woman harassing a drive-thru worker for cheese, or a guy who is so lawsuit-brained that he ventures back into his burning vehicle for a bigger payday. Dilly-dallying has never felt so good.

![GTA 6 protagonist Jason Duval.](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/zBHTPBa3a2HQBFRPhKPqEh-1200-80.jpg)

![PC Gamer headshot - Morgan Park](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/s7GRLBmCqDU2ovJs9ixGcF-140-80.png)

GTA 6's fishing is pretty much unchanged from Red Dead Redemption 2, but I'm already doing it a lot more here because of Jason and Cal's ongoing fishing rivalry. We're catching somewhat big fish, sending pics, and talking trash about how the other guy will never find a bigger one.

It's wonderful and totally optional, which is indicative of how GTA 6 imbues many activities that would otherwise just 'be there' with social incentives. Every task, chore, or hobby involves talking to someone or sharing your day, and yes, it's pretty cosy. And if you're looking for a better fishing reel, check the sports shop near Jason's house.

![Grand Theft Auto 6 launch gallery: A diver nears a zebrafish in the Leonida Keys.](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/6anZmeg4KXNGPLGN9g8xHF-1200-80.jpg)

![PC Gamer headshot - Phil Savage](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/4sHLcbB96zPH9vjoBAiMYC-140-80.png)

I love when a game gives you a broad objective to just go earn money—also known as pulling a Baldur's Gate 2. It's a great excuse to diverge from the main path, to actually spend time getting to know a place without feeling pressured to progress the story in some meaningful way. In my last session, I spent most of my time exploring old shipwrecks for treasure—just me, my diving gear, some lost relics and the occasional shark.

Which, sure, it's an open world game with a lot of things to do on the side. This is not exactly a revelation. But, as always with Rockstar, the difference is in the production value and the attention to detail. The level of immersion is such that it doesn't feel like you're ticking off tasks on a checklist, because everything happens through the filter of Jason and Lucia's day-to-day. Every journey distracts you with surprising interactions and interesting things to check out. Every text message and social post builds the illusion of a place rich with life and detail. And so, yeah, when you finally decide to go do a main quest and the bullets start flying, it's almost disappointing to be back doing the things GTA has done for decades.
