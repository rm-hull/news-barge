Helen Beck, chair of Barnham Residents' Association, with the village's new flag

**Villagers vowed "this is only the beginning" after voting overwhelmingly to "leave the UK" in protest at plans to house migrants at a former airbase.**

Residents in Barnham, Suffolk, held a symbolic poll on Saturday to decide whether or not to declare independence.

The referendum was called after RAF Barnham, near Bury St Edmunds, Suffolk and Thetford, in Norfolk, was earmarked by the government as a site to house up to 931 male asylum seekers.

Two-hundred-and-fifty-seven people - 94% of those who took part – voted in favour of leaving, 16 voted against and one vote was spoiled.

Following the result, which is not legally binding, residents unveiled a flag - a Stone-curlew against a Suffolk blue background - to celebrate the village's newfound "independence".

Barnham is the second village to hold a symbolic poll over migrant housing plans, after residents of Piddington in Oxfordshire voted to leave the UK in September.

John Bauer, chair of the parish council, has said the Suffolk village will now declare itself the Bailiwick of Barnham.

![A sign which reads Polling Station positioned in front of a village hall, which is set behind a mound of grass.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/133c/live/e5f5d2c0-e5da-11f1-9ab8-8737221634d6.jpg)

The voting took place at Barnham Village Hall, as journalists gathered outside

Helen Beck, chair of Barnham Residents' Association, was delighted with both the outcome and the conduct of all involved.

"I'm really very, very pleased, but also pleased the residents who didn't agree [with leaving] felt able to come and vote accordingly," she said.

"I would hate to think we weren't prepared to listen to all voices.

"The community has come together around this, and I'm really, really grateful for everybody's support."

![A head-and-shoulders image of a man wearing glasses and a blue top while standing in front of a village hall and looking into the camera.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/778f/live/2ef315b0-e5da-11f1-9ab8-8737221634d6.jpg)

Ken Jordan said winning the result had given residents a huge boost to campaign even harder against the plans

Campaigner Ken Jordan said the vote marked "just the beginning and the real work starts tomorrow".

He added: "It's raised the profile, and it's given us a starting block now to launch the more serious side of our campaign."

The government first put forward plans to house "single adult male" asylum seekers at RAF Barnham back in June.

The proposals triggered demonstrations outside the former base.

It is part of the government's drive to end the use of hotels to house asylum seekers – a scheme which, at its peak, was reportedly costing £9m per day.

![A head and shoulders image of a woman wearing a cream coat with a brown fur collar. She is standing in front of a village hall and looking into the camera.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/b37b/live/51726a00-e5da-11f1-9ab8-8737221634d6.jpg)

Pat Webb told the BBC she would be scared to leave her home alone if migrants were moved into RAF Barnham

Barnham resident Pat Webb, 69, stressed that the village was "not racist" but claimed it would be "overrun" if asylum seekers were allowed to move onto the base.

"The women in this village are scared," she said.

"I've got a little dog I walk round the village, and I'm not going to be able to do that unless my husband comes with me because I won't dare be out on my own.

"We've had a lovely retirement here so far, and it's all going to change, and for the worse, which is such a shame."

![A head and shoulders image of a woman wearing a purple jumper and a striped scarf. She has sunglasses resting on her head.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/ed04/live/6115b5c0-e5da-11f1-9ab8-8737221634d6.png)

Patricia Gilson has lived in Barnham for "20 odd years"

Patricia Gilson also voted in favour of leaving the UK.

"I'm mainly concerned about all these young men with nothing to do," she said.

"My neighbours with kiddies are worried to death, but the government isn't listening, but you just have to keep battling."

The independence vote attracted national and international media attention.

Zia Yusuf, Reform UK's spokesperson for home affairs, who was in Barnham as the result was announced, said it "tells you everything you need to know about how disenfranchised many people are".

Barnham's Labour MP Peter Prinsley said he was "not surprised".

"I'm due to meet the immigration minister this week when I will express the opposition felt by people in Barnham. I'm still optimistic the decision will be that this is not a suitable place for asylum seekers," he added.

![A head and shoulders image of a woman wearing a green jacket and a white top. She is looking into the camera.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/1bbc/live/715345b0-e5da-11f1-9ab8-8737221634d6.png)

Stephanie Donaldson said the Home Office had not consulted with residents as much as it said it had

Barnham resident Stephanie Donaldson, 31, said the vote had been "to try to draw attention" to the strength of "discontent" in the village.

"We live directly opposite [RAF Barnham] and obviously there's welfare and safety concerns for our children," she said.

"I think if [the facility] was properly secure without [people] being able to wander, perhaps that would've been better."

The government said a final decision had not yet been made, but if it did proceed the "safety and security" of the community would be "of the utmost importance".

A Home Office spokesperson said: "We recognise the strength of feeling in local communities and are listening carefully to the concerns being raised."
