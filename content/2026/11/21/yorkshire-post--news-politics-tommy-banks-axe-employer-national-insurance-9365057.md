# Tommy Banks: axe employer national insurance for under 25s to tackle Neets crisis

## Michelin-starred chef Tommy Banks has criticised the Government for its attitude to the hospitality business.

Andy Burnham should scrap employer national insurance contributions on under 25s if he really wants to tackle the youth unemployment crisis, one of Yorkshire’s leading chefs has said.

Tommy Banks runs the Black Swan in Oldstead and Roots in York, both of which have Michelin stars, as well the Abbey Inn in Byland and the General Tarleton pub near Knaresbrough.

Mr Banks has criticised the Government for their attitude to the hospitality business, particularly over the rise in National Insurance contributions, the minimum wage and business rates.

“Young people are the future of this country and giving them opportunities should be an absolute priority,” he told The Yorkshire Post.

“Hospitality can play a huge part in that. I started as a kitchen porter at the Abbey Inn, which we now own. That first job can open the door to a whole career.”

Earlier this week, Alan Milburn completed his review into youth unemployment, saying it was a “moral crisis” that almost one million young people are not in employment, education or training, the so-called Neets.

He called for a “five-year glide path to lower national insurance costs for workers aged 21 to 24 – starting in the retail and hospitality sectors which together provide 37 per cent of jobs for young people aged 16 to 24”.

The former cabinet minister said while he did not believe “an immediate across-the-board national insurance cut is the best use of several billion pounds of public money”, employers had warned him “higher payroll costs have made recruitment more difficult”.

The rate of employer national insurance rose from 13.8 per cent to 15 per cent in April 2025, and the threshold at which it is paid was cut from £9,100 to £5,000.

Employers do not pay national insurance contributions for their workers aged under 21 unless their salary is above a £50,270 threshold.

“Reducing employer national insurance is absolutely the right thing to do, but I would go further,” he explained.

“Increasing it in the first place was completely the wrong decision. If you increase the cost of employing young people, businesses will employ fewer young people.

“I would scrap employer national insurance altogether for under 25s. That would give businesses a meaningful incentive to recruit them and invest in their development.

“Alongside that, I’d like to see more reward for companies running successful apprenticeship schemes.

“If a business is investing in young people, helping them achieve qualifications and keeping them in meaningful employment, that should be recognised and financially rewarded. We need to incentivise both creating jobs and providing good training.”

However, reports suggest that the Treasury is already pushing back on Mr Milburn’s recommendations around national insurance.

The Financial Times reported that No11 Downing Street insiders have briefed that any changes would be “hugely expensive” and not cost effective, on the grounds that the cut would be untargeted.

Allen Simpson, chief executive of the UK Hospitality trade body, responded: “No NICs [national insurance contributions] cut means no Milburn review, it becomes meaningless.”
