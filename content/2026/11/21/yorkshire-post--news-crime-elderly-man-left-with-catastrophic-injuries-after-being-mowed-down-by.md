# Elderly man left with catastrophic injuries after being mowed down by driver who fled scene and tried to cover tracks

## An elderly man suffered catastrophic injuries after being struck by a car while crossing a Yorkshire high street, with the driver and his passenger later attempting to conceal evidence of the crash.

Luke Scott, 34, of Brickside Way, Northallerton, was jailed for 26 months at Teesside Crown Court on Thursday (Nov 19), after pleading guilty to causing serious injury by careless driving and perverting the course of justice.

His passenger, Hayley Richardson, 34, of Berry Close, Great Yarmouth, pleaded guilty to perverting the course of justice and received a nine-month prison sentence, suspended for 18 months, alongside 80 hours of unpaid work.

At around 8.14pm on Tuesday (Mar 12), 2024, the victim, now aged 81, was crossing Northallerton High Street after an evening out with his wife when he was struck by a black Ford Fiesta driven by Scott.

The impact threw him into the air, leaving him unconscious and suffering devastating injuries, including a brain haemorrhage, multiple skull and facial fractures, and fractures to his pelvis and legs.

Rather than stopping at the scene, Scott and Richardson drove away.

As police launched an investigation, CCTV footage captured the vehicle immediately after the crash.

The following day, Wednesday (Mar 13), officers received several reports of a partially burned Ford Fiesta that appeared to have been abandoned in Northallerton Rigg.

Investigators linked the vehicle's registration and insurance details to Richardson.

![Luke Scott](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/jpim-static/image/2026/11/21/11/18/luke-scott-16121991.jpg?trim=168,4,195,0&width=640&quality=65)


Further enquiries revealed that Scott and Richardson had visited The Buck Inn before the crash, where they were seen drinking alcohol.

Both were subsequently arrested, interviewed and charged.

In a heartbreaking victim personal statement, the man's wife described how the crash had destroyed their retirement and left her husband unable to speak about the impact of his injuries.

She said: “Since that day, our lives have changed completely and bears no resemblance to what it was before. We are no longer able to do any of the things that we previously enjoyed as a couple or as individuals and the disruption that has been caused to us cannot be measured.

“This is not way that either of us anticipated spending our retirement. He is not capable of making his own statement about how his life has been ruined by the callous actions of others. He no longer has the mental ability to do so. I have tried to speak on his behalf as best as I can but I don’t believe I could ever do it justice.”

She continued: “All I can say that is that his life, my life, the lives of our children and our grandchildren have been ruined. Two complete strangers have taken away any enjoyment of life that he might have once had, taken away his hobbies, his friends, his ability to think. Through doing this to him they have also destroyed my life and my enjoyment of it.”

Detective Sergeant Ed Drake, of North Yorkshire Police's Serious Collision Investigation Unit, said: “The selfish and callous actions of these two individuals has caused irreparable damage to not only the victim but to his family and all those who knew and loved him and their lives will never be the same again.

“This case demonstrates our determination to pursue every available line of enquiry to establish the truth and ensure those responsible are held accountable. We will continue to do everything in our power to seek justice for victims and their families, and to bring offenders before the courts, no matter how long it takes.”
