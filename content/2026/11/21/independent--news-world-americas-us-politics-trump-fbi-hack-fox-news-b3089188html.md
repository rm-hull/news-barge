After the Federal Bureau of Investigation was hacked for the second time in recent months, President Donald Trump said “maybe we’ll learn things.”

Speaking by phone to Fox News on Saturday morning, Trump addressed new reporting that the FBI’s National Crime Information Center appeared to have been breached.

The reported hack would mark the second intrusion into an FBI system since September. The alleged hack also follows a string of high-profile technology disruptions affecting Microsoft, JPMorgan Chase and air traffic control systems, among many others.

“Well, we just heard about it, and I will say it’s not good. I don’t like that,” Trump told his former White House press secretary and Fox News personality Kayleigh McEnany. “But maybe we’ll learn things that we would have never learned otherwise.”

The 80-year-old president then ticked through a list of what he called “hoaxes,” referring to claims of Russian interference in the 2016 election and his 2019 impeachment over allegations he pressured Ukraine to investigate Joe Biden.

![President Donald Trump told Fox News ‘maybe we’ll learn things’ after the FBI was hacked for the second time in recent months](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/21/15/download..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“Because you know you had the Russia, Russia, Russia hoax. You had all of these other hoax,” Trump said. “We call it Ukraine. The Ukraine, Ukraine, Ukraine hoax. I was, you know the perfect phone call as they say.”

“You have so many other things, and maybe we’ll learn about them,” he continued. “Maybe we’ll learn about voter fraud, all the voter fraud that took place.”

The president has long claimed that widespread voter fraud plagues U.S. elections and that the 2020 election was “rigged” as a result. Multiple investigations have failed to substantiate those claims, which officials in his own administration also rejected.

“And so I always like to put things in a positive spin,” Trump added.

The suspected breach, first reported by CNN on Friday, targeted a nationwide database that allows federal, state and local law enforcement agencies to share criminal justice information. The FBI had not publicly confirmed the incident or responded to the outlet’s request for comment.

The hack, which the bureau learned about Thursday, came two months after the hacking group ShinyHunters claimed to have stolen sensitive personal information about FBI employees and job applicants from the bureau’s recruitment website.

![FBI Director Kash Patel told Fox News ‘we are staying ahead of the curve and making up for lost time’ after the bureau learned of the latest hack](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/10/19/10192843-16aad49a-7ad2-48ed-b518-ad1ebd9c0a57.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

After the bureau learned of the suspected breach, FBI Director Kash Patel appeared on Fox News on Friday to tout the agency’s security safeguards.

“The FBI and the intelligence community is going to continuously monitor those activities for nefarious actors,” Patel said. “And when and if such an actor comes up, you can rest assured that this FBI is going to hunt them down.”

“We are staying ahead of the curve and making up for lost time,” he added.

The Trump administration has sought in recent days to reassure the public after a string of high-profile technology disruptions grounded flights, locked workers out of their email and left customers unable to access their bank accounts.

The White House and top administration officials have said there is no evidence they were caused by “malicious foreign cyberattacks” or artificial intelligence.

Last week, White House Chief of Staff Stephen Miller reportedly sidelined Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin after he told Fox News that super intelligence — Trump’s preferred term for AI — may have been to blame for the outages, citing “one guy who is very smart with tech.”
