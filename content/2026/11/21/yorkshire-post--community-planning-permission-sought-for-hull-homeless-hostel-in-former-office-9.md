# Planning permission sought for Hull homeless hostel in former office

## Plans revealed for major new homeless shelter near Hull Ice Arena v.1

A large homeless hostel could be built in Hull, close to the city’s Ice Arena, if an ambitious plan is given the green light. Hull City Council has applied for planning permission for the project at the old St James Enterprise Centre.

The building was previously used as an office, studio and classrooms as part of an education and enterprise training facility. But it is believed to have stood empty for a number of years.

Hull City Council’s planning application is for a large-scale homeless facility that will tackle rough sleeping and homelessness in the city. The facility will be central, St James Street being sandwiched between the Clive Sullivan Way and Albert Dock.

![A ten-dormitory homeless shelter could be built in Hull, if the council give a plan the green light: image c/o Google Maps](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOjM5OTc1OGQ0LWYzYjYtNGM0Yi05Mzg3LTFhNzU5YzM0YjRiMDpjZWI2YzI2NS0xNTFhLTQyMjEtYjlkYy03YjkxZTA4NmYwMjk=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


It is not clear exactly how many homeless people the hostel will accommodate. But the application asks for “a change of use of former education centre to create hostel accommodation (emergency bed provision for rough sleepers) including ten dormitories (ground floor) and welfare sanitary facilities (ground and first floor) with associated first floor place of safety.”

Documents submitted to the authority’s planning portal make clear work has already started on the project, noting October 19th 2026 as the day building works began.

### **A stark challenge for Hull’s decision-makers**

Hull City Council’s Joint Strategic Needs Assessment lays bare the stark challenge facing decision-makers on a local level. The waiting lists for accommodation are currently high in Hull with demand significantly outstripping supply.

![Work is underway to develop a large new homeless shelter, close to Hull's Clive Sullivan Way: image c/o Hull City Council planning portal](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOjgwM2MxODUwLTE3NGMtNDAzNS05NDUxLTVkNjU3MmUyZjZkZDo0MzVmOGY1NC1lNGY2LTQ5MTgtYmRiNC1kYTlhNzQwYmQ4Y2E=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


It was estimated that there were 36 rough sleepers in Hull during Autumn 2026, which was approximately double the levels seen before Covid 19 lockdowns.

And the authority’s needs assessment states that over the last three years (2023 to 2025) there were 41 deaths among people in Hull who were experiencing homelessness (rough sleeping or using emergency homelessness accommodation around the time of their death).

“Homelessness is more likely in populations who already experience wider inequalities, for example, those with mental health or substance treatment needs, experiencing domestic abuse, involved with criminal justice system and those with multiple and complex needs, Gypsies and Travellers, sex workers, and migrant workers, refugees and asylum seekers,” the Joint Strategic Needs Assessment explains.

In October it was revealed Hull City Council is to receive almost £700,000 as part of a national drive to get rough sleepers off the streets. The funding award was announced as part of a major push by Andy Burnham.

The Prime Minister hosted a landmark rough sleeping summit at Downing Street, bringing together local council leaders, charities, and senior business figures to coordinate an urgent nationwide response.

Cllr Shane McMurray, who is Hull Labour’s communities spokesperson, said he was hopeful the funding would have a lasting impact on the streets of East Yorkshire: “It reflects Andy Burnham’s genuine commitment to make sure no one is sleeping on the streets this winter. I hope that during this period of stability, people can also receive ongoing support to help improve their future prospects,” he said.

Residents can share feedback on the proposal for the St James Enterprise Centre through Hull City Council’s online planning portal, with a deadline of December 11th for any comments.
