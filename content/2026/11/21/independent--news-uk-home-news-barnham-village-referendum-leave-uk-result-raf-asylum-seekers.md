A village in Suffolk has voted to leave the UK in a symbolic referendum reacting to controversial plans to house asylum seekers on a nearby RAF base.

Barnham held a Yes/No poll asking residents whether they support a petition for “crown dependency status” following a proposal to house 931 asylum seekers at RAF Barnham.

The results showed that 94 per cent of those who took part in the poll (257 people) voted to leave the UK, while 16 voted against and one ballot was declared spoilt. Between 400 and 500 of Barnham’s 584 residents were eligible to vote in the symbolic poll.

The vote follows in the footsteps of a similar referendum in Piddington, Oxfordshire, in September, when residents elected to secede from the UK after the government announced plans to house 1,256 male asylum seekers at a nearby former military base.

Communities secretary Angela Rayner went on to approve full planning permission for MoD Bicester to be used for housing asylum seekers.

![Barnham Parish Council is holding a symbolic ‘referendum’ on declaring independence from the UK](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/21/15/2305613560..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The symbolic vote, held at Barnham Village Hall on Saturday, carries no legal weight. But leader of the opposition Kemi Badenoch shared her support for Barnham residents, saying “the Conservative Party is on your side”.

“We know that the problem of the boats needs to stop and we have got a plan to stop it,” she told the Press Association on Friday. “We are seeing more and more undocumented young men being put in asylum centres next to villages.

“Piddington, as an example, only has 130 women,” she said. “Putting about 1,300 men right next to that we know is not appropriate and I know that it’s not appropriate for Barnham, too.

“But instead of the government spreading the problem around, it needs to stop the boats.”

![Barnham residents unveil symbolic flag](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/21/16/35/Screenshot-2026-11-21-at-16-42-33.png?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

John Bauer, chair of Barnham Parish Council, previously said the independence referendum was organised by Barnham Residents’ Association.

He said people were voting on whether they support a petition for “crown dependency status” and described this as “a bit like Jersey, Guernsey, Isle of Man whereby we retain loyalty to the crown while still removing ourselves from the Home Office jurisdiction”.

Mr Bauer said: “Assuming we have a positive result, we will declare ourselves the bailiwick of Barnham.

“Not the principality of Piddington. This is going to be the bailiwick of Barnham.”

Asked what he hoped it would achieve, Mr Bauer said: “It’s to express the concern of the village at the way we’re not being consulted, the treatment by the Home Office, the whole thing, we feel we’re being totally ignored.”

He said the proposal for RAF Barnham, which prompted the referendum, was initially for 1,256 male asylum seekers to be housed at the base though the figure is now 931.

![The permanent protest camp with signs and flags is found outside the gates of the former RAF base on 21 November 2026 in Barnham](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/21/16/2305613766..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“I don’t know why they chose that particularly precise figure, but they have done,” Mr Bauer said.

“It makes very little difference, however, when you’ve only got 584 residents in the village whether it’s 1,256 or 931.

“It’s still going to completely swamp the village and change things beyond recognition.”

A final decision on the proposal has not yet been made.

Helen Beck, chair of Barnham Residents’ Association, which was formed on 26 October, said that the work villagers had put into the referendum was “truly humbling”.

“I want to thank all of the people who supported us today, both manning the desks and doing the count,” she said. “The community has come together around this and I’m really, really grateful for everybody’s support, so thank you very much.”

Residents unveiled a new flag in celebration of the result, featuring a stone-curlew bird against a Suffolk blue background that they named Andy Barnham.
