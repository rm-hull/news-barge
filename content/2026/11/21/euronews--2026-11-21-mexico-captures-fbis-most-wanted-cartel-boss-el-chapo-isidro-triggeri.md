## Fausto Isidro Meza Flores, one of the FBI’s ten most-wanted fugitives, was arrested in a military operation involving US intelligence cooperation. Gunfire, burning vehicles and panic among civilians followed the raid in northern Mexico.

Mexican security forces have captured notorious cartel leader Fausto Isidro Meza Flores, better known as ‘El Chapo Isidro’, in the north-western state of Sinaloa, triggering a wave of violence that sent residents fleeing for safety.

The arrest took place in Guasave on Friday night, 20 November, during an operation involving Mexican military and security forces. The Mexican government said intelligence provided by the administration of US President Donald Trump had contributed to the operation, although it has not disclosed precisely how the two countries co-operated.

The arrest was followed by armed confrontations, burning vehicles and roadblocks. Disturbing footage circulating on social media showed spectators fleeing in panic as gunfire interrupted a baseball match in Guasave.

## Military operation and US intelligence cooperation

According to Mexican authorities, security forces used what they described as ‘stealth and surprise’ to apprehend Meza Flores.

The operation involved specialist units from the Mexican National Guard and Army. Officials said their personnel came under attack from armed individuals using firearms and explosives, believed to be cartel operatives.

Following the exchange of fire, security forces entered a property and arrested Meza Flores, along with other individuals.

The detainees were subsequently evacuated aboard a Mexican Air Force aircraft to Mexico City, according to the authorities.

The Mexican government acknowledged that intelligence supplied by the Trump administration had played a role in locating and capturing the cartel leader. However, officials have so far provided few details about the intelligence-sharing arrangements or the extent of American involvement.

Mexican news outlet N+ also reported that intelligence agencies from Mexico's Defence Ministry and the United States had co-operated in the operation.

The development highlights the importance of intelligence cooperation between Mexico and the United States in tackling organised crime, particularly given the cross-border nature of the drug trade.

## Gunfire interrupts baseball match as residents flee

The arrest quickly turned into a night of violence in Guasave.

Videos and photographs shared online showed civilians running for cover as gunfire erupted during a baseball match. Spectators could be seen leaving the venue in fear as the situation deteriorated.

Other footage showed vehicles set alight and roads blocked, while armed confrontations reportedly spread beyond the immediate area of the operation.

According to Mexican newspaper *La Jornada*, the violence included shootings, vehicle fires, roadblocks involving burning lorries and clashes between federal forces and armed groups.

Local authorities urged residents to remain indoors and avoid travelling on affected roads.

The scenes illustrate the immediate risks that major operations against organised crime can pose to civilians living in areas where criminal groups maintain a strong presence.

No reliable overall casualty figure was established in the initial reports.

## Who is Fausto Isidro Meza Flores?

Fausto Isidro Meza Flores, known as ‘El Chapo Isidro’, is considered one of Mexico's most significant organised-crime figures.

He is associated with the Beltrán Leyva criminal organisation and leads a network also known as the Meza-Flores organisation or the Guasave Cartel.

The group has operated primarily in north-western Mexico, particularly in Sinaloa, and has been linked by US authorities to the trafficking of several illegal drugs, including cocaine, methamphetamine, heroin and marijuana.

Meza Flores rose to prominence following the fragmentation of the Beltrán Leyva organisation and the wider conflicts between rival criminal factions in Mexico.

His organisation has been accused of using violence, intimidation and territorial control to protect its criminal activities.

In February 2025, the FBI placed Meza Flores on its Ten Most Wanted Fugitives list, underlining the importance US authorities attached to his capture.

The US government had offered a reward of up to US$5 million for information leading to his arrest.

## What happens next?

Although the arrest is a major achievement for Mexican security forces, it remains to be seen how it will affect the balance of power between criminal organisations operating in Sinaloa.

The capture of a senior cartel figure does not necessarily mean the dismantling of the organisation he leads. Criminal networks can remain operational following the arrest of their leaders, while rival groups may attempt to exploit any resulting power vacuum.

The immediate unrest highlights the continuing challenges facing the country in its fight against organised crime.
