Republican Rep. Tim Burchett claimed adults who allow their children to play the long-awaited, record-breaking video game *Grand Theft Auto VI* are “bad parents.”

The Tennessee congressman made the comments Wednesday to TMZ, one day before the game's release, arguing that its graphic depictions of crime and violence could desensitize young players. *GTA VI* is the franchise's first new installment in 13 years and one of the most highly anticipated video games in years.

“These kids’ parents, are they wolves or something, raising their kids? I think it’s really just more of a how-to video,” Burchett said, comparing the game to a guide to committing crimes.

Asked whether he thought parents who let their children play the game were “bad parents,” he replied, “I believe so.”

Burchett also claimed that research has repeatedly linked violent video games to real-world violence, despite leading scientific organizations and research finding insufficient evidence that shows any causal link between the two.

![Tennessee GOP Rep. Tim Burchett called parents who let their children play ‘Grand Theft Auto VI’ ‘bad parents’ Wednesday, arguing the game's graphic violence could desensitize young players](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/21/17/2304454300..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“It desensitizes kids. They've done study after study,” Burchett said. “Parents need to start raising their children.”

*GTA VI* follows characters Jason and Lucia as they get caught up in the criminal world of Leonida, a fictional state based on Florida. Players can roam around the open-world setting, taking part in everything from car thefts and armed robberies to shootouts.

The game received an M for Mature rating from the Entertainment Software Rating Board, making it recommended for players 17 and older. The rating covers intense violence, blood and gore, nudity, sexual content, strong language, mature humor, and drug and alcohol use.

On Thursday, the White House got online backlash after posting a 30-second video of President Donald Trump edited in the style of *GTA*, with social media users calling the Wednesday clip “cringe” and “a little stale.”

Burchett was not alone in raising concerns about the game's content. Several Fox News personalities also criticized its depictions of graphic violence and sexual material, questioning whether they could influence young players.

![‘GTA 6’ is rated M for Mature, making it recommended for players 17 and older, due to the game’s intense violence, blood and gore, nudity, sexual content, strong language, mature humor, and drug and alcohol use](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/21/15/2026-11-21T090013Z_567669327_RC2VQOACJAZH_RTRMADP_3_GAMING-GTA-VI.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“Kids have these games, and what are you teaching them?” Fox News co-host Rosanna Scotto questioned during Thursday's episode of *Outnumbered.*

“How to steal a car, how to shoot someone, how to do something illegal. And, I don’t know, does it destigmatize crime?” she said.

“My son will be nowhere near anything like this while in my household,” added Kayleigh McEnany, the show’s co-host and a former White House press secretary.

Fox News contributor Paul Mauro claimed: “This is almost the equivalent of giving them a gun, at an age where they don’t understand responsibility.”

But Florida Democratic Rep. Maxwell Frost had a different take, saying he was looking forward to playing the game and even planned to buy a PlayStation 5 just to play it.

“Look, I wasn’t allowed to play *GTA*my entire life. I had to play* The Simpsons Hit and Run*… it’s like* GTA* for kids. That’s what I played. So now I have to play a lot to, you know, I have to make up for lost time,” Frost told TMZ.

He also disagreed that violent video games are behind America’s gun violence problem, saying parents should get to decide what games their kids can play.

“Every country has these games and has these movies. We’re the only [country] that has them that actually has a real gun violence problem,” he said.

![Burchett compared ‘GTA 6’ to a guide for committing crimes](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/19/8/24/GTA-VI-2d61063d.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Despite Burchett's claims, the connection between violent video games and real-world violence remains debated among researchers.

The American Psychological Association said in a March 2020 update that there was insufficient scientific evidence to prove violent video games cause real-world violence.

While some studies have found a small link between violent games and aggressive behavior, such as yelling or pushing, the organization cautioned against blaming video games for more serious acts of violence.

“Violence is a complex social problem that likely stems from many factors that warrant attention from researchers, policymakers and the public,” APA President Sandra L. Shullman said in a statement at the time.

“Attributing violence to video gaming is not scientifically sound and draws attention away from other factors, such as a history of violence, which we know from the research is a major predictor of future violence,” Shullman added.
