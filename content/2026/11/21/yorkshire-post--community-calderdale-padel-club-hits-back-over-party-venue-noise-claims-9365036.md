# Calderdale padel club hits back over 'party venue' noise claims

## A Calderdale padel club has defended plans for an booze licence after residents complained of ‘thwacking’ sounds and ‘unrest’ over parking.

Goat Haus, on South Lane in Elland, has applied to Calderdale Council for permission to sell alcohol and host music between 6am and 10pm, seven days a week.

A racquet sport originating in Mexico which combines elements of tennis and squash, padel is one of the fastest-growing sports in the UK.

But council papers, released ahead of a licensing sub-committee next week, show the application has attracted objections from two ward councillors and five residents who claim the venue is causing disruption.

![Goat Haus padel club is located on South Lane in Elland](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOmQyNjMwNjk4LWZmZTItNDY0Yi1iMjY3LWRmOTVlYWM0NGRmODphZjFiOThiMi01YzY5LTQwY2YtYTIzYS1lODc0NGNlNDcwOWQ=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


Councillor Nigel Hatfield (Reform, Elland) said he had received complaints from at least 11 residents, supported by photographic evidence, alleging ongoing nuisance, including “loud and repetitive noise” and “dangerous and inconsiderate parking”.

Councillor Peter Hunt (Reform, Elland) also claimed there is a “parking crisis” around the venue “bordering on low level unrest”, with customers opting for the pavement instead of an overflow site at the nearby Working Men’s Club.

“The thwacking of ball against an interior wall creates a sound that annoys neighbours,” he added.

Neighbour Andrew Barry said noise from previous events “severely impacted” him trying to relax in his garden on the weekend, while Majella O’Malley complained of shouting and talking by customers as early as 6am.

![Reform councillors for Elland Nigel Hatfield (left) and Peter Hunt (right)](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOmYzODhlM2Q5LWFiMDAtNGIwNS05NzE5LWQ0NDFlM2U2ZTdhNzo2MTQyNGRiZC1kODE3LTQ5YjktODc4OS04YWFkMmY3MTI5M2U=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


Liam O’Malley also questioned why a sports venue required permission for live music and alcohol sales from 6am until 10pm every day.

Responding to the concerns, entrepreneur and Goat Haus owner Gabriel Moore told the Local Democracy Reporting Service measures had already been introduced in response to neighbourhood feedback.

“We did initially have some noise complaints. After these we have made sure our shutter stays down at all times to not impede on our neighbours,” he said.

“Even with the event on the 31st we had no complaints on noise as we are doing our best to make sure people’s peace isn’t disturbed.”

Moore also accepted parking had been an issue but said there was an overflow parking agreement in place with the nearby working men’s club, and that customers were reminded of it with signage, on social media, and in person.

“Given that the street does not have any yellow lines, people take the view they can park there. We, to our best efforts, have definitely reduced this, but as I said we can’t physically stop people,” he added.

Addressing concerns over the scale of the licence application, Moore said Goat Haus had requested the full operating hours “for ease” and not because it plans to offer live music from 6am.

Moore also rejected suggestions it was evolving into a party business.

“We are most certainly a padel club firstly not an entertainment venue,” he said.

Moore said the club has hosted “a couple” of events, including a recent charity fundraiser for Andy’s Man Club which raised £5,000.

He said the most recent Halloween-style event featuring drinks, DJs and live music had been organised properly with a TEMP licence.

Council officers said the objections principally relate to public safety and the prevention of public nuisance.

But the report notes that issues such as whether the venue is better described as a sports centre, bar or events venue, whether the change is commercially necessary, and general competition for parking, should carry less weight.

“If members conclude that the identified risks can be adequately addressed through reduced hours, exclusion of an activity or appropriate enforceable conditions, the application may be granted subject to those controls,” the report adds.

The licencing committee will decide on the application next Friday (November 27).
