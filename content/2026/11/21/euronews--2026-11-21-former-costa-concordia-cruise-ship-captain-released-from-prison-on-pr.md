## The case dates back to January 2012, when the Costa Concordia ship struck rocks off the coast of Isola del Giglio in the Tyrrhenian Sea. The disaster claimed 32 lives.

After spending more than nine years behind bars, Francesco Schettino, the former captain of the Costa Concordia cruise ship, left Rome's Rebibbia prison on Friday evening.

According to a report from the Italian outlet Quotidiano Nazionale (QN), the Rome Supervisory Court granted Schettino release under probation after a thorough review of his rehabilitation process and his conduct during his time in prison.

Francesca Carnicelli, a lawyer for Schettino, welcomed the decision, saying prison staff had given an extremely positive assessment of his participation in the rehabilitation programme.

"In our view, all the conditions were in place for this alternative measure to be granted, as it represents the correct continuation of the process after the conviction," Carnicelli said.

The case dates back to January 2012, when the Costa Concordia ship, owned by the Costa Crociere cruise line, struck rocks off the coast of Isola del Giglio in the Tyrrhenian Sea.

The impact ripped a hole around 35.59 metres long in the port side of the ship's hull, causing it to list heavily before it ran aground on a rocky outcrop near Punta Gabbianara, where it partially sank.

The disaster claimed 32 lives, including passengers and crew members.

In the subsequent legal proceedings, Schettino was sentenced to 16 years in prison

The sentence was upheld by the Florence Court of Appeals on 31 May 2016, along with a five-year ban from working in the maritime industry. The provisional compensation awarded to survivors who joined the proceedings as civil claimants also increased to between €40,000 and €65,000 each.

The conviction was finally upheld by the Court of Cassation on 12 May 2017.
