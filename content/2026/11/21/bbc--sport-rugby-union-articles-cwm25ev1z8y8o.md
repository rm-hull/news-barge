**Nations ChampionshipWales (7) 26Tries:** Lake 2, Murray, Elias**Cons:** Costelow 3**Australia (12) 29Tries:** Tupou, Paenga-Amosa, Jorgensen, Ikitau**Cons:** Lonergan 2, Gordon**Pens:** Gordon**Wales could not emulate their famous victory against New Zealand as Australia claimed the Nations Championship spoils in Cardiff.**

The Wallabies ended a four-match losing run thanks to tries from hooker Brandon Paenga-Amosa, prop Taniela Tupou, wing Max Jorgensen and centre Len Ikitau, while fly-half Carter Gordon kicked a late penalty.

Wales captain Dewi Lake scored two tries, while full-back Blair Murray also crossed following a superb assist by wing Louis Rees-Zammit.

Replacement hooker Ryan Elias grabbed a late try but Australia held on for victory as Wales paid the price for not capitalising on attacking chances.

Wales finished fifth in the northern hemisphere section and will play Japan at the Allianz Stadium in Twickenham on Saturday (13:10 GMT).

Australia finished third in the southern hemisphere table and play Ireland on Friday (20:10 GMT).

## Fans flock back to the Principality Stadium

Highlights: Wallabies edge Wales in Cardiff

After all the hysteria and celebrations to mark a first victory against the All Blacks in 73 years, the attention turned towards whether Wales could back it up against Australia.

There was a vibrancy on the streets of Cardiff before the game not seen for a while, which reflected in the crowd.

After crowds of 52,425 against Japan and 55,152 against New Zealand, the 66,307 attendance against Australia was the highest of the autumn.

The All Blacks win proved instrumental in a surge of tickets sold for a 20:10 GMT kick-off, the latest for a Wales rugby home game at the Principality Stadium.

Head coach Steve Tandy made four changes to revert to the side that beat Japan with flanker James Botham the most notable absentee following his player-of-the-match against New Zealand.

Botham was expecting the birth of his first child this week but Tandy stated his omission was for rugby reasons with Cardiff colleague Alex Mann replacing him.

Fit-again number eight Aaron Wainwright replaced Taine Basham, while lock Ben Carter and wing Josh Adams returned for Adam Beard and Gabriel Hamer-Webb.

Australia had freshened things up with five forward changes as flanker Carlo Tizzano, lock Tom Hooper and the front-row trio of Isaac Kailea, Paenga-Amosa and Tupou came in.

## Australia open scoring after Llewellyn yellow card

Wales lost centre Max Llewellyn inside two minutes to a yellow card for a high tackle on Wallabies full-back Tom Wright.

Wallabies had plenty of early possession against 14 players but the home defence comfortably dealt with the attack.

Mann was caught with some friendly fire by a finger in the eye from team-mate Carter but continued despite appearing to have vision issues.

Rees-Zammit almost squeezed into the corner but just failed to ground the ball under pressure from Gordon, before Lake was held up over the Australia line.

Mann, despite his eye seeming to be closing, was still able to force a turnover penalty but Wales were guilty of early handling errors.

After Wales won a scrum penalty, the officials then either missed Tupou striking Mann with a body jab, or deemed it did not warrant further action.

Tupou stayed on the field to open the scoring in the 21st minute as he evaded Llewellyn's tackle to cross for an unconverted try.

## Wales bounce back but Wallabies lead at half-time

![Wales captain Dewi Lake celebrates scoring a 11th Test try in his 37th international](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/cccd/live/2a2956a0-e5f0-11f1-b1d8-fd34c88a50a0.jpg)

Wales captain Dewi Lake has now scored 12 tries in 37 internationals

Wales retaliated quickly with Morgan breaking away and feeding scrum-half Tomos Williams, who was held up by superb visiting defence.

There were important turnover penalties from Tizzano and Tupou who benefitted from a huge tackle from Australia captain Harry Wilson on opposite number Wainwright.

The hosts held the upper hand in the first-half aerial battle but Wales were not as clinical as they were against New Zealand.

Australia centre Joseph-Aukuso Suaalii gained an intercept before being hauled down by Rees-Zammit, who conceded a penalty.

Wallabies demonstrated a more ruthless approach as Paenga-Amosa was driven over.

Mann continued to wind up the Australia forwards, which helped gain 10 metres for dissent, but Wales continued to waste chances as an attacking line-out was stolen.

Morgan was a Wales carrying colossus in the first half with a series of impressive bursts and the home side finally gained a reward in the 40th minute when Lake dived over with Costelow converting.

Australia led 12-7 at half-time with Wales having an on-pitch huddle before heading to their changing room.

## Wallabies aerial dominance proves crucial

Suaalii towered over Murray to take an early second-half attacking high ball but Wallabies were denied a try by a brilliant Morgan turnover.

Australia scrum-half Lonergan missed a penalty before Wallabies' second-half aerial dominance was rewarded when Jorgensen rose above Adams before finishing off the move to score.

Wales brought on a raft of replacements, including an early introduction for scrum-half Reuben Morgan-Williams.

The changes worked when Lake was driven over for his second score before Wales followed up with a magical try.

Rees-Zammit was the architect with a stunning outside break before feeding Adams who provided the try-scoring pass to Murray.

Costelow's conversion gave Wales the lead for the first time.

## Australia hold on for first win in five matches

Australia brought on experienced forward replacements with Fraser McReight and Angus Bell making immediate impacts.

Prop Allan Alaalatoa appeared to have scored on his 99th international, but it was ruled out following a knock-on by replacement scrum-half Kalani Thomas.

The Wallabies wave continued with Wales duo Eddie James and Archie Griffin producing impressive tackles before Ikitau restored the visitors' lead.

Morgan left the field for a head injury assessment (HIA) to be replaced by Kane James, who was in tears when the anthem was played as he marked his first Wales appearance in Cardiff.

Fellow replacement Louie Hennessey knocked on to give Australia an attacking platform before Wainwright conceded a penalty which Carter kicked to take the Wallabies two scores clear.

Morgan returned and Wales responded as they battered the Australia line with Wilson shown a yellow card.

Elias was driven over to secure two losing bonus-points and set up a grandstand finale, but Wallabies held on for a vital victory for head coach Les Kiss.
