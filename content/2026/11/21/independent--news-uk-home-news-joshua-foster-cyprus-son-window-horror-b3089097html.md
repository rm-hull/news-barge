A British father who was prosecuted in Cyprus after his two-year-old son fell from a window in Cyprus has spoken of his horror and grief after returning to the UK.

Joshua Foster, 37, lost control of his son Jaxon after holding him up to an open window to look at a nearby waterpark, mistakenly believing the opening was covered by glass.

Jaxon plummeted nearly 40ft from the King Evelthon Beach Hotel & Resort in Chlorakas, and was pronounced dead at the scene on 12 July, the first day of the Fosters’ family holiday.

Mr Foster, a senior accountant at KPMG, was arrested and later pleaded guilty to causing death by a reckless act. Last month he was fined €3,000 (£2,500), the minimum punishment for the ­–offence, and allowed back to the UK to mourn his loss with his family.

“I have a burden. I’ll have that my whole life. But we want to make sure this doesn’t happen again,” he told the *Daily Mail*. “It’s very traumatic. My mind doesn’t want to go there. I just ­remember the despair of him falling and watching him fall.

![Joshua Foster arriving at Paphos District Court in Cyprus (James Manning/PA)](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/16/13/1a1fb452e7abdc9bfbe3e78967931acdY29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzkyMjM2MzI4-2.86201845.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“It was just such a shock that the window … when the glass wasn’t there. I put him up thinking he was going against a glass pane … and then he wasn’t there.”

Mr Foster said he “screamed” and then ran to the veranda where his son had fallen: “I picked him up. I held him and screamed,” he said.

He claims he was then questioned by police who initially accused him of swinging the boy around. He said he was held in custody for eight days immediately after the incident, adding that while grieving his child, he had to call his daughter from custody to explain what had happened.

![Joshua Foster has now returned to the UK](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/16/09/01M51VTNJWMME1VVSEPB38K5DD.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

He was charged but was later able to return to the UK to bury Jaxon under bail conditions.

After pleading guilty he avoided a jail sentence after Judge Froso Christodoulidou said such a punishment would be inappropriate given the circumstances surrounding the incident and Mr Foster’s character.

Since returning to the UK, Mr Foster said the family’s focus has been on supporting their daughter, who had lost her “best friend”.

“Dishing up the dinners … to begin with I’d get his bowl out and then you just realise,” he said. “Our dog started barking the other night and I said: ‘Shh, you’ll wake the children up.’ Because you forget.

“It’s very difficult. She [his daughter] waves at the sky and says: ‘I love you’ to him and blows a kiss. She’s lost her best friend. She’s gone from having a sibling to being an only child,” he said.

He added he wants to make sure “nothing like this ever happens again”.
