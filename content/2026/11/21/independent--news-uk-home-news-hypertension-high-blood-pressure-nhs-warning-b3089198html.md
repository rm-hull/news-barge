The NHS has issued a warning after recording a three quarter rise in emergency hospital admissions for high blood pressure.

The health service’s analysis shows emergency admissions rose from 24,481 in 2019/20 to 43,306 in 2025/26 – up 77 per cent.

Women made up a third more cases than men, data showed, but the NHS said many people remained unaware they are living with the condition until it causes serious health problems.

High blood pressure is one of the leading preventable causes of heart attacks, strokes, heart failure and chronic kidney disease - often called a “silent killer” because it has no symptoms.

The health service issued an appeal for adults aged 40 and over to take up their free NHS Health Check, as checking blood pressure can help people to understand their risks and take action early.

Only a third of people invited for their NHS Health Check in 2025/26 took up their offer (1.3 million out of 3.8 million).

![NHS leaders are calling on people aged 40 and over to get a free health check following an "alarming" rise in hospital admissions for high blood pressure](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/21/17/01M7YHYYGBQCAEPH6AH28KSZZK.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Helen Williams, national clinical director for Cardiovascular Disease Prevention at NHS England, said there had been an alarming rise in people needing emergency care from high blood pressure.

“The increase of people needing emergency care due to high blood pressure is alarming – high blood pressure is a major risk factor for heart attacks, strokes, heart failure and chronic kidney disease, and in many cases it can be detected and treated earlier.

“We are working to support more people at risk from this silent killer before they become seriously unwell by identifying those with high blood pressure sooner, and then providing appropriate treatment and support to make positive changes to their health.

“Knowing your numbers is one of the most important steps people can take to protect their heart health – a simple blood pressure check could help prevent a life-changing event later on”.

In the 12 months to July this year, community pharmacies in England carried out more than 3.3 million blood pressure checks through the Hypertension Case Finding Service. Of these, more than 375,000 people were identified as needing additional monitoring via the pharmacy, to be analysed by a GP.

Blood pressure checks are available through NHS Health Checks, GP practices and through the Hypertension Case Finding Service in most community pharmacies.
