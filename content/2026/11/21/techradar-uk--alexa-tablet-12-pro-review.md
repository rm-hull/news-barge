### TechRadar Verdict

On paper, Amazon’s Alexa Tablet 12 Pro might not seem terribly exciting; most specs are middling, Alexa’s inclusion is far from a selling point and, based on Amazon’s track record, the company’s services can do more to interrupt a product’s usefulness than inform it. However, between its gorgeous nanomatte screen, stylish design, and clever features (especially for Kindle fans), the Alexa Tablet 12 Pro is a surprisingly exciting device and a solid start for Amazon’s post-FireOS era.

### Alexa Tablet 12 Pro review: One-minute review

When Amazon announced its new Alexa Tablet 12 Pro, I found myself at a crossroads. On the one hand, despite my criticisms of the company’s product ecosystem, I’ve used Amazon Echo smart speakers and displays to control my smart home for several years, and I do appreciate the brand’s approach to providing a broad spectrum of price points in its device lineup.

But I don’t like that many of these devices are treated as billboards for Amazon’s wider products and services, and Kindle Fire tablets are (or were) the biggest offenders; FireOS was a sluggish, bloatware-loaded platform that barely functioned in itself, let alone as a tablet OS with its limited app store.

So, hearing that Amazon had launched an Android-based tablet came as a genuinely welcome surprise, and I was particularly excited to try its top-of-the-line 12-inch nanomatte screen model. I’ve spent two weeks testing the Alexa Tab 12 Pro, and while it’s still far from perfect, it’s a surprisingly competent and well-thought-out device for the price. With time (and perhaps a second-generation model), Amazon could finally be making its mark in the tablet market.

![Alexa Tablet 12 Pro with nanomatte coating](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/v2/t:69,l:126,cw:1069,ch:601,q:80,w:1200/utPWf5gLAx4ugdhrNSbfuH.jpg)

The Tab 12 Pro’s design is simple but slick, its specs are decent enough and help keep the price low, and some of the added features (including those that drive the user towards Amazon services) feel smartly integrated into the tablet. And the nanomatte display variant? It’s a triumph for Amazon: clear and crisp, defiant to most reflections unless under direct light and wonderfully vibrant.

I’ve not seen the non-nanomatte version of the Alexa Tab 12 Pro, but I can’t imagine it lives up to the model I tested; in all likelihood, nanomatte is every bit worth the $50/£50 upgrade. Equipped with it, the Tab 12 Pro’s 12-inch display is one of the best screens in its category, and really, that’s what a lot of everyday customers are looking for versus performant chipsets, high-spec cameras, and powerful speakers.

That being said, the cracks do show in some of the Tab 12 Pro’s lower-end specs, and it feels a little like Amazon’s focus on delivering a well-rounded product comes at the expense of the Alexa Tablet 12 Pro having a real, hard-hitting selling point — and perhaps its longevity, too.

For instance, while its blue light-reducing reading profiles, 3:2 aspect ratio, paperlike screen texture, and slick Kindle experience make it great for reading books and documents alike, it’s too heavy to use one-handed and stash away like a typical e-reader. For streaming video, the screen is bright and clear, but that vibrancy I mentioned before can mean colors are a little overblown, even if you tune them down with the different profiles in settings, and the tablet’s speakers are pretty flat if you want to listen aloud. Even for its main selling point, its productivity usecases, the chipset doesn't offer the longevity it needs to keep pace in 2-3 years time under heavy loads.

This is a thread that will follow through much of my commentary on Amazon’s best-ever tablet; being a ‘jack of all trades, master of none’ device doesn’t *really* do much for me, even in a general-use tablet. Still, the Alexa Tab 12 Pro is far from a disappointment overall, and especially for Prime members, Kindle lovers, or those wanting a great household management-come-productivity slate, it’s a really solid Android tablet.

### Alexa Tablet 12 Pro review: Price & availability

* **Starts at $499/£449 for the 12-inch model without nanomatte**
* **Alexa Tablet Pro keyboard is $149.99 / £129.99**
* **Alexa Tablet Pro Pen is $89.99 / £79.99**
* **Available at Amazon**

The Alexa Tablet 12 Pro comes in at $499/£449 in its base configuration with 128GB storage and its standard display; for $/£50 more you can add the nanomatte display, and for $/£100 more you can upgrade to 16GB RAM / 256GB storage.

There are optional peripherals to factor in too; sold separately, the keyboard costs $149.99 / £129.99, and the stylus is $89.99 / £79.99, but you can bundle these in with the tablet for an added cost of $150/£140. These are fairly steep standalone prices, so if you think there’s a chance you’ll benefit from these peripherals, I’d buy them outright.

While a lot of the Tablet 12 Pro’s specs are middling, at its price point and versus the competition, this is a pretty solid value proposition; Amazon will often trim the fat from its hardware to reach low prices, and it’s a similar story across the other smaller tablets in this lineup. The Alexa Tablet 8 starts at $229.99 / £199.99 with 4GB RAM and 64GB storage, and the Tablet 11 comes in at $329.99 / £289.99 with 8GB RAM and 64GB storage. These smaller models can be bought without ads for an extra $20/£20 a piece.

By comparison, Samsung’s Galaxy Tab S10 FE is slightly less powerful, has a slightly smaller battery and a 90Hz screen, but does offer a slightly better front camera; it starts at $549.99 / £539. Apple’s latest iPad Air comes in at $749 / £749, and while it does offer the more powerful M4 chipset, it’s smaller at 11 inches and has a static 60Hz display versus the Alexa Tablet 12 Pro’s 120Hz.

I’ve seen a lot of discussion around whether the Tablet 12 Pro’s low price is worth paying for a product that will inevitably become more of an ad platform as new updates roll out. With the freedom of Android OS, it’s a little easier for consumers to have their say on what they see by downloading a different launcher, but I’d be doing a disservice to those who have been burned by aggressively targeted ads (especially on the Echo Show) if I didn’t at least acknowledge how ads may factor into the value proposition here. That being said, I’ve barely seen anything ads-wise in my time testing the Tablet 12 Pro…

* **Value score:** 4/5

### Alexa Tablet 12 Pro review: Design

* **Slick and simple design**
* **A little on the heavier side**
* **Magnetic peripherals (added cost) for productivity**

Amazon’s not changing the game with its Alexa Tablet design aesthetically, but there are a few things of note about its slate.

Its 3:2 aspect ratio (270.8 mm x 183.85 mm x 6.5 mm) makes it a great tablet for productivity; you can read documents and books with ease, and there’s plenty of room for multitasking in its horizontal orientation. It’s a little heavy at 550g, but it does feel well-built, offering IP52 dust and splash resistance. There’s an elegance here I’m not quite used to in Amazon’s products; from the cursive Alexa branding on the back panel to the premium-feeling aluminum casing, which is surprisingly smudge-proof.

Embedded into the right-hand side bezel, the 8MP front camera is positioned just off-center when the tablet is stationed horizontally, and on the rear of the tablet, there’s a 13MP main camera — both offer 1080p full HD video.

On the right side of the tablet’stop edge, there’s a fingerprint sensor lock button, and at the top of its right-hand side is the volume rocker. On the bottom edge, you’ll find its USB-C 3.2 Gen 1 port (the smaller screen models have USB-C 2.0, for what it’s worth) and along the left-hand side are four pogo pins for attaching the folio keyboard.

![Alexa Tablet 12 Pro with nanomatte coating](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/DWXq9xHadcceio7viHsLpH-1200-80.jpg)

Speaking of which, Amazon sent me the keyboard casing and stylus to test for this review, and while the price to add them both is a little steep, they unlock a great deal of functionality in the tablet. I wish Amazon had added a backlight at this price point, but broadly speaking, I’ve very few complaints.

The chiclet keyboard is soft and clicky but sturdy, featuring a dedicated Alexa+ key in lieu of a right control button as well as a writing assist key in the top-right hand corner. The trackpad is responsive and smooth, and there are ample shortcuts for productivity users. I don’t love the magnetically attaching rear cover and its fold-out kickstand; a few too many times, this popped off of the tablet while I was adjusting the height, and it doesn’t offer much stability if you want to use the tablet on your lap, or indeed any uneven surface.

The stylus magnetically attaches and charges, and offers a nice weight for a natural drawing experience. Its top end can be used as an eraser, and if it’s missing, you can find it by triggering an admittedly soft and quiet ping.

* **Design score**: 4/5

### Alexa Tablet 12 Pro review: Display

* **Optional nanomatte display is well worth the upgrade**
* **Smooth and super responsive**
* **Great features for reading**

The finest feather in the Alexa Tablet 12 Pro’s cap is its main screen, an opinion which is colored, by and large, by my love for the optional nanomatte display.

The tablet’s 12-inch 2.8K LCD screen offers a 2800 x 1840 resolution, 280ppi (pixels per inch), and a buttery smooth 120Hz touchscreen. I’ve only seen and tested the nanomatte version, and that’s a real boon when it comes to anti-glare; in every circumstance barring direct sunlight or direct indoor light like nearby table lamps, it’s easy to see from most viewing angles, even despite only offering 500 nits of brightness.

The default color profile is Boosted; I found this to be a bit too vibrant in most settings, in particular while watching TV series, but the Natural profile is much better unless you’re in an especially bright environment.

The gritty, paper-like nanomatte texture makes it easy on the eyes when reading, too — and even offers a decent bite for drawing and writing with the Alexa Tablet Pro Pen. That being said, I noticed there’s poor palm detection while drawing in apps like Clip Studio Paint, so if you’re looking for the best possible tablet for drawing, you’re better off with more specialized slates like the iPad Pro or Samsung Galaxy Tab S10 Ultra.

* **Display score**: 4.5/5

### Alexa Tablet 12 Pro review: Performance

* **Yay for Android…**
* **But app compatibility issues and remnants of Amazon bloatware remain**
* **Makes the most out of some middling specs**

Unlock the Alexa Tablet 12 Pro, and you’re greeted by its default launcher with Amazon services and Alexa baked in; these elements are omnipresent, with a widget in the top-left corner for recently viewed media on Amazon platforms as well as the Ask Alexa search bar in the bottom-right corner, which pops out into a chat window when pressed.

I don’t hate these features as much as other reviewers and customers seem to, but that’s in part because I often *do*want to hop back into my last-read Kindle book or quickly dive back into playlists, movies and TV series. And besides that, the Tablet 12 Pro is* far* less egregious with its advertising (at least, for now) than other Amazon devices I’ve used. Plus, thanks to Android OS, you can just switch out to another launcher if you really hate Amazon’s footprint.

As well as Amazon's own apps, Google takes up a fair amount of real estate by default, with its own app folder as well as a Google search bar in the top right corner. Swipe left, and you land in the content center with recommended content from Prime, Kindle, and Luna, and swipe up for your app tray; all fairly standard for Android tablets overall.

![Alexa Tablet 12 Pro Nanomatte](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/v2/t:15,l:392,cw:884,ch:497,q:80,w:1200/iGdk67DsQANFfMYoR8fbmN.jpg)

The Kindle experience is a particular highlight. When you open books in the app, the screen will automatically toggle into reading mode, which reduces blue-light emission, silences distractions like notifications, and extends the battery life. Were the tablet a little lighter and more portable, I’d say it’s a great replacement for Kindle users who want more than just e-reading, but I wouldn’t personally stash this in my backpack to read while on the go quite as readily as I would Amazon’s latest thin-and-light e-readers. What’s more, most of these features are exclusive to the Kindle app.

As an entertainment hub, the Tab 12 Pro is decent enough; the 3:2 aspect ratio isn’t ideal, as you do see quite a bit of letterboxing while streaming content, and the sound quality from the built-in speakers is a little tinny and bassless.

The Alexa Tablet 12 Pro is no powerhouse; it’s middling at best, but at its price and for vast swathes of its target market, that’s perfectly fine. Its MediaTek Dimensity 8400 processor is perfectly capable for light multitasking, moderately demanding apps and some AI tasks, but don’t expect iPad Pro- or Samsung Galaxy S10 Ultra-grade speeds here.

![Alexa Tablet 12 Pro Nanomatte](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/Jzan3MUioQSQRFPspgKAqN-1200-80.jpg)

Still, the Tablet 12 Pro is a very capable productivity machine, whether in split-screen or desktop mode, and especially with its peripherals in tow. I played games like *Wuthering Waves*and used Clip Studio Paint and, broadly speaking, performance was fine in both applications with some tweaks in settings – even in desktop mode with a hefty update running in the background for* Wuthering Waves*, YouTube playing in windowed mode and typing on GDocs, the tablet handled the load pretty well, though it did get a little warm in the process. Gaming will need lower performance presets, but at this price that's to be expected, to an extent.

In my time with the Tab 12 Pro, I also encountered some stuttering when switching between apps, a few crashes, and some software lag, which could be any combination of the less powerful chipset and Amazon’s software. This, plus the long-running woes of Android app compatibility limiting the library of optimized apps, can cause friction in use.

Generally speaking, though, the Tab 12 Pro is pretty capable. I’m not convinced that will be the case in 3-4 years, which does give me pause, but only time will tell how these specs hold up.

### Other features

## Battery

I struggle a little with Amazon’s promise of 16 hours of video playback; just 4 hours of streaming Netflix series wiped out 50% of my battery life, and I was completely out after 8 hours. Combined with the tablet’s 2-3 hour recharge time, this isn’t ideal, but it will still last you throughout the workday so long as you’re not running lots of power-hungry apps at once without turning on power-saving mode.

## Alexa and AI services

Alexa has the same issues here as it does across Amazon’s platforms; namely, that it’s built more as a shopping assistant than anything else. For smart home control, timer setting, and simple requests, it’s broadly fine, and you can download third-party assistants if you want more demanding tasks completed. You can access Gemini’s writing tools natively from the keyboard peripheral, too.

![Alexa Tablet 12 Pro Nanomatte](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/iK4rrxTfQmC9gY2xhBhziN-1200-80.jpg)

## Cameras

Far from being standout features, the tablet’s cameras are decent enough; the 8MP front camera is good enough for video calls, and the 13MP rear camera is fine for shooting a quick photo or video in a pinch, albeit the results will be quite saturated. This is far from a deal-breaker in a general-use tablet, but if you like a crisp video feed for business calls, you’re better off with a more pro-grade slate.

* **Performance score**: 3.5/5

## Buy it if…

**You use a lot of Amazon services** If you’re a Prime subscriber who uses Kindle services, Prime Video and/or Luna, this is a great companion to fit into your wider Amazon device ecosystem.**You want a solid productivity tablet without a high price tag** At its price, this is a competitive productivity slate, even with the added cost of peripherals, and it’s capable of moderate loads as well as multitasking.**You do a lot of reading**   
With its nanomatte coating in particular, the Alexa Tablet 12 Pro is great for reading in and out of its optimized Kindle experience. The 3:2 aspect ratio lends itself to documents and books in both horizontal and vertical orientation, and the screen is very easy on the eyes.

## Don’t buy it if…

**You don’t like Amazon services** Yes, you can switch to a different launcher, but at its core, this is an Alexa-first package. It’s in the name, and though there are limited ways Amazon can mess with Android’s open design philosophy, you might prefer something that’s less Amazon-y out of the box.**You want an entertainment hub before all else**  
Streaming is fine on the Alexa Tablet 12 Pro, but its aspect ratio isn’t optimal for TVs and movies, and the speakers are middling at best.

### Alexa Tablet 12 Pro review: Also consider

**Samsung Galaxy Tab S10 FE** It’s comparable in terms of specs and price; while it has a slightly less powerful chipset and a slightly smaller 90Hz screen, it does have better IP68 water and dust resistance, 45W fast charging, and comes with the S Pen included. Plus, it’s less Amazon-y.**Apple iPad Air**   
The smaller, 11-inch iPad Air is certainly more powerful than the Alexa Tablet 12 Pro thanks to Apple’s M4 chip, but it has a static 60Hz 264ppi display and, of course, runs iPadOS rather than Android.

### How I tested the Alexa Tablet 12 Pro

* **I used the Alexa Tablet 12 Pro for two weeks**
* **I used it as a productivity device as well as an entertainment device**
* **I benchmarked against mid-range tablets**

During my two weeks with the Alexa Tablet 12 Pro, I used it to write much of this review while exporting files and using various productivity apps, as well as for gaming and streaming content and using apps like Clip Studio Paint to test the drawing experience. I tried its advertised features, including desktop mode and split-screen, the built-in Alexa chat function, and Amazon app experiences like Kindle.

I benchmarked the device against comparable mid-range tablets like the Samsung Galaxy Tab S10 FE and the iPad Air (M4), playing demanding games like *Wuthering Waves* and completing multitasking workloads to see how it performs under pressure. I ran the battery down to 0% while streaming content on Netflix and recharged the device fully to measure battery performance.

I’m a regular iPad Pro user and have tested a variety of mobile computing devices in my five years at TechRadar, including Android slates, drawing tablets and beyond.
