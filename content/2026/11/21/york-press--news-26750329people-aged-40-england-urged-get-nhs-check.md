NHS leaders are urging people aged 40 and over in England to take up free health checks amid a sharp rise in hospital admissions.

The plea follows a 77% increase in emergency admissions for high blood pressure, with cases rising from 24,481 in 2019/20 to 43,306 in 2025/26.

High blood pressure is a major risk factor for heart attacks, strokes, heart failure and chronic kidney disease, and often goes undetected until a serious health event occurs.

Helen Williams, national clinical director for cardiovascular disease prevention at NHS England, said: "The increase in people needing emergency care due to high blood pressure is alarming.

"We are working to support more people at risk from this silent killer before they become seriously unwell by identifying those with high blood pressure sooner, and then providing appropriate treatment and support to make positive changes to their health.

"Knowing your numbers is one of the most important steps people can take to protect their heart health – a simple blood pressure check could help prevent a life-changing event later on."

## What do NHS health checks involve?

NHS health checks should be offered every five years to adults aged 40 to 74 who do not already have conditions such as heart disease or diabetes.

The check, which takes 20 to 30 minutes, includes measurements of height, weight, waist circumference, blood pressure, cholesterol, and sometimes blood sugar.

It also involves questions about family medical history, smoking, alcohol consumption, and physical activity.

Despite 3.8 million people being invited for a health check in 2025/26, only 1.3 million attended.

Juliet Bouverie, chief executive of the Stroke Association, said: "High blood pressure is the leading cause of stroke, playing a part in around half of all cases.

"Someone in the UK has a stroke every five minutes – and this figure is rising – yet around 90% are preventable.

"NHS health checks offer an important opportunity to assess risk factors of stroke, including high blood pressure, so we encourage everyone eligible to attend.

"Preventing stroke means preventing the devastating impact the brain injury caused by stroke has on both survivors and their loved ones."

People who have not been invited for a health check are encouraged to contact their GP practice.

Blood pressure checks are also available via a pharmacy.

## Does high blood pressure have symptoms?

The NHS shares that high blood pressure does not usually cause any symptoms.

Rarely, high blood pressure can cause symptoms such as:

* headaches
* blurred vision
* chest pain

However, the only way to find out if you have high blood pressure is to get your blood pressure checked.

## What happens at a blood pressure check?

During a blood pressure test, an inflatable cuff is placed around your upper arm to measure the pressure in your blood vessels.

If your reading indicates possible high blood pressure, you may need further checks. This could involve taking regular readings at home for several days, returning to your GP surgery for another test, or wearing a monitor that records your blood pressure over 24 hours.

The results can then be used to determine whether you have high blood pressure.
