# Major change approved at North Yorkshire railway station

## BIG changes are coming to Scarborough Railway Station after planners gave the green light to a major upgrade programme at the historic site.

The works form part of a wider effort to modernise facilities at the town’s 180-year-old transport hub while protecting its Victorian character.

The scheme will see new electrical infrastructure installed throughout the Grade II\*-listed station, while the existing standby diesel generator and its fuel storage tank are removed.

Parts of the current infrastructure are approaching the end of their operational life, with rail bosses arguing a more reliable permanent power supply is needed, according to submitted plans.

![An Aerial View Of Scarborough Station. Align Property Services](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOjU1NzliMDMxLTI3ZGYtNGVkZi1iMzkxLTQyZjdkYzBiMzMxZTplZDQwYjNlMC02NDdhLTQ0ODQtOTYwNy02MWQ1Y2E5ZjMyZDg=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


Council planners have now granted listed building consent for the project, concluding it would preserve the station’s historic character.

The station, designed by Victorian railway architect George Townsend Andrews and opened in 1845, is one of Scarborough’s most recognisable landmarks and serves as the main gateway for thousands of visitors arriving in the resort every year.

But behind the historic façade sits a network of ageing cables, electrical equipment and backup power systems which operators say now need replacing.

Under the plans, new underground cabling, power distribution equipment and associated infrastructure will be installed across the site.

![Scarborough Station, Current Frontage. Align Property Services](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOjExMDZlM2Y3LTgwZmUtNDk5OS05ZTg0LWY0YjI1MWJlZmMwODpmNDI1MzdiNC1jMWI3LTRkNTItYTc0YS0zYzc3NTMwYzYzZjE=.png?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


The most visible change will be the removal of the diesel generator and fuel tank, which planners said contribute nothing to the station’s historic significance and currently create visual clutter around the site.

“Their removal will reduce operational clutter and will preserve the architectural interest of the Grade II listed building,” a council report notes.

Documents submitted by TransPennine Express and Network Rail say the works will also help support longer-term ambitions for the Scarborough Station Gateway regeneration project.

The land currently occupied by the generator and fuel tank could potentially be freed up for future improvements around the station, which has been identified as a key gateway arrival point for the town.

![Scarborough Station. Courtesy TPE](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOmI3ZTA1MWM1LTFjNTUtNDhmYy05NWEzLTM4ZWZlNzliY2Y5Yjo4MzAyNjMxOS0yMDA5LTRmYzQtOGYwMS1hNDcyOWIzNmRlZWE=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


No objections were submitted by members of the public during the application process, while Scarborough Town Council formally backed the plans.

Council officers concluded the upgrade would modernise essential infrastructure and approved the plans, subject to conditions.
