Temperatures plummeted to -10C in parts of rural Scotland early on Saturday morning as the UK faced its coldest night of the autumn so far.

Freezing temperatures were recorded widely across the north of England and Scotland in the early hours of the morning as many wake up to frost and wintry conditions.

The mercury plunged to -10C in Tulloch Bridge in the Scottish Highlands, beating the season’s previous low of -8.4C, recorded in the same place overnight on Thursday.

Keswick in the Lake District saw lows of -3C and -1C was reached in Bedford, while temperatures largely lingered just above freezing across the south of England.

Met Office chief forecaster Matthew Lehnert said: “Patches of freezing fog could form overnight, particularly across Northern Ireland, eastern England and around the Solway Firth, with isolated patches of dense fog possible further south.

![Temperatures plunged to -10C in Tulloch Bridge on Saturday morning](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/21/8/36/Screenshot-2026-11-21-at-08-23-05.png?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“After a cold and frosty start on Saturday, most places will enjoy a largely dry and bright day.

“It will stay chilly, particularly in north-eastern parts of the UK, while temperatures in the South West will be closer to average.”

A yellow cold health alert is in force for the North West, North East and Yorkshire and the Humber until 9am on Tuesday.

![Parts of the UK can expect a chilly start to the day on Sunday](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/21/8/33/Screenshot-2026-11-21-at-08-25-00.png?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The alert means weather conditions could lead to an increased use of healthcare services and a greater risk to life for vulnerable people, according to the UK Health Security Agency (UKHSA).

Rain is forecast for some on Sunday, with light showers in the north of England and more persistent rain expected in the South West, the Met Office said.

About a dozen flood alerts remained in place for England on Friday after a spell of wet weather and more could be issued over the weekend.

The Environment Agency said local flooding is possible from rivers and surface water in parts of England on Sunday.
