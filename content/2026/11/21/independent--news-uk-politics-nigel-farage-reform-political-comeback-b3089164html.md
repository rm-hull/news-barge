Over a gin and tonic in the 5 Hertford Street club – a favourite Mayfair hang out for many leading Brexiteers – a close ally of Nigel Farage slightly misquoted the famous American author Mark Twain.

“Reports of Nigel’s political death are greatly exaggerated," they said.

And according to a number of Reform UK sources, work is well underway to deal with the mistakes which took the party collapse from dominating the polls with up to 35 per cent of support to a post-election nadir of 19 per cent last week in just a year.

After all, almost every commentator is writing the party off and speculating whether Mr Farage will give it all up next year when he realises his chances of getting into Downing Street are non-existent and go to make money in America.

![Brexit has brought Farage's enthusiasm back](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/21/14/2026-11-20T161849Z_1151328403_RC2RROACRUGQ_RTRMADP_3_EU-BRITAIN-REFORM-FARAGE.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

But the “not dead yet” optimism is born of a party that knows its leader has had both highs and lows, but has never been politically killed off and always found a way to come back.

A number of issues are already being dealt with internally and in terms of messaging while they genuinely believe a new Brexit debate instigated by Andy Burnham will provide a way back to the top of the polls.

## Make peace with Rupert Lowe

*The Independent* has been told that behind the scenes talks are underway to end the feud with Rupert Lowe and his Restore Britain Party, which has been eating away at Reform UK’s support. But the real surprise is who is being asked to mend fences.

According to insiders, home affairs spokesperson Zia Yusuf is the one who is being pushed to reach out to Mr Lowe and bring him back into the Reform UK fold.

Back in January last year it was the fallout between the two men that led to Mr Yusuf actually reporting Mr Lowe to the police, a move that saw the Great Yarmouth MP walk out of Reform.

A source told *The Independent*: “Zia is the one reaching out now. I am not sure what it will take but I suspect a public apology and paying Rupert’s legal fees would do the trick.”

![Rupert Lowe has been a problem for Farage](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/21/14/01M2K66RHW2K2TQ3N7CZV2R203.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The move is necessary because Restore is winning support from the right of Reform and is currently polling at about 5 per cent with help from Elon Musk and his X platform’s algorithms.

Added to that there is a recognition that Restore has got into the heads of the Reform UK leadership and forced them into a core vote hardcore immigration strategy on the right rather than appealing to people in the centre.

## The ‘anti-Putin party’

There is a recognition that for too long Mr Farage and Reform have been viewed as “pro-Putin”, with no serious effort to counter it.

Even as far back as the 2024 general election, the then-Tory chair Richard Holden told *The Independent* that a perception that Reform supported Putin and not Ukraine was the one issue pushing voters back to the Conservatives at the last minute. In fact, in the final days the attacks in that regard saw Reform support drop by five points.

It has not been helped by Mr Farage making admiring comments about Putin in the past, raising questions about support for Ukraine and the former Reform Welsh leader Nathan Gill going to prison for taking Russian money.

But a senior Reform figure has privately confirmed *The Independent’s* reporting earlier this month that the appointment of new defence spokesperson, wing commander Angus McIntyre, was deliberately aimed at dealing with that problem.

![Reform has been described as pro-Putin and anti-Ukraine](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/21/14/2026-11-20T080014Z_652753456_RC20ROAKHMH7_RTRMADP_3_UKRAINE-CRISIS-RUSSIA-LOGISTICS.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

McIntyre spent two years working at Nato HQ as a direct link with Ukraine and has been involved with maintaining international support for president Volodymyr Zelensky’s efforts.

Days after McIntyre’s appointment, Reform published policies on how they would take a hard line with Putin.

A source told *The Independent*: “We know we had a problem to deal with, so we are dealing with it. We will make sure this slur does not continue.”

## New key personnel

After the enforced departure of chief of staff Dan Jukes and policy director James Orr after Channel 4 caught them in a sting over claims of illegal foreign donations, there has been a final recognition that the party needs to professionalise.

Part of this has involved kicking people out – most notably the party’s chief executive Tom Waterhouse, who had been in charge of the debacle over candidate selection and development, was sacked or “left to spend more time with his family”. Meanwhile, the underperforming media operation has seen new people come in.

Investigative reporter Miles Goslett, went from helping Lord Ashcroft research books including a recent one on Mr Farage, to being in charge of media strategy.

“He has been working behind the scenes but has been impressing everyone,” an insider said. “The whole operation is tightening up, becoming more responsive and more professional overall.”

![Miles Goslett has been overseeing Reform’s media operation](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/07/03/18/26/6753076-library.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

It is understood that Ed Sumner has been moved over running the press operation to support Mr Farage more now that Jukes is out and has impressed in his new role.

Meanwhile, one of Robert Jenrick’s staff, Tom Milford is the new head of press and has noticeably brought a new level of professionalism to the operation particularly in responding to allegations quickly.

“There was an issue of the party just not responding to journalists on many occasions,” one insider noted. “That had to stop.”

Added to that the Reform TV operation – a party broadcasting platform spread across various social media channels – has been slowly pushing ahead.

There had been hopes that long term ally Raheem Kassam would return from Washington DC to be Mr Farage’s new formal chief of staff, and while he does not have an official role he is understood to be a significant voice behind the scenes in righting the ship.

There is still a need for a chief of staff (and unlikely to be one because Mr Farage hates the idea) or chief executive to oversee the details of the operation and make tough decisions while Mr Farage fronts it.

Meanwhile, the candidates department was last described as “a work in progress” after a string of problems with councillors quitting, others being caught in an ITV investigation exposing racism and misogyny among party ranks and one high profile case of a senior councillor being charged with a dozen serious sex offences.

## The return of Brexit

*The Independent* has been told that if one thing that has happened which “will ensure Nigel definitely does not go away” it is the re-emergence of Brexit as an issue.

The Reform UK leader was in Brussels for Andy Burnham’s deal enthused again as he took on the fight, which made his political career.

He is set to launch a new version of his old Leave.EU campaign with allies Arron Banks and Andy Wigmore this week and, according to allies, “wants to make the next election about Brexit”.

![Arron Banks and Farage are Brexit allies](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/02/10/2206745925..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

While Reform UK has focussed on immigration they believe a row over rejoining the EU will have a much wider cut through with the public, bring voters back and deal with some of the issue of tactical voting, which has bedevilled Reform and lost them key by-elections in Caerphilly, Gorton and Denton and Makerfield.

Crucially, they also see Brexit as a weakness for Kemi Badenoch. Her desire to tinker with the reset not scrap it, the advice to her to avoid the subject and her record as business secretary to block EU regulation being stripped away are all now being ruthlessly attacked on social media.

Mr Farage recently said in an interview that his party rose too quickly in the polls in the aftermath of the 2024 election.

But he now believes that he has a root back and is preparing to stick to the fight even if it means he has to run in another Clacton by-election once the Standards Commissioner has ruled on his £5m personal donation from crypto billionaire Christopher Harborne.
