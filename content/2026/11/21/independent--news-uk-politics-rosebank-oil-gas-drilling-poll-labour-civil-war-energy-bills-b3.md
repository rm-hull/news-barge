A new poll has revealed that overwhelming public support for the government to allow drilling in the Rosebank oil and gas field in the North Sea would evaporate if it fails to lower bills.

The survey by Survation comes as Andy Burnham is being warned that Labour MPs are “on the brink of a civil war” over the decision in what is set to be the first political crisis of his premiership.

But former Labour donor and eco entrepreneur Dale Vince has warned that the prospect of lower bills as a result of allowing drilling is “a false promise”, which could backfire badly on Mr Burnham.

*The Independent* has been told that Labour MPs are evenly split on the whether to allow drilling in Rosebank after permission was granted for the Jackdaw oil and gas field last month.

It is understood that while 75 MPs wrote to Mr Burnham opposing more drilling, a similar number strongly support going ahead with it.

![Energy secretary Miatta Fahnbulleh has to make a decision on Rosebank](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/21/11/01M72D7A8BGN63V4MRHXS73CSF.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

One MP told *The Independent*: “We have done the numbers. It is about 100 strongly against and 100 strongly in favour. The rest don’t mind either way.

“But those are big numbers and we have told the whips that they need to calm down the opponents because if those of us who support drilling start speaking up and kicking up a fuss then the prime minister will have a civil war on his hands.”

Another MP in favour said: “We are quietly making our point behind the scenes but we feel as strongly, if not more strongly about this than those who are signing letters against it.”

There were reports last week that eight cabinet ministers are against drilling in Rosebank and a belief that the current energy secretary Miatta Fahnbulleh is one of them but cannot express a view because she has a quasi-judicial role over whether to allow it to go ahead. Another opponent is believed to be her predecessor Ed Miliband, who is now foreign secretary.

*The Independent* has been told that the divisions in Mr Burnham’s ministerial team “run much deeper”, with even junior ministers letting the government know their position on what is becoming a totemic issue.

“There is a chance we could see resignations over this issue,” one senior MP told *The Independent*.

But the message that drilling for oil and gas in Rosebank could bring down energy bills appears to have persuaded the majority of the public to support it.

The new national poll of 2,011 UK adults, conducted by Survation and commissioned by Mr Vince’s Green Britain Foundation, shows how far public attitudes to Rosebank are being shaped by the belief that new North Sea developments will bring down household energy bills.

![Activists unfurl giant banners spelling "STOP ROSEBANK" in London](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/10/10/2026-11-10T102603Z_1869399592_RC2WKOAKQS45_RTRMADP_3_BRITAIN-OIL-ROSEBANK.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Overall, 52 per cent support approving Rosebank and 19 per cent oppose it, a lead of 33 points.

But over a third (35 per cent) believe approving Rosebank would lower their energy bills, and among this group support is overwhelming: 73 per cent back approval and just 10 per cent are opposed.

Among everyone else, support falls to 41 per cent and opposition rises to 23 per cent.

Mr Vince has warned that oil from the North Sea is sold on international markets, so the price British households pay for energy is not set by whether individual projects such as Rosebank go ahead.

Equinor, Rosebank’s majority owner, has itself said the field’s output would be sold at world market prices and would not cut UK energy prices.

Mr Vince warned: “The findings raise serious questions about the impact of right-wing political rhetoric around North Sea oil and gas, particularly the repeated suggestion that more drilling will mean cheaper energy for British households.

“This poll exposes how heavily public support for Rosebank depends on the expectation of cheaper energy, on a false promise that new drilling cannot deliver.

“The polling suggests the case for Rosebank is far less convincing once the promise of cheaper bills is taken out of the equation. Successive governments have failed to make plain the facts about new drilling in the North Sea - specifically that it cannot lower our energy bills.”

![Dale Vince has warned Andy Burnham over false promises](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/09/28/08/2296975918..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

He claimed that Labour “has more to lose than to gain”.

He said: “Labour risks alienating the voters it most needs if it approves the oilfield. Andy Burnham risks pushing away the voters behind Labour's rapid recovery under his leadership. The big picture is that there is nothing to be gained politically, economically, or environmentally from approving Rosebank.”

The findings also raise questions about the political case for Labour approving Rosebank. With Labour on 27 per cent in the poll, the party needs to win support beyond its current voters to secure a second term in government.

The polling also suggests that approval would also damage Labour’s standing on climate. Under support for approval, 18 per cent say it would make them think the government takes climate change more seriously and 22 per cent less seriously.

Under support for rejection, 25 per cent say more seriously and 15 per cent less.

A Downing Street spokesperson said: “Rosebank is subject to a live quasi-judicial regulatory process. So we can't comment while that process ongoing.

“The energy secretary is reviewing all the relevant evidence and will make a decision in due course.”
