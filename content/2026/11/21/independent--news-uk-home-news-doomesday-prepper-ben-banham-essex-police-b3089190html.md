A doomsday prepper has been jailed for more than two years after he made a homemade explosive device and manufactured 2kg of gunpowder.

Ben Banham, 38, had planned to "use shock and awe tactics” and “long bangs and blinding flashes” as part of a scheme called “Project Prometheus” which he believed would provide "an effective defence of the family home", Basildon Crown Court heard.

Essex Police officers discovered the material and device after intelligence indicated Banham was acquiring chemicals capable of being used to manufacture explosive substances, detective constable Georgina Stroud, of Basildon CID said.

![Jailed: Ben Banham](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/21/15/59/ben-banham-custody-photo.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“This was a proactive investigation which identified a significant quantity of homemade gunpowder and a viable explosive device before anyone came to harm,” she said.

Officers from Basildon Neighbourhood Policing Team searched Banham’s address in Rokescroft, Pitsea, in March earlier this year when he was not present.

The force not only found a homemade explosive device and large quantities of homemade gunpowder, they found Banham also had the means to manufacture a further 2kg. They also found several air weapons, a crossbow, knives and stockpiles of food and provisions.

DC Stroud added: “The evidence recovered by officers demonstrated the seriousness of Banham's actions and the risk posed by the materials being stored and manufactured at his home.”

Banham’s handwritten diary had detailed notes about his plans and ambitions relating to the manufacture of explosive substances.

Officers also located a homemade explosive device wrapped in black tape and fitted with a fuse.

![Banham had detailed diary entries revealing 'Project Prometheus'](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/21/18/25/banham-diary-entry.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The Ministry of Defence’s Explosive Ordnance Disposal team was deployed after the device was discovered, and a 100-metre cordon was established. The force evacuated 80 homes while the device and other potentially dangerous materials were assessed and made safe.

A bomb disposal robot was deployed to safely deal with the device, while specialist teams recovered the chemicals and homemade gunpowder.

Banham was later arrested at work. As part of the investigation, officers discovered Banham had conversed extensively with an AI chatbot, which positively reinforced his plans.

DC Stroud concluded: “This case demonstrates that we will act decisively where information suggests someone is unlawfully manufacturing explosive substances.

“Through thorough investigation and partnership working, we were able to prevent a potentially dangerous situation from escalating and ensure Banham was brought before the courts."

Banham admitted making an explosive substance and was found not guilty after trial at Basildon Crown Court of having the substance with intent to endanger life.

He also admitted possession of a knife in a private place. He was sentenced on Thursday to a total of 28 months in prison.
