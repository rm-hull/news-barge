Pilotless fighter jets will fly in UK airspace from next year, according to the Royal Air Force chief.

Air chief marshal Sir Harv Smyth has said that uncrewed fighter jets will start flying in UK airspace in 2027 as the RAF adapts to new challenges. It comes as the war in Ukraine has seen unmanned aerial vehicles become more significant.

Writing for *The Telegraph, Sir Harv said**:* “By far, uncrewed systems promise the greatest change. Ukraine’s inexpensive drones gather intelligence and reach far beyond the front line.

“I firmly believe that AI-enabled, uncrewed aircraft will transform military aviation as profoundly as the shift from propellers to jets,” he continued. “Uncrewed fighter jets will start flying in UK airspace next year.”

![](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/05/01/21/2266159471.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Typhoons and F-35s will be combined with autonomous systems, Sir Harv explained, with AI and machines tackling the most dangerous tasks instead of humans.

He caveated that humans would not become less important in the RAF though: “Technology must enhance human judgment, not replace accountability.

“This will be a key initiative of the UK’s collaboration with Italy and Japan to spearhead the future of fighter jets as part of the Global Combat Air Programme.

“The GCAP jet is perhaps the last crewed fighter we will ever build, not because people will become less important, quite the opposite.”

He went on to say that the RAF still hoped to grow, with around 300 trainees graduating monthly.

“We will ensure they have excellent equipment, training and support: ready to deter and, if deterrence fails, to fly, fight and win.”

![Typhoon fighter jets will be combined with autonomous systems](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/02/17/2301303588..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The RAF debuted a remotely piloted aircraft called Protector in October, which tracked Russian shipping through the English Channel for 10 uninterrupted hours in controlled airspace in its first UK operational mission, he said per the newspaper.

“With endurance of up to 40 hours, the potential of this uncrewed system is phenomenal.”

It’s the latest move in automation from the Ministry of Defence, days after it announced £25 million investment in drones and “cutting edge battlefield technology”.

The funding came from the Defence Investment Plan to equip British forces protecting NATO’s eastern flank in Estonia.

Defence secretary Wes Streeting MP said on Tuesday: “This investment will increase our deterrence on NATO’s front line, with our troops in Estonia being kitted out with the latest drones and capabilities.

“It will mean our Army can move faster, be more agile and strike the enemy harder. And it shows how we’re investing today to step up our deterrence and readiness for tomorrow.”

The Ministry of Defence did not offer any further comment.
