A campaign to take Britain back into the EU is set to launch as the debate around the UK’s future relationship with Europe reignites.

*The Independent* has learnt about plans for the formation of the umbrella group, which is expected to front a rejoin campaign if a referendum is called on whether to reverse Brexit.

The group has been named “Get In!”, inspired by shouts from the football terraces. A number of pro-EU organisations have signed up to campaign under the banner, which already has suggested a logo, website, social media and slogan.

While currently a directory of groups rather than a formal coalition, the grouping is the start of the official campaign to rejoin.

Among the ideas is a suggestion to ape the infamous Vote Leave red bus slogan of “£350 million a week for the NHS”, with their own “£520 million a week to spend on public services” – a figure the group says would be generated for the UK through economic growth and additional tax receipts should it rejoin the EU.

The campaign has been brought together rapidly over recent weeks to ensure a push to rejoin, which currently has majority support in polls of UK voters.

![The new campaign umbrella group supporting rejoining the EU](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/21/9/16/Screenshot-2026-11-21-094139.png?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

A source said: “Partners have been talking about this for weeks. It has been deliberately under the radar, but it is about getting the structure and tone right to make sure it is about collaboration, grassroots, openness and representation.”

It comes after Andy Burnham’s historic deal in Brussels on Friday, which secured the removal of food and drink trade barriers, agreed a youth mobility scheme for 100,000 under-31s from both the UK and the EU, brought in an emissions-sharing scheme for energy, and finalised a data-sharing scheme to tackle human traffickers.

But the prime minister has already said that he wants the UK to rejoin the EU and is about to launch a national conversation on the future of Brexit a decade after the referendum.

Any pledge to rejoin would have to appear in a future election manifesto, after Sir Keir Starmer won the 2024 general election promising red lines of not joining the EU, Single Market, Customs Union or accepting free movement.

The Get In! directory of pro-EU groups will feature Britain’s two largest anti-Brexit organisations: European Movement UK and Best For Britain.

But others already signed up include Stay European, Save British Farming, the European Movement in Scotland, Black Europeans, Bremain in Spain, Wales for Europe, and the Young European Movement.

More are expected to join up.

A spokesperson for one of the organisations said: “The prime minister has started the long-overdue conversation on what to do about the UK’s sluggish growth, poor economic performance, and daily hassle of travelling or doing business outside the European Union.

“This directory of organisations is a timely reminder that we represent the majority of the public who think Brexit has been a mistake, through institutions that can convene millions of voters, businesses and consumer interest groups that are pushing the government to take us back into the heart of Europe.”

It is also a direct riposte to the pro-Brexit campaign group launched by Lord Michael Gove to replace Vote Leave called Stay Out.

![Michael Gove is leading Stay Out](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/18/22/2297503183..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

One source involved behind Get In! told *The Independent*: “Stay Out is so negative, it sounds like an old farmer telling someone to keep off their land or someone telling kids they cannot play football in a field.

“Get In! is very positive. Like when someone scores a goal. We want to emphasise that rejoining is a positive thing and contrast with the Project Fear of the other side.”

Currently the hope is that the campaign is “citizen-led” rather than being fronted by a politician.

“This has got to be a grassroots thing and not become tied up with the career of a politician making it about themselves and their political ambitions,” a source said.

“The Brexiteers can do that with Gove if they want. It won’t help them much.”

There has been a sense of lessons being learnt from the failed 2016 Remain campaign, which centred around George Osborne, David Cameron and ex-M&S boss Lord Rose.

It is already noted that the “Project Fear” message that Remain was accused of in 2016 is being deployed by the Brexiteers instead now, with fears over the cost of EU membership or immigration from places like Albania.

“The Brexiteers are now the ones trying to defend the status quo and scare people off changing back even though Brexit has been a complete failure,” another source noted.

Nigel Farage is also set to launch a rival pro-Brexit campaign group this week, in similar fashion to how the leave campaign was run under two separate banners – Vote Leave and Leave.EU – in 2016.
