A top Democrat warned Elon Musk that “every” U.S. government contract with SpaceX has been called into question, the latest sign that congressional Democrats are preparing to investigate Donald Trump’s administration officials and his allies.

Connecticut Rep. Jim Himes, who is poised to lead the House Intelligence Committee in January, issued the warning in a letter to the SpaceX CEO Friday, demanding answers about last month’s disruptions in Ukraine to Starshield, a U.S. government-purchased service that uses SpaceX’s Starlink satellite network.

Musk acknowledged in an October post on X that the company intentionally restricted its Starlink service in Ukraine, hindering military efforts to combat Russia. He claimed Ukraine violated Starlink’s terms by using the civilian commercial service for “offensive drone strikes.” Starshield, he added, is controlled by the U.S. government.

“I write to formally request that senior SpaceX officials meet with me to explain the Starshield service disruptions in Ukraine in October 2026,” Himes wrote in his letter. “My committee staff have requested this briefing on multiple occasions but have received no substantive response from your company.”

Himes said Starshield’s reliance on Starlink satellites — more than 10,000 of which are in orbit — does not give SpaceX the right to make “unilateral” changes to the government-contracted service.

![A top Democrat warned Elon Musk that ‘every’ U.S. government contract with SpaceX has been called into question following service disruptions in Ukraine last month](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/21/20/52/GettyImages-2299248528.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“My view, which I have conveyed to senior Administration officials, is that SpaceX’s potentially unilateral modification of a U.S. Government contract, combined with its lack of responsiveness to congressional oversight, calls into question every contract the U.S. Government has with SpaceX,” he wrote.

“For that reason, it is difficult for me to continue supporting the authorization of funding for ... SpaceX-developed, government-owned systems until I receive satisfactory answers,” he added.

SpaceX, which went public in June, had about $22 billion in government contracts as of last February.

“SpaceX has done more to defend Ukraine than any private company on Earth BY FAR,” Musk wrote on X last month. “That said, I have nothing but UTTER CONTEMPT for the virtue-signaling evil warmongers who push for endless slaughter and offer no realistic solution to the conflict.”

The war in Ukraine, which began with Russia’s full-scale invasion in 2022, has dragged on for more than four years, claiming hundreds of thousands of lives. Repeated efforts by the Trump administration to broker a peace deal have so far failed.

*The Independent* has contacted the company for comment.

Himes’ letter is the latest sign that Democrats plan to probe Trump administration officials and allies when they take control of Congress in January following their midterm election victories.

Asked about Musk last week, House Speaker-designate Hakeem Jeffries vowed to investigate “everyone” involved in the “MAGA extremist movement.” Musk, who spearheaded DOGE and donated millions to Republicans, recently declared he is “not MAGA” despite campaigning for the president in his signature black MAGA hat.

Also last week, Rep. Robert Garcia, the Oversight Committee’s top Democrat, wrote letters to a dozen administration officials and others in Trump’s orbit, requesting that they preserve documents, signaling an investigation could be forthcoming.

Among those who received letters were President Donald Trump’s eldest sons, Vice President JD Vance and Attorney General Todd Blanche.

In an apparent bid to thwart Democratic investigations, the White House has instructed federal officials and government contractors to obtain permission before testifying on Capitol Hill.

Blanche has said the Department of Justice will cooperate with Congress but insisted “we will not back down” from Trump’s agenda.
