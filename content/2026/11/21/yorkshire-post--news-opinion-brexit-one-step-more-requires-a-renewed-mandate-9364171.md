# Brexit: one step more requires a renewed mandate

![From right, European Council President Antonio Costa, Britain's Prime Minister Andy Burnham and European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen. Credit: AP](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOjhhZjM0ZjVhLWU2ZjgtNGU1ZC1iNjZhLTIyNDEwY2I0NjQ2Yzo5YjhmNWZjYi03M2FmLTRhZjgtOWY3Ni1lYzUxYzAwOTFjMzk=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=990&quality=70&enable=upscale)


## With Prime Minister Andy Burnham yesterday beaming from ear-to-ear, hands interlocked between those of Antonio Costa, President of the European Council and Ursula von der Leyen, President of the European Commission, the body language was clear: unity. That Mr Burnham’s European counterparts, too, were delighted following yesterday’s summit in Brussels suggested all parties came away pleased from the negotiations.

Describing his Brexit reset as an ‘historic moment’, and a ‘win-win’ arrangement for both the United Kingdom and for the European Union, Mr Burnham moved quickly to the size of the prize as he sees it, pointing to a £17bn boost to the UK economy in this Parliamentary term alone from the closer ties forged yesterday with our European neighbours.

A decade on from the acrimonious EU Referendum vote, in which these self-same protagonists were castigated as belligerent bogeymen intent on enslaving Britain to the big, bad European project - rule takers not rule makers, etc - the picture painted yesterday of those across the water could not have been more different. In truth, what happened was more of an opportunity to deploy a litmus test into the pool of public opinion in this country when it comes to any potential future relationship for the UK and the EU. Ignore the cries of betrayal and surrender; this was fig-leaf realignment, in the grand scheme of EU-membership, that changes little in policy terms, but does an awful lot in bilateral relationship terms.

Brexit burned bridges, fractured relationships and dissolved trust on both sides of the English Channel, creating a separation and subsequent drift apart of countries that have so many shared interests.

But without a mandate from the British people to go further, Mr Burnham must now halt his Euro-train and decide: does he believe he can win one?
