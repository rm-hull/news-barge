## A number of countries, including France and Panama, released a letter on Friday insisting that a deal include "a clear transition away from fossil fuels."

The COP31 climate talks have pushed into Saturday as countries race to secure a deal after a draft text dropped references to fossil fuels, prompting pushback from nations including France and Panama.

Negotiators from nearly 200 countries have struggled to work through deep divisions over fossil fuels and climate finance at the conference, which is being held in Antalya, Turkey.

"We are close to the finish line, but we are not there yet," COP31 President and Turkish Environment Minister Murat Kurum told an assembly late on Friday.

A new agreement is expected to be published on Saturday.

A draft deal unveiled earlier this week had called for a sped-up phasing out of fossil fuels. But a second version released on Thursday removed all mentions of the energy sources, forcing negotiators back to the bargaining table.

The European Union said the revised draft risked "turning this climate COP into a fossil summit."

A number of countries, including France and Panama, shared a letter on Friday insisting that the deal include "a clear transition away from fossil fuels."

Panama has also threatened to walk away from the talks, with its environment minister, Juan Carlos Navarro, accusing a "petroleum mafia" of blocking progress.

Australian Climate Minister Chris Bowen, who is leading negotiations, said he would work "for as long as it takes, to get the job done."

It came as the OPEC oil cartel said its Secretary General Haitham al-Ghais had met with Russian climate envoy Ruslan Edelgeriev at COP31 to discuss "the importance of inclusive and balanced approaches across all multilateral environmental fora."

"Discussions focused on exchanging views on the UN climate negotiations and enhancing the cooperation between OPEC and the Russian Federation to advance international climate action and sustainable development," it said in a post on X.
