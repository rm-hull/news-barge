# Exclusive:Brexit: use EU deal to sell more Yorkshire Tea, northerners tell poll

## The polling comes as Andy Burnham announced a new deal on food and drink exports with the EU.

Yorkshire Tea is the famous product that northerners want to see Europeans buy more of after Andy Burnham’s EU deal is set to slash red tape and costs around food and drink exports.

The Prime Minister said it was a “good day for Britain and a good day for Europe”, as he signed an agreement alongside continental chiefs in Brussels yesterday.

This covers food and drink exports, emissions trading, and a youth experience scheme which could open the doors for 100,000 young Europeans to temporarily work in the UK, with Britons able to do the same on the continent.

This will see Britain align with the EU on trade rules on sanitary and phytosanitary (SPS) goods, such as food and plants, from March 2028, cutting red tape and bureaucracy, while reducing the UK’s input.

![Prime Minister Andy Burnham and Chancellor of the Exchequer John Healey attending the EU-UK summit in Brussels, Belgium (Hannah McKay/PA)](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/jpim-static/image/2026/11/20/16/v2-image-production-555cb90bb70f6f1e200fc326cdf1dc4cY29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzk1Mjc0ODY3-2.86548669.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


The Government said it would see costly export health certificates scrapped and allow British favourites, like sausages, burgers and seafood, to be sold once again in Europe and Northern Ireland.

Retailers welcomed the trade deal, including managing director of Marks & Spencer Food, Alex Freudmann, saying it is a “significant step” towards smoother trade and less bureaucracy.

He wrote: “We’ve long championed a UK-EU food trade deal, and today’s announcement is a major step forward.

“Getting our Spaghetti Bolognese from Lincolnshire onto a shelf in Dublin takes hundreds of extra miles, mountains of paperwork, a vet’s signature and even the Latin name of a cow.

The Yorkshire Post can reveal that the product northerners are most proud of to see across the Channel is Yorkshire Tea.

![Yorkshire Tea. Credit: Getty](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/jpim-static/image/2026/11/20/17/00/GettyImages-1074405004.jpg?trim=0,0,0,0&width=640&quality=65)


A survey of 1,869 adults across the North of England, by Fortius Global, found that 40 per cent would most like to see Europeans buying more of the iconic beverage brand from God’s Own Country.

That was comfortably ahead of Sheffield steel on 31 per cent, which will be freed up through the carbon emissions trading scheme agreement, and Wensleydale cheese

Other northern favourites included Cumberland sausages, Newcastle Brown Ale, Vimto and Barbour jackets.

Mr Burnham said: “This deal is a win-win – and it cuts costs for business, helps with the cost of the weekly shop, and creates opportunities for young people.”

However, the Prime Minister faced criticism from farming groups such as the Country Land and Business Association.

President Gavin Lane said: “In the short term, there will be significant benefits to exporters but it's naive to think that there won't be challenges in the long term.

“When the EU comes up with a new regulation that may damage British farmers or producers, we will have no recourse. This doesn't bode well for democracy.

“The political instability this will cause risks overshadowing many of the benefits the deal will bring.”
