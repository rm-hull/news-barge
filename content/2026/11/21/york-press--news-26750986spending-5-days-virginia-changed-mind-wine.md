Alcohol is something I can take or leave, and if you’d have asked me a few weeks ago if I liked wine, I’d have said no and not budged on my answer.

However, as I’ve gotten older, I’ve tried to push my palate with drinks, taking a liking to coffee in the last couple of years, which I once very much avoided, and now wine, apparently.

Having been invited to explore Virginia’s wine region, I jumped at the chance, not only to explore a new area of the US I’d never been to before but to finally indulge in the world of wine and see if I could actually get on board with a glass (or three).

The thing is, I’d only ever previously tried a very small sample of wine and more often than not, my nose had scrunched up, and it wasn’t uncommon for me to be laughed at for my reactions.

![](https://www.oxfordmail.co.uk/resources/images/21719944.jpg?type=mds-article-620)I tried lots of wines on the trip and was pleased to find some I really liked *(Image: Newsquest)*

But I knew deep down I’d not given the popular beverage even half a chance.

So I set off to Virginia for five days of vineyard visits and wine tastings with Virginia Wine, which I’m pleased to say was a fantastic experience.

While I found some brilliant wines that I loved, met some incredible people and had a great time away, Virginia’s wine region taught me a lot more than what a nice wine tastes like.

Join me as I debrief my trip to the wine region that is making some fantastic wines and hopes to share its creations with the wider world.

## 5 things Virginia’s wine region taught me

### 1. Stepping out of your comfort zone can lead to incredible opportunities

First of all, as cliché as it sounds, going on this trip taught me that stepping outside your comfort zone can be hugely rewarding.

Of course I’ve heard this said a dozen times, but on this trip, I actually felt it.

Learning what I need to be looking for in a glass and how to describe the flavours is just the tip of the iceberg of things I found out in Virginia, which was at first something that I felt was a little out of my depth.

By the middle of the week and certainly by the end, I felt much more comfortable, and as though I’d really tapped into a huge knowledge gap I didn’t realise I had and, best of all, I was enjoying it.

### 2. Togetherness and community are the keys to success

While I know how important working as a team is, the warmth of the community in Virginia takes this to a whole other level.

You’d think that several winemakers all trying to make the best wine in the region would have some stiff competition, but I didn’t get this impression at all.

At every vineyard, I was amazed at how supportive each producer was of the other vineyards down the road.

Not only do they know the winemakers, but they know how they work and what they are making, and it's clear that the relationships are genuine.

Virginia’s wine region is doing all it can to share its creations with more people, both in the US and abroad, and it’s clear to see that the winemakers are supportive of each other and hope for the same success.

Maybe we should all take a leaf out of Virginia’s book.

![](https://www.oxfordmail.co.uk/resources/images/21719942.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Virginia's countryside offers some truly stunning and tranquil spots *(Image: Newsquest)*

### 3. The power of the countryside

Virginia’s wine region really does have some of the best countryside views I’ve been fortunate to witness.

Not only was the warm October sunshine very welcome, but the rolling hills, vineyards and green space all around made me really wish we had more of that in the UK.

While we have some stunning landscapes here, it was unusual to see two houses built next to one another when I was being driven between different towns.

The houses I did see were huge, with lots of land and idyllic surroundings, making me think I’d fallen onto a film set.

There was definitely a relaxing air about Virginia.

### 4. Wine terminology

Speaking with winemakers on visits to their vineyards and over dinner really opened my eyes to the terminology used to describe wine and its array of flavours.

From the tanks and barrels used to make and store wine to the levels of acidity in each glass, there really was a lot to learn.

I enjoyed learning about the process of making and storing the wine, including how the weather and climate change impacts the harvest each year.

Before going to Virginia, I’d never done a wine tasting, so I had no clue what a spit bucket was for, but after tasting sometimes as many as 20 or so wines a day, I can see its importance!

Like a sponge, I tried to take in all the new phrases and, of course, aim to impress next time my friends open a bottle of wine.

![](https://www.oxfordmail.co.uk/resources/images/21719945.jpg?type=mds-article-620)I visited several of Virginia's vineyards and wineries on the trip, all of which had a cosy feel to them *(Image: Newsquest)*

### 5. Appreciation for wine makers

Virginia’s wine region taught me so much more about wine than what a label on a bottle ever could.

It made me appreciate the hard work that vineyard owners and winemakers put into making wine.

Not only is it a challenge to make and offer something different, but they face other challenges such as the weather and wanting to sell their wine far and wide.

Overall, I had a fantastic time in Virginia and would love to go back, to drink more wine, frolic in its open countryside and see its tourist hotspots.

I’m sad that the Virginian wines I loved will be difficult, if not impossible, to buy in the UK for a while longer, but next time I’m in a bar, I’ll be looking at the labels on the wine bottles more closely, hoping to stumble across one.
