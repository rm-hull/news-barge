It’s a different kind of war on Christmas.

A holiday-loving North Carolinian is suing his homeowners’ association after the group tried to restrict his annual festive light display.

Neil Heuer has decked out his home in Aero Plantation, part of an exclusive community in Waxhaw, about 30 miles south of Charlotte, for the past five years. His Halloween and Christmas light shows include about 60,000 synchronized LED lights, special effects and festive music that transmits to spectators’ car radios.

However, Heuer’s holiday cheer has led to a bitter battle with the Aero Plantation Association. The HOA determined in September that his display, which he promotes on social media as “High Flying Lights,” violated neighborhood rules on exterior lighting and signs and attracted unwanted traffic, Heuer told *The Independent.*

Neighbors in the expensive fly-in community — which features its own air strip for planes to land and take off from— are now also being asked to vote on new rules that would completely ban Heuer’s festive displays, as well as other holiday lights.

![North Carolina homeowner Neil Heuer filed a lawsuit against his HOA after it tried to restrict his festive holiday decorations](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/18/20/06/North-Carolina-holiday-decorations-lawsuit.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

In Heuer’s opinion, the conflict hardly has anything to do with holiday lights but stems from a previous dispute he had with the HOA over improvements it was making to the neighborhood’s golf course.

When Heuer felt his concerns about the high cost weren’t being heard, he wrote an anonymous newsletter to vocalize his questions about the project.

“Some people learned about my identity, and they, I believe, shortly thereafter, they started to use the HOA as a weapon against me and my light show,” Heuer told *The Independent.*

Heuer says he loves the holidays and getting into the spirit. He puts on the show simply to bring joy to the neighborhood.

“I want everybody to be happy and like it,” he said.

While the current dispute has garnered some negative feedback, mainly from the HOA, he has also been met with positivity.

“One of my neighbors stopped two days ago and I didn’t know who she was. She rolled down her window and I got nervous ... .and she said to me, ‘Thank you for bringing joy to our neighborhood.’ and then she said, ‘I’m sorry you have to go through what you’re going through,’” he said.

![Heuer says he feels he is being unfairly targeted over his holiday lights due to previous issues he raised with his homeowners’ association](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/18/20/33/North-Carolina-holiday-decorations-lawsuit.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Another neighbor sent him a video of her 93-year-old mother “giggling and laughing while watching the show,” Heuer said.

“Those interactions make you feel good,” he added.

While Heuer had been putting up the holiday displays for several years, things took a turn after he got into a disagreement with the HOA vice president over a proposal to “rejuvenate” the community’s golf course. Heuer opposed the plan to use reserve funds on improvements. The vice president later offered to pick up the tab — and insisted that the annual maintenance on the course would be about $8,000.

“What I did was create an anonymous newsletter that was asking questions that were somewhat critical of the HOA. I feel like our HOA wasn’t transparent,” he said.

Heuer later learned that the golf course’s maintenance would cost $16,000 — twice as much as promised — leaving residents of the expensive neighborhood “saddled with somebody else’s project that we have to maintain.”

Heuer believes the current dispute over his light show is simply retaliation for his earlier disagreement with the HOA.

Earlier this year, the HOA held a hearing on the holiday display, claiming that it violated the community guidelines regarding exterior lighting, signage and brought in unwanted traffic and visitors to the exclusive neighborhood.

![Heuer says he previously created an anonymous letter that was critical of the HOA, and says he believes that is why his decorations are being targeted now](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/18/20/49/North-Carolina-holiday-decorations-lawsuit.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The HOA warned that future lighting violations could bring fines of $100 per day, so Heuer, trying to get ahead of the conflict, offered a compromise.

In a letter to the HOA, Heuer’s attorney said he would operate his holiday light display at 30 percent of its maximum brightness. He also offered to get rid of powerful moving head lights that could project beams into the sky, as well as a large banner promoting the light show.

Heuer tested a powerful light fixture intended for his Halloween display September 28 — and was hit with a $100 fine the following day. Three days later, his proposal was rejected by the association.

Heuer filed a lawsuit against Aero Plantation Association in Union County Superior Court October 6, claiming that the HOA overstepped its authority and failed to provide proper notice and hearing procedures. He also argued that the restrictions being enforced against him have never been formally adopted in the bylaws. He is seeking a judicial resolution over the dispute.

While the case is ongoing, the HOA board sent out a notice to residents that it was adopting new parking rules in the community specifically targeting non-resident traffic. The HOA is also asking homeowners to vote on two new bylaws.

![Residents of the Aero Plantation neighborhood have until January 30 to vote on new rules that could limit holiday decor allowed outside of homes in the future](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/18/20/24/North-Carolina-holiday-decorations-lawsuit.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The bylaws would create “standards for public attractions and activities that generate substantial or recurring nonresident traffic within the community” and “standards for flashing, strobing, pulsating, and high-intensity exterior lighting.”

Residents have until January 30 to vote on the new rules, meaning Heuer is holding at least one more big holiday spectacle.

“I’m really hope that people will realize that it’s ridiculous. The ridiculous rules. We don’t need more rules,” Heuer told *The Independent.* “In the future, when somebody goes to buy a home in that neighborhood, they’re going to remember that Aero Plantation is the neighborhood that doesn’t allow Christmas lights.”

“And I think people are going to say, ‘I want to have Christmas lights,’ and ‘I don’t know that I want to live with people that hate Christmas lights so much.’” Heuer added.

In a statement to *The Independent*, HOA president David Arno said: “Mr. Heuer filed a lawsuit against the Association, to which the Association will respond through the appropriate legal process. The North Carolina Planned Community Act authorizes an HOA’s members to implement additional changes to a Declaration, which is what members are being asked to decide.”

He added: “The Board does not intend to comment further other than to remind members to voice their opinions by deciding if they want to approve the proposed changes or not.”

*The Independent* has also contacted the HOA’s attorney Benjamin Karb for comment.
