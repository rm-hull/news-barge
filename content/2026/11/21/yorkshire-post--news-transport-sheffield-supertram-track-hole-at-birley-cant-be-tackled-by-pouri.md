# Sheffield Supertram track hole at Birley “can’t be tackled by pouring concrete down it”

## The ‘Birley tram hole’ that has disrupted Supertram services is a complicated problem that can’t be solved by pouring concrete down it, South Yorkshire Mayor, Oliver Coppard, says.

He was asked by the Local Democracy Reporting Service about the issue during his announcement of the “revolutionary” changes to South Yorkshire bus services that will begin from next September.

That is when buses join Supertram services as part of the South Yorkshire People’s Network, after the service is brought back under public control.

The mayor and colleagues outlined how buses will be reliable, cheaper and reach more places and that more services will run on Sundays and evenings.

![Tram hole at Birley Lane. Credit: Oliver Coppard](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOjk0Yzg3Mzk1LTJmMjQtNDc2Zi1hZWJhLTQ5NDA4ZjNlNmM3MTo4N2M4MmMxMi1jNjMyLTRmMmEtOWFkOS1mMjRmNzVjZTVjYjA=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


To find out now how the changes will affect your service, go online to [https://www.southyorkshire-ca.gov.uk/explore\_network-changes](https://www.southyorkshire-ca.gov.uk/explore\_network-changes).

Interactive maps show the changes taking place over the next two years.

The mayor said that the old mineshaft found under the tram tracks at Birley in August actually shows how much attitudes have changed under public management of Supertram.

“The Birley tram hole was a problem that was literally unearthed as a result of tram drivers telling us there was an issue there,” he said.

“It was something sat under there for over 30 years. We tackled that as swiftly as humanly possible.

“It is taking a significant amount of money to run replacement bus services. We are absolutely determined to get people back on the tram network.”

Replacement bus BL1 is operating between Gleadless Townend and Halfway while the blue route remains suspended past Gleadless Townend.

Complex engineering solutions to deal with the problem are being explored at pace, said Mayor Coppard,

“It’s clearly not just as simple as pouring concrete down it, as people have suggested,” he said.

“We need people back on the tram safely.”

He said he hoped that the issue could be resolved by February.

Mayor Coppard also said that he has spoken to traders at Crystal Peaks about how the issue has affected them.

Supertram is a main public transport route to the shopping centre.

The centre manager said the overall effect on trade is only small but it’s a significant impact for individual businesses, the mayor added.
