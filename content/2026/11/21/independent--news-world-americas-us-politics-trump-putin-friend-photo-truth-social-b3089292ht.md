Donald Trump doubled down on calling Vladimir Putin “my friend” with an intimate photo tribute posted to Truth Social on Saturday.

The Republican president raised eyebrows with his warm greeting for the Russian leader at a summit in China this week, as Moscow’s war in Ukraine stretched beyond its fourth year.

Entering a room in Shenzhen, Trump paused, pointed at Putin and jokingly asked “Who is he?” before breaking into a smile. As he approached his Russian counterpart, he declared, “He’s my friend!”

Back at the White House on Friday, Trump was asked whether Putin was also a friend to the United States. “I can't speak to America,” he replied, while emphasizing his “very good” personal relationship with the Russian leader.

Trump underscored that sentiment on Saturday, posting an image of himself standing close to Putin and shaking his hand beneath a caption in large type: “My Friend.” It was one of dozens of photos he shared from the summit throughout the course of the day, many of which depicted him alongside the Russian leader and Chinese President Xi Jinping.

![Donald Trump doubled down on his remark calling Vladimir Putin 'my friend' in a Truth Social post Saturday](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/21/22/24/fc84069ff8f1ecc2.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Democrats widely condemned Trump’s remarks, pointing to Putin’s autocratic rule and his ongoing war against Ukraine, which the U.S. has backed.

“He’s the man who has abducted tens of thousands of children, killed tens of thousands of civilians, targeted civilian infrastructure across Ukraine, detained Americans wrongfully, and continues to do so daily,” Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer wrote on X.

New Hampshire Senator Jeanne Shaheen described Putin as a “murderous war criminal.”

“Putin is not my friend. He’s not your friend,” Arizona Senator Mark Kelly added. “He’s not a friend of this country, and when the president of the United States refers to him in that way, it emboldens him.”

Trump on Friday, however, insisted that his personal relationships with foreign leaders were key to achieving peace.

“You know, you're very lucky to have somebody that has a relationship with the heads of the most powerful countries,” he said.

Secretary of State Marco Rubio, who joined Trump for the Friday press conference, echoed that sentiment.

“I want everybody to understand the relationships between heads of state are invaluable," Rubio told reporters. “It sets the tone for the entire relationship. It sets the tone for our trade negotiators. It sets the tone for our diplomats.”

Those personal relationships, however, have yet to deliver the peace Trump promised in Ukraine, where the war has dragged on for more than four years despite his campaign trail claim he could end it within 24 hours.

![The president shared dozens of photos from the summit, many of which depicted him alongside the Russian leader and Chinese President Xi Jinping](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/21/23/53/2c79e8548121f03d-d72585665dc8.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

According to a January YouGov survey, just 8 percent of Americans have a favorable view of Putin, while 77 percent hold an unfavorable view.

For years, Democrats have accused Trump of cozying up to foreign dictators while alienating America’s democratic allies, arguing that his praise for leaders such as Putin and his threats to abandon NATO partners undermine U.S. leadership and embolden its adversaries.

The GOP president has long pushed back on such criticism. In 2018, he said: “Getting along with Russia would be a good thing, not a bad thing.” He has also accused Democrats of promoting what he calls the “Russia hoax,” referring to investigations into Russian interference in the 2016 election and his campaign’s ties to Moscow.
