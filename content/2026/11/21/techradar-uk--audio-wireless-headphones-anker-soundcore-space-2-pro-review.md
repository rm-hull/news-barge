### TechRadar Verdict

The Anker Soundcore Space 2 Pro feel a bit messy. While they offer some of the best ANC and call quality I’ve experienced at this price-point, their overzealous bass, weakened battery life, and hilariously bad AI Chat integration mean that they fail to be the perfect upgrade over their predecessor. They still sound decent with some tuning, and remain incredibly comfortable, but given their price increase (in some markets) over the original Space One Pro, I can’t recommend them outright.

## Anker Soundcore Space 2 Pro review

The Anker Soundcore Space 2 Pro appear to be a massively ambitious pair of headphones when you consider their relatively moderate asking price. They offer adaptive ANC powered by eight microphones, higher-res audio playback, and, according to Anker, “the world’s clearest calls”.

Yes, it really feels like Anker is hyping up this model with everything it's got, and given how much I enjoyed the Space 2 Pro’s predecessor, it’s safe to say that my expectations were set pretty high for these.

So can the Anker Soundcore Space 2 Pro blast off to new heights and take on the best headphones around? Or will they struggle to take off altogether? Here’s my verdict after two solid weeks of testing.

![Anker logo on the Anker Soundcore Space 2 Pro](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/KWULDS9UJdDYoKHLcyzo8R-1200-80.jpg)

Perhaps the biggest change this model makes over the original Anker Soundcore Space One Pro is the inclusion of the major tech firm’s Thus chip. This provides the Space 2 Pro with considerably higher processing power than their predecessor and new AI capabilities. So let’s go through the headline features one by one.

First up, we’ve got AI-powered clear calls, which use a six microphone array to reduce background noise in real time and deliver squeaky clean speech. And I’m happy to report that this works very well indeed. On a phone call, I was told that my voice sounded clean-cut, with no pesky sounds in the background obscuring my words. When I took a recording, my speech sounded natural and well-defined once again.

“World’s clearest calls” may be a stretch — whether they have a Guinness World Record or not. I’ve definitely experienced even better clarity from premium cans like the Sony WH-1000XM6, but they’re double the price of these.

Something else the Thus chip powers is the Space 2 Pro’s active noise cancellation. According to Anker, the chip cuts out 384,000 noise signals per second with the help of an eight-mic system, so you’d expect pretty great results, right? Well, you should, because the ANC on the Space 2 Pro is absolutely sublime.

When listening to music on my walk to work — along an incredibly busy road — the Space 2 Pro cut out traffic noise effortlessly. Everything from zippy scooters to hulking trucks zoomed past without interrupting my jam at all. While bumping tunes at middling volumes, I also couldn’t hear the sound of typing or chatter in my office whatsoever. The ANC here is significantly better than it was on the original Space One Pro, and it’s the best I’ve experienced from any headphones at this price-point.

There’s also a super-effective transparency mode for when you need to be more aware of your surroundings and a wind-reduction option for listening on gusty days, giving you exceptional control over your listening experience.

And it doesn’t stop there, with so many options at your fingertips in Anker’s excellent companion app. Soundcore’s HeardID 5.0 tests your hearing and preferences to generate a bespoke EQ curve, but there are also a bunch of presets and an eight-band custom equalizer.

Pair that with LDAC for higher-res Bluetooth listening and 3.5mm lossless wired audio, customizable button controls, and wear detection, and you’re getting far more than most rivals offer at this price-point.

The Space 2 Pro have proven to be mighty impressive thus far, haven’t they? But it’s time to burst your bubble a little bit. Because for all of the improvements these headphones make over their predecessors, there are some issues that I have to point out.

First off, let’s talk about battery life. Here, you’re getting 30 hours of playtime with ANC on and 60 hours with ANC off, which is fairly unremarkable, and an actual downgrade on what the original Space One Pro offered. I assume that this is a result of the increased power that the Thus chip demands, but a lot of similarly priced competitors offer far longer playtime — you get 75 hours with ANC on from the Nothing Headphone (a), for instance.

One of the biggest features that the Thus chip provides is AI Chat. Just say ‘Hey Anka’, (see what the company did there?) and you can ask the headphones to make sound adjustments, tell you a random fact, and the like. And this is implemented horribly.

The AI Chat function works *sometimes* — I asked for some facts about fish and got a very comprehensive analysis back from Anka. But the things that actually matter, such as asking for volume adjustments, sonic tweaks and the like, straight up don’t work. I asked Anka to turn the volume up (I was at about 50%), to which it responded “setting volume to 10%”. What? Then it continued to repeat that very phrase a further three times, though nothing actually happened. So not only did it misunderstand my simple request, but it repeated itself, and failed to make any changes.

I also found that AI Chat would carry out requests I never asked for. It told me it was unable to set a timer, though I never once asked it to set one up. It gave me a minute-long speech about its capabilities without provocation, and it would just talk at me without reprieve despite me asking it to stop.

Would I actually use AI Chat in my day-to-day life? Even if it worked, probably not — and I’m sure most wouldn't either. But if you’re going to make it a key feature, at least get it right.

![Earcup of the Anker Soundcore Space 2 Pro](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/TcHnEDfrqggEAvKxbVuKtP-1200-80.jpg)

And when it comes to sound, I was actually left feeling a little bit disappointed. I really liked the original Space One Pro; they definitely leaned into a bass-heavy sound, but they still provided clear mids and expressive highs right out of the box. But the Anker Soundcore Space 2 Pro just seem overzealous in the low-end.

The bass is very much in your face with the signature tuning here, and it just doesn’t quite work in my view — even with a deeper track like *Pia* by Deep Active Sound. Here, the low-end just didn’t have the balance, texture, or refinement I was looking for, with an almost boomy quality on display. Bass was undoubtedly powerful, sure, but not regimented or clean enough to truly immerse me in the track.

It’s a shame, as before the bass entered into the mix, I was really impressed with the tight, controlled drums in the treble range, which were weighted nicely, and synths in the mid-range had a solid sense of space, but I was left wanting a bit more balance. With a custom EQ, I was able to get better balance across the frequency range, but still don’t expect the most detailed or intricate low-end performance from the Space 2 Pro.

When tuning into *Black Eye* by Allie X, I found vocals in the mid-range to be pleasantly clear and fairly detailed with LDAC switched on, with the breathiness of the performance replicated nicely. Again, the bass was a bit heavy, and didn’t have the agility that this energetic track requires, but a custom EQ did remedy this in part.

In *Weather Report* by Fishmans, the Space 2 Pro performed fairly well, though. Towards the end of the song, there’s a lot going on, with distorted guitars, deep bass, funky drums, and haunting vocals. And the headphones managed to replicate all of this decently, with each element coming through clearly even at high volumes. I didn’t experience the brilliant instrument separation that more premium cans like the Sonos Ace Ultra can muster, but I was left satisfied overall.

It’s worth noting that there’s a spatial audio mode available in the Soundcore app, but this isn’t great — it provides a bit more width, but weakened fidelity, so I’d just stick with LDAC and a custom EQ personally.

All in all, the Anker Soundcore Space 2 Pro sound alright — bass, while not the most refined, is still capable of hitting impressive depths, mids come through with solid amounts of detail, and treble is articulate. But I was expecting more of an upgrade, and the sound just doesn’t quite click for me out of the box.

Before we sum up, a quick word on design. This is certainly an area where the Space 2 Pro excel in my view, with a pretty stylish look that’s more premium than their price suggests, thanks to a luxurious-looking headband and plastic earcups with a beautiful finish. Said headband and earcups also come with generous padding for a supremely comfortable listening experience, even if you’re listening for many hours on end.

The Space 2 Pro are foldable, and although they don’t fold flat like their predecessors, they’re still plenty compact enough, and can easily slot into a small bag. Speaking of, they also come with a neat travel case in the box which adds to their perceived value.

When all’s said and done, the Anker Soundcore Space 2 Pro have left me feeling conflicted. They’ve taken one step forward with enhanced ANC and calling, but also taken a step back in terms of battery life, and their sound quality didn’t get the boost I was hoping for. Some other features, like AI Chat and spatial audio also didn’t hit for me, so I’d be more tempted to recommend the original headphones over these.

Something that makes the original even easier to recommend is that they were cheaper than their successor at launch, in some markets at least. The Space 2 Pro have a list price of $199.99 / £199.99 / AU$349.99, which is £50 more in the UK at launch — and I don’t think they do enough to justify that overall.

So while the Anker Soundcore Space 2 Pro offer amazing bass at this price-point, and have a comfortable design to match, I still wouldn’t recommend them outright just yet — though they could be worth snapping up on sale. Be sure to check out the ‘also consider’ section for some alternatives then, or take a look at the original Soundcore Space One Pro for great quality at an easy-to-stomach price.

## Buy them if…

**You want amazing ANC without paying top dollar** The ANC on the Space 2 Pro is seriously impressive. Their ability to cut out traffic noise, surrounding chatter, and ambient sound is some of the best I’ve experienced at this price-point.**You prioritize comfort when it comes to headphones**  
If comfort is number one for you, then the Space 2 Pro will certainly fit the bill. Their generously padded headband and earcups feel fantastic and never felt irritating — even during the longest of listening sessions.

## Don’t buy them if…

**You want premium sound quality** If top-class sound is what you’re after, then you may want to look elsewhere. The bass on the Space 2 Pro lacks the texture and balance I was hoping for, and you’re not going to get the best instrument separation or detail out of these either.**You’re looking for headphones with long battery life**  
The Space 2 Pro will give you 30 hours of playtime with ANC on, which is totally unremarkable. Headphones in this price-range often give you considerably more, but the Thus chip onboard seems to be pretty power hungry.
