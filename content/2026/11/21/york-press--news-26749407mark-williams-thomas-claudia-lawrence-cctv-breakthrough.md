## Expert weighs in on Claudia Lawrence CCTV breakthrough claims

Claudia Lawrence was last seen or heard from on March 18, 2009 *(Image: Supplied)*

FRESH claims that the disappearance of Claudia Lawrence could be a step closer to being solved have sparked renewed hope among those who have followed the case since the York chef vanished in 2009.

The latest information centres on new analysis carried out by experts commissioned by criminologist Professor David Wilson and Martin Frizell for their ‘This Much Is True Crime’ podcast, prompting speculation that investigators could finally be closer to identifying a key figure captured on CCTV.

As reported by The Press , forensic experts have digitally enhanced compressed CCTV footage recorded on the evening of March 18 and the following morning close to Claudia’s home in Heworth Place.




The technology used was not available at the time Claudia disappeared, Professor David Wilson said.

![Fresh forensic analysis has unveiled 'previously unseen' details about a person spotted near Claudia's home on March 18](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21739223.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Fresh forensic analysis has unveiled 'previously unseen' details about a person spotted near Claudia's home on March 18 *(Image: This Much is True Crime)*

He added that from the analysis the person seen walking down Heworth Place on March 18 is believed to be a man.

While specialists have been unable to determine the person’s height because of the quality of the compressed footage, they have identified previously unseen features including what appears to be a receding grey hairline or bald head.

The same person was seen just over a minute later walking back towards the junction of Heworth Place and Heworth Road, carrying what appeared to be a rucksack over their shoulder.

![The latest information centres on new analysis carried out by experts commissioned by criminologist Professor David Wilson and Martin Frizell for their ‘This Much Is True Crime’ podcast](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21386494.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The latest information centres on new analysis carried out by experts commissioned by criminologist Professor David Wilson and Martin Frizell for their ‘This Much Is True Crime’ podcast *(Image: Ecademi)*

Police footage shows that they come to an abrupt stop in the middle of Heworth Place as a person crosses the road ahead – something described as “suspicious” by Professor Wilson.

In the podcast documentary, host Martin Frizell said: “And then he is picked up again [on CCTV] in this very same location at 5 o’clock in the morning the next morning.”

However, former detective and investigative journalist Mark Williams-Thomas has pointed to North Yorkshire Police's response to the new potential lead.

Mr William-Thomas is well-known for his work on cases such as the disappearance of Madeline McCann and the death of Nicola Bulley and a documentary which led to Operation Yewtree police investigation against Jimmy Saville and Rolf Harris.

Discussing the latest developments in a video posted online, Mr Williams-Thomas said the force was not involved in the new analysis.

He said: "Analysis which has been done by Professor Wilson's commissioned experts.

"North Yorkshire Police were not involved in this analysis and in fact have said: 'The man in the footage has yet to be identified.

"'We continue to urge anyone with information that could support our enquiries to come forward.'"

Williams-Thomas said the wording of the force's statement could indicate investigators remain focused on identifying a single individual.

He added: "So that would tend to suggest very clearly from that statement that North Yorkshire Police already know that they're actually looking for one person and that one person remains unidentified."

The ‘This Much Is True Crime’ podcast has reignited discussion around the disappearance of Claudia, who vanished aged 35 after failing to arrive for work at the University of York on March 19, 2009.

Despite numerous appeals, searches and investigations over the years, nobody has ever been charged in connection with her disappearance and her body has never been found.

News of the latest analysis prompted strong reactions from followers of the case on social media.

## 'There’s more than one person knows what’s happened to her'

One person said: "I hope one day this mystery will be resolved. I find it hard to believe with all the technology in the world today this has still not been solved. It must be horrendous for her family/mother to live with."

Another wrote: “There’s more than one person knows what’s happened to her and it’s time they come forward and give her mother some relief.”

A third commented: “Let's hope advances in technology and science will unlock the truth finally.”

While the latest claims have renewed hopes of a conclusion to the case, North Yorkshire Police's position remains unchanged, with detectives continuing to appeal for information that could help identify the man seen in the footage and advance the investigation.

A spokesperson for the force said: “We continue to urge anyone with information that could support our enquiries to come forward.

“The investigation into the disappearance and suspected murder of Claudia Lawrence has encompassed numerous lines of enquiry over many years, and has undergone extensive independent review.

“Despite the passage of time, we remain committed to finding the answers Claudia’s family and friends deserve.”
