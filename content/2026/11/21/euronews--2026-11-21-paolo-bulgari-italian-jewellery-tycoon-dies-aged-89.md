## The grandson of Bulgari's founder helped transform the family jewellery business into one of the world's most prestigious luxury brands. He led the Roman maison as chairman from 1984 and remained passionate about jewellery design throughout his life.

Italian jeweller and businessman Paolo Bulgari has died in Rome at the age of 89, his family announced on Saturday, 21 November 2026.

The family paid tribute to him as "the greatest jeweller of our time", praising his humanity and his talent as an entrepreneur who loved Italy and served as its ambassador around the world.

Bulgari was the grandson of Sotirio Bulgari, who founded the eponymous jewellery business in Rome in 1884. Born in the Italian capital in 1937, Paolo belonged to the third generation of the family behind the luxury house.

## From family workshop to global luxury empire

Paolo Bulgari joined the family business in 1960, initially working alongside his father, Giorgio. He developed a particular expertise in precious stones and became internationally recognised for his knowledge of gemstones.

In 1984, he became chairman of the company, working closely with his brother Nicola and nephew Francesco Trapani to expand the business internationally.

Under their leadership, Bulgari developed beyond its traditional jewellery business, adding watches, perfumes, leather goods and accessories to its portfolio. Its distinctive designs, often inspired by ancient Greek and Roman art, became synonymous with Italian luxury.

The brand became particularly famous for its colourful gemstones, bold gold jewellery and distinctive use of cabochon-cut stones. Among its most celebrated admirers was Hollywood actress Elizabeth Taylor, whose association with the jewellery house helped cement its international reputation.

## Sale to luxury giant LVMH

A major turning point came in 2011, when the Bulgari family sold its controlling stake in the business to French luxury conglomerate LVMH in a deal worth approximately €3.7 billion.

Paolo Bulgari later described the decision as painful, but said it was made partly out of responsibility towards the company's employees and concerns about the difficulties of managing the generational transition.

Despite relinquishing family control, he remained closely connected to the brand and continued designing jewellery well into his later years. He also enjoyed occasionally working behind the counter at the company's historic boutique on Rome's Via dei Condotti, sometimes introducing himself simply as a shop assistant.

## Philanthropy and commitment to Rome

Beyond the luxury industry, Bulgari devoted considerable attention to social causes, particularly education and opportunities for young people.

In 2019, he founded the Paolo Bulgari Foundation with his wife. The organisation supports educational regeneration and vocational training in disadvantaged neighbourhoods of Rome.

One of its major projects involved the redevelopment of Largo Mengaroni, the main square in the Tor Bella Monaca district, completed in 2023 in cooperation with local schools, associations and the municipal authorities.

Paolo Bulgari is survived by his six children from two marriages. His funeral will take place on Monday, 23 November, at the Basilica of Santa Croce in Gerusalemme in Rome.
