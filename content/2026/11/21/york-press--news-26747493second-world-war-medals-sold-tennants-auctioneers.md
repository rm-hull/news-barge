## Second World War hero’s medals to go under hammer in Leyburn

A Second World War DFC Casualty Group of Five Medals – Wiliam Lewis – Estimate: £3,000-4,000 >(Image: Tennants Auctioneers)

A Second World War hero's medals are going under the hammer.

The Distinguished Flying Cross Casualty Group of Five Medals, awarded to Squadron Leader William James Lewis, will be auctioned at Tennants Auctioneers on December 9.

The medals, recognising Squadron Leader Lewis's service and sacrifice, include the Distinguished Flying Cross, 1939-45 Star, Air Crew Europe Star, Defence Medal, and War Medal.

They are expected to fetch between £3,000 and £4,000.

Bill Lewis enlisted for pilot training in the RAFVR in 1937 and was commissioned in 1941.

He piloted a Lancaster bomber that was shot down in Gerheiden, Belgium, in November 1943.

He was found and cared for by a local farmer, Emile Schoolemans, but unfortunately succumbed to his injuries.

He was hailed as a hero by the villagers for avoiding the village during the crash.

The medal will be sold with copies of his logbooks and a letter from Schoolemans to Bill’s widow.

The auction will also feature other notable items, including medals awarded to Major and Quarter Master Thomas Edward Bunting of the Royal Irish Fusiliers, a collection of Neolithic Flint Tools found in East Sussex, and a Victorian Scottish Dirk to an Officer in the Highland Light Infantry.
