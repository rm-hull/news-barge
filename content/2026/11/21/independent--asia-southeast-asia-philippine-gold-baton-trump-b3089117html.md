The Philippine army ruled out a gold baton for US president Donald Trump after he complained during a visit to Manila that he had not been given the award that American general Douglas MacArthur received in 1936.

Mr Trump complained on Wednesday at a state lunch at Malacañang Palace, the presidential residence in Manila, hosted by Philippine president Ferdinand Marcos Jr, that he had not been given the gold baton and field marshal rank.

"Of course, it was right here, this very palace, that President [Manuel L] Quezon bestowed Douglas MacArthur with a gold baton and the rank of field marshal in the Philippine Army,” he told the gathering.

“I'd like to have you do that to me if you don't mind. I'd love that. I mean, why the hell shouldn't I get that?" he said as other attendees laughed.

The army said in a statement later that the position of field marshal and its baton were not carried over into its later rank structure and are no longer part of its honours system.

"This distinction was given as part of MacArthur's duties as military adviser to the Commonwealth government in organising and developing the Philippine army."

The army added that the baton "remains a historical symbol" of its heritage and of the long-standing defence ties between the Philippines and the US.

![](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/18/15/2305146337..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Quezon led the Philippine Commonwealth, the self-governing government that ran the country under US rule before independence in 1946, and appointed MacArthur as its military adviser to help build a national army.

The field marshal title is the only such rank in the country's history.

The baton Quezon gave MacArthur is now displayed in a museum dedicated to the general in Norfolk, Virginia.

MacArthur, who served as supreme commander of the Southwest Pacific during the Second World War, is a venerated figure in the Philippines.

The Manila Hotel has a MacArthur Suite, and a park on Leyte island hosts a life-size bronze statue of him. He evacuated to Australia in 1942 after Japanese troops overwhelmed American and Philippine forces, vowed to return, and came ashore on Leyte two years later.

Mr Trump's complaint followed a tribute to the relationship between the two countries.

“From William Howard Taft to Dwight D Eisenhower, some of the best minds and foremost leaders of our nation, that our nation has ever produced … They always knew that this was a place of great potential,” he said.

Mr Trump also announced at the lunch that the US had sealed a "very big trade deal" with the Philippines.

He was in Manila for the first stop of an Asia trip that included the Association of Southeast Asian Nations (Asean) summit, and Mr Marcos had greeted him on arrival on Tuesday with fried chicken from Jollibee, the Philippines' largest fast-food chain, which he described as a taste of Manila.

Mr Trump flew to the Chinese city of Shenzhen later on Wednesday for the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (Apec) summit, where he held talks on Thursday with Chinese president Xi Jinping and Russian president Vladimir Putin.

Mr Trump has been given gold gifts before.

When he visited South Korea last year, president Lee Jae Myung presented him with a gold crown. He has also renovated the Oval Office at the White House with gilded interiors, including gold-painted carvings.
