During his campaign for sheriff in North Carolina against a long-serving incumbent aligned with the Trump administration’s immigration measures, Shannon Long found his marked campaign van presented an unexpected barrier with local voters.

Distrust between law enforcement and the expanding Latino population in Alamance County had grown so severe, Long explained, that traveling in a vehicle bearing the word "sheriff" made residents hesitant to engage in conversation.

"They’ve been living in fear. They feel like they don’t have a voice. They have been suppressed, oppressed; a lot of them are depressed. So they feel that they live in a community where they don’t belong," Long, a pastor, business owner and former deputy, said following his defeat of Republican Sheriff Terry Johnson. Activists, voters and local officials indicated the race swung in part on immigration enforcement policies and the treatment of Latino residents.

Johnson was among a handful of sheriffs across the nation ousted by opponents critical of their cooperation with President Donald Trump’s efforts targeting mass deportations of people in the country illegally. Though limited in overall number, the election outcomes could signal challenges for other law enforcement leaders participating in federal crackdowns and potentially undermine agreements central to federal enforcement efforts.

![Alamance County Sheriff-Elect Shannon Long promised to curtail cooperation with ICE](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/21/17/download.-2..jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Johnson was among the early group of local officials to authorize deputies to enforce federal immigration law under 287(g) agreements with U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement. Long, who ran as an independent, pledged to reduce joint operations with ICE. The federal agency continues to rely heavily on local partnerships to maintain arrest figures, despite receiving a $75 billion funding boost from Congress and adding roughly 12,000 officers.

Federal authorities maintain more than 2,700 such agreements with state and local agencies, expanding from 135 when Trump began his second term. Located primarily in the South, the pacts have deputized over 29,000 local officers and generated more than 6,600 arrests in July alone—representing approximately 15% of all ICE arrests, according to government figures collected by the Deportation Data Project and analyzed by advocacy group fwd.us.

A review of approximately 250 sheriff contests held this month in jurisdictions with 287(g) agreements identified races in Indiana, Maryland, North Carolina, Wisconsin and other states where immigration pacts contributed to candidate defeats. Many incumbents ran unopposed, but these federal agreements are expected to resurface as key campaign issues in two years, when sheriffs in states like Florida and Texas stand for election.

Alamance County is a long-standing Republican stronghold of nearly 200,000 residents situated between Greensboro and the Research Triangle region. The county has grown more politically competitive as its Hispanic population expanded from 750 in the 1990 census to nearly 25,000 by 2020. The sheriff's office currently maintains a 287(g) agreement, a spokesperson confirmed, though it terminated a separate agreement last year to house federal immigration detainees.

![Alamance County Sheriff Terry Johnson was defeated in the recent election after 24 years in office](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/21/15/Immigration_Enforcement_Sheriffs_54844.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Johnson, who was first elected in 2002, has remained a prominent and polarizing figure. He has maintained that he welcomes legal immigrants while denying allegations of racial profiling, which were previously the subject of a Justice Department lawsuit that ended in a settlement.

The area earned the local moniker "No Chance Alamance" due to a perceived history of inequitable policing and a lack of political representation for Black and Latino residents, according to school board member Seneca Rogers.

Johnson initially acknowledged that immigration enforcement might have influenced the outcome, but later attributed his defeat to his long tenure and a difficult political climate for Republicans in North Carolina, where Democratic former Gov. Roy Cooper won a U.S. Senate seat by nearly 20 percentage points. Johnson stated he always operated within the law to protect public safety.

Community advocates and long-time residents noted widespread apprehension among minority communities regarding police encounters, describing frequent traffic stops and detentions. Community organizer Leslie Arevalo noted some residents feared dialing 911 or going to work.

Voter Renee Russell expressed enthusiasm for new leadership, noting that previous policies created an unwelcoming image of the community. Local media reported that Long will become the county’s first Black sheriff.

![Protesters gather at First Ward Park for the "No Border Patrol In Charlotte" rally before marching through uptown on November 15, 2025 in Charlotte, North Carolina](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/21/17/01/GettyImages-2246351255.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Republican state Sen. Amy Galey defended Johnson's record, citing improvements in deputy training and mental health programs, while suggesting federal ICE actions elsewhere may have raised broader public concern.

In Frederick County, Maryland, the sheriff’s office had participated in the 287(g) program since 2008, transferring 1,884 individuals to federal authorities, according to Republican Sheriff Chuck Jenkins. That partnership ended after Democratic Gov. Wes Moore signed legislation banning state and local immigration enforcement pacts.

Jenkins lost his reelection bid to Democrat Karl Bickel, a retired U.S. Department of Justice employee who pledged to end ICE cooperation.

"In general, people are fed up with the sheriff’s negativeness about immigrants," said retired social worker Carol Antoniewicz, who campaigned for Bickel.

In Wisconsin’s Winnebago County, the arrest of a local schoolteacher by ICE weeks before the election stirred public outcry. Local advocate Molly Smiltneek noted, "Any kind of perceived alignment definitely is considered a liability at this point." Democrat Jason Rippl won the race after promising to terminate the department's 287(g) agreement.

Long stated he intends to enforce the law while stepping away from the 287(g) program, focusing on outreach to the Latino community.

"I want them to see the sheriff’s office in the community, creating a partnership, letting them know that they have a voice. … There’s no need to operate in fear," Long said.
