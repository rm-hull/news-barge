## Real Madrid have widened the financial gap over Bayern Munich, generating more than €1.2 billion in revenue in the 2025-26 season. The German champions are also approaching the billion-euro mark, while celebrating a record 500,000 members.

European football's financial heavyweights are celebrating another record-breaking season. Real Madrid reported operating revenue of €1.221 billion for 2025-26, comfortably ahead of Bayern Munich's €988.2 million.

## Real Madrid sets €1.25bn budget after record revenue

Real Madrid president Florentino Pérez announced the record figures at the club's annual general meeting, while presenting a budget of €1.25 billion for the 2026-27 season.

For the first time, the Spanish giants have surpassed the €1.2 billion revenue mark, a figure Pérez described as unprecedented for a sports organisation.

The club also recorded its highest-ever EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortisation), at €287.4 million. Net profit after tax reached €26 million, an increase of eight per cent compared with the previous season.

Pérez highlighted Real Madrid's international standing, citing Forbes, Deloitte and Brand Finance rankings. According to Forbes, the club is the world's most valuable football club for the fifth consecutive year. Deloitte ranks it first in revenue, while Brand Finance considers it the world's most valuable and strongest football brand.

Pérez said the club's partnerships with companies and technology providers would help it apply new expertise to its operations, particularly its sporting activities.

The figures underline the commercial strength of both clubs, although the Spanish giants remain out in front. Real Madrid's revenue increased by 3.1 per cent compared with the previous season, while Bayern recorded a more modest rise of around one per cent.

The difference amounts to approximately €233 million, although the clubs' published accounting figures are not entirely like-for-like: Real Madrid explicitly excludes player-transfer income from its operating revenue figure.

## Real Madrid outstrip Bayern Munich in record revenues

Real Madrid's growth has been driven largely by its commercial operations and the redevelopment of the Santiago Bernabéu stadium.

Marketing generated €539 million, an increase of six per cent, while stadium revenue reached €363 million, up 11 per cent. Television rights and revenue from international competitions contributed a further €319 million.

The stadium's commercial performance is particularly significant: its revenue has more than doubled compared with the final season before the redevelopment began in 2018-19.

Real Madrid have now recorded a profit for 26 consecutive financial years, according to the club.

## Bayern close to the billion-euro mark

Bayern Munich are not far behind, having increased their consolidated revenue from €978.3 million to €988.2 million.

The club's sponsorship income was particularly strong, rising by almost €30 million to €268.4 million. Merchandising also reached a record €152.8 million.

However, the financial picture was more mixed when it came to profits. Bayern's operating result (EBITDA) increased from €187.8 million to €190.8 million, but its profit before tax fell by 16.9 per cent to €35.3 million. The group's net profit declined by 15.9 per cent to €22.8 million.

Bayern explained that the lower profit was largely due to exceptional revenue in the previous financial year from the European Championship and the Champions League final, which did not recur in 2025-26.

CEO Jan-Christian Dreesen nevertheless described the results as another record year *(source in German)*, emphasising the club's financial stability and its continued investment in the men's and women's teams.

Bayern also highlighted a decline in revenue from the Bundesliga's central media marketing, which fell by €10 million to €93 million. Dreesen said German clubs needed to address their competitive disadvantage in international television marketing.

## Bayern reach a historic membership milestone

Away from the financial figures, Bayern celebrated another milestone: the club has surpassed 500,000 members for the first time.

According to Bayern, this makes it the largest sports club by membership worldwide. Its membership has doubled over the past 12 years, from around 250,000 in 2014.

The milestone also underlines the difference between the clubs' structures. Bayern is majority-owned by its members' association, FC Bayern München eV, while Real Madrid is entirely owned by its members, known as socios.

At Bayern, members also have voting rights at the annual general meeting, giving them a direct say in the club's affairs.

## Two different models of financial success

Although Real Madrid currently have the larger revenue stream, both clubs demonstrate how Europe's leading football institutions are expanding beyond traditional sources of income.

Real Madrid have benefited considerably from their renovated stadium and global commercial reach. Bayern, meanwhile, have built particularly strong sponsorship and merchandising businesses while maintaining a cautious approach to spending.

The substantial difference in turnover does not translate into an equally large gap in profitability. Despite generating around €233 million more revenue than Bayern, Real Madrid reported net profit after tax of €26.3 million, only €3.5 million more than the German club's €22.8 million.

This suggests that higher revenues do not automatically result in significantly higher profits, as operating costs, player salaries, transfer spending, financing and other expenses also play a major role. Both clubs nevertheless ended the 2025-26 financial year in the black, demonstrating their ability to remain profitable while operating on a huge financial scale.

For Bayern, the next major target is clear: breaking through the €1 billion revenue barrier. Real Madrid, meanwhile, have already moved beyond it and are setting their sights on further growth.
