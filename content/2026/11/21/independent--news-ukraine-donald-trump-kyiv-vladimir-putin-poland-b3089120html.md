A Russian strike on the center of Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy’s hometown, Kryvyi Rih, killed two people and wounded 60 others, local officials said Saturday.

A 7-year-old boy and a 20-year-old woman were killed in the attack Friday night on the city in central Ukraine, Oleksandr Vilkul, the head of the city administration, wrote on Telegram. Oleksandr Hanzha, head of the regional military administration, said 60 people were wounded, including six children.

The strike caused four fires and damaged about 25 apartment buildings, a hospital, schools and an industrial facility, as well as businesses and vehicles, Vilkul said.

The attack fits the pattern of Moscow’s heavy responses to eye-catching Ukrainian attacks, such as the ones this week on the Kerch Bridge linking Russia to illegally annexed Crimea.

The missile attack came hours after two Russian missiles and a suspected drone came down in Poland, in what its prime minister said was likely a provocation timed to coincide with European military exercises in his country for a possible postwar mission in Ukraine.

European troops were taking part Friday in an unprecedented two-week military exercise that began Nov. 9 in southeastern Poland. Forces are preparing for a possible postwar mission in Ukraine aimed at helping to stabilize the country and rebuild its military. European countries previously have expressed alarm at what they deem to be Russia’s attempts to destabilize the continent.

The development stoked tensions between Europe and Moscow just as the U.S. tries to add momentum to talks it is mediating on ending the war that began with Russia’s all-out invasion of Ukraine nearly five years ago.

U.S. President Donald Trump discussed the war with Russian President Vladimir Putin and Chinese leader Xi Jinping at a summit in China on Thursday, according to his envoy Steve Witkoff. Trump called Putin his “friend” during the meeting and continued to tout their relationship at a White House news conference Friday.

Asked about Trump’s remarks in an interview with CNN, Zelenskyy called Putin “a killer.”

“For us, he is an enemy. And of course, for us, he is a killer,” Zelenskyy said Friday. He warned that describing the Russian leader in friendly terms could mean “he doesn’t feel enough pressure.”

Elsewhere in Ukraine, 11 people, including two children, were wounded when a Russian drone attacked a five-story apartment building in the Kharkiv region, Ukraine’s State Emergency Service said Saturday. One person was wounded in a Russian attack on the capital, Kyiv, according to the service.

In Russia, local officials said Saturday that Ukrainian drone attacks wounded three people in the Bryansk and Belgorod border regions.
