Mind in Harrogate District's festive 5K is on Sunday 29 November at Conyngham Hall, Knaresborough. Adults £15, children £12, under-5s free.

Mind in Harrogate District’s Mental Elf Festive Fun Run is on **Sunday 29 November** at Conyngham Hall in Knaresborough, with the run starting at**11am**.

It is a 5K in fancy dress, and you can run, jog or walk it. The money goes to the charity’s mental health service for children and young people, Mind in Harrogate District says.

## Overview

* **When:** Sunday 29 November. Registration opens at 9.30am and the run starts at 11am.
* **Where:** Conyngham Hall, Knaresborough, HG5 9AY.
* **Cost:** adults £15, children aged 5 to 15 £12, under-5s free.
* **Distance:** 5K over two loops. You can stop after one loop and still get the medal.
* **Book:** through the Mental Elf page on the charity’s website.

## The morning

The charity gives this running order:

| Time | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| 9.30am | Registration opens |
| 10am | Pre-race entertainment, hosted by Your Harrogate |
| 10.30am | Fancy dress competition, including best-dressed dog |
| 11am | Run starts |

Arrive by 10.30am at the latest to register and get to the start line, the charity says.

## The route

The course is the same one used by the Conyngham Hall parkrun, whose volunteers help set it out. It is roughly half tarmac and half trail, with some narrow and uneven parts.

The run is not timed. Children can take part with an adult, and dogs are welcome on a short lead.

The charity says the parkrun advises the course is not suitable for buggies. Some families have managed it with three-wheeled all-terrain buggies, it adds, but wet or muddy ground makes that harder.

## Getting there and parking

Conyngham Hall is a short walk from Knaresborough station and the High Bridge bus stop. Sunday services may be thinner than on a weekday.

There is pay and display parking at Conyngham Hall, but it filled up last year, so the charity asks people to share cars. Extra parking is at Henshaws Arts and Crafts, 50 Bond End, where payment is enforced by number plate cameras.

## What the money pays for

Last year’s run raised **£6,500**, according to the charity. Its fundraising page for this year’s event had passed**£1,074** online on Saturday, with 14 people fundraising (JustGiving).

The page says the money supports:

* counselling for young people aged 11 to 18
* the charity’s young people’s mental health drop-in
* mental health and wellbeing workshops

The charity says £10 could help pay towards a young person’s counselling session, and £100 could help fund one of its weekly drop-in sessions.

The entry fee covers a medal, which doubles as a Christmas decoration, and a goody bag. Any money left after the event’s costs goes to the young people’s service.

## What it means for you

If you want to take part, book through the charity’s page and plan your parking early. The charity would like every runner to set up a fundraising page, with no minimum amount. The top fundraiser wins a pair of running shoes.
