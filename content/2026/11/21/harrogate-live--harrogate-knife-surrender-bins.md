The Dragon Road knife bin in Harrogate had taken 3,951 blades by May. More than 11,000 have now gone into bins across the county, the council says.

More than **11,000** knives and other sharp items have been dropped into the county’s knife surrender bins since they were rolled out in 2025, North Yorkshire Council said on Friday.

The scheme started in Harrogate. The first bin went into the Dragon Road car park in January 2023 as a trial. By May this year it had taken **3,951** bladed items on its own, according to the York and North Yorkshire Combined Authority.

## Overview

* Harrogate, Knaresborough, Ripon and Boroughbridge all have an orange knife surrender bin.
* You can post any blade anonymously: kitchen knives, scissors, and legal or illegal knives.
* Police collect the contents and destroy them.
* The council’s figure for the whole county is now over 11,000 items.

## Where the bins are

The council’s release lists bins in Harrogate, Knaresborough, Ripon and Boroughbridge, among 19 towns in York and North Yorkshire. The locations published for our area are:

If you are driving to the Harrogate bin, the Dragon Road car park itself is closed for Convention Centre exhibitions from 16 to 27 November and again from 15 to 18 December, according to the council’s closure dates.

## Why it is in the news this week

The 11,000 figure came out during Operation Sceptre, a national week of action on knife crime, which runs until Sunday 22 November.

North Yorkshire Police officers are visiting schools across the county during the week. In Tadcaster, young people used virtual reality headsets to explore what can happen when someone carries a knife.

The council runs the bins with City of York Council and North Yorkshire Police. They were paid for with more than **£15,000** from the York and North Yorkshire Office for Policing, Fire, Crime and Commissioning, the council said.

Cllr Heather Phillips, the council’s executive member for corporate services, said: “While North Yorkshire remains a safe place to live, it is important that we continue to take proactive steps to raise awareness of the risks associated with carrying knives and help prevent harm before it occurs.”

Detective Chief Inspector Matthew Wilkinson, of North Yorkshire Police, said: “While incidents involving knives remain relatively low in North Yorkshire, one incident is one too many.”

## What it means for you

The bins are anonymous. You do not give your name or any details. Police said in the December message that kitchen knives and scissors can go in, including old ones you are replacing after Christmas.

When the county rollout began, North Yorkshire Police advised wrapping knives in cardboard before taking them to a bin. Sergeant Paul Evans said at the time that “you won’t get into trouble for transporting knives to these locations”.

To report knife crime or concerns about weapons, call 999 in an emergency or 101 otherwise. You can also report anonymously to Crimestoppers.
