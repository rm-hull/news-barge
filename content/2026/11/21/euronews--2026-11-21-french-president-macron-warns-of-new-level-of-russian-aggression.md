## The warning came after a series of airspace violations, reported sabotage attempts and an alleged assassination plot made headlines.

French President Emmanuel Macron warned on Friday of a "new level" of Russian aggression following a spate of incidents across Europe this week.

"Over the past 24 hours, several of our European partners and allies have once again faced serious incidents that signal a new level of aggression on Russia’s part," Macron wrote in a post on social media.

The warning came after a series of airspace violations, reported sabotage attempts and an alleged assassination plot made headlines.

On Friday, Polish authorities announced that two Russian missiles and a jet-powered drone had crashed in Poland as allied nations held military exercises in the eastern European country.

Denmark's Security and Intelligence Service (PET) also accused Russian intelligence services of being behind an assassination attempt targeting a Ukrainian American citizen north of Copenhagen in September.

For its part, the Estonian government announced its decision to suspend all road border crossings with Russia from 1 December in the wake of what Tallinn said was a "GRU-ordered" sabotage attempt targeting drone manufacturer Threod Systems last week.

Earlier this week, Moldova also shuttered its national airspace after an attack drone was detected heading towards the capital Chișinău. President Maia Sandu said "several Russian drones" had violated Moldovan airspace over the course of Tuesday evening.

Macron on Friday expressed his solidarity with the impacted nations while also condemning Russia.

"By intensifying its war of aggression against Ukraine and pursuing an aggressive agenda on European territory, Russia is taking an irresponsible gamble that can only further isolate it and strengthen the resolve and unity of Europeans - and of all those who wish to work toward peace and stability on our continent," Macron said.

## Russian strikes on Ukraine

Macron's comments followed large-scale Russian strikes across Ukraine from Thursday night into Friday morning, which killed at least seven people in Kyiv and damaged key bridges across the Dnipro River.

Zelenskyy said Russia had fired more than 400 attack drones and over 110 missiles during the assault, which he called "one of the largest attacks" of the war so far.
