A Florida judge on Thursday handed down a death sentence to a plastic surgeon for murdering an attorney inside a legal practice bathroom before dumping his body in an Everglades refuse bin, where it has never been found.

Dr Tomasz Kosowski killed attorney Steven Cozzi in the bathroom of Mr Cozzi’s firm in March 2023, shortly after both men participated in a telephone call regarding a bitter, protracted dispute over medical billing, prosecutors stated.

State attorneys said Kosowski took part in the call from a Toyota pickup truck parked outside the building, equipped with items to execute the murder, including rubbish bags, a syringe containing a paralysing drug and a trolley for the body.

Detectives used mobile phone records and CCTV footage to trace Kosowski to an isolated spot west of Miami on the Tamiami Trail, also designated as US Highway 41. Investigators believe Kosowski threw the corpse into a commercial bin there that was later emptied by a refuse lorry, whose driver noted an exceptionally "vile" odour at that specific collection stop.

Waste from that route is usually transported to a landfill in Collier County, but officials who searched the location explained that the facility regularly compacts its rubbish, making recovery virtually impossible. Forensic officers added that Mr Cozzi’s blood and DNA were detected inside the law firm’s bathroom and in the garage at Kosowski’s private home.

Kosowski was convicted in September after choosing to represent himself during trial, offering no formal defence beyond asserting in his closing address that the prosecution had failed to establish his legal guilt.

Pinellas Circuit Judge Joseph Bulone was tasked with deciding whether to sentence Kosowski to death or to life in prison without the possibility of release.

![The law office of attorney Steven Cozzi in Largo, Fla., is seen on July 9, 2023. (AP Photo/Curt Anderson, File)](https://static.the-independent.com/2026/11/20/20/Lawyer_Slain_Doctor_Charged_53373.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Kosowski declined to present any mitigating evidence on his own behalf, but a special counsel appointed by the court highlighted his lack of criminal history and his medical work. A graduate of Dartmouth College medical school, Kosowski specialized in reconstructive breast surgery.

The judge acknowledged in court that there were several mitigating factors, but said none outweighed the aggravating factors — most importantly, that the crime was committed in a “cold, calculating and premeditated manner, without any pretense of moral or legal justification.”

“The court concludes that the defendant, under the laws of the State of Florida, has forfeited his right to live," Bulone said.

Investigators said that when they arrested Kosowski, he had $280,000 in cash, masks, duct tape, firearms, a ballistic vest with “EMS” written on it, law enforcement patches and a vial of succinylcholine, which is a paralyzing drug. A search of his home turned up about 200 guns, according to court documents.

Cozzi, meanwhile, seemingly disappeared without a trace. His keys, wallet and cellphone remained on his law office desk and a work file was open on his computer. His husband never heard from him.

The missing lawyer represented a Dunedin, Florida-based medical practice that Kosowski alleged had shorted him thousands of dollars in billings and damaged his reputation as a doctor.

Bulone appointed the public defender's office to represent Kosowski in his appeals.
