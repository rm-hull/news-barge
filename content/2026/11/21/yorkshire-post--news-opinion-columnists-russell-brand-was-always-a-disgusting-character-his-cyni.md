# Russell Brand was always a disgusting character - his cynical use of Christianity is latest appalling behaviour: Christa Ackroyd

## I wasn’t going to write about him. I wasn’t going to comment at all. Justice was seen to have been done and those who were ignored, those who were brave enough to come forward, have been vindicated.

Instead I was happy that the sentence imposed was long enough to both be a punishment and act as a warning to others.

I was particular happy when the judge reinforced what is the absolute truth, but has so often failed to be recognised, when he said that no matter the circumstances, no matter what women wear or indeed where they go or whatever they have been drinking they retain the right to say no.

At last I believed society and the justice system, so often at odds, were moving in the right direction and so it needed no comment from me.

You know I prefer where I can to use this column for positivity, to celebrate the best of people and ignore, where possible, the worst.

But you also know me well enough by now that when something triggers me, when something needs calling out, when it cannot be ignored I will, with all my might, do so.

And last weekend something struck me so deeply, I cannot help but address it.

Russell Brand used the excuse of his fame, as the judge noted, as though it was mitigating circumstances.

When he was finally cornered and at last those who had the power to employ him were dragged kicking and screaming into deciding, what was always the case, that he should have no place in the world of broadcasting, he cynically reinvented himself.

He is not stupid, as his prodigious use of flamboyant wordage has shown.

He dropped the lewd act and began to portray himself first as the voice of the people – as if he could ever speak for us – and then as some sort of born again saviour of humanity preaching quite literally online via what appeared to be his very own brand of church with his poor wife alongside him.

So forgive me as we move towards the most important date in the Christian calendar, as we begin to busy ourselves with shopping and planning what, for many, is still of religious significance, I cannot stay silent.

Russell Brand was always a disgusting character.

I like my comedy to be funny. And he never was.

He was both arrogant and blatant in his attitudes towards women and sex if you watch the documentaries on him that appeared on both ITV and Netflix this last week.

He was a man who epitomised the definition of misogyny wrapped up in what was dismissed as laddish humour. He was as one documentary proclaimed in the title, hiding in plain sight.

He was also, as we now know, worse than the worst we could ever have imagined.

And here, like so many, I pay tribute to the women he didn’t silence and the journalists that tenaciously worked to ensure their voices were heard.

All against the backdrop that he was now a changed man, a family man who had found God.

And who the establishment was out to silence. And still he continues in the same vein from his prison cell.

Last weekend he used God as blatantly as he used women to send a message via his wife on their quasi religious Sunday broadcast to firstly protest his innocence and secondly to hint at dark forces at play by beginning with the statement: “As you know I am not guilty. .. so something strange is happening.”

What is strange, dare I say it, is that women were believed and the full force of the law was meted out to the predator who preyed upon them. But that is what not angered me so.

It was the quote he insisted his wife read out from Philippians written from prison by Apostle Paul where he was being held by the Romans for being an agitator against their rule. I was sickened to my stomach. I have to quote it for you to understand the full force of my disgust.

Paul writes: “What has happened to me has actually served to advance the gospel, I am in chains for Christ. And because of my chains most of my brothers and sisters have become more confident in the Lord. I am in chains but what does it matter ?”

And so Russell Brand, as one of the sisters mentioned in the Bible passage you asked your wife to read, I am here to tell you what does matter and yes, why we have every right to now feel more confident… and it is for none of the reasons quoted.

It ‘matters’ because at least one more cynical predator is behind bars. It ‘matters’ because it not only encourages other women to call out abuse against them, but it gives us all ‘confidence’ that we will not be seen as hysterical liars, troublemakers, can’t take a joke kind of women but as victims of a power imbalance that has meant for too long and too often they either dare not report or were brushed aside.

Brand is a rapist and abuser of women who was caught.

His poor wife, and yes she is a victim too, will no doubt cling onto the hope that the truth as has been shown, is a lie.

It is not. A jury and a judge said so.

And there is not a right thinking woman in the land who does not celebrate that which we have been forced to suffer in silence for so long, the demise of a man who was enabled, yes enabled or at least excused as regards his behaviour on the grounds of ‘that’s just who he is’.

Well now the joke is on him.

MPs are debating whether they can change the law to make an older man preying on a younger woman a criminal offence.

No need. Just introduce a new offence of coercive control or take out the age limitation of 16 on the grooming bill and we will be getting somewhere.

We don’t want new systems in place in broadcasting. We need safeguards against that sort behaviour we have all seen or experienced for too long in all walks of life.

And a promise we as women will be listened to.

Russell Brand signed off the statement with a sickening ‘Yours in Christ.’ May I remind him of something Christ himself said before he disappears for good out of harms way, his power stripped, and on the sex offenders register for life.

Instead of protesting his innocence may I quote from the Christ he claims to have turned to.

It comes from Luke Chapter 3 verse 3. ´Unless you repent you too will all perish’.
