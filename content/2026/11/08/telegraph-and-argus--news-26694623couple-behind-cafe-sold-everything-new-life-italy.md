A Bradford couple who sold their home, business and possessions to start a new life in Italy say they have never regretted taking the leap.

Before building a life together, Joanna from Baildon and Antonio from Wibsey hit it off at JB's - a popular Bradford nightclub in the early 90s.

For a time, Antonio ran hair salons in Bradford, while Joanna worked for a pharmaceutical company before the pair opened Friars Café in Bingley.

But after years spent juggling work, business commitments and welcoming in their young son, the couple began dreaming of a simpler existence.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21665558.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Joanna and Antonio Nobile shortly after meeting in Bradford almost 30 years ago. *(Image: Joanna Nobile)*

Now, 30 years later, Joanna spoke to the T&A about how it all began.

She said: "We were just on the treadmill, it was all work and no play....we just thought, what do we actually need?

"We found ourselves asking: what would happen if we just sold everything and started again?"

## A map of Tuscany and a bottle of Chianti

The decision to leave Yorkshire behind came after an evening in 1996 spent looking at a map of Tuscany over a bottle of Chianti.

Joanna said: "We got a map of Tuscany and put it on the floor of the living room, we had a bottle of Chianti and sat looking at the map. We were several glasses in and said, 'Let's look at Lucca'."

After booking a flight to Italy, the couple spent time exploring the region and viewing properties.

They eventually stumbled across an abandoned former cowshed in the hills near Bagni di Lucca.

Joanna explained: "The cow stalls were still there and the hay was still on the floor where the cows had left 60 years ago...it was a massive project, but we looked at it and said, 'We can do this'."

## 'If we didn't do it then, we'd never do it'

Returning to Bradford, the couple made a life-changing decision.

"We put everything on the market," Joanna said. "The car sold, the house sold, everything."

Their son, Massimiliano, was still young, allowing the family to relocate before he became established within the school system.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21665562.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Antonio Nobile harvesting grapes at the family's Tuscan home. *(Image: Joanna Nobile)*

The family packed up their belongings and drove across Europe to begin their new life.

Joanna said: "It was quite liberating. Of course, it was scary because we were stepping into the unknown, but at the same time it felt like if we didn't do it then, we might never do it at all."

After spending a couple of years, renovating, living off their land and selling chutneys to the locals, Antonio and Joanna took the plunge to turn their Airbnb into a holiday retreat, called Stalla Di Guzzano.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21665556.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Despite growing up in Tuscany, Massimiliano remained a devoted Bradford City supporter. *(Image: Joanna Nobile)*![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21665559.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Massimiliano Nobile meets former Bradford City player Chris Kamara.*(Image: Joanna Nobile)*

## Bradford roots remain close to home

Despite spending nearly three decades in Italy, the family's connection to Bradford remains as strong as ever.

Antonio is a lifelong Bradford City supporter and was among those who escaped the Valley Parade fire in 1985, while son Massimiliano was raised as a dedicated Bantams fan.

Speaking on the recent West Yorkshire Derby, Antonio jokingly joined the call to say they would not be discussing the Huddersfield match after the defeat.

The family's loyalty to the club has endured despite the distance, with Bradford City flags often hanging from their Tuscan home and regular trips back to England whenever the club reached Wembley.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21665561.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Antonio and Massimiliano Nobile working together among the olive trees at their Tuscan home. *(Image: Joanna Nobile)*

## Embracing Tuscan life

Today, the family are firmly embedded in the local community, with Massimiliano rowing for a club near Pisa while Joanna and Antonio embrace local traditions in Bagni di Lucca and help organise retreats, photography workshops and writers' events through The Tuscany Collective.

Looking back on the moment they decided to swap Yorkshire for Italy, Joanna believes it was one of the best decisions they ever made.

"It has been quite a journey," she told the T&A.

And for anyone considering following a similar path?

She said: "If people are thinking about taking the plunge and moving to Tuscany, I'd be very happy to share what we've learnt."
