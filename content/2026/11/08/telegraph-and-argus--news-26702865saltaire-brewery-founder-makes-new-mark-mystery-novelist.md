A FORMER solicitor who went on to found a brewery is now making his mark as a mystery novelist.

Tony Gartland, who established Saltaire Brewery after a career in law, is the author of The Spiral Path, the second title in his Aunt Lily Yorkshire Mystery series, written under the pen name Anthony Aberford.

Mr Gartland arrived in Leeds in 1979 as an articled clerk and later became a corporate partner at Eversheds before moving into property investment and, eventually, brewing.

After founding Saltaire Brewery, he ran the business for more than 15 years, earning a reputation for its award-winning beers, popular beer club, and autumn festivals.

He sold the brewery in 2024 after the impact of COVID-19 and the cost-of-living crisis.

He said: "The books are quirky and full of larger-than-life characters and humour, but they have the tension you'd want from a good mystery."

His writing career began during the pandemic, when he flew back and forth to the UK to care for his parents and began work on his first Inspector George Zammit novel.

Written under the name AJ Aberford, the series now includes six books and features a Maltese detective.

Mr Gartland said: "Malta and the Southern Mediterranean are such a rich source of stories.

"All you have to do is read the front page of the Times of Malta and the plot lines are there in black and white."

The Inspector Zammit novels combine humour with crime and geopolitics, following a reluctant hero across cities including Milan, Istanbul, and Tripoli.

His latest series brings him back to Yorkshire.

The Aunt Lily mysteries centre on an elderly amateur detective who investigates supernatural events across the North of England.

Set in the Yorkshire Dales, The Spiral Path follows Aunt Lily and her companions from an Ilkley wellness centre as they explore unusual goings-on in the countryside.

A third book in the series is scheduled for release in spring 2027.

Before launching Saltaire Brewery, Mr Gartland spent decades working in law and property.

He started out in Leeds in 1979 and later became a corporate partner at Eversheds, before moving into property investment with a firm backed by Deutsche Bank.

Following the sale of the business in 2003, he enrolled in a brewing science course and went on to launch Saltaire Brewery.

In 2015, he relocated to St Julian’s, Malta, and gradually stepped back from the day-to-day running of the brewery.

Writing became a new focus during the pandemic, leading to the creation of two crime series set in the Mediterranean and Yorkshire.
