One of Britain's most prominent secularist campaigners, who has long argued for a ban on the niqab, has said she would never support Reform UK's call for Scotland to consider restricting it.

Maryam Namazie, the Iranian-born founder of the Council of Ex-Muslims of Britain, told The Herald that the party was "an opponent of women's rights, not a champion of them".

The comments come after MSPs on Wednesday rejected a motion asking the Scottish Government to assess the potential benefits of further restrictions on full-face coverings in public places, including the burqa and niqab.

Instead, the Parliament backed a Government amendment, by 92 votes to 15 with eight abstentions, which acknowledges that it is not for Holyrood to legislate for what women choose to wear.

Ms Namazie has campaigned for decades on the rights of women from Muslim backgrounds and has previously described the chador and burqa as "like a mobile prison and body bag".

Asked about the motion brought forward by Reform UK, Ms Namazie said: "I think it matters enormously that Reform UK is raising this issue, particularly given the party's record on women and minorities and its wider politics.

"My concern is with a politics of hate that directs public anger towards the most vulnerable while failing to address the structural causes of people's suffering.

"Families are struggling to feed themselves, public services are under enormous strain, and people are facing the consequences of years of austerity, inequality and economic insecurity.

"Yet we are once again being encouraged to focus on a minority population, this time on what a small number of Muslim women wear."

She said that was "a familiar political pattern".

"Migrants, Muslims and minorities become convenient scapegoats for problems rooted in political and economic structures and policies.

"It is also worth examining the distance between Reform's anti-establishment image and its record. Farage previously held majority ownership of the company through which Reform operated. He has substantial earnings and received a £5 million personal gift, for example.

"When it comes to women's rights, Reform's record deserves particular scrutiny. Only this week, Reform councillor George Madgwick was charged with 35 offences, including 12 counts of rape.

"ITV's recent undercover investigation also recorded Reform councillors and candidates making racist, violent and sexually degrading remarks.

"Nigel Farage has questioned the existing abortion time limit, while other Reform representatives have opposed measures extending abortion rights. These are relevant to the discussion.

"Whatever my views on the burqa and niqab, I would never support Reform UK's proposal to consider further restrictions on them.

"Women's emancipation is a struggle for feminist and progressive movements committed to equality, irrespective of background and belief.

"In my view, Reform UK, with its politics of scapegoating and division, is an opponent of women's rights, not a champion of them."

Responding to the comments, Thomas Kerr, the party's Scottish deputy leader, said: "The burqa and niqab are medieval garments, too often forced on women by men in their own families.

"Both are designed to erase women from public life and strip them of basic dignity.

"Reform UK will never apologise for standing up for women's rights simply because that argument is unpopular or uncomfortable for some.

"I remain clear that Scotland should ban all face coverings in public in the interest of equality, security and social cohesion."

![Reforms Helen McDade, Malcolm Offord and Thomas Kerr at Scottish Parliament](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/20940032.jpg?type=mds-article-620)MSPs rejected Reform's motion on Wednesday *(Image: Jane Barlow/PA Wire)*

During Wednesday's debate, Helen McDade, the Reform MSP who moved the motion, told Holyrood: "We in Reform are proposing that no face coverings should be allowed in public places."

She described canvassing in the Gorton and Denton by-election, where she said she saw women in burqas, and called the area "a ghetto".

The Minister for Victims and Community Safety, Kirsten Oswald, said the motion was "deliberately divisive, racist, Islamophobic and designed to foster hostility". She pointed out that it named only the burqa and niqab and made no mention of balaclavas.

Green MSP Maggie Chapman said the evidence from France, which has had a ban for 16 years, showed that women who wear religious clothing had experienced more verbal abuse, harassment and physical assault, including having their veils pulled off and being spat on.

SNP MSP David Linden, who sits on the Criminal Justice Committee, said the most recent census recorded 58,165 Muslim women in Scotland and that research suggests one or two% of them wear the niqab or burqa, meaning the debate concerns roughly 580 women.

He said he had asked the Scottish Police Federation at that morning's committee meeting whether its members were having problems with women wearing the niqab or burqa.

"It was very clear that they were not."

On Tuesday, the Crown Prosecution Service said it had charged Mr Madgwick, leader of Reform UK in Hampshire, with the offences, which it said took place between 2008 and 2021 in Portsmouth and Wales.

It said the 37-year-old faced charges including rape, sexual assault, controlling or coercive behaviour, and making indecent images of a child against four victims - who were aged 16 and 17 at the time.

Mr Madgwick, who is due to appear at Portsmouth Magistrates' Court on 25 November, denies any wrongdoing. Reform UK said he had been suspended from the party "with immediate effect".
