York and areas of North Yorkshire have been hit by a fog warning which will be in place until Monday morning.

The Met Office has placed the region under a yellow weather warning, which will last until 11am on Monday (November 9).

The warning stretches just south of Easingwold and covers York, Tadcaster and Selby.




A Met Office spokesperson said: “Fog patches will form readily again during Sunday evening and overnight becoming extensive by Monday morning.

“Some dense patches of fog are expected with visibility down to around 50 metres or less in some places. Fog thinning slowly later on Monday morning.”

![The Met Office has placed the region under a yellow fog warning, with drivers warned to allow for extra journey time](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21686426.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The Met Office has placed the region under a yellow fog warning, with drivers warned to allow for extra journey time *(Image: Met Office)*

Drivers have been advised to check the condition on the road before driving, leaving extra journey time or amending plans for the work commute tomorrow.

Commuters have also been warned that buses and train services may be affected, along with flight and ferry travel.

Niamh Murray, Met Office operational meteorologist, said: “With autumn and winter and getting fog during this time of year, we get much longer nights, and that allows temperatures to sort of fall away.

“We get a longer period of cooling and also the temperatures are much lower, so they’re much closer to the temperature that we need for the air to become saturated and therefore form fog.

“Another thing that we need to form fog is quite light winds, not absolutely no winds.

“We just have the perfect recipe at the moment: light winds, clear skies, long nights, to allow that fog to develop. And particularly if we see the negative numbers, it can quite stubborn to clear the next day.”
