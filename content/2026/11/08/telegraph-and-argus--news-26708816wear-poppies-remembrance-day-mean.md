The poppy has become a powerful symbol of remembrance, worn to honour those who have lost their lives in conflict.

Each November, many people wear the red poppy to remember those who have died in war.

The Royal British Legion’s annual Poppy Appeal raises money for the Royal British Legion.

This is an organisation that helps those impacted by conflicts and all the servicemen and women who are still alive today and whose lives were changed by wars they fought in.

## Why do we wear poppies?

The tradition began after the First World War, when red poppies were seen growing on the battlefields where so many soldiers lost their lives.

Inspired by John McCrae’s poem In Flanders Fields, the poppy became a lasting symbol of remembrance for all those who have died in service to their country.

## When should you wear a poppy?

The Royal British Legion asks supporters to wear a poppy during the Remembrance period, which runs from the last Friday in October until November 11.

Poppies may also be worn at veterans’ funerals and other commemorative events.

## What do the different coloured poppies mean?

Although red is the most commonly worn remembrance poppy, there is a choice of colours that all have a different meaning.

### Purple Poppy

The purple poppy commemorates animals that have been victims of war, including horses, dogs and pigeons used during conflicts, particularly the First World War.

Money raised through the Purple Poppy Appeal, organised by the War Horse Memorial, is donated to charities such as World Horse Welfare and Blue Cross.

### Black Poppy

The black poppy remembers the contributions of Black, African and Caribbean communities to the war effort, including those who served in the military and those who contributed as civilians.

### White Poppy

The white poppy commemorates people who have lost their lives through war and conflict.

It is worn by some people who feel the red poppy can glorify war and is distributed by the Peace Pledge Union, a charity that campaigns for peace and promotes alternatives to conflict.
