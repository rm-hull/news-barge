With miles of countryside to explore and beautiful views found in every corner of the country, England is a great place for walking.

Luckily, in the North East, we're spoilt fo choice when it comes to coastal walks and countryside spots that are ideal for a long autumnal walk.

If you are struggling to choose where to head to next, The Telegraph has released a list of the best walks in each county.

Upper Teesdale made it onto the list for County Durham thanks to the area being home to “England’s most impressive waterfall” - High Force.

![Farmland in Upper Teesdale.](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21668138.jpg?type=mds-article-620)There are lots of walking trails you can follow. *(Image: Getty)*

## What is Upper Teesdale like?

On the area, The Telegraph said: “Plunging 21m down Whin Sill, High Force is England’s most impressive waterfall.

“You can visit it on a short walk, but better is to make a longer loop around Upper Teesdale.

“Walk west from Bowlees through classic North Pennines scenery – hay meadows, old lead-mining heritage, grassy hillocks, craggy scars.

“Return along the Pennine Way, which follows the Tees past dramatic High Force and charming Low Force.

“Finally, cross the river via Wynch Bridge, a suspension bridge dating to 1830.”

High Force waterfall is a must-visit on this walk so you might want to leave some extra time to fully enjoy it and its surrounding scenery.

The waterfall is hugely popular with it having a rating of 4.2 out of 5 on Tripadvisor with people saying it is “one of the best waterfalls” they have ever been to.

Other reviewers said it is a “truly astonishing place of great beauty”.

Low Force waterfall is just as worth visiting during the walk and is rated even higher on Tripadvisor with a score of 4.8.

The waterfall has been praised by visitors for its “very impressive scenery” with people being told not to “overlook” it on a trip to the area.

The area also has plenty of other pathways to follow ranging from easy walks along the riverside to more challenging hikes.

While exploring the area, you might get to see some of the birdlife that has made Upper Teesdale home and if you visit in spring, you can enjoy the wildflowers that grow there.
