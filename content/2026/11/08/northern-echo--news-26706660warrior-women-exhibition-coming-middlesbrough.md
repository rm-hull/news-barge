An exhibition will celebrate the global influence and achievements of Black women.

Warrior Women, organised by Taste of Africa North East, is coming to Middlesbrough’s Hillstreet Shopping Centre.

It will highlight the contributions of Black women from across history and the present day, with the exhibition running from 11am to 3pm on Saturday, November 14.

The display will showcase handmade costumes, live performances, spoken word poetry, and stalls offering food, jewellery, and crafts.

Sade Sangowawa, founder of Taste of Africa North East, said: "Warrior Women is the result of several months of conversations, creativity and collaboration as we’ve explored the incredible Black women who have shaped history and continue to make a difference in our communities.

"We explored especially Black and Caribbean women past and present who have fought wars and influenced society but often are not remembered or mentioned.

"We want to shine a light on their achievements, especially as many of their stories are often overlooked.

"Warrior Women is about recognising and celebrating the impact Black women have had on the world and right here in our Teesside community."

The exhibition also includes a newly commissioned art installation and will feature stories of historical figures such as the Agojie of Dahomey, the all-female military regiment of West Africa, and Queen Nzinga of Angola.

Contemporary figures, including Beyoncé and Rihanna, are also celebrated for their impact on culture and industry.

Ms Sangowawa said: "Many of the young people also said their mums were Warrior Women, and I couldn't agree more.

"When you look around any community, it is often women who are the glue that holds everything together, yet their contributions frequently go unnoticed."

The Creative Youth Leadership team at Taste of Africa North East worked with local artists to develop the exhibition.

Vaina Lumbiwa, who leads the team and is part of the organisation’s executive group, said: "During the Durham University Residential, our young people learned how to identify and tell their own stories through proverbs.

"From this, they created personal Adinkra symbols that beautifully reflect their identities, experiences and aspirations.

"It has been an incredible privilege to work alongside these young people as they discover their inner warrior and celebrate the wonderful Warrior Women from all various aspects of life who have inspired them."

The event is supported by Hillstreet Shopping Centre and funded by Arts Council England and Middlesbrough Council.
