AN NHS allotment project has won a national award.

Bradford District Care NHS Foundation Trust’s Fell Lane Allotment has won an NHS Forest Award in the category ‘collaborative green space project,’ which celebrate the role of green spaces in healthcare.

The project was recognised for its success in bringing together a broad network of organisations to support people cared for by mental health teams.

Joe Courtney, green therapy lead at the trust, said: "We are absolutely delighted that Fell Lane Allotment has received this recognition.

"The project is a fantastic example of what can happen when organisations come together with a shared vision.

"Our partners have brought so much to the project, and this award recognises the collective effort, creativity and commitment that have helped make it what it is."

Fell Lane Allotment is part of the trust’s green therapy work and has established partnerships with groups including the Canal & River Trust, the Yorkshire Dales National Park Authority, the Pieces Project, YorGreen CIC, the Aire Rivers Trust and B&Q.

The project has also teamed up with poet Andy Craven Griffiths and Word Up North to incorporate creativity and the arts into its activities.

The project has created opportunities for people to connect with nature and engage with their environment through hands-on, therapeutic activity.

This award reflects the collective work of staff, partners and everyone who has contributed to making the project a success. The award also recognises the value of creative and arts-based approaches to therapy.
