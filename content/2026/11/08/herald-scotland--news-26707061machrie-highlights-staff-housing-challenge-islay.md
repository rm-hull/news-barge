For golf resorts in some of Scotland’s most spectacular locations, attracting visitors is only half the battle. Finding somewhere to live for the people who keep the operation running can be just as important.

That challenge is spelled out unusually clearly in the latest accounts from The Machrie on Islay, where the availability of experienced hospitality workers is identified as one of the principal risks facing the business. The resort, owned by boutique hotel group Another Place, says its location presents “clear resource challenges” and describes staff accommodation as a “significant driver” of its ability to attract workers.

The company has taken a step towards addressing the problem, with planning permission secured in May 2024 to build and install 13 staff supervisor accommodation units, a staff recreation unit and nine staff caravans.

![Business of golf logo](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/20731906.jpg?type=mds-article-620) *(Image: Damian Shields)*

The latest accounts do not indicate whether work has begun, but the move highlights an issue that extends far beyond Islay. Many hospitality and tourism employers in rural Scotland face the additional difficulty of finding suitable and affordable housing for the workers they hope to recruit.

For a destination golf resort, that can make staff accommodation part of the infrastructure required to operate the business. Attracting golfers to an island course is of limited benefit if there are not enough people available to run the hotel, restaurant and other services when they arrive.

The Machrie employed an average of 64 people during the year to January 2026, compared with 65 previously. Its wages and salaries bill rose to £1.95 million from £1.89m.

The operation was bought out of administration in 2011 by former BBC chairman Gavyn Davies and Sue Nye, a former political secretary to Gordon Brown. The couple invested in the resort before selling it in 2023 to boutique hotel operator Another Place, which re-launched the resort the following year.

Another Place is owned by holding company Actev II Ltd, of which Mr Davies and Ms Nye are directors and persons with significant control. They therefore remain significant figures in the ownership structure despite the change of operator.

The Machrie's focus on staffing comes as the latest figures point to an improvement in the resort’s financial performance.

Turnover rose by around 13%, from £3.52m to £3.99m, while its pre-tax loss narrowed from £4.79m to £1.17m. However, the previous year’s results included a £2.76m impairment of the freehold property, with no equivalent charge in the latest period.

Despite the improvement, the accounts underline the continuing financial pressures on the business. Cash stood at £27,254 at the year-end, while net current liabilities increased from £1.07m to £1.96m. The company said continuing financial support was available from its parent and investor shareholder, via inter-company loans, when required.

### Musselburgh Links faces £150,000 irrigation problem

![Musselburgh Links Old Course](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21681615.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Musselburgh Links Old Course *(Image: East Lothian Council)*

The future of Musselburgh Links Old Course, recognised internationally as the world’s oldest golf course, has come under scrutiny after investigators found that water from its irrigation system was damaging the greens.

The system draws from the River Esk and nearby Pinkie Burn, but tests found sodium levels capable of damaging fine turf. The sprinklers have not been used since July 2025, forcing staff to water the course manually.

A report to East Lothian Council warns that this approach is unsustainable and that the course’s condition, reputation and future could be put at risk without an independent irrigation system. The proposed replacement is expected to cost about £150,000.

### The end of the road for LIV Golf

![LIV Golf](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21681786.jpg?type=mds-article-620)LIV Golf has announced plans for an “orderly wind-down” of its operations *(Image: PA)*

And for anyone who has been living under a rock this past week, LIV Golf is officially heading for the exit.

After four turbulent years, the breakaway tour has announced an “orderly wind-down” after its proposed rescue deal with BC Partners collapsed amid an exodus of leading players. Jon Rahm, Tyrrell Hatton, Sergio Garcia, Ian Poulter, Louis Oosthuizen and Lee Westwood are among those to have departed.

It brings an extraordinary chapter in professional golf towards its conclusion. LIV Golf arrived in 2022 with Saudi backing, enormous player contracts and ambitions to overturn the traditional structure of the sport. Four years later, its remaining assets are heading for sale.

### Happy birthday to Around the Greens

![Around the Greens logo](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/18712832.jpg?type=mds-article-620) *(Image: Damian Shields)*

There was a wee milestone this week as Around the Greens reached its second anniversary. Since its launch at the end of October 2024, the series has been getting under the bonnet of Scotland’s golf industry, looking at the clubs, businesses and people behind the game. You can now find all of the series together here.

This week’s story takes us to Midlothian, where Newbattle Golf Club is trying to take control of its own future. The club has leased its course for around 130 years, but after the land was put up for sale members decided they wanted to buy it outright.

Newbattle has now approached around 130 fellow golf clubs for help, asking them to donate four-balls or similar prizes for a silent auction as it seeks to raise up to £40,000 towards professional and legal costs. It has already made an informal offer below the land’s current £700,000 asking price, and is awaiting responses from two prospective lenders.
