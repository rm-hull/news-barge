A MAN has been reunited with the ambulance crew who helped save his life.

Philip Raw suffered a cardiac arrest at home on August 5, 2026.

He was at his home in Sedgefield, County Durham, with his son Thomas when he began experiencing chest pain, which he initially believed was heartburn.

Mr Raw said: "I initially thought it was heartburn and took a tablet, but when the pain came back, I knew something wasn’t right.

"That’s when I asked Thomas to phone an ambulance."

His 16-year-old son stayed calm, called 999, and spoke with the health advisor while waiting for help to arrive.

When the first North East Ambulance Service (NEAS) crew reached the scene, Mr Raw’s condition deteriorated, and he went into cardiac arrest.

He said: "When Thomas said the ambulance crew had arrived, he ran out to get them and that’s when I collapsed.

"I believe that’s when my heart stopped and the crew started CPR."

The ambulance team began immediate resuscitation and took Thomas into another room to shield him from the distressing scene.

The crew later praised Thomas for his composure during the emergency.

He said: "I think instinct just kicked in when Dad became unwell and I knew I needed to call 999.

"I tried to stay calm, listen to what the health advisor was telling me and answer their questions so we could get Dad the right help as quickly as possible."

Mr Raw recently met members of the ambulance crew to thank them in person and to acknowledge the vital role Thomas played that evening.

Survival rates are low, with only one in 10 people leaving hospital alive after a cardiac arrest.

Adam Hall, one of the paramedics who attended, said: "Philip had suffered a heart attack and, shortly after we arrived, his condition deteriorated and he went into cardiac arrest.

"We immediately started CPR and lifesaving treatment to give him the best possible chance of survival."

Mr Raw was taken to James Cook University Hospital and has since made a good recovery.

He said: "I just want to thank them for saving my life and for the work they do every day.

"I’m so grateful to them all.

"If I’m honest, I try not to think about it because I realise how lucky I was.

"If it wasn’t for my son Thomas and the ambulance crews, I wouldn’t be here to tell my story."
