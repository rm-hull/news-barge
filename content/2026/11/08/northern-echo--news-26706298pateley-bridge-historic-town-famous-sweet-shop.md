Located away in a North Yorkshire valley is a historic market town where independent shops, centuries-old buildings and one of Britain's oldest sweet shops continue to attract visitors.

Pateley Bridge sits in the heart of Nidderdale, surrounded by steep hills, rolling countryside and the River Nidd.

The North Yorkshire town, around 15 miles north-west of Harrogate, is known for its picturesque high street, traditional businesses and access to some of the county's most impressive scenery.

But behind its appearance lies a history stretching back hundreds of years.

Pateley Bridge received its market charter in 1320, during the reign of King Edward II, establishing a trading tradition which continues today.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21680021.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Pateley Bridge *(Image: Andrew Quinn/CAMERA CLUB)*

Its steep high street remains one of the town's most distinctive features, with independent shops, cafés and traditional buildings lining the route uphill from the river.

And despite the challenges facing high streets across Britain, Pateley Bridge has maintained a strong reputation for its independent businesses.

The town has previously received national recognition for its high street, while local shops and food producers continue to attract customers from across Yorkshire and beyond.

However, one of its most famous businesses has been trading for almost 200 years.

The Oldest Sweet Shop, situated at 39 High Street, first opened in 1827 and has continued selling confectionery through generations of change.

The business was recognised by Guinness World Records in 2014 for its long history.

Inside, traditional glass jars filled with sweets line the shelves, while old-fashioned fittings help preserve the atmosphere of a shop from another era.

The town is the only market town within the Nidderdale National Landscape, a protected area covering more than 200 square miles of countryside.

The surrounding valley is sometimes described as "Little Switzerland" because of its dramatic hillsides and scenery.

The River Nidd flows past the foot of the high street, with riverside paths offering opportunities for shorter walks.

For more adventurous visitors, the town is also the starting point for the Nidderdale Way, a 53-mile circular walking route through the surrounding countryside.

The trail passes through villages, woodland and open landscapes, giving walkers an opportunity to explore the area's natural beauty.

Nidderdale was once an important centre for lead mining, with the surrounding hills containing the remains of former workings and industrial sites.

The area also developed connections with quarrying, textiles and railway transport.

One of the best places to discover this history is Nidderdale Museum, housed inside a former Victorian workhouse on King Street.

The building dates from 1863 and now contains displays exploring life in the valley, including farming, traditional trades and local industry.

The museum is run by volunteers and has become an important centre for preserving Nidderdale's heritage.

Nearby workshops and galleries also reflect the town's continuing connections with traditional crafts and independent makers.

A former Methodist chapel has found a new use as a theatre, providing another example of how historic buildings have been adapted.

Beyond Pateley Bridge, visitors can explore some of North Yorkshire's best-known countryside attractions.

Brimham Rocks, with its natural rock formations, is a short drive away.

Other nearby destinations include Gouthwaite Reservoir, Stump Cross Caverns and the dramatic Coldstones Cut sculpture overlooking a working quarry.
