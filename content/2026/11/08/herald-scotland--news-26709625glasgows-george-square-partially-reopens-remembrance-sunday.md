**"We shall remember them." The closing of Binyon's Lines reverberated around George Square as the Minister of Glasgow Cathedral's booming voice filled it on Remembrance Sunday.**

The service couldn't be held at the Cenotaph last year because the square was closed for the ongoing redevelopment works; instead, it was held at the Cathedral.

As Rev. Mark Edward Johnstone led the service, he spoke of the “transformed” square and asked that people remember not only those who fell long ago, but those who “have woken up today bearing the loss of war across the world".

Addressing the gathered crowd in the partially reopened square after a "season of celebration", Rev. Johnstone contrasted Glasgow's vibrant public life with the quiet reflection Remembrance demands.

"We celebrated the great achievements of this city and the achievements of its people, its learning, its industry, its ships, its songs, its humour, and its warmth," Rev. Johnstone said.

"Today we gather in a quieter spirit in this great city. We pause to remember the countless men and women who gave their all so that we could live in peace and in freedom."

Marking 110 years since the opening day of the Battle of the Somme on 1 July 1916, Rev. Johnstone recalled how local Glasgow battalions of the Highland Light Infantry went into battle together, suffering severe casualties that reverberated across whole neighbourhoods.

"They were from homes in this city, from this city's trades and offices," he said

"They joined up together, and many of them fell together. Street by street, workplace by workplace, this city learned what war costs."

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21685063.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The Last Post was playing and echoed through the Square *(Image: Robert Perry)*

Speaking to ongoing global conflicts, Rev. Johnstone highlighted Glasgow's identity as a refuge for families fleeing violence worldwide.

"In too many places around our world, families are grieving this morning for those that they have lost in conflict," Rev. Johnstone added, while commenting that many who now call Glasgow home arrived seeking escape from war elsewhere.

"Today, we recognise those things that unite us."

Concluding the service, Rev. Johnstone challenged the community to keep faith with the fallen by actively demonstrating unity and kindness in daily life.

"May we find unity in being peacemakers. May we find unity in being kind to one another," he urged

"These are the things they gave their lives to protect. These are the ways we keep faith with them."

At 11am, George Square fell silent for two minutes as the city lined the streets behind the heras fencing and remembered those who have lost their lives in conflict, paying tribute to all who have served.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21685062.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Deputy First Minister of Scotland Jenny Gilruth lays the Scottish Government wreath *(Image: Robert Perry)*

The silence was followed by wreath-laying at the Cenotaph, including a wreath laid by Deputy First Minister Jenny Gilruth.

Lord Provost of Glasgow, Jacqueline McLaren, acting as Lord-Lieutenant on behalf of Glasgow's citizens, also laid a wreath at the Cenotaph.

She said: "Remembrance Sunday is one of the most important and poignant occasions in Glasgow's civic calendar. It is a time for our city to stand together and remember all those who made the ultimate sacrifice, as well as generations of men and women who have served our country and those who continue to do so.

"It was particularly significant this year to be able to gather once again at the Cenotaph in George Square, in the heart of our city, to pay our respects together."

1st Scots Guards provided a Guard of Honour, with music from The Combined Lowland and Highland Bands of the Royal Regiment of Scotland and the Police Scotland and Federation Pipe Band.

Last week, Glasgow's Garden of Remembrance opened outside Kelvingrove Museum and Art Gallery, where PoppyScotland also launched its 2026 appeal.

The Garden provides a dedicated place for people to pause, reflect and remember during the Remembrance period.

The centre of Edinburgh also fell silent for two minutes as Scotland paused to remember those who have died in conflict on Remembrance Sunday.

At the annual service of remembrance outside the city chambers, a two-minute silence was observed from 11am.

Ahead of the ceremony, First Minister John Swinney said he would honour those who made the “ultimate sacrifice” to defend freedom.

He also paid tribute to the D-Day veteran Charlie Horne, who died aged 100 around a week before Remembrance Sunday.

Mr Horne, who served on a minesweeping vessel off the coast of Omaha Beach, was one of Scotland’s last surviving D-Day veterans.

On Sunday morning, a military parade marched down the Royal Mile from Edinburgh Castle, led by the RAF Central Scotland Pipes and Drums.

Among those who attended the ceremony was 100-year-old Second World War veteran George McLeod, formerly of 4th Battalion, the King’s Own Scottish Borderers.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21685157.jpg?type=mds-article-620)100-year-old George McLeod who served with Kings Own Scottish Borders holds a wreath during the Remembrance Sunday ceremony at the Stone of Remembrance outside the City Chambers in Edinburgh *(Image: PA)*

Speaking ahead of the ceremony in Edinburgh, Mr Swinney said: “Today at 11 o’clock, I’ll be remembering those who made the ultimate sacrifice so that we can enjoy the peace, the liberty and the freedom that we enjoy today.

“Also, to express my appreciation to members of the Armed Forces who continue to act on a daily basis to make sure that we are protected and that we are safe, in a world that is much more conflicted than many of us would like to see. One that requires the enduring commitment of the Armed Forces to keep us safe.”

Mr Swinney said he had the “extraordinary privilege” of meeting Mr Horne in 2024 at the Normandy commemorations.

He said: “Although these events are a long time ago, we should never forget their significance.

“Because our freedom today is rested upon those sacrifices that were made all those years ago.”

He added: “He was an incredibly joyful and buoyant man…

“His family were immensely proud of his contribution.”
