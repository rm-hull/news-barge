A catering van was banned from serving customers with food allergies after inspectors found a string of hygiene and food safety failures.

Jalebe Junction was handed a one-star food hygiene rating after inspectors uncovered a series of safety concerns.

The food truck, registered at 121 Southfield Lane in Canterbury, Bradford, was given one star out of five - meaning “major improvement necessary” - after a council inspection.

Environmental health officers visited on June 30 and raised concerns about food handling, the unit’s condition and safety procedures.

## What the report said

According to the inspection report, staff were observed handling money and open food without washing their hands in between.

Inspectors also found food handlers without suitable protective clothing and no hand-drying facilities beside the wash basin.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21675193.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The food truck on Southfield Lane *(Image: T&A)*

The report highlighted a number of structural issues, including a broken and dirty microwave, a floor requiring a deep clean and a hole beneath a sink that had been temporarily covered with a plastic bottle.

Officers said the hole must be permanently sealed to prevent pests getting in.

Inspectors also raised concerns about the management of food safety.

## Banned from serving customers with allergens

The business was unable to provide evidence of a documented food safety management system and could not provide allergen information for food served by the business.

The report states it is a legal requirement for allergen information to be available for all foods served and says staff must receive training to demonstrate an understanding of allergen management procedures.

Until those requirements are met, the business was told it "must not serve persons declaring allergens".

Further issues identified by inspectors included the lack of a temperature probe for monitoring food and missing supplier traceability information.

The business was informed that inspectors intended to revisit on August 13 and would expect legal requirements highlighted during the inspection to have been completed.

## The T&A visits the site

The August inspection results have not been published, but the business still has a one-star rating on the Food Standards Agency website.

When the Telegraph & Argus visited the site this week, the food truck was located in the car park of a Premier Convenience Store.

The unit appeared boarded up, although the T&A understands the business is still operating.

The T&A has attempted to contact the owners of Jalebe Junction for comment but has been unable to make contact.

**A spokesperson for the business is welcome to get in touch if they wish to respond to the findings of the inspection.**
