Review: SCO/Manze City Halls, Glasgow Keith Bruce four stars

PRINCIPAL Guest Conductor Andrew Manze is with the Scottish Chamber Orchestra for a fortnight with two programmes featuring two cellists – Sheku Kanneh-Mason’s return to the stage from injury being the attraction this coming weekend.

Norwegian Amalie Stalheim may be a less familiar name, but her performance of works vintage and modern on a beautiful 18th century instrument once owned by Mstislav Rostopovich made this concert a highly memorable one.

Exactly two years ago, Manze conducted the Viola Concerto of Swedish composer Anders Hillborg in this venue for Nordic Music Days, with Lawrence Power the soloist. Hillborg’s Cello Concerto is a year older, composed in 2020, and shares ingredients, with first cello Philip Higham a crucial partner to the soloist at its opening, just as principal viola Max Mandel was with Power.

The work has an ethereal start before the solo part takes off in a burst of virtuosity with the wind section in crucial support.

With percussive punctuation from piano, bells and gong, Manze was always careful with the level of volume behind the soloist, whose part ranged from high harmonics to the rich resonance of the lower strings – all superbly played by Stalheim.

After the interval, she was equally at home with Tchaikovsky’s Rococo Variations, in the version radically revised by its dedicatee Wilhelm Fitzenhagen.

It and the Tchaikovsky Serenade for strings that followed are both works in which the Russian was looking to the models of earlier music by Mozart and Haydn, just as Hillborg’s concerto clearly referenced early choral music in its cadences, so this programme was much less a game of two halves than it first appeared.

Both Tchaikovsky pieces also contain tastes of his best known ballet music, and were once much more frequently heard than they are now.

Manze brought to the Serenade something of a period instrument sensibility, with brisk and clean playing from the SCO strings and the third movement Larghetto lush, but not too much.

The conductor, who lived and worked in Sweden for many years, opened this concert with the 1966 Lyric Fantasy by Lars-Erik Larsson, an illustrious predecessor of Hillborg. His very precisely scored writing has a chamber music feel ideal for the SCO and also looked back, if only to music of earlier in the 20th century.

First horn Lauren Reeve-Rawlings led out the solo voices in the winds and there was some marvellous pizzicato playing from the SCO’s low strings.
