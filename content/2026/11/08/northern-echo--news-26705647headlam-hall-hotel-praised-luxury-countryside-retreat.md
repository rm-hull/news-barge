A historic country house hotel near Darlington is winning praise for its stunning grounds, homegrown produce and family-run atmosphere.

Headlam Hall Hotel, in Headlam near Gainford, combines more than 400 years of history with modern luxury, including bedrooms, a spa, a golf course and award-winning dining.

The hall dates back to the turn of the 17th century, when Henry Birkbeck and his wife, Anne Brackenbury, built the property after the estate passed to the Birkbeck family.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21545575.jpg?type=mds-article-620) *(Image: HEADLAM HALL)*

An elaborate oak fireplace installed by Henry as a gift to his wife remains one of the hall's most striking historic features. It bears the Birkbeck coat of arms.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21481312.jpg?type=mds-article-620) *(Image: HEADLAM HALL)*

The estate passed through several prominent families, including the Brocketts, who made major Georgian alterations, before Lord and Lady Gainford acquired it in 1912.

Local farmers John and Ann Robinson bought the property in 1977, when it was in poor repair.

Their efforts to save the historic building grew into a thriving hospitality business.

In the early days, Ann Robinson cooked for guests and visitors served themselves at an honesty bar.

Investment transformed the hall, adding luxury bedrooms, a spa, a golf course, restaurants and event spaces.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21481313.jpg?type=mds-article-620) *(Image: HEADLAM HALL)*![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21481310.jpg?type=mds-article-620)*(Image: HEADLAM HALL)*

Still family-owned, Headlam Hall offers bedrooms and suites, a spa with a 15-metre pool, sauna, steam room and hydrotherapy pool, plus a picturesque nine-hole golf course.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21481317.jpg?type=mds-article-620) *(Image: HEADLAM HALL)*

Dining is a major attraction, with the kitchens using locally sourced ingredients and seasonal produce from the hotel’s own gardens.

Guests can eat in the Orangery Restaurant, take afternoon tea in the lounges or book a private dining experience.

The mix of heritage and modern comforts has won over visitors.

One guest called it the "perfect place for a country retreat" in a five-star review.

They said: "The architecture of the building is impressive and the food served, amazing.

"Strongly recommend to visit this classy place."

Another guest praised the hotel’s sustainability efforts and homegrown produce.

They said: "The building and grounds are immaculate.

"I really enjoyed knowing that the food I was eating was sourced either locally or from the hotel's own gardens.

"It's exceptional that the produce is grown just about 100 metres away from the kitchen.
