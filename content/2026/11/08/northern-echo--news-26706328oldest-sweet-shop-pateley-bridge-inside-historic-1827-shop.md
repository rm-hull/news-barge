A tiny sweet shop tucked away on a North Yorkshire high street has been selling traditional treats for almost 200 years, and still looks similar to how it did generations ago.

The Oldest Sweet Shop, in Pateley Bridge, first opened in 1827, when George IV was on the throne, and Queen Victoria was still a child.

Nearly two centuries later, customers can still step through its doors to find shelves packed with colourful jars of old-fashioned sweets.

And despite its small size, the business has earned international recognition for its history.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/20505065.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The Oldest Sweet Shop, in Pateley Bridge *(Image: TRIPADVISOR)*

The shop, at 39 High Street, was recognised by Guinness World Records in 2014 as the world's oldest continuously trading confectionery shop.

It has since become an attraction in its own right, drawing visitors keen to experience a piece of Yorkshire history.

The shop occupies a historic building dating back to 1661, with traditional wooden counters, old-fashioned weighing scales and vintage advertising signs.

Glass jars line the shelves, containing many of the sweets which generations of British customers remember from childhood.

Among its traditional favourites are pear drops, humbugs, cola cubes, sherbet lemons and rhubarb-and-custard sweets.

Customers can also find liquorice, bonbons and other confectionery associated with traditional sweet shops.

Rather than relying entirely on modern packaged products, the business has retained the experience of choosing sweets from jars and having them weighed out.

The shop has survived enormous changes since opening in the early 19th century.

When it first began trading, Britain's railway network was still in its infancy, while many of the sweets now regarded as traditional favourites had yet to become widely available.

It also continued operating through the difficulties of the Second World War, when sweet rationing restricted the amount of confectionery customers could buy.

Rationing remained in place until 1953, eight years after the war ended.

But the shop's long history eventually brought it recognition far beyond Nidderdale.

Its historic interior and traditional approach have also attracted attention from television programmes and national publications.

The business is now owned by Ben Howie, who bought it in 2021.

He grew up in nearby Knaresborough and remembered visiting the business as a child.

According to stories associated with the premises, it is reputedly haunted by a pipe-smoking ghost, with occasional reports of the smell of tobacco.

There is no evidence to establish the tale as anything more than local folklore, but it adds another unusual detail to the building's history.

From pear drops and humbugs to sherbet lemons and liquorice, it remains a place where visitors can rediscover sweets remembered from childhood.

The Oldest Sweet Shop is at 39 High Street, Pateley Bridge, North Yorkshire, HG3 5JZ.
