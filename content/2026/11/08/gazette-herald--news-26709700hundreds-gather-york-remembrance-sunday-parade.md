HUNDREDS of people gathered across York to remember the men and women who made the ultimate sacrifice in service of their country as the city marked Remembrance Sunday.

Onlookers watched as veterans and members of the armed forces community marched from Clifford’s Tower via the Mansion House to the City War Memorial today (Sunday, November 8).

The remembrance event featured those currently serving from regiments across York, along with invited representatives from local groups, and The Band of the Royal Yorkshire Regiment and the York Railway Institute Band, who played during the parade and service.




A two-minute silence was observed at 11am following the sounding of The Last Post at the memorial.

![The service at the war memorial including a wreath-laying ceremony](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21685688.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The service at the war memorial including a wreath-laying ceremony *(Image: Newsquest)*

Services of remembrance were also held at York Minster and Acomb War Memorial, with residents, visitors and dignitaries coming together in recognition of those who laid down their lives to protect our country.

The parade later returned to St Helen’s Square, where the Mansion House flag was lowered to half-mast, as the Lord Mayor, Civic Party and guests saluted the parade as it continued along Coney Street.

![The York Railway Institute Band joined the parade](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21685682.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The York Railway Institute Band joined the parade *(Image: Newsquest)*

Councillor Claire Douglas, Leader of City of York Council and Armed Forces Champion, said: “This annual service of remembrance carries particular significance in York, a city with a 2,000-year-old military history.

“On Remembrance Sunday and later in the week, on Remembrance Day itself, we reflect on conflicts past and present, and honour those who have served and fallen, and those who continue to serve.

“We do this as we proudly support our Armed Forces community by working with partners to ensure that those who serve, or have served, are treated fairly and with respect.”

![The parade was led to the City War Memorial for a service of remembrance](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21685663.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The parade was led to the City War Memorial for a service of remembrance *(Image: Newsquest)*

In London, Robert Taylor, from Selby, joined a 50-strong group representing Help for Heroes in the Cenotaph Parade.

The former soldier, who is now chair of Skipton Building Society's Armed Forces Network, comes from a family with a strong military background.

Born in West Germany while his father was serving overseas, Robert joined the Army when he was 16.

His dad, Athlyn Taylor, served in the Royal Signals for 20 years and was awarded the British Empire Medal for his work supporting Second World War veterans.

His uncle, Sergeant Robert W Taylor, served in the infantry and was stationed in Northern Ireland, Germany, Cyprus and Aden.

![Robert's father, Athlyn Taylor, receiving his British Empire Medal at Fulford Barracks in York](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21685691.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Robert's father, Athlyn Taylor, receiving his British Empire Medal at Fulford Barracks in York *(Image: Supplied)*

Robert said he was proud to represent both the armed forces community and his colleagues at Skipton Building Society during the parade.

He said: “I feel really proud to be part of the Help for Heroes contingent at the Cenotaph.

“As chair of our Armed Forces Network, I’m marching for my Skipton colleagues who have served and spouses of former or currently serving personnel and reaffirming our commitment to the Armed Forces community.

“I'll be thinking about my dad and my uncle, and former colleagues who served, like Corporal Maheshkumar ‘Mick’ Islaina and his six-month-old daughter Nivruti, who were both killed when IRA gunmen opened fire on their car at a petrol station in Wildenrath, West Germany, in 1989.”

## 'It's so important that we commemorate the men and women who lost their lives while serving our country'

This year's London parade was the largest ever attended by a Help for Heroes contingent and included some of the UK's most seriously wounded veterans.

Robert said: “It's so important that we commemorate the men and women who lost their lives while serving our country - and at Help for Heroes we also remember those still fighting their own battles today.

“We remain at the side of veterans and their families who are struggling with painful injuries, mental trauma, isolation, and more, providing life-changing support for as long as it takes.

“That’s why it’s more important than ever that the public makes a promise to stand alongside our veterans to ensure they get the support they deserve.”
