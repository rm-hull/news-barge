Amanda Owen's restoration of Anty John's has uncovered more than the challenges of transforming a derelict farmhouse.

The historic Yorkshire Dales property has a fascinating connection to Anthony Clarkson, a mapmaker whose work documented the landscape of Swaledale generations before the Owen family became famous.

Clarkson's story has become an important part of Our Farm Next Door: Amanda, Clive and Kids, with the programme exploring the lives of people who occupied the building long before its modern restoration.

But who was Anthony Clarkson, and why is his name associated with the farmhouse?

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21670877.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Anty John's *(Image: CHANNEL 4)*

The building dates back to the early 18th century and was once home to Clarkson, a land surveyor and commissioner associated with the mapping of Swaledale.

His work included tithe maps, historical documents used to record land and property.

These records provide an insight into the way the Yorkshire Dales landscape was organised and used during the 19th century.

Clarkson's documents have survived, with historical reporting identifying material held in the National Archives.

The farmhouse itself has also survived, although not without considerable deterioration.

By the time Amanda and Clive Owen purchased the property in 2020, it had been empty for decades.

Parts of the roof had collapsed, leaving the family facing an extensive restoration project.

The building was designated Grade II listed in 1986, recognising its architectural and historic interest.

As the restoration progressed, Amanda began investigating its former occupants and the stories associated with the property.

Clarkson's diaries have become a particular focus.

The broadcaster said the historical material would draw parallels between the lives of the farmhouse's former occupants and the modern Owen family.

It offers a different perspective on the renovation, connecting the work of today's Yorkshire Dales farmers with people who lived in the same landscape around 200 years earlier.
