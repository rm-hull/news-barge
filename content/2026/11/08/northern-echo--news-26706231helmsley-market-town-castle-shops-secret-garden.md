Just off a busy North Yorkshire road lies a picturesque market town where medieval castle ruins, independent shops and a hidden garden offer visitors plenty to do.

Helmsley sits on the A170, around 14 miles from Thirsk, on the edge of the North York Moors, and has consistently been listed as one of the best places to live by national rankings.

But despite its relatively small size, the town has a top collection of attractions, from a castle dating back to the 12th century to a five-acre walled garden hidden beneath its ruins.

At the heart of Helmsley is its traditional marketplace, surrounded by old sandstone buildings, independent shops, cafés and pubs.

The town has retained much of its historic character, with narrow streets leading away from the square.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21679943.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Helmsley Castle *(Image: Peter Giroux Images/CAMERA CLUB)*

However, one of Helmsley's most impressive landmarks stands just a short walk from the centre.

Helmsley Castle has overlooked the settlement for hundreds of years.

Today, its surviving walls, towers and earthworks provide a reminder of the town's importance during some of England's most turbulent periods.

Visitors can explore the ruins and discover how the castle changed over the centuries.

But tucked away beneath the historic fortress is another attraction which many motorists passing through Helmsley may never realise exists.

Helmsley Walled Garden covers five acres and was originally established in 1758 as a kitchen garden for the Duncombe Park Estate.

Now operated as a charitable attraction, it contains colourful borders, orchards, historic glasshouses and areas devoted to growing fruit and vegetables.

The garden has also appeared on screen, having been used as a filming location for the 2020 adaptation of The Secret Garden.

Visitors can explore its different planting areas, including a collection of traditional Yorkshire apple varieties.

There is also a café inside one of the historic glasshouses, while the gardens are maintained with significant support from volunteers, whose contribution was recognised with The King's Award for Voluntary Service in 2024.

The town also marks the starting point of the Cleveland Way National Trail, a 109-mile walking route which travels through the North York Moors before reaching the Yorkshire coast.

Walkers can follow a section of the trail through woodland and countryside towards Rievaulx Abbey, approximately three miles away.

The remains of the former Cistercian monastery stand in a secluded valley and provide another glimpse into the region's medieval past.

Closer to town, Duncombe Park offers extensive parkland and woodland, while the National Centre for Birds of Prey is another popular attraction.

Despite these historic and natural landmarks, Helmsley remains a working market town rather than simply a tourist destination.

Independent food shops, traditional businesses, cafés and pubs continue to give the centre its character.

The town is also home to a local brewery and an arts centre offering films, performances and exhibitions.

For visitors arriving from Thirsk along the A170, Helmsley offers an opportunity to leave the main road behind and explore somewhere with centuries of history.
