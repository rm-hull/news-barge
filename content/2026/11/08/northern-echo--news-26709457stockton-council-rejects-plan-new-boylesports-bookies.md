A plan to turn a shop into a betting office has been refused by Stockton Council, which argued it would harm a regenerating High Street’s character.

BoyleSports (UK) Ltd applied to turn a retail unit on Dovecot Street in central Stockton into a betting office open from 8am to 10pm. Stockton Council rejected the application, saying it would create an “over-concentration of gambling establishments” and harm the “character and amenity of the Stockton town centre”.

Cleveland Police urged the applicant to introduce measures to “reduce the opportunities for crime and anti-social behaviour”. Public health officers objected to the proposal, saying the prominent site was “already characterised by significant deprivation, health inequalities and vulnerability” and covered by a public space protection order because of anti-social behaviour and street drinking.

The council report said: “The application site occupies a central position overlooking the fountain and Stockton High Street. Approval would introduce a further betting office into an area that already contains a high concentration of gambling premises and would also increase the potential risk of gambling-related harm to children, young people and vulnerable adults.”

Officers said it would undermine efforts to improve community safety, despite bringing an empty premises back into use. The council said the town centre was among the 1% most deprived neighbourhoods in England, had a male life expectancy of 67 and seven gambling-licensed premises on or near the High Street.

Officers said Stockton Recovery Service, three minutes’ walk from the proposed office, was supporting 1,735 people with drug and alcohol problems. About 12 per cent of Stockton residents faced some level of gambling risk, while problem gambling cost the town more than £3 million in 2023.

The report said: “Within Stockton town centre there are 59 premises that provide some form of gambling opportunity, including lottery terminals and scratch cards, alongside the traditional betting shops, casinos and bingo premises. This represents the highest concentration of gambling opportunities within the borough.

“Approval of an additional betting office would further increase the clustering of gambling premises around the fountain area. Gambling venues would be visible in virtually every direction from this central location.

“Public health considers that the proposal would disproportionately increase exposure to gambling opportunities among those most vulnerable to gambling-related harm.”

Officers said the applicant’s measures—including CCTV, Think 25, staff training, self-exclusion schemes and responsible-gambling checks—did not address the population’s “heightened vulnerability”. They said it would also normalise gambling in a “sensitive location” often used by children.

John Beach of the applicant’s agent, Lichfields, said in a rebuttal statement: “The proposed development simply seeks to restore the number of betting offices to its previous level of four… The clustering of betting offices is a common feature of town centres.

“The proposed development would aid the regeneration of the town centre by reusing a long-term vacant unit. Betting offices are a footfall-generating use, which complements the retail function of town centres and supports a healthy retail environment.”

The applicant said in a planning statement that the betting office was a “suitable and appropriate use”. It said the plan would bring “clear and tangible economic benefits”, including three full-time jobs, three part-time jobs and 26 temporary construction jobs.

But the council said adult gaming centres within 200m of the High Street operated on the same principle as betting offices. The council rejected the plan, saying: “There is currently significant investment taking place within Stockton town centre, in respect of securing its future within the ever-challenging retail climate.

“To allow the increase of further gambling establishments would, for the reasons given, be to create a harmful cluster and would create harm to the character and appearance of the central area. The proposed development would not make a positive contribution to Stockton’s significant regeneration.”
