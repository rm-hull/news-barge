THE Remembrance Service at Undercliffe Cemetery will pay tribute to servicemen buried at the historic site.

The ceremony, attended by children from local schools, will also highlight the work of Undercliffe Cemetery Charity Honorary President Allan Hillary - whose 90th birthday is on Armistice Day.

There are known to be 93 First World War burials and 43 Second World War burials in the cemetery. Also buried and commemorated there are more than 400 casualties of many international conflicts - including a soldier who survived the Battle of Waterloo in 1815, and those who fought in the Crimean War, the Boer Wars and other campaigns.

The Remembrance Service will take place on Wednesday, November 11 at the cemetery’s Cross of Sacrifice and Stone of Remembrance.

It starts at 10.30am, with a welcome by Chair of Undercliffe Cemetery Charity Tim Hardy. Guests include Amanda McLaren, Deputy Lieutenant of West Yorkshire, who will read from For the Fallen.

John Jackson of Undercliffe Cemetery Charity said: “The Service of Remembrance at Undercliffe Cemetery is one of the most numerously attended in the Bradford district, and this is largely due to one person - Allan Irving Hillary - whose 90th birthday is on November 11.

“It was during his time as Lord Mayor of Bradford in 2003-04 that Allan became intimately associated with Undercliffe Cemetery. He felt it was important to pay fitting tribute to the hundreds of service personnel who are buried or commemorated in the cemetery by fostering an annual Service of Remembrance at the Cemetery’s Cross of Sacrifice.

“During his lengthy tenure as Chair of Trustees, Allan saw the Charity go from strength to strength and the number of volunteers working to ensure its conservation increase considerably.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21681659.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Allan is Honorary President of the cemetery charity

“In recognition of his dedication to the preservation of Undercliffe Cemetery, and that of the numerous volunteers, the Charity received The Queen’s Award for Voluntary Service in 2022. This prestigious award was a fitting tribute to Allan who that year stepped down from the role of Chair to become Honorary President of the Undercliffe Cemetery Charity, a position he continues to occupy.”

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21681660.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Allan receiving the Queen's Award for Voluntary Service

The Remembrance Service will remember Bradford soldiers including Matthew Hughes, a Victoria Cross recipient, who is buried in the cemetery. Born in Bradford in 1822, Hughes enlisted with the 7th Royal Fusilier. He was wounded in the Crimean War and on June 26, 1857 he was one of 61 soldiers who had the Victoria Cross medal pinned to their chests by Queen Victoria - the only time she did this. More than 100,000 people attended the ceremony in Hyde Park.

Hughes was buried in an unmarked grave in Undercliffe Cemetery but in 1997 a campaign led to a headstone with the VC logo inscribed on it placed on his grave.
