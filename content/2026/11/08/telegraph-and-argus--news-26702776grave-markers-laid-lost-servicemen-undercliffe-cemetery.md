UNDERCLIFFE cemetery in recent years has been going through a long period of restoration by a dedicated team of volunteers, who have been rewarded for their efforts with the Queen’s Award for Voluntary Service.

This historic cemetery, with its fine monuments, has now become a thriving heritage centre for the city of Bradford.

However, some of these volunteers have also been working in co-operation with the Commonwealth War Graves Commission (CWGC) on rather a special project...

The cemetery contains 136 graves of the Fallen from both world wars as well as a Cross of Sacrifice and a Screen Wall commemorating 20 of them - these with no formal headstone within the cemetery.

Such Screen Walls in cemeteries are quite common. Most people today believe it must not have been possible to erect a CWGC headstone for all of the war dead due to ‘ground difficulties’ - but this was not so.

The reality was such that servicemen were buried in existing family plots or ‘Common/Company’ grave plots that would be still ‘open’ for further burials for many years to come. These then would be commemorated collectively on a ‘Screen Wall’. This part had been forgotten.

Alan Raistrick, Undercliffe Cemetery volunteer, noting a name on the Screen Wall and then finding him in the burial register, visited one such grave site - and he realised there were no ‘ground difficulties’ to prevent a formal commemoration.

He then found the same with others. Further investigation by the Undercliffe team showed that these graves were also ‘closed’ some time ago. A century later, it would now be possible to now formally commemorate these men where they were buried.

This is what the Undercliffe Cemetery team now set out to do. They would fund these new commemorations themselves and do all the necessary work, so that these ‘lost’ servicemen would be found.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21675496.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Ready to go: Plaques bearing the names of servicemen

The team have been providing proper grave markers for those soldiers commemorated on the Undercliffe Screen Wall.

This programme now continues grave by grave, each one also providing the opportunity for researching each serviceman’s story, adding these to the growing information on the Fallen buried at Undercliffe Cemetery.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21675499.jpg?type=mds-article-620)A 'lost serviceman' grave found after a century

This is pioneering work, ensuring that every serviceman here at Undercliffe is commemorated as an individual and that all such commemorations continue to be maintained into the future in conjunction with the CWGC.

In 1914, at the beginning of the Great War, Bradford raised its Pals and Territorial battalions.

Bradford saw these battalions smashed, both on the Somme in July 1916 and at Arras in May 1917. Thousands of Bradford men served in many other units too - over 36,000 of them.

Bradford paid a terrible price in the 1914-18 war, and paid a second one in the 1939-45 war too.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21675495.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Pte George Brewer Duke of Wellington’s Regt, died November 17, 1918

The Fallen at Undercliffe cemetery were Bradford people who gave everything in the world wars. They are not forgotten. Today, thanks to the efforts of this remarkable team at Undercliffe cemetery, this price to Bradford has been recognised once more - by Bradford people, for Bradford people.

This research and commemoration project has been a massive team effort. The key volunteers engaged are:

Tim Hardy, Andy Tyne, Debbie Sterling (Undercliffe Cemetery leads, admin work etc.)

Alan Raistrick, Stephen Wood, David Close, Robbie Kelly, Mark Young, Ian Stubbington, Graham Swain and his team (provision of memorials and groundwork)

Margaret Holden and David Whithorn (historical research).

\* Undercliffe Cemetery Remembrance ceremony takes place on Wednesday, November 11.

Those attending are asked to assemble at the cemetery lodge at 10.30am. A wreath will be laid on behalf of the Lord Mayor of Bradford, Cllr Chris Herd, and the Bradford district.

The service and two-minute silence will begin at 11am.
