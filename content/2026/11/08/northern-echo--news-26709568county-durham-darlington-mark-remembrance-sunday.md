Thousands gathered across County Durham and Darlington today to mark Remembrance Sunday and honour those killed in conflict.

Communities fell silent at memorials, churches and cenotaphs across the region to honour Armed Forces personnel who died serving their country.

Durham Cathedral was packed out with members of the public and forces representatives.

The annual parade down Owengate, Saddler Street and through the Market Square set off at 11.30am after the service and wreath-laying.

The parade featured a record number of Armed Forces representatives, including detachments from The Rifles, the Royal Lancers, the Light Dragoons and 607 (County of Durham) Squadron, Royal Air Force.

County Durham Lord-Lieutenant Brigadier Michael Butterwick CBE DL led the salute in the Market Place.

Three bands, including Esh & Bearpark Brass Band, provided the parade's music.

Meanwhile, hundreds of people attended a service at Darlington's Cenotaph in the grounds of the Memorial Hospital, where wreaths were laid and a two-minute silence was observed.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21684640.jpg?type=mds-article-620) *(Image: NORHERN ECHO)*

Across the country, King Charles III led the nation in remembrance at the Cenotaph in Whitehall, London.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21684669.jpg?type=mds-article-620) *(Image: PA/AARON CHOWN)*

Members of the Royal Family, senior politicians and thousands of veterans joined the King as the nation remembered the fallen.

A gun was fired to mark the end of the two-minute silence before the Last Post was sounded by the Buglers of the Royal Marines.

Wearing his field marshal’s uniform, the King laid the first wreath at the Cenotaph to honour service personnel killed in conflicts since the First World War.

The Prince of Wales followed him with a wreath of his own.

The King’s wreath closely resembled one made for King George VI, with 41 paper poppy petals on black leaves.

Prime Minister Andy Burnham paid tribute to members of the Armed Forces community and veterans during the national commemorations.
