More than 1,500 maintenance defects are outstanding across Scotland’s prison estate as the jail population runs more than 700 inmates above design capacity.

Scotland’s prisons are grappling with severe overcrowding, rising violence and staff shortages, with Linda Pollock, chief executive of the Scottish Prison Service (SPS) warning the system was “close to breaking point”.

New figures reveal 1,525 unresolved maintenance issues across the prison estate, including 445 classed as “medium” risk and six as “high”.

High risk defects represent “critical failures” that jeopardise the immediate safety of staff or inmates, breach primary security or completely shut down facilities. Medium is functional defects that impair daily operations or living conditions.

It includes 413 outstanding defects at HMP Edinburgh, the highest recorded figure, followed by 251 at HMP Perth and 149 at HMP Glenochil.

The maintenance burden comes as Scotland’s prison population stands at 8,536, some 731 above the estate’s stated design capacity of 7,805.

Twelve of the country’s 17 prisons are currently operating above their design capacity, underlining the continued pressure on the jail estate.

HMP Barlinnie remains the most overcrowded, currently holding 1,416 prisoners despite having a design capacity of 987, equivalent to 143.5%. It also had 78 outstanding maintenance defects, as of mid-October, including four of the six classed as high risk.

HMP Edinburgh, meanwhile, has both the highest number of unresolved maintenance problems and is operating above capacity, holding 928 prisoners against a capacity of 867.

Figures on planned preventative maintenance also show that 73.72% of work orders were completed on time in 2025/26, compared with 76.94% in 2021/22.

Despite current levels of overcrowding, Scottish Government projections estimate the prison population could rise to 9,450 next year – 1,645 over design capacity.

Stephen Kerr, the justice spokesman for the Scottish Conservatives, said: “These staggering figures show Scotland’s prison estate is crumbling on the SNP’s watch.

“Their savage cuts are putting hardworking officers and inmates at risk as these issues spiral out of control.

“Prisons are bursting at the seams due to the SNP’s failures to build the capacity Scotland needs over two decades and now the problems are coming home to roost.

“Justice Secretary Neil Gray needs to fight for the prison service to get the funding it needs in the SNP’s upcoming Budget to urgently fix these issues.”

Yi-pei Chou Turvey, the justice spokeswoman for the Scottish Liberal Democrats, said: "These numbers are horrifying, as is the lack of any coherent government plan to rescue our overcrowded prison estate.

"The SNP's failure to confront these issues is already having dangerous consequences. And the fact that the justice secretary chose not to tell parliament about the recent violence at HMP Polmont suggests the government is still treating this crisis primarily as a political risk."

In September, Scotland’s worst prison riot in a decade took place at the young offenders’ institution based at HMP Polmont.

Around 30 inmates were involved and prison officers from four other jails were called in to restore order, with Ms Pollock stating the riot was “not unexpected”, adding it was an example of “bubbling tension” caused by increasing overcrowding within the estate.

Justice Secretary Neil Gray said the disorder at Polmont was “unacceptable” and “unusual in scale”, but said: “The Scottish Prison Service (SPS) is equipped to deal with such incidents, and, as they said at the time, it was resolved quickly and safely.”

He said the Scottish Government was considering reforms to “achieve a sustainable prison population”.

The SPS said it has systems in place to monitor statutory maintenance requirements across the estate, with compliance overseen through facilities management arrangements and regular reporting.

However, the SPS said it does not hold an overall estimate for the value of deferred maintenance across the estate.

It said calculating such a figure would require officials to review individual maintenance records and create a new assessment.

A Scottish Prison Service spokesperson said: “Our staff work hard to ensure that our establishments are well maintained and provide safe, secure and decent environments for those who live and work within them.

“These figures represent all outstanding maintenance issues recorded across the prison estate, ranging from minor routine repairs to planned preventative tasks, at a single point in time.  
“With several of our establishments now more than 100 years old, we continue to invest in their upkeep and improvement, while also delivering major capital projects that will provide modern, safe and fit-for-purpose prison accommodation for the future.”

The Scottish Government has been asked for comment.
