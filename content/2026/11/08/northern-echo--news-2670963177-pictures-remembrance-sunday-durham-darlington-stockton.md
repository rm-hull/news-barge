Thousands of people gathered in Remembrance events across the North East to honour members of the Armed Forces who lost their lives in conflict.

Communities fell silent at churches, war memorials and cenotaphs as residents, veterans, serving personnel and civic leaders paid their respects.

Services and parades were held in towns and villages across the region.

One of the biggest events filled Durham Cathedral, where the public joined Armed Forces representatives for a remembrance service.

After the service and wreath-laying ceremony, the annual parade travelled through Durham city centre along Owengate, Saddler Street and Market Place.

Detachments from The Rifles, the Royal Lancers, the Light Dragoons and 607 (County of Durham) Squadron, Royal Air Force, took part.

County Durham Lord-Lieutenant Brigadier Michael Butterwick CBE DL led the salute in Market Place, while Esh & Bearpark Brass Band was one of three groups providing music.

In Stockton, the public attended a service at Stockton Parish Church before gathering at the town’s cenotaph.

The Mayor of Stockton, other dignitaries, veterans and residents laid wreaths and observed the two-minute silence.

Hundreds lined the High Street for a parade before the outdoor service.

Large crowds gathered at Darlington’s Cenotaph in the grounds of Memorial Hospital.

Residents laid wreaths and observed the national two-minute silence in tribute to those killed in conflict.

In Sunderland, veterans, serving personnel, community groups and residents attended the annual Remembrance Sunday parade and service at the Burdon Road War Memorial.

Personnel from 4th Regiment Royal Artillery and 3rd Battalion The Rifles took part; both hold the Freedom of the City. The Eleventh Hour Band played as the parade headed to the memorial.

Captain Joe Giles of 3 Rifles read the First World War poem For the Fallen before two 4th Regiment Royal Artillery field guns began the two-minute silence.

Wreaths were then laid at the War Memorial and Memorial Wall, which honours personnel killed since the end of the Second World War.

King Charles III led the national Remembrance Sunday ceremony at the Cenotaph in Whitehall, London. Members of the Royal Family, politicians and veterans joined him to honour those killed in conflicts since the First World War.

The King laid the first wreath, followed by the Prince of Wales, after the Last Post and a gun marked the two-minute silence.

Mayor Andy Burnham paid tribute to service personnel and veterans, praising the "selflessness and courage" of those who had devoted their lives to protecting others.
