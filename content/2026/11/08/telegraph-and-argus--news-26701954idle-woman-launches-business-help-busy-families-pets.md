A Bradford woman who spent almost three decades working in corporate learning and development has launched a new business aimed at helping busy families, pet owners and older residents with everyday tasks.

Eleanor Carr, 52, from Idle, decided to start her own venture after being made redundant and realising she no longer wanted to return to office-based corporate work.

After nearly 30 years of corporate life, she said the decision marked a significant change in direction.

Ms Carr told the T&A: "I just thought, I don't want to go into another corporate job…I think I've done my time in learning and development. I had a great career and thoroughly enjoyed, but I'm a people kind of person."

Her new business offers pet feeding, dog walking, gardening, decorating, household chores, ironing and other practical help for people struggling to fit everything into busy schedules.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21674380.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Eleanor Carr at Diddly Squat Farm. *(Image: Eleanor Carr)*

She said: "It was more about what do people need? When they're away on holiday, perhaps, or they've got to go into the office for the day, something a little bit last minute.

"Or it's some sort of housework or a pile of ironing that needs doing and you've got three kids and no time on your hands whatsoever."”

## **Inspired by volunteering during Covid**

The idea was heavily influenced by Ms Carr's experiences volunteering during the Covid-19 pandemic.

While on furlough, she joined a volunteer network organised through Holy Trinity Church in Idle, helping vulnerable residents. During its peak, the initiative involved more than 100 volunteers.

"I really enjoyed it," Ms Carr admitted. "I got a lot out of helping people, doing people's shopping and trying to get their order correct. That kind of thing just really appealed."

## **'I worry about community feeling being lost'**

Having lived in the area for 26 years, Ms Carr said her love of the local community was another key motivation behind the business.

She said: "I'm a firm believer that if we all showed a little bit of kindness and compassion to the people around us, whether they be your friends or family, or a stranger on the street, we can make a big difference."

Ms Carr recalled helping an elderly friend navigate technology and online forms, saying many people still benefit from having someone they trust to lend a hand.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21674381.jpg?type=mds-article-620)After nearly 30 years of corporate life, Eleanor Carr has launched a new business supporting people with everyday tasks. *(Image: Eleanor Carr)*

She added: "I do really worry about this community feeling being lost. I'd really love a community, and I think we've got a lot of it in Idle and Thackley particularly."

Fully insured and DBS-checked, Ms Carr hopes to build trusted relationships with customers and become a go-to helping hand for residents.

She said: "I just think it's nice to be nice, and I will want to build up that relationship with customers to the point where they just think: 'I'll ask Eleanor.'"
