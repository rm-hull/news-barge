A beloved restaurant has made its long-awaited return to Durham city centre.

Emilio's, a Durham favourite for more than 30 years, reopened this week at 96 Elvet Bridge, replacing Notch Americano and giving owners Emilio Arceri and Dan Stafford a fresh start.

The pair announced earlier this year that Notch Americano would close, citing mounting pressures in the hospitality industry, but chose to revive the restaurant that holds decades of history and affection in Durham.

Just days after opening its doors once again, the response from customers has exceeded expectations.

Emilio said: "We've had quite a few people come in for breakfast and everyone has been loving the new breakfast menu.

"The French toast in particular has gone down really well."

The warm reception has extended into evening service, with tables filling quickly and bookings continuing to roll in.

He added: "Opening night was a real success.

"We were really busy and we're super happy with how it went.

"We've had a lot of bookings come in since then for the weekend as well."

While the strong demand is welcome, it is the emotional connection from returning customers that has stood out most.

Almost immediately, diners who remembered the original Emilio's began returning, eager to relive fond memories and share stories from years gone by.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21660441.jpg?type=mds-article-620) *(Image: Emilio's)*

"We've had people walking in telling us they used to come here years ago," Emilio said.

"We had one gentleman come in and say he used to visit with his wife 30 years ago.

"It's bringing back loads of fond memories."

For Emilio, the reopening has been a deeply personal experience.

"It means so much to me," he said.

"I used to come here long before I even worked here.

"To see Emilio's back again is very heart-warming and very nostalgic.

"I just hope I can live up to my family's name."

The return of Emilio's marks the end of one era and the start of another for Emilio and Dan, who built a loyal following under the Notch Americano brand before deciding to restore a piece of Durham's culinary heritage.

Their decision appears to have struck a chord not only with long-time residents who remember the restaurant from its heyday, but also with customers discovering it for the first time.

With tables filling up and old stories being retold, the owners are hoping Emilio's can once again become part of the fabric of city life, creating new memories while celebrating the legacy that made it a Durham favourite for more than three decades.
