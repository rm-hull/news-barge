Hundreds gathered in Bradford city centre today to honour servicemen and women who lost their lives in conflict.

Veterans, cadets, dignitaries and residents attended the Remembrance Sunday service at Bradford Cenotaph.

The service, led by Dr Andy Jolley, included a two-minute silence at 11am as crowds paid tribute to the fallen.

Led by Revd Canon Jonathan Triffitt, the ceremony began with "The Gathering" before the Last Post sounded across the city centre and attendees stood in reflection around the cenotaph.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21685516.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Kiel Gopez, of the Royal Yorkshire Regiment, with his parents ahead of the ceremony. *(Image: T&A)*

## Wreaths laid at cenotaph

Wreaths were laid by civic leaders, veterans' organisations, serving military personnel and community groups.

Among those taking part was Kiel Gopez, of The Royal Yorkshire Regiment, who had the honour of laying a wreath.

Mr Gopez said: "It's good that we remember all those who fell before in the war. I think it's really important that we always should remember them, not just once a year."

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21685519.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Shreya Patel (right), the Lord-Lieutenant's Cadet for West Yorkshire, pictured with fellow cadets ahead of the Remembrance Sunday service in Bradford city centre. *(Image: T&A)*

## Keeping remembrance alive

Among those attending was Army veteran Darren Brookes, who served in Northern Ireland, Bosnia and a number of overseas deployments during his long military career.

Mr Brookes said: "It's about making sure people don't forget the sacrifices that were made."

Reflecting on Remembrance Sunday, he said: "We need to still say what we did years ago so everyone in the world can be at peace."

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21685520.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Natalie Coe (second right) and her fellow Army Cadets, ahead of Remembrance Sunday. *(Image: T&A)*

## Veterans and cadets take part

Shreya Patel, the Lord-Lieutenant's Cadet for West Yorkshire, attended alongside the Lord-Lieutenant as the King's representative.

She said: "It's a really great honour and it's a really great opportunity to be able to represent the Lord-Lieutenant and the organisation. I'm really looking forward to it."

Army Cadet Natalie Coe, who was taking part in her fourth Remembrance Sunday parade, said: "The reason we are here is to remember those who lost their lives in war.

She added: "If it wasn't for them, we wouldn't be stood here right now, so I think it's important to remember those who sacrificed themselves for the sake of the country."

## Community comes together

The service concluded with a march past through City Park, with the salute taken by the Lord-Lieutenant of West Yorkshire, the Lord Mayor of Bradford and a Royal British Legion representative.

After a morning dedicated to honouring those who served and sacrificed, the message at the heart of the service remained clear: "We will remember them."

A further service will be held at Bradford Cenotaph on November 11.
