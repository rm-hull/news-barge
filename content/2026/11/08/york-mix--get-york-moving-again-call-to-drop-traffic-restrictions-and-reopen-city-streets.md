A call has gone out to end traffic restrictions and reopen roads to ‘get York moving again’.

City of York Council’s Conservative Group is calling for the “reopening of The Groves”.

They say: “All residents have seen how much worse the road closures have made traffic in the area, which has been especially concerning for people using the hospital.

“Whilst Conservatives believe we must encourage public and sustainable transport this cannot simply be done by bashing the motorist as many of York’s road changes have been.”

Cllr Martin Rowley is delivering a motion to Full Council on Tuesday (10 November).

**Cllr Rowley said:** “This is a common-sense motion that deserves cross party support. It’s well thought out, deliverable, safe, and good for the environment.

“I call on other parties to lay aside ‘political point scoring’ and take action to get York moving again.”

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/penleys-grove-street-york-traffic-restrictions-gsv.webp)

The Groves became a low traffic neighbourhood via an experimental order in 2020. This stopped it being used as a through-route.

The order was made permanent in 2022, after councillors heard that traffic had reduced in the area, air quality had improved, cycling had increased, and more community activities have taken place.

The motion says: “Council believes the current prolonged gridlocks at certain times of the day on Wigginton Road, Monkgate, and Lord Mayor’s Walk are creating unnecessary delays and unacceptably levels of exhaust emissions from non-electric cars that are waiting.

“The last administration closed minor roads in the Groves area which mean vehicles both local and visitors must use other roads.

“The widening of the footpath and creation of much needed cycle paths on Wigginton Road have increased gridlock, particularly with traffic turning into the main entrance for York Hospital.”

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/york-council-martin-rowley.webp)

It commits the council to:

1. Reopen Vyner Street to one way traffic from Wigginton Road to Haxby Road with a 20mph speed limit. “This would cut queues into York from the traffic lights at the junction of Crighton Avenue.”

2. Reopen Fountayne Street to one way traffic from Haxby Road to Wigginton Road with a 20mph speed limit. “This would alleviate traffic queues heading out of the city via Wigginton Road.”

3. Reopen Penley’s Grove Street and Lowther Street to one way traffic with a 20mph speed limit. “This would result in reduced traffic on Monkgate and Lord Mayor’s Walk and alleviate the gridlock.”

“These measures would improve traffic flow (including for emergency vehicles) and reduce in harmful exhaust omissions caused by idling cars,” the motion says.

“There would likely be a reduction in anti-social behaviour in the Groves area, and finally, a boost to local traders and businesses who have seen footfall drop since the closures have been in place.”

## ‘Undoing hard-earned gains’

**Guildhall Ward councillor, Labour’s Dave Merrett said:** “The Conservatives’ proposal will be discussed by the Labour Group before it’s debated at Full Council next week.

“However, these proposals risk undoing hard-earned gains in air quality for Guildhall ward residents and would also not achieve their intended outcome.

“Streets like Vyner Street are one way and too narrow for handling large volumes of traffic, without removing half of the resident parking.

“While this is a motion cloaked in the wording of environmental responsibility, it is in fact the opposite and would be damaging both to the environment and to the public’s health in these communities, which have as much right to breath clean air as those who live in suburban and rural parts of the city.

“The real solution to local congestion is providing reliable local bus service alternatives for staff and visitors to the hospital and the city centre, something the Mayor is looking to address through his Bus Service Improvement and Local Transport Plans”.
