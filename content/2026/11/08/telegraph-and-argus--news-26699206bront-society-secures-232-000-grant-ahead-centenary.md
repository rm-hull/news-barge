THE Brontë Society has been awarded £232,058 to help preserve its internationally significant literary collection.

The grant, from The National Lottery Heritage Fund, will support the creation of new, environmentally controlled storage for the society’s more than 8,000-object collection and fund a site-wide masterplan for the Brontë Parsonage Museum and nearby buildings in Haworth.

Rebecca Yorke, director of the Brontë Society and Brontë Parsonage Museum, said: "As we approach the Brontë Parsonage Museum's centenary in 2028, we have a responsibility not only to celebrate everything that has been achieved over the past one hundred years, but also to ensure this remarkable collection is protected for the next century and beyond.

"'The Road to Resilience' is about laying those foundations.

"Through developing plans for improved, bespoke storage and preserving the specialist knowledge of our team through dedicated time spent capturing decades of expertise from the curatorial team, we can ensure the Brontës' extraordinary legacy continues to inspire staff, researchers, visitors and future generations."

The project, titled The Road to Resilience, will also explore the conservation and reuse of nearby historic buildings and document specialist curatorial knowledge for long-term use.

A part-time project curator has already been appointed to oversee the careful relocation of the collection, update curatorial records, and help capture institutional knowledge.

Helen Featherstone, director, England, North at The National Lottery Heritage Fund, said: "The Brontës hold a special place in the literary heritage of England, so we are delighted to support the Brontë Society with a grant to work towards a strong and resilient future for the organisation and the Brontë Parsonage Museum.

"It is fantastic news that thanks to National Lottery players, the Society can now develop plans to shape its future and continue to inspire the next generation as it approaches its centenary."

During the process of transferring, logging, and digitising the collection, there will be opportunities for volunteers to engage with the material, which includes personal items belonging to Charlotte, Emily, and Anne Brontë.

The collection features early writings, poetry manuscripts, diary papers, correspondence, clothing, jewellery, locks of hair, and household possessions.

The Brontë Society has preserved the legacy of the Brontë family for more than 130 years.

The Road to Resilience project is set to be delivered between autumn 2026 and 2027, ahead of the museum’s 100th anniversary in 2028.
