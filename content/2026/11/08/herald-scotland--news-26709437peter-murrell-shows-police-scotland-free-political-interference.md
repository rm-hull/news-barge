The conviction of former SNP chief executive Peter Murrell shows that Police Scotland is free from political interference, according to Chief Constable Jo Farrell.

Murrell, the estranged husband of former first minister Nicola Sturgeon, was sentenced to more than five years in prison in June after he admitted embezzling £400,310.65 from the SNP over a period of 12 years.

Ms Sturgeon – and former party treasurer Colin Beattie – were arrested in connection with Operation Branchform, the long-running police probe into SNP finances. However, no further action was taken against the pair.

Now, the Chief Constable has said the prosecution of Murrell demonstrated that nobody was beyond the reach of the law, regardless of their political connections.

“Regardless of who you are, where there’s wrongdoing and criminality, we will investigate and work with the Crown in order to bring you to justice,” the Chief Constable told The Times.

Ms Farrell suggested that Police Scotland’s governance arrangements, under which the Chief Constable is held to account by the Scottish Police Authority, meant there was less potential for political interference than in England, where she has spent most of her career.

She added: “I think the conviction of Murrell… puts paid to any thought that this organisation can’t and doesn’t operate independently.”

“I think the governance arrangements in Scotland are the best way to ensure that there is some clear blue water between the Scottish Government and policing, and that my operational independence is maintained.”

The Chief Constable added: “I guard operational independence and ensure to the best of my - and our - ability that we police by consent, without fear or favour and with a human rights approach, and we insulate ourselves from the politics.”

Murrell resigned as chief executive of the SNP in March 2023 following a row over the SNP’s disclosure of its membership figures during the contest to succeed Ms Sturgeon as party leader.

In April that year, he was arrested for the first time as part of Operation Branchform, with a large blue and white forensic tent erected outside of the couple’s front garden.

Mr Beattie was also arrested in April, and Ms Sturgeon in June 2023.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/20961526.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Police Scotland searched the home of Nicola Sturgeon and Peter Murrell *(Image: PA)*

It is reported that Ms Sturgeon gave no-comment answers during her interview under caution at Falkirk Police Station.

She then provided both Police Scotland and the Crown Office and Procurator Fiscal Service (COPFS) with what she said was a "very detailed written document answering the questions that had been put to me".

A standard prosecution report was submitted to the Crown against Murrell on May 23, 2024. It outlined a single charge of embezzlement covering hundreds of purchases.

A separate report **covering Ms Sturgeon and Mr Beattie made no recommendation for charges**, instead sought "advice and guidance" on whether there was sufficient evidence to continue inquiries.

Police Scotland had considered there was enough basis for arrests and questioning. However, the Crown ultimately determined there was insufficient admissible evidence of criminality to justify further action.

The former first minister has since said she has been “utterly cleared” and was “not even considered for prosecution”.

Murrell was re-arrested and charged in April 2024 and confessed to the 12-year embezzlement – between August 2010 and October 2022 – in May this year.

Following his conviction, a 126-page list of the items he purchased using SNP cash was released to the media.

Items purchased included a £95,000 Jaguar car and a £125,000 luxury motorhome.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/18087211.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Chief Constable Jo Farrell. *(Image: PA)*

Ms Sturgeon said she had no knowledge of the crimes of her estranged husband and said she had been “deceived, betrayed and lied to”.

In court, Murrell’s lawyer John Scullion KC said: "His plea of guilty is life changing. It has ended his career and future career prospects.

"He has been ostracised by his friends and former colleagues."
