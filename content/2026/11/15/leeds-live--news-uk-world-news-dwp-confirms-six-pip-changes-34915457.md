# DWP confirms six PIP changes claimants don't need to report - full list

## The Department for Work and Pensions (DWP) has confirmed there are several changes

The Department for Work and Pensions (DWP) has confirmed that Personal Independence Payment recipients never need to declare certain changes in their circumstances.

DWP PIP is available to individuals aged 16 and above, but below State Pension age, who require extra support with everyday activities or mobility due to a long-term illness, disability, or mental or physical health condition.

A successful PIP or ADP claim is currently valued at between £30.30 and £194.60 weekly in extra financial assistance, equating to between £121.20 and £778.40 for each four-week payment cycle. While numerous claimants may be familiar with the list of circumstantial changes that must be disclosed to the DWP to maintain continuous payments, there are several alterations that don't require notification, reports Birmingham Live.

### Changes you do not need to report to DWP

PIP isn't a means-tested benefit and can be received regardless of whether the claimant is employed, so there's no requirement to notify the DWP if you:

* Start a new job
* Stop claiming other benefits
* Change roles at work - unless the amount of help you need has changed
* Leave a job
* Are made redundant
* Take retirement

### Changes you must report to DWP

Nevertheless, guidance on the GOV.UK website indicates you must contact the PIP enquiry line if:

* Your personal details change - for example, your name, address or doctor
* The help you need or your condition changes
* Your condition has worsened and you’re not expected to live more than six months
* You go into hospital or a care home
* You go abroad (for more than four weeks)
* You are imprisoned or held in detention
