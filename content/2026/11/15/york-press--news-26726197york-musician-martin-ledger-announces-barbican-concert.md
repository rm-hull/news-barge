A musician’s journey from the streets to the stage has taken him across Europe and now back home to York.

On September 4, 2027, Martin Ledger will take to the stage at York Barbican with his most ambitious project yet: The Music of Dire Straits – An Orchestral Celebration.

The show will be performed by Alchemy, Mr Ledger’s Dire Straits tribute band, alongside a specially assembled orchestra.

Mr Ledger has performed before 25,000 people at V Festival in Chelmsford and entertained audiences across the continent, but it was during a difficult period closer to home that he rediscovered his passion for music.

The journey to this point began during the Covid pandemic, which wiped out Mr Ledger’s live bookings and left him without another source of income.

At age 56, Mr Ledger took to busking in York’s King’s Square.

He said: "I didn’t know what to expect or really how to go about it.

"But I pulled myself together, went out there and just played my favourite songs."

Busking became a way to earn money while performing the Dire Straits songs he loved, and also led to powerful encounters.

One day, while playing Mark Knopfler’s "Going Home," a family told him they were in York to remember their late father, a Dire Straits fan.

Mr Ledger said: "To hear it played in a completely unexpected situation like that had really made their entire weekend."

Another listener, who recognised his note-for-note rendition of "Tunnel of Love," revealed he had attended the original 1983 Hammersmith Odeon concert.

Mr Ledger said these moments stayed with him as a long-discussed tribute project finally began to take shape.

He and drummer Neil Scott had spoken for years about forming a Dire Straits tribute band, with lockdown giving them the time to make it happen.

Mr Ledger added: "I thought, who would be the best people I could possibly get to play in this band?

"I’ll ask them, thinking none of them would be interested.

"Every single one jumped at the chance."

Alchemy’s debut show took place on May 13, 2022, at Bishopthorpe Sports and Social Club.

Busking helped spread the word, as Mr Ledger handed out leaflets and listeners later bought tickets.

Alchemy has since grown into a touring theatre show.

Mr Ledger’s musical roots go back to age 14, playing in bands including Harlequin, Huge, and the Bogus Brothers.

His career with the Bogus Brothers has taken him around the world, including a charity dinner in Moscow and performances for Eddie Jordan.

Closer to home, he has supported many charity events, including fundraisers for St Leonard’s Hospice, Yorkshire Air Ambulance, and Macmillan Cancer Support.

York Barbican has been a constant presence throughout his career, from early performances with Huge in the 1990s to more recent technical work behind the scenes.

Now he will take centre stage with his own show.

He said: "The chance to stand centre stage and present my own show to the people of York is a dream come true."
