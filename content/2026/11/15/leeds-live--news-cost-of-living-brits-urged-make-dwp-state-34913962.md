# Brits urged to make DWP state pension age check in new update

## The Department for Work and Pensions (DWP) has warned Brits that the State Pension age differs

The Department for Work and Pensions (DWP) has reminded Britons that State Pension age varies according to date of birth, urging people to verify when they become eligible to claim it. The State Pension is administered by The Pension Service (part of the DWP) and offers regular income to individuals above a certain age, typically if they've accumulated sufficient National Insurance contributions.

According to the GOV.UK website, you'll require 10 qualifying years on your National Insurance record to receive any new State Pension. The full new State Pension rate stands at £241.30 per week, although the sum you get may vary, based on: whether you were contracted out before 2016; how many National Insurance qualifying years you possess; and whether you contributed to the Additional State Pension before 2016.

Your State Pension sum is determined by your National Insurance record. You'll be eligible to claim the new State Pension upon reaching State Pension age if you're a man born on or after 6 April 1951; a woman born on or after 6 April 1953.

Those born earlier will get the basic State Pension, reports The Express. However, the DWP points out that State Pension age itself can vary. In a post on X, the DWP cautioned: "The State Pension age isn't the same for everyone. This Talk Money Week, check yours using your date of birth. It only takes a minute and could help you plan for your future."

A link to a State Pension age checker was provided. The State Pension age is presently rising from 66 to 67 for both men and women. Below, you'll find the dates when individuals whose birthdays fall during the transitional period will become entitled to the State Pension.

The list displays the range of birth dates impacted, along with when each group will reach state pension age.

* April 6, 1960 – May 5, 1960: 66 years and 1 month
* May 6, 1960 – June 5, 1960: 66 years and 2 months
* June 6, 1960 – July 5, 1960: 66 years and 3 months
* July 6, 1960 – August 5, 1960: 66 years and 4 months
* August 6, 1960 – September 5, 1960: 66 years and 5 months
* September 6, 1960 – October 5, 1960: 66 years and 6 months
* October 6, 1960 – November 5, 1960: 66 years and 7 months
* November 6, 1960 – December 5, 1960: 66 years and 8 months
* December 6, 1960 – January 5, 1961: 66 years and 9 months
* January 6, 1961 – February 5, 1961: 66 years and 10 months
* February 6, 1961 – March 5, 1961: 66 years and 11 months
* March 6, 1961 – April 5, 1977: 67 years.

An additional State Pension age increase from 67 to 68 is currently scheduled to occur between 2044 and 2046, although the Government has indicated this remains under review.
