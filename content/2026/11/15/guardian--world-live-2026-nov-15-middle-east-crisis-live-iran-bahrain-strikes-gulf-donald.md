The state-run Bahrain news agency is also carrying the statements of each country in the region responding to Iran’s actions targeting their Gulf neighbour this morning.

Jordan, Egypt, Saudi Arabia, and Qatar have all strongly condemned the attack and expressed solidarity with Bahrain.

Qatar’s Ministry of Foreign Affairs said in a statement on the Qatar news agency (QNA):

The continuation of these attacks represents a dangerous escalation that will complicate efforts to contain the tension and undermine political and diplomatic endeavors aimed at achieving security and stability in the region.

I’m going to wrap up coverage for now. Here’s a summary of what you need to know as Iran steps up strikes against US allies in the Gulf again.

Iran launched a six-drone attack on a US airbase in Bahrain early on Sunday, prompting fears of a return to full-blown fighting after a two-month lull in strikes

Bahrain said it had intercepted “several aerial attacks” and destroyed the drones before they reached their targets, and officials did not immediately disclose any damage or injuries

However the tiny Gulf kingdom, which hosts a major US naval command, was forced to send out public warnings and sound missile warning sirens ahead of the incoming strikes

Iran said it had targeted a radar facility at the Al-Rifaa helicopter base. The US is yet to respond, but said there had been no damage from the first attempted strike on Friday.

Iran’s Revolutionary Guards have claimed the sinking of an oil supertanker which hit a mine in the Strait of Hormuz. There’s no independent verification of this yet.

The US military had claimed recently that it had cleared all mines from the main routes through the waterway.

But the IRGC said Sunday that two ships had hit its mines while travelling through the strait, “resulting in massive explosions and fire”.

It said the super tanker had been carrying more than two million barrels of oil when it sunk in the Gulf. Iran has made several announcements in recent weeks about ships hitting mines.

Tehran has heavily restricted shipping traffic through the strait since the start of the war, demanding vessels seek permission and travel via designated pathways. It has fired on ships trying to circumvent Iranian detection.

The US has been supporting ships going through the strait. In the past 24 hours, Centcomm posted on X a picture of F-16 fighter jets and said that more than 1.7 billions of crude oil “have been shipped through the Strait of Hormuz by Gulf partners with U.S. protection.”

As I mentioned earlier, Iran’s Revolutionary Guards have said its strikes this morning were retaliation for US aggression.

Foreign Ministry spokesman Esmaeil Baghaei said at a press conference in Tehran on Sunday that Iran would continue to respond to any military aggression by the US or other states.

He was asked whether the strike on Tehran, the second this weekend, showed a change in Iranian direction as negotiations with the US have stalled.

Mr. Baghaei said that Iran was “not waiting for anyone” and was focused on its security.

The US has launched strikes on Iranian naval bases in the Strait of Hormuz in recent weeks, as well as along Iran’s southern coast. This has been seen as efforts to try and degrade Iran’s striking capability as well as offering cover for commerical ships still venturing through and out of Hormuz.

President Trump has refrained from major military escalation, describing those sporadic strikes as minor. However, American media have reported that both the US and Israel have discussed plans for a fuller resumption of strikes.

However the base was struck heavily during Iran’s retaliatory strikes at the start of the war, and it’s unclear whether it’s fully recovered.

Below is the full statement from Bahrain’s foreign ministry on the Iranian drone strikes today, which were intercepted and shot down. This has been published in Bahrain’s various state-controlled media outlets.

The Ministry of Foreign Affairs expressed the Kingdom of Bahrain’s strongest condemnation of the continued Iranian aggression against its territory, including the attack launched today, Sunday, November 15, 2026, by drones that were intercepted and destroyed by air defense systems in the Kingdom’s airspace.

This comes two days after a similar attack at dawn on Friday, November 13, 2026, by two drones that were also intercepted. The Iranian Revolutionary Guard explicitly claimed responsibility for these attacks. The Ministry praised the efficiency, vigilance, and capability of the Bahrain Defense Force in confronting today’s attack and its complete readiness to deter any aggression.
The Ministry considered these attacks a resumption of Iran’s heinous aggression against the Kingdom and countries in the region, disregarding the provisions of international law and the principles of the UN Charter.

The Kingdom views them as a blatant attack on its sovereignty and territorial integrity, a flagrant violation of Security Council Resolution 2817, which condemned Iranian attacks on the Kingdom of Bahrain and neighboring countries and demanded their immediate cessation, a targeting of innocent citizens and residents, a sabotage of de-escalation efforts, and a serious threat to the region’s security and stability.
The Ministry affirmed the Kingdom of Bahrain’s categorical rejection of the flimsy pretexts and baseless claims put forward by the Iranian Revolutionary Guard, emphasizing that no pretext justifies aggression against the sovereignty and territorial integrity of states.

It stressed that freedom of navigation and passage, by sea and air, in straits used for international navigation is a right guaranteed by the rules of international law and is not subject to the will or conditions of any state.

The Kingdom also rejects the transformation of its territory into an arena for settling scores and holds Iran fully responsible for these attacks and their consequences.
The Ministry also affirmed the Kingdom of Bahrain’s inherent right, both individually and collectively, to self-defense in accordance with Article (51) of the Charter, and to take all necessary measures to safeguard its sovereignty and the security of its citizens and residents, in close coordination with its brothers in the Gulf Cooperation Council, based on the Joint Defense Agreement, which considers an attack on any member state an attack on all, as well as with its allies and partners. The Ministry noted that the Kingdom of Bahrain, as a member of the UN Security Council, calls upon the Council to condemn these repeated attacks and demands that it assume its responsibilities and take decisive measures to ensure Iran’s full compliance with Resolution 2817, and to halt its attacks, threats, and support for proxy groups in the region. The Ministry of Foreign Affairs added that the Kingdom of Bahrain affirms that its security is a red line, that its sovereignty is non-negotiable, and that it will remain, as it has always been, a proponent of good neighborliness, respect for the sovereignty of states, and non-interference in their affairs, and is committed to the security and stability of the Arabian Gulf.

The US is maintaining a highly expensive military operation in the Gulf, with a naval blockade that aims to stop Iranian oil ships leaving the Persian Gulf, thereby hurting the Iranian economy, and through a missile and drone operation targeting Iran’s southern coastline aimed at protecting and helping commercial ships get out. Oil flows are said to be back near their prewar level.

But Iran hasn’t strayed from its demands, and the conditions it wants to end the war: a measure of control over Hormuz, removal of the blockade and economic sanctions, having its assets unfrozen, and reparations.

Yemen government forces dropping back from Bab el-Mandeb to shore up Aden capital

I mentioned earlier the withdrawal of Saudi-backed Yemeni government forces from the areas around Bab el-Mandeb where they’ve been attempting to stave off the Houthis.

A lightning-strike offensive back in September saw the Houthis sweep almost the entire Red Sea coast and almost seize control of the critical Red Sea strait, which has become another chokepoint in the flow of oil and and energy supplies from Gulf producers.

Here’s the full statement from the Yemeni forces, who are now redeploying towards Aden, where their government is based.

The Houthis are gathering steam in the south, where most areas remain under government control.

The Souther Yemeni forces said:

The Southern Armed Forces announce the implementation of a redeployment ‌operation for its forces from Dhubab Airport and the Bab el-Mandeb area, west of Taiz Governorate.

This step is taken based on the priority of securing the capital, Aden, and the neighbouring governorates, as well as strengthening the front lines and addressing existing threats.

The Houthis’ stronghold currently remains most of northern Yemen, where they control the capital Sanaa and other populous areas. Since the civil war erupted in 2014, the Houthis have pushed the internationally-recognised Yemeni government south and stunned the Saudi-backed administration with their push south in September.

That prompted a military fight back operation, and Saudi Arabia striking Houthi areas in Yemen as the Houthis targeted airports and oil supplies in the Gulf kingdom.

Israel kills Hamas commander in strike on Gaza refugee camp

Hamas has confirmed the killing of Samir Mahmoud Hassan Abu Younis, commander of the group’s Rafah Brigade, in an Israeli airstrike on a Gaza refugee camp on Saturday night.

The Israeli military said it had taken steps to “mitigate harm to civilians” in the strike. It said it had also targeted other Hamas militants in the strike but gave no further detail.

Israel’s military first announced the killing on Sunday, saying they had targeted Younis who they said had been working on restoring Hamas and getting members to “advance and carry out terror attacks”.

Younis had been one of the senior Hamas figures who helped plan and oversee the October 7 attacks. He had only been the Rafah brigade leader role for two months, after Israel killed his predecessor, Nael Abu Obeid.

In their statement announcing his death, Hamas said Younis was “one of the most important commanders involved in planning and overseeing the 7 October attack”.

Earlier this week, an Israeli strike on Nasser Hospital, the largest operational hospital in southern Gaza, killed at least six people, including a surgeon and patients, and injured 30, local officials said. The IDF said it had been targeting a commander of Hamas’s armed wing.

Israel is still striking Gaza on a near daily basis despite the the US-brokered ceasefire having come into action since October last year. More than 1,620 people have been killed in Gaza since the ceasefire, and more than 74,650 people have been killed in Gaza since the war began.
