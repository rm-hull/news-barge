A well-known property developer is aiming to bring life back to long-empty rooms above shops in York’s city centre.

Helmsley Group, which is behind the major Coney St-Riverside project, has submitted plans to convert the upper floors of 77-79 Goodramgate into two duplex apartments.

77 Goodramgate, opposite the Old White Swan, is Grade II-listed and dates back to the mid-18th century.

It is currently owned by Trinity Syndicate, part of Helmsley Group.

Max Reeves, development director at Helmsley Group, said: "These floors have been empty for years, and buildings like this deteriorate when nobody lives in them.

"Putting people back into the upper floors is the most reliable way to look after them.

"These upper floors are full of character, with the Minster and the Shambles a short walk away.

"That combination is hard to replicate in new build, and it makes for fantastic apartments.

"Listed conversions are slow, and the heritage constraints shape almost every decision so that is where we start when considering how to approach these schemes.

"Goodramgate is one of several upper floor schemes we have in planning across the centre of York.

"There is a good deal of empty space above the city's shops, and we see a real opportunity to bring it back into use as homes."

The upper floors have been vacant for some time and are in varying states of repair.

Despite this, much of the original structure remains intact, including the central timber staircase, lath and plaster ceilings, original architraves and skirtings, and a shaped Dutch-style brick gable to the rear.

The proposed apartments would each span two floors and include an open-plan living area, a kitchenette and en-suite bathrooms.

Helmsley Group has submitted applications for both planning permission and listed building consent to City of York Council.

The project forms part of a wider portfolio of upper floor residential conversions currently in planning across York city centre.
