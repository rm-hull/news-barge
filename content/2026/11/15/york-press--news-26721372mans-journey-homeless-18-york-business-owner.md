Andy Beech was 18 when he found himself homeless. He had a breakdown in his family relationships and faced the frightening prospect of having nowhere to sleep.

But fortunately for Andy, a kind-hearted volunteer, called Sandra, offered him a room at her home in Scarborough.

“She was so warm when she opened the door and there was a lovely made-up bed,” he recalled. “She was like, ‘I’ve got a pizza in the freezer.’ And I was like, ‘Yeah. That is probably the best thing you could offer.’”

Andy was in Scarborough with Sandra three hours after he contacted the charity SASH (Safe and Sound Homes), which pairs youths at risk of being homeless with their trained hosts who offer emergency ‘Nightstop’ accommodation.

The quick response brought relief from the immediate crisis and gave Andy the chance to think about what came next.

With somewhere safe to stay and support from SASH, he began applying for jobs and attending interviews, gradually rebuilding his confidence and planning for his future.

Andy, now 33, has been a chef for the past decade and is the proud owner of vegan restaurants Junk Soul York at Spark and Junk Soul Beverley.

He said Junk Soul is “not just a place to come and buy a beer or to come and buy a burger. It’s a place to come and stay as long as you want for a long space.

"I don’t want to just make money – I want to build something that’s community based and that’s important.”

He credits SASH for his success and continues to support the charity.

“SASH gave me a safe place to stay that first night, but also the stability and support I needed to start moving forward,” he said. “I’ll always be grateful to SASH for being there when I needed them most.”

## 'The ladder was broken, but SASH helped fix it'

It was a similar story for Jade. At 17, Jade became homeless after a breakdown in family relationships meant they no longer felt safe living at home.

SASH provided Jade with emergency Nightstop accommodation, followed by longer-term supported lodgings with a SASH host.

With a more stable home environment, Jade was able to continue their education and look beyond the immediate challenges they had faced.

“Previously the ladder was broken,” Jade said, “but SASH helped fix it.”

Jade went on to university and achieved a master’s degree, became an advocate for others and eventually joined SASH as a trustee.

Today, Jade helps shape the charity’s future and ensures young people’s voices are heard.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21705386.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Jade who was made homeless at 17 and is now a SASH trustee *(Image: Supplied)*

Meanwhile for Ollie, homelessness came nine days before his 16th birthday. It followed Ollie losing his dad at 13 and his relationship with his mum breaking down.

The teenager left home one night before sitting his GCSE exams.

Ollie was sofa surfing for a while, never knowing where he would sleep from one night to the next.

Eventually, he moved into a local hostel, but the experience had a serious impact on his mental health, and he struggled through some incredibly difficult times. Then SASH stepped in.

Through SASH’s supported lodgings service, Ollie moved in with his host, Helen, where he found safety, stability and someone who believed in him.

“When I came into supported lodgings,” Ollie recalled, “I felt warm, like a family was around me, because you know they’re there to support you.”

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21705387.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Ollie who was made homeless days before his 16th birthday *(Image: Supplied)*

Ollie worked with SASH over 18 months to develop the skills, confidence and support networks needed to move towards independence.

He received practical and emotional support that helped him rebuild his future, develop his independence and improve his relationship with his mum.

Today, Ollie has a full-time career, has rebuilt his family relationships and recently married his long-term partner.

“The person I was then and who I am now are two completely different people,” Ollie said. “That time was the worst period of my life so far. Now I’m happy, I feel positive.”

He continues to support SASH as an ambassador, taking part in events, sharing his story and helping raise awareness of youth homelessness.
