They’re already the favorites to reach the WTC final.

With both Tests being played in the Highveld, conditions are expected to favor fast bowling. South Africa have plenty of firepower, with Kagiso Rabada, Marco Jansen, Anrich Nortje returning from injury, and all-rounder Wiaan Mulder in the squad. Tony de Zorzi is also back after recovering from a hamstring injury, while David Bedingham and Kyle Verreynne have been left out.

But the Proteas won't have it easy. Bangladesh shook Australia at Darwin earlier this year and have a formidable pace attack of their own, with Hasan Mahmud, Taskin Ahmed, Shoriful Islam, and Ebadot Hossain. However, they will be without Nahid Rana, who continues his recovery from a side strain.

Their batting also has plenty of potential, with Towhid Hridoy arriving in outstanding form after scoring an unbeaten 350 in South Africa in an A-team game and following it up with an unbeaten 242 against the West Indies, Bangladesh's highest individual Test score. Captain Najmul Hossain Shanto, Mominul Haque, Mushfiqur Rahim, and Litton Das are no strangers to tough batting conditions either.

Can the Tigers upset the defending WTC champions and Temba Bavuma, who has lost just one of his 15 Tests as captain?
