# Life in Leeds neighbourhood plagued with leaves as residents call for major clear-up

## Locals have spoken of the 'thick' build up of leaves

Leeds residents have said their neighbourhood is plagued with fallen leaves and have called for a major clear-up.

Leeds City Council is launching a scheme to improve all 33 wards in the city called ‘Love Leeds’. It aims to bring each community together to target issues facing each ward. The scheme aims to harness “community and volunteer action”.

Residents in Adel have said the biggest issue the area is facing, during the autumnal season, is the “thick” build up of leaves. They’ve said it poses a danger to older and more vulnerable residents, and have called for the leaves to be cleared.

Sandra Clement and Sally Norfolk have lived in the area for around 50 years. In terms of what can be helped in the Love Leeds scheme, they think there’s too much traffic parked on the side streets, there needs to be more playparks and the leaves need to be swept up to protect the vulnerable.

Sandra said: “I’ve just walked along Adel Lane and it’s dangerous for the older people. They don’t seem to be a priority.”

Sally added: “A lot of people are going up towards Holt Park where the school and the library is. The footpath is always thick with leaves. There’s lots of little ginnels and things that don’t get cleared. I know they can’t do everything but there’s a lot of older people around.”

Margaret, who moved to Adel at the turn of the millennia 26 years ago, said: “If they could get around and do the leaf clearing, it would look quite nice and be tidier.”

The great-gran-of-two added: “It’s something that offends me, I like to sweep the pavement in front of my house but the other people don’t and leaves blow along.”

Anne Gascoyne, 72, thinks the area needs a “nice pub” to bring the community together. She says there’s a range of nice bars in the area but doesn’t think they have the same effect on the community. Anne also mentioned there was a lot of congested traffic near Adel Primary School close to where she lives with people “reversing into her drive…and one even bumped the gate once.”

Anne said the pavements on St Helen’s Lane are “really thick with leaves but the kiddies love it.” She has noticed people struggling however, Helen said: “Pushchairs. I saw a lady today. A little toddler wanted to walk and kick through the leaves but she was struggling to push the pushchair getting clogged up through the leaves.”

She added it wasn’t a “big bug bear” of hers but felt sympathy for the vulnerable who have to struggle through.

Leeds City Council said leaf fall is a “significant issue” across the city and it “recognises that it presents challenges for safety and accessibility”. They have an annual Leaf Fall Programme across the city including road sweeping, path sweeping and leaf-blowing. The local authority added it prioritises areas around NHS facilities and schools to help keep pedestrians and cyclists safe, using street-cleaning crews, gully tankers, leaf blowers and mechanical sweepers.

A Leeds City Council spokesperson said: "The programme began at the end of October and runs over a 12-week period. Across all areas of the city, including Adel, we target the roads and footpaths most affected by leaf fall. The routes have been developed over a number of years using local knowledge, operational experience, and feedback from ward councillors and communities.

"The specific dates and times of programme delivery need to be flexible in order to adapt to changing weather conditions and areas of greatest need across the autumn, but regular updates are provided to ward councillors to keep communities up to date on progress."

"We encourage any residents who have concerns about leaf build-up in a particular location to report it to the Council. We use this feedback, alongside reviews from our local management teams, to adapt our schedule and add additional work where it is needed."
