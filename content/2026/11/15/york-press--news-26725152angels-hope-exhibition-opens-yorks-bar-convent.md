## Young York artists to showcase Angels of Hope exhibition at Bar Convent

Ben Sehnal with an Angel artwork

Young artists and students from across York will take centre stage this Christmas as a new exhibition celebrating hope, creativity and community opens at the city's historic Bar Convent.

The Angels of Hope project, organised by York charity New Visuality, will transform the Bar Convent's Chapel and Gregory Gallery into vibrant spaces filled with artwork, animation and stories created by young people.

The exhibition connects York with Viborg in Denmark through the two cities' shared status as UNESCO Cities of Media Arts, while giving young people the opportunity to explore the people, places and experiences that inspire hope in their own lives.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21711543.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Ben Sehnal with an Angel artwork

At the heart of the exhibition is a five-minute film, Angels of Hope in York, which combines illustrations created by York College English for Speakers of Other Languages (ESOL) students with animation produced by New Visuality's team of young artists.

### At a glance 👀

🎨 **What is it?** *Angels of Hope* is a new exhibition featuring artwork and animation created by York's young people.

📍 **Where?** The exhibition will be held in the historic**Bar Convent's Chapel and Gregory Gallery** in York.

🌍 **Why is it special?** It links York and**Viborg, Denmark**, through their shared UNESCO City of Media Arts status.

📅 **When?** The exhibition opens to the public on**December 1** and celebrates themes of hope, creativity and community.

💡 **Must see:** A five-minute animated film combining artwork by York College ESOL students with animations by New Visuality's young artists.

The project was celebrated at York College on November 4 during an event attended by the Lord Mayor of York, Councillor Margaret Wells, York College principal Ken Merry and Viborg representative Henrik Holmskov.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21711540.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Lord Mayor with ESOL art from the Angels of Hope project. Image supplied

New Visuality director Greg McGee said the idea began with a challenge from partners in Denmark.

"Viborg threw down a challenge to UNESCO's Media Arts Cities around the world: 'Who is YOUR Angel of Hope?'" he said.

"We recognised that citywide conversations are rarely started or led by a city's most vulnerable citizens, so we decided to hand the steering wheel to York College's ESOL students.

"They have responded with style, wit and sensitivity."

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/12080232.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Greg McGee exhibition organiser

The initiative has also provided valuable opportunities for young people to gain practical experience and develop confidence through Better Connect's Trailblazer programme.

Nineteen participants have helped organise events, develop creative projects and prepare the exhibition, while learning valuable skills in communication, presentation and event management.

Among them is Niall, who joined the project after a period away from education.

He said: "Before this I was unsure just what to do. It has really opened my eyes and made me a lot more confident with what opportunities are available."

Niall and fellow participant Miles have worked on coordinating artwork and animation and hope to continue volunteering in York's cultural sector.

Henrik Holmskov praised the project, saying: "It is about young people finding a voice through creativity."

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21711542.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Celebrating Angels of Hope Exhibition. Image supplied

For the Bar Convent, the exhibition also provides a meaningful link between York's history and the hopes of a new generation.

Lisa Coombs, learning officer at the Bar Convent, said: "We are delighted to be welcoming Angels of Hope to the Bar Convent this Christmas and to continue our longstanding relationship with New Visuality and our creative partners in Viborg."

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21711544.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The exhibition will take place at The Bar Convent in Blossom Street, York

She said the theme resonates strongly with the convent's history, including the story of Archangel Michael, who is said to have protected the site from attack in 1696.

"For more than 300 years this has been a place shaped by the courage, determination and vision of women who believed in creating opportunities for others," she added.

"It feels particularly fitting that our house should now provide a space for the voices and artistic talents of York's next generation."

The Angels of Hope exhibition opens to the public on December 1 in the Bar Convent's Chapel and Gregory Gallery.
