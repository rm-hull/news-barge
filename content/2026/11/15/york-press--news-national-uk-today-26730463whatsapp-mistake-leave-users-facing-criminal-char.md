## WhatsApp users warned this group chat mistake could lead to criminal charges

New laws affecting WhatsApp users came into force on November 6. *(Image: Getty Images)*

A simple mistake in a WhatsApp group chat could leave people facing criminal charges under new laws, a legal expert has warned.

Ruth Peters from Olliers Solicitors said an image automatically saved from a group chat can lead to serious charges, even if the recipient did not ask for it.

New offences covering possession and publication of specified pornographic images came into force in England and Wales on November 6 under the Crime and Policing Act 2026.

They do not apply to every explicit image, and receiving an unsolicited image does not automatically mean someone has committed an offence.

But those found in breach of the new laws could find themselves facing criminal charges and time in jail.

## What do the new laws cover?

The new rules cover pornography that explicitly and realistically depicts strangulation or suffocation, certain incest-themed penetrative sexual activity, or sexual activity involving someone who is, or is pretending to be, under 16.

The people depicted must appear to a reasonable person to be real, according to government guidance.

Whether someone possesses an image is a question of fact for a court, but the guidance says it could include material stored on a device, in a personal account or in cloud storage.

## What is the penalty for breaching new pornographic image laws?

The maximum sentence for possession is two years for material depicting strangulation or suffocation or certain relatives, and three years for material depicting someone who is, or is pretending to be, under 16.

Publication carries a maximum sentence of five years for all three offences.

Ms Peters said Olliers Solicitors had encountered around 10 current or ongoing cases in which people faced serious consequences after images from group chats were found on their phones.

She recalled a case involving an image of bestiality found during a phone examination in an unrelated investigation that resulted in no further action.

The Disclosure and Barring Service dealt with the image offence with a caution, but was subsequently considering whether to place the person on a barred list, potentially affecting their employment, she said.

Ms Peters said: “The important thing is to understand that possession can be a continuing act.

“It is not necessarily about whether you actively went looking for something or whether you downloaded it yourself.

“People need to be aware of what is on their devices and understand that images shared in group chats can have serious consequences.”

## What if an image is saved automatically?

The Ministry of Justice says possession can be a continuing act and advises people to permanently delete or otherwise get rid of images covered by the offences.

WhatsApp users may have a defence if they were sent an image without asking for it and did not keep it for an unreasonable length of time.

There may also be a defence if they had not seen the image before and did not know or have cause to suspect that it was covered by the offences.

Ms Peters said: “People arrested for offences where the police need to examine their phones will often have their devices checked by officers.

“An image automatically saved from a group chat can lead to very serious charges.

“While receiving an image you have not asked for is not necessarily an offence, if that image stays on your device for a period of time, you could potentially face prosecution.

“Even if you didn't specifically opt to save that image, it may still remain on your device.”
