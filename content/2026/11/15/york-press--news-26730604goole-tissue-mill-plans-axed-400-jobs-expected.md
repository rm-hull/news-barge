Plans for a major tissue papermaking facility that would have created 400 jobs in East Yorkshire have been axed.

Metsä Tissue of Finland has abandoned plans to build a 240,000 tonnes/year tissue paper mill in Goole, within its Humber Freeport site.

The project, announced in September 2023, was expected to become the UK's largest tissue paper manufacturing facility.

Metsä Tissue has mills mainly in Germany, Poland, Slovakia, Finland and Sweden. Its UK growth was focussed on the private label customer brand products and the Metsa Lambi brand.

The company operates UK manufacturing and distribution sites in Boston and Kings Lynn, employing more than 200 people.

In a statement reported on Friday, Tobias Lüning, Senior Vice President (Commercial) and Managing Director of Metsä Tissue GmbH, confirmed the company's decision to withdraw from the Goole project.

Mr Lüning said: "Since announcing our plans in 2023, we have worked closely with the local community, businesses and authorities across East Riding of Yorkshire to bring the tissue mill project in Goole to life.”

"However, after extensive pre-engineering, permitting processes and financial evaluations, we have decided not to proceed with the project."

The statement follows the scrapping of the scheme being reported in company accounts for the year ended December 31 2025, which were posted on Companies House on October 6.

The strategic report said: “Metsa Tissue completed the pre-engineering and planning phase of the UK Goole Mill project in 2025.

“As a result of the work performed and following review by the Board, it was determined that the project will not be progressing to the construction phase. The associated costs were fully impaired in the 2025 financial statements and subsequently reimbursed by Mets Tissue Corporation Oyj.”

The UK mill was expected to have cost several hundred million pounds and was to occupy a 211-acre site within the Humber Freeport and be responsible for around a fifth of the UK’s existing tissue consumption.

The facility was expected to create more than 400 direct jobs, with thousands of additional indirect jobs in construction, its supply chain and knock-on effects on the local economy.

A full planning application for the development was submitted by Metsä Tissue to East Riding of Yorkshire Council in February 2025, with approval given in September this year.

Wykeland Group, the developer behind the Goole project, expressed disappointment over Metsä Tissue's withdrawal but confirmed it is already talking to possible alternative investors for the site.

The leading Hull-based development company holds the long-term development rights.

Wykeland Managing Director Dominic Gibbons said: “We’re disappointed that Metsä Tissue has decided not to proceed with this development following the substantial amount of work, over the past three years, by ourselves and other stakeholders to deliver a detailed planning consent in early September.

“However, the site now has full planning consent for 3m sq ft of manufacturing and high-bay warehousing space, and benefits from attractive Freeport financial incentives, so it offers a very appealing investment proposition.

“We had put plans in motion in anticipation of Metsä’s decision and we’re now talking to prospective alternative end users who are interested in realising the potential of this key site.”
