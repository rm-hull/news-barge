# Met Office snow warning as 23 areas facing Tuesday travel disruption - full list

## The Met Office has issued a yellow snow warning for 23 areas across England and Scotland

Millions across England and Scotland are bracing for potential travel chaos on Tuesday following a yellow snow warning issued by the Met Office covering 23 areas. The alert, taking effect from midnight on November 17 until midday, warns that up to 15cm of snow could accumulate on elevated terrain.

Drivers and commuters have been cautioned that certain roads and rail services may experience disruption, with extended journey times anticipated as the mercury plummets. Forecasters indicate snowfall will predominantly affect upland areas, though some lower-lying locations might also witness snow throughout the morning hours.

The warning encompasses sections of northern England, the Midlands and southern Scotland, with locations such as Greater Manchester, North Yorkshire and the Scottish Borders amongst those impacted. Travellers passing through these zones have been advised to anticipate potential hold-ups, especially on routes traversing higher elevations.

A yellow warning indicates weather conditions capable of disrupting daily travel, though not all residents within the designated areas will necessarily experience snowfall. The Met Office states that certain roads and railway lines are likely to face difficulties, with prolonged journey times possible for those travelling by car, bus or train.

Higher-altitude routes are expected to bear the brunt of the conditions, meaning those journeying through rural and hilly areas could encounter more challenging circumstances. Anyone planning to travel on Tuesday is urged to verify road conditions and public transport updates before departing, reports the Express.
