## Mercedes-Benz Vito 119 SELECT L2 review: a premium van that makes work feel easier

Mercedes-Benz Vito

The Mercedes-Benz Vito 119 SELECT L2 is a large van that makes a convincing case for itself as a premium working tool: quick enough to be genuinely pleasant, well equipped, and surprisingly composed on the road.

At £48,410 before options, it is not cheap, but it feels like a van built to make long days behind the wheel less of a chore.

The Vito’s smart, purposeful appearance is one of its strongest assets. In Alpine Grey, with colour-coded bumpers, a chromed radiator grille, 17-inch 20-spoke alloys and subtle chrome detailing, it looks more like a polished commercial vehicle than a bare transit-style workhorse.

The SELECT trim adds enough visual polish to make it feel at home outside a client’s premises, yet it does not stray into pretension.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21720481.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Mercedes-Benz Vito

At 5,140mm long, 2,244mm wide and 1,915mm tall, it is unmistakably a large van. But Mercedes has done a good job of disguising that bulk.

Indeed, the Vito leans into bends with a confidence that makes it feel more like a large estate car than a 3.0-tonne gross vehicle weight commercial machine.

The steering is electrically assisted and speed-sensitive, so it is light enough for town work but gains reassuring weight at higher speeds.

The 2.0-litre diesel produces 190hp and a meaty 440Nm of torque, sent through a nine-speed 9G-TRONIC automatic gearbox to the rear wheels.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21720479.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Mercedes-Benz Vito

That is a healthy amount of urge for a van of this size, and it makes overtaking, hill climbs and fully loaded departures far less stressful than in less powerful rivals. The automatic transmission is smooth and unobtrusive, shifting cleanly without hunting for gears.

Ride comfort is similarly impressive. The comfort suspension absorbs broken surfaces without becoming wallowy, and the Vito remains settled over undulating roads. It is not a luxury car, but it is a van you could happily drive for several hours without feeling battered.

At 6ft 3in, I found the cabin genuinely accommodating. There is reasonable legroom even for taller drivers, and the comfort seats provide decent support over longer journeys. The driving position is upright in the traditional van manner, but visibility is good and the controls fall easily to hand.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21720480.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Mercedes-Benz Vito

The MBUX multimedia system is a welcome upgrade over the basic screens found in many rivals. Smartphone integration, a colour instrument display and clear graphics make it feel modern rather than utilitarian. TEMPMATIC air conditioning, heated electrically adjustable mirrors and ambient lighting add a touch of refinement that you would not expect from a work van.

The 6.0 cubic metre load volume is competitive for this class, and the wood flooring is a practical touch that will appeal to tradespeople. The left-hand sliding door and rear double-wing doors that open flat against the sidewall make loading and unloading straightforward, even in tight spaces.

Mercedes has equipped this Vito generously. Active Brake Assist, Attention Assist and Blind Spot Assist come as standard, while the optional Driving Assistance package adds route-based speed adjustment and extended automatic start-up on motorways.

The parking package with 360-degree camera costs £2,025 as an option, which is steep, but for a van of this size it is arguably money well spent. Manoeuvring something this large into a tight bay is far easier with a full surround view than relying on mirrors alone.

The 39.2mpg WLTP combined figure is respectable for a van of this size and power, though real-world figures will likely sit lower, especially when laden.

The 70-litre fuel tank gives a useful range between fill-ups, and the 188g/km CO2 figure is in line with expectations for a diesel van of this capacity.

Insurance group 44E is on the higher side, which is worth factoring into overall running costs. At nearly £50,000 list price before options, this is a premium van, and buyers will need to justify the outlay against cheaper alternatives.
