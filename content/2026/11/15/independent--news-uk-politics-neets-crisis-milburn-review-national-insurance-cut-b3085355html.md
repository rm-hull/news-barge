The rate of employer National Insurance (NI) on under-25 workers should be lowered to incentivise companies to create more jobs for young people, a major review will say.

Former Labour health secretary Alan Milburn is set to unveil a review recommending radical welfare changes to tackle youth unemployment, after warning that reducing the number of young people not in employment, education or training will require a 10-year plan and billions in investment.

The report will call on the government to gradually reduce NI costs over a period of five years for employees between the ages of 21 and 24, the BBC reported.

Employer National Insurance Contributions (NICs) are a mandatory tax paid by businesses on top of their employees earnings and are not deducted from an employee’s wages.

Mr Milburn will recommend doubling the threshold at which companies begin paying NI for this age group from £5,000 to £10,000, but only “when fiscal conditions allow”. He will say that an immediate cut would not be the most cost-effective method.

Due to be published on Tuesday after being worked on for a year, it will propose that the reforms begin with retail and hospitality businesses.

The so-called Neets crisis could take a decade and billions of pounds to resolve, Mr Milburn has said, arguing that “crude cuts” would merely result in young people showing up elsewhere in the Government ledger.

The report will also propose reducing the UK’s benefits bill, with Mr Milburn pointing out that £22bn is spent each year to support many of the one million 16 to 24 year olds that are not in jobs.

The former Labour health secretary has said some young people will receive less financial support under his proposals, which include moving them off some health-related benefits and onto a new “youth allowance”.

Mr Milburn is set to say that under-25s should not be able to claim health parts of Universal Credit or personal independence payment (Pip), according to the broadcaster.

This would instead be paid at the standard rate of universal credit, while under a separate recommendation the Government would have a “legal” duty to guarantee under-25s a job, employment support or training.

![As a report on Neets from Alan Milburn, right, is awaited, No 10 said the Prime Minister ‘has already been clear that we need fundamental change in how we support young people to grow up in this country’ (PA)](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/12/15/92d9f55c12942cea3c0bab943674bd45Y29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzk0NTc3ODE4-2.85437412.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Mr Milburn is set to make clear in his report that the move by Sir Keir Starmer’s government to raise NI for companies did not cause the crisis of young people not in employment, education or training, commonly known as Neets.

The prime minister has said the politically sensitive review will be a “watershed” moment but stopped short of promising to accept its recommendations in full.

He has also insisted paying for the reforms would not mean “more money” but “spending differently” in government when pressed on the large upfront investment they are expected to require.

Mr Burnham added that the government would respond to Mr Milburn’s review in “the coming weeks”, adding: “But I can say now that our response will not be in the manner of traditional Department for Work and Pensions reform.

“It will be societal and generational. We will rethink supporting childhood – shifting government spending from paying for failure to investing in success.”

Mr Milburn added: “It’s a 10-year programme for a reason, because that’s the amount of time that we think it will take to get to a reasonable level of young people in this country being Neet.”
