New Beginnings Peer Support, a Harrogate charity for women rebuilding their lives after domestic abuse, is one of 250 groups given the 2026 King's Award for Voluntary Service.

New Beginnings Peer Support, a Harrogate charity for women and children rebuilding their lives after domestic abuse, has been given the King’s Award for Voluntary Service. It is the highest award a voluntary group in the UK can receive.

The charity is one of 250 groups on the 2026 list, announced on Saturday. It is named under North Yorkshire in the London Gazette supplement that carries the full list. Its citation reads: “A peer-led after-crisis domestic abuse service for women and their children living in North Yorkshire.”

## Overview

* **Who:** New Beginnings Peer Support, a charitable incorporated organisation
  (charity number 1198378) registered at Cornwall Road, Harrogate.
* **What:** the King’s Award for Voluntary Service 2026, which the
  government says
  is equivalent to an MBE.
* **What it does:** long-term support for women who have left an abusive
  partner, run largely by volunteers who have been through it themselves.
* **Where:** one-to-one support across the county, a community group in
  Harrogate, and peer social groups in Ripon and Skipton.

## From one weekly group to a county charity

The service began as a pilot under the former Harrogate Borough Council in February 2019, the charity’s own history says. It was a weekly support group for women and their children in Harrogate. It was set up by Lindsay Oliver and Mel Milner, a former housing support officer.

Demand grew through the pandemic and the cost of living crisis. The group became a charitable incorporated organisation on 25 March 2022, the Charity Commission register shows, and now works across North Yorkshire.

For the year to 31 March 2025 the register records:

No trustee is paid, the register says.

## What the charity does

New Beginnings is an after-crisis service. It supports women who are no longer in a relationship with their abuser and no longer live at the same address, whether they left recently or years ago, its website says. Women who need help to leave an abusive partner are pointed to other services.

Its services include:

* one-to-one support at the charity, at home or in the community, from peer mentors who have lived through abuse themselves
* peer social groups in Ripon and Skipton, and a volunteer at a community group in Harrogate
* the Own My Life course, run online and in person in Harrogate, for groups of 8 to 12 women over 12 to 14 sessions
* local group chats, and family days and activity packs

Volunteers also deliver Christmas hampers, clothing, toys and household items to help women set up new homes, its volunteering page says.

## About the award

The King’s Award for Voluntary Service goes each year to groups that have volunteered for at least three years. Nominations are assessed first by the Lord-Lieutenant of each county and then by a national committee, the government’s announcement says.

New Beginnings is the only winner on this year’s North Yorkshire list based in the Harrogate district. The others include Cleveland Mountain Rescue Team, the Ryedale Festival Trust and Active Filey.

## What it means for you

* **If you have left an abusive relationship** and live in North Yorkshire,
  you can contact the charity through
  its website or at
  [enquiries@newbeginningspeersupport.com.](mailto:enquiries@newbeginningspeersupport.com.)
* **If you need help now**, IDAS, the independent domestic abuse service for
  North Yorkshire, is on 03000 110 110 and the National Domestic Abuse
  Helpline is on 0808 2000 247. In an emergency, call 999.
* **If you want to volunteer**, the charity takes peer and community
  volunteers aged 16 and over, from one-to-one support to fundraising and
  deliveries.
* **Nominations for the 2027 awards** are open on the
  King’s Award website
  until 1 December 2026.
