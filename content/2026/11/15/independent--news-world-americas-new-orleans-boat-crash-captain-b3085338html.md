The heroic captain of a riverboat that capsized at the Port of New Orleans stayed with passengers until they were safe, despite being injured himself.

Capt. Leonard Stewart of the American Heritage called for help and kept the ship alongside the dock for as long as he could, making sure the nearly 200 passengers and other crew members survived after a 520-foot tanker crashed into the riverboat on Friday. Officials said 32 people were injured, but none were in critical condition.

“We would like to recognize Captain Leonard Stewart, whose immediate and decisive actions with the crew allowed for the rapid evacuation of the vessel,” American Cruise Lines, the boat’s operator, said in a statement.

Passengers wearing life vests were seen on video swimming to safety after the American Heritage capsized on the Mississippi River.

The Atlantic Asphalt tanker apparently lost its steering and hit the American Heritage while the riverboat was docked, according to officials.

“It was just chaos,” Devon Flanagan, who was working nearby and rushed over when the crash happened, told the Associated Press.

“You had all types of ships, like other barges and other companies down there trying to help out, which also was a little convoluted because you had people still in the water.”

The National Transportation Safety Board is investigating the crash.

!['We would like to recognize Captain Leonard Stewart, whose immediate and decisive actions with the crew allowed for the rapid evacuation of the vessel,' said American Cruise Lines, the boat’s operator](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/15/22/45/GettyImages-2304497207.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

NTSB member John DeLeeuw thanked federal, state and local authorities, as well as crew members on private vessels, for their quick response.

“It’s quite an amazing story to see the people that responded not within hours but in minutes to this accident,” DeLeeuw said. “We had a number of private vessels — nine of them, I believe — that came out and helped with the folks that were impacted by the accident.”

The NTSB has recovered the tanker’s voyage data recorder, which contains audio from the bridge of the vessel and information about the vessel’s speed.

The Port of New Orleans has also filed a lawsuit so federal officials could seize the tanker, which is owned by a subsidiary of a Chinese company, the Associated Press reported. The lawsuit alleged that the tanker caused at least $1 million in damage to the New Orleans terminal.
