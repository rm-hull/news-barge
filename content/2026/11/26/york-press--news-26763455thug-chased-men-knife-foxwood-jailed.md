A thug who chased two men with a knife in York has been jailed.

Joshua Tyssen could have killed someone when he went “running amok in the street with a knife” in Foxwood, the Recorder of York, Judge Sean Morris, said.

Prosecutor Kelly Clarke said four people, including Tyssen, were involved in the incident in Thoresby Road on December 15, 2024.

She said Tyssen, 37, of no fixed address, had pointed a knife at two of the men who were holding metal bars.

Tyssen chased after the two men as he held the knife, Ms Clarke said.

She said police arrived and searched the area leading to the arrests of Tyssen and the three other men in St Stephen's Road.

Ms Clarke said the knife, a black-handled kitchen knife, was found in bushes nearby.

She said a metal pole, an 89cm-long rusted scaffolding pole, was also recovered by police with DNA on it matching Tyssen.

Tyssen pleaded guilty to possession of an offensive weapon and to affray.

## 'Knife crime has to stop,' judge says

Judge Morris told Tyssen things could have gone “badly wrong” by his decision to carry a knife during the incident.

He jailed Tyssen for 17 months on Wednesday (November 25), saying only an immediate jail sentence would match the seriousness of the crime.

“Knife crime has to stop,” the judge told Tyssen. “Men and boys in North Yorkshire who go out armed with knives need to know they are running the risk in court of an immediate prison sentence. It is too serious to suspend.”

Tyssen, who appeared in court via video link from prison, made no reaction as he was handed the sentence.

Judge Morris said Tyssen has an “unattractive record”, noting his 39 previous convictions for 73 offences, one of which is a knife offence.

Tyssen’s barrister, Zareen Alam-Cheetham, said the previous knife offence was in 2012.

She said Tyssen was addicted to crack cocaine for the last three years.

Tyssen has also had an alcohol addiction since he was 14, Ms Alam-Cheetham said.

She told the court Tyssen has been in an “endless cycle” of homelessness and drug addiction.

"Sadly," Ms Alam-Cheetham said, “this is someone who is 37 years of age and has no support network … No one has been to visit him since he has been in prison.”

Ms Alam-Cheetham said without support, it is likely that Tyssen will reoffend after he has been released from prison.

She added that Tyssen, who has been in jail since he was remanded in custody on September 24, has put his time in custody to good use and has been clean since he has been in prison.
