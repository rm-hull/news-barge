## Israel continues to struggle to form a government after the 27 October election. Netanyahu has denied wrongdoing in all three cases since his trial began in May 2020.

Judges overseeing the long-running corruption trial of Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu have urged prosecutors to reconsider major parts of the case.

The Jerusalem District Court suggested prosecutors consider dropping the bribery allegation in one of the cases, known as Case 4000, which alleges Netanyahu used his position to provide regulatory benefits to telecoms company Bezeq in exchange for favourable coverage on the Walla news website.

It is the most serious of the three cases against Netanyahu and the only one involving a bribery charge.

In this instance, the court questioned the strength of the prosecution’s evidence, particularly testimony from former Communications Ministry director-general Shlomo Filber, citing inconsistencies and doubts over whether any quid pro quo had been proved.

In another case, known as Case 2000, concerning alleged negotiations with Yedioth Ahronoth publisher Arnon Mozes over favourable coverage, the judges questioned whether the conduct described actually amounted to a criminal offence.

The court also suggested that prosecutors seek an agreement over the remaining case, Case 1000, which concerns allegations that Netanyahu received expensive gifts, including champagne, cigars and jewellery, from wealthy businessmen.

The court stressed that its comments did not amount to an acquittal and no charges have yet been formally dismissed. Netanyahu has denied wrongdoing in all three cases. His trial began in May 2020.

Attorney General Gali Baharav-Miara said prosecutors would review the judges’ comments, while Netanyahu’s lawyer has called for criminal mediation.

## Still no clarity over last month's election result

The intervention by the judges comes at a sensitive political moment in Israel, with the country still unable to form a government after the 27 October election.

Opposition leader Gadi Eisenkot currently has the mandate to form a government, but his four-party bloc holds only 48 of the Knesset’s 120 seats, short of the 61 needed for a majority.

A weakening of the prosecution case could strengthen Netanyahu’s position in coalition negotiations and reduce one of the biggest legal uncertainties hanging over his political future.
