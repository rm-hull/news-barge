Scott Peden has been campaigning for tighter regulations since the fire

**When Scott Peden thinks of the night his family died in a fire caused by an e-bike battery, he has one overriding regret: "That I lived, and they didn't."**

His girlfriend Gemma Germeney, 31, and their children Lilly, eight, and Oliver, four, died after the battery exploded in their Cambridge home on 30 June 2023.

An inquest recorded conclusions of death by misadventure.

Peden, 33, says: "If I'd known about how dangerous electric bikes were, I would have been content riding a [pedal] bike.

"And on the night the only regret is that I lived and they didn't. The only regret is buying the damn electric bike and being the only one that lived."

Describing his partner, he says she "never had a bad word to say about anyone", adding: "She was artistic, she loved to draw, she loved to colour, she had such ambition with creativity."

Lilly, he says, was "the cheekiest little girl you've ever met" while "Oliver loved his motorbikes, he loved his cars and, most of all, he loved his sister.

"They had such an amazing relationship. It's unlike any siblings I've ever seen. Most siblings argue and fight, but these two just didn't."

Peden's partner Gemma Germeney, and their children Oliver, four, and Lilly, eight, were killed in the fire

Peden bought the e-bike in order to travel to and from his job in retail.

It had been plugged in under the stairs to recharge.

He says the night of the fire at their Sackville Close home was "just a normal night", but about 45 minutes before his early-morning alarm for work was due to go off, the family were woken by a bang.

"It shook the house and scared everyone in it," he says.

He went out of the bedroom to see what was happening and was confronted by flames.

"The only way I could describe it is an old World War Two scene where they've got flame-throwers. It was just shooting up the stairs with such ferocity," he says.

It appeared the walls were "dripping fire" and he ran to the window to call for help.

"I looked back and Gemma was panicking trying to get the kids together and I told her I'll be right back," he said.

"I didn't realise how bad it was."

He jumped out of the window, went through the back door and saw the house engulfed in flames.

When he got to the bike, he says he "realised there's no way I can put this out".

He was unable to get it out of the building and even tried to kick the charger out of the wall.

But realising "there's nothing I can do", he called to Germeney to jump.

"I never heard from them again," he says.

![Aerial image of the house after the fire, with fire damage visible.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/624/cpsprodpb/b835/live/66f5e3b0-c16e-11f1-a003-8be783290413.jpg)

Peden jumped from an upstairs window before trying to tackle the fire

Peden, who broke an ankle jumping from the window and also suffered burns, was placed in an induced coma for four weeks and woke to be told that his family and their two dogs had been killed.

In that moment, he says, he felt "lost".

"I'd been in a relationship with Gemma for 14 years and we were talking about marriage. She was the one."

He is still dealing with the physical effects of the fire and says: "I'm not the same person that jumped out that window. That person has died.

"The person I am now is a nerve-stricken mess. Work have to let me know every time they are doing a fire drill because if I hear a fire alarm, my heart starts jumping."

On the purchase of the e-bike, Peden says he feels he has been "punished for three years for trying to make my life a little bit easier".

The inquest heard it had been modified and the battery was a second-hand replacement, but the coroner said there was insufficient evidence for her to find that repairs done to the battery had caused the blaze.

Prior to the inquest, Cambridgeshire Fire and Rescue Service wrote to the coroner, requesting they push for better regulation of online sales of e-bike batteries.

Assistant chief fire officer Stuart Smith told the BBC he had the same concerns about the secondhand market in lithium ion batteries.

He said there also needed to be "a cultural change", particularly around charging phones, tablets and other devices overnight.

"The advice from us, really, is please follow the manufacturer's guidance and only use the charging device supplied with the battery," he explained.

"Make sure you are alert and awake so you are able to switch it off when it is 100% charged, and can keep an eye on the temperature, and think about where you are charging them, should something happen."

Since the fire, Peden has campaigned for tighter regulations, highlighting the death of Elisha Brabin and three of her children in Bootle, Merseyside, last month, caused by an e-scooter lithium-ion battery that was on charge at the time.

"It seems like no-one's learnt a thing in the three years since," he says.

He is fundraising to cover the cost of his legal representation at the inquest, saying: "I'm just so determined that this doesn't get swept under the rug as another tragic accident. I won't allow it. And I won't stop."

Speaking after the inquest concluded, he welcomed coroner Elizabeth Gray's request for submissions about a possible prevention of future deaths report.

"We need this... report to come out and the government and the Secretary of State [for Business] to actually pay attention this time," he said.
