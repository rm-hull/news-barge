London Falling, Patrick Radden Keefe’s bestselling investigation into the mysterious death of a London teenager, has been named Waterstones book of the year.

![London Falling cover](https://i.guim.co.uk/img/media/fd4bf597bb1bd4bec2ada4c82c18a02d5841e7b2/0_0_2764_4251/master/2764.jpg?width=120&dpr=1&s=none&crop=none)

The book follows the parents of Zac Brettler, a 19-year-old who fell from the balcony of a luxury London apartment after pretending to be the son of a wealthy Russian oligarch, as they discover the murky, dangerous underworld of the city that their son had been drawn into.

In a Guardian review, Ian Thomson called it “a scrupulously researched work of narrative nonfiction”. “Grimly absorbing from start to finish,” he wrote, the book “opens a window on to a world of financial dirty work and Walter Mitty-like fantasies of aspirational wealth.”

Waterstones’ head of books, Bea Carvalho, said its word-of-mouth success “has been one of the defining features of the literary year”. Reading nonfiction is “vital, enriching, gripping and the buzz surrounding London Falling has helped so many readers discover this,” she added.

![Taiwan Travelogue cover](https://i.guim.co.uk/img/media/574f7af2cc2660518483f05959cdb965a305d462/0_0_3047_4677/master/3047.jpg?width=120&dpr=1&s=none&crop=none)

Radden Keefe, a staff writer at the New Yorker whose previous book Empire of Pain won the Baillie Gifford prize, said that at heart London Falling “is a story about family – about parents and children, about the perils of contemporary adolescence, and about how little we sometimes know the very people we love most”. The book was shortlisted for this year’s Baillie Gifford prize.

Meanwhile, Taiwan Travelogue by Yáng Shuāng-zǐ, translated by Lin King, which won the International Booker prize earlier this year, was named fiction book of the year. Yáng said it was “especially precious to see this story about Taiwan embraced and adored by the booksellers at Waterstones”.

Is This a Plum? by Oscar-nominated director Dan Ojari and his eight-year-old son, Finn, was named children’s book of the year. The cut-out picture book began when the pair realised “a plum looks a lot like a hippo’s bum”, said Dan. “Me and my dad made this book together – it’s very easy to read and will probably make you laugh at least once,” added Finn.

![Is This A Plum? cover](https://i.guim.co.uk/img/media/27d79af14d66a3e4d98797c6737533b69836354a/0_0_2799_2787/master/2799.jpg?width=120&dpr=1&s=none&crop=none)

The winners will receive the “full and committed backing of Waterstones shops and booksellers across the UK”, according to the company.

Also on the shortlist were Yesteryear by Caro Claire Burke, Stations by Louise Kennedy, The Book of Birds by Robert Macfarlane and Jackie Morris, Redwood by Ben Macintyre, Land by Maggie O’Farrell, May We Feed the King by Rebecca Perry, Ottolenghi Simple Too by Yotam Ottolenghi and Verena Lochmuller, The Things We Never Say by Elizabeth Strout and John of John by Douglas Stuart, among others.

Both the shortlisted titles and the book of the year are nominated and chosen by a panel of Waterstones booksellers.

Last year’s winner was The Artist by Lucy Steeds, while The Café at the Edge of the Woods by Mikey Please was named children’s book of the year.
