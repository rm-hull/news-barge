Donald Trump has claimed he urged Russian president Vladimir Putin to stop his aggression against Europe during their recent talks in China.

President Trump wrote on Truth Social on Thursday that he hoped Moscow would “pull back all military aggression against” unnamed “territories throughout Europe” in the hopes of making a deal to end the Ukraine war.

The conflict, approaching its five-year anniversary in February, has spilled over in recent months with drone incursions and cyber attacks that Europe has blamed on Russia. The Kremlin denies deliberately targeting Europe.

“I really believe this is a strong possibility — Thousands of people are being killed each and every month, A COMPLETE BLOODBATH — and will be a Great Tribute to everyone!” Trump wrote of a potential deal.

Trump and Nato chief Mark Rutte earlier issued a statement on the conflict, following a meeting at Mar-a-Lago on Wednesday.

They said they had discussed “the importance of Russia and Ukraine avoiding any actions that intentionally, or unintentionally, expand the War — and the importance of obtaining a peaceful resolution to the War, as rapidly as possible!”

The statement also touted the US’ investments into military equipment and munitions.

“Our “cupboard” will soon be full and, when it is, our Allies and Partners throughout the World will be benefitting greatly,” it read.

## Five key moments in Ukraine and Iran that sent energy bills soaring

The energy price cap is set to jump by 26 per cent from 1 January 2027 for households in England, Scotland and Wales, the energy regulator has said.

Ofgem said the price cap will rise by £443 per year, meaning an increase in annual bills from £1,723 to £2,166 if this level was sustained for a year.

The regulator said the ongoing conflict in Iran and the Middle East was the “primary driver” for the wild fluctuations in oil and gas prices which have intensified the cost of living crisis across the world.

Analysts say that although prices first started rising in August 2021 - when rumours began strengthening that Moscow was preparing an invasion of Ukraine - developments in the Middle East in the past year have seen them skyrocket.

## Russian losses increasing as war drags on

Ukraine’s general staff reported on Thursday that Russia had taken 2,091 casualties in the past day alone, reflecting a steady upward trend since 2022.

By comparison, Moscow was taking a mean average 415 casualties each day in October 2022; 709 in October 2023; 1,354 in October 2024; and 1,008 in October 2025, based on estimates from the Center for Strategic and International Studies (CSIS).

Ukraine assessed in September that Russia had taken 300,000 casualties so far this year - 18% of the casualties in 16% of the time since the invasion. On Tuesday, 24 November, the figure had risen to 432,519 casualties.

Western officials believe the toll is becoming unmanageable for Russia and in August estimated that the Kremlin was losing six thousand more troops per month than it was able to enlist.

## Russian casualty rate climbs as war held to impasse

A leak of 11 million death certificates collected from February 2022 to September 2026 suggests that Russia’s casualty rates have steadily increased over the duration of the war.

The information was initially obtained by the hacking groups Cyber Partisans and Silent Crow, who claimed to have obtained mortality data within dozens of terabytes of information from Rosstat.

The upward casualty rate seems to have peaked at 5,352 deaths per week in March of last year, according to the BBC, which notes delays in the reporting of deaths thereafter and incomplete data for 2026.

## Russia's oil refinery in Krasnodar region on fire after drone attack

Fire broke out at Slavyansk ECO oil refinery in Russia's southern Krasnodar region after a drone attack, inflicting serious damage, the company said on social media on Thursday.

It said the attack took place during maintenance and stoppage which had been in place since a September drone strike.

## Top story: The devastating scale of Putin’s war on Ukraine laid bare as leaked figures show 500,000 Russian soldiers dead

Nearly 500,000 Russian soldiers have been killed in almost five years of war with Ukraine, according to confidential government figures leaked this week.

Data from the state statistics agency Rosstat shows that at least 490,334 Russian soldiers have been killed in the conflict since February 2022, according to analysis by the BBC.

The figures show how the invasion became the leading cause of death for 18-24 year olds in Russia, with 65% of those who died last year killed in the conflict.

It also sheds light on the monumental losses sustained to make limited advances into Ukraine, with some 20,000 soldiers dying for control of 10 villages with a total pre-war population under 12,000.

And the Ukrainian military estimates that Russia has now taken more than 1,643,000 casualties, including dead and wounded - more than three times the figure last reported by Kyiv.

## Ukraine says Russia hits two grain ships near Odesa ports

Russia attacked two foreign cargo ships carrying more than 100,000 metric tons of grain overnight near Ukraine's Black Sea port of Odesa, Ukrainian Prime Minister Sergiy Koretskyi said on Thursday.

Koretskyi did not say where the grain originated nor where the vessels had loaded their cargo.

Ukraine has said grain shipments from its key Black Sea ports around Odesa have all but stopped since late July after Russia sharply intensified attacks on grain terminals and merchant vessels.

Russia says it does not attack civilian targets.
