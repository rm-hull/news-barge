Heavy rain, winds, flooding and high tides are hitting the eastern and western US, snarling travel and leading to the cancellations of at least two commercial Thanksgiving parades.

On the East Coast, authorities in Philadelphia ordered the city’s Thanksgiving Day parade in Philadelphia off the streets and will be featured as an “alternative TV event”.

In New York, the Macy’s parade of vast, inflatable characters was postponed to Saturday. One estimate put the cost of re-inflating the balloons with helium at $1.7m.

The New York governor, Kathy Hochul, declared a state of emergency in several parts of the state due to severe weather.

“I urge everyone to stay prepared, stay alert, stay informed and stay vigilant,” she said.

The New York City mayor, Zohran Mamdani, said in a travel advisory that those traveling should “budget extra time and use caution. Leave early and prepare for the possibility that you may have to stay late. There is no destination worth risking your safety.”

In New Jersey, governor Mikie Sherrill declared a statewide emergency. She asked residents of the state to “stay off the roads as much as possible once the storm arrives, and follow every directive from local officials, including any order to evacuate.”

Residents in flood-prone areas of Atlantic City strongly urged residents to voluntarily self-evacuate.

“We’d rather be safe than sorry. This is more like a tropical storm than a nor’easter,” the Atlantic City mayor, Marty Small Sr, said.

On the West Coast, strong winds and king tides flooded communities, prompted flood warnings and demolished part of a municipal pier in California’s Pacifica Beach.

Authorities warned residents that “debris may be underwater or wash ashore. If you see debris, leave it in place and report it.”

North of San Francisco, Marin county officials issued an evacuation warning for residents of beachfront properties in Stinson Beach, saying large waves expected through the afternoon could send water and debris into roads and homes.

The National Weather Service said the storm was expected to affect air travel at eastern US airports Thanksgiving Day into Friday morning. Adverse aviation conditions include reduced visibility, greater turbulence, strong winds and aircraft icing.

FlightAware reported 596 delays within, into, or out of the United States on Thursday and 844 cancellations. On Wednesday, more than 6,000 flights were delayed.

The US secretary of transportation, Sean Duffy, said the coastal storms will challenge holiday travel. “If you’re flying, take it slow on your way to the airport and stay in touch with your airline for the latest updates,” he said in a post on X.

Duffy said he recognized and sympathized “with the inconvenience any lost time causes travelers who are understandably anxious to be with their families, but our top priority will always be your safety.”

Winter storm warnings and watches were also posted for parts of Missouri and Illinois, with up to 13 inches of snow expected around St Louis by Thursday afternoon.

South Florida is facing another day of constant rain, flooding and possible tornadoes. Areas around Miami are anticipating rainfall totals of 2 to 4in, with isolated areas picking up between 5 and 6in, according to the National Weather Service in Miami.

A tornado brushed the ground in Boynton Gardens in Palm Beach county, 25 miles north of Mar-a-Lago, where Donald Trump is celebrating the holiday.

On the upside, high pressure will develop over the central/southern Rockies by Sunday evening, the service said, with clear skies and light winds producing freeze warnings over southern New Mexico and south-eastern Arizona. A freeze watch is in also in effect for parts of western Texas.
