First-time buyers purchasing their initial home are increasingly selecting properties they are less likely to outgrow quickly, new figures indicate.

Rather than opting for a traditional flat, a higher percentage of new buyers are choosing houses, according to mortgage data from Barclays, suggesting many are attempting to bypass early steps on the housing ladder.

The lender’s analysis reveals that houses accounted for 82.6% of first-time buyer purchases in October 2026, climbing from 77.4% recorded two years earlier.

Semi-detached properties proved to be the most sought-after option.

Meanwhile, the average deposit put down by first-time buyers reached £64,261 in October.

Flats comprised 22.6% of first-time buyer property purchases in October 2024, declining to 17.4% by October 2026.

Jatin Patel, head of mortgages, savings and insurance at Barclays, said: “Some first-time buyers are knocking rungs off the property ladder, with starter homes doubling as forever homes.

“After years spent saving for a deposit, many are questioning the logic of buying somewhere they could quickly outgrow, and are instead turning to a property that can work for the next chapter of their lives.”

David Fell, lead analyst at property firm Hamptons, said: “With would-be first-time buyers renting for much longer than they used to, they are increasingly in their mid-30s by the time they finally purchase a home.

“As a result, many already have children and require more living space than a typical starter flat can usually offer.

“By skipping the first rung and buying a house straight away, buyers can also make a significant stamp duty saving by only purchasing once instead of twice.

“Some new buyers also have concerns around the unpredictability of leasehold service charges, alongside worries about the future resale potential.

“As higher borrowing costs and living expenses continue to stretch the time it takes to save for a deposit, the traditional ‘step-by-step’ climb up the property ladder has become increasingly tough.”

Ian Harris, National Association of Estate Agents Propertymark president, said: “Changing working patterns, including the growth in home and hybrid working, are also influencing where people want to live, with space and flexibility becoming greater priorities for some buyers.

“Flats still have an important role to play, particularly for those who value being close to shops, leisure facilities and transport links.”
