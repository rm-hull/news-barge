## What did taxpayers get for an £85,000 trip to India?

*(Image: Supplied)*

A taxpayer-funded trade mission to India cost more than £85,000 as regional leaders sought to secure investment and business opportunities for York and North Yorkshire.

The York and North Yorkshire Combined Authority spent £85,102 on flights, stays at five-star hotels, and meals on a trade mission to India in October, a Freedom of Information request has revealed.

The costs covered accommodation, travel, and subsistence for the York and North Yorkshire delegation consisting of nine Combined Authority staff, including Mayor David Skaith, and 12 external delegates, including business and education leaders.

The 200-person trade mission to Hyderabad and Mumbai by Northern political leaders, businesses, universities and industry representatives sought to strengthen trade links following the new UK-India free trade agreement.

The FOI request, submitted by the Local Democracy Reporting Service (LDRS), revealed that the delegation led by the mayor spent £34,707 on accommodation at a cost of around £1,600 per person for 10 nights.

Mr Skaith, who sought to secure a share of a potential £170 million annual economic boost for the region, stayed at the luxury five-star chain of Taj hotels during the visit.

![Mayor David Skaith On South Bay Beach. Courtesy Numminen, LDRS](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21682055.jpg?type=mds-article-620)David Skaith, the elected Mayor of York and North Yorkshire

Travel, including economy class flights with Virgin Atlantic and internal flights with low-cost airline IndiGo, cost the Combined Authority £27,122 in total.

David Skaith, Mayor of York and North Yorkshire, said: “The free trade agreement between the UK and India that came into force in July has opened the door for northern mayors to form direct links with one of the world’s fastest-growing economies.

“In York and North Yorkshire, we are already seeing the benefits of the Great North’s trade mission with mayors to India, with new relationships emerging between businesses, universities and potential investors.”

He told the LDRS: “This is devolution delivering as our county will benefit from direct relationships being forged to unlock new opportunities for our businesses, create high-quality career pathways, and support leading research and innovation.”

The YNYCA said the trade mission had enabled it to explore new relationships for growth sectors within the county, deepen links, and support new opportunities for universities with the world’s sixth-largest economy.

The mayor previously said the visit could help the sale of fertiliser created on the North Yorkshire coast and help to boost crop yields globally.

Representatives from Anglo American’s Woodsmith project used the trade visit to strengthen relationships with government, industry and research organisations.

The mission represented the first major regional response to the UK-India Free Trade Agreement (FTA), which came into force this summer, and included fellow metro mayors Tracy Brabin, Oliver Coppard and Kim McGuinness.

The Combined Authority said it would continue to report on the successes of the trade mission as its key relationships and opportunities developed.

The third largest expense category was national and regional contributions at £12,333, while professional fees amounted to £5,000 and incident expenses £2,443.

A spokesperson told the LDRS: “The trade mission has been hugely positive for our county.

“This is the first time that York and North Yorkshire has been able to undertake such a mission.

“We will continue to report on the benefits as they develop further.”
