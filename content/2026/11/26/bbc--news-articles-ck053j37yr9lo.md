**A knifeman has been found guilty of attempting to murder 14 people, including 10 on board a train to London.**

Passengers on the Doncaster to King's Cross service fought off Anthony Williams, 33, with a frying pan and a toaster as the attack unfolded on 1 November 2025.

Some hid in toilets and bike racks before the driver was forced to make an unscheduled stop in Huntingdon, Cambridgeshire, where Williams was arrested by armed police.

As well as the train attacks, Williams, of Langford Road, Peterborough, was found guilty of attempting to murder four other people in separate incidents and will be sentenced at Cambridge Crown Court on 4 and 5 February.

Jurors deliberated for 15 hours and 53 minutes before delivering seven guilty verdicts, and then returned an hour later to give the final seven.

The judge, Mr Justice Calver KC, said the victims handled themselves with "great dignity throughout" the trial.

Aside from the attack on the LNER train, Williams also attempted to murder a boy and two men in Peterborough on 31 October 2025, and then a teenager on a DLR train in east London hours later.

The defendant, who appeared at court via a video link from a secure hospital, showed little emotion as each verdict was delivered.

Reacting to the verdicts, Ben Samples, of the Crown Prosecution Service, said: "The fact that nobody was killed in his rampage is down to the courage, quick thinking and good fortune of the people he attacked."

It has now emerged that in the month before the attacks, Williams twice contacted his GP to say the anti-psychosis drugs he was taking for schizophrenia were not working.

A spokesperson for NHS East of England said it would be investigating the care provided to Williams.

Cambridgeshire Police has also apologised for missed opportunities "that may have prevented" the stabbings.

Assistant Chief Constable Leighton Hammett said an alleged assault in Peterborough on 27 September 2025, several weeks before the train attacks, had been linked to Williams.

Darryl Preston, police and crime commissioner for Cambridgeshire and Peterborough, said two officers faced misconduct proceedings over their handling of the incident and the investigation remained ongoing.

Williams's knife attack on the LNER service began when he stabbed Jonathan Gjoshe – then a Scunthorpe United footballer – at 19:37 GMT.

The defender later informed police his whole arm was "drenched with blood".

Other victims included Scott Bletcher, stabbed six times in his seat as he returned from a Christmas shopping trip.

Bletcher told jurors he thought, "This is what it feels like to die" after being knifed.

The court also heard from Nottingham Forest football fan Stephen Crean, who decided "he had to try to stop" the attack and "launched himself towards and at" Williams.

Crean said Williams asked, "Do you want to die?" as he stabbed him several times.

Stephen Crean told the BBC he felt the knife "twist and turn" in his hand

CCTV footage played to the jury showed LNER staff member Samir Zitouni use a frying pan to fend off Williams.

He said to the knifeman "we love you, we can help you" as he thought it was "better than swearing at him".

After the train stopped, Zitouni fell between the train and the platform. His lungs were punctured and he needed four blood transfusions, chest drains and the assistance of a ventilator.

Having been arrested in Huntingdon railway station's car park, Williams was said to be "acting strangely" and "completely unfazed by what had occurred... he was rocking and laughing".

One police officer noted he "appeared to be talking to himself" and spoke about the devil.

Consultant forensic psychiatrist Dr Bernard Chin told the trial Williams, who did not give evidence in his defence, was "suffering from an acute relapse of schizophrenia" at the time of the attacks.

Jurors heard Williams had "the delusional premise that his life was at risk by a wide-ranging conspiracy by the devil".

Williams was confronted by staff and passengers using saucepans and kitchen equipment to defend themselves

As well as Gjoshe, Bletcher, Crean, and Zitouni, Williams was also convicted of attempting to murder Raza Aslam, Sachin Balakrishnan, Kevin Deely, Scott Green, Michael Paffett and David Presland on the train.

Williams also attempted to murder Dawid Taborski, William Ogelby and a 14-year-old boy, who cannot be identified, in Peterborough the night before.

The boy, who was about to go trick or treating with a friend, told police: "This random guy… he just pulled out a knife and stabbed me."

He said it "didn't feel like [Williams] was mentally sane. His eyes were wide open, like he was on something."

After those attacks, Williams boarded a London-bound train where he was heard saying: "I am the baddest, no-one is as bad as me".

![Williams wearing a black coat with the hood up inside a barber shop. He is holding a large silver knife.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/44d3/live/629ff790-e9b1-11f1-859a-37130cd3a1d7.jpg)

Williams brandished a knife inside a barber's shop in Peterborough in the build-up to his attacks

A passenger texted British Transport Police (BTP) reporting a male behaving erratically, but the man dropped down on to the tracks at Stevenage and police officers deemed it unsafe to pursue him.

Then, in the early hours of 1 November, Williams sliced the face of teenager Isaiah Idris on a DLR train in east London with a knife.

Idris described how his attacker had repeatedly asked: "Are you beefing the devil?"

Det Supt Sam Blackburn, from BTP, said Williams' offending had sparked "one of the most significant and complex" investigations in the force's history.

"The events that unfolded onboard the train on 1 November were unlike anything previously encountered before, and Anthony Williams created what I can only describe as a terrifying scene of fear and violence."
