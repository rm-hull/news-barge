Plans have gone in to give one of York city centre’s busiest Greggs bakeries a makeover.

The popular high street bakery chain has applied to City of York Council for consent to refurbish its established shop in the historic building 18 Pavement.

It wants to bring the branch up to date with a fresh internal refit and some exterior maintenance.

The proposals include reconfiguring the layout. Shoppers can expect new counters, display units, fixtures and fittings, as well as new wall, floor and ceiling finishes.

Digital display screens will also be relocated inside, and internal air-conditioning units will be installed to serve both the customer area and staff spaces.

The plans should ensure level access throughout, with door widths to make the store more accessible for wheelchair users and those with mobility aids.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/greggs-branch-pavement-york-gsv.webp)

Outside, the building is famous for its ornate upper floors, which feature decorative pargeting, timber framing, carved sphinxes, winged devil finials and representations of the York City Arms.

Under the listed building consent plans, none of those historic upper elevations will be altered.

At street level, the traditional timber shopfront will be retained and maintained to match its existing finish, while the original carved pilasters framing the entrance will be cleaned down and made good. New vinyls will be added.

Greggs has been a fixture on Pavement for more than two decades.

Founded in Tyneside in 1939, Greggs has grown into the UK’s leading bakery food-on-the-go retailer with more than 2,500 shops nationwide, including several popular spots across York.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/greggs-betfred-pavement-york-gsv.webp)

The building itself has a fascinating past. The three-storey property forms part of 18, 20 and 22 Pavement, a Grade II listed building dating back to 1893 that actually incorporates the remains of an older house from around 1700.

According to historic listing records, the building originally operated as a public house before being converted into shops and offices.

The planning application says: “The works have been designed to facilitate the continued commercial use of the premises whilst respecting the character and special architectural and historic interest of the Grade II listed building.”
