A cabinet minister who worked alongside Peter Mandelson for 30 years has said he is so disgusted by Mandelson's links with Jeffrey Epstein he wishes he had never met him.

Scottish secretary Douglas Alexander told *The Independent* he felt a “cold dispassionate fury” with Mandelson for “betraying” his country and former Labour prime minister Gordon Brown.

Files released in the US earlier this year showed that Mandelson shared secret information with Epstein obtained from his role as a member of Mr Brown's cabinet.

Mr Alexander, who was also in Brown's cabinet, said in an exclusive interview with *The Independent’s* editor-in-chief Geordie Greig that the strength of his feeling at Mr Mandelson's betrayal was in part as a result of his closeness to Mr Brown.

![Scottish secretary Douglas Alexander speaking to The Independent editor-in-chief Geordie Greig for The Talk Show](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/26/15/52/s8.png?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Mr Mandelson was present, along with Mr Alexander, with Mr Brown in No 10 on the day he was forced to admit defeat after the 2010 election.

“On the final day of Gordon's premiership, if any of us had known for a second... that Peter Mandelson was betraying his prime minister, betraying his cabinet colleagues and betraying the country by sharing information with Jeffrey Epstein, all of us would have felt then what I think all of us feel now.

“Which is I literally wish I'd never met the man.

“If you look at what has emerged in terms of the crimes of Jeffrey Epstein and the horrors of what was perpetrated against women by Epstein, I feel cold, dispassionate fury with Peter Mandelson...”

Mr Alexander was international development secretary in Mr Brown's administration and a long standing close personal and political ally of the former prime minister.

Like Mr Mandelson, Mr Alexander was a member of the cabinets of the administrations of Tony Blair and Mr Brown and both were given key posts by Sir Keir Starmer. Mr Alexander said his contempt for Mr Mandelson went beyond his association with convicted paedophile Epstein.

“He was known to be an exotic character within the Labour family but people thought that he was a Labour person, and he wasn't,” he said in an interview with Independent editor-in-chief Geordie Greig.

Files released by the US Justice Department in February showed that far from bolstering Mr Brown in No 10 as he tried to cling to power after narrowly losing the 2010 election, Mr Mandelson was emailing Epstein claiming credit for ousting him.

He emailed Epstein on May 10 2010: “Finally got him (Brown) to go today.”

Hours later Mr Brown announced he was stepping down as Labour leader, paving the way for the Con-Lib Dem coalition of David Cameron and Nick Clegg to take over.

Mr Mandelson was recalled as business secretary by Mr Brown in 2008. The following year Mr Brown appointed him first secretary of state, making him the de facto deputy prime minister and giving him vast access and authority throughout Whitehall.

According to the files released in the US earlier this year Mr Mandelson kept Epstein up to date on dramatic developments on an almost minute by minute basis in the days following the 2010 election stalemate.

He told Epstein on May 9: “GB now having 'secret' talks with Clegg in Foreign Office.”

The next morning he told Epstein: “Finally got him to go today.”

Hours later, Mr Brown announced he was to stand down as Labour leader, while remaining PM.

It was a last ditch attempt to stay in office by opening coalition talks with the Lib Dems. But it went nowhere.

Then Tory-leader Mr Cameron formed a coalition with Mr Clegg instead and Mr Brown resigned as PM.

The files released in February show Mr Mandelson's flippant tone in exchanges with Epstein after the 2010 election.

![Sir Keir Starmer with Peter Mandelson](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/26/15/01M1EK05FV5210119PSP29M06Q.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Mr Mandelson told Epstein he had spent “all day first with [Gordon Brown] then with Libs. They are all pretty bonkers…”

When Epstein emailed him: “Why not let tories govern with minority, no coalition,” Mr Mandelson replied: “cos GB thinks British economy will collapse without [him] at helm.”

In the run up to polling day Mr Mandelson appeared to discuss removing Mr Brown as Labour leader or running for leader himself.

He told Epstein: '”If I continue as now people will say I am one of the few (only) big figures. And I have to deliver a reasonable campaign that only he [expletive]'s up. And that I perform well in the campaign itself. That I was bigger than the lot of them.”

Epstein allegedly replied: “Let’s not dwell on him, I am only interested in how this plays out for you.”

Mr Mandelson responded: “He cannot take the truth. He needs 5 years of therapy...”

The files also showed Mr Mandelson forwarded an internal memo on selling off government-held assets in order to raise public funds to Epstein.
