A Russian constellation of satellites similar to Starlink could soon allow the Kremlin to fly lethal drones over London, according to one of Ukraine’s most senior military intelligence officers.

Major General Vadym Skibitsky, deputy chief of the HUR military intelligence service, told *The Times* that Russia would be able to carry out attacks all over the world if it gains a fully operational network of its own.

Skibitsky said that Russian president Vladimir Putin has a ten-year plan running to 2035, in which he hopes to deploy 924 satellites to bring Rassvet fully online - creating Russia’s answer to Elon Musk’s Starlink.

“It would allow Moscow to operate naval drones or unmanned aircraft in regions far beyond the Black Sea — in Europe, Africa, the Middle East or the Far East,” he told the newspaper.

![Geran-2 unmanned combat aerial vehicles (Shaheds) on vehicles driving across the Red Square during the Victory Day military parade in central Moscow on May 9, 2025](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/26/11/2213582548..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Such a system could allow an operator in Russia to pilot a drone as far away as London at speeds of up to 250kmh - unless Ukraine’s special forces can thwart Putin’s star-gazing ambitions, Skibitsky said.

Some of Russia’s Iranian-made Shahed drones, designated ‘Geran’, can already travel up to 2,000km with a 50kg warhead, and European nations have already reported UAVs entering their airspace as spillover from the war.

Moscow to London is around 2,500km as the crow flies.

Typically these cheap one-way drones are not operated by a remote pilot but follow pre-determined targets. They are slower and less damaging than missiles, but cost a fraction and can overwhelm air defences in numbers.

Skibitsky says the HUR has now identified 49 Russian businesses working to expand that capacity by building the network that would give Putin the ability to steer drones with a clear feed from a safe location within Russia.

![Vadym Skibitsky (left, in May 2025) said Putin has a ten-year plan running to 2035, in which he hopes to deploy 924 satellites](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/26/11/2214775145..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Ukraine has apparently been working on developing its own version of Starlink after last month reporting trouble flying drones at speed using the network.

As Russia and Ukraine jostle for control of the skies, Western intelligence services warned this week that Russia plans to put a satellite carrying a nuclear warhead in space by the end of 2026, according to a report.

The German military and Federal Intelligence Service have already told other Nato states that Russian weapon could be in orbit by December, the report from Germany’s NDR, WDR and Sueddeutsche Zeitung said.

Such a weapon detonated in low orbit would have devastating consequences for both military and civilian infrastructure on Earth, the head of US Space Command warned earlier this year, referencing similar intelligence.

The Kremlin said the reports should not be taken seriously.

Separately, new satellite images of Russian military facilities suggest the long-awaited "Satan II" nuclear missile may be entering service with an estimated range of around 18,000km, or 11,000 miles.

The RS-28 Sarmat is a nuclear-capable intercontinental ballistic missile (ICBM) that has been in the works for years. Successful tests were carried out in 2022 and 2026, but repeated failures had cast doubt on its reliability.

The International Institute for Strategic Studies (IISS) reported this month that the missile could now be entering service with the 62nd Missile Division of the Strategic Rocket Forces (RVSN) at Uzhur.
