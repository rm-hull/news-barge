Parents and coaches in Harrogate are being urged to make play a bigger part of children’s sport, after new research found almost half of parents believe children’s right to play is undervalued.

The Ipsos study for Play Their Way and UK Coaching found that 46 per cent of UK parents feel play is undervalued, and 69 per cent want sports coaches to make time for free play in sessions and lessons outside school.

Heather Douglas, Head of Coaching & Policy at Leeds-based UK Coaching, added: "Play is how children explore, imagine, experiment, connect and discover what they're capable of. It's not just something they enjoy. It's their right.

“We are calling on coaches, teachers and parents to support their children and young people have the freedom to create, adapt and shape their experiences.”

The findings were released for World Children’s Day (November 20), which marked the anniversary of the UN Convention on the Rights of the Child.

Article 31 of the Convention ensures every child has the right to play, rest and take part in leisure activities, and sport is one of the main ways children can use that right.

Red Roses legend and new mum Shaunagh Brown was among those who called for play to be a bigger part of children’s sport.

Brown, 36, played for England from 2017 to 2022 and joined former England U18s coach David Reed for a child-first coaching session on World Children’s Day.

Pupils helped create their own games, showing parents and teachers what a child’s right to play looks like in practice.

Brown said: “Play is where my love of sport started as a child. Since primary school, it’s been a huge part of my life across athletics and rugby, but I always wonder how many children don’t have the chance to find theirs.

"Giving children facilitated playtime in school PE and grassroots sport is crucial to helping them lead active, positive lives.

“Every child should have the right to play. Play Their Way’s child-first approach is enabling children and young people to have a voice, so they can determine how they take part.”

The Play Their Way movement was launched in May 2023 to transform the way children and young people are coached by prioritising their rights, needs and enjoyment in a ‘child-first’ approach.

The campaign is funded by Sport England and The National Lottery and is led by organisations that make up the Children’s Coaching Collaborative (CCC). Coaches, teachers and parents can find free training, guidance and session ideas in the Play Their Way app.
