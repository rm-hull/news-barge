## Kuwait's military said four people were injured after "a number of hostile" Iranian drones were detected and intercepted in its airspace. Flights crossing Iraq toward the Gulf were given new headings ahead of Kuwait, shifting the international corridor.

Flights on the key air traffic corridor between Europe, the Gulf region and Asia crossing Iraq towards the Gulf were suddenly redirected south on Thursday afternoon amid reports that Kuwait’s airspace was closed.

The Kuwait military has since issued a statement that four people were injured after "a number of hostile" Iranian drones were detected and intercepted in its airspace.

"The Iranian aggression targeted a vital facility, causing falling shrapnel that injured four people," said a statement by the defence ministry, adding that the injured were taken to a hospital for treatment while the incident caused unspecified "material damage".

Two Italians working for German defence group Rheinmetall died, Italy's foreign ministry said.

Since the war in Iran broke out in late February, most international flights crossing Europe have been flying toward major hubs in Doha, Abu Dhabi, Dubai and other destinations via a route running through Turkey, Iraq, Kuwait, Bahrain and the UAE.

All flights on the corridor were given a new heading, prompting them to make a sharp turn ahead of Kuwait's airspace and move the entire international corridor toward neighbouring countries, including a flight on which Euronews reporters were travelling.

The drone attack on Kuwait comes just one day after the US Virtual Embassy in Iran said Tehran, Yemen's Houthis and other aligned groups may target US interests and citizens in the region, including companies and institutions.

Washington warned citizens across the Middle East to exercise heightened vigilance after Iran attacked US assets in Bahrain and Kuwait and Yemen's Houthi forces struck a tower in Riyadh's financial district, wounding 11 people.

US citizens in the region were urged to monitor airline and airport operations and prepare for possible flight cancellations and airspace closures.

The war has seen the Gulf states caught in the crossfire, with US military facilities and energy installations on their soil targeted.

The US and Iran are in a standoff over the vital Strait of Hormuz, with the US military working to escort ships transiting the waterway in defiance of Tehran's attempts to close it.

Flights bound for the Gulf countries carry extra fuel for emergency diversions, in case they need to enter a holding pattern near their destinations if airports and airspaces close due to military activity.
