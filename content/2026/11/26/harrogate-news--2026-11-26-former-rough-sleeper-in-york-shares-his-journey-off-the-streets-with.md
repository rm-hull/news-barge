**Damien Goudy, once homeless and living on the streets of York, describes how City of York Council’s housing navigators helped him move into supported accommodation and eventually his own home.**

Damien Goudy’s life changed dramatically when he lost his job as a qualified construction worker, followed quickly by the loss of his home and the car he had been living in. These cascading misfortunes led him to the streets of York, where he spent months initially living off the generosity of strangers rather than claiming benefits.

Reflecting with wry humour, Damien said:

When the Covid-19 pandemic began, City of York Council offered him a flat. However, his attempt to share this accommodation with others did not work out, and he lost the flat, returning once again to street homelessness. Despite this setback, the Council’s housing navigators maintained contact, continuing to support him through a difficult period.

Damien experienced temporary accommodation intermittently before accepting support at the Peasholme Centre, a supported accommodation scheme in York. Adjusting was challenging, particularly after enduring freezing nights of -14°C outdoors and a life without fixed responsibilities or bills. However, he accepted help with his health needs and sobriety eighteen months ago and eventually moved into his own flat.

He continues to receive assistance from the familiar team of housing navigators, who now help him with practical matters such as job applications and accessing dental care.

Scott McCrorie, a City of York Council Housing Navigator, explained that their team works closely with individuals who are rough sleeping, often meeting them on the streets and building trust through daily contact. They provide personalised support tailored to each person’s circumstances, encouraging them when they are ready to move into accommodation and prepare for independent living.

> **Damien values that helping hand:**
>
> When you’re trying to deal with everything yourself, and someone who knows everything helps you, that makes all the difference.
>
> The Housing navigators are really good at relating to you: not everyone can.

Scott and his colleagues offer ongoing support, whether that involves helping with benefits, addressing mental health or addiction, or facilitating engagement with work and social activities. The support can include practical solutions, like arranging a bicycle for volunteering or a gym membership to improve fitness and social connections.

City of York Council also runs a severe weather provision each winter, offering emergency beds from November until late March regardless of weather conditions. These beds supplement the 120 existing supported accommodation spaces available throughout the year. The navigators actively offer these emergency beds alongside their broader assistance.

> **Cllr Anna Perrett, Executive Member for Housing at City of York Council, said:**
>
> As Damien’s story shows, people experience knocks in life and sometimes need a helping hand to get back on track. No one who wants a safe place to sleep need stay overnight on the streets. As a council we’re deeply committed to providing the support that makes rough sleeping rare, brief and non-recurring, and this means understanding and responding to people’s individual circumstances and needs.
>
> The Government’s new, three-year funding stream to support our work tackling rough sleeping will significantly enhance what we can do and brings welcome certainty on addressing what is a shared local and national commitment.
>
> This year’s allocation of almost £500k will be spent on continuing to do what works well: offering accommodation, including tenancies, and tailored support to meet each individual’s needs. This work is detailed in our updated Homelessness and Rough Sleeping Strategy action plan, which outlines how we’ll support each person sleeping rough in York to get back on their feet.

She added that the Government’s new three-year funding stream, allocating nearly £500,000 for this year, will enhance the Council’s ability to continue providing accommodation and tailored support to reduce rough sleeping in York.

Members of the public who see someone sleeping rough in York are encouraged to contact the Council’s housing navigators on 07511 161169 or email [housingrshnavigators@york.gov.uk.](mailto:housingrshnavigators@york.gov.uk.) Alternatively, reports can be made via the national charity StreetLink website ([https://thestreetlink.org.uk](https://thestreetlink.org.uk)) or by calling 0300 0500 0914, which alerts local teams.

The Council’s updated Homelessness and Rough Sleeping Strategy action plan, detailing how York supports people experiencing homelessness, will be discussed at the Council’s executive meeting on 1 December 2026 at 4:30pm. The document is available publicly through the Council’s website.

This story illustrates the persistent challenges and the vital role of tailored, ongoing support in helping individuals transition out of homelessness in York.
