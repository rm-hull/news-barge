# Watch: Warning issued as footage shows teen doing star jumps on level crossing in Yorkshire

## Footage has been released of a teenage girl doing star jumps on a level crossing as a warning to young people not to risk their lives.

The warning comes after footage from Methley North level crossing, near Leeds, captured a young person doing star jumps on the crossing and another stopping to take a photograph rather than crossing promptly and safely.

Network Rail has been in contact with schools in the area since the incident in September to support staff in reinforcing the dangers of distraction and deliberate misuse around the railway.

There were 72 level crossing safety incidents involving children and young people on the route in 2025/26, an increase of more than a fifth from the 2017/18 figure of 59, a Network Rail spokesperson said.

Darren Robertson-Wilson, level crossing manager for Network Rail’s North & East route, said: “Level crossings are safe when people follow the instructions, remain alert and cross without stopping, but the consequences of distraction or deliberate misuse can be devastating.

![The girl was spotted doing star jumps on the Methley North level crossing.](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/jpim-static/image/2026/11/25/11/31/Methley-North-header.jpeg?trim=0,20,0,180&crop=&width=640&quality=65)


“The behaviour captured at Methley North shows why this campaign is so important. Stopping on a crossing to take a photograph or treating the railway as somewhere to play puts lives at risk. We work with schools and communities to make young people aware of the risks and help them make safer choices. There are no second chances on the railway.

“Many people do not realise that seemingly everyday behaviour, such as looking at a mobile phone, wearing headphones or talking to friends, can reduce their awareness and place them in danger at a level crossing.”

Network Rail is currently supporting the Transpennine Route Upgrade as overhead line equipment is energised between Cross Gates and Church Fenton, making it particularly important that young people understand the increased risks and make safe choices around the railway.

Their community safety team has also raised concerns that mobile phone use and headphones are becoming an increasing risk at level crossings, alongside people ducking beneath or jumping over barriers, going around barriers after they have begun to close, or carrying bicycles over them.
