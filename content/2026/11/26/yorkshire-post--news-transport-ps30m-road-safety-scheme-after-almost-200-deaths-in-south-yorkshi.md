# £30m road safety scheme after almost 200 deaths in South Yorkshire in four years

## A South Yorkshire-wide plan to tackle road safety, backed by £30m in funding, has been approved, after statistics reveal 191 people died in the region between 2021 and 2025.

One of the fatalities is 81-year-old Graham Slinn, who was hit by a car in April 2025 while crossing the road on a green man near Todwick, Rotherham.

He died just weeks before he was due to celebrate his diamond wedding anniversary.

Graham’s daughter Nicola said: “To lose any loved one is a shock, but to lose someone so vibrant, so active, and to know their death was 100 per cent avoidable is doubly cruel.

“He was a wonderful man. This should never have happened.”

South Yorkshire Mayoral Combined Authority (SYMCA) has now approved an “ambitious” plan to deliver safer speeds and safer journeys, with the aim of reducing deaths and serious injuries by 20 per cent by 2029.

The plan, supported by a £30 million in national government ‘transforming city regions’ funding and approved at a board meeting on November 24, sets out a range of measures including safe speed management, investment in safety cameras and improvements to roads and roadsides.

It will fund road safety engineering works focusing on “high-risk” routes, corridors and locations.

![Graham Slinn.](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOjIxMDBmYzQ2LTcwYWQtNGEwMi1hNTUxLWRkODQxMjEwOTc5MzoxMTE1MWRlZi00YjU3LTQyMzctODI3ZC00ZDVjNGYyZjI5Njg=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


According to the plan, a methodology to judge which areas are priority for improvements will be agreed and in place by March 2027.

It will also see the launch of a campaign across 2027-2029 focussed on the ‘fatal four’ – speeding, drink and drug driving, mobile phone use and seatbelts.

This is intended to shift the focus from changing the behaviour of vulnerable road users, to drivers themselves.

The scheme will also pilot new enforcement technology to monitor the ‘fatal four’ behaviours, like technology that automatically detects when drivers are using their mobile phones or not wearing a seatbelt

SYMCA also plans to continue an ongoing programme of lower speed limits.

It will also set up, a ‘fatal review panel’ by June 2027, to examine road accidents and learn lessons from fatal collisions.

By March 2028, all vehicles owned or operated by South Yorkshire’s local authorities, emergency services and public sector organisations are planned to have a high standard of safety and maintenance, with all drivers fully trained to drive safely.

According to data from the Department for Transport, out of the 3,962 people killed or seriously injured in South Yorkshire, 515 were under 15 years old.

Children were by far the biggest age group injured whilst walking, and South Yorkshire active lives commissioner Ed Clancy said his work with many local schools.

“So many tell me they don’t feel safe near our roads. They worry about speeding traffic, difficult crossings and dangerous driving,” he said.

DfT data also found young male drivers aged 17 to 24 are around four times as likely to be killed or seriously injured compared with all car drivers aged 25 or over.

Part of the campaign element will focus on schools, to develop and deliver programmes for children from next September, aligning with similar campaigns on active travel.

At a SYMCA board meeting, Sheffield council leader Fran Belbin welcomed the plan and said it aligns well with Sheffield’s own strategy.

She said: “We’re looking at a 20mph strategy and refreshing our approach to that.

“Expanding camera capabilities is really welcome,” she added.

Coun Belbin said she would like to “stay coordinated” and see partnership working on enforcement because “we all know the policy is nothing without enforcement.”

Kilvinder Vigurs, Deputy Mayor for Police and Public Safety said the plan was “fundamental” to safer, fairer and more connected communities.

“As chair of the safer roads partnership, I’ve been incredibly encouraged by the way partners have come together to develop this plan.

It’s built on strong evidence, robust data and proven approaches, giving us a clear roadmap for reducing deaths and serious injuries,” she said.

“This action plan represents our first steps toward our ambition to create roads and streets that bring people together and support opportunities to connect without fear of injury or worse.”
