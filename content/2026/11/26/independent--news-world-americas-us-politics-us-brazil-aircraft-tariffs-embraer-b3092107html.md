A 10% duty on finished Brazilian aircraft is at the center of new tariff proposals the United States unveiled on Wednesday, targeting goods from Brazil. Washington said Brasilia had failed to rectify what it calls "unreasonable" trade practices.

The move caught Brazil off guard a month after a presidential election in which U.S. tariffs were a major campaign issue. It forms part of a wider round of tariffs the Trump administration announced on goods from more than a dozen trading partners.

Brazil's government said Washington's announcement was unacceptable and aimed to pressure Brazil into changing its regulatory measures. Trade Representative Jamieson Greer said his office was proposing changes to tariffs imposed in July after Brazil made "no meaningful progress" on the issues.

![The United States proposed adding new tariffs on goods from Brazil on Wednesday, ​including a 10% duty on finished aircraft](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/25/17/50/GettyImages-2301532592.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The changes would give Washington more leverage to pressure Brazil to drop the practices, he said. Greer said Washington remains open to talks. Finished Brazilian aircraft, which are currently exempt, would face the 10% duty under the proposal.

Aircraft are Brazil's fourth-largest export to the United States. The measure could hit planemaker Embraer, opens new tab hard. Embraer's shares fell more than 3% on Wednesday afternoon.

The company said it was reviewing the announcement, adding that Embraer "continues to believe in and strongly defends maintaining the zero-tariff regime for the global aerospace industry, which has been ​in place since the 1980s." Two people at the company, who spoke on condition of anonymity, said the announcement surprised them.

## New sugar duty proposed

Aerospace has largely escaped the tariffs that have hit other sectors such as autos, steel and aluminum. Tariffs on Embraer commercial jets would pose problems for U.S. airlines, which have no domestic alternative for flying regional routes to smaller cities.

![Brazil was the first country targeted under U.S. President Donald Trump's new tariff strategy](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/07/30/15/53/SEI306887542-copy.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Brazil was the first country targeted under U.S. President Donald Trump's new tariff strategy, which relies on Section 301 of the Trade Act of 1974, an unfair trade practices statute. The U.S. Supreme Court struck down Trump's previous global tariffs under an emergency law, including duties of up to 50% on Brazilian goods, in February.

The July tariffs followed months of fruitless negotiations between U.S. and Brazilian officials. They had maintained and expanded exemptions for Brazilian beef, coffee, aircraft and other goods. Brazil asked on Tuesday for the World Trade Organization to create a dispute panel to examine the July Section 301 tariffs.

The U.S. blocked the move the same day. Brazil also faces an additional 12.5% tariff under a separate Section 301 investigation into connections to forced labor in the supply chains of dozens of countries. The U.S. TR will hold a hearing on January 14 before the duties can take effect.

## Outrage in Brasília

Washington is also proposing a new tariff rate on out-of-quota raw and refined sugar entering the U.S. on a nondiscriminatory basis, the U.S. Trade Representative's office said. This would ensure that sugar from Brazil could not be shipped through third countries into the United States. The impact may be limited, however.

![Brazilian President Luiz Inacio Lula da Silva and his wife Rosangela "Janja" da Silva react as supporters gather to celebrate his 81st birthday, in Brasilia](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/26/11/2026-10-27T215635Z_1797426099_RC2XBOAX71CW_RTRMADP_3_BRAZIL-ELECTION-LULA-SUPPORTERS.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Brazil's sugar exports to the U.S. have dwindled to just over 146,000 tons through the end of October 2026, down from more than 1 million metric tons in 2024, according to industry body UNICA. The U.S. already plans to cut Brazil's tariff-free raw sugar quota for 2027 by about a third, to 100,000 tons.

Renato Cunha, CEO of industry group NovaBio, said the new tariff will not directly affect producers in Brazil's north and northeast, which benefit from the quota. He called the tariff "negative" and said it "highlights the unpredictability of these actions interfering in the market."

Washington accuses Brazil of unfair practices on a range of issues from illegal deforestation to Brazil's instant payment system, Pix, which the U.S. government argues disadvantages credit card companies. Brasilia has rejected all the allegations.

Brazil's government expressed its "outrage" at the announcement in a statement on Wednesday evening. It said the proposed tariffs aimed to pressure Brazil to change regulations on issues including Pix and digital trade.

Brazil and the U.S. established a working group in September to drive negotiations over tariffs and a broader bilateral trade framework after an August call between President Luiz Inacio Lula da Silva and Trump. Brazilian officials say a proposal from Washington that Brasília had expected never materialized.
