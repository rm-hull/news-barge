# MPs call for urgent meeting with minister over gas storage closures

## MPs have called for an urgent meeting with Energy Minister Michael Shanks over plans by SSE to start mothballing gas storage caverns in East Yorkshire next summer.

The eight caverns, carved out of an ancient underground salt layer of rock salt at Atwick near Hornsea, in the 1970s can store around 309 million cubic meters of gas.

Atwick Gas Storage first entered commercial operation in 1979. It was purchased by SSE in September 2002. Together with the nearby facility at Aldbrough, they account for around 40 per cent of Britain’s onshore gas storage capacity. However even when gas prices are high, the price gap between buying gas to store it and selling it later is not wide enough for SSE to profitably run the Atwick site.

The energy giant, which is worth around £31bn, confirmed a plan to start “rewatering” – pumping water or brine back into caverns to displace the remaining gas and maintain structural pressure – from next summer. However, no decision had been taken on decommissioning or closure. Under current plans the site would continue to operate well into the 2030s.

Its interim results stated: “SSE continues to engage the UK Government with urgency on the need for policy intervention to support a resilient gas system; without which assets are expected to progress towards a multi-year programme of rewatering and then decommissioning.”

Beverley and Holderness MP Graham Stuart and East Yorkshire MP Charlie Dewhirst said it is a “question of national energy security”.

A letter to the Energy Minister said: “East Yorkshire helps keep Britain’s homes warm. Norwegian gas comes ashore at Easington and Atwick and Aldbrough provide reserves we can draw on when a cold snap drives up demand or another source of supply is interrupted.”

The MPs said the Department for Energy Security and Net Zero was considering several options, including a “cap and floor that could keep the sites running and allow taxpayers to recover money in profitable years”.

Mr Stuart said the Atwick site was losing money. “There is a genuine and serious risk that it would close.

"The government needs to engage with SSE seriously, work out how much they would be prepared to pay if necessary for SSE and look at how they would construct an agreement with SSE to minimise the risk to taxpayers but ensures the asset is maintained.”

He said the cost to the taxpayer would be a “relatively small number of millions” against the cost of “tens of millions” to bring a water or brine-filled cavern back into operation.

The Minister is understood to have met with SSE this week to discuss the issue.

A DESNZ spokesperson said: “Britain has secure and diverse gas supplies this winter. National Gas has confirmed we have enough gas to meet demand this winter, including in extreme weather scenarios.

“We recently confirmed plans to explore commercial support for storage and the wider sector, to help protect our gas security. We will set out proposals in due course”.
