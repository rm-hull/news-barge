A memorial service for Ann Widdecombe will be attended by current and former politicians at Westminster Cathedral.

Nigel Farage and Richard Tice of Reform UK are expected to attend, alongside Conservative Party chairman Kevin Hollinrake and other political figures.

Beginning at 2pm on Thursday, the event will include eulogies and a performance by the Westminster Cathedral choir.

Miss Widdecombe, 78, died on 8 July after she was hit over the head with a hammer 21 times at her home in Haytor on Dartmoor, Devon.

Joshua Kerry, 28, of Rotherham, South Yorkshire, was charged with her murder.

![Reform UK leader Nigel Farage during a tribute to Ann Widdecombe at their party conference](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/12/18/d5c3dae2dcc6b90d0198894bdbc19144Y29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzkxOTEyMzYz-2.85739941.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

A trial before a High Court judge is scheduled for 8 June next year, and a plea and case management hearing on 29 January. Kerry has also been charged with a terror plot against Mr Farage.

Miss Widdecombe’s family announced plans for a public memorial service following her death.

In August, a congregation of around 100 family members, friends and colleagues, including former Tory ministers and broadcasters, has attended the funeral service for former Tory minister Ann Widdecombe.

Figures from politics and broadcasting gathered at Buckfast Abbey in Devon, a Catholic abbey where she worshipped regularly and described as her "safe haven".

Mourners wearing black arrived for a requiem mass – a Catholic service in which prayers are offered for the soul of the dead – honouring the former MP, who went on to become a Reform UK spokeswoman.

![Miss Widdecombe, 78, died on 8 July after she was hit over the head with a hammer 21 times at her home in Haytor on Dartmoor, Devon.](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/03/09/4f9d8745b78264858dc240deeacdd8cfY29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzkxMTAxNDQ2-2.76291005.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

High-profile figures who attended include the shadow chancellor Mel Stride, Conservative former justice minister Crispin Blunt and broadcaster Iain Dale.

Prior to the service, which lasted approximately 90 minutes, police officers using sniffer dogs searched the grounds.

She was first elected as a Conservative MP in Kent in 1987, was a prisons minister in the 1990s and later became an MEP for the Brexit Party.

At the time of her death, she was a spokeswoman on immigration and justice for Reform UK.

She also found fame outside politics on *Strictly Come Dancing*and* Celebrity Big Brother.*
