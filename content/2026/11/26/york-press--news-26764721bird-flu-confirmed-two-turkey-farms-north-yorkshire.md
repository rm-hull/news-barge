Bird flu has been confirmed at two commercial turkey farms in North Yorkshire.

The cases were reported near Thirsk and Northallerton, prompting the introduction of disease control zones.

North Yorkshire Council is urging all bird keepers to follow strict biosecurity measures and housing requirements to help prevent further spread.

Richard Foster, the council’s executive member for managing our environment, said: "These measures are vital to protect birds across North Yorkshire.

"Avian influenza can have a devastating impact on both commercial and small-scale bird keepers, and we urge everyone to follow the requirements, maintain good biosecurity and ensure their birds are registered.

"Whether you keep thousands of birds or just a few hens at home, everyone has a role to play in preventing the spread of disease.

"By following the housing and biosecurity requirements, we can help protect our poultry sector, rural communities and local businesses while keeping North Yorkshire free from further spread of the disease."

Protection and surveillance zones have been established around the affected farms, restricting bird movements and introducing enhanced controls.

A nationwide Avian Influenza Prevention Zone also remains in place.

Housing requirements apply to flocks of 50 birds or more, and to smaller groups if poultry or eggs are supplied to people outside the keeper’s household.

Bird keepers are being advised to monitor their flocks closely for signs of illness and contact a vet immediately if they have concerns.

Louise Wallace, director of public health for North Yorkshire Council, sought to reassure the public that the risk remains very low.

She said: "However, avian influenza can be serious, and residents are advised to follow national guidance by not touching sick or dead wild birds, avoiding contact with feathers or droppings, and washing your hands thoroughly if contact cannot be avoided to help reduce any risk.

"We are working closely with partners, including the UK Health Security Agency, the Animal and Plant Health Agency and Defra, to monitor the situation and provide advice to protect both public and animal health."

The UK Health Security Agency has also stated that the risk to the public remains very low.

Properly cooked poultry, poultry products and eggs are safe to eat.

Any suspected cases of bird flu in kept birds must be reported immediately to the Animal and Plant Health Agency by calling 03000 200 301.

It is a legal requirement to register all kept poultry and captive birds.

Those who have not yet registered their birds are urged to do so as soon as possible via the Government website.

Members of the public are also reminded not to touch sick or dead wild birds.

Dead wild birds can be reported by calling 03459 33 55 77 or online through the appropriate Government channels.
