Donald Trump will speak at the groundbreaking of a new Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Co. computer chip foundry in Arizona on Dec. 4, according to the White House and the Taiwan-based company. The visit marks the start of construction on the fifth phase of TSMC's Arizona expansion.

The president has staked his economic policy on growth in the artificial intelligence sector. The trip closes a week in which AI has faced scrutiny in Washington after Trump intervened to get his ally Jensen Huang reinstated as Nvidia's CEO.

Huang is slated to testify at a Senate Banking Committee hearing on Dec. 3. He will face questions about how his company has been selling its chips abroad, and his affair with a woman suspected of being a Chinese spy. Beijing has denied it.

![President Donald Trump plans to visit Arizona next week to speak at the groundbreaking of a new computer chip foundry for Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Co](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/25/18/APTOPIX_Trump_7381_.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

TSMC has expanded aggressively in the Phoenix, Arizona, area, using its U.S. facilities to manufacture chips for tech companies such as Nvidia, Apple, AMD, Broadcom, and Qualcomm. The company announced plans in July to spend another $100 billion on expanding its manufacturing capacity in the United States.

That brings its total pledged investment in American chipmaking to $265 billion. The investment has grown as the development of artificial intelligence has stimulated demand for more computer chips.

Taiwan's dominance in chipmaking has led countries including the U.S. to address supply chain vulnerabilities by producing more domestically. Trump has sought to promote investment through his tariffs.
