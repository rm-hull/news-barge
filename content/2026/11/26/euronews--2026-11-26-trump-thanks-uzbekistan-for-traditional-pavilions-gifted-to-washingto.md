## Crafted from the wood of 200-year-old walnut trees, the structures stand near some of Washington's best-known monuments. The gift commemorates the independence anniversaries of both countries.

US President Donald Trump has thanked his Uzbek counterpart Shavkat Mirziyoyev for two traditional wooden pavilions presented as a gift to mark the 250th anniversary of US independence.

Crafted from the wood of 200-year-old mountain walnut trees, the structures have been installed in Washington's West Potomac Park, near the Tidal Basin, with views of the Jefferson Memorial and the Washington Monument.

Known as aywans, the open-sided gazebos are a traditional feature of Uzbek architecture, with intricately carved columns and roofs decorated with arabesque patterns.

According to the US National Park Service, Trump accepted the gift on behalf of the American people. It also commemorates the 35th anniversary of Uzbekistan's independence.

In a post on his Truth Social platform, Trump said Mirziyoyev was Uzbekistan's “highly respected president” and praised the Uzbek craftsmen who spent several months working on the structures in difficult weather conditions.

“They had real talent,” Trump wrote.

“Rather than keep this gift as ‘private,’ I wanted to donate these beautiful gazebos to the Public,” he added.

The unveiling ceremony last week drew US Secretary of the Interior Doug Burgum and Uzbekistan's Ambassador to the United States, Furqat Sidikov. Trump said he had flown over the site aboard Marine One during the ceremony.

He also thanked the Uzbek Embassy in Washington and the US National Park Service for their work alongside the craftsmen.

Trump has invited Mirziyoyev to attend the G20 summit in Miami on 14 and 15 December as a guest, although Uzbekistan is not a member of the group.
