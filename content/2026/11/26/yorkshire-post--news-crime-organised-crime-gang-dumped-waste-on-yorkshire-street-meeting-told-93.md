# 'Organised crime gang' dumped waste on Yorkshire street, meeting told

## A town centre street in Yorkshire was used by an organised crime gang to store and burn waste – leading to vermin infestations.

Around 30 skips worth of waste have since been cleared from the site in the heart of Keighley.

The shocking act of criminality was detailed in a recent report on environmental enforcement actions that have taken place in the town in the past year.

Roper Street is an unadopted road between High Street and Russell Street – and is just yards from North Street.

![Roper Street](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOmY4MmQ0YWZmLTEwZjQtNDJlMi04MTZiLWFlZDAyMWZmNTNlNjplMGZhNTNhNC0zOGI4LTRhZDAtODE1Zi02N2M3NDdiN2UzZGQ=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


This summer, Bradford Council received reports that a “significant” amount of waste being stored on the street was leading to “vermin” in an area of the town that includes numerous local businesses.

There were also reports that waste was being illegally burned, and the issue soon attracted “considerable local attention.”

CCTV installed by local businesses to catch the offenders in the act was “disabled.”

Bradford Council’s Keighley area committee were given an update on environmental enforcement actions in the constituency.

A report to members included details of the town centre waste site – which has since been cleared after the “OCG (Organised Crime Group) which were believed to be bringing waste to this location left the premises.”

The report to members said: “In July 2026 an issue was reported to the environmental enforcement team regarding vermin, a significant accumulation of waste and the burning of waste on an unadopted highway close to the centre of Keighley.

“This caused concern for ward councillors, local residents and surrounding businesses, and attracted considerable local attention.

“A coordinated multi-agency response was subsequently established involving Bradford Council, West Yorkshire Police and West Yorkshire Fire and Rescue Service.

“Previous efforts to install surveillance cameras at the location by a neighbouring business resulted in the camera being disabled.

“The environmental enforcement team worked with the council’s CCTV provider to identify a location which enabled the majority of the affected area to be observed while maintaining the security of the device.

“Environmental enforcement officers liaised with highways enforcement officers in the use of statutory powers to serve notice on abandoned caravans at the location.

“People Can (a community initiative) and the council’s waste collection teams removed a significant volume of waste resulting in the area being restored.

“The OCG (Organised Crime Group) which were believed to be bringing waste to this location subsequently left the premises which was rented in the area.

“No further waste has been deposited at the location since the clearance, and local businesses have now installed further CCTV cameras to track activity in the area, and it remains subject to ongoing monitoring.

“The resolution demonstrates the value of a coordinated approach involving partner agencies, local businesses and council departments.”

When the waste was cleared, Councillor Amjad Zaman posted about the issue on social media.

His said the enforcement team cleared over 30 skips’ worth of rubbish from the site.

In a warning at the end of his post, he said: “We’ve got cameras around the areas now, so if anyone decides to fly tip I’m going to ask the council to throw the book at them.

“This is our town. We’ll keep it clean.”
