## How music lessons help children find their voice and believe in themselves

Fun at the Make Music Day in York

*From anxious pupils to confident performers, thousands of children are transforming their lives through music in York schools, reports MAXINE GORDON*

WHEN Ben feels overwhelmed, he reaches for music.

For years, the Year 5 pupil struggled to regulate his emotions in school. Frustration could quickly boil over into shouting and distress, making everyday classroom life difficult.

Today, music is helping him take control.

Teachers noticed that classical music had a calming effect during assemblies. Gradually, they began using music more throughout the school day. Over time, Ben learned to use it himself.

Now, when he feels anxiety building, he turns to music before his emotions escalate.

"He can spot his own dysregulation coming and remove himself into music before it escalates," his teacher explained.

For Ben, who is autistic, that breakthrough has been life-changing. Inspired by his experiences, he has even started violin lessons, opening up a new world of confidence, creativity and self-expression.

His story is just one of thousands highlighted in a new impact report from York's Richard Shephard Music Foundation, which reveals how music is transforming lives across the region.

Since launching five years ago, the charity has helped more than 13,000 children access high-quality weekly music lessons, many in schools serving some of the area's most disadvantaged communities.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21762259.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Children are thriving with music lessons

What began in just 12 schools in 2021 now reaches 40 schools, with more than 9,200 pupils taking part during the last academic year alone.

And while the numbers are impressive, education leaders say the real impact is seen in the children themselves.

One headteacher told the Foundation: "You see quieter children finding their voice.

"You see children who may struggle elsewhere in school thriving in music lessons.

"You see children learning how to express themselves, how to work together, and how to believe in themselves."

## **🎵 At a glance**🎶** 13,000+ children helped**  
More than 13,000 young people have received weekly music lessons since 2021.

🏫 **40 schools involved**  
The programme has expanded from 12 schools to 40 across York and the wider region.

💷 **£1.5 million invested**  
The Foundation has secured more than £1.5m in charitable funding for music education.

❤️ **99% saw confidence grow** Almost every teacher surveyed reported improvements in children's confidence and wellbeing.**'Music makes me feel strong and in control'**

The programme is delivered in partnership with specialist provider Sing Education and introduces children to singing, instruments, rhythm, composition and musical notation from an early age.

According to teacher feedback, the results go far beyond musical skills.

This year, 99 per cent of teachers surveyed said pupils' confidence had improved, while 94 per cent reported better mental and emotional wellbeing.

More than nine in 10 teachers also said children's aspirations had increased.

Pupils themselves spoke powerfully about the difference music has made.

One child said: "It helps me relax and feel good for the rest of the day."

Another said: "Music makes me feel strong and in control."

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21762257.jpg?type=mds-article-620)A session at the Foundation's Make Music Day in York

The Foundation says those responses underline the importance of ensuring music education is available to every child, regardless of family income or circumstance.

Around half of participating schools have more than 30 per cent of pupils eligible for free school meals.

**Finding a voice through music**

Another pupil featured in the report is Sonny, a Year 6 child who excelled academically but often struggled when asked to work with others.

Group activities frequently ended in frustration, and he preferred to complete tasks independently.

That began to change during a music composition project.

Encouraged by his teacher, Sonny worked alongside classmates to create an original piece of music. Initially reluctant, he gradually learned to listen to others, collaborate and value different ideas.

The project culminated in a successful performance, but the benefits did not stop there.

Teachers reported improvements in his friendships, confidence and teamwork skills, with those gains carrying across into other areas of school life.

An independent evaluation involving 189 children and 20 adults found strong evidence that the programme is improving wellbeing, communication, concentration, confidence and social skills.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21762262.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The Foundation's Make Music Day in York enjoyed by children

Researchers also highlighted its positive impact on children with additional needs and the way performances help strengthen links between schools, families and local communities.

**'We're creating opportunity through music'**

The wider benefit is significant too.

A Social Return on Investment study found the Foundation's work creates an estimated £3.6 million of social value every year, generating £5.40 of social value for every £1 invested.

Looking ahead, the charity plans to continue expanding opportunities, particularly in communities where access to arts education can be limited.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21762258.jpg?type=mds-article-620)– The Foundation's Make Music Day in York Minster

Chief executive Cathy Grant said: "We're creating opportunity through music and ensuring that every child has the chance to discover what they are capable of.

"Five years after its launch, thousands of children across the region are already proving just how powerful that opportunity can be."
