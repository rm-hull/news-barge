A woman has been banned from every Marks & Spencer in the country after attempting to trick staff into refunding items.

Danielle Marshall, 32, requested money back for items she hadn’t purchased at branches of the department store in Manchester and London.

She has now been banned from all stores for five years, and must not enter the Trafford Shopping Centre, including any of the grounds, bus station or car parks.

After appearing in court, she was handed a Criminal Behaviour Order (CBO), with conditions including that she must use her real name and her real address when creating online accounts for retail purchases.

A CBO is a civil order but breaching one is a criminal offence, and can result in arrest, charges, and punishments including imprisonment, fines, or community service.

![](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/26/10/2232613837..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

She was also sentenced to a 12-month Community Order, 140 hours of unpaid work, she must also pay compensation of just over £400, and was ordered to pay £85 in courts costs, and a £114 victim surcharge.

Inspector Paul Ellis from our Trafford Centre Policing Team, said: "Working together and supported by the Trafford Centre security teams, and the M&S Security Operations Centre, officers from the Trafford Centre Policing team were able to identify a pattern of offending and bring Marshall to justice.

"I hope that her sentence today, and the strict conditions placed on Danielle by the court order, will strengthen confidence across the retail industry, that GMP is working robustly with partners to tackle these crimes."

In April, Marks & Spencer’s retail director said crime has become “more brazen, more organised and more aggressive” with staff on the high street facing daily abuse and violence.

In a statement on the retail giant’s website, Thinus Keeve said: “Without a government seriously cracking down on crime and a Mayor that prioritises effective policing we are powerless.

“We need a stronger, faster and more consistent police response, using tools that already exist to target repeat offenders and crime hotspots.

“And we need far greater transparency on crime so the true scale and impact is understood and can be used to target resources.”
