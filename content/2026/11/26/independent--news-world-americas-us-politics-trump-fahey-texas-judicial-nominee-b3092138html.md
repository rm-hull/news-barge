Steve Fahey, a former prosecutor, is in line for a Dallas-based federal judgeship after President Donald Trump moved to replace one of his Texas judicial nominees.

Chuck Grassley, the Senate Judiciary Committee chairman, said late Wednesday that his panel had received partial nomination paperwork for Fahey, backed by outgoing Republican senator John Cornyn. Trump had previously nominated T. Elliot Gaiser, a Justice Department official, for that seat.

Cornyn last week withheld a so-called blue slip backing Gaiser, preventing the original pick from advancing. The move marked the latest instance of a Republican senator opposing a judicial nominee in the lawmaker's home state.

![Senator John Cornyn witheld backing Trump’s original pick months after the president pulled his support for the outgoing Republican](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/09/06/22/2289541071..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Cornyn, who lost a primary to a Trump-backed challenger, objected to Gaiser, saying he was not even from Texas and that the White House "was trying to jam him down our throat without adequate vetting for a lifetime job."

The opposition has complicated White House efforts to confirm as many of Trump's remaining judicial nominees as possible before Democrats take control of the Senate in January.

Democrats assume Senate control on January 3 after winning a 56-seat majority in the recent election. The White House has already been forced to replace one nominee opposed by his state's Republican senators.

Grassley's office said the Iowa Republican would consult with his colleagues to determine whether Fahey and another newly announced nominee, North Carolina's Jim O'Neill, have viable paths to confirmation. The White House did not respond to a request for comment.

![Cornyn, who lost a primary to a Trump-backed challenger, objected to Gaiser, saying he was not even from Texas and that the White House "was trying to jam him down our throat without adequate vetting for a lifetime job"](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/25/16/57/GettyImages-2305643148.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Trump nominated Gaiser to a seat on the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of Texas after the election, with the support of Texas' other Republican senator, Ted Cruz. Gaiser, a former Ohio solicitor general, previously clerked for conservative U.S. Supreme Court Justice Samuel Alito.

He currently heads the Justice Department's Office of Legal Counsel, a key unit that has frequently signed off on the legality of the Trump administration's actions. Under Senate custom, home-state senators play an influential role in selecting nominees for federal courts in their states.

They can effectively block trial court picks they oppose by refusing to return a blue slip signaling support. Across his two terms, Trump has secured 295 judicial nominees to lifetime positions. Those appointments include three members of the U.S. Supreme Court's 6-3 conservative majority.
