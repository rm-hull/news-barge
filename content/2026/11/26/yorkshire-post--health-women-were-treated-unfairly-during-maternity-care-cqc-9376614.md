# Almost one in five women were treated ‘unfairly’ during maternity care, CQC survey finds

## Almost one in five women believe they were treated “unfairly” when seeking maternity care, a new survey from the Care Quality Commission has found.

This includes women who felt they were treated unfairly or differently from others while pregnant, in labour and birth and during the postnatal period.

Some 14 per cent of women who said they had been treated differently or unfairly said that they felt this was because of their age and another 14 per cent who said they had been differently or unfairly treated felt it was because of their race.

It is the first time that the national maternity survey for England has asked women if they felt they were treated differently or unfairly by health professionals.

It comes amid a review into maternity and neonatal services at Leeds Teaching Hospitals NHS Trust following a number of deaths of babies and mothers in the hospital’s care.

The survey, from the Care Quality Commission (CQC), was completed by 16,759 women who gave birth in January and February this year.

The CQC said the poll found a number of improvements compared with the 2025 survey, including in women’s experiences of communication, compassion and the involvement of their partners.

But the survey also revealed “key areas for improvement”, including 17 per cent who said that if they raised a concern during labour and birth it was not taken seriously

Almost a quarter said they did not have the opportunity to ask questions about their labour and birth after their baby was born.

More than a third said they considered making a complaint about their maternity care, unchanged from 2025.

Senior midwife Donna Ockenden is leading the independent review into failures at LTHT’s maternity and neonatal services.

A BBC investigation revealed the deaths of at least 56 babies and two mothers over the past six years may have been prevented, while legal firm Irwin Mitchell is now representing more than 50 families in action against the trust.

This includes assessing how concerns were handled by LTHT, as well as improving clarity around the lines of accountability.

Health Secretary Yvette Cooper said: “The improvements in maternity care experiences over the last two years are really important, but too many women are still not getting all the support they need or being fully listened to when they raise concerns.

“That has to change. Under this Government, the NHS has recruited almost 2,000 more midwives and invested £271m to make maternity and neonatal units safer.

“But there is much more to do, that is why we are establishing a new maternity commissioner with new powers to improve care, so that every mother and baby gets the safe, compassionate care they deserve.”

Dr Toli Onon, the CQC’s chief inspector of hospitals, said: “Too many women still tell us their concerns are not being taken seriously, and some feel they have been treated unfairly.

“That is not good enough. Safe, high-quality maternity care must be the experience for everyone.”
