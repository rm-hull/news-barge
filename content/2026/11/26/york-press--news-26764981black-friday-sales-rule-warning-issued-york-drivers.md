Bargain hunters in York using their cars to collect their Black Friday purchases could find the money they've saved is wiped out by penalties and fines from lesser-known motoring rules.

The warning has come from a leading motoring expert at driving experience provider TrackDays who highlights the risks, such as overloading a vehicle.

A TrackDays spokesperson said: "Most drivers associate overloading with a weight issue, however overloading also includes inside the vehicle if boxes in the boot are blocking the rear screen.

"Although this is not illegal if the car has two fully functional wing mirrors, it could invalidate the insurance if involved in an accident and the view is obstructed.

"Overloading can be contrary to the Road Traffic Act which states that it is an offence to use a vehicle that does not adhere with the construction and use requirements regarding weight, and additionally specifically if the weight, position or distribution of a load, or the manner in which it is secured, is a danger.

"Those found guilty of dangerous driving as a result can face three penalty points, an unlimited fine and even a prison sentence."

Motoring expert at TrackDays Imran Malek said: "Motorists need to remember some basic driving rules in the rush of bagging some Black Friday bargains and transporting them back home.

"One of the main motoring pitfalls is overloading a car, whether that's in the boot or on its roof, so if you've got many boxes, or large boxes, make sure you have clear vision and that the boxes are securely stored."
