Strobe Talbott, a former Time magazine reporter who later served as deputy secretary of state under Bill Clinton and then headed the Brookings Institution, died Thursday. He was 80.

His son, Devin Talbott, confirmed he died peacefully after a long illness in his East Hampton, New York, home.

The elder Talbott was educated at Yale and Oxford, where he bunked with Clinton. While at the British university, after interning for Time in Moscow, the magazine asked Talbott to help translate the leaked memoirs of Nikita Khrushchev, the former first secretary of the Communist party of the Soviet Union.

Talbott did, and it landed him a job at the magazine.

After 21 years at Time, where he focused on Soviet-American relations and held positions including Washington bureau chief and a foreign-affairs columnist, he joined the Clinton administration.

Talbott joined the US state department in 1994, swiftly becoming deputy secretary of state. In that role, he was the architect of the administration’s approach to Russia and other formerly Soviet countries and conducted negotiations between the US and India after the latter’s nuclear tests in 1998.

He served as the president of the Brookings Institution, one of the US’s most prominent thinktanks, from 2002 to 2017. In that time, he also chaired the US state department’s foreign affairs policy board.

Talbott authored over a dozen books. His last book was titled Our Founders’ Warning: The Age of Reason Meets the Age of Trump, which was published in 2020.

He was born in Dayton, Ohio, in 1946, and is survived by two sons, Devin and Adrian; stepdaughter Rebecca Ascher-Walsh; siblings Kirk, Marjo and Page Talbott; and eight grandchildren, according to the New York Times.

His first wife, Brooke Shearer, died in 2009, and he later married author Barbara Lazear Ascher.
