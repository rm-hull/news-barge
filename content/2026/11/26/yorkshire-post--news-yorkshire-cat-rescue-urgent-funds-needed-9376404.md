# Yorkshire Cat Rescue: urgent funds needed - can you help save kittens’ lives?

![Urgent appeal: any donation, however small, will go towards helping vulnerable kittens (photo: YCR)](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/jpim-static/image/2026/11/26/14/04/GetImage-2-ashx.jpeg?trim=187,0,135,3&crop=&width=990&quality=70&enable=upscale)


## The Yorkshire Post has been asked to help call upon animal lovers around the region to help save lives by Yorkshire Cat Rescue after it discovered its kitten house and maternity ward facilities are dangerously unfit for purpose.

In an appeal sent the this newspaper, the rescue shelter said this: “We desperately need to raise £40,000 to replace our kitten house and maternity ward, which are no longer safe or suitable for the vulnerable cats and kittens in our care.

Sadly, we have discovered that the laminated wooden pods within our existing facilities have become contaminated deep within the wood itself, contributing to the spread of dangerous contagious viruses, including Feline Parvovirus. Despite our best efforts, cleaning alone cannot resolve the problem, and we simply cannot risk any more lives.

As a result, our kitten house is now closed and cannot reopen until we can afford to replace the existing facilities.

This is about so much more than replacing buildings. It's about protecting vulnerable pregnant cats, giving new mums and their babies a safe place to recover, and ensuring tiny, sick and orphaned kittens have the hygienic, suitable environment they need to survive.

The reality is that over 500 innocent lives could be turned away in 2027 if we cannot raise these funds. These are cats and kittens who may desperately need our help but who we simply won't have the facilities to accommodate.

We have professional plans, a confirmed quote and a company ready to build our new purpose-built facilities using veterinary-grade acrylic and stainless steel. Our fundraising target will cover the replacement kitten pods, maternity facilities and flooring, allowing us to provide safer accommodation and significantly improve infection control.

We are therefore asking whether you would consider helping us by sharing our appeal through your newspaper, website, social media channels or any other platform available to you. We would also be incredibly grateful if you would consider featuring our story to help raise awareness of the situation and encourage people to support our fundraising efforts.

The more people who hear about our appeal, the greater our chances of reaching our target and ensuring we can continue helping the hundreds of cats and kittens who rely on us.”
