MPs will decide next month on the first phase of the restoration and renewal programme for the Houses of Parliament, the Commons Leader has revealed.

The vote will follow a debate in the Commons on Tuesday, 8 December. Sir Alan Campbell claimed the process had been delayed over many years because “politicians can’t make their mind up and then stick to it”.

The Public Accounts Committee (PAC) warned earlier this month that without action the historic building would crumble away into managed decline.

The weekly cost of maintenance on the parliamentary estate is expected to grow from the current £1.5 million to £2 million in coming years, the committee said.

A different committee, Parliament’s Restoration and Renewal Client Board, has noted that in the decade since 2016, 36 fire incidents had been recorded in the Palace of Westminster, in addition to 12 asbestos and 19 stonemasonry incidents.

Speaker Sir Lindsay Hoyle has also expressed frustration with delays in making repairs to the Victorian-era building, telling BBC Radio 4’s *The Week In Westminster*: “We spend more time doing surveys.”

!['All the invasive surveys, including the foundations, need to be completed now'](https://static.independent.co.uk/s3fs-public/thumbnails/image/2015/08/29/21/Parliament-GEtty.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

MPs first voted to leave Parliament for the building to undergo extensive repairs in 2018 but there has been continued disagreement about whether this should go ahead.

Speaking in the chamber on Wednesday, Sir Alan said: “We’ve been through this process for long enough, what we need to do is make a decision.”

He added the Government was not “closing off something at this point”, adding: “It’s not a fixed plan. It will adapt over a period of time, but the key point is: are we going to get on with it?

“What we will be putting before the House on December 8 is an initial package of phase one works lasting seven years, capped at a maximum of £3 billion.

“This is essential groundwork and investment in resilience and enabling works, which must happen no matter which final route we take.”

One of the recommended routes for the restoration and renewal programme is a “full decant”, costing up to £15.6 billion and lasting up to 24 years.

Another option is a partial decant – called “enhanced maintenance and improvement plus” – which would cost up to £39 billion and last up to 61 years.

Sir Julian Lewis, the Conservative MP for New Forest East, said “two or three or four generations of MPs” would need to be decanted, “and they will be put in modern facilities”.

He added: “When the time comes to come back, they won’t want to do it.

![Sir Alan Campbell claimed the process had been delayed over many years because ‘politicians can’t make their mind up and then stick to it’](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/29/13/2286337820..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“So, if this building is indeed the heart of our parliamentary democracy, and if we wish to preserve it and to prevent it from becoming anything other than an over-expensive museum to the sort of ‘political system we used to have’, we must ensure that MPs remain at least in partial occupation of the building whichever option we finally decide to choose.”

The debate on the restoration and renewal of the Palace of Westminster had been brought forward by the Conservatives.

Shadow Commons leader Jesse Norman had proposed a 90-day “rapid” review of the restoration and renewal programme “bringing together existing engineering assessments, cost estimates, procurement plans and safety requirements, subjecting them to rigorous challenge”.

Sir Alan said this would cost £100 million and “would not guarantee progress at the end”.

He accused the Tories of offering a “warm bath of endless review”, adding: “At what stage will they accept the stark warnings of the National Audit Office, the Public Accounts Committee and every independent assessment the House has ever commissioned?”

Father of the House and Conservative MP Sir Edward Leigh urged his parliamentary colleagues to “get on with it”, adding: “By the time we decant I’ll be in my grave.”

He said he was opposed to MPs being decanted: “It’s the Palace of Westminster. This isn’t just a building; it’s a working building. It is not just a monument. It’s not just a museum which some people want to create it.

“This is about the mother of parliaments. It has to be occupied.”

The Conservatives’ motion on Wednesday, to express “grave concern at the enormous projected costs of the restoration and renewal programme”, was rejected by 291 votes to 102, majority 189.
