The FBI has assessed that U.S. intelligence does not show Alice Huang was an officer or an asset of Chinese intelligence, according to two people briefed on the matter.

The question of whether she was a Chinese spy remains unanswered, however, days after President Donald Trump declared Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang was not a security threat to the United States. Alice Huang, a former executive at a major Nvidia customer, is at the center of that question.

Senior U.S. national security officials, including at the White House, the FBI and the CIA, have debated in recent days whether she was working for or passing information to China's premier spy agency, according to five people familiar with the matter, who spoke on the condition of anonymity.

![Questions remain over Jensen Huang’s relationship with an alleged Chinese spy](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/24/14/2026-11-24T110016Z_703340578_RC2G8EABEPTL_RTRMADP_3_NVIDIA-CEO-BOARD.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The U.S. has collected intelligence on Alice Huang and her contact with individuals associated with China's Ministry of State Security, two people briefed on the intelligence said. That intelligence has been described for months in reports from several intelligence agencies, including the CIA, the people said.

Reuters could not determine the details of the intelligence reports.

Key officials, including those at the FBI, the CIA and Marco Rubio, Trump's national security advisor and secretary of state, have taken different positions on what the information gathered by U.S. spy agencies means, while agreeing on the underlying facts.

Those differing viewpoints were not formally discussed or debated within the administration before Huang's ousting from Nvidia, the five people said. The interpretation of the facts is important to understanding Trump's decision to push for Huang's reinstatement as Nvidia's CEO, three of the people said.

![This photo illustration shows the Nvidia logo displayed on a smartphone screen](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/26/10/2306379029..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The intelligence will likely be central to any congressional investigation into Nvidia and the president's actions. The CIA's view is more nuanced than the FBI's.

The agency says the reporting does not clearly show that she possessed sensitive national security information or passed it to China, but it left open the possibility that new evidence may emerge, the two people said.

Some officials believe Alice Huang's contact with the Chinese spy agency indicates cooperation with Beijing, even if U.S. intelligence doesn't show her passing information. A classified memo written by Rubio earlier this year indicated that the MSS had recruited Huang, potentially as an asset, according to an earlier *New York Times* report.

The intelligence reports were held closely inside the intelligence community and among cabinet officials. They were not shared with Congress, the two people said. The White House, CIA, FBI and State Department declined to comment.

## Intelligence was not widely shared

Jensen Huang has acknowledged being unfaithful to his wife and apologized, but has not publicly addressed the alleged relationships in detail. China has denied the espionage allegations. Attempts by Reuters to reach Alice Huang have been unsuccessful.

![Jensen Huang has acknowledged being unfaithful to his wife , Lori, shown here](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/24/12/2305156633..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Different interpretations of U.S. intelligence highlight what current and former intelligence officials say has become a problem for the Trump administration: classified information is now shared and debated less widely inside the intelligence community.

The National Security Council, which has faced significant cuts since the start of Trump's term, has historically held meetings where officials can scrutinize and debate intelligence on sensitive matters, but now does so less often than in previous administrations.

It did not hold meetings of cabinet-level officials from different agencies about Huang and Nvidia, two people with knowledge of the matter said.

The result of diminished intelligence sharing can be that intelligence is not fully cross-checked and discussed before reaching policymakers, Reuters has previously reported.

![Jensen Huang embraces Chinese businesswoman Alice Huang after delivering his keynote speech at COMPUTEX 2024 in Taipei, Taiwan](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/26/10/2026-11-26T033541Z_215935177_RC2138ASMLJV_RTRMADP_3_NVIDIA-CEO-USA-INTELLIGENCE.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The CIA's analysis indicates that Alice Huang, a Chinese national, was in touch with China's spy agency because Chinese law requires citizens to cooperate with the country's intelligence authorities.

One source said CIA reporting focused mainly on Alice Huang's role at her former employer, Megaspeed, and its diversion of U.S. chips to China.

## Competing narratives

Many officials have argued that those actions pose a greater national security threat than the Nvidia CEO's affair. Megaspeed has said it conducts legitimate and lawful business, including complying with U.S. export controls.

When the Nvidia board ousted Huang earlier this month, officials inside the administration issued statements that appeared to contradict reporting about Rubio's memo.

![Donald Trump’s Attorney General Todd Blanche has determined that Huang does not pose a national security threat](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/25/17/2306565417..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Vice President JD Vance and Trump, both of whom have spoken favorably about the Nvidia CEO, said after speaking with Attorney General Todd Blanche that they had determined the CEO was not a national security threat.

Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton said it was unclear whether Alice Huang was an asset of the Chinese spy services. Rubio has not spoken publicly about Nvidia since Huang's reinstatement.

When the *New York Times* broke the news about that memo earlier this month, it caused uproar inside the administration and among board members of Nvidia who questioned whether Huang knew he was having an affair with someone who was passing information to China, according to two people familiar with the the conversations.

Trump convened Vance, Blanche and Clayton to discuss the intelligence, pressing them on whether the CEO was under investigation and whether there was any threat to U.S. national security, the two people said.

Blanche told Vance that Huang posed no threat, as Reuters has previously reported. Rubio was not involved in those conversations and, according to one of the people, was caught off guard by Huang's reinstatement. The State Department did not respond to questions about Rubio's involvement in the conversations.
