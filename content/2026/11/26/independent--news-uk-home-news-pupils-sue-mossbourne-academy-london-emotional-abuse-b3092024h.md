A number of former pupils two east London schools that enforced a “strict” behavioural policy are suing the academies for “emotional and psychological abuse”.

Students at two Mossbourne Federation schools in Hackney claim they have been left with “psychological injuries” as a result of the treatment after the schools brought in “no-excuses” policies in a bid to raise standards.

The claim submitted by Leigh Day alleges a “consistent pattern of intimidatory shouting and excessive punishments” that left students with “lasting” psychological injuries. It claims the strict discipline policies particularly impacted students who were vulnerable or had special educational needs.

Documents claim many former pupils self-harmed and experienced panic attacks while at school, which they say was due to the stress and anxiety caused by their treatment.

They cite the case of one student who had anorexia and was allegedly forced to deviate from their medically instructed eating plan and put in an exercise programme, despite their eating disorder.

![Former pupils at Mossbourne Federation schools have initiated legal action](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/26/10/07/Mossbourne_community_academy_1.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

A strict toilet policy is also alleged to have resulted in several occurrences of pupils urinating on themselves, being unable to leave the classroom to deal with menstruation, and pupils vomiting in the classroom.

The claims are brought by people who were pupils at Mossbourne Community Academy (MCA), and Mossbourne Victoria Park Academy (MVPA).

It comes after a report last year found staff at MVPA routinely used measures that “humiliated and intimidated” pupils and created a “climate of fear” at the institution.

The review identified a number of “harmful practices” including "desking", which sees pupils placed at desks in corridors as punishment, and punishing disability-related behaviours.

“The approach of senior leaders in prioritising strictness has superseded all else, with an accompanying attitude that practice is beyond reproach as pupils get good grades,” the review noted.

Leigh Day said the claim is being brought on the basis of intentional infliction of injury and harassment, and will argue that the Mossbourne Federation breached the duty of care it owed to children and was negligent in preventing them from coming to harm.

Abuse team partner at the firm Dino Nocivelli, who represents the former pupils bringing the claims, said: "The claims brought by these former pupils are deeply concerning, and appear to show a pattern of excessive intimidation and punishment at Mossbourne Federation schools. These experiences have had a lasting impact on the former pupils, who in some cases continue to live with psychological injuries they say were inflicted during their time at school.

"These claims are not about debating how a school manages its discipline. They are about addressing situations where it is alleged the treatment of pupils had gone beyond discipline and amounted to abuse."

A spokesperson for the Federation said: "The health, safety and wellbeing of our students is our top priority, and we have a strong commitment to safeguarding across all of our academies.

“We take any concerns raised with us seriously and would encourage anyone who has any to contact us directly. It would not be appropriate for us to comment on any potential legal matters or on the circumstances of any individual students."
