More than 120 students and staff members at a North Carolina elementary school were reportedly sickened following a norovirus outbreak, prompting officials to temporarily move classes online.

At least 122 students and staff at L.J. Bell Elementary School in Rockingham, North Carolina, reported gastrointestinal symptoms, according to Richmond County Schools. Because of the illnesses, the school transitioned to remote learning on Monday and Tuesday, CBS affiliate WBTV reported.

“On Thursday, November 19th, an unusually large number of students at L.J. Bell Elementary began experiencing gastrointestinal symptoms within a short period of time,” the district stated in a social media post.

Local health officials investigated the cluster of illnesses and confirmed the cause was norovirus, a highly contagious pathogen that causes inflammation of the stomach and intestines.

School district officials advised parents to monitor their children for symptoms, including vomiting, diarrhea and a fever of 100.4 degrees Fahrenheit or higher.

![Classes at LJ Bell Elementary School in Rockingham, North Carolina, were temporarily moved online after a norovirus outbreak left more than 120 students and staff members ill](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/25/21/20/L-J-Bell-Elementary-School.png?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&auto=webp)

Often referred to as the "winter vomiting bug," norovirus typically causes sudden-onset nausea, vomiting and diarrhea, occasionally accompanied by stomach pain, headaches and body aches. While most healthy people recover within one to three days, young children, older adults and individuals with weakened immune systems face a higher risk of severe illness and dehydration that may require hospitalization, *The Independent* previously reported.

There are currently no approved vaccines or specific antiviral treatments for the infection. Recovery relies on rest and replacing lost fluids with water, electrolyte drinks or oral rehydration solutions.

![Once on hard surfaces such as stainless steel or ceramic, norovirus can survive for up to two weeks and requires temperatures of roughly 140 degrees Fahrenheit to be killed](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/21/21/19/iStock-2246537877.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The virus spreads easily through close contact or when microscopic particles from infected feces or vomit contaminate food, hands or surfaces. Research indicates that as few as 10 viral particles can trigger an infection, and droplets from vomiting can travel up to 10 feet. Once on hard surfaces such as stainless steel or ceramic, the virus can survive for up to two weeks and requires temperatures of roughly 140 degrees Fahrenheit to be killed.

Because alcohol-based hand sanitizers are largely ineffective against norovirus, health experts recommend frequent handwashing with soap and hot water for at least 15 seconds, particularly after using the restroom and before handling food.

![With no approved vaccines or specific antiviral treatments available, recovery relies on rest and replacing lost fluids to prevent severe dehydration](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/08/31/12/46/iStock-2233007611.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

To prevent household transmission, contaminated areas and high-touch surfaces — such as faucets, light switches, toilet handles and doorknobs — should be disinfected regularly with bleach-based cleaners. Soiled clothing or bedding should be washed separately on the longest possible cycle at 140 degrees Fahrenheit or higher.

Those infected are advised to stay home, avoid preparing food for others until at least 48 hours after symptoms subside and continue disinfecting bathroom surfaces, as the virus can still be shed in stool for up to two weeks after recovery.
