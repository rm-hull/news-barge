## Kim accused Washington of encouraging its regional allies to acquire nuclear-powered submarines and of pledging to share its own nuclear weapons with them.

North Korea's leader has toured facilities that produce fissile material and demanded a bigger nuclear arsenal, days after Pyongyang left Donald Trump's offer of a meeting unanswered.

The message, carried by state news agency KCNA on Thursday, is a fresh sign that Kim has no intention of giving up the weapons programme on which his authoritarian rule rests.

According to the state media, Kim visited two nuclear material production plants on Wednesday, one newly opened and the other being expanded. He also toured a nuclear-powered equipment production base and a nuclear research institute.

Photos showed him and senior military officers walking past dense rows of what appear to be centrifuges used to make weapons-grade uranium and viewing halls with what were believed to be warheads. Some images were blurred.

Pyongyang did not say where the plants are. It has revealed enrichment sites several times since showing one at Yongbyon to visiting American scholars in 2010.

When it disclosed parts of another site in June, outside experts concluded it was probably a second enrichment facility at Yongbyon.

## **Pyongyang: drive to expand arsenal, "irreversible** reality"

Kim presented the visit as a response to growing nuclear threats around the Korean Peninsula accusing Washington of encouraging its regional allies to acquire nuclear-powered submarines and of pledging to share its own nuclear weapons with them.

He described North Korea's status as a nuclear power and its drive to expand the arsenal in both quality and quantity as an "irreversible reality."

He also called for the urgent deployment of low-yield battlefield nuclear weapons across multiple domains, citing the danger on the border with South Korea.

Pyongyang has focused on tactical weapons in recent years as hostility with Seoul has deepened.

South Korea's Defense Ministry said it takes the remarks on battlefield weapons seriously and remains militarily ready, backed by its alliance with the US.

The Joint Chiefs of Staff declined to analyse the facilities, saying the information is confidential and shared with Washington.

## **Trump says meeting with Kim** 'not really the right time'

Kim has accelerated the build-up of his nuclear forces since his high-stakes diplomacy with Trump collapsed in 2019 and has turned towards Cold War-era allies Moscow and Beijing.

Trump again voiced his wish to resume talks while in China last week for the APEC summit, but no meeting took place and Pyongyang did not respond publicly.

Speaking to the New York Post this week, Trump rejected the idea that Kim had ignored him, saying it was "not really the right time" during a busy two days.

He added that he could restart their exchange of letters at any moment, though he admitted they had not written to each other recently.

Kim has previously said he could return to the table if Washington abandons its "hostile policy" and accepts North Korea as a nuclear state. Experts say he is seeking recognition as a nuclear power so that he can demand an end to international sanctions.

Last week, International Atomic Energy Agency chief Rafael Grossi said North Korea has been expanding its original enrichment facility at Yongbyon, calling it a "serious concern."
