A Florida teenager went after his mother with a chainsaw after she confiscated his gaming console following the release of Grand Theft Auto VI, police said.

The incident happened this past weekend in the DeLand area of Volusia County, which encompasses Daytona Beach.

“Deputies responded to a report of a DeLand-area 16-year-old chasing his mom with this chainsaw and threatening to harm family pets after she took away his Xbox,” the Volusia Sheriff’s Office said in a Monday statement.

Police posted an image of a Greenworks electric chainsaw laid out on the ground.

“He was charged with aggravated assault with a deadly weapon and transported to the Volusia Family Resource Center,” the sheriff’s office added.

![A Florida teenager chased his mother with a chainsaw and threatened to injure pets after she took away his Xbox, police said](https://static.the-independent.com/2026/11/26/17/00/926500968_1810804656798838_8019777565104690728_n.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The statement, posted on Facebook, drew hundreds of comments.

“Anyone know if mom is selling that xbox?” wrote one user. Another chimed in: “That's GTA withdrawals. Worse than drugs…”

The identity of the teenager has not been released, and it is unclear what game he may have been playing.

The confrontation came just days after the release of Grand Theft Auto VI, the most popular video game of the year, which launched worldwide on Thursday.

Developed by Rockstar Games and arriving more than a decade after GTA V, the sequel is an open-world crime adventure that lets players roam a sprawling map. While playing, they complete missions and wreak havoc — including by stealing cars and gunning down enemies. It is set in Leonida, a fictionalized version of Florida.

![A Florida teen chased down his mother with a chainsaw when she tried to stop his play Grand Theft Auto VI, police say](https://static.the-independent.com/2026/11/26/17/74665c0af50dd2763ef1e00823951966Y29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzk1Nzk4Mjc0-2.86579906.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Rockstar and its parent company reported $3.79 billion in first-day retail sales, describing it as the biggest entertainment launch ever. The companies also said a record 32.5 million units were sold pre-release.

GTA is most popular with young adults, particularly men and boys.

Ahead of the game's release, *The New York Times* reported that many students and workers planned to stay home on Thursday to play it. The newspaper dubbed the phenomenon “GTA flu.”
