## Ryedale House could close as council reveals plans for new hub

Proposals have been drawn up to relocate services from Ryedale House, pictured, to Stanley Harrison House, creating a new customer hub and bringing services together under one roof. *(Image: North Yorkshire Council)*

Plans have been put forward for a new town centre hub to improve services in Malton and Malton.

North Yorkshire Council has put forward proposals to relocate services from Ryedale House to Stanley Harrison House, creating a new customer hub and bringing services together under one roof in a more accessible town centre location.

The plans form part of wider efforts to make the best use of council-owned buildings, improve the experience of residents and staff, and support the potential redevelopment of the Ryedale House site.

The proposals remain at an early stage and will be presented to members of the Thirsk and Malton area committee on Friday next week (December 4), before being considered by the council’s executive early next year.

North Yorkshire Council’s executive member for property and finance, Cllr Peter Wilkinson, said: “This is about creating better facilities for residents and staff while ensuring we continue to have a strong presence in the heart of Malton.

“Ryedale House has served the area well for many years, but the building is now in poor condition. It is becoming increasingly expensive to run, is significantly underused and would require substantial investment in the future.

“We have looked at a range of options and believe this represents the best way to deliver services in Malton for the future. By relocating front-line services to Stanley Harrison House, we have an opportunity to create a modern hub in a more central location that will make it easier for residents.

“It will also make better use of taxpayers’ money by reducing running costs and supporting the regeneration of the Ryedale House site. This is not about reducing our presence in Malton – it is about creating a sustainable base that keeps services in the heart of the town and supports our ambition for a joined-up, one front door approach to public services.”

In March, councillors approved the purchase of Resolution House as part of wider plans to modernise the council's property estate and reshape service delivery across the Scarborough and Malton area.

The programme aims to reduce the cost of running ageing and underused buildings while creating better workplaces and unlocking that could support future regeneration.

Having completed the first phase of the project in Scarborough, attention is now turning to Malton, where proposals have been developed for a new customer hub at Stanley Harrison House, near the town's railway station and the future of Ryedale House.

Ryedale House currently accommodates a range of council services including the customer hub, registrars service, wedding ceremonies, committee meetings and workspace for back-office staff.

On average, the building now receives about 13 face-to-face customer enquiries a day, while desk occupancy can fall to around 35 per cent, highlighting how much of the building is no longer being fully used.

With the condition of the building continuing to deteriorate and the annual running costs of about £290,000, on top of maintenance costs, moving services from Ryedale House to Stanley Harrison House could generate significant savings of £250,000 a year.

In addition to this £250,000, millions of pounds will be saved through reduced maintenance liabilities.

It has been proposed that the council’s customer hub would relocate to Stanley Harrison House, allowing residents to continue accessing the same services locally from a modern, accessible and more centrally located town centre facility.

The registrars service would also relocate, maintaining existing provision for births, deaths and marriage registrations.

Citizens Advice is already based at Stanley Harrison House and the proposals could create opportunities for other organisations to work more closely alongside council services.

Engagement with staff based at Ryedale House is ongoing, while discussions are continuing with partner organisations including the Department of Work and Pensions and North Yorkshire Police as part of ambitions to maintain a joined-up approach to delivering public services in Malton.

North Yorkshire Cllr Keane Duncan, who represents Norton, said: “I’ve been calling for greater transparency over plans to close Ryedale House and working to ensure council, police and Jobcentre services are protected in our area.

“Today’s public report is welcome. It gives us more information and shows local concerns are being listened to - but it also shows there is still lots to be worked out.

“We have a duty to look after our council staff, many of whom have worked at Ryedale House for years. They must be properly consulted and supported through these changes.

“Where we can keep teams based locally, we should. And for those who do need to move, this should not happen prematurely. We must see a fully agreed plan for North Yorkshire Police and the Jobcentre first.

“I am seeking further details and assurances as I continue to stand up for the future of services in Malton, Norton and the wider Ryedale area.”
