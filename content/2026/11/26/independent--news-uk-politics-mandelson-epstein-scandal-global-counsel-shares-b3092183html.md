Peter Mandelson was promised a “light touch” approach over his potential conflicts of interest by the government despite officials raising numerous concerns over his former clients and shares in a lobbying firm, new bombshell files reveal.

The latest tranche of files on the scandal surrounding the former US ambassador’s sacking, released on Thursday, also reveal that the disgraced peer failed to sell his shares in the firm Global Counsel, despite being expected to do so.

Lord Mandelson was sacked last September over his relationship with a paedophile financier, after emails showed he sent supportive messages to Jeffrey Epstein even as he was facing jail for sex offences.

![Lord Mandelson was sacked as US ambassador due to his links with Jeffrey Epstein (James Manning/PA)](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/26/13/01M8BTN5N4WQ3TA4G2CQ88TB86.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

No 10 said the emails revealed “materially different” information from what was known when Lord Mandelson was appointed to the key role of ambassador.

The scandal played a significant role in the eventual resignation of Sir Keir, with questions raised about how much the then-premier knew about their ties when he made the appointment.

The key revelations from the papers are:

* Mandelson meddled in Starmer’s cabinet reshuffle – days before being sacked over Epstein scandal
* He amended a key speech, including references to China, after taking advice from a director of his lobbying firm
* He tried to set up an ‘under the radar’ meeting Mandelson between a Trump adviser and the lobbying firm
* A White House official told him his reputation as the “Prince of Darkness” could not be “farther from the truth”

On Thursday, prime minister Andy Burnham vowed to tackle the “vested interests” he said contributed to the scandal, promising “political reform” as part of his 10-year plan, as he warned “there’s a lot further to go” in changing the political culture that gave rise to the appointment.

Lord Mandelson was arrested earlier this year on suspicion of misconduct in public office, following claims he leaked sensitive government information to Epstein. He denies wrongdoing.

The documents show that the head of the Foreign Office Sir Olly Robbins was concerned Mandelson did not sell his shares in Global Counsel, which advised high-profile clients including Chinese-owned TikTok, US tech firm Palantir and pharmaceutical giant GSK.

A letter sent last March, after Mandelson was already in post, shows that the Foreign Office thought the “ideal” solution to prevent a conflict of interest would be for Global Counsel to buy Mandelson out.

However, it adds, the company has not made an offer “Lord Mandelson considers commensurate with the fair value” of the shares.

Sir Olly later wrote to Mandelson setting out “a process we [could] agree on … that protects you against perceptions of benefitting GC in some way while in public office and for the (hopefully short) period you remain a significant shareholder”.

Sir Olly added: “If this looks sensible to you, I will sign and send and we can get this up and running in a light touch way.”

While he still held shares he tried to organise an “under the radar” meeting between a member of the firm and an economic adviser to Donald Trump, according to the documents.

Earlier the US official has told him: “ It was a delight to meet you... "Prince of Darkness" couldn't be farther from the truth!”

The files also show that while in office Lord Mandelson sent a copy of his speech to a director of the lobbying firm, who told him to tone down “parts of your apologia of the Trump administration”.

![Mandelson sent supportive messages to Jeffrey Epstein as he was facing jail for sex offences](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/05/19/16/83614e97f0967ab2f548bda74016dfd1Y29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzc5Mjg4NjA1-2.67647057.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Mandelson later replied: “Thanks. I will look at changes.” He also added that he would “soften” the language on China.

The speech included a call for a US-UK technology partnership to win a battle with China he said would shape this century.

Emails also show that Foreign Office officials saw the vetting process as something not intended as a “retrospective on someone’s whole life”.

On September 11 last year, the day Lord Mandelson was sacked, chief property and security officer Ian Collard told No 10’s private secretary for foreign affairs Alisa Terry: “I would note that the vetting system predominantly looks at one’s close contacts of recent years. It is not intended (in all areas of review at least) as a retrospective on someone’s whole life.”

The same day *The Independent* contacted Sir Keir Starmer’s then director of communications, Tim Allan, and later that same day ran a front-page story revealing that Lord Mandelson had failed vetting.

Questions over how Sir Keir could have failed to have known about the issue when his top spin doctor was told directly and the story became headline news contributed to his fall from office earlier this year.

The documents also show the Labour grandee weighing in on Sir Keir’s reshuffle days before he was sacked as ambassador.

In an exchange of WhatsApps on September 5 2025 between Mandelson and Sir Keir’s chief of staff at the time, Morgan McSweeney, he tells Mr McSweeney to “solve your Darren issue” – referring to former chief secretary to the prime minister Darren Jones.

He also wrote: “Are you taking peter Kyle away….?” and asked where Mr McSweeney was, telling him: “I am in upstairs meeting room”.

![Sir Olly Robbins, the former top civil servant at the Foreign Office, suggested a process to police conflicts of interest ‘in a light touch way’ (Dominic Lipinski/PA)](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/07/13/11/a8f2790ba95cadeaf6a7545007f6970aY29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzg0MDIyNzcz-2.84292036.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The messages were exchanged three days after the first batch of Epstein files were released by US lawmakers.

Days later, September 9 2025, the US government released documents which showed Mandelson describing Epstein as “my best pal”, and sent him other supportive messages, even as Epstein faced jail for sex offences.

He was sacked as US ambassador on September 11 amid growing pressure on the then-prime minister to remove him from office.

Chair of the Commons Foreign Affairs Committee and Labour MP Dame Emily Thornberry said: “Today’s publication is yet more evidence of just how wholly inappropriate the appointment of Peter Mandelson as Ambassador to the US was.”

“Officials in the FCDO seemed to be well aware of the risks that Mandelson’s business interests posed. This didn’t stop Mandelson from continuing to consult staff from Global Counsel, allowing them to advise on the content of speeches and offering to set up meetings in the Embassy in Washington... we the British people put a great deal of trust in those who seek to keep us safe. And we trust that they will do it efficiently and effectively and with discipline. We have not seen that in relation to the Mandelson scandal. It is becoming increasingly clear that trust we've placed in some people has been abused.”

She added that there were still “unanswered questions as to how the supposed mitigations were handled in practice. What meetings was he restricted from, what information was withheld from him? Why was he able to organise and attend unrecorded meetings with Palantir, a well-known client of Global Counsel, when he still retained a stake in the business?”
