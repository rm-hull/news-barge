A 44-year-old man who was trying to help his son navigate an icy patch of a southern California mountain trail slipped and fell roughly 500ft to his death, according to local authorities.

Guillermo Batres, an avid hiker, set off on Sunday morning to summit Mount Baden-Powell in the San Gabriel mountains with his teenage son, according to Steve De Jong, a lieutenant with the Los Angeles county sheriff’s department.

Batres recorded a video on Sunday, obtained by the Los Angeles Times, where he panned across the wintry mountain trail, surrounded by green pines. His wife had turned back early into the hike.

In the clip, Batres’s son, Sebastián, could be seen holding trekking poles and smiling faintly beneath his jacket, which was bundled up toward his nose.

The video cut off abruptly as Batres warned his son to be careful in Spanish.

Sebastián had slid down the side of a hill and was clinging on to a tree, De Jong said.

Batres attempted to aid his son, before losing his footing on the ice and falling down the mountain into a gulch.

The Los Angeles county medical examiner’s office determined Batres died from blunt force injuries sustained in an accident.

Two hikers who passed by Sebastián after the deadly fall helped him put spikes on his shoes and get to safety, according to De Jong.

Jennifer Morse, Batres’s niece, wrote in a GoFundMe that their family has been left “shocked, grieving and facing an unimaginable loss”.
