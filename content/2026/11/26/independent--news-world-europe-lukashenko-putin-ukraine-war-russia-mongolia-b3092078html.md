Belarusian president Alexander Lukashenko appeared to mock Vladimir Putin’s historical claim to Ukrainian land in a rare criticism of his key ally, warning that by his logic Russian land should be returned to Mongolia.

It was a clear rebuke to the reasoning behind the Russian president’s claim that some European land belongs to “historical Russia”, and marked a highly unusual intervention from Lukashenko - who let Russian forces use his country’s territory for the February 2022 invasion of Ukraine.

“Listen I am a historian, our state was born in the Middle Ages in the 11th and 12th centuries, from Kievan Rus’. That is an indisputable fact,” Lukashenko said in the interview with *Le Point*.

“And to say today that this land has been Russian since the dawn of time. Well, they were Russian at one point,” he said. “But if we look further back, Russian lands were once Mongol and Tatar. You remember that? Then we'd have to give all of Russia back to Mongolia.”

![Russian President Vladimir Putin and Belarus President Alexander Lukashenko are close allies](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/26/10/2304125745..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

He added: “Putin understands this perfectly well.”

The Russian president has repeatedly justified his invasion of Ukraine by claiming that Ukraine and the former territories belonging to the Russian Empire and the Soviet Union belong to what he describes as “historical Russia”.

During the interview, Lukashenko also discussed the situation on the frontline and ongoing efforts to end the war in Ukraine.

He said Russian forces were making slow progress, but were winning the war.

"President Putin, he is still winning at the front. Be that as it may — it's him," Lukashenko said.

![From left: Kazakhstan's President Kassym-Jomart Tokayev, Kyrgyzstan's President Sadyr Japarov, CSTO Secretary General Taalatbek Masadykov, Russian President Vladimir Putin, Tajikistan's President Emomali Rahmon and Belarusian President Alexander Lukashenko, attend the Collective Security Treaty Organization (CSTO) earlier this month](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/11/22/download.-2..jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

He also made a proposal that he could join Ukrainian president Volodymyr Zelensky for trilateral talks with Putin, claiming this would be more effective than European involvement.

Putin has long suggested that Zelensky come to Moscow to negotiate a resolution of the conflict. Zelensky rejects such a proposal, while saying he is prepared to meet the Russian leader in "more neutral" locations.

"Volodya (Volodymyr) Zelensky and I could simply go and fly to Moscow. The two of us. As a guarantor of his safety, I could sit with him and fly to Moscow," Lukashenko said in comments reported by Belarus' state Belta news agency.

In response to a suggestion that Zelensky was unlikely to agree, he said: "If he doesn't agree, let him keep fighting. Let him go to the front with an automatic weapon and fight. The war is going on. It needs to be stopped. And we could come to an agreement."

Criticising the ongoing efforts to bring a war to the end, Lukashenko added: "We would reach an agreement faster than with you (Europeans) regarding the end of this war and the future arrangements and so on. And people would accept it."

Zelensky has denounced Lukashenko's role in the conflict and in June threatened Ukrainian action unless the Belarusian leader removed what he described as tracking equipment to help Russia launch attacks.
