## The other 16 crew members are reported to have survived. The authorities have activated emergency plans and are monitoring the risk of marine pollution.

A sailor died in the early hours of Thursday after a vessel carrying around 7,000 metric tonnes of diesel fuel sank off the coast of Ibiza, according to emergency services in the Balearic Islands.

The ship sank between 25 and 30 nautical miles north of the island.

The other 16 crew members survived the sinking, which prompted emergency protocols to be activated because of the risk of a fuel spill.

The Balearic government's director general for Emergencies and Interior, Pablo Gárriz, told reporters that the alert was received at around 1:20 am from Ibiza's harbour master. He said the vessel had suffered a breach which eventually caused it to sink.

The accident prompted a response from Spain's maritime rescue service by sea and air, with support from several ships in the area.

Of the 17 crew members rescued, six were taken to Ibiza airport and another was taken directly to Can Misses Hospital, where he was later pronounced dead, Gárriz said.

## Balearic Islands activate civil protection plan over spill risk

The authorities’ main concern now is the possible environmental consequences of the sinking, given the amount of fuel the vessel, named Med Emre, was carrying.

"We are monitoring the possible environmental consequences that this accident may have," Gárriz said.

The sinking led to the activation of the National Maritime Pollution Plan at level 2, while the Balearic government activated the Territorial Civil Protection Plan of the Balearic Islands (PLATERBAL) at level 0.

The full scale of any environmental impact is not yet known, and authorities are assessing whether additional measures will be needed.

"Right now we are waiting for the assessments that are being made and for the possible evolution of this episode," Gárriz said.
