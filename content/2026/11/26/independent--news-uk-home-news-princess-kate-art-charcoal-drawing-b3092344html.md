The Princess of Wales spent an afternoon trying her hand at charcoal art and grinding natural pigments during a visit to a charity in the Scottish Borders.

Kate was at the Hugo Burge Foundation in Berwickshire on Thursday afternoon to celebrate the healing power of creativity.

The charity is dedicated to supporting craftsmanship and creativity across the UK.

It was established by the late Hugo Burge, who dedicated the last decade of his life to creating a cultural hub on the Marchmont Estate in Berwickshire.

He founded what would become the Hugo Burge Foundation in January 2022 to support local makers, champion heritage crafts and widen access to the arts.

Kate was greeted by chief executive Lucy Brown and creative director James Fox before setting out on a nearly two-hour tour of the estate.

![Kate used a mortar and pestle during the visit](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/26/16/17fb24f7e34f232fb78b531e1965a9aaY29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzk1Nzk0Mjk3-2.86614466.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

She walked through the walled garden, which is interspersed with striking works of art, and met head gardener Toby Loveday.

In the glass houses, she was introduced to charcoal artist Tansy Lee Moir, who was leading a mark-making with charcoal course.

Kate tried using natural tools, such as feathers and plant stems, to create lines and impressions on paper.

A participant was overheard telling the princess that charcoal art was “powerful” and “mindful”.

The princess was then introduced to natural pigment artist Siobhan McLaughlin in her residency workshop.

Ms McLaughlin stitches together materials to create her canvases and uses natural pigments she has gathered from the earth to paint them.

![Kate meeting head gardener Toby Loveday during a visit to The Hugo Burge Foundation](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/26/16/05a91737c90a9f756376b4b865f7951fY29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzk1Nzk1NjIx-2.86614066.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

She spoke to Kate about the healing and meditative properties of art and how it has played a role in her own life, before letting the princess try her hand at using the materials on paper.

Kate also used a mortar and pestle to grind some of the materials Ms McLaughlin will use to make her pigments.

The tour continued to the silversmithing studio, where Kate met senior silversmith Katie Watson and current springboard silversmith tenants Freya Jeavons, Jessica Awaritefe, and Iona hall.

Her final stop was the foundation's common room, where she had a cup of tea and a chat with more artists and residents to talk about their work.

The visit comes after Kate described how being “faced with your own mortality” means accepting life is not infinite, which “softens your heart”, in an upcoming documentary.

![The princess enjoying a cup of tea and a chat with artists](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/26/16/0b11fdf00687d46d1ea260ee32094ceaY29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzk1Nzk1Nzc5-2.86614603.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

A trailer released at the weekend showed her undertaking the Three Peaks Challenge – climbing the highest mountains in Scotland, England and Wales within 24 hours – and reflecting on her cancer experience alongside others navigating their own health journeys.

Kate was diagnosed with cancer after undergoing major abdominal surgery in 2024.

She had chemotherapy at the Royal Marsden Hospital in Chelsea, south-west London, and announced she was in remission at the start of 2025.

The 30-minute documentary, *Heal*, will premiere on the BBC in the UK on Sunday 29 November, before airing internationally via NBCUniversal in the US on 30 November, Channel 9 in Australia on 1 December and CTV in Canada on 7 December.
