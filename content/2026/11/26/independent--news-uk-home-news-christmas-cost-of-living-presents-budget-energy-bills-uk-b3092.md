Children in households across the UK are asking for less this Christmas because they understand that money is tight, stark new research has suggested.

A poll of low-income parents by Action for Children and Nationwide found that more than three-quarters (77 per cent) said their child has changed, reduced or stopped asking for presents due to financial pressures.

The findings come as inflation hit a one-year high of 3.6 per cent in October, driven largely by rising energy costs. Regulator Ofgem confirmed this week that its price cap will rise by 26 per cent from January, adding £443 to a typical annual bill to reach £2,166.

While over eight in 10 (84 per cent) of the 2,000 parents surveyed said they are actively trying to hide their money worries from their children, the cost of living squeeze is forcing families into difficulty in the run-up to December 25.

There are currently 3.5 million working-age parents living in poverty in the UK, and around four million children, according to the latest statistics from the Joseph Rowntree Foundation.

![Children in households across the UK are asking for less this Christmas because they understand that money is tight, stark new research has suggested](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/26/15/17/christmas-tree-decorating-stock.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&auto=webp)

Louise, a 25-year-old single mother from London, said trying to shield her six-year-old daughter from financial stress is a constant struggle.

She added: “I was told and it was hard. So I don’t want [my daughter] to know that there are hard times and essentially that you haven’t got enough. It’s devastating.”

Despite working full-time at an energy company, Louise still struggles with the cost of living. She explains how she has to navigate situations like her daughter asking for a new iPad for Christmas.

“I try to keep the magic alive by not telling her that we can’t afford this right now and try to say things like ‘let’s get this instead’ or ‘maybe wait a few weeks’,” she said.

“For me it was taken away quickly at an early age and so as a parent I am trying to preserve that magic of Christmas.”

According to Action for Children’s survey, one in six (17 per cent) parents have skipped or reduced meals to pay for Christmas, while the same proportion have borrowed money.

Nearly one in five (19 per cent) have turned down their heating or taken on extra work hours, and 16 per cent have gone without new clothes or shoes.

![One in 10 parents surveyed say they intend to use the government’s newly-announced £100 energy payment to pay off Christmas debt](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/20/07/e66fd0e31127cfd401e90aa3a5096ecbY29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzk1MTg1MDY0-2.86331177.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Almost all those surveyed (98 per cent) have altered plans for their child’s main gift this year – with 36 per cent opting for a cheaper alternative, 32 per cent buying secondhand, and 36 per cent turning to credit or buy-now-pay-later schemes.

Among polled parents expecting the government’s £100 household energy payment, one in 10 said they plan to use it to pay off Christmas debt.

But post-Budget analysis by *The Independent* with the Resolution Foundation found that just under 700,000 of the poorest households are set to miss out on the payment due to the decision to link it to “outdated” council tax bands.

Amid the growing financial pressures, Action for Children says it has seen a 27 per cent jump in requests to its Nationwide-funded Family Fund over the past year. Requests for clothing alone – including winter coats and school shoes – have surged by 65 per cent in that time.

Catherine Joyce, national director for England North at Action for Children, said: “The magic of Christmas begins in childhood. It should be about creating memories and positive experiences, but four million children in the UK now live in poverty. Too many families are at breaking point and every day in our services we see families making impossible choices.”
