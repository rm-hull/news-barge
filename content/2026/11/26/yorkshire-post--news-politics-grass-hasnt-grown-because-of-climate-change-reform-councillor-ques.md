# 'Grass hasn’t grown because of climate change' - Reform Councillor questions lawnmower purchase

## A Reform UK councillor has questioned why a council has purchased a new fleet of lawnmowers – arguing that global warming means grass doesn’t grow.

Members of Bradford Council’s Keighley area committee were discussing the parks service at a meeting last week (Nov 19), when the debate about grass growth took place.

Officers had told the committee that the department had recently purchased new ride on mowers to replace the service’s ageing fleet.

But Councillor Angela Baker questioned this purchase, saying the need for council grass cutting was declining due to climate change.

![Councillor Angela Baker](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOmY2MTE4ZDhjLWIwMmUtNGExMS04MDMwLTg4YjJkMTMyNmRmZjoyMjQ4NWJiZi01YThhLTRlMjItOTU0OC01NTcyOTVmNWRjNzA=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


A report to the committee said: “A new fleet of ride on mowers were purchased in April 2026, improving downtime rates for parks fleet and reducing the issues previously faced with grass cutting.”

Members heard that the existing mowers were over a decade old, and often had to be taken out of rotation for repairs.

At the meeting Catherine Smith, head of parks and street cleansing services, said the new mowers were ride on John Deere models.

She told members that grassed areas including highway verges, parks, open cemeteries and recreational grounds were usually cut 10 times a year.

Numerous droughts and heatwaves meant that in 2026 these cuttings were reduced to seven.

Coun Baker questioned why new mowers had been purchased, saying: “Grass hasn’t grown because of climate change.”

Mrs Smith said: “The previous mowers were over 10 years old – they were off the road more than they were on the road.”

Coun Baker replied: “The grass literally hasn’t grown.”

She pointed out that she keeps horses, and had seen first-hand how grass growth was much less this year.

Mrs Smith replied: “Just because we had a hot summer this year doesn’t mean we’ll have one next year. If we didn’t cut the grass we’d receive a lot more complaints from residents.”

Coun Baker replied: “But why buy new mowers?”

Mrs Smith said: “We still need to cut grass, it does grow.”

David Priestley, parks and cleansing manager for Keighley: “The life span of a mower is around five years before they become uneconomic to run.

“We’ve waited 10 years to replace them as a way of saving money. Next year could be really wet, with grass growing as fast as we’re cutting it.”

The committee was told the mowers were paid for through the department’s existing budget.
