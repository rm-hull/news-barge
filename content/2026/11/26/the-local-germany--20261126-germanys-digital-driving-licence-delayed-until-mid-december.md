Germany's long-awaited digital driving licence is running late. It had been expected by the end of November, but the Federal Ministry of Transport has now told the German Press Agency (dpa) to expect it by mid-December.

The idea is to make everyday life easier for drivers and to move more government paperwork onto our phones. Here's what you need to know.

**What's changing for drivers?**

Drivers will soon be able to store their German driving licence in the i-Kfz app, run by the Federal Motor Transport Authority (KBA).

The app is free, works on Android and iOS, and can already hold your digital vehicle registration certificate. In theory, once your licence is in there as well, showing your phone at a traffic stop will be enough. The plastic card can then stay at home.

Using the app is also entirely optional. The physical licence will remain valid.

To set it up you'll need an NFC-enabled smartphone, plus an ID card with the online ID function switched on and its six-digit PIN. The app reportedly doesn't run on Huawei phones or older Motorola models.

You hold your ID card against the phone – the app confirms who you are and pulls your licence data from the official register.

A photo or scan of your licence won't count. And foreign residents should also bear in mind that the scheme is aimed at holders of a German card licence issued from 1999 onwards. You won’t be able to load a foreign licence into the app, in other words.

In addition, the digital licence is only valid only in Germany for the time being. Anyone planning to drive abroad will still have to carry the plastic card.

**Why has the launch been delayed?**

The Ministry says the technical requirements are in place and the pilot scheme went well. The hold-up is the photo. To create a digital licence, the KBA draws on the central driving licence and driver fitness registers.

It can also fetch your photo from the passport and ID authorities, but making that work reportedly needs a little more time.

It’s possible this issue won’t have been fully resolved when the new service finally launches. If that’s the case, you'll still need to carry some form of official ID with a photo in the interim, so that police can match the name on the digital licence with your face.

**What happens if I'm stopped without my licence?**

Unlike in the UK, where drivers aren't generally obliged to carry their licence, in Germany you're legally required to have it with you when you drive and hand it over when asked. Forgetting it is an offence, but it’s a minor one – so there's no need to panic.

Generally you'll face a €10 warning fine but no points on your licence or a driving ban.

After the app is live, a dead phone battery could count as not having your licence with you. So it might be a good idea to keep a charging cable in the car – or the physical card as a back-up.
