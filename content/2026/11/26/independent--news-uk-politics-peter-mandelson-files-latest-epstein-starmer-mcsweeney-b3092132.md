The latest tranche of files on Lord Peter Mandelson show the peer weighing in on then-prime minister Sir Keir Starmer’s reshuffle days before he was sacked as US ambassador.

A third release of documents on the appointment of Lord Mandelson as US ambassador, published on Thursday, has revealed a short exchange of WhatsApps on September 5 2025 between Lord Mandelson and Sir Keir’s chief of staff at the time, Morgan McSweeney.

He tells Mr McSweeney to “solve your Darren issue” – referring to former chief secretary to the prime minister Darren Jones.

The messages were exchanged three days after the first batch of Epstein filed released by US lawmakers. Lord Mandelson was sacked as US ambassador a week after his messages to Mr McSweeney.

Mr McSweeney has previously denied Lord Mandelson was involved in the restructure of Sir Keir’s frontbench team, telling MPs earlier this year he “did not respond to any of Mandelson’s texts”.

According to the new documents, Lord Mandelson wrote: “Are you taking peter Kyle away….?”

![The latest tranche of files on Lord Peter Mandelson show the peer weighing in on then-prime minister Sir Keir Starmer’s reshuffle days after his close links to Epstein were publicly revealed](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/26/11/01M8BTPMZXVZB0ZFR4TH620R49.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

In a second message, he said: “You should solve your Darren issue and put him at DSIT (Department for Science, Innovation and Technology).”

Around 45 minutes later, he asked where Mr McSweeney was and added: “I am in upstairs meeting room”.

Much later in the evening, Mr McSweeney replied: “I think we seized that day quite well.”

Lord Mandelson was inside No 10 on the day of the cabinet reshuffle, but Mr McSweeney has denied his “ideas” affected the outcome.

Days later, September 9 2025, the US government released documents which showed Mandelson describing Epstein as “my best pal”, and sent him other supportive messages, even as Epstein faced jail for sex offences.

He was sacked as US ambassador on September 11 amid growing pressure on the then-prime minister to remove him from office.

Previously-released documents showed Mr Jones, then-chief secretary to the prime minister, contacting the peer to tell him he had done a “great job” on the day of his firing.

The latest release of papers cover the vetting process into Lord Mandelson before he was sent to the US.

![Mr McSweeney has previously denied Lord Mandelson was involved in the restructure of Sir Keir’s frontbench team, telling MPs earlier this year he “did not respond to any of Mandelson’s texts”.](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/06/01/19/2277692443..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Downing Street said the prime minister’s thoughts “are, and always will be, with Jeffrey Epstein’s victims”.

“And the appointment of Peter Mandelson, as you know, was made under the previous administration,” the prime minister’s spokesperson said.

“But we are clear: nothing like this can ever happen again. Which is why we will ensure lessons are learnt, and the release of these documents today is is part of our ongoing commitment to comply with the humble address in full. And we remain committed to supporting the ongoing police investigation.”

Elsewhere in the documents released on Thursday, internal emails showed that Foreign Office officials saw the vetting process as something that is not intended as a “retrospective on someone’s whole life”.

On September 11 last year, the day Lord Mandelson was sacked as ambassador, chief property and security officer Ian Collard told Number 10’s private secretary for foreign affairs Alisa Terry: “I would note that the vetting system predominantly looks at one’s close contacts of recent years.

“It is not intended (in all areas of review at least) as a retrospective on someone’s whole life.”

In a different exchange, a director of his lobbying firm told him to tone down “parts of your apologia of the Trump administration”.

Ahead of his speech for the Ditchley Foundation annual lecture, the email from August last year added: “Scepticism of it is now so deep – at least in the public policy-interested world outside government – that it would be counterproductive/unpersuasive if you came across as having drunk the Kool Aid.”
