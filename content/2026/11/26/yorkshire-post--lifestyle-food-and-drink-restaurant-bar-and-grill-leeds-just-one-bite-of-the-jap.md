# Restaurant Bar and Grill, Leeds: ‘Just one bite of the Japanese dumplings was enough to convert me’

## It took just one bite of the Japanese dumplings at Restaurant Bar and Grill in Leeds and I was converted.

I was invited on behalf of The Yorkshire Post to try the new Japanese and British inspired menu at Restaurant Bar and Grill in Leeds last week.

As someone who doesn’t often eat Japanese food, this was a little out of my comfort zone, that’s why I invited my friend along who enjoys this Asian cuisine.

We were first served drinks and as it was a ‘school night’ as it were, I went for an alcohol-free sparkling rosé called Lady Zero Rosé - which had a refreshing, fruity taste with a slightly tangy edge.

![Liana Jacob at Restaurant Bar and Grill, Leeds.](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/jpim-static/image/2026/11/26/13/17/NLYP-26-11-2026-Liana-Jacob-at-Restaurant-Bar-and-Grill-Leeds-YORupload.jpg?trim=176,57,69,61&width=640&quality=65)


For starters, I chose the Gyoza Wagyu Beef, these were Japanese pan-fried dumplings filled with Wagyu beef, aromatics, and vegetables, and let me tell you, I became a convert after one bite. The spices and flavours bursting out were delightful.

Normally, I don’t have a starter as it manages to fill me up before my main meal, but with this dish, it was light and went down so easily that I was still ravenous afterwards.

My friend chose the Kimcheese Croquettes with a molten cheese centre and she told me that the cheesiness balanced with the tang and hint of chilli from the kimchi.

I tried the pickled Daikon on the side and I agreed with her that it was satisfyingly crunchy and refreshing, helping to break up the heaviness of my meaty dumplings.

For the main dish, I was tempted by the RBG Cheeseburger, a beef rib patty topped with bone marrow onions, miso mayo, burger cheese, and gherkin, and served with beef dripping chips.

This was a substantial meal with a lot of flavours to dissect - I considered this a ‘posh burger’. The size surprised me, it was smaller than I expected in a great way. One thing that I don’t enjoy about burgers is their thickness and how unmanageable they are to eat.

As for the filling, I thoroughly enjoyed the beef; it was cooked perfectly and the flavours coming out were rich in all the right ways. Personally, I felt the miso mayo to be too rich for my taste, though this was remedied with the juiciness of the gherkin.

![Gyoza Wagyu Beef and Kimcheese Croquettes at RBG.](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/jpim-static/image/2026/11/26/13/27/NLYP-26-11-2026-Gyoza-Wagyu-Beef-and-Kimcheese-Croquettes-at-RBG-YORupload.jpg?trim=92,52,146,55&width=640&quality=65)


At first, I struggled to pick out the ‘beef dripping’ element of the chips, but as I got close to the bottom of the bowl it was more evident and I enjoyed it.

My friend had the Crispy Tofu Salad and thought it was like a flavour bomb with a vibrant dressing mix of heat from the fresh red and green chillies, along with sweet, sour, salty notes and the crispy tofu cubes coated in five spice were a good contrast.

I was very eager for the dessert, which is very unusual for someone who can’t even stomach two courses. The Chocolate Brownie, paired with white chocolate, sour cherries, and pistachio ice cream, was calling my name even before we had ordered our starters - and it certainly didn’t disappoint. The sweetness of it helped to break up the heaviness of the overall meal.

![The Cheeseburger at RBG.](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/jpim-static/image/2026/11/26/13/31/NLYP-26-11-2026-The-Cheeseburger-at-RBG-YORupload.jpg?trim=98,61,145,54&width=640&quality=65)


My favourite spice is cinnamon - not just because it reminds me of the festive season, I could indulge in cinnamon-based food throughout the year. The cherries were bursting with a cinnamon flavour and it perfectly balanced with the chocolate of the brownie. I also really enjoyed the pistachio ice cream.

Overall the meal had a perfect balance of flavours that suit every palette, enough that I will be keeping an eye on other Japanese restaurants to try other traditional dishes.
