Sampling results following the U.S. Army's detonation of a stockpile of lead azide in Iowa are expected to start coming in “as early as tomorrow,” the U.S. Environmental and Protection Agency said in a statement Thursday.

EPA personnel and contractors took over 1,500 samples of air, water, soil and dust before the detonation of the nation’s entire stockpile of lead azide, a chemical used to make explosives. After the detonation, they took hundreds of samples including from homes, parks and drinking water, with more samples expected to be collected Thursday, the EPA said.

Samples were sent to labs for testing, the EPA said.

“The Trump EPA will continue its presence on the ground for as long as needed to ensure the proper environmental monitoring is taking place following the successful controlled detonation,” the agency said in a statement.

The EPA said there continues to be “zero” airborne lead detected in the region since Wednesday’s controlled detonation.

In addition to lead exposure, the EPA is also testing for other compounds that could have been released in the explosion.

“The agency is testing for everything we were anticipating could potentially be released as a result of the controlled detonation,” the EPA said.

The Army said detonating the stockpile was the safest option because it was degrading and might explode uncontrollably as the weather got colder.
