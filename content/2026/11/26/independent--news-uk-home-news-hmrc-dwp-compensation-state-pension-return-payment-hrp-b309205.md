Hundreds of thousands of parents are missing out on a state-pension top-up averaging £8,000, HMRC has confirmed, as it launched a new wave of letters urging them to act.

More than 300,000 letters are being sent to people who may have been underpaid due to a historical record-keeping error after a similar drive saw low response rates.

The issue relates to Home Responsibilities Protection (HRP), which was available between 1978 and 2010 for people receiving Child Benefit or with caring responsibilities.

The scheme reduced the number of qualifying National Insurance years needed for a basic state pension, accounting for time spent out of work to care for children or family members.

HRP was meant to be applied automatically to Child Benefit claimants, most of whom were mothers.

However, in 2021, the Department for Work and Pensions (DWP) found the protection had not been recorded in an estimated 194,000 cases, largely because National Insurance numbers were not always recorded on Child Benefit claims made before May 2000.

HMRC has confirmed that the average arrears payout for successful claimants so far has been around £8,000.

Official DWP figures published in May 2025 showed that out of 370,018 letters sent during the earlier phase of the campaign, 52,935 applications were processed – meaning nearly 86 per cent of recipients did not apply.

By March 2025, 12,379 underpayments had been identified, with £104m paid out in arrears at an average of £8,377 per person.

Not everyone who is missing HRP from their National Insurance records will see their state pension amount increase, HMRC advises, for instance if they already have enough qualifying years or have inherited a higher state pension from their spouse.

Since the campaign began, more than 25,000 people have had HRP added to their records, with only around half of these receiving an arrears payment.

The DWP’s latest annual report shows that between April 2025 and March 2026, just over 800 pensioners received an HRP correction totalling £7m in arrears.

Official research found that many non-respondents feared the letters were scams, did not understand how to apply online, or worried that acting could reduce their current pension entitlement.

To address these issues, HMRC’s latest batch of letters will now include a physical paper claim form so recipients can apply directly by post.

The tax authority has also issued a warning over third-party claims companies charging fees to submit applications on pensioners' behalf, with the process being free when done directly.

An HMRC spokesperson said: “We regularly encourage people to check their eligibility for missing Home Responsibilities Protection, including writing to hundreds of thousands of people. We're now sending further letters inviting customers to check their entitlement and apply directly with HMRC.

“Claiming via GOV.UK is quick and easy, and those who are successful receive 100% of any money owed rather than paying large fees to third parties."

Anyone who believes they may be affected can check their eligibility and apply for free via the online tool on GOV.UK – which takes around 15 minutes – or request a paper form by calling HMRC’s National Insurance helpline on 0300 200 3500.
