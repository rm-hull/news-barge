*The Independent*’s Christmas campaign has already raised nearly £40,000 to help tackle the issue of youth homelessness in Britain.

The major milestone has been reached less than two weeks after the campaign was launched thanks to the generosity of our readers.

Project Nest, a partnership between young person’s charity EveryYouth and *The Independent*, aims to fund the renovation of three dilapidated houses in Fife, Sheffield and Basingstoke, which will be transformed into accommodation for young people struggling with homelessness.

It brings us one step closer to making the project a reality.

The campaign received a major boost from philanthropists Mike and Helen Brown, who pledged to match all individual donations to the campaign for the first £10,000 raised.

![The Independent’s Christmas campaign has reached £40,000 in donations](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/25/13/45/project-nest.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

It has also received backing from scores of high-profile names, including prime minister Andy Burnham.

Writing in *The Independent*, he said: “I’m proud to support* The Independent*’s Christmas appeal for Project Nest, and the brilliant work EveryYouth does for young people with nowhere safe to call home.

“My first instruction as prime minister was to end rough sleeping. We have to stop accepting it as a fact of life. But government can’t do it alone, and ending homelessness for good means making sure young people never reach the streets in the first place.

“That’s what Project Nest is about: not just putting a roof over young people’s heads, but giving them the support to get into education, training or work and build the lives they deserve.”

![Prime minister Andy Burnham threw his support behind the campaign](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/25/15/2026-11-25T142032Z_1650817513_RC2BCOA8JJZC_RTRMADP_3_BRITAIN-POLITICS-BURNHAM-SOCIALMEDIA.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

BBC radio presenter Shaun Keaveny said: “As my eldest gets ready to leave the nest of our home, I think more and more about how hard it must be for a lot of young people to go out into the world in 2026 with all its complications; especially ones who don’t come from a safe and supportive family background.

“Project Nest wants to tackle these problems directly. It takes derelict properties and re-purposes them for young people in need to live in so they can train, gain skills, build their confidence, to save money so they can make a start in adult life.

“Project Nest understands that home is more than just walls, it’s a sense of safety, and for a young person who has perhaps experienced homelessness or neglect, those things must feel a very long way away. It will provide safe homes for young people to live in, support and guidance to pursue training and employment, all whilst renovating wasted buildings.

“A total win-win situation.If you possibly can, please support this campaign by donating, spreading the word, or reading young people’s stories.

Among other high-profile backers of the campaign is actor and presenter Baroness Floella Benjamin, who said: “I truly believe Project Nest is a brilliant initiative that turns empty, forgotten properties into safe, welcoming homes where young people experiencing homelessness can work or train, save towards independence, get support and find stability. It’s about so much more than providing a roof over someone’s head – it’s about giving them hope, opportunity and a fresh start.”

She added: “Giving gives so much satisfaction and is amazingly rewarding, knowing that every contribution will help change a young person’s life and will show what's possible when we all come together. Childhood lasts a lifetime so please join me in supporting this wonderful appeal and donate whatever you can.”

More than 123,000 young people were homeless or at risk of homelessness in the UK in 2024 to 2025, according to Centrepoint. At the same time, more than 700,000 properties across the UK stand empty long-term, wasting crucial housing stock that could provide a young person with the stability they need to launch their independence.

Our campaign, which is aiming to raise £200,000, will tackle both these problems head-on, transforming dilapidated properties blighting city centres into purpose built spaces that will give young people a safe place to call home.

EveryYouth currently runs more than 60 bed spaces across 21 homelessness hubs around the UK, most of which are in houses rented from local councils at a peppercorn rate.
