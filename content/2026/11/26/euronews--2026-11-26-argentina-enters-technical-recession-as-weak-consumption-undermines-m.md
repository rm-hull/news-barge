## Argentina's economy shrank 1.8% in the third quarter, its second straight contraction. Weak consumption, falling purchasing power and strain in key sectors are slowing growth and clouding Javier Milei's prospects.

Argentina's economy has entered a **technical recession after two consecutive quarters of contraction**, despite the solid performance of key sectors such as oil and mining. The Monthly Economic Activity Indicator (EMAE), published by the National Institute of Statistics and Census (Indec), shows a fall of 1.8% between July and September, after the 1% decline recorded in the second quarter.

Although activity picked up by 1.1% in September compared with the previous month, it was still **1.2% below the level of the same month in 2025**. The final gross domestic product (GDP) figures, which will make it possible to confirm officially how the economy is evolving, will be published on 16 December.

The government of Javier Milei has called for caution before declaring that the country is in recession. However, economists believe that the EMAE data clearly point to that situation, as they show **two consecutive quarters of contraction**.

## Weak domestic demand

The downturn is largely due to weak domestic demand, which contrasts with the growth of activities linked to the exploitation of natural resources. In September, **the hydrocarbons and mining sectors grew by 11.1% year on year**, but their momentum was not enough to offset the declines in other branches of activity.

Among the most affected sectors is **fishing, with a collapse of 48.6%**. Agriculture also fell, by 2%, and manufacturing industry by 1.8%. These activities carry considerable weight in Argentina's economy, so their poor results offset the dynamism of energy and mining exports.** The fall in consumption is another of the main factors** behind the contraction. Buying on instalments, which had sustained part of demand in recent years, has lost steam. At the same time, interest rates on personal loans are around 64% a year and around six million people have fallen into arrears.

## Loss of purchasing power and a labour market under strain

Price trends also make it harder to restore purchasing power. Although **inflation has fallen considerably** from the 211% year-on-year rate recorded in 2023, forecasts for 2026 have worsened. At present, an average price rise of 31% is expected, compared with the 22.4% that was anticipated at the start of the year. In October, monthly inflation stood at 2.2%, up from 1.9% in September.

Wages have seen only a limited recovery. According to consultancy LCG, **registered pay improved by 2.5%** in real terms up to September, compared with December last year. However, it remains below November 2023 levels, especially in the public sector, where it has fallen by 15.4%.

The economic deterioration is also reflected in the labour market. **Unemployment reached 7.9%** in the second quarter of 2026, its highest level since Milei arrived at the Casa Rosada. In addition, the economy shed more than 300,000 jobs in the private sector and nearly 70,000 in the public sector, while around 30,000 companies closed, according to official figures.

## Milei's economic challenges for 2027

The recession represents **a setback for the Argentine president's economic programme**, although it does not wipe out some of his main results, such as the first fiscal surplus in 14 years, achieved in 2024, and the sharp reduction in inflation.

On top of domestic difficulties comes **a deterioration in market confidence**. Argentina's country risk recently exceeded 1,100 points, its highest level since 2025, while the official dollar rate passed 1,630 pesos.

Growth prospects have also weakened. According to the newspaper 'El Tiempo' the consultancy LCG **expects GDP to grow by at most 0.7% in 2026**, far from the 3% target set by the government. For 2027, while the executive projects growth of 4%, its estimates put the increase at around 1.5%.

The combination of **weak consumption, lagging wages and difficulties in key sectors** therefore poses one of the main challenges for Milei's economic policy and his re-election ambitions.
