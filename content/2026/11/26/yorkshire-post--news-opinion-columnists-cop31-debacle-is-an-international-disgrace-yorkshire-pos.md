# COP31 debacle is an international disgrace: Yorkshire Post Letters

## Bryn Glover, Dallowgill, Kirkby Malzeard, Ripon.

The debacle at Antalya is an international disgrace. The failure of the delegates at COP 31 to agree on a final communique is to their collective shame; they could not even agree to endorse any of their previous decisions, such as those from the landmark conference in Paris.

The blame is mostly on the few nations which have clearly decided to place their own economic interests before the existential needs of the rest of humanity.

These selfish people appear willing to disregard the devastation that will happen as temperatures inexorably climb, just so long as they can continue to accumulate their already vast stashes of oil and coal cash.

I would include the two host nations, Turkey and Australia, in that criticism.

But the United Nations itself must take some of the blame. It has 197 members and its rules state that any decisions taken must be endorsed by all participants; they like to refer to this as 'consensus' agreement.

What it means in practice is that just a few pariah states have an effective veto over the wishes of the vast majority of the world's people.

The UN needs to move to a more democratic, majority system. This need not be a simple majority; it could be two-thirds or even 90 per cent needed for a proposition to be carried.

The crisis looming over humanity is too serious for old niceties; we need to make far more rapid progress than has been achieved to date, and if that means dragging the worst offenders kicking and screaming alongside us, then so be it.

**Clark Cross, Springfield Road, Linlithgow.**

COP31 has proved to be yet another expensive talking shop to add to the previous 30. There were a record 92,934 people attending, enjoying the food and drink and meeting old friends and pledging to come back next year to COP32 in Addis Ababa, Ethiopia. These COP attendees certainly know how to run up their expenses and air miles. Strange that there are never any details of the extra greenhouse gases these COP conferences create or the costs. One gem emerged. Sara Shaw who is Interim Director of Campaigns and Networks at 350.org said: "Carbon capture is one of the biggest scams of the overshoot era and our leaders are on the verge of falling for it". Developing countries were only there for the money.

They have demanded at least $300 billion annually in climate finance by 2035 to support developing countries' industrial capacity, infrastructure and living standards.

They also called for adaptation funding to triple by 2035 to help vulnerable nations recover from climate disasters they say was caused by the developed nations. The ABBA song "Money, money, money" would have been appropriate background music.
