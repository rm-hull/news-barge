The government has faced warnings that a drop in international students coming to study in the UK is "damaging" the economy as well as British universities.

According to Office for National Statistics (ONS) data, the number of overseas students from outside the EU arriving in the UK fell by roughly 33,000 during the 12 months to June this year.

The statistics also demonstrate that 292,000 non-EU nationals left the country over the same timeframe, with approximately half (53%) having originally arrived on study-related visas.

Further figures from the Home Office, released on Thursday, reveal that 335,801 study visa grants were issued in the year ending September 2026 – representing a 23% fall compared to the previous year.

Zaid Kadhum, policy manager at the Russell Group which represents some of the most selective UK universities, stated that several years of policy reform has "reduced demand" for British higher education.

“This is damaging universities, the UK’s soft power, and the economy,” he warned.

Mr Kadhum added: “We’re watching the sudden rise in visa refusal rates closely.

![The Russell Group has called on the government to give universities “policy stability” to ensure institutions can continue attracting talented overseas students](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/08/16/14/2290035063..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“A robust immigration system is in everyone’s interests, but decisions must be fair and transparent, and we should be careful not to deter genuine students in the process”.

The Russell Group has called on the government to give universities “policy stability” to ensure institutions can continue attracting talented overseas students.

Under changes introduced by the former Conservative government in January 2024, most international students were no longer able to bring family members to the UK.

A spokesperson for Universities UK said: “The very substantial fall in net migration will be welcomed by government, but it has not come without consequences.

“Part of that reduction reflects a sharp fall in the number of international students choosing to study in the UK.

“The priority now should be rebuilding international recruitment sustainably.

“Government should give prospective students confidence that the UK remains open and welcoming, and avoid further measures – such as the international student levy – that would weaken our competitiveness at precisely the point we need to rebuild.”

Favour Samuel, president of National Union of Students England, said: “Quite frankly, international students are keeping our universities financially afloat and the stark drop in numbers will be making chief financial officers at universities very nervous.

“The drop in numbers is as a direct result of the UK government’s hostile immigration policies for international students.”

![International students are keeping UK universities financially afloat, NUS said](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/09/30/07/01M3RECZZQKY50VYZ0GM7CYJGY.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

She added: “Unless the government change their course, their hostile immigration policies will collapse the higher education sector.”

A government spokesperson said: “We welcome international students who make a positive contribution to our universities, our economy and society as a whole. That’s why we’re extending the graduate visa from 18 months to two years for eligible universities.

“This government is committed to creating a secure future for our world-leading universities so they can deliver for students, taxpayers and the economy. We have taken action to put the sector on a secure financial footing, including raising the maximum cap on tuition fees and refocusing the Office for Students to support universities’ financial stability.”

It comes as Prime Minister Andy Burnham said on Thursday that Brexit did nothing but “weaken control” of UK borders, as he hailed plummeting net migration figures.

The figure dropped to an estimated 77,000 in the year to June, the lowest level in nearly 30 years excluding the coronavirus pandemic, according to the Office for National Statistics (ONS).

Telling reporters “Cameron’s probably looking at this”, the Prime Minister said there was now a “sufficient degree of control over the system such that all forms of migration are down”, and the latest figures “begin to answer what the public has been saying”.

Thursday’s figures indicate UK net migration is down nearly two thirds (62%) from 204,000 in the previous 12 months.

It is the lowest annual total – calculating the difference between the number of people arriving and leaving the country – since 1997, when 48,000 was recorded.

This is except for a period during the Covid-19 outbreak when travel restrictions saw the figure drop briefly as low as 35,000.

It comes more than 16 years after David Cameron famously staked his political reputation on bringing net migration below 100,000.

Ahead of the 2010 general election he vowed a Conservative government would radically cut the figure to “just tens of thousands” – something he then doubled down on while prime minister during the Liberal Democrat coalition years.
