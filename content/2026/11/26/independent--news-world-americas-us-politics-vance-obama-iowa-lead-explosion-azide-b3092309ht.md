Vice President JD Vance has been slammed after trying to blame former president Barack Obama for the toxic lead that was detonated by the Army in Iowa Wednesday.

In the run-up to the controlled explosion, which saw a near 5-ton stockpile of lead azide blown up in Middleton, concerns were raised the blast could contaminate the environment on the eve of Thanksgiving

Given that the Army discovered part of the stockpile had crystallized - making it at risk of spontaneous detonation - in March, Vance was asked why the administration waited so long to carry out the detonation.

The VP dealt with the question by looking back 17 years and taking a swipe at the former president.

“I think you have to remember that this was a problem; the can had been kicked down the road,” Vance told reporters Wednesday. “If you go back to 2009 under the Obama administration, we stored this highly unstable stuff with no real plan for how to move it, no real plan for how to neutralize it.

​“When I say we, I mean the Obama administration - the United States government did this back in 2009,” he continued. “Now, we became aware - meaning the Trump administration - we became aware that there really wasn’t a good option here about six months ago.”

![Vice President JD Vance has blamed former President Obama after a planned explosion of lead azide stored at a military facility in Iowa](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/26/16/13/Screenshot-2026-11-26-at-15-59-28.png?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&auto=webp)

​Vance’s swipe at the Obama administration is a reference to a 2009 decision to consolidate the military’s remaining lead azide stores at the Iowa Army Ammunition Plant.

​However, Vance’s critics were quick to point out on X that Trump has led two of the three administrations that have succeeded Obama.

​“There've been three admins since @BarackObama left office, and two of them were led by @realDonaldTrump,” David Axelrod, Obama’s former chief strategist, wrote. “Gimme a break.”

​Joyce Carol Oates, a celebrated author and fierce critic of Trump, poked fun at the Trump administration’s attempt to shift the blame onto Obama.

​“Oops! They're skipping back a generation to blame now, not Biden, but Obama,” she wrote. “If voters had given GOP two more years, they'd soon be blaming Bill Clinton, eventually FDR.

​“Confirming what a good idea the ‘blue wave’ was, & will be in 2028.”

![Concerns had been raised that the explosion could create a dust plume that could contaminate the nearby environment](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/26/16/2306837029..?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&auto=webp)

​Journalist Brian J. Karem also poked fun at Vance’s remark by joking that the Trump administration could blame other former presidents.

​“I thought it was Clinton's fault! Or maybe Kennedy, or FDR or Hoover…” he quipped.

​Scott Greenfield, a criminal defense lawyer, remarked: “JDV is going to feel really silly when he finds out there was another Republican administration between Obama and now that could have dealt with it but failed and ‘kicked’ the problem too.”

​However, Vance is not the only member of the incumbent administration to blame their predecessors. Trump himself has also pointed the finger at Obama.

​In a post uploaded to Truth Social, Trump referenced concerns that exploding the stockpile of lead azide could release a dust plume carrying carcinogenic debris.

![The stockpile was stored at the Iowa Army Ammunition Plant in Middletown](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/26/16/2026-11-25T133000Z_467042053_RC20VOA52QT5_RTRMADP_3_IOWA-ARMY-DETONATION.JPG?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&auto=webp)

​“0.000% of Obama caused carcinogens were released into beautiful Iowa air after the Perfect detonation. WE ALL WORKED HARD!” he wrote early on Thursday.

​Environmentalists had filed lawsuits in an attempt to stop the blast, while former MAGA loyalist Marjorie Taylor Greene claimed that the Trump administration plotted to detonate a “dirty bomb” in Iowa.

​In a statement, the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency confirmed real-time air monitors reported “zero detection of airborne lead” following the explosion.
