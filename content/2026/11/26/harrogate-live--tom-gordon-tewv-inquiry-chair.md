Tom Gordon has met Judge Potter, chair of the TEWV mental health inquiry, about its terms of reference. He urges anyone affected in Harrogate to take part.

Tom Gordon, the Liberal Democrat MP for Harrogate and Knaresborough, has met Judge Potter, the chair of the statutory public inquiry into mental health care at Tees, Esk and Wear Valleys NHS Foundation Trust (TEWV), to discuss the inquiry’s terms of reference. He is urging anyone in the constituency affected by care at TEWV to feed into the inquiry, and says his office will help them do it.

## Overview

* **The meeting:** Mr Gordon met Judge Potter to discuss the inquiry’s terms of reference, which set out its exact scope and are still to be agreed and signed off.
* **Who can take part:** the MP says the chair is open to hearing from people who are not “core participants” to the inquiry.
* **How to get help:** constituents affected by care at TEWV can email [tom.gordon.mp@parliament.uk](mailto:tom.gordon.mp@parliament.uk) to flag an interest in contributing.
* **Support:** Samaritans can be reached free, day or night, on 116 123.

## “They deserve answers”

Mr Gordon called the meeting a key milestone on the road to answers for families and patients who have been let down.

“Meeting Judge Potter is a key milestone. For too long, people across Harrogate and Knaresborough have told me about failings in the mental health care they or their loved ones received. They have shown real courage in coming forward, and they deserve answers,” he said.

“I used my very first question at PMQs to back the call for this inquiry. Since then I’ve chased minister after minister to get a chair appointed and the inquiry moving. Now the focus shifts to making sure it works for the people it is meant to serve.”

## The terms of reference

The terms of reference have not yet been agreed. Mr Gordon said he told the chair they need to be wide enough to cover everything his constituents have told him.

“We had a constructive discussion about the terms of reference. I was clear they need to be broad enough to capture the full range of experiences my constituents have shared with me, so that no family is left out,” he said.

Many constituents have contacted his office with first-hand accounts of failings and poor care they or their loved ones received from TEWV, and he says he has raised those experiences with Judge Potter.

## Taking part without core participant status

Core participant status gives families and former patients a bigger role in the inquiry, including the right to see evidence that is not available to the public. The MP said he was reassured by Judge Potter’s openness to hearing from people who do not have that status.

Chris Howden, Mr Gordon’s senior caseworker, said: “We’ve had families who want to contribute but who might find the ‘core participant’ status, its intensity and high barrier to entry a bit much to handle in already tragic circumstances. Judge Potter’s openness to hearing from a wide range of people who were impacted is very reassuring. Giving folk a small stake in the process is vital to building back that trust with health services.”

## How the MP pushed for it

Mr Gordon used his first question at Prime Minister’s Questions to back calls for a statutory inquiry, then chased several ministers to get a chair appointed and the inquiry started. After it was secured, he held a public meeting to hear constituents’ experiences directly.

“If you or someone you love has been affected by care at TEWV, please make your voice heard. Feed into the inquiry, or get in touch with my office and my team will help you,” he said.

Constituents can email [tom.gordon.mp@parliament.uk](mailto:tom.gordon.mp@parliament.uk) to say they want to contribute. Anyone who is struggling can call Samaritans free, day or night, on 116 123.
