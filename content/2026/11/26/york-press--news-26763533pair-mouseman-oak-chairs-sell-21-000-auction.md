## 99-year-old Mouseman chairs sell for thousands in York auction

Coralie with the chairs *(Image: Provided)*

A PAIR of oak chairs commissioned for ten guineas in 1927 have sold for £21,000 at auction.

The chairs, each fetching £10,500, were commissioned in 1927 by Bradford solicitor George Bolton from furniture maker Robert Thompson - better known as the Mouseman of Kilburn.

They were part of a sale that saw 140 Mouseman lots sell for an overall total of £199,000.

Coralie Thomson, auctioneer, said: "The direct link between Robert Thompson and George Bolton made these pieces very special, particularly as they remained in the Bolton family until they were entered in the sale.

"Furthermore Thompson had not finalised and patented his mouse trademark at the time he made them – he would do that with the help of George Bolton in 1931 – so the mice on these pieces are early versions."

![Coralie with the detail](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21765029.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Coralie with the detail *(Image: Provided)*

She added that the auction was "quite phenomenal," with interest and bids coming in from across the country as many of the pieces "sailed passed estimate" more than a week before the auction took place.

Other pieces in the sale included an oak monks chair, an oak octagonal dining table, a 5ft sideboard, a 1937 adzed oak refectory table, a pair of oak octagonal candlesticks, and oak pin tray.
