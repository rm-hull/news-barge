Hundreds of people have voiced strong opposition to dogs being taken to York's packed Christmas Market.

More than 350 readers have shared their views in response to **a letter published by The Press,** questioning why so many visitors bring their dogs into the packed city centre during the festive season.

The letter was from one of The Press's most regular correspondents, D M Deamer, who argued that dogs and large crowds “simply don’t mix” in York’s confined market and narrow streets, saying animals could be at risk of injury.

Mr Deamer accepted that some visitors have little choice but to bring their pets, but also raised concerns about clashes between dogs.

The letter prompted a strong reaction from fellow readers, many of them dog owners themselves.

Val Roebuck Dootson said she agreed dogs should not be taken into the crowds after accidentally treading on one she had not seen.

She said: “The vast majority of the dogs I saw were petrified. Just be kind to your lovely pets and leave them at home in peace.”

Another dog owner, Skewbald Que, said they had arranged care for their two dogs while visiting - and were glad they had.

They said the market was extremely crowded, adding: “There were so many dogs with tails between their legs, ears back, they were so worried.”

DeeDee Connaught was equally forthright, saying: “Anyone who takes a dog to a place like this is just cruel.”

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21763952.jpg?type=mds-article-620)York Christmas Market is no place for dogs, according to hundreds of Press readers. *(Image: Newsquest)*

She said dogs were surrounded by a “mass of human legs” while owners also had to concentrate on the crowds and market stalls, concluding: “Leave your dog at home when you go to the market.”

Rosa Neal, who owns two small dogs, said she would never take them into somewhere so crowded because of the danger of them being stepped on or causing somebody to trip.

She said: “They’re both happier at home keeping warm.”

Garry K Young highlighted several potential problems, including dogs having their paws stepped on, becoming frightened or being forced into close proximity with other animals.

He said owners sometimes failed to read their pets’ body language when they were scared and wanted to stop.

Jen Anderson said she was a “huge dog lover” but, after visiting York Christmas Market on a Saturday, would not want to subject a dog or small child to such a busy environment.

Others described seeing animals struggling amid the crowds.

Rich Stockill said the number of dogs at the market on a Saturday was “insane” and claimed he witnessed a couple being stood on.

June Parker said she had been “furious” at seeing dogs “getting dragged round, being stood on, kicked and pushed about”.

But several readers pointed out the situation is not always straightforward, particularly for visitors staying away from home.

Adele Adamson said people staying in York for the market may have little choice if their holiday accommodation does not allow dogs to be left there alone.

John Murphy, himself a dog owner, said he would not personally take his medium-sized dog to the Christmas Market because it was too crowded.

However, he added: “People come to visit the market from all over Yorkshire and may not have a choice in leaving their dog at home so it has to come with them. Who are we to criticise people’s choices?”

Some readers rejected the idea that dogs and the market cannot mix at all.

Helen Murphy said her medium-sized dog loves people and other dogs, asking: “Why shouldn’t we take her?”

Keeley Yoward said her family takes its dog every year without problems but deliberately chooses quieter times.

She said they visit later, after many coaches have departed, and their larger dog wears high-visibility gear so he can easily be seen.

“Dogs can absolutely go and go safely if people are sensible about it,” she said. “Just go when it’s quieter.”

Dawn Green suggested the argument went beyond York Christmas Market, saying the real problem was “dogs and any crowds”.

She said busy places could put both animals and pedestrians at risk because people do not always see dogs beneath them.

The comments suggest that while readers may disagree on an outright dog-free approach, many share one concern: whether a trip into York’s festive crowds is really enjoyable for the animal at the end of the lead.
