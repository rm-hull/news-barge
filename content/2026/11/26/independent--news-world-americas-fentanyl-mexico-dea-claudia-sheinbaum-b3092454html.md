Mexico's president has accused the U.S. Drug Enforcement Administration of failing to tackle fentanyl and corruption within the United States, after the agency set up anonymous tip lines in her country.

President Claudia Sheinbaum made the accusations in harsh comments on Thursday, reading excerpts from a new investigation by The Associated Press.

That investigation revealed the DEA allowed at least 1.4 million counterfeit fentanyl pills to go unseized while spending eight months tracking a drug trafficking ring that was flooding the Southwest with deadly drugs.

“The DEA has plenty of work to do in its own country. A lot,” Sheinbaum said. “The first thing it should do is investigate corruption in the United States linked to organized crime, or what they call terrorist groups.”

The outburst came less than 24 hours after DEA Administrator Terry Cole announced the tip lines on his X account.

Cole encouraged Mexicans to denounce members of criminal networks in Sinaloa state, the corrupt officials who support them and people who launder money to finance narcoterrorism.

![Mexico’s President Claudia Sheinbaum](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/26/21/2305284310..?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&auto=webp)

“Your information matters. Your identity and your safety matter,” Cole said, adding that tips submitted via Signal, WhatsApp and email would be handled confidentially.

Cole also said a channel for submitting tips about the state of Jalisco would be made public Thursday.

## US offers to deploy troops in Mexico

Jalisco is a stronghold of the powerful Jalisco New Generation Cartel, known by its Spanish initials CJNG, which Washington also designated a terrorist organization.

The DEA did not immediately respond to a request for comment.

The United States has stepped up pressure on Mexico to fight the cartels and has even offered to deploy troops on Mexican soil.

Sheinbaum has ruled out that deployment while maintaining that there will be security cooperation with Washington, “but without subordination.”

![A burned truck blocks the Mexican Federal Highway 15 following the capture of Fausto Isidro Meza Flores, known as "El Chapo Isidro", in Guasave, Sinaloa state](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/26/21/2026-11-22T023856Z_1842618416_RC27RBA873U7_RTRMADP_3_MEXICO-CHAPOISIDRO.JPG?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&auto=webp)

On November 20, Mexican military forces captured drug lord Fausto Isidro Meza Flores, alias “El Chapo Isidro,” a key ally of a Sinaloa cartel faction who was on the FBI's Ten Most Wanted Fugitives list.

Mexico requested Washington's support for the operation, and the United States sent a drone to help gather information about the drug lord's whereabouts.

The U.S. has requested Meza Flores' extradition, but Mexican Attorney General Ernestina Godoy said he will be tried in Mexico.

Although cooperation between the two countries went smoothly in that operation, tensions arose between Mexico and Washington in late April.

Federal prosecutors in New York made public a drug trafficking indictment against Sinaloa Governor Rubén Rocha Moya, a member of Mexico's governing party.

He and nine other officials were accused of collaborating with a faction of the Sinaloa cartel, which the Trump administration has designated a foreign terrorist organization.

## The ‘Fast and Furious of Fentanyl’

![Sinaloa Governor Rubén Rocha Moya has taken an indefinite leave of absence](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/04/30/19/2175312557.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Amid the controversy, Rocha Moya took an indefinite leave of absence, and his bank accounts in Mexico were frozen.

Mexican authorities refused to carry out the U.S. request to arrest him.

Former Sinaloa Security Secretary Gerardo Mérida Sánchez surrendered to U.S. authorities in May.

DEA whistleblowers in June revealed how agents in New Mexico in recent years permitted hundreds of thousands of fentanyl pills to go unseized.

At the time, the DEA denied it ever knowingly allowed the trafficking of a synthetic opioid the White House has designated a “weapon of mass destruction.”

That denial was further contradicted by new findings that the DEA permitted at least 1.4 million additional pills to be trafficked between August 2024 and April 2025 as it closed in on the largest fentanyl bust in U.S. history.

The tally was based on thousands of pages of court documents, investigative records, detailed wiretap reports and 12 hours of DEA surveillance footage.

The footage showed dealers moving large packages of pills in plastic bins and duffel bags that passed through highly transited hotels, apartment complexes, Airbnb rentals and self-storage facilities in six states.

In her comments Thursday, the Mexican president called the DEA's decision to permit fentanyl to hit the streets the “Fast and Furious of Fentanyl,” a reference to the 2011 gun-walking scandal.

In that scandal, straw buyers smuggled some 2,000 assault weapons into Mexico with the intent of tracing the firearms to cartel leaders.

“What is the United States doing in the United States?” Sheinbaum asked. “We have a prosecutor’s office here, and the Security Ministry works here. So they should pay close attention to the other side of the border.”
