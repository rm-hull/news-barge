A widow has won a long-running High Court battle over control of a family business empire.

Shaheen Shan, widow of Yorkshire businessman Ali Akbar Shan, has secured legal victories against her former brothers-in-law, Aftab and Sajad Shan.

This comes following disputes over multiple companies, including the £80-million Yorkshire Halal Meat Supplier Ltd (YHMS) and Shans Supermarket Ltd in Keighley.

## Widow reacts to court win

Mr Shan died on January 26, 2022, aged 55, leaving behind a substantial business portfolio.

Mrs Shan, who was represented by Schofield Sweeney solicitors, said: "Ali worked every day of his life to build these businesses and to provide for our family.

"It is deeply painful that, within hours of losing him, people he had trusted set about trying to take control of what he had built and to cut me and our children out of it.

"Aftab and Sajad did not build what Ali built, but they tried to take it using documents that the courts have now rejected."

She continued: "I have had to spend years in court simply to protect what is rightfully his estate and our children's future.

"I am grateful that the courts have now looked at the evidence carefully and reached such clear and emphatic conclusions."

## Urge to other business owners

She urged other business owners to take steps to protect their families.

She said: "I would urge every family business owner to speak to their solicitors now and make sure that their wishes are properly recorded, so that their families are protected and never have to face what mine has faced."

## Wrongly appointed company directors

The High Court examined a series of actions taken at Companies House immediately following Mr Shan’s death.

Mr Justice Leech noted that AP01 forms were filed on January 25 and 26, 2022, appointing Aftab and Sajad Shan as company directors.

The court found that neither brother had been validly appointed.

Mr Justice Leech said: "Aftab, having resigned as a director in 2013, was not entitled to be recorded as a director following Ali's death.

"Similarly, Sajad was not validly appointed a director following Ali's death."

The court also ruled on share ownership in YHMS.

The judge recorded Mrs Shan's position as holding 150 shares as executrix of her husband’s estate and an additional 45 shares in her own name.

The judge ordered that the company register be updated to reflect this.

## Injunction prevented Mrs Shan from holding company meetings

In April 2023, Aftab and Sajad obtained an injunction preventing Mrs Shan and YHMS from holding company meetings.

The injunction, issued by Mr Justice Edwin Johnson, remained in place until May 5.

Mr Justice Leech said: "On 20 April 2023, Edwin Johnson J made a short term injunction until the afternoon of the following day restraining Shaheen Shan and Yorkshire Halal Meat Supplier Limited from attending, pursuing, organising, arranging or conducting any general or other meeting in respect of the company at any time before midnight on 5 May 2023."

## Gold, cash and jewellery removed from safe

Mr Justice Leech found that Aftab Shan was present at the supermarket on the evening of April 6, 2023, and that individuals seen on CCTV removing DVRs the next day were acting on his instructions.

The court concluded that Aftab or his agents removed the company’s share register, other records, cash, gold, jewellery, and computer hardware, including CCTV devices.

Mr Justice Leech said: "I find that Aftab or his agents removed from the Supermarket (i) the share register of SSL and other books and records of the company, (ii) cash, gold and jewellery from at least one safe and (iii) computer hardware and other devices including DVRs containing CCTV footage from earlier on April 6, 2023."

## 'Evasive and unhelpful' evidence

The judge was also critical of some of the witness evidence.

He described Rukhsana Shan, wife of Sajad Shan, as "evasive and unhelpful" during cross-examination.

He also questioned the authenticity of lease documents presented by Aftab Shan, suggesting they were prepared within days of each other and not as claimed.

The legal battle has spanned more than two years and covered issues of company ownership, directorship, shareholder rights, and asset control.
