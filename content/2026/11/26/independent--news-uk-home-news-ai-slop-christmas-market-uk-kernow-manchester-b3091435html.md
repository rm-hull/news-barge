Christmas markets across the UK are facing backlash ahead of the festive season over their alleged use of ‘AI slop’.

Between the mulled wine and Bratwursts, disgruntled guests have taken to social media to criticise the imagery used to promote different offerings including some of the gifts available.

In Belfast, one user has even taken to filming an ‘AI slop hunt’, taking his followers along with him as he points out examples of AI-use at his local market.

“I would rather a crappy photo of your food than AI slop,” Jack McClelland said in a Facebook video. “Just a tip”

Meanwhile, market stalls in Manchester have also faced criticism over their alleged use of AI.

A social media post of a Christmas market stall by a *Manchester Evening News* journalist caused a particular stir, with one X user writing in the comments: “Can we all agree to boycott places with those awful AI posters?”

“The German classic according to ChatGPT,” another read.

When asked about the posters, an owner of the stall featured in the post told MEN: "It is the first year we have used it like this, but we have tried to make sure everything looks right. We have already taken some images down because they did not look the same.”

Further south, a similar story emerged. Kernow Christmas Market in Redruth, Cornwall, was advertised on Facebook with posters created by AI, showing a wonderful winter scene complete with festive stalls, bright lights and decorations.

However, many were left underwhelmed when - in reality - they arrived at what appeared to be markets around the sparse warehouse space.

![Kernow Christmas Market in Redruth, Cornwall, was advertised on Facebook with posters created by AI](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/23/15/34/Kernow-Christmas-Market-ef423091.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Responding to the criticism, organisers of the event said in a statement on Facebook: “As this is the first time we have undertaken something like this, we appreciate that there have been a few misunderstandings and perhaps different expectations of what the indoor setup would look and feel like.”

On Tuesday, the organisers issued another statement, saying attempts to bring a “little festive magic into Pool Market” had not worked as planned and current bookings will be refunded.

The statement added: “We are gutted, just as much as you all that things didn’t go as planned. The criticism we have faced and the words that have been seen on social media and across the news make us look at the world slightly differently.”

![Organisers of the event said: ‘We appreciate that there have been a few misunderstandings and perhaps different expectations of what the indoor setup would look and feel like’](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/23/15/07/Kernow-Christmas-Market.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

So, why has the use of AI drawn so much backlash? *The Independent* spoke to AI and marketing experts to find out.

## **Why might customers be put off by the use of AI at Christmas markets?**

“People go to Christmas markets for the real thing: local, handmade, what you see is what you get,” says Bill Gidda, photographer and founder of Eyemedia Studios. “An AI picture of food breaks that promise before anyone has even tasted it.”

He adds: “People are getting better at spotting fake images, and once they spot one, it puts doubt in their minds about everything else on the stall.”

For Paul Pennell, co-founder of AI strategy firm The Fusion Labs, it’s about sameness.

![Christmas markets across the UK are facing backlash over their alleged use of ‘AI slop’](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/25/14/03/belfast-15064787.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“Anyone can make an AI poster for free in a few seconds, so you end up with the same glossy food shots and the same tell-tale fonts,” he says. “One poster on its own might look fine. Walk past twenty of them and you can’t tell whose stall is whose.”

## **What does this mean for the future of businesses like Christmas market vendors?**

“I can’t blame small vendors for using it. Many could never afford a designer, and AI gives them access to visuals that used to need a real budget,” says Michael Bismuth, art director and AI lead at production studio cuz.

“The trick is to photograph the real product and let AI do the rest. The product has to be real, the magic around it can be created. AI used the smart way won’t affect your business.”

He adds: “Coca-Cola learned this the hard way. The brand that made the modern Santa famous used generative AI for its Christmas ads two years in a row, including a remake of its classic “Holidays Are Coming”.

“The numbers were good but the reaction was mixed. This year it went back to filming real people. At Christmas, how you make the ad is part of the message. If Coca-Cola is choosing authenticity, a market stall should think twice.”
