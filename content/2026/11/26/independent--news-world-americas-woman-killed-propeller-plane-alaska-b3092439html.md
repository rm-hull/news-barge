An Alaska woman died Wednesday after she walked into the moving propeller of an airplane in what officials believe was an accident.

The incident happened at Big Lake Airport, a small airport with a single runway about 20 miles from Anchorage, according to a news release from the Alaska Department of Public Safety.

Christina Blake, 38, of Wasilla, was driving on a taxiway when a Taylorcraft F19 landed and taxied toward her. The plane stopped, and the pilot waited for the vehicle to move out of the way.

“Blake exited the vehicle, approached the running aircraft, and walked into the airplane's propeller while it was stationary,” officials said. First responders tried to save her, but she was pronounced dead at the scene.

Her body was taken to the state medical examiner's office for an autopsy, and her family has been notified.

![A 38-year-old woman died after she walked into an airplane propeller at Big Lake Airport in Alaska on Wednesday, state troopers said](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/26/20/51/Screenshot-2026-11-26-at-3-11-15-PM.png?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

State troopers do not believe she stepped into the propeller on purpose, Department of Public Safety spokesman Austin McDaniel told the *Anchorage Daily News*. She reportedly gestured toward the pilot shortly before her death.

Blake does not appear to have any connection to the airport, and it’s unclear what brought her there, McDaniel said.

The pilot, who tried to shut off the engine as Blake walked toward the plane, was not hurt, according to the newspaper. No passengers were aboard.

The National Transportation Safety Board was notified, officials said.
