French actor Gérard Depardieu has been ordered to stand trial on a charge of raping an actor at his Paris home in 2018, one of his lawyers says.

The news comes less than two weeks after the film star abandoned his appeal of a sexual assault conviction in a separate case.

Defence lawyer Delphine Meillet said that the 77-year-old actor would face trial over an incident reported by actor Charlotte Arnould.

Depardieu, who has denied the rape allegations, had challenged an investigating judge’s August 2025 decision to send him to trial.

The Paris Court of Appeal upheld that decision on Thursday in relation to one of two alleged rapes.

“He disputes the decision,” Ms Meillet said, without specifying whether Depardieu would file a further appeal.

![Depardieu, 77, has denied the rape allegation](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/26/15/2026-11-26T140738Z_816864359_RC2XKDAULW27_RTRMADP_3_PEOPLE-DEPARDIEU.JPG?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&auto=webp)

Ms Arnould first lodged a complaint in 2018, when she was 22.

The case has become one of the most prominent in France’s reckoning with sexual violence, as the #MeToo movement has faced resistance in the country, particularly in the arts.

For half a century, Depardieu was a towering figure in French cinema.

With his hulking frame, crooked nose and commanding screen presence, he became one of the country’s most recognizable cultural exports, earning an Oscar nomination for *Cyrano de Bergerac*and a Golden Globe for* Green Card.*

But accusations of sexual misconduct and his subsequent conviction have increasingly overshadowed his legacy.

Earlier this month, Depardieu dropped his appeal against a conviction for sexually assaulting two women on a film set in 2021, accepting an 18-month suspended prison sentence and issuing a public apology.

![Actor Gerard Depardieu arrives with his lawyer Jeremie Assous, right, to face trial for the sexual assaults of two women on a film set in 2021](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/16/12/EU_France_Depardieu_29312.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“I was found guilty,” Depardieu said in a written statement on 16 November.

He said that he accepted the sentence and offered “my deep and sincere apologies to those who may have suffered”.

Depardieu was convicted in May 2025 of groping a set decorator and an assistant during the filming of *Les Volets Verts*(* The Green Shutters*). The case was widely seen as a test of the French film industry’s response to the #MeToo movement.

“On film sets, I have long hidden behind provocation and crude humor. I believed my words and some of my actions had no consequences. But they did, particularly coming from someone who held as much power as I once did,” Depardieu said in his statement.

Depardieu’s behavior toward women came under renewed scrutiny in December 2023 after a television documentary showed him repeatedly making obscene remarks and gestures during a 2018 trip to North Korea.

French President Emmanuel Macron drew criticism from women’s rights activists at the time when he defended Depardieu, saying the actor “makes France proud”.

Mr Macron invoked the presumption of innocence and said that he would not participate in a “manhunt”.
