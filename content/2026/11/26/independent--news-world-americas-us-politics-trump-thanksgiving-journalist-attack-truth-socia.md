President Donald Trump rang in Thanksgiving by listing what he regards as his achievements in office and berating a “lowlife” journalist for failing to cover them.

The president took to Truth Social in the early hours of Thursday morning to list his grievances with David E Sanger of *The New York Times*, while touting his own successes.

“David E. Sanger, the mediocre reporter for the Failing New York Times, didn’t mention in his recent story on me the successful closing of the Border, best crime stats in years (lowest murder rate since 1900 – 126 years!), biggest Tax and Regulation Cuts, EVER, the most successful naval blockade in history, the destruction of Iran’s Navy and Air Force (and more!), the ONE DAY WIN against Venezuela, and today’s big success in straightening out the Obama caused mess in Iowa,” the president fumed.

“David Sanger is a lowlife that only writes what he is told to write by his bosses. Also, until the Election, best Market, EVER, 82 days, settled 8 Wars, and the USA is respected again like never before!”

Sanger is the *NYT’s* chief Washington correspondent and has been part of three Pulitzer Prize-winning teams over the course of his long career at the newspaper.

![President Donald Trump kicked off Thanksgiving by attacking a journalist](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/24/20/2306703736..?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&auto=webp)

His latest article is about Trump’s push to reinstate Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang and the short-lived market rebound it inspired.

The veteran journalist noted that Huang’s return to the chipmaker initially caused its stock price to soar by more than 30 percent Monday but that it then dropped 5.9 percent Tuesday and 5 percent Wednesday.

He warned Trump that bragging about his economic influence was “risky business” and observed that he has a habit of “embracing markets for instant validation of his decisions… and then seeks other explanations when the inevitable downturn raises the inevitable question of whether presidential responsibility is a two-way street.”

*New York Times*spokesperson Nicole Taylor told* The Independent*: “If readers want to understand the market effects of President Trump’s interventions, including Nvidia’s reversal, his disruption of international trade, and his decision to go to war with Iran, then David Sanger’s latest report is an essential read.

“David has covered five American presidents in more than four decades at the *Times*; his coverage of the ways in which the administration is shaping foreign policy and national security is unmatched. Noisy name-calling is just an attempt to distract from the news.”

In his attack on Sanger, the president mentioned the successful detonation of dangerously degraded lead azide by Army engineers in Iowa earlier in the day, a subject he posted about several times Wednesday, blaming his predecessor Barack Obama for failing to deal with it during his presidency.

Obama, meanwhile, spent Wednesday helping volunteers at Dion’s Chicago Dream food bank in Illinois pack boxes of fresh fruit and vegetables for families in need, wishing everyone a happy Thanksgiving.

![Barack Obama and Michelle Obama spend Thanksgiving eve packing boxes of fruit and vegetables at a Chicago food bank](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/26/9/28/Obamas-Thanksgiving.png?quality=75&width=640&trim=0%2C0%2C0%2C0&auto=webp)

Trump also used the controlled explosion to launch another broadside against the media, writing in an earlier post: “Fake News CNN was hoping for a bad outcome in Iowa. Their pre-coverage was inaccurate and deplorable.

“When a PERFECT detonation was completed and there for all to see, they went CRAZY with anger. They want our Country to lose. ‘Another win for Trump!’”

The president is well known for his unorthodox messaging in moments of national celebration, tweeting in May 2015: “I would like to wish everyone, including all haters and losers (of which, sadly, there are many) a truly happy and enjoyable Memorial Day!”

Earlier this week he pardoned two turkeys, named Freedom and Feathers, and delivered a rambling speech in which he also reeled off his policy achievements.
