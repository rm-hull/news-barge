Chancellor John Healey announced more funding for the British Business Bank (BBB) in the Budget last month.

The UK government-owned bank is based in Sheffield and aims to boost the economy by offering financing to small businesses which helps to either get them started or to scale up.

So far the BBB has supported financing totalling £1.25bn through the Start Up Loans programme, split across more than 125,000 SME businesses.

Many different types of businesses are able to seek financing through Start Up Loans, so it’s worth knowing exactly how to go about it.

## What does the British Business Bank do?

The BBB is the UK’s economic development bank. Through partner organisations, it supports loans to UK companies through schemes such as the Start Up Loans programme and the Growth Guarantee Scheme.

For SME firms, the Start Up Loans option targets start-ups which either have not started trading or have been doing so for less than five years. The loans range from £500 to £25,000 per person involved in the business.

The interest rate is fixed at 7.5 per cent per year (correct at the time of writing), with terms of one to five years available. Mentoring can be arranged to go alongside the money itself.

In addition, the BBB also operates the Growth Guarantee Scheme (GGS) for larger more established businesses. Up to £2m in financing guarantees can be obtained via this route, again through participating lenders.

Following the Covid pandemic, the BBB was a central part of the bounce back loans scheme for companies trying to recover from the lockdowns.

## How to apply for a loan from the BBB

The BBB works through over 200 partner organisations, including banks, specialist lenders and venture capital firms.

Applications can be initiated by going to the Start Up Loans website. The site will take you through a process of supplying information about your personal circumstances and business plan.

You must, of course, have a clear and workable strategy, as well as evidence of the ability to make repayments and a reasonable credit history.

The Start Up Loans programme is open to financing a large range of business types, but there are some areas it won’t touch. These include property investment schemes, gambling businesses and regulated financial services firms.

If a loan is provided, the obligations do not end there and some updates are required as time goes on - as well as making repayments on time of course.

Louise McCoy, managing director for start up loans products at the British Business Bank, has provided a clear set of tips for *The Independent* which lay out how founders should approach an application.

“First, assess and understand the market for your business. Be clear about who your customers are, what evidence you have of demand and why they would buy from you,” Ms McCoy explained. “Test your assumptions before committing to funding - a strong idea needs a credible market.

“Secondly, develop a well-thought-through business plan. Set out how your business will operate and develop, and what the funding will help you achieve. Obtain expert advice and support to challenge and strengthen your plan, which should be treated as more than just an application requirement.

“And finally, understand your funding needs and how you will manage any risks. Know how much finance you need, how you will use it and, if you are borrowing, how you will afford repayments. Have your cash-flow forecast challenged and consider what would happen if sales were lower or costs higher than expected.

“Seek independent, specialist advice to fill out any gaps in your knowledge, and use mentoring as a sounding board for your decisions.

“Start Up Loans can assist you with all of these, from providing pre-application business support to help you explore your market opportunity and strengthen your business proposition to support with developing your business plan and cash-flow forecasting as part of the application process.”

## What are alternatives for getting funding?

If the Start Up Loans programme turns out not to be for you, there are other options for finding financing to scale up a side hustle into a proper business. Most high street banks have specialist teams for making small business loans, separate from any involvement with the BBB.

The Barclays Business Prosperity Fund is one example. The banks has £22bn available to support business growth among its clients and says it is committed to supporting 1.5m businesses and entrepreneurs by 2030, “from idea to IPO.”

Barclays say lending to smaller businesses rose more than 5 per cent year-on-year in the July-September period of 2026, with an expected uplift again coming in 2027 as firms put forward their plans to expand. According to the Barclays Business Prosperity Index, that’s in spite of some firms having concerns around the economy, higher interest rates and operational costs, like energy.

As is usual, there would of course be a full lending assessment and application process before any money is offered by any provider.

Away from the traditional banks, there are specialist lenders focused on small and mid-sized companies. Funding Circle is one example of this. They offer fixed-rate business loans from £10,000 to £750,000 over terms of two to 72 months.

And a third possibility is what are known as Angel Investors. These are individuals, usually wealthy, which lend directly to start-ups.

Often they will require you to grant them a stake in your company in exchange for the money, rather than paying them back in cash.

Angels will generally offer to help you to grow the business using their know-how and connections, as it is very much in their interest for you to succeed if they have shares in your company.

This route is generally only available to start-ups which have a realistic possibility of quickly scaling to a point where serious money can be made, so it only suits certain types of business, but the UK Business Angels Association is a good place to start if seeking this kind of financing.
