As Secretary of Commerce, Howard Lutnick is 10th in the presidential line of succession and oversees such government offices as the National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration, the Census Bureau and the National Institute of Standards and Technology — the little-known agency responsible for maintaining official standards of weights and measures.

As a Cabinet member, he receives a security detail from within a dedicated group at the Commerce Department that has been charged with protecting the Secretary of Commerce since the Reagan administration.

But Lutnick, who ran President Donald Trump’s 2024 transition team and then unsuccessfully campaigned for the Treasury job, wanted a security detail that looked more like the Secret Service detail assigned to the man who did get that job, Scott Bessent — or the sharply-dressed Army Criminal Investigation Division agents who surround Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth.

The former Cantor Fitzgerald boss Lutnick — one of the wealthiest men in Trump’s cabinet of billionaires and millionaires — wasn’t going to be ignored, so his department obliged his desire for a bigger entourage by taking armed agents off the job of keeping advanced computer chips out of China, according to a report Tuesday in *The Atlantic* that cited five people with knowledge of the arrangement.

It’s just the latest case of a Trump administration official taking comfort and status from taxpayer-funded resources intended to serve Americans.

![Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick reportedly demanded a bigger security detail early on in his tenure](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/25/18/2026-11-24T192933Z_70575736_RC2JUOAC1D5Z_RTRMADP_3_USA-TRUMP.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Kristi Noem’s Homeland Security Department spent $464 million on jets that sat parked for months, FBI Director Kash Patel faces a congressional inquiry over his use of government jets, and Cabinet members have moved onto military bases, one of them after an “emergency” home paint job, taking space meant for senior admirals and generals who in some cases have spent years earning their positions.

In each case, the Trump team’s rush for more and more perks caused the government to lose operational agents, aircraft and other resources that were redirected to an official’s convenience.

Lutnick’s version came after he alienated members of his regular security detail — and some of them quit.

Commerce then reassigned agents from its Bureau of Industry and Security (BIS), who are armed and carry arrest powers, to serve as his bodyguards, usually three to four agents at a time per eight-hour shift.

BIS has fewer than 200 special agents, most of them in 10 field offices and about a dozen overseas, and a current official told *The Atlantic*: “They’re not trained for protective detail. These are professional investigators.”

Commerce has its own Office of Security which has made executive protection arrangements for secretaries since 1986, though it’s traditionally been with a lighter footprint than what national-security officials receive.

One Commerce official said Lutnick faced “the highest level of credible threats in the Department’s history” and that some agents were “temporarily detailed to augment executive protection” in response to those threats.

But Lutnick’s need to be validated with more armed guards has come at a cost to national security.

![FBI Director Kash Patel drew scrutiny for using government aircraft to fly roundtrip between Tennessee and Pennsylvania to see his girlfriend, Alexis Wilkins, sing at a wrestling event at Penn State](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/25/22/2281047861..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

A June report from his department’s inspector general found that BIS hasn’t taken adequate action on export controls for goods bound for China and Hong Kong, and a May report found similar failures on Russia and Belarus.

And last week, the inspector general began evaluating BIS oversight of advanced-computing chips. Enforcement actions ran at about 100 a year in 2023 and 2024 and at about half that pace from March 2025 to the start of August 2026, *The Atlantic* reported. In April, Lutnick told a House Appropriations subcommittee that BIS needed 290 new enforcement officers. He didn’t mention the agents guarding him. His financial disclosure lists a $25 million mansion in Washington’s Berkley neighborhood, bought in 2024, plus properties in New York, the Hamptons and South Florida.

Kristi Noem’s DHS made a similar trade at the expense of taxpayers — only with aircraft. The department told the public it had an “urgent need” for deportation flights and spent $464 million on 10 jets through no-bid contracts, but ICE barely used them, and it already relied on private charters and sometimes military aircraft. Seven Boeing 737s sat in Lake Charles, Louisiana, for months with no staff to operate them, according to an Aug. 12 letter from Senate Appropriations Democrats. Four were meant for congressional delegations and three for deportation flights.

Nearly $200 million went to two Gulfstream G650s, one leased to Patel and one handed to border czar Tom Homan. A Boeing 787-8 was fitted with a bedroom, shower, kitchen, bar and flat-screen televisions to “serve dual missions — both as ICE deportation flights and for cabinet-level travel.”

Now, Noem is gone and DHS is removing the bedroom and loaning the plane to the Defense Department. The senators wrote that DHS “has not provided information on why it is necessary or a permissible use of funds” for the congressional planes, and said the department was “running an in-house private jet service.” DHS said the no-bid Daedalus Aviation contract “was made and approved by department leadership” before Markwayne Mullin was sworn in as secretary after Noem’s ouster, but contract records show it grew by $303 million the day he took the oath.

![Former DHS Secretary Kristi Noem arranged for the lease of several luxury aircraft that are now used by top administration officials for their own travel](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/24/14/34/GettyImages-2264701827.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Patel, who got one of those Gulfstreams, once held a different view of executive jets. In 2023, he said former FBI Director Christopher Wray’s “private jet that he pays for with taxpayer dollars to hop around the country” should be grounded. Two years later, flight records showed a Justice Department-registered jet flying from Manassas, Virginia, to State College, Pennsylvania, on Oct. 25, 2025, and on to Nashville about two and a half hours later.

Patel’s girlfriend, Alexis Wilkins, who lives in Nashville, had performed at a wrestling event at Penn State that he attended that weekend. Patel told lawmakers that “Congress made it mandatory” for him to use a government aircraft to get there.

Democrats on the Judiciary committees laid out the cost to the FBI in a July 8 letter. Rep. Jamie Raskin and Sen. Dick Durbin said Patel demanded “perks” on international trips, including “VIP snorkeling” in Hawaii, and that the “concerns and strains” of his prioritizing “personal entertainment” may have led to the resignation of the head of the FBI’s international operations. The FBI called the allegations “completely false” and said Patel had “reimbursed ALL personal travel and expenses.” MS NOW reported in November 2025 that FBI agents normally assigned to a SWAT team were also guarding Wilkins, with the FBI citing “hundreds of credible death threats” against her.

Military housing is another area where Trump high-ups have turned with security again the stated reason.

The Atlantic counted at least six senior appointees living in military housing by October 2025. Noem moved into the Coast Guard commandant’s residence at Joint Base Anacostia-Bolling, and Sen. Richard Blumenthal wrote her on Aug. 19, 2025, that she was living there rent-free while still paying rent on her apartment. “Your position as Secretary does not grant you the special privilege of taxpayer-funded housing,” he wrote. DHS spokeswoman Tricia McLaughlin said Noem was “horribly doxxed and targeted” and “no longer able to safely live in her own apartment.”

The Army told Congress in early 2025 that it would spend about $137,000 repairing a century-old house at Fort McNair that Hegseth would occupy, including $49,900 for an “emergency” paint job. Defense and Army officials said the house had sat vacant about a year and that Hegseth hadn’t personally requested the work. Reps. Debbie Wasserman Schultz and Rosa DeLauro asked why the painting needed to be done on an emergency basis and wrote that “many service members and their families currently live in unacceptable housing conditions.”

Some of the protection is warranted. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent moved onto a military base this month, according to two people familiar with the matter, after a senior intelligence official warned in a late-October memo that Iranian plots against U.S. personnel had reached an advanced stage.

A Treasury spokesperson said “the secretary will not be deterred by the Iranian regime’s threats,” and a source said security concerns grew after an aerial photo made it possible to locate the $12.5 million Georgetown house he bought after his confirmation.

Commerce makes the same argument about Lutnick, but its statement doesn’t say why the agents came from export enforcement rather than the department’s own Office of Security.

An official also cited reporting on the location of Lutnick’s house in Washington as justification for the security escalation, though it’s worth noting that much of that reporting was due to the fact that Lutnick had purchased a $25 million mansion from Fox News anchor Bret Baier — the most expensive home sale in Washington at the time.

But another official told *The Independent* that the moves by most — but not all — of the top Trump aides have been part of an arms race to extract the most privileges from their government service.

“They are like children — if one gets something, everyone else wants the same,” they said.
