Business confidence in Yorkshire and the Humber remains high despite ongoing challenges.

According to new data from Barclays, 87 per cent of business leaders in the region are optimistic about their prospects for the year ahead.

However, 82 per cent say that difficulty hiring skilled staff is limiting their growth.

Matt Hammerstein, CEO of Barclays UK Corporate Bank, said: "Businesses of all sizes increased borrowing in Q3, and more firms tell us they are ready to invest now that the Budget is behind them.

"But much of the investment planned for this year still hasn’t happened, and that is the gap to close."

The Barclays Business Prosperity Index shows that training existing staff is a key focus, with 48 per cent of businesses planning to invest in workforce development over the next 12 months.

In Yorkshire and the Humber, loan volumes rose by 3.7 per cent year-on-year in Q3, with loan balances up 8.9 per cent.

The research also highlights that 60 per cent of businesses describe current demand as strong for the time of year.

However, 42 per cent cite economic uncertainty as a major challenge, higher than the UK average of 35 per cent.

Rising operating costs—such as energy and raw materials—are also a concern, with 32 per cent of businesses in the region affected, compared to 28 per cent nationally.

Looking ahead to 2027, 40 per cent of business leaders identify recruitment and retention of skilled staff as a top priority, alongside growing sales and expanding operations.

Improving productivity and efficiency follows closely at 39 per cent.

Barclays is offering support through its Business Prosperity Fund, designed to help businesses invest in growth and resilience.

The bank has £22 billion available for lending to Business Banking and UK Corporate Banking clients in 2026, subject to normal lending assessment, status and application.
