## Decision on petrol station’s 6am alcohol licence plan

Gill Sherratt, Licensing Consultant And Russell Elliott, Penny Petroleum Compliance Manager.Courtesy Anttoni James Numminen.

A CONTROVERSIAL plan to sell alcohol from a petrol station has been approved despite warnings from a nearby Conservative Club that it could fuel anti-social behaviour, littering, and disorder.

Penny Petroleum has been granted a licence to sell alcohol from 6am to 11pm at Whitby Service Station on Flowergate Cross after councillors ruled that the business would primarily operate as a convenience store rather than a filling station.

The decision follows objections from local residents and the Whitby Conservative Club, which argued the proposal could add to existing problems involving drinking and vandalism in the area.

Representing the Whitby Conservative Club, Michelle Dolan argued that the licence risked adding to existing problems in the area. She told councillors the club had already dealt with incidents involving drinking, littering and vandalism, and warned that later alcohol sales could increase noise and disturbance for nearby residents.

“The club has already experienced anti-social behaviour involving youths drinking, littering and vandalising club property,” she said.

Ms Dolan also raised concerns about traffic and public safety around the forecourt, telling the hearing that she had personally witnessed vehicles mounting pavements and queuing back towards the nearby roundabout.

However, Penny Petroleum insisted the redevelopment – which could see the site open next June – would have the opposite effect, creating a modern convenience store with permanent staffing, extensive CCTV coverage and increased supervision of the site.

Representing the company, licensing consultant Gill Sherratt said the project was about far more than alcohol sales.

She told councillors that the business planned to invest around £150,000 in redeveloping the site, expand the store to around 170 square metres, and create more than a dozen new jobs, in addition to management roles.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21768697.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Gill Sherratt, Licensing Consultant And Russell Elliott, Penny Petroleum Compliance Manager. Courtesy Anttoni James Numminen

Russell Elliott, compliance manager for Penny Petroleum, said the Whitby site would operate more like a convenience store than a traditional petrol station once redevelopment was complete.

They presented evidence from comparable sites within the company’s network of 96 forecourts, arguing that most customer transactions would relate to shopping rather than fuel purchases alone.

The licensing sub-committee ruled that the redeveloped premises would not primarily operate as a petrol station and approved the licence.

Committee chairman Cllr Subash Sharma said: “This is an application for a proposed business and it’s clear that the applicant plans to make significant changes to the business.

“Penny Petroleum is an experienced operator and having had regard to the application and the representations, we have decided that the proposal is appropriate for the promotion of licensing objectives, and is granted as applied for.”

Speaking after the meeting, Ms Sherratt said local concerns were understandable but argued the investment would ultimately benefit the town.

She told the Local Democracy Reporting Service (LDRS): “Residents have a right to be concerned about new things coming in, and I say that genuinely.

“They are worried about change but trust me, they will become the best customers of that lovely new facility, because Penny Petroleum is a great family-owned business.”

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21556599.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The Penny Petroleum Whitby Service Station That Has Applied For A 6am Premises Licence From North Yorkshire Council. Google Maps

During the hearing, company representatives argued that a well-run business with trained staff, counter-service sales and modern security measures would help deter rather than encourage anti-social behaviour.

No objections were submitted by local authorities, including North Yorkshire Police.

Ms Sherratt added: “People in Whitby should be excited for a new service as well as jobs and investment for the local economy.”
