Farmers have been urged to put safety first and take practical steps to protect themselves on the job.

The advice was shared during the Future Farmers of Yorkshire’s Autumn Debate, held at the Great Yorkshire Showground in Harrogate on Monday, November 23.

The event, titled ‘Heads Up: Practical Steps for a Safe and Productive Farm,’ focused on day-to-day actions that can improve safety, wellbeing, and efficiency.

Jane Gurney MBE, founder of the Tilly Trailer Pass scheme, spoke at the debate and said: "Remember to do your daily checks before you go out, like a HGV driver.

"Daily checks are incredibly important."

The Tilly Trailer Pass, launched after the death of Ms Gurney’s son Harry Christian-Allan in a trailer accident, is now a national scheme promoting trailer safety on farms.

Ms Gurney also highlighted the importance of supervision for young people using machinery.

She said: "If you’re going to have a young person on a machine, there should be someone there who can responsibly ‘hold their hand’ in case there’s a problem."

Rhiannon Wilson, health and safety office manager at CXCS Group, warned against complacency, saying: "A problem we have sometimes in farming is that we say ‘I’ve done this a thousand times before’.

"I think this is one of the most dangerous things a farmer can say."

She pointed out that farming as a way of life can sometimes blur safety boundaries, especially around children.

Ms Wilson added: "You would never take your children to a construction site, but we do on a farm because ‘we’re a family farm and that’s just what we do’."

The debate was chaired by Sally Conner, business development executive at McClarrons.

She said: "An accident on the farm can have a life-changing impact.

"Please be proactive, not reactive, in every aspect of your business."
