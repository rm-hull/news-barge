# Donald Trump has a lot to answer for as we face big energy bill rises this winter: Carl Les

## As winter approaches, and the first frosts and minus temperatures hit our shores, the rising cost of keeping warm is a concern for many households across North Yorkshire.

For older people, particularly those living alone or on a fixed income, deciding when and sometimes whether to put the heating on can be difficult. Nobody should have to choose between staying warm and paying for other essentials.

As Chair of Trustees at North Yorkshire Citizens Advice and Law Centre (NYCALC), I am very aware of the challenges facing our communities. I also know how important it is that people can access support before a difficult situation becomes a crisis.

One message worth remembering during the colder months is to heat the person, not the home. Thinking about how to keep ourselves warm without heating every room can help manage energy costs. However, this is not always straightforward, particularly for people with health conditions, mobility difficulties or limited incomes.

The scale of the challenge is clear from the people NYCALC support on behalf of North Yorkshire Council`s Warm and Well initiative. Of the households advised by its Warm & Well team, 20 per cent have someone of pension age living in them, not surprising in the demographics of our county. Meanwhile, 74 per cent include someone with a disability or long-term health condition.

These figures highlight the close relationship between energy affordability, health and wellbeing.

For rural communities, heating can be particularly expensive. Many households rely on heating oil rather than mains gas, and prices can fluctuate considerably.

Between July and October this year, heating oil rose from around 69p to 111p per litre. An order I placed this week was 116p per litre. With minimum orders often starting at 500 litres, sometimes plus administration and delivery charges, this can mean a substantial bill for households already on tight budgets.

President Trump has a lot to answer for with his ill-thought through war. It may be a worthy goal to stop an irrational government developing nuclear weapons, but it seems the tactics were not drawn up by military strategists, more by a TV presenter.

Rising energy bills add further pressure, particularly for those without a fixed tariff. Combined with inflation in food, transport and other essentials, it is easy to understand why many people are struggling.

This is where NYCALC’s Warm & Well team is making a real difference. Working on behalf of North Yorkshire Council, the team distributes funding through the Crisis and Resilience Fund, providing financial support to eligible low-income households experiencing a crisis.

This can include help with prepayment meter top-ups, monthly or quarterly energy bills and essential heating fuels such as oil, logs, coal and LPG.

Alongside financial assistance, the team also offers practical energy advice to help people reduce costs and make homes more efficient. Sometimes small changes, such as draught-proofing doors and windows or installing radiator reflectors, can help reduce wasted energy and save money. The team can also advise on larger improvements, including insulation and more efficient heating systems.

For those looking to plan ahead, Warm & Well can help people understand their home’s Energy Performance Certificate (EPC), which highlights how energy efficient a property is and identifies potential improvements. The team can also discuss grants and other support that may be available.

There is help for people who may need additional assistance during a power cut, too. The Priority Services Register is a free service provided by energy suppliers and network operators to help ensure eligible customers, including older people and those with health conditions, receive additional support during a power cut. Warm & Well can help people understand whether they are eligible and get registered.

I would encourage anyone worried about heating bills, struggling to keep their home warm or unsure where to turn for help to get in touch with the team. You do not need to wait until you are in crisis. Seeking advice early can make a real difference, and there is no shame in asking for support.

Finally, I want to offer my heartfelt thanks to the volunteers, charity workers, community groups and everyone who gives their time, skills or resources to help others across North Yorkshire. Whether you are checking in on an older neighbour, volunteering at a local organisation or simply making time for a friendly conversation, your contribution matters.

Winter can be a difficult and lonely time for some, but the kindness and generosity of people across our communities make an enormous difference. North Yorkshire is a better place because of those who give so much to help others.

Let’s continue looking out for one another and make sure nobody has to face the challenges of winter alone.

The dreadful news from Bradford this week is sobering. Ibrar Ali, a 52 year old councillor was stabbed whilst doing community work in his ward. A neighbour described him as “a very kind, warm hearted man whose goal is to serve, to help everyone”.

No one of any party or none should face such an attack. I wish him a speedy recovery to full health and service.

Carl Les is the leader of North Yorkshire Council
