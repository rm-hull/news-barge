A 33-year-old man has been found guilty of seven counts of attempted murder after he stabbed 10 passengers at random on a London-bound train last year.

Anthony Williams of Peterborough admitted stabbing 14 people over two days on 31 October and 1 November 2025, including 10 passengers on an LNER service from Doncaster to London. He denied 14 counts of attempted murder.

After a six-week trial at Cambridge crown court, a jury found Williams guilty on seven counts. Deliberations on the other seven are continuing.

The jury found Williams guilty of the attempted murder of Jonathan Gjoshe, 22, Sachin Balakrishnan, 29, David Presland, 73, Scott Bletcher, 50, Scott Green, 21, Stephen Crean, 61, and Samir Zitouni, 48, who were all on the LNER service, which made an emergency stop in Huntingdon last year.

Williams, who psychiatrists said was experiencing “acute psychosis as a result of his schizophrenia” at the time, previously admitted to wounding with intent to cause really serious bodily harm to 12 people and attempting to do so to two others.

During the trial, the court was told how passengers on the train from Doncaster to King’s Cross on 1 November 2025 were subjected to a “storm of violence” that left some with serious injuries and many fearing for their lives.

In the 24 hours before the train attack, Williams had attacked three people in Peterborough and a 17-year-old boy on the Docklands Light Railway in London in the early hours of 1 November, jurors were told.

Christopher Paxton KC, for the prosecution, told the court: “Williams, armed with a knife, caused terror and fear on an unimaginable scale, moving up and down a busy train from which there was no escape, stabbing several people as he did so.”

Crean was one of several passengers praised during the trial for their “extraordinary bravery” when trying to wrestle the knife off Williams. Among his injuries, he was stabbed to the left side of his scalp within “0.79cm of the brain”, jurors were told.

Green was also stabbed while he attempted to grab the knife from Williams. A member of the train crew, Samir Zitouni, was also stabbed several times as he tried to use a frying pan to stop Williams, jurors heard.

Paxton said: “In the face of that threat and the defendant’s sheer brutality, some stood tall to protect others trying to de-escalate his rampage, defending themselves and putting themselves in harm’s way to protect strangers.”

Gillian Jones KC, for the defence, said Williams had been “in the clutches of an acute psychotic episode” and that he “didn’t intend to kill, he didn’t intend to bring about death”. Williams was not called on to give evidence during the trial.

Dr Bernard Chin, a psychiatrist instructed by the defence, said Williams had been experiencing “paranoid persecutory delusions, meaning he had ideas and beliefs that people were going to harm him and that this was an organised conspiracy that came about due to … the devil”.

Jones said Williams’s actions during the mass stabbing, including the fact he attacked one of the people on the LNER train before walking away, was evidence that he had not intended to kill. This was “consistent with a delusional belief he was under attack”, she said.

Mr Justice Calver instructed the jury to continue their deliberations on Thursday, and said he would accept a majority of 11 or 10 on the remaining counts.
