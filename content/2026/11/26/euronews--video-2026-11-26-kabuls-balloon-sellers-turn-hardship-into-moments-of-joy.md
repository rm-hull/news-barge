Clusters of brightly coloured balloons floated above the bustling streets of Kabul, Afghanistan, on 23 and 24 November 2026, as vendors carried them by hand or tied them to bicycles.

Among the sellers was 49-year-old Shamsuddin Alizadeh, who spends his days filling balloons with helium. Growing up amid war and poverty, he dreamt of having one to play with. Today, selling them is his livelihood.

For 19-year-old Omran Alizadeh, the trade is about more than making a living. His shop sells between 600 and 700 balloons a day, he says, before he heads out with another 100 to sell at busy intersections.

For him, one of the greatest rewards is seeing the smiles on children's faces when they buy a balloon.
