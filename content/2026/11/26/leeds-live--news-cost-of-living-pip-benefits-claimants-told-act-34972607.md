# PIP benefits claimants told to act now over £164.30 weekly payment change

## PIP claimants have been urged to check their details

Personal Independence Payment (PIP) recipients are being advised to verify their information is current following a potential £165 payment adjustment. Benefits specialists are encouraging claimants to take immediate action regarding this significant change to their entitlement.

Money Wellness, a financial support organisation, has urged recipients to determine whether they might qualify for higher payments. The organisation stressed the importance of understanding how the system operates, as proactive measures could prove beneficial, reports the Mirror.

Sebrina McCullough, director of external relations, said: "If your condition has got worse or you need more help with everyday activities, you can ask the DWP to look at your PIP award again.. 'Don't wait'".

## 'Don't wait'

"You don't have to wait for your next review. Instead, contact the PIP enquiry line and report a change of circumstances. You'll usually be sent a form to explain what's changed and may need another assessment."

PIP offers variable payments designed to assist with additional expenses arising from long-term health conditions or disabilities. The amount awarded depends on how significantly your condition affects two key areas: mobility and daily living activities.

Should your circumstances have substantially altered recently, you may be eligible for a considerable increase to your existing award. PIP consists of two components - daily living and mobility - each offering standard and enhanced rates depending on how significantly you're affected in these areas. It's possible to receive nothing for one component while claiming either rate for the other.

## £8,500 payment increase

Payment amounts can differ considerably:.

The minimum weekly rate stands at £30.30 if you're only receiving the standard mobility component.

The maximum weekly amount reaches £194.60 if you qualify for the enhanced rate of both components, comprising £114.60 weekly for daily living and £80 weekly for mobility.

This represents a weekly difference of £164.30, equating to £657.20 per four-week payment cycle, totalling an additional £8,500 annually.

## 'Don't put it off'

Ms McCullough stressed the importance of notifying the DWP straight away if your circumstances change in a way that could increase your entitlement. She advised: "Make sure you do it as soon as there's a change – don't put it off - as the date you tell the DWP can affect when any increase takes effect.

"Generally, your increased needs must have been present for at least three months and be expected to last for another nine. Just be aware that the DWP can look at your whole award again, so your payments could go up, down or stop."

Given the possibility your award could shift in either direction, the specialist suggests consulting a free benefits adviser before contacting the DWP.

## Government review

A significant examination of the PIP system has recently been released, with the initiative jointly led by DWP minister Sir Stephen Timms. The investigation has put forward a number of modifications, including enhancing the application and assessment procedures.

Two principal recommendations proposed to strengthen the assessments were:

* Reduce unnecessary award reviews for individuals with lifelong or degenerative conditions
* Restructure award reviews to focus on what things have changed in a person’s circumstances and functional impact, rather than carrying out a full review against all the activities and descriptors for the benefit.

The complete report is available on the Government website.

At the Autumn Budget, the Government confirmed it will publish a policy paper next year in response to the review's conclusions, implementing any required adjustments to the benefit.
