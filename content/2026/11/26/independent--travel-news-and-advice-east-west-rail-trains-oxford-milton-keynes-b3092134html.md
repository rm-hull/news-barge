Passengers will board East West Rail services this weekend, nearly two years after opening was delayed by a trade union dispute.

Fare-paying passenger services running between Oxford and Milton Keynes have not yet launched due to an ongoing argument regarding whether guards will be employed on the line by the train operator.

As this conflict remains active, the Chiltern Railways services designated for the route are still currently unable to operate.

However, Great Western Railway (GWR) services diverted between South Wales, Bristol and London will travel along the track while engineering works are carried out in the capital.

Construction teams handed control of the line to Network Rail in December 2024.

Chiltern Railways, which operated as a private company until nationalisation in September this year, was chosen to run services in March 2025.

The firm remains in a row with the Rail, Maritime and Transport union (RMT) over plans for driver-only operated (DOO) services, a system where the train driver is required to verify that closing the doors is completely safe without receiving assistance from a guard.

![Trains with fare-paying passengers on board are yet to use the line between Oxford and Milton Keynes because of a disagreement over whether the operator will employ guards on the route](https://static.independent.co.uk/2025/08/05/12/05121135-671af8c3-1112-475c-ae33-bd3612fd3d5c.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

A Department for Transport spokesperson said: “We are supporting Chiltern Railways as they work with unions and other industry partners to get these services up and running as soon as possible.

“The East West Rail project will unlock thousands of jobs and homes and kickstart hundreds of thousands of pounds of economic growth across England but we need services to be allowed to start before we can start seeing those benefits.”

While the line is built between Oxford and Milton Keynes, work continues to build the full route eastwards to Cambridge.

Eddie Dempsey, the RMT general secretary, has previously warned DOO represented “an unacceptable weakening of safety standards and threatens the vital role train managers play in protecting passengers and ensuring the safe operation of services”.

GWR trains to London from Bristol and South Wales usually arrive at Paddington, via Reading.

But with Paddington shut for works, trains will instead call at Oxford and reverse at Milton Keynes, en route to Euston.

According to GWR’s website, “rail improvement work will impact all train services for London Paddington”.
