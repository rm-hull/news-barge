# Father tells planning inspector he ‘won’t make it’ if family forced from traveller site

## A father told a planning inspector he “won’t make it” if his family is forced to leave an unauthorised traveller site following the tragic death of his teenage son.

A planning hearing took place in Richmond on Tuesday (Nov 24) following an appeal against an enforcement notice issued to James Miller.

Mr Miller Snr and his wife Naomi moved onto land near Hudswell earlier this year.

Hardcore was laid on part of a field south of White House, two wooden sheds were erected and a static caravan was brought onto the land. Two touring caravans are also now at the site.

![The couple have moved onto land near Hudswell, which they've called Miller's Meadow.](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOmEyM2M3NWU1LTNiNDktNDNjOS1hMjY2LWUyM2FkMTVhNDkwNzoyZjg1NWQ0NS00NGY5LTRlYzQtOTUxOS1lMGVjY2IwZjkzNjM=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


North Yorkshire Council issued an enforcement notice requiring the land to be returned to agricultural use because the development did not have planning permission.

This prompted the Millers, who have Romani heritage but say their families have lived in Skipton for five generations, to appeal to the Planning Inspectorate.

The couple argue there is no suitable alternative Gypsy and Traveller accommodation available for the family locally or elsewhere in North Yorkshire, and that losing their current home would leave them with nowhere appropriate to go.

The hearing was told that the couple’s 19-year-old son Alfie died in September following a crash near Selby.

![Alfie Miller.](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOjhjNmYwMDRkLWZmNWYtNDZhZi1hMjk5LTJiY2I0MDA4NzQxYzo5NjU2MWVhNS0xM2Y5LTRlNGQtYmM2ZC1jOThjOTM4M2NiNmY=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


He was travelling in a 4×4 which collided with the rear of a tractor towing a large trailer loaded with hay bales, North Yorkshire Police said.

Emergency services were called to the crash on the A163 at Barlby, near the junction of Whitemoor Lane, at 7.30pm on Monday but Alfie was pronounced dead at the scene.

Another 19-year-old man travelling in the 4×4 and the driver of the tractor were also taken to hospital with injuries, police said in an earlier statement.

Tuesday’s council meeting also heard that Mr Miller Snr had a number of health problems.

Asked by planning inspector Thomas Shields about the impact on his health if he lost the appeal, Mr Miller Snr said: “I won’t make it, that’s the impact of it. And now my son’s gone, I don’t have anything to go on for.”

Mr Miller Snr said he had not realised he was doing anything wrong by moving onto the land.

He said: “Genuinely, as I’m sat here now, I never knew that I’d done anything wrong. They’re all on wheels, and they’re two wood sheds — I still don’t get it.”

Mr Miller told the hearing that Alfie had been living at the site at the time of his death, sharing a caravan with his 14-year-old brother.

He said his eldest son, James Miller Jnr, his wife and their two young children would now be living at the site to support the family following the tragedy.

Mr Miller Jnr told the hearing at the council’s offices at Mercury House: “We wanted to purposely get the point across that we are not a family that arrived over the Bank Holiday weekend and suddenly set up on land.

“Miller’s Meadow was bought legitimately with our money, and my dad spent about four months working on it to make it a home for our family.”

Planning agent Mike Carr, of MAT Design Limited, argued that changes to national planning policy introduced after the enforcement notice was served had implications for the case.

The appeal claims the former Richmondshire area cannot demonstrate a five-year supply of deliverable Traveller pitches and that the most recent Gypsy and Traveller Accommodation Assessment dates from 2013.

The council has argued the site is in an unsustainable countryside location with insufficient access to services.

However, the planning agent disputed this, pointing out that Hudswell has a pub, shop and a regular bus service to Richmond.

The agent also argued that new planning policies recognise that Gypsy and Traveller accommodation may be appropriate outside established settlements, particularly where there is an unmet need.

He claimed North Yorkshire Council was relying on older local planning policies that conflict with the new national framework and should therefore carry very limited weight.

Upper Dales councillor Yvonne Peacock attended the hearing as an observer.

Speaking afterwards, she said: “So many of our residents follow the planning rules about building in the open countryside, which is why this development at White House should be removed because they have not applied for planning permission.”

The planning inspector is expected to announce his decision on the appeal before Christmas.
