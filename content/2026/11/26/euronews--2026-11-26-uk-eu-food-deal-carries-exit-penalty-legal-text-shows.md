## Political parties Reform UK and the Conservatives have both signalled they would consider unpicking Andy Burnham's Brexit reset if they win power at the next general election in 2029.

Britain and the European Union would each have to pay to rebuild border checks if they walked away from their new food and drink deal within the first 10 years, according to the legal text published on Thursday.

The termination clause requires whichever side quits early to cover "reasonable and proportionate costs" of restoring the infrastructure the deal removes. Any dispute over the bill would go to an international tribunal, UK officials said.

"That termination clause is a standard practice in international treaties, and this one applies equally to both sides, including the EU," Prime Minister Andy Burnham's deputy spokesperson told reporters.

The sanitary and phytosanitary (SPS) agreement was one of three deals Burnham signed with EU leaders in Brussels, Belgium, on 20 November. The other two link the two sides' carbon markets and allow young people to study and work across borders.

The government says the SPS deal, due to take effect in 2028, could add £5.1 billion to the UK economy by cutting red tape on food and drink exports.

The clause was published days after Reform UK leader Nigel Farage vowed to "repeal" the reset. Conservative leader Kemi Badenoch said on Thursday she "does not support" it.

Charles Grant, director of the Centre for European Reform think tank, told AFP the provision was "an insurance policy" for the EU, given Britain's continuing debate over its relationship with the bloc.

Legislation to put the deals into effect was introduced in parliament on Thursday, and MPs are due to debate it on 7 December. It would align the UK with EU rules in the sectors covered. British experts would be consulted on new laws but would have no vote, which critics say makes Britain a "rule taker".

Burnham, who replaced Keir Starmer in July, has said the agreements are probably the most that can be achieved in this parliament. He plans a year of informal brainstorming with EU leaders before setting out options for the long-term relationship. These range from keeping things as they are to rejoining the bloc, which he has said would be "unthinkable" without a referendum.
