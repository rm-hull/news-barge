A flash mob is set to take over York city centre this weekend.

The public dance performance will take place on Sunday, November 29, in St Helen's Square as part of a campaign to highlight violence against women and girls.

The eye-catching event is being held in support of York-based charity Independent Domestic Abuse Services (IDAS) which supports victims of domestic abuse and sexual violence.

It was co-organised by IDAS, SK Studio York and York Dance Company, as part of the international 16 Days of Activism campaign.

Performances will take place every 15 minutes in St Helen's Square, starting at 2pm until 4pm.

Sibylle Kockjeu, founder and director of SK Studio York, said: "If we can use dance to start conversations, raise awareness and make even one person feel less alone, then we are doing something truly meaningful.

"The response to last year’s flash mob was incredibly moving and a powerful reminder of what we can achieve when we come together.

"I’m proud that we can once again use our voice to support the vital work of IDAS."

This is the second year the flash mob has taken place.

Last year’s event attracted more than 100,000 views online and generated a wave of public support, with messages from survivors and members of the community.

IDAS said events like this play a vital role in uniting people and showing visible support for victims and survivors.

Through movement and music, the performance aims to inspire solidarity, spark conversation, and shine a light on ongoing efforts to create a safe society.

The organisation began in the 1970s as a women’s refuge in York and has since grown to support individuals across North and South Yorkshire.

In 2025, IDAS managed more than 20,762 referrals.

Members of the public described last year’s performance as "a fantastic, powerful performance and what a great service". Another person said: "As a DV survivor I found this really powerful."
