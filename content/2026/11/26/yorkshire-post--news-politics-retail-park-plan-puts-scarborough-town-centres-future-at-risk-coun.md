# Retail park plan puts Scarborough town centre’s future ‘at risk’, council warned

## Plans for a major retail and commercial development in North Yorkshire could create a rival shopping destination and damage Scarborough town centre, councillors have warned.

Scarborough town councillors have unanimously voted against the proposed development at the Filey Road Park and Ride site – despite concerns about “appearing anti-business” – and called on North Yorkshire Council to refuse the application unless a series of concerns are addressed.

The scheme would see a supermarket, shops, commercial units, a petrol station and electric vehicle charging facilities built alongside the existing park and ride facility.

The proposal has already attracted significant opposition from nearby residents, with concerns raised about traffic, noise, light pollution and the impact on local businesses.

Proposing the motion, Coun William Stuart told members the development posed a “real risk” to Scarborough town centre.

He said: “Scarborough town centre is already struggling with falling footfall. “We can’t afford to allow that risk.”

He argued that concerns raised by an independent review commissioned by North Yorkshire Council had not yet been satisfactorily addressed.

According to the motion debated at a full meeting of the town council, questions remain over whether suitable town centre sites had been properly considered, whether the developer had shown sufficient flexibility over the scale of the proposal, and whether the cumulative impact on the town centre had been fully assessed.

Speaking at the meeting on Tuesday, November 24, he added: “This is not opposition to investment. It is opposition to putting town centre businesses at risk.”

Councillors also criticised the developer’s reliance on a public consultation carried out in 2025, arguing it related to an earlier version of the scheme that included a hotel and family pub which have since been removed.

The meeting also heard concerns about traffic around the site.

Coun Sarah Mason said: “Filey Road is horrific as it is with the traffic,” adding that councillors needed to listen to local residents.

The motion notes that members of the Wheatcroft Community Association voted overwhelmingly against the scheme, with 81 per cent opposed, nine per cent in favour and 10 per cent undecided.

Reform’s town mayor, Thomas Murray, said he was concerned about the impact on existing businesses.

He told the meeting: “I worry for our town centre businesses.”

Not all councillors were convinced the town should oppose out-of-town developments in principle, and Coun Chris Clark questioned whether Scarborough risked being seen as hostile to investment.

He asked: “Is there a risk the town is seen as investment-averse and future developments go elsewhere?

“Are we being a little too restrictive?”

However, supporters of the motion insisted their concerns related to weaknesses in the evidence supporting the application rather than opposition to development itself.

The town council’s motion calls on North Yorkshire Council to require the applicant to address concerns about the impact on Scarborough town centre, the consideration of alternative sites and the assumptions underpinning the transport assessment.

Councillors also requested that any significant new evidence or amendments be made publicly available and that the town council be allowed to comment before a final decision is taken.

The application remains under consideration, and no date has yet been set for its determination.
