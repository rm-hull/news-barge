Thousands of army reservists have been sent a letter from the Ministry of Defence in what Wes Streeting said was a “necessary precaution” amid a “heightened threat environment” facing the UK.

The defence secretary confirmed the letter – believed to be the first of its kind since the Cold War – had been sent out, asking them to update their contact details and share their current skill sets and employment.

It comes amid increasing threats from Vladimir Putin’s Russia, with British defence businesses being told to urgently step up their security arrangements during secret briefings with MI5.

Mr Streeting said there is “no doubt” that the country is “operating in a heightened threat environment” and stressed the public should find the letter “reassuring rather than alarming”.

“We have written out to our strategic reserve,” he told Sky News.

“These are people who previously served in our armed forces. We’ve written to them to make sure their details are up to date and also we have an understanding of the experience and the skills that they’ve developed since leaving the armed forces.

![The defence secretary confirmed the letter had been sent out](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/26/08/01M86NJHAD5Z6YS64Q8ZDXVHKN.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“This should be information that I think the government ought to hold as standard, whatever the threat environment.

“But there’s no doubt, and I’ve talked about this – as have all of my counterparts across Nato in recent days, weeks and months – we are operating in a heightened threat environment.

“And so this degree of readiness, I think, is a sensible, necessary precaution.

“People should find it reassuring rather than alarming.”

An army source told *The Telegraph*: “Just because you were once a tank driver in the Army, your civilian skills now could be completely different and actually more useful to defence.”

It comes as it’s announced that the UK’s nuclear submarines will spend less time undergoing maintenance and more time at sea under an £8 billion investment aimed at bolstering Britain’s nuclear deterrent and warfighting readiness.

Mr Streeting said what he expected to be “the single biggest procurement decision of this Parliament” will see submarine maintenance and repair facilities upgraded, with dry dock yards to run around the clock.

![Reservists have been asked to update their contact details and share their current skill sets and employment](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/07/23/14/2284897703..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Calls to accelerate submarine maintenance have intensified after HMS Vengeance, one of four submarines that carry Britain’s Trident nuclear missiles, earlier this month concluded a record seven-month deployment.

At least one nuclear-powered and armed ballistic missile submarine is at sea at all times to protect the UK and its Nato allies, but the deployments have lengthened as refits of sister boats have overrun.

The five-year contract awarded to British defence giant Babcock is expected to create around 3,000 highly-skilled jobs, mainly in Plymouth, Glasgow and Bristol, as well as 2,500 apprenticeship and graduate opportunities, according to the MoD.
