After two weeks of voting that bracketed the launch of Grand Theft Auto 6, Rockstar Games employees have voted overwhelmingly against a recognition agreement with the IWGB Game Workers Union.

25% of Rockstar employees voted in favor of the recognition agreement, the union said, while 75% voted against it. 1,544 employees took part in the vote, representing a turnout of just over 93%.

Rockstar workers actually founded a union earlier this year—the Rockstar Game Workers Union, a subsidiary of the Independent Workers Union of Great Britain—and it will continue to operate as it has. The IWGB said the outcome will not prevent Rockstar workers from organizing or exercising union rights, but the company will not be required to regularly meet with union representatives for collective bargaining or other relevant matters.

Despite the vote, the union said it will continue with organizing efforts at Rockstar.

"For anyone at Rockstar who believes in a fairer workplace, a fairer sector and a fairer society—this isn’t a sign to retreat, it’s a sign to double down on what we stand for. Hundreds of our coworkers voted in favour of our union at Rockstar, yet sadly we recognize we have more work to do to build understanding and win the trust of more of our colleagues," the IWGB Rockstar Game Workers Union said. "We’re ready to put in the effort to make that happen.

"Whilst we haven’t emerged with a recognition deal, this ballot has put our union on the radar of every worker, and opened the door for new kinds of conversation to start happening. People entirely unfamiliar with unions were for the first time debating employment conditions, collective bargaining rights, and our contracts. We heard from many more colleagues about what mattered to them, and we hope people will continue to engage so that we can see where these new discussions can lead us."

In a separate statement, Rockstar head of publishing Jennfer Kolbe said the studio is "extraordinarily proud of our team, the culture we've built together and the launch of Grand Theft Auto 6."

"This result reflects our team's strong support for continuing the direct relationships, collaboration and open dialogue that have helped make Rockstar Games so innovative and strong," Kolbe said. "As we look ahead, we're excited to build on all we've accomplished together."

Rockstar, which said it worked with the IWGB to ensure employees were able to make an informed decision in a fair and transparent vote, came under fire in October 2025 for firing more than 30 employees, all of whom were members of a private trade union Discord channel. The IWGB called it "the most ruthless act of union busting in the history of the UK games industry," while Rockstar says they were fired for leaking information about "upcoming and unannounced games." That matter is now before an employment tribunal; closing submissions were made last week.
