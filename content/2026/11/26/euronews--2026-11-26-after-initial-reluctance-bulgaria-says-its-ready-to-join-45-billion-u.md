## Bulgaria will contribute to the new €45 billion support loan to Ukraine, Prime Minister Rumen Radev confirmed on Thursday. But the country will not help pay for weapons and ammunition.

Bulgarian Prime Minister Rumen Radev has confirmed his country will join the European Union's new €45 billion support loan to Ukraine but will refuse to contribute to the purchases of weapons and ammunition.

The loan is meant to cover two-thirds of Kyiv's 2027 wartime deficit.

"We are ready to join it," Radev said on Thursday.

Radev had initially expressed reservations about the financial scheme, arguing his country had to rein in its public spending, which is under intense scrutiny.

As a result, Bulgaria was coupled with Slovakia, Hungary and the Czech Republic in the group that opposes the new loan altogether.

But on Thursday, Radev announced his country would accept the proposal, provided it remained exempt from its military component.

"We have a huge historical diaspora of 300,000 Bulgarians living in Ukraine. And our wishes and our request (are) to concentrate our support in the areas of the Bulgarian population in Ukraine," Radev said at **a summit in Brussels**.

"So we are going to contribute, but again, no money from Bulgaria for weapons, no money for (the) war in Ukraine," he added.

The European Commission has split the new €45 billion loan into €25 billion for military aid and €20 billion for budget aid. Altogether, it will generate between €2 billion and €2.5 billion in annual payment interests, which **will fall on member states**.

Kyiv will not be asked to repay the principal until Moscow provides war reparations.

Radev welcomed the loan's dual structure and said his country would chip in the €20 billion allocated for budget aid, intended to cover public services and salaries.

Radev said he would discuss the matter with Commission President Ursula von der Leyen during Thursday's summit, where support for Ukraine is high on the agenda.

Von der Leyen, who presented the loan last week, had publicly offered reluctant member states the option to opt in *à la carte* depending on their preference. She said she was "very confident" that all 27 countries would back her proposal.

Radev's confirmation comes as political pressure intensifies on Slovakia, Hungary and the Czech Republic to join the loan.

Germany, the Netherlands, Sweden, Denmark and Ireland are among those who have said the next EU budget, also under negotiation, cannot be agreed until support for Ukraine is locked in for 2027.

"It's crystal clear that the Russian aggression in Ukraine is one of the biggest challenges that we face. And Ukrainians are fighting for their own freedom, but also for European democracy and values," Dutch Prime Minister Rob Jetten said on Thursday.

"We need to stand shoulder to shoulder with the Ukrainians (who) will need more military and financial support in the years to come. And that is something that no EU country can opt out of. Everyone needs to be in solidarity with the Ukrainians."

It remains to be seen whether leaders can bring Slovakia, Hungary and the Czech Republic into the fold. Unlike Bulgaria, the three countries are already exempted from the previous €90 billion support loan and are bent on continuing their opt-out policy.
