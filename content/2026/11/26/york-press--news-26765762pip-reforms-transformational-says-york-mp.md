Fresh proposals to change who can claim disability benefit could be "transformational for disabled people", according to Rachael Maskell MP who rebelled against the previous government’s proposed welfare reforms.

Ms Maskell lost the Labour whip from Sir Keir Starmer’s government after leading a rebellion against the welfare reforms, which were later abandoned following backlash from MPs.

The York Central MP appeared to welcome the findings of the year-long Timms review which found reforms to the personal independence payment (Pip) system could involve a “rebalancing process” to ensure payments better reflect a person’s needs.

But the report also acknowledged some claimants might get less money in future, with Ms Maskell saying she will work with ministers to avoid people losing out.

Ms Maskell told The Press: “Eighteen months ago, having listened to disabled people, I prevented government from cutting support from those who need it through personal independence payments.

“From this, the Timms review was determined to co-produce a fresh approach to ensure that social security bridged the gap for the additional costs of living in being a disabled person.”

She called the Timms review a “more intelligent approach” which has “paid dividends and has recognised that the current system is dehumanising and fails to accurately reflect the support people need”.

Under the approach, Ms Maskell said: “Those with enduring long-term conditions and nearing the end of life will not have to go through reassessment processes, while others will engage more in a conversation about the support they need to function in life and at work.

“This new approach has the potential to be transformational for disabled people.”

The MP did, however, note that the Timms review has “thrown up a difference in the approach” of the Milburn Review, which recommended barring under-25s from Pip altogether in favour of a new youth allowance.

Ms Maskell said: “I will work with government to ensure that no matter your age, that employment support and additional support will be available, so our young people can also live independently as they seek work and live in our community.”

Meanwhile, York Outer MP Luke Charters welcomed the Timms review’s recommendation of faster Pip payments for those with the most severe conditions and terminal illnesses.

"It seems like common sense to me that someone shouldn't have to repeatedly prove they have a condition that isn't going to get better," the Labour MP said. “Ultimately, I want my constituents to spend less time fighting for the support they need and more time being getting on with their lives.”

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21050678.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Luke Charters, MP for York Outer *(Image: Supplied)*

Mr Charters said he had “first-hand just how stressful and exhausting the [Pip] process can be”.

“I've long been clear that our welfare system needs reform, both to ensure it is sustainable and, crucially, that it properly supports those who need it.

“For many of my constituents, PIP is essential to maintaining their independence. I'm pleased that the review recommends keeping PIP as a cash-first benefit.

“Disabled people know their own needs better than anyone else, and whilst greater access to personalised support is welcome, that must be a genuine choice.”
