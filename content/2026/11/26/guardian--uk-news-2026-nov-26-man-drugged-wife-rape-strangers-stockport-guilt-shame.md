A man who drugged and raped his wife, and invited other men into the family home to assault her, sobbed in court as he told a jury he felt “guilt and shame” over “the pain I’ve caused”.

The man, who cannot be named to protect the anonymity of his victim, looked gaunt and red-faced as he gave evidence in a trial of nine other men who are alleged to have raped or sexually assaulted the woman, or encouraged the abuse online.

He held his head in his hands at times, sighed heavily, and fought back tears as the prosecutor Louise Oakley KC read out a list of the 62 charges he had pleaded guilty to, all of which relate to his wife of four decades.

His wife watched him give evidence via a live stream from a different courtroom in the same building at Manchester Minshull Street crown court, flanked by several members of her family.

The man said he wanted to give evidence “so the court knows the truth of what happened to my wife, so my wife knows, my children”.

He said speaking to police about his crimes had been “very difficult”, because he felt “guilt and shame” over “the pain I’ve caused to [my wife]”.

The man, who is in his 60s, initially denied the offences after his arrest in October last year, but later pleaded guilty to some, and then all, of the charges, which span a period of more than 20 years.

He said he initially pleaded guilty to only some of the charges “because I felt guilty, I felt I needed to let people know that I was guilty”, but had denied some of them because he wanted to be able to participate in the trial to “tell the truth of what happened”.

When asked by Oakley whether his wife, who is also in her 60s, had been aware at the time he was committing the offences, he replied: “No she wasn’t,” and when asked whether she was consenting, he replied: “No.”

Oakley asked him whether his wife had known “any of the men you invited round”, to which he replied: “No she didn’t” and added that “none of them ever spoke to her”.

When he was asked whether his wife would “ever have consented to swinging”, the man responded: “No, not at all.”

He said he had posted on internet sites to connect with people who were local to his home in Stockport, Greater Manchester, “to come and see [my wife] and abuse her”.

While he did not explicitly state that he wanted her to be raped and sexually assaulted in the posts, he said, he was aware “sexual assault and rape was possibly going to take place”.

The offences involving the nine men on trial are alleged to have taken place between 2015 and the husband’s arrest in 2025.

Two of the men, Daniel Rayner, 42, from Whitstable in Kent and David Graves, 59, from Ilkeston in Derbyshire, are alleged to be “non-contact offenders” who are accused of encouraging the abuse online.

The other men are alleged to have visited the couple’s home and participated in the abuse of the victim.

They are Philip Wild, 59, from Stockport; Jordan Wallace, 31, from Manchester; Alan Keelan, 42, from Manchester; Jonathan Kirk, 43, from Stockport; Mohammed Sabir, 28, of no fixed abode, and Richard Townsend, 38, from Heywood in Greater Manchester.

Earlier this week Robert Stewart, 70, from Stockport, pleaded guilty to rape, and conspiracy to administer a substance with intent. He denies a charge of conspiracy to rape.

All the men deny the charges against them.

Four other men, Sean Peers, 38, from Stockport; Keith Fotheringham, 59, from Dundee; Graham Brougham, 74, from Northwich in Cheshire, and Tommy Golden, 59, from Salford, have admitted to abusing the woman. Karl Lindsay, 56, from Somerset has also pleaded guilty to “non-contact” offences.

The trial continues.
