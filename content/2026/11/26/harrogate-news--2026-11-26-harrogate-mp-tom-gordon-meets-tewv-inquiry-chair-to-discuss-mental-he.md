**Tom Gordon, MP for Harrogate and Knaresborough, has held a meeting with Judge Potter, Chair of the public inquiry into mental health care at Tees, Esk and Wear Valleys NHS Foundation Trust. Discussions centred on the inquiry’s terms of reference and involvement of affected families.**

Tom Gordon, the Liberal Democrat Member of Parliament for Harrogate and Knaresborough, recently held a key meeting with Judge Potter, Chair of the statutory public inquiry into mental health care at the Tees, Esk and Wear Valleys NHS Foundation Trust (TEWV). The discussion focused on the inquiry’s terms of reference, which define its scope and are yet to be finalised and formally approved.

The MP described the meeting as a significant step towards obtaining answers for families and patients who have experienced failings under TEWV’s care. Of particular note was Judge Potter’s willingness to listen to contributions from individuals who are not designated as “core participants” in the inquiry. Core participant status typically allows enhanced participation, including access to evidence not available to the public, but the designation can be demanding, and some families may find the process difficult to navigate.

> **Tom Gordon’s Senior Caseworker, Chris Howden, emphasised the importance of Judge Potter’s openness:**
>
> We’ve had families who want to contribute but who might find the “core participant” status, its intensity and high barrier to entry a bit much to handle in already tragic circumstances. Judge Potter’s openness to hearing from a wide range of people who were impacted is very reassuring. Giving folk a small stake in the process is vital to building back that trust with health services.

Since his election, Mr Gordon has campaigned actively for the statutory inquiry. He used his first question at Prime Minister’s Questions to support calls for the investigation and has consistently pressed ministers to appoint a chair and advance the inquiry. After the inquiry was established, the MP held a public meeting with local constituents to hear directly about their experiences.

Many residents have shared with Mr Gordon accounts of failings and inadequate care received from TEWV. These experiences have been communicated to Judge Potter, with the MP encouraging anyone affected to engage with the inquiry or seek assistance from his office.

> **Mr Gordon commented on the meeting:**
>
> Meeting Judge Potter is a key milestone. For too long, people across Harrogate and Knaresborough have told me about failings in the mental health care they or their loved ones received. They have shown real courage in coming forward, and they deserve answers.
>
> I used my very first question at PMQs to back the call for this inquiry. Since then I’ve chased minister after minister to get a chair appointed and the inquiry moving. Now the focus shifts to making sure it works for the people it is meant to serve.
>
> We had a constructive discussion about the terms of reference. I was clear they need to be broad enough to capture the full range of experiences my constituents have shared with me, so that no family is left out.
>
> If you or someone you love has been affected by care at TEWV, please make your voice heard. Feed into the inquiry, or get in touch with my office and my team will help you.

Those wishing to contribute to the inquiry or seek assistance can contact Tom Gordon’s office at [tom.gordon.mp@parliament.uk.](mailto:tom.gordon.mp@parliament.uk.) The terms of reference for the inquiry have not yet been agreed or signed off. For anyone struggling, the Samaritans provide a free, 24-hour helpline on 116 123.
