Watch: CCTV captures knifeman Anthony Williams making his way through the carriages

**Footballer Jonathan Gjoshe, 22, was on the 18:25 train from Doncaster to London King's Cross on 1 November last year.**

Just weeks into his first season at Scunthorpe United, Gjoshe was travelling to his home in London alongside 150 other passengers.

He was sitting at a table seat in Coach J when a figure appeared over his shoulder. The figure, dressed in black, was Anthony Williams, who had boarded the train moments earlier at Peterborough at 19:26.

CCTV captured the horror of what happened next.

The London-bound train was on time, travelling through Cambridgeshire at about 122mph (196km/h), when Williams began to walk through the carriages, stabbing people with a kitchen knife.

![Map showing journey of 18:25 train from Doncaster to London King's Cross ](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/0d96/live/e7ac1ae0-e772-11f1-a094-5fdc692f3e33.png)

Gjoshe was the first person to be attacked. In the ensuing terror, passengers darted for cover, hiding in locked toilets, bike racks or under seats. Others tried to flee.

When they could run no further, some tried to defend themselves as well as other passengers by using frying pans and even throwing toasters.

Williams, who has schizophrenia, has now been found guilty of 14 counts of attempted murder.

Using court testimony and first-hand accounts from the victims, this is the story of what happened during five terrifying minutes on the train.

## Coach J

![Header showing location of Coach J on the train. It reads 19:37 Williams starts attack, injuring four people  ](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/4a9c/live/036fa7f0-e9a9-11f1-99b6-a35a62fa4264.png)

At 19:37, Williams walked into Coach J, where Gjoshe was sitting.

The footballer was leaning slightly into the aisle when Williams appeared over his shoulder. The pair briefly made eye contact before Williams, clutching a large kitchen knife, stabbed Gjoshe seven times, including to the shoulder and bicep.

Gjoshe's adrenaline kicked in and he jumped over the table before shouting: "I've been stabbed – run!" He later told police some passengers thought he was joking and remained seated.

Sitting across from him was Dinaria Soares, who, seeing the attack, jumped to her feet and ran towards the front of the train, away from Williams.

Two rows away was 29-year-old Sachin Balakrishnan, who had been on his way to see friends. He had his headphones on and was speaking to his partner. Hearing the screaming, he stood up to see what was happening.

Williams stabbed him in the forearm and the chest. The knife passed just centimetres away from his heart, puncturing a lung. In a desperate attempt to flee he said he then "runs and runs".

David Presland, 73, was also in Coach J. In the chaos, he fell to his hands and knees in the middle of the aisle.

Williams, now standing over him, stabbed him multiple times. Presland said he was left thinking he was going to die.

Williams ignored other passengers, including a woman and child, as he moved through the carriage.

![CCTV grab showing Williams standing in the aisle as passengers try to flee  ](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/f273/live/3fab7560-e813-11f1-99b6-a35a62fa4264.jpg)

Williams stood behind David Presland in the middle of the aisle, before stabbing him multiple times

Scott Bletcher, 50, who was sitting in a far corner seat of Coach J, was on his way home to Letchworth Garden City. He had been Christmas shopping in Newark and said he had been looking forward to eating the tapas he had bought.

He presumed there had been some sort of fight or disagreement when he saw people running down the aisle. Then Williams moved in front of him. Neither of them said a word before Williams stabbed him six times to his arm and body and moved on.

Bletcher called 999, but while he was on the phone to police he told them: "Oh my God, he's coming back." Bleeding, he managed to hide in a bike rack.

William's attack in Coach J lasted just 40 seconds. In that time, at least 15 wounds had been inflicted.

## Coach H

![Header showing location of Coach H on the train. It reads 19:38 Williams attacks  two passengers](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/613d/live/996ae1d0-e773-11f1-a094-5fdc692f3e33.png)

Scott Green was sitting a few seats into Coach H with his cockapoo, Poppy, who had been restless as fireworks exploded outside. It was four days before Bonfire Night.

Like Bletcher, the 52-year-old initially thought a fight had broken out. Then he felt a sudden jab to his side as Williams stabbed him.

Green tried to grab the blade but was unable to take it from Williams, who he said appeared dead in the eyes.

Poppy, his dog, went into attack mode, barking at Williams who continued down the train.

Green then picked up his bag and was thrown into a toilet by a woman. Amid the chaos, came a moment of levity.

"I don't usually get in with men I've just met," she told him.

When the attack began, Kevin Deely, 58, had his earphones in and was watching a video on his phone. He tried to help an injured David Presland and later remembered telling Williams: "You don't have to do this."

Deely threw a piece of luggage as Williams continued his attack, before he too was stabbed while trying to protect another passenger, Maisie Harrison.

The first alarm on the train was pulled at 19:38. At first, the driver, Andrew Johnson, had assumed someone was messing around.

But suddenly there was banging on his cab door and a voice on the intercom shouted: "There's a knifeman stabbing people."

Johnson had just 10 seconds to decide what to do next, quickly switching the train to the slow line towards Huntingdon.

Many of the passengers on board were returning from a football match between Nottingham Forest and Manchester United. One of them was Forest fan Stephen Crean, 61, who was sitting near the buffet car. He had just bought himself a £6 meal deal.

![CCTV grab of passengers crowding the buffet car, with Williams just out of sight](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/f7c8/live/4f4ba950-e812-11f1-99a1-253766e7ad8b.jpg)

Passengers hid in the buffet car as they tried to get away from Williams

![CCTV footage of Crean lunging at Williams ](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/2116/live/6ce50600-e812-11f1-a094-5fdc692f3e33.jpg)

Passenger Crean left the buffet car and wrestled with Williams outside

The carriage quickly filled around him as people were running for their lives through the train carriages, including some of those who were already injured.

Crean suddenly found himself at the door, with nothing between him and the vestibule between Coach H and G.

Faced with Williams, he began to grapple with the knifeman.

He said Williams seemed to be "on a mission". As they wrestled, Williams asked him: "Do you want to die?"

During the tussle, Crean's phone fell to the floor. Williams told him to pick it up.

## Coach G

![Header showing location of Coach G on the train. It reads 19:39 Williams turns and walks back down train ](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/9d94/live/f2119590-e773-11f1-a094-5fdc692f3e33.png)

Williams then continued into Coach G, where passenger James Tomlinson heard him say: "You're all going to die today."

Williams then turned around and headed towards the front of the train, passing through Coach L where CCTV showed him swigging from a bottle of fortified tonic wine.

Now hiding inside a toilet, Crean also called 999. "I'm scared," he told the operator.

## Coach M

![Header showing location of Coach M on the train. It reads 19:41 Williams injures a member of staff and two people ](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/ec09/live/0b81baf0-e774-11f1-a094-5fdc692f3e33.png)

With nowhere else to go, passengers in first-class Coach M were preparing to defend themselves. Staff member Samir Zitouni, 48, had managed to collect frying pans from the kitchen area to use as weapons. He also placed a walking frame in the aisle in an attempt to slow Williams down.

As Williams entered the carriage, Christina Jones, who also boarded the train at Peterborough, said she would throw her hot drink at him. "He's coming this way," someone else shouted.

Car delivery driver Raza Aslam, 44, who was travelling for work, said he had thought about taking shelter in a toilet when the attack began but decided he would not be able to live with himself if he did - especially as others were injured.

For protection, he stuffed a plastic bag with an empty coffee cup down the front of his jacket and told himself: "Better than nothing."

He was handed a frying pan by Zitouni to defend himself with - but he was quickly stabbed by Williams. He said he was in disbelief at how fast the attack had happened.

![CCTV footage shows staff member Samir Zitouni holding a frying pan ](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/3301/live/772a3430-e823-11f1-a094-5fdc692f3e33.jpg)

Staff member Samir Zitouni collected frying pans and handed them out to passengers to use as weapons

![CCTV grab showing two passengers, one holding a plastic bag and the other a frying pan  ](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/ae68/live/81788d20-e813-11f1-99a1-253766e7ad8b.jpg)

Passenger Aslam stuffed a plastic bag with an empty coffee cup down the front of his jacket for protection

By now, the first-class carriage was full of people trying to flee from Williams.

Michael Paffett, 34, one of the few passengers in the first-class section prior to the attack, suffered a fracture to his cheekbone when Williams slashed his face.

As the chaos continued, Zitouni told Williams: "We love you, we can help you." He said he thought that was a better strategy than swearing at him.

He then tried to knock the knife out of Williams's hand.

Williams launched a "prolonged, persistent and brutal" attack, stabbing him multiple times. Medics who later treated Zitouni worried for his life.

Behind Zitouni was passenger Michael Gawthorpe, who told Williams: "Please stop - we've got kids." He remembered how Williams had said: "They're trying to kill me."

Gawthorpe had been handed a toaster from the kitchen, which he threw at Williams. He said the knifeman looked back at him and he later remembered thinking: "That was me done - I've made a mistake here." He said his mind turned to his wife and daughter.

Suddenly, the train abruptly stopped.

After 15 seconds, the doors opened and passengers flooded out on to the platform at Huntingdon train station.

![CCTV grab of passengers flooding out on to the platform at Huntingdon train station](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/c330/live/c57376d0-e812-11f1-a094-5fdc692f3e33.jpg)

Passengers flooded out on to the platform at Huntingdon train station

Zitouni walked down the platform with his white shirt stained red and collapsed, falling through the gap between the train and the platform.

Williams, now standing in a doorway, threw a bottle of tonic wine he had been swigging from.

Leaving the train, he jumped over a fence into the railway station car park.

Armed police arrived within minutes and Williams was shot with a baton gun, tasered and handcuffed.

His violent attack was brought to an end at 19:50.

Police had initially been told that nine people would likely die as a result of the attack. Yet all the victims survived.

Each had their evidence heard in court as Williams was convicted. His trial heard how Williams had "brought terror" to the train.

However, many of those onboard had tried to fend him off, putting themselves in harm's way to protect strangers.

They had, the court was told, shown extraordinary bravery.
