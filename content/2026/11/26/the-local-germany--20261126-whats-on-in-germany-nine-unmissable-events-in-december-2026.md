Between the *Glühwein*, the gingerbread and the twinkling stalls in practically every town square, it's easy to assume December in Germany is one long Christmas market.

But beyond the markets – and Christmas concerts, processions and age-old traditions – the month also sees a fascinating line-up of non-holiday related cultural and sporting events.

From a world-cinema festival and a chance to meet Hollywood stars to a ski-jumping spectacle and the Berlin Philharmonic's New Year's Eve concerts, here's our pick of the best non-Christmas events taking place in the run-up to the new year.

**November 26th – December 6th: Around the World in 14 Films, Berlin**

Now in its 21st year, this independent festival brings fourteen standout films from Cannes, Sundance, Locarno and Venice to Berlin.

Think of it as a year of cinema in 11 days, with films from countries including Chile, Canada, Rwanda, Nepal and the Philippines. Almost all are Berlin premieres, and each is introduced by a guest "patron".

Screenings take place at three cinemas: the CineStar in the KulturBrauerei, Delphi Lux and Neues Off. Many are listed as original language with English subtitles (marked "OmeU") and there’s also a surprise film screening on December 4th.

The full programme is available here.

Billed as Germany's biggest chocolate festival, chocolART is now in its 19th year. White pagoda tents fill Tübingen's half-timbered old town, with roughly 90 to 100 chocolatiers from places like Italy, Belgium, Ecuador and Peru.

This year's focus is "Colors of Latin America" – the programme includes a tasting led by an Ecuadorian cocoa expert and a cocoa ceremony, both of which can be booked in advance.

You can also watch confectioners at work in the chocolateROOM on the Marktplatz, and the old town will be lit with light art in the evenings. Opening hours run to 10pm on Saturday and 6pm on Sunday.

**December 5th – 6th: German Film & Comic Con, Dortmund**

Ever fancied a selfie with a Hollywood star? The German Film & Comic Con is a fan convention that has been held in Dortmund since 2015. Its winter edition fills Messe Dortmund from 10am to 6pm on both days.

This year's guests include Ewan McGregor, John Boyega, John Cena, John Cleese and Dolph Lundgren, plus some of the actors behind characters from series such as "One Piece".

Essentially, it’s an opportunity to have an autograph signed in front of you, or to have a professional photo taken with a star and printed on the spot. There are also panels, a stage programme, cosplayers, fan groups, artists and traders.

It shows around 30 short and feature documentaries at the Babylon cinema**,** near Rosa-Luxemburg-Platz, with screenings at about 8pm.

Tickets are €4, and the shorts nights include a free drink at the bar.

The films tackle current social issues, and the festival also streams online from December 7th to January 7th.

**December 17th – 20th: Louis Lewandowski Festival, Berlin**

In its 16th year, the Louis Lewandowski Festival celebrates Jewish choral music, with the theme "Psalms in space and time".

Choirs from Israel, the US and Germany perform in unusual Berlin venues. The opening concert on December 17th at 6pm in Schöneberg Town Hall is free.

On December 19th there are three concerts at €15 each, at 4pm, 5pm and 7.30pm, in Wuhlgarten, Pankow and Oberschöneweide. Tickets for the closing concert, on December 20th at 5pm in the Rykestraße Synagogue, cost €30.

**December 25th: Dark Storm Festival, Chemnitz**

While much of Germany settles in for a quiet family Christmas Day, Chemnitz does something different.

The Dark Storm Festival is a one-day indoor gathering for fans of goth, EBM (electronic body music) and industrial music.

Now in its 28th year, it takes place at the Stadthalle Chemnitz, with doors opening at around 3.30pm and the music starting at 4.15pm. Billed acts include VNV Nation, Blutengel, She Past Away, Solar Fake, Agonoize and Hocico.

Standing tickets for the event start at around €70.

At the end of December, hackers, artists and campaigners gather at Hamburg Messe for the 40th Chaos Communication Congress (40C3), titled "Model Citizens".

The Chaos Computer Club and volunteers have organised this world-famous event since 1984. It typically draws more than 17,000 people a year, making it the largest gathering of the hacker community in Europe. Last year's edition featured over 160 talks.

![people walk past a rocket in the hall of an exhibition cenrte](https://apiwp.thelocal.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/566223788.jpg)

Alongside the talks, there are workshops and "assemblies" (semi-open spaces where groups work on projects together). Visitors are welcome to join in, and the official site has information pages in English and German.

The Four Hills Tournament – a ski-jumping institution since 1953 and a great spectator event – turns 75 this year.

Competitors earn points for distance and style, and the overall winner is whoever has the most after four jumps. The men's contests are head-to-head duels, so every jump is a mini showdown.

This year the women will also compete at all four venues for the first time, with their own overall title and a €30,000 bonus for the winner.

The event is ticketed. Oberstdorf hosts the opening round and is known for its lively crowds. The women jump on Monday, December 28th, with their competition starting at 3.15pm, and the men follow on Tuesday, December 29th, with their main event at 4.30pm.

The tournament then moves to Austria for the “third” and “fourth” hills.**December 29th – 31st: Berlin Philharmonic New Year's Eve concerts, Berlin**

From December 29th, chief conductor Kirill Petrenko and the orchestra say goodbye to the year "with humour and irony".

The 2026 programme includes Prokofiev's *Symphonie classique*, Shostakovich's concerto for piano and trumpet, and Richard Strauss's* Till Eulenspiegel*. Pianist Alexander Malofeev makes his debut with the orchestra alongside principal trumpeter David Guerrier.

Concerts are at 8pm on December 29th and 30th, and at 5.30pm on December 31st, with tickets from €66 (€113 on New Year's Eve).

The first two evenings run about 2.5 hours with a 20-minute interval, and the 31st runs about 2 hours 10 minutes without an interval.
