A disabled man has been stripped of his Motability car after he claimed it had been taken on an “unauthorised joyride” when seized by police.

Officers stopping the car found the driver was not insured and told Motability Operations, the company that runs Motablity, that the vehicle had been taken without the leaseholder’s approval.

As a result of the breach in contract, the hire agreement was terminated and the man, who suffered several medical conditions, was barred from rejoining for four years.

In an appeal to the financial ombudsman, the man claimed the keys had been taken from his home without consent, and that he was the victim of an “unauthorised joyride”, according to papers attached to the decision.

But ombudsman Benjamin John dismissed his appeal, judging that the man, who was not named in the papers, had given consent for the vehicle to be used, given he did not report it at the time of the incident.

He wrote: “I am satisfied that Motability Operations was entitled to rely on the terms and conditions of the agreement and to terminate it following the breach.”

![Motablity Operations, which runs Motability, terminated the man’s hire agreement](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/03/27/8/19/iStock-2235244903.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Motability has more than 900,000 on the scheme which is open to those who claim a qualifying mobility allowance, most commonly through Personal Independent Payment (PIP).

Users pay monthly for the scheme out of their benefits, but can also pay an upfront amount themselves for a better vehicle. Under the rules of the hire agreement, up to three drivers such as friends and carers.

In this instance, the financial ombudsman service heard how the man had acquired his car for a minimum three years with an advance rental payment of £2,495 in February last year.

In the same year, Motability informed the man about a speeding incident, and that the car had been seized by police who found the driver not to be insured.

The man intially identified the driver, and said he was using his own insurance policy - however, after he was told that police had different information, he accepted he had been mistaken about who was driving the car.

## What is Motability?

The Motability scheme allows benefit claimants to use their allowance to pay for a car on a three-year lease.

They must be claiming one of just six specific mobility-related allowances.

This could be through PIP, the Disability Living Allowance, War Pensioners’ Mobility Supplement and a few others.

He later told the ombudsman’s investigators that the supply of incorrect information was due to him being in shock.

The records from the ombudsman said: “He [the disabled man] said the police decided against criminal prosecution because the driver was known to him.

“He said the individual had taken the keys from his home without his consent and that it was a one-off incident. Mr E [the disabled man] said Motability Operations decision to terminate the agreement was a disproportionate response to him being the victim of an unauthorised joyride.”

Mr John, however, the disabled man was responsible for ensuring all drivers comply with requirements of his hire agreement, and that Motabilty Operations had acted fairly.

He wrote: “Taking all of the evidence into account, I think it is more likely than not that Mr E was aware of the incident and had given his consent for the vehicle to be used.

“I also note that Mr E acknowledged that he had previously allowed an uninsured driver to use the vehicle.”

He concluded: “I have seen no persuasive evidence that would lead me to conclude that Motability Operations acted unfairly, unreasonably, or in a manner that was inconsistent with those obligations.”

The man’s complaint was not upheld, and his hire agreement remained terminated.
