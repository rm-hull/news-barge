Nine hundred jobs are to be created at Faslane as part of an £8 billion investment to improve the maintenance and availability of the UK’s submarines.

The Ministry of Defence (MoD) has awarded a contract of around £8 billion to UK firm Babcock.

The MoD said Royal Navy submarines will get back to sea faster through the Gateway Agreement deal, which will transform the way submarine maintenance and critical naval base services are carried out.

![Faslane is Scotland’s largest military establishment (Andrew Milligan/PA)](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/26/08/01d323aa42f062e1828d7eb63c3a36b8Y29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzk1NzY3MjM3-2.6454884.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The five-year contract will see the creation of around 3,000 highly skilled roles around the UK, the MoD said.

This will include 900 jobs and 450 apprentice and graduate opportunities at HMNB Clyde, known as Faslane, which is home to the Royal Navy’s Submarine Service and the UK’s nuclear deterrent.

Prime Minister Andy Burnham said: “With this new agreement, we’re not just investing in our national security, we’re also making a huge investment in our people.

“We’re providing opportunities to get young people into work, to bring jobs to communities in Clyde, and to boost growth across the UK.

“Keeping the public safe will always be my top priority.

“This investment is a clear demonstration of that commitment – supporting not just the submarines that maintain our nuclear deterrent but also the people who build and maintain them.”

The MoD said the Gateway Agreement will also support delivery of the wider Clyde Transformation Programme, which will see more than £15 billion invested at HMNB Clyde over the next decade.

![Defence Secretary Wes Streeting said the Government ‘will prioritise our nuclear deterrent and getting Britain warfighting ready’ (Ben Birchall/PA)](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/26/08/2e2e1e8389d16c86c29cc349e6b4c32eY29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzk1NzY4MjE5-2.86550874.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Faslane has around 8,000 military and civilian personnel and is Scotland’s largest military establishment.

Speaking on BBC Radio Scotland on Thursday, Defence Secretary Wes Streeting said: “As part of the £8 billion announcement we’re making today for submarine repair and maintenance, we can expect to see around 900 new jobs on the Clyde, in addition to 450 new early career opportunities, things like apprenticeships and graduate job roles that will be supported and sustained through the contract.

“Today’s announcement is good news for the Royal Navy because it improves the availability, the repair, the maintenance of our submarine and ship fleet, so that’s good news for them.

“But it’s also good news for jobs, for opportunities, and I want to make sure that we’re investing more in defence, that we are investing in those jobs and opportunities right across the length and breadth of the country.

“Defence is a big part of the industrial picture in Scotland, £2.5 billion of investment went into Scotland through defence last year alone and we know that sustains just over 12,000 jobs overall.

“This is great news for the Royal Navy, great news for jobs, great news for Scotland.”

Scottish Secretary Douglas Alexander welcomed the news.

He said: “In an increasingly volatile world, our submarines are the UK’s first and final line of defence, and the Clyde is where they are kept ready.

![Scottish Secretary Douglas Alexander said the UK is future proofing defence (Yui Mok/PA)](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/26/08/828a5de292c2ce0228a33c3fff7bb98aY29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzk1NzY3NTk3-2.85367745.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“This £8 billion deal will get them back to sea faster while providing a real defence dividend for Scotland – with new skilled jobs, new opportunities for our young people, and investment flowing to Scottish suppliers.

“Backing the home of the Royal Navy Submarine Service and the UK’s nuclear deterrent means backing our security, our Nato allies and the communities around the Clyde.

“Alongside a wider £15.1 billion UK Government programme to modernise the base over the next decade, we are future-proofing our defence, improving living and working conditions for our submariners and driving reindustrialisation in Scotland.”

Babcock said the deal is the largest contract award in its history.

The company said it comprises an £8 billion contract and £0.7 billion framework.

Harry Holt, Babcock chief executive, said: “We are very proud at Babcock of the critical support that we provide to the Royal Navy, in particular our unwavering support to the continuous at-sea deterrent – the UK’s highest defence priority.

“The Gateway Agreement will enable us, in collaboration with the MoD, to continue our support for the deterrent, increase submarine availability, and develop the skills, infrastructure and capability to support the Royal Navy’s submarine force for the years ahead.”

SNP Westminster leader Dave Doogan hit out at the UK Government’s “nuclear obsession”, claiming it was “putting these islands at risk” as he pushed for more investment in conventional defence.

He added: “Scotland is home to some of the world’s most advanced manufacturers, engineers and complex warship builders with the £10 billion order from the Norwegian government in the Glasgow BAE Systems shipyard a demonstration of the excellence of our yards.

“Scotland is a net contributor to the cost of UK defence and while Westminster will make an argument that nuclear weapons support jobs, conventional forces offer a far greater rate of employment and would deliver security to our high north which has been left exposed by the Labour Government.

“The priority must be to work with the EU on defence spending instead of finding ourselves isolated from our European partners – that’s the pragmatic way forward and with independence we will join the EU and Nato, ensuring Scotland is actually defended and our defence sector thrives.”
