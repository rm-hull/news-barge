Breakfast chain Denny’s is being ridiculed after wading into the Nvidia scandal, with a statement praising former employee turned CEO Jensen Huang.

Huang began his career as a teenage dishwasher at a branch of the family restaurant in Portland, Oregon, before becoming a tech billionaire. The 63 year-old is said to have dreamed up the idea for his microchip empire in another branch in San Jose, California, as he ate breakfast with friends Chris Malachowsky and Curtis Priem.

The executive found himself mired in scandal earlier this month when a series of extramarital affairs came to light, forcing him to resign from the company he has led since 1993. President Donald Trump then intervened on his behalf to persuade Nvidia’s board of directors to reinstate him, arguing his position was of national importance.

Denny’s previously installed a plaque in the San Jose booth in which Huang is said to have had his eureka moment, with the tech boss proudly attending the unveiling in 2003.

The plaque read: “The booth that launched a $1 trillion company. Congrats Nvidia! Who knew that an idea started here would change the world?”

The memorial was taken down earlier this month by a manager in response to the entrepreneur’s personal fall from grace, only for Denny’s to move to reinstate it and declare in its statement: “Some things you just don’t take off the menu. Jensen Huang is one of them.”

![Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang began his career as a dishwasher at Denny’s and joined its boss Kelli Valade for the unveiling of a plaque honoring him at its branch in San Jose, California, in 2023](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/26/12/02/huang-dennys.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&auto=webp)

The post recounted the entrepreneur’s past association with its restaurants, declaring “we couldn’t be prouder of the history we share,” adding that the plaque had been restored soon after its removal.

“Think about all the people who’ve washed dishes in America’s Diner over the years,” Denny’s said. “Every one of them matters to us. One of them went on to change the world, and he still remembers what he learned at the sink.

“How many diners get to say Jensen Huang calls them his first alma mater? We don’t take that lightly. Jensen, your booth is waiting and the coffee’s on. We’re always open, 24/7.”

The post drew mockery on social media with the top comment on X asking: “Did you pour syrup on the boots before you licked them?”

Many agreed the tone of the statement was “weird”, with one user adding: “I had to check like five times to make sure this wasn’t a parody account because what the hell did I just read.”

“Might actually be one of the cringiest things I’ve ever seen on this website, and I’m a Disney Adult so that’s saying a lot,” another joked.

A further post on X wondered whether Denny’s was “trying to put themselves out of business” while others pledged to boycott its restaurants.

The post was no better received on Instagram, where the company was called “bizarre and out of touch” and accused of failing to “stand by its morals.”

Huang has long held affection for the chain, recalling at the plaque’s unveiling with Denny’s CEO Kelli Valade: “Denny’s has taught me so many lessons.

“I was a dishwasher, I was a busboy, I waited tables. No one can carry more coffee cups than I can… Start your first job in the restaurant business. It teaches you humility, it teaches you hard work, it teaches you hospitality.”

He was also pictured dining at an Abu Dhabi branch of Denny’s in February 2024 and the company paid tribute to the chip impresario last year by offering a limited-edition menu item, “Nvidia Breakfast Bytes,” a plate of sausages and pancakes dipped in maple syrup “mirroring Huang’s favorite way to eat the dish.”
