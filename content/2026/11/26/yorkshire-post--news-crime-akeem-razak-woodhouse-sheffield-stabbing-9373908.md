# Teen who stabbed 14-year-old in senseless knife attack jailed for six years

## A teenager who stabbed a 14-year-old boy seven times in the chest in a senseless attack has been jailed for six years.

It was around 10pm on March 18 this year when Akeem Razak, of Grange Road, Beighton, was eating pizza on a bench on Woodhouse’s Market Square with a friend.

Moments later he was fleeing after stabbing a boy three years younger than him seven times in the chest.

“It was an utterly unprovoked attack,” said The Recorder of Sheffield, His Honour Judge Jeremy Richardson KC in disbelief during sentencing at the city’s crown court on November 24.

![Akeem Razak, 18, of Grange Road, Beighton, was jailed for six years and nine months after stabbing a 14-year-old boy in a senseless attack on Market Square, Woodhouse.](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/jpim-static/image/2026/11/25/11/16/Untitled-Design-(62).jpeg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


“That’s the risk - young men take knives out with them and, and in a flash decide to use them.

“If ever there was a case that demonstrates the dangers of young men carrying knives in a public place, this is it.”

The senselessness of the attack was laid bare in court as it was revealed the victim had disengaged with police and refuses to speak to officers - meaning there is little to no record of what caused the savage incident.

But CCTV played in court showed how Razak had been relaxed on the bench with the victim just out of sight by a nearby shop front in one moment, and up on his feet stabbing wildly in the next.

![This was the scene in Market Square, Woodhouse, on March 19, 2026. Police descended on the shopping square follow reports a 14-year-old had been stabbed seven times.](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/jpim-static/image/2026/03/19/11/05/onecms_43d8cba1-62fe-420f-a452-756fd0483712.jpeg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


The 14-year-old - who was a complete stranger to Razak - was reportedly arguing with his girlfriend when Razak said something to challenge him on it.

“[The victim] said words to the effect of ‘it’s got nothing to do with you’,” said defence barrister Craig Bell.

However, at that time Razak, then 17, was carrying a knife, and this provocation was all that was needed for him to run straight at the boy and stab him seven times in the chest and legs.

The boy’s terrified girlfriend immediately ran to a nearby shop and called police. She also reportedly refuses to speak with authorities.

Shocking bodyworn footage from a police officer at the scene showed how first responders kept the boy stable as blood soaked his clothes and the pavement around him.

“They must be praised publicly”

After watching the bodyworn footage of the three first responders fighting to stop the boy’s bleeding, His Honour Judge Jeremy Richardson paused proceedings.

“I want to say this - I think these three crew members, each and every one of them is deserving of the highest commendation for their actions,” said the judge.

“I know they are trained and it is their job, but but they were doing their level best during in difficult circumstances.

“This was not a training exercise, and they saved that young man’s life.

“They need to be praised publicly for their conduct.”

Incredibly, the boy was awake and spoke to the emergency services throughout - and, despite a stay in intensive care, none of his wounds required stitches.

Meanwhile, Razak and his friend fled the scene, and handed himself in to police a week later.

He was charged with attempted murder, but instead pleaded guilty to wounding in late October.

Judge Richardson said he had received several references and reports attesting Razak was otherwise “an ordinary lad who helps his family”.

“So do we just have no idea why this happened?” Judge Richardson asked the prosecutor, Laura Marshall.

“No,” she replied. “Other than he had a knife that night and he used it.”

“Well, that’s what happens when young men carry knives,” said the judge.

“Someone says something, and they decide to use it.”

Razak, who sat with his head held low for much of the hearing, was jailed for six years and nine months in a young offenders’ institute.

His family wept from the public gallery.

“For reasons that are completely unclear, you had that knife on you,” said the judge.

“It may be they were having an argument, it may be that something was said - you, for no reason, intervened.

“You had no business to do so -and, even if you had, getting out a knife was not the right reaction.

“And you did not do so out of public spirit - you intervened with violence. Gratuitous violence.

“I sincerely hope you make full use of your time.”
