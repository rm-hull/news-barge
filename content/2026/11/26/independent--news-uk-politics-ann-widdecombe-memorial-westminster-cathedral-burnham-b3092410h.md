Andy Burnham and a number former prime ministers were among around 600 mourners who gathered at Westminster Cathedral on Thursday afternoon for a memorial service honouring late ex-minister Ann Widdecombe.

Former premiers Lord David Cameron, Rishi Sunak, Boris Johnson and Liz Truss attended, alongside Home Secretary Shabana Mahmood, Reform UK leader Nigel Farage and deputy leader Richard Tice.

Speaker of the House of Commons Sir Lindsay Hoyle and former Conservative Chancellor Philip Hammond were also present for the service.

During the memorial, which lasted around 70 minutes, tributes were delivered by independent crossbencher Lord Alton of Liverpool and Reverend Roger Widdecombe, who is Miss Widdecombe’s nephew.

In a moving eulogy, Mr Widdecombe said his aunt’s death was “a tragedy”, but added the service was to “pay tribute to Aunt Ann” and “celebrate and thank god for a life well lived”.

He said: “Words such as integrity, brave, kind, delightful, indefatigable, interested, tireless, fun, all express something of the amazing woman that Ann was.”

![(left to right) Prime Minister Andy Burnham, Home Secretary Shabana Mahmood, Lady Cherie Blair, David Cameron, Rishi Sunak, Boris Johnson and Liz Truss](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/26/18/01M8CB3W29H73QNBSRNTTWXK3K.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

He added his aunt was “certainly a lot of fun”, and recalled a few family anecdotes which prompted guests to laugh.

He said Ms Widdecombe lived in a house with a pool to encourage family visits.

“And when the kids asked for a slide into the pool, she got a slide,” he said.

“When they requested a trampoline, that arrived. A rope swing, and so on.

“The drinks fridge was always stocked with far too many sugary fizzy drinks, and her rationing of them was non-existent.”

Mr Widdecombe also told those gathered in the cathedral he was often asked what his aunt was “really like”, and said he would respond: “Exactly as you see on TV.”

He added: “She was the same with her family as she was everyone else.”

At the end of his eulogy, Mr Widdecombe said of his aunt: “We loved her very much, and we’ll miss her.”

![Lord Alton of Liverpool delivers a eulogy at a Service of Remembrance for Ann Widdecombe](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/26/17/882827e77fd5b7f584d2b628ff7c4f6dY29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzk1ODAxNjgw-2.86615010.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Miss Widdecombe, 78, was found dead at her home in Haytor on Dartmoor, Devon, on July 9.

Joshua Kerry, 28, of Byrley Road, Rotherham, south Yorkshire, was charged with her murder.

A trial before a High Court judge is scheduled for June 8 next year, and a plea and case management hearing on January 29.

Kerry has also been charged with a terror plot against Mr Farage.

Miss Widdecombe’s family announced plans for a public memorial service following her death.

In his tribute, Lord Alton described the late politician as a “dedicated” and “hard-working” member of Parliament who had “character and tenacity”.

He said: “I remain shocked to the core to have lost another close personal friend to such savagery.”

Bishop James Curry, the Auxiliary Bishop of Westminster, led the service which featured a motet, a psalm and two hymns sang by the Westminster Cathedral choir.

Archbishop Richard Moth – who is Archbishop of Westminster and president of the Catholic Bishops’ Conference of England and Wales – was originally expected to lead the memorial, but “felt unwell” and was taken to hospital on Wednesday, the diocese said.

Guests reflected as the bishop read prayers, and many accompanied the choir when it sang Amazing Grace before the organ marked the end of the service with a solo piece.

After the memorial, Mr Tice told the Press Association: “Ann Widdecombe was a very dear friend, a very special person, and to have, give or take 1,000 people here in Westminster Cathedral, including the Prime Minister, a number of former prime ministers, shows the remarkable nature of her personality, what she achieved, what she stood for, the horror at her passing.”

He added: “In a sense, she’s like the very best of democracy: deep conviction, always told the truth as she felt it, but an incredible courtesy to others who she disagreed with, and we can all never stop learning from that.”

During Thursday’s service, Archbishop Emeritus of Cardiff George Stack, who previously served at Westminster Cathedral and knew Miss Widdecombe, said her funeral had been “beautiful” but “very sad”.

He said Miss Widdecombe was “passionate” and of “impeccable integrity”, adding she lived “a life of dedication and commitments, in so many ways”.

He also referred to the late politician’s stints on Strictly Come Dancing and Celebrity Big Brother, saying: “In that career she didn’t take herself too seriously.

“She was prepared to laugh at herself and, equally important, to allow others to enjoy the laughter too.

“Although she took life seriously, she didn’t necessarily take herself so.”

Miss Widdecombe was first elected as a Conservative MP in Kent in 1987, was a prisons minister in the 1990s and later became an MEP for the Brexit Party.

At the time of her death, she was a spokeswoman on immigration and justice for Reform UK.
