The founder of Browns Brasseries, who is accused alongside his sister of attempting to murder their elderly mother by administering morphine, said they were only trying to give her pain relief because she was in “obvious” agony, a court heard.

Oxford restaurateur Jeremy Mogford, 79, and Sarah Pickering, 72, are on trial for the attempted murder of their mother Pamela Mogford in Bledlow Ridge, Buckinghamshire, in February 2022.

The 100-year-old died at about half-past midnight on February 15 2022, after she was given a dose of oral morphine solution by her children.

It is alleged the defendants gave their mother the equivalent of “at least four times” as much morphine as her GP had given her the evening before she died in an effort to “speed up” her death.

Mogford and Pickering maintain they only gave their mother morphine after the GP left as pain relief.

Giving evidence at Oxford Crown Court on Thursday, Mogford said his mother’s last night was a “rollercoaster”, with the elderly woman “stirring” and “crying out” in pain soon after her GP gave her a dose of morphine.

An ambulance had been called earlier on 14 February as it was thought Mrs Mogford might have had a stroke, and although paramedics wanted to take her to hospital she refused.

Jurors also previously heard that Mrs Mogford’s GP visited her three times that day, giving her injections of morphine on the second and third visits as he thought “she might be dying”.

“We were surprised in the way that the morphine hadn’t kicked in,” the restauranteur, describing his mother making “sounds of pain” after the GP left, told the court in his evidence.

“Sometimes you couldn’t feel something other than her hand gripping your arm, but you knew she was in pain.”

Mogford’s defence barrister Trevor Burke KC then asked him: “Was she in need of morphine?”

“Oh, absolutely,” Mogford replied.

“It was very distressing to see her in what I would describe as agony.”

Asked about his motivation in giving his mother Oramorph on the night of her death, Mogford replied: “Purely and utterly pain relief.”

He called the allegation that he and his sister tried to kill their mother “just nonsense”.

Mogford said he struggled to recognise his mother when he met her at her home on the afternoon of 14 February, saying that her face looked “completely grey” and she was “barely conscious”.

“I was not sure, in fact, if she recognised me,” Mogford further told jurors.

“This was the end: it was obvious.”

He said paramedics told him there was “really nothing more” they could do other than make Mrs Mogford “comfortable”, and left.

The court heard Mrs Mogford was warned the risks of not going to hospital included that she might die, to which she replied: “Good.”

The court further heard Mrs Mogford’s wishes not to be taken to hospital, and not to be resuscitated, had been recorded for years prior to her death.

Mogford further told jurors on Thursday his mother’s attitude towards living had deteriorated after the death of her husband in 2015.

He said: “She couldn’t see any point, she got no pleasure out of life.

“She just lost her mojo.”

Jurors previously heard from Mrs Mogford’s long-term live-in carer that she would take up to eight paracetamol a day for pain, spending her days in the lounge containing her bed.

When asked by his defence barrister whether he was aware of the level of pain his mother was in, Mogford replied: “I was aware of pain and she was an extremely brave, stoic character who would keep it all to herself most of the time.

“But pain had been part of her life for the past 12 years.”

Mogford further told jurors his mother was lucid “right up until the end” and that she took a keen interest in his business affairs, as well as politics.

“There was not an election that went by her, she would predict the winner,” he said.

His mother was also “outspoken”, and her personality was “very similar” to his sister’s, Ms Pickering.

“She didn’t mince her words, and that’s carried on within the family,” he said, adding the two women shared the “same sense of humour, which was often controversial”.

Mogford also described his relationship with his mother as being “very, very close”.

“Much closer towards the end of her life, when I visited her a lot,” he added.

Mogford opened the first Browns Brasserie in 1973 in Brighton with then-business partner John Mayhew.

He sold the Browns chain, which has a large presence in university towns such as Oxford and Cambridge, to Bass Brewery for £35 million in 1996.

He is also the founder and chairman of The Oxford Collection, a hospitality group which comprises two luxury five-star hotels – The Old Bank and The Old Parsonage – and three restaurants – Quod, Gees and Parsonage Grill – in Oxford.

The prosecution and defence both agree there was “absolutely no financial motive to kill Pamela Mogford”, jurors previously heard.

The siblings were arrested after texts were discovered from Pickering on the evening of her mother’s death which included: “I am actually killing her”, and: “I shall wait until Foyle is over then I can go to my grave knowing I have topped someone. It’s certainly on my bucket list.”

The court previously heard experts cannot conclude that the level of morphine given to Mrs Mogford was enough to kill her.

The trial continues.
