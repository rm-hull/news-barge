Fresh data shows that 959,000 young people were not in education, employment or training (Neet) between July and September, a drop of 22,000 from the previous quarter.

According to the Office for National Statistics (ONS), 12.7 per cent of 16 to 24-year-olds in the UK were classed as Neet, remaining broadly stable compared with the same period last year.

However, the ONS reported that the overall total was 17,000 higher than in July to September last year.

Addressing the high proportion of people classed as a Neet is now a key political concern, with a recent report by former health minister Alan Milburn calling for urgent action.

Sweeping changes to the benefit system and improved employment support are central to the proposals, which ministers will be expected to set into action during this parliament.

![Alan Milburn speaking at Google’s offices in central London about his final report into young people not in employment, education or training (Neets)](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/17/17/768417a4cab360cb69d8fbb52f5c3f0eY29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzk1MDIwNzQ3-2.86519499.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The Milburn review proposed widespread changes to help get young people into work across the UK, saying there should be an aim to reduce the rate of to about 5% by 2036.

TUC General Secretary Paul Nowak said: “Now is the time for serious upfront investment in young people. The evidence is clear: high-quality employment support, more training opportunities and a strong, growing economy with decent job vacancies are all essential if we want to see Neet levels coming down.

“Milburn rightly made the case for a downpayment on our young people. A legal guarantee of a job, training place or apprenticeship could be a game changer for young people struggling to get a foothold in the labour market. Targeted support for employers to recruit and retain young people is also vital.”

Hannah Larsen, skills policy officer at the British Chambers of Commerce, said: “While the 22,000 drop in the number of young jobless people is welcome, there is long way to go and the current level of Neets remains unacceptable.

“This stubborn youth engagement challenge shows the importance of delivering Alan Milburn’s recommendations to get more young people on the right path to success.

“Hiring appetite among businesses remains weak due to high employment costs, which have been largely driven by government policy, and the Employment Rights Act.”

![The Milburn review proposed widespread changes to help get young people into work across the UK, saying there should be an aim to reduce the rate of to about 5 per cent by 2036](https://static.independent.co.uk/2025/03/05/17/51/GettyImages-167315111.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Ben Harrison, of the Work Foundation at Lancaster University, said: “Today’s figures underline the scale and persistence of the UK’s youth employment crisis and the urgent need for a fundamental reset.

“Despite the overall number of young people aged 16 to 24 out of education, employment or training falling slightly on the quarter due to a fall in youth unemployment, the UK continues to face elevated levels of youth worklessness.

“In particular, the number of economically inactive young people outside education or training has risen to a near record high of 596,000, leaving too many exposed to the long-term scars of missed opportunities and lost earnings.”

Iona Ledwidge, chief executive of the youth employment charity Spear, said: “While any decrease in these figures is welcome, they remain far too high. Rolling out the support that Alan Milburn recommended can’t come soon enough. Even six months out of work damages a young person’s wellbeing.”

Work and pensions secretary Pat McFadden said: “Almost a million young people are out of education, employment, or training today, and every one of them deserves better. That’s why we’re fundamentally reforming how we support young people to grow up in this country.

“As part of our response to the Milburn Review, we’re building a new settlement that invests in young people’s success instead of paying for failure.

“That includes a Jobs Guarantee offering eligible young people on Universal Credit a fully funded six-month job, as well as thousands more work experience placements, and youth hubs bringing employment, health, and skills support under one roof.”
