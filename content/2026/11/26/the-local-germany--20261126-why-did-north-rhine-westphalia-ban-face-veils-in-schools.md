Wearing a face veil in schools is now prohibited in the west German state of North Rhine-Westphalia (NRW).

The rule was enacted as part of a law passed by state parliament on Wednesday evening, which also gives more power to school administrations to deal with students who make threats and aims to empower young students with disabilities.

The new law, that was passed with backing from the conservative Christian Democratic Union and Green parties, is titled "Law to Strengthen Educational Equity and Democracy Education".

Among it's more direct impacts on schools is that it bans the wearing of face coverings in schools in Germany's most populous state.

**Why ban face coverings?**

The draft law justifies the veil ban by arguing that students' dress at schools should not interfere with clear communication.

"Communication is more than the mere perceptibility of the spoken word," the bill states. "It also requires capturing body language, especially facial expressions."

Therefore face coverings that "hinder communication or violate [students'] duty to participate" are banned from school grounds.

Exceptions are in place for respiratory masks that students might wear in a science lab, or while sick.

Previously, at the end of 2024, the Düsseldorf Administrative Court had ruled that a vocational college was allowed to prohibit a 17-year-old student from attending classes with a face veil.

**Was a ban really necessary?**

While headscarves (hijabs) are common in many schools in North Rhine-Westphalia and elsewhere in Germany, full face veils (niqabs) are virtually absent from classrooms in the country.

Asked in 2017 how many students attend schools in a full face covering, the state government of NRW admitted is was not aware of any such cases.

A comment published by the immigration-focused outlet Migazin quipped, "How many students in NRW show up for history lessons wearing closed knight's helmets is also unknown."

Furthermore, schools were already empowered to prohibit face coverings on their campuses in NRW. This was also confirmed in legal precedent by the 2024 case at the Düsseldorf Administrative Court mentioned above.

That the ban on face coverings was already enforceable is also confirmed by the justification for the new law itself, which includes the line, "No substantive legal changes are associated with this."

So, in terms of its impact on schools' actual dress codes and their enforcement, the new law may not actually change anything. Instead it establishes "legal certainty" on the issue, according to the NRW Ministry of Education.

**Symbolic politics**

While the need for a 'new' ban on face veils may be questionable, being seen making an effort to bring such a ban is aligned with the CDU's political positioning.

The conservative party's Women's Union had previously called for a ban on full face coverings in public – specifically naming the wearing of the burqa, niqab and comparable items.

Similar efforts to limit the wearing of traditionally Muslim clothing items have also been undertaken elsewhere in Europe, including in Austria and in France.

The quoted comment from Migazin suggests that the new law is problematic in that it targets Muslim female students in particular, and that it amounts to a political focus on religious clothing when there are arguably plenty of more pressing issues to be addressed.
