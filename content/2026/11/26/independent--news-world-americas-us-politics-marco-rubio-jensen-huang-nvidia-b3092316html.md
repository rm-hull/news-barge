Secretary of State Marco Rubio was “particularly angry” when he learned of Jensen Huang’s reinstatement as Nvidia’s CEO, having previously warned the Trump administration he was involved with a suspected Chinese spy, a report claims.

Huang made a dramatic comeback to the chipmaker powering the global AI boom Monday, less than two weeks after resigning over the alleged affair.

It subsequently emerged both President Donald Trump and Vice President JD Vance had played a pivotal role in convincing the tech company’s board of directors to take him back over the weekend, insisting only Huang could restore market confidence in Nvidia.

CBS News reports that neither Rubio nor CIA Director John Ratcliffe were part of the administration’s discussions about Huang’s return.

This reportedly left Rubio incensed because earlier this year he had prepared a classified memorandum warning about the alleged affair with a Megaspeed executive accused of funneling Nvidia’s chips to China.

![Secretary of State Marco Rubio was reportedly angry about the reinstatement of Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/26/16/2306104728..?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&auto=webp)

CBS cites allies of Rubio and Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent as saying the two men had “expressed private concern about aspects of Huang’s return,” although neither has criticised the decision in public.

Rubio has yet to comment on the scandal, despite being asked by reporters whether Huang posed a security risk. *The Independent* has reached out to the State Department and Treasury for comment.

Bessent, for his part, originally advised Nvidia to look “outside” for a new CEO, only to then row back on that position slightly during an appearance on CBS’s *Face the Nation* Sunday, when he told anchor Margaret Brennan: “Jensen is on the outside now.”

He has also criticized another Cabinet member not involved in Trump’s strategizing for Haung’s return, Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick, for failing to do enough to stop China obtaining advanced semiconductor chips or blacklist Megaspeed before this week.

![Rubio and Huang descend the steps of Air Force One with other Trump administration officials in May after arriving in Beijing for the president’s high-stakes summit with Xi Jinping](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/11/19/30/GettyImages-2275450491.jpg?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&auto=webp)

Huang’s return initially caused Nvidia’s stock price to soar more than 30 percent Monday, leading to fresh accusations of the president enriching himself through his actions in public office given his large shareholding in the firm, which the White House has denied.

The market rebound proved short-lived, however, with the company dropping in value 5.9 percent Tuesday and 5 percent Wednesday, prompting the president to lash out against a *New York Times* journalist who pointed it out.

Huang was meanwhile welcomed back by employees at Nvidia’s California headquarters, where he promised to be “a better CEO the second time.” He will appear before House and Senate committees in Washington, D.C., next week to take questions from lawmakers on export controls, Chinese chip smuggling and national security.

Rubio’s political stock has risen since November’s crushing midterms defeat for Republicans, causing him to pull ahead of Vance in a recent Emerson poll of possible 2028 GOP presidential contenders.

The secretary previously said he will not run for the White House against the VP but has impressed supporters of late with his answers on the recent spate of tech outages and by managing to remain largely untarnished by the deeply-unpopular Iran war.
