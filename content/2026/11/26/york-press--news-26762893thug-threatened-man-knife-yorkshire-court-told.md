A thug threatened a man with a knife before stamping on him during an “ambush” outside a property in North Yorkshire, a court has heard.

George Moon, 21, “repeatedly threatened” the man with a knife outside the address in Scarborough, the Recorder of York, Judge Sean Morris, said.

Prosecutor Rachael Landin said Moon went to the address shortly before 5am on Sunday, September 13, with Vanessa Shelland, 54, who was looking to buy drugs on “tick” (on credit).

Ms Landin said Moon arrived at the property wearing a balaclava “bouncing around and shouting, ‘Come on then’”.

The prosecutor said Moon had contacted a woman inside the property to say there was a “surprise outside”.

Ms Landin said Moon then “swaggered” towards the woman with Shelland as she came outside.

Shelland grabbed the woman by the neck and proceeded to attack her, the court heard.

The woman was punched and kicked by Shelland with both women pulling each other’s hair and rolling around on the ground, Ms Landin said.

Meanwhile, the court heard Moon attacked the man who had left the property during the altercation between Shelland and the other woman.

Moon ran at the man with a knife and “stamped” on him, Ms Landin said.

She said Shelland and Moon then left the scene.

The woman was left distressed and with cuts to her body when police arrived. She told paramedics she was bitten on her ear, the court heard.

The hearing was told the man had a small cut to his hand.

Moon and Shelland both pleaded guilty to affray.

Moon also pleaded guilty to two counts of threatening a person with a knife.

Judge Morris jailed Moon, of no fixed address, for 18 months.

He told Moon, who has a previous conviction for possession of a bladed article in January 2024: “Young men need to know, if you carry a knife you’re going away – because knife crime has to stop.”

Shelland was jailed for one year suspended for two years. The judge also passed an order for Shelland to undergo six months of drugs rehabilitation.

## A 'disgraceful act of violence', judge says

Judge Morris said Shelland, of Wreyfield Drive, Newby, Scarborough, had taken Moon to an “ambush” and called the altercation a “disgraceful act of violence”.

Shelland also “held something to her [the woman’s] throat but it cannot be said what”, the judge said.

But Judge Morris said he had no option but to suspend the sentence due to a new law requiring all prison sentences under 12 months to be suspended, except under certain circumstances.

Passing the suspended sentence, Judge Morris told Shelland: “While I would deeply love to teach you a lesson by locking you up, I think it would be for the sake of four weeks – not acting in the spirit of the legislation that Parliament passed. And I’m here to uphold laws passed by Parliament.”

Zareen Alam-Cheetham, defending Moon, said he was addicted to cocaine and spice as well as to gambling at the time of the incident.

He was also homeless and is autistic, which affected his decision making, she said.

Ms Alam-Cheetham said Moon’s “downfall is substance abuse, which he has abused since he was 14”.

“He accepts full responsibility for his abysmal actions on this day,” she added. “He is remorseful, regretful and has had time to reflect.”

Ms Alam-Cheetham said Moon has sought help for his gambling addiction and has been clean from drugs since being remanded into jail.

For Shelland, Benjamin Bell said her offending stems from her being a drug addict.

But he said: “This is a lady who is able to stay out of crime and has done [for] a period of about four years.”
