More than 500 crown court trials in England and Wales are not scheduled to be heard until 2030, official data reveals.

The cases form part of an overarching backlog of nearly 10,000 trials that will not commence until 2028 at the earliest, encompassing more than 1,000 cases that involve rape allegations.

Victims’ Commissioner Claire Waxman warned that public faith in the justice system was being pushed "close to breaking point", highlighting that she has spoken with individuals forced to wait nine years "from the point of reporting until their day in court".

Legal professionals have similarly described the delays as "intolerable", calling upon the government to step up efforts to address the growing crown court backlog.

The statistics were secured by the Press Association from the Ministry of Justice (MoJ) following a freedom of information request, reflecting court listings recorded on October 5 2026.

They indicate that 23,884 trials are currently scheduled in crown courts across England and Wales for next year, while 6,964 are set for 2028, 2,370 for 2029, and 555 for 2030.

At the time the dataset was compiled, no trials had yet been scheduled for 2031. Nevertheless, 2026 has witnessed a dramatic surge in the volume of cases listed for 2030.

![Victims’ Commissioner Claire Waxman](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/24/10/24100452-ebe72f5f-bc69-45e6-ba95-e8cd1c246343.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

An earlier freedom of information request submitted by PA revealed that, as of January 29 2026, a total of just 29 trials were scheduled to start in 2030.

The new figure of 555 is likely to have increased further still in the time since the data was disclosed.

The list of trials scheduled for 2030 includes 216 drug cases, 83 for charges of violence against the person, 58 for possession of weapons and 49 for theft.

There are also 25 public order offences, 15 robberies, five sexual offences including one the MoJ has labelled “rape related”, as well as trials for criminal damage, arson and fraud.

The list for 2029 includes 354 trials for sexual offences, of which 172 are rape related, as well as dozens of cases for drugs, fraud, weapons, thefts and violence.

The 2,200 trials for sexual offences listed for 2028 include 1,078 containing rape allegations.

A trial is listed once a defendant’s plea hearing has taken place.

Ms Waxman told the PA: “As the crown court backlog hits record highs, thousands of victims and bereaved families are bearing the human cost of delay.

![They are among a backlog of nearly 10,000 cases that will not begin until 2028](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/25/16/46/iStock-2188391092.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“With large numbers of cases now listed into 2030, I’ve heard from victims who are waiting nine years from the point of reporting until their day in court.

“The consequences of court delays extend far beyond the courtroom.

“When victims are forced to wait years for justice, many lose confidence and withdraw altogether.

“That means fewer prosecutions, fewer convictions, and greater risk that dangerous offenders remain in our communities.”

Without “urgent and systemic reform to meaningfully reduce delays” she warned victims will “remain trapped in trauma and prolonged uncertainty, while public confidence in justice pushes close to breaking point”.

Separate MoJ data published in September showed the total number of cases waiting to be heard at crown courts in England and Wales stood at a record 80,829 at the end of June.

The number has nearly doubled in size in the past six years, having stood at 43,403 at the end of June 2020.

Kirsty Brimelow KC, chair of the Bar Council, said: “Decades of slash and burn of criminal justice system funding has resulted in these intolerable delays.

“It is appalling for complainants, victims, defendants and witnesses.

“We continue to urge the Government to focus on the measures that will reduce delays in the crown court rather than diverting time and attention to overloading the magistrates’ courts where their backlog is at a record high and is accelerating.”

Last month Prime Minister Andy Burnham scrapped plans to cut jury trials amid opposition, reversing proposals introduced during Sir Keir Starmer’s premiership in a bid to curb the court backlog.

At the time Justice Secretary Alex Norris said he decided to “change course” after hearing the “strength of feeling on the proposals” and insisted the move would see the backlog reduced faster.

His predecessor David Lammy previously championed the move, telling MPs the reforms were “desperately needed” to prevent victims facing “agonising delays”.

The MoJ said: “We inherited a courts system on the brink of collapse, leaving victims facing intolerable delays. We recognise delays like these can be deeply distressing for victims and their families, as well as for defendants waiting for justice.

“That is why we are acting on all fronts – record financial investment, system modernisation and pragmatic structural reform through the Courts and Tribunals Bill – to tackle the crown court backlog and deliver swifter justice for all.”
