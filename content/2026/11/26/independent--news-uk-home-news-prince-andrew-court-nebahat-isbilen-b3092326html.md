A Turkish businesswoman has told the High Court she was misled into believing a £750,000 payment she made to Andrew Mountbatten-Windsor was for his help in getting UK residency.

Nebahat Isbilen, 81, made the payment to the former prince in 2019, but claims she was misled by her former adviser, Selman Turk, about its purpose.

Mrs Isbilen is suing Mr Turk at the High Court, alleging he misappropriated tens of millions of pounds of her funds and did not explain where the money went.

Mr Mountbatten-Windsor, who is due to give evidence on December 14, is not accused of wrongdoing and has since paid back the £750,000 sum.

Mrs Isbilen says Mr Turk dishonestly claimed the money paid to the former Duke was needed as a “gift” for the former prince’s help for Mrs Isbilen to stay in the UK.

Mr Turk denies dishonesty, arguing that Mrs Isbilen knew the money was being paid in the hope that Mr Mountbatten-Windsor could help her imprisoned husband in Turkey.

Giving evidence on Thursday through a Turkish interpreter, Mrs Isbilen said she is part of the Evyap family, which owns a major soap and cosmetics company in Turkey.

She said she joined the board of the Evyap Group after her father’s death in the 1990s but sold her shares in 2015 due to political disagreements with her family.

In a witness statement, she said she met Mr Turk when she was “extremely vulnerable” after her husband was imprisoned on “politically motivated” terrorism charges and she moved to London in 2018 due to “political persecution”.

Mrs Isbilen said that, on November 15 2019, she authorised a payment of £750,000 to the then Duke of York to help with her application for UK residency.

In her written evidence, she said her old passport had been seized by Turkish police but she had later left the country without one.

She said: “Mr Turk had the picture of my old passport. Mr Turk told me that he received help from the Duke of York to show or send the picture of my old passport to Turkey and that this service would normally be worth £2.0 million, but it would cost less if we made the payment by way of a gift.

“I did not understand the nature of the services received by then and have also difficulty… explaining it now.”

![Andrew is alleged to have received money from Selman Turk](https://static.independent.co.uk/2022/04/02/09/newFile-3.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Mrs Isbilen said she has now seen an email from Mr Turk to her bank explaining that the transfer was “a wedding gift to Princess Beatrice owing to the close connection between our families”.

Mrs Isbilen said the email is “entirely false”.

In her statement, she said: “I do not claim against Mr Turk for this transfer as I have since received this money back pursuant to a settlement agreement which I entered into with Andrew Mountbatten-Windsor and others in 2022.”

She added: “Mr Turk told me that he and Mr Mountbatten-Windsor had travelled by private jet to Turkey in order to discuss my husband’s situation. I now do not believe that Mr Turk told me the truth about his relations with Mr Mountbatten-Windsor.

“To my knowledge, neither I nor my husband received services or assistance from Mr Mountbatten-Windsor.”

Mr Turk’s lawyers say Mrs Isbilen knew the money was being paid in the hope that Mr Mountbatten-Windsor could help her husband.

In her written evidence, Mrs Isbilen said Mr Turk made two payments from her account totalling 1.1 million US dollars (£840,000 at current rates) to a company called Alphabet Capital Limited.

She said that Mr Turk has said in evidence in proceedings that one payment related to real estate property and the other was “a loan to Andrew Mountbatten-Windsor for the purchase of a chalet in Switzerland”.

She said: “Mr Turk says in his witness statement that the latter payment was made because I was keen to strengthen my relationship with Andrew Mountbatten Windsor in the hope that a high up connection in the UK would eventually lead political pressure being applied on Turkey to help my husband.

“This is not true. I did not know about, or authorise, either of the payments to Alphabet Capital Limited.”

Mrs Isbilen also said that Mr Turk had set up an online banking business which she believes he invested her funds in without consent.

She said in the winter of 2019 and 2020 she attended an event to promote the businesses at St James’s Palace and said a WhatsApp picture sent to her suggests the event was hosted by the then Duke of York.

She said: “I can only wonder if there is any connection between this event and the Duke of York transfer on November 15 2019.”

Joshua Hitchens, for Mr Turk, said in written submissions that Mrs Isbilen’s claim was “a mess” and should be dismissed as her allegations “cannot withstand scrutiny”.

He said that she knew the true purpose of the payment to Mr Mountbatten-Windsor, adding: “Paying public figures for preferential treatment was well within the claimant’s modus operandi.”

He told the court: “The claimant understood that she was paying £750,000 for a payment that on any view was corrupt.”

Mrs Isbilen is due to continue her evidence for two weeks, and the trial before Mr Justice Adam Johnson is due to conclude in March, with a written judgment at a later date.
