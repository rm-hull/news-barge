Peter Mandelson was warned to tone down his praise of Donald Trump in a key speech because it sounded like he had “drunk the kool aid”, the latest tranche of files on the scandal surrounding his sacking shows.

In the Ditchley Foundation annual lecture, shortly before Mr Trump’s second state visit to the UK, Lord Mandelson, who was then the UK’s ambassador to Washington, described the US President as a “risk-taker” sounding a “deafening wake-up call” to a stale “status quo”.

“The president may not follow the traditional rulebook or conventional practice, but he is a risk-taker in a world where a ‘business as usual’ approach no longer works,” he said.

![Lord Mandelson was told to tone down pro-Trump speech](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/26/11/01M8BTPMZXVZB0ZFR4TH620R49.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“Indeed, he seems to have an ironclad stomach for political risk, both at home and abroad – convening other nations and intervening in conflicts that other presidents would have thought endlessly about before descending into an analysis paralysis and gradual incrementalism.

“Yet – and this is not well understood – although the Trumpian national security strategy is called ‘America First’, it does not actually mean ‘America Alone’.”

It has now emerged that, ahead of the event, Lord Mandelson sent a copy of his speech to a director of his lobbying firm, who told him to tone down “parts of your apologia of the Trump administration”.

![Lord Mandelson was warned to tone down ‘parts of your apologia of the Trump administration’, the files show](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/25/17/50/GettyImages-2301532592.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The new tranche of files show that, in an email dated August 28, 2025, the unnamed director wrote: “As you will see, I have made suggestions on toning down some parts of your apologia of the Trump administration.

“Scepticism of it is now so deep – at least in the public policy-interested world outside government – that it would be counterproductive/unpersuasive if you came across as having drunk the Kool Aid.”

Lord Mandelson later replied: “Thanks. I will look at changes.” He also added that he would “soften” the language on China.

The speech also included a call for a US-UK technology partnership to win a battle with China he said would shape this century.

Lord Mandelson was sacked last September over his relationship with the paedophile financier Jeffrey Epstein.

Then prime minister Sir Keir Starmer fired the Labour peer after the publication of emails that showed Lord Mandelson sent supportive messages to Epstein even as the latter was facing jail for sex offences.

No 10 said the emails revealed “materially different” information from what was known when Lord Mandelson was appointed to the key role of UK ambassador to the US.

The speech praising Trump came comes at a delicate time in US-UK relations as UK officials sough to secure a carve-out on tariffs for British steel, which remaines at 25 per cent despite a trade deal agreed between Sir Keir Starmer and Mr Trump which slashed levies on a number of other goods.

![Mandelson was sacked over his links to Epstein (Departamento de Justicia de EE. UU./PA).](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/05/19/16/83614e97f0967ab2f548bda74016dfd1Y29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzc5Mjg4NjA1-2.67647057.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The latest files also include a letter sent to Starmer by Mandelson on 21 July 2025, about President Trump’s visit to Scotland later that month.

In it, Mandelson urges the prime minister to use the visit to “make progress on our goal of landing an ambitious UK-US Technology Partnership”, explaining how Trump had been “flooding the zone” with a “record-breaking number of executive orders” in the opening months of his second term as president.

Mandelson says Trump had succeeded in the “notable achievement” of passing his flagship legislation, the “One Big Beautiful Bill Act”, and advises Starmer he may wish to mention this “landmark advance”.

However, he adds that while the Trump administration “grapples with complex issues at home” it is key the UK government retains a “narrow focus on a select number of key UK interests”. These include an economic prosperity deal, technology partnership, defence and security, and nuclear power - in which he says the US and UK have a “one-in-a-generation” opportunity to “regain our historical leadership”, he says.
