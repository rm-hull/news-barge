US President Donald Trump says he has told Russian leader Vladimir Putin to “pull back all Military aggression against territories throughout Europe” in the hope this could lead to a quick settlement of the war in Ukraine.

“During my conversations in China with President Vladimir Putin, of Russia, I spent a lot of time talking about the fact that I would like to see President Putin pull back all Military aggression against territories throughout Europe, in the hopes that a Deal can be made between Russia and Ukraine, and made quickly,” Mr Trump wrote in a Truth Social post.

“I really believe this is a strong possibility – Thousands of people are being killed each and every month, A COMPLETE BLOODBATH.”

The US president has frequently urged a negotiated end to the war in Ukraine.

On Thursday, however, he used broader language about “territories throughout Europe”, without specifying what that meant.

![Russia's President Vladimir Putin and US President Donald Trump](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/23/8/39/GettyImages-2305266500.jpg?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&auto=webp)

Mr Trump’s remarks followed closed-door talks at his Mar-a-Lago resort on Wednesday night with Nato secretary general Mark Rutte, after which the two leaders issued a joint statement stressing the importance of Russia avoiding any actions that “intentionally, or unintentionally” expand the war.

Since the Russian invasion of Ukraine started in 2022, other countries occasionally have accused Russia of provocative behaviour.

Poland, a Nato member, this week accused Russia of deliberately violating Polish airspace, while Estonia recently linked an arson attack to Russia’s military intelligence service.

But despite Mr Trump’s optimism that an agreement can be reached “quickly,” events on the ground on Thursday underscored how far apart the two sides remain.

In Kyiv, Ukrainian president Volodymyr Zelensky warned that Russia is preparing another major wave of strikes targeting Ukraine’s energy infrastructure, logistics networks, and critical public services as crews work to restore power following overnight ballistic missile and drone attacks across six regions.

Meanwhile, the Kremlin has sought to shift blame for widening the conflict onto Kyiv, with Russian foreign minister Sergey Lavrov stating that Washington is approaching negotiations with an understanding of the need to eliminate the “root causes” of the war – including “the creation of threats to Russia’s security right on our borders”.

Mr Trump made headlines when he warmly greeted Russian leader Vladimir Putin as “my friend” during the Apec summit in China.

The commander-in-chief's enthusiastic reception of Mr Putin was condemned by commentators and former diplomats who labelled it an embarrassment for America.

![Russia's President Vladimir Putin and US President Donald Trump shake hands on the sidelines of the Apec Leaders' Summit in China](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/26/17/2026-11-26T162433Z_1015617438_RC2TQOANQQ5V_RTRMADP_3_UKRAINE-CRISIS-TRUMP.JPG?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&auto=webp)

Former US ambassador to Russia Michael McFaul said that his decision to call Mr Putin his “friend” was radically out of step with American society.

Back at the White House, Mr Trump was asked whether Mr Putin was also a friend to the United States.

“I can't speak to America,” he replied, while emphasising his “very good” personal relationship with the Russian leader.

He later posted an image of himself standing close to Mr Putin and shaking his hand beneath a caption in large type: “My Friend.”

It was one of dozens of photos he shared from the summit throughout the course of the day, many of which depicted him alongside the Russian leader and Chinese President Xi Jinping.

The warm overtures have also coincided with growing alarm in Kyiv over stalled US military support, after reports this week that Pentagon chief Pete Hegseth has held up the delivery of roughly 60 Patriot air-defence interceptors already pledged to Ukraine and paid for by European allies.

On Wednesday, Mr Trump’s son-in-law and lead diplomatic envoy, Jared Kushner – who has been mediating the Russia-Ukraine talks alongside fellow envoy Steve Witkoff – praised Mr Putin as a “grandmaster of politics” and argued the Russian leader had shown “restraint” by agreeing not to use nuclear weapons during an appearance on Axel Springer’s *MD Meets* podcast.

However, Mr Kushner acknowledged that peace talks have hit “a little bit of a deadlock” over the fate of occupied Ukrainian territory, adding: “What [Putin] is saying is peace, but I think it’s always peace on what terms.”
