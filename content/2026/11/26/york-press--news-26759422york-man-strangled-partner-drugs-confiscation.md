AN addict strangled his girlfriend until she nearly blacked out after she flushed his drugs down the toilet, York Crown Court heard.

Nathan James, 37, told the York university student he had taken crack cocaine, said Henry Fernandez, prosecuting.

She couldn’t breathe when he pressed her throat with one hand so hard it left marks.

She estimated he did so for about a minute as she tried to fight him off.

“I cannot adequately describe the terror of believing that my life was ending,” the woman said in a personal statement.

Judge Simon Hickey said the woman believed James may have taken ketamine because of the “strange” way he was behaving.

She had taken James’s drugs and flushed them down the toilet – and James had then strangled her.

Mr Fernandez said after the strangulation, the woman was so afraid of James, she got him out of her home and paid for a taxi to take him away before running back into her home and ringing police.

When officers found James shortly afterwards, he called one of them by homophobic names as he resisted their efforts to get him into a police van.

Bricklayer James, who now lives in Highfield Green, Sherburn-in-Elmet, pleaded guilty to strangulation and using threatening words or behaviour.

He was given a 25-month prison sentence suspended for 18 months with a probation requirement and on condition he does a rehabilitation programme on how to conduct domestic relationships.

His barrister Zarreen Alam-Cheetham said: “He has moved out of the area because he is serious about starting a new life and has no intention of restarting the relationship.”

He had had a difficult childhood and had been addicted to alcohol for 20 years.

He said he had had some success in dealing with his drug and alcohol addictions.

“This is someone who clearly needs help from professional services in order to ensure the history of his childhood doesn’t continue to repeat itself,” she said.

Mr Fernandez said on other occasions before the strangulation in September, James had grabbed the woman by the face, slapped her and kicked her head.

None of the incidents had led to convictions.

On September 23, the woman collected James and took her to her home.

He brought drugs with him and got angry when she confiscated them.

Ms Alam-Cheetham claimed James had brought the drugs for both of them to take.

He had been mixing with the wrong crowd at the time.

Since then he had reflected on his actions and was “devastated for everyone involved”.

He was also remorseful.
