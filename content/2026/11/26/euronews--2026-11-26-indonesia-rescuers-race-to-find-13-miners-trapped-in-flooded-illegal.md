## The disaster began late on Monday, when heavy rain made the Ciseureuh River burst its banks and pour into a mine shaft, which is estimated to be around 100 metres deep.

Rescuers in Indonesia on Thursday were contending with rising water, darkness and steep, slippery ground as they combed the site in Bogor regency, a mountainous area south of the capital Jakarta.

Rescuers say they are searching for 13 more miners believed trapped by flooding in an illegal gold mine where 13 bodies were recovered earlier.

The disaster began late on Monday, when heavy rain made the Ciseureuh River burst its banks and pour into a mine shaft, which is estimated to be around 100 metres deep. Miners were digging for gold at the time.

Some escaped from one section of the excavation before the shaft filled with water.

Authorities said the illegal nature of the operation meant they were not alerted straight away. Local miners and residents first tried to pump out the water themselves until the situation spiralled out of control.

Police received the first reports of trapped miners some 16 hours after the flooding. The nearby state-owned mining company PT Antam passed a formal report to the Jakarta Search and Rescue Agency shortly after midnight on Wednesday, which set a full-scale operation in motion.

## **Search focuses on two shafts**

Teams have been using submersible pumps to drain the shaft and working to seal openings where groundwater is seeping in.

On Thursday, 325 personnel concentrated on two adjacent shafts, using rope-access techniques to reach victims, said agency chief Desiana Kartika Bahari.

The flooded shaft lies close to facilities run by PT Antam's Pongkor gold mining unit. The company said the incident had nothing to do with its operations.

Informal mining is widespread in Indonesia, where thousands of people work in hazardous conditions.

Miners risk landslides, flooding and tunnel collapses, while gold processing often exposes them to toxic mercury and cyanide with little or no protective equipment.
