Downing Street has confirmed it is unable to stop EU imports, such as foie gras, from being sold in the UK under a new agrifood deal with the bloc.

The production of the French delicacy, which is made from ducks and geese that have been force-fed, was banned in the UK under the Animal Welfare Act 2006.

While a ban on imports of the luxury product was not part of Labour’s 2024 manifesto, during the 2024 general election campaign, the then-shadow environment secretary Steve Reed said he would “ban the commercial import of foie gras, where ducks and geese are aggressively force-fed” if elected.

Downing Street has now confirmed that the agrifood deal clinched by Prime Minister Andy Burnham with the bloc last week will mean that imports from the EU cannot be stopped.

The Prime Minister’s spokesperson said on Thursday: “We are keeping our long-standing domestic ban on foie gras production and using force feeding.

![The agrifood deal clinched by Prime Minister Andy Burnham will mean that imports from the EU cannot be stopped](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/26/00/fd8348d18427be34cf125d95dea94629Y29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzk1Njg5ODk0-2.86541381.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“The deal does mean we cannot introduce measures to stop EU goods such as foie gras from being sold in Great Britain.

“But we will use our ability to shape EU rules and keep sharing the UK’s views and experience as we work with the EU on protecting and improving animal welfare.”

The Animal Welfare Act 2006 made it a criminal offence not to provide for an animal’s welfare needs or to allow an animal to suffer unnecessarily.

Elisa Allen, vice president of programmes and operations at Peta (People for the Ethical Treatment of Animals), said: “Every kind and decent person agrees that there is no place in modern Britain for foie gras, a product of appalling cruelty.

“By failing to make good on its promise to ban the import of this ‘torture in a tin’, the Government is betraying not only gentle ducks and geese, who desperately need a caring nation to defend them, but also the public, which has made its opposition to foie gras clear.”
