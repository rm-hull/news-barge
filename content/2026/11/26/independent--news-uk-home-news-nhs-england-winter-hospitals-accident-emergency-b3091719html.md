Health chiefs have warned that proposals to move care out of hospitals and into the community have arrived "dangerously late", as they said the NHS must prepare for its busiest winter on record.

Voicing "frustration" over how "overwhelmed" the health service has become, leaders noted that demand has grown faster than "anyone could have imagined".

NHS bosses warned that the service in England is at "risk of regressing" while resources are "stretched to the limit".

At the same time, trust leaders expressed concern about scaling back services due to financial limitations and staffing pressures.

A new survey of health chiefs across England revealed that over half (58%) fear demand could outstrip capacity this winter.

One trust boss said: “Last winter was the busiest winter on record. This winter will be busier. Therefore, this winter will be the busiest on record.”

Meanwhile, 79% of 184 NHS leaders said they do not believe the NHS can simultaneously recover waiting time targets and deliver the Government’s three major shifts in care with current funding, according to the survey by NHS Alliance.

Labour’s health plan promised three shifts for the NHS:

* Analogue to digital
* treatment to prevention
* hospital to more community care.

Speaking anonymously as the poll was released, one NHS boss told journalists: “We are dangerously late, I think, in terms of trying to shift care outside of hospital.

“There is no plan for the investment in community and primary care services in order to do that.”

“I fear that A&Es will become overwhelmed before we put in the alternatives that would keep people sensibly out of hospital when they don’t need to be there.”

“I think the number of over 65s and over 85s from the population projections is frankly scary… I think we are seeing that Baby Boomer generation live longer at this particular time – they do not benefit from going into hospital, for the most part, not because hospitals don’t do brilliant things. But if you’re old and frail, you do not benefit from being admitted to hospital and decompensating while you’re there.”

Another NHS chief said they were “both prouder, but also more frustrated” than they had ever been with the health service, adding: “The frustration comes from how overwhelmed the NHS is at the moment, and I do feel we’re at risk of regressing, whether it’s general practice, acute community, mental health, or beyond.

“Our services are clearly stretched to their limits.”

Experts described A&Es as “extremely busy”, saying these services can become “overwhelmed” when people struggle to access GP care and there are high levels of deprivation.

Referring to neurodiversity services for children, another leader described how the NHS trains staff who are lured away to private services offering higher salaries and better working conditions.

Another added: “There aren’t a whole load of therapists that are sitting out there waiting in coffee shops to be employed by the NHS when we find money, so there is a resourcing problem.”

The NHS Alliance poll also found that half of trust leaders do not believe the NHS will be able to meet its constitutional standards over the next five years.

“We have the reality that demand is growing faster than probably anyone imagined when they designed the constitutional standards,” one trust leader said.

![A new survey of health chiefs across England revealed that over half (58%) fear demand could outstrip capacity this winter](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/06/15/01M45AFZA865K7Q9ANFT5A36G8.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Another health leader said: “Many of these constitutional standards have been in place for 22 years now, the NHS and society have changed immeasurably over those 22 years, and I think it’s worth reviewing them.”

In 2024, Labour pledged that 92% of patients in need of pre-planned care such as a hip or knee replacement would be treated within 18 weeks by July 2029.

One trust leader declared that “as things stand, it looks unlikely (we will) deliver it”.

NHS Alliance said that health leaders have expressed concerns about unbudgeted extra cost pressures including pay settlements, higher than expected inflation, rising energy and medicine bills and continued growth in patient demand.

Sir Ciarán Devane, chief executive of the NHS Alliance, said: “Local NHS leaders are warning that the hard-fought gains of recent years are in peril due to a series of extra costs that are not covered in the budget.

“This is opening up a widening gap between what the NHS is being asked to deliver and the resources it has available.

“Without realistic expectations, sufficient workforce capacity and sustained investment in reform, there is a growing risk that progress on both performance recovery and long-term transformation will be slower than patients, politicians and policymakers want to see.”

Royal College of Nursing general secretary and chief executive, Professor Nicola Ranger, said: “Nursing staff have repeatedly warned about how the failure to invest in community services would lead to unsustainable pressures in hospitals, but government has been too slow to provide the investment needed.

“It is incredibly worrying to hear service leaders speak of cutting back services further in England.

“Registered nurse roles in hospitals are already being reduced due to financial pressures and this must be reversed, not made worse.

“There is a clear link between increased mortality and nurse shortages and patients will be put at risk if further cuts take place.”

Matthew Reed, chief executive of Marie Curie, said: “Alarming but not surprising, these repeated warnings from the frontline of our NHS must be urgently heeded.

“As the NHS braces for its busiest winter on record, patients and families will continue to be failed in the most appalling way.

“Without 24/7 care in the community, dying people are left relying on A&E, where attendances by people at the end of life have risen 21% since 2014.

“Of more than 1,000 frontline nurses polled this summer, more than half had delivered end of life care in a corridor.”
