A memorial service for Ann Widdecombe will go ahead at Westminster Cathedral under a change of leadership.

Bishop James Curry, the Auxiliary Bishop of Westminster, will lead the service in place of Archbishop of Westminster Richard Moth, who is undergoing tests in hospital after having “felt unwell”, the diocese said.

Reform UK’s Nigel Farage and Richard Tice are due to attend the service on Thursday, along with Conservative Party chairman Kevin Hollinrake and other political figures.

A notice from Westminster Diocese said: “Archbishop Richard felt unwell and was taken to hospital on Wednesday. He is currently undergoing preliminary tests.

“While awaiting the results, he is in good spirits and looks forward to returning to Archbishop’s House.

“We pray for his full recovery.”

![Archbishop of Westminster Richard Moth is undergoing tests](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/02/13/09/e7ec4db59077fe3a097ad1320abe7370Y29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzcxMDU5NzQ4-2.83448699.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The service will begin at 2pm, with eulogies to be delivered and the Westminster Cathedral choir set to sing.

Miss Widdecombe, a former Conservative minister and Reform UK’s justice spokeswoman, was found dead at her home in Haytor on Dartmoor, Devon, on July 9, aged 78.

Joshua Kerry, 28, of Byrley Road, Rotherham, South Yorkshire, was charged with her murder.

![Joshua Kerry is also accused of murdering Ann Widdecombe](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/02/19/87139b1e1b6fde76ec33c184af0a494dY29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzkxMDUyNzY5-2.85542805.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

A trial before a High Court judge is scheduled for June 8 next year, and a plea and case management hearing on January 29.

Kerry has also been charged with a terror plot against Mr Farage.

Miss Widdecombe’s family announced plans for a public memorial service following her death.

She was first elected as a Conservative MP in Kent in 1987, was a prisons minister in the 1990s and later became an MEP for the Brexit Party.

At the time of her death, she was a spokesperson on immigration and justice for Reform UK.

She also found fame outside politics on Strictly Come Dancing and Celebrity Big Brother.
