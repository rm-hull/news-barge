The Macy’s Thanksgiving Parade has been postponed as a nor’easter barrels toward New York City, with local officials insisting that safety comes first.

The historic decision was made to cancel the 100-year Turkey Day tradition hours after crews filled the iconic balloons with helium in preparation for Thursday morning.

A source at NBC confirmed the postponement to *The Independent* late Wednesday afternoon and said that the network will, instead, air last year’s parade in all time zones tomorrow morning.

The parade has been rescheduled for Saturday morning, with festivities kicking off at 8:30 a.m. Eastern Time. The decision was made for the safety of the performers and spectators, *The Independent* was told.

Would-be attendees told local news that they were upset that the weather upended their plans to see the parade in person on Thursday as they won’t be able to make it on Saturday.

![The decision was made for the safety of the performers and spectators, The Independent was told](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/25/22/11/GettyImages-2306281221.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

New York City is expected to be hit with rain, which can turn heavy at times, on Thursday. One to two inches of rain is possible, according to the National Weather Service.

The wind is also supposed to pick up to between 28 and 34 mph, causing a headache for parade operators if the show was to go on as scheduled. The New York State Division of Homeland Security and Emergency Services warned of winds up to 60 mph in portions of New York City Thursday, which could cause scattered power outages.

During a press conference Wednesday night as crews were gearing up to deflate the balloons, Will Coss, vice president and head of Macy’s Studios, said the company was “heartbroken” by the postponement, saying “this parade has been built with love.”

“We're saddened by the shift, but we're optimistic about our opportunity to deliver the most spectacular 100th Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade, the best and biggest parade in our history,” he added.

New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani, who walked up to the podium to cheers, said, “Extreme weather has forced us to reschedule for Saturday instead.

“This decision, as you heard, was made in coordination with Macy's, the NYPD, and many agency partners. Safety is and will always remain our top priority, and this will be a heavy storm.”

It will be mostly sunny Saturday with a high near 55 degrees Fahrenheit.

Coss said the balloons will be deflated later Wednesday, returned to a facility in New Jersey, and then returned to New York for the parade on Saturday.

He then sidestepped the possible issue of fewer parade participants, given that many bands from all over the country flew to Manhattan to perform Thursday and booked return flights for Friday.

“We've been in communication with all of our participants, from our bands to our performance groups to our performing talent,” Coss said. “We are optimistic that we're going to be able to maintain the parade that we set out to produce and to deliver to New York City on Thanksgiving.”

New York City Police Commissioner Jessica Tisch reassured the public that there are “no specific credible threats to the parade at this time.

“New Yorkers will see an increased police presence, with NYPD officers posted along the entire two and a half mile parade route and on the surrounding blocks.”

Tisch said that the NYPD will have to call in more than 1,000 officers who weren’t scheduled to work Saturday to help with the parade.

“Our NYPD officers always step up for New York City, and Saturday will be no different,” she said.

Mamdani thanked the crews who have a heavier workload because of the delay, adding, “We know that this is an inconvenience for many who come to visit our city. This decision, however, was made with safety being paramount.”

“What I would say to city workers is what I would say to them every single day, which is, ‘Thank you,’” Mamdani said. “We are speaking about hundreds of thousands of New Yorkers who work for this city, who make events like this possible, and you can see the palpable excitement of New Yorkers and those who have come to our city in advance of the 100th anniversary of this parade, and it's city workers that are going to make it possible.”

With all eyes on him, Mamdani used this opportunity to also warn New Yorkers of the dangerous conditions ahead and remind them to stay safe.

“We are expecting widespread moderate coastal flooding at tomorrow morning and tomorrow evening's high tides, and we are anticipating moderate to heavy rain from tomorrow morning into Friday. Flash flooding is possible,” Mamdani said.

“If you are traveling, please budget extra time and use caution. Leave early and prepare for the possibility that you may have to stay late. There is no destination worth risking your safety. If conditions become severe, please stay indoors and wait until it is safe to travel.”

The first Macy’s Thanksgiving Day Parade was held in 1924, but back then it was called the Macy’s Christmas Parade. There were Macy’s employees dressed in costumes, a jazz band and even tigers. For the grand finale, Santa climbed the marquee of Macy’s flagship store on West 34th Street to unveil the store’s holiday window display.

The parade stretches 2.5 miles through the heart of Manhattan, starting near Central Park and ending at Macy’s Herald Square location.

This year’s parade will mark the 100th year of the celebration. It was only canceled three times from 1942 to 1944 due to World War II, but has never been postponed due to weather — until now.

*The New York Post*, which was the first to report the postponement.

The beloved parade balloons have only once been grounded because of inclement weather in 1971, but the show continued with floats and other festivities.

Strong winds are taken seriously by parade operators following the infamous 1997 incident when the Cat in the Hat balloon crashed into a lamppost, critically injuring a spectator. Restrictions were subsequently put in place that grounded parade balloons when sustained winds exceed 23 mph or gusts reach 34 mph.

There have also been several instances when Macy’s lowered the height of its balloons because of the weather. In 2006, parade inflatables were flown lower to the ground because of an all-day downpour. The balloons were lowered again in 2019 due to gusty winds.

Macy’s plans to kick off its 100th parade with a record 100 inflatables depicting everyone’s favorite characters like the famed Snoopy balloon.

"Snoopy has always been my personal favorite," Jen Neal, the executive vice president of live events and specials at NBC and Peacock, said in a promotional article. "I grew up watching the Parade with my family, so there’s something about seeing Snoopy that immediately takes me back.”

While some spectators at Wednesday’s balloon inflation were disappointed by the postponement, they were also grateful that the parade will be rescheduled.

One spectator told CBS News New York, “It's our favorite tradition to be here. We're so grateful, and we're really happy that everybody will be safe now, and the balloons can fly safely, and no one will have to be outside in the bad weather.”

“I'm really upset about it because so much goes into the parade each year and it's like a tradition each year to watch, like get up early and watch the parade with my whole family. So I'm a little bit disappointed,” another said.

But she added, “I'm really excited that they're changing it because so much work goes into it every year, and people like come from all over to help with the balloons.”
