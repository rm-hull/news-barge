## EU leaders are clashing at the European Council over a record-high long-term budget, divided on defense, farm subsidies, and Ukraine aid. Germany threatens a veto unless all sign on to Ukraine funding, while Spain’s Sánchez skips the summit ahead of Sunday’s election, delegating his vote to Macron.

European Union leaders have convened at the European Council for high-stakes negotiations over the bloc's next long-term budget, with sharp divisions threatening to derail a record-high spending package.

The summit has quickly exposed deep fault lines among member states over what to prioritize and what to cut, as funding for Ukraine, agricultural subsidies, and defense spending all hang in the balance. Speaking ahead of the talks, Italian Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni underscored the scale of the deadlock, warning that leaders remain "light-years away from a solution."

Tensions have been further heightened by German Chancellor Friedrich Merz, who sparked controversy prior to the summit by insisting that future financial support for Ukraine be tied directly to the broader EU budget, and threatening to veto the entire package unless every member state signs on.

Meanwhile, the negotiations are unfolding without Spanish Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez. Facing critical national elections on Sunday that could cost him his parliamentary majority, Sánchez has opted to stay in Spain, delegating his voting powers to French President Emmanuel Macron.
