The marching band for the University of Southern California Trojans football team has been ousted from an upcoming Rose Bowl game with its rival team, the UCLA Bruins, over a prank involving a lewd phrase.

USC President Beong-Soo Kim announced in a letter to the school community Wednesday that the Trojan Marching Band will not perform at the pre-game and halftime shows at the famed stadium in Pasadena Saturday after university officials learned that part of UCLA’s campus was vandalized by student band members.

“This conduct runs contrary to USC's values and is unacceptable,” Kim wrote.

The prank involved removing letters from a sign on the Wasserman Football Center, so it read “a\*\* man,” according to UCLA students.

![University of Southern California President Beong-Soo Kim announced in a letter to the school community Wednesday that the Trojan Marching Band will not perform at the pre-game and halftime shows at the famed stadium in Pasadena Saturday after university officials learned that part of UCLA’s campus was vandalized by student band members](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/26/16/44/GettyImages-2244085445.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

"At midnight it said 'a\*\* man.’ At 5 in the morning, when I was walking back that same way, the 'man' part had been removed," Eli Lynch, who was helping guard the UCLA Bruin statue from pranks Tuesday night into Wednesday morning, told KABC.

USC students also ripped down banners and put soap inside a fountain, Lynch said.

Lynch celebrated USC’s decision to ban its marching band from the game.

“Their fight song should qualify as noise pollution so I think it's good for everyone that we're not gonna have to hear that multiple times throughout the game,” he said.

USC and UCLA students have been on high alert ahead of Saturday’s game as pranks are common in the rivalry. At USC, students duct tape their Tommy Trojan statue and at UCLA, the Bruin Bear statue is hidden under a wooden box. Students at both universities stand guard over the statues for extra protection against any shenanigans.

Kim acknowledged the rivalry between the two schools, but suggested the pranks had gone too far.

“We want to express our deepest apologies to the entire UCLA community. Our rivalry is one of the great traditions in college athletics, and it has always rested on mutual respect. What happened on UCLA's campus last night has no place in this tradition,” the university president said.

Kim said the school is “cooperating fully” with police and that an investigation has been opened.

“Appropriate interim actions will be or have already been taken, and any student ultimately found responsible will be subject to discipline under university policy,” Kim warned.

Saturday’s game will be the first time since 2022 that the Bruins and Trojans have faced off as ranked teams, ESPN reported. Last time, USC defeated UCLA 48 to 45. But this time, the Bruins — No. 9 in the College Football Playoff rankings — may come out on top against the No. 15 Trojans.
