It’s easy to be seduced by the idea of hopping between Croatia’s islands, and Split is a handy place to start.

Direct summer flights from Leeds Bradford to Split take around three hours. From there, Hvar, Brač and Korčula are all within reach by ferry. Visiting them takes more planning than picking a few names, though. Sailing times, accommodation and how much of the trip you spend travelling all need thinking about. Whether you book everything separately or compare Croatia package holidays, a little planning leaves more time for Split’s old town, island swims and long dinners.

## Give Split More Than a Passing Visit

Split is often treated as a stop on the way to the islands, which is a shame because the city deserves attention in its own right. The old town sits inside Roman walls that now surround shops, cafés and homes. Peškarija, the city’s fish market, is worth a look in the morning. You can spend an afternoon walking through the narrow streets before heading to the Riva waterfront for a drink overlooking the harbour.

Marjan Hill is another good place to visit, especially if you’d like a break from the busy centre. Its walking paths lead through pine trees to viewpoints overlooking the city and nearby islands. Stay at least one night before catching a ferry. You’ll have time to enjoy Split and avoid turning your first day into a race between the airport and the port.

## Decide How You Want to Spend Your Time on Hvar

Hvar Town draws plenty of summer visitors, and it’s easy to see why. The harbour is lined with restaurants, boats fill the marina, and the old streets climb towards the Spanish Fortress. Head up there for views across the rooftops and out towards the Pakleni Islands, where boat trips take visitors to secluded swimming spots.

The island has quieter places to stay if you’d prefer evenings away from the busiest waterfront. Stari Grad has a historic centre, a relaxed harbour and the nearby Stari Grad Plain, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, while Jelsa offers easy access to beaches and local restaurants. Both make good bases for a few days of swimming and exploring.

## Add Brač or Korčula to Your Route

Brač is an appealing choice if you want to spend plenty of time at the beach. On the north coast, Lovrečina Bay near Postira has a rare sandy beach and clear water. Above Bol, Vidova Gora rises to 778 metres, the highest point on any Adriatic island, with views across to Hvar and Vis. The island also has stone villages and quieter coastal stretches worth exploring.

Korčula is a good choice if you enjoy historic towns and long evenings over dinner. Its old centre is enclosed by medieval walls, with narrow lanes leading to small squares and waterfront restaurants. Look out for local Grk wine, produced around Lumbarda, or try žrnovski makaruni, a hand-rolled pasta from the village of Žrnovo, and leave time to explore beyond the town itself.

For a seven-day holiday, choose one of these islands alongside Hvar, or spend longer in a single place. Changing hotels every night eats into the time you have for enjoying the coast.

## Check Ferry Times Before Booking Hotels

Ferry schedules shape an island-hopping itinerary, so check them before committing to accommodation. Passenger catamarans connect Split with several islands, while car ferries serve routes where taking a vehicle makes sense. Services vary by season, and the summer timetable usually offers more departures. The fast catamaran from Split to Hvar Town takes around an hour.

A week-long trip works well with Split and two island stays. Give each stop at least two nights, then return to the mainland before your flight home. If you’re flying out of Split, staying near the city the previous evening saves a stressful early-morning transfer. Direct flights back to Leeds Bradford usually run only a couple of days a week, so plan the route around your return date.

You probably won’t need a hire car for the entire trip. Ferries and local buses cover many popular routes, and a car adds another booking to manage when moving between islands. Check how far your accommodation is from the port before you reserve it, especially if you’re travelling with large suitcases.

## Explore Beyond the Beaches

Croatia has plenty to keep you occupied when you want a change from swimming and sunbathing. Dubrovnik’s old town is an obvious choice for a day of sightseeing, with its defensive walls, historic buildings and limestone streets. The wall walk is worth doing, although an early start helps you avoid the strongest afternoon heat and summer crowds.

Krka National Park, near Šibenik, offers a different day out, with waterfalls and walking paths along the river. On Mljet, much of the western part of the island is protected as a national park, with woodland trails and saltwater lakes. Both destinations suit travellers who enjoy spending time outdoors.

When you’re back by the coast, make a point of trying local dishes. Dalmatian restaurants serve grilled fish, seafood risotto and meals prepared with regional olive oil. A family-run konoba is a good place to try traditional cooking, especially if the menu features the day’s catch. On Hvar, look out for gregada, a traditional fish stew made with potatoes and olive oil.

## Pick a Month That Suits Your Budget

July and August are the busiest months on the Croatian coast. Beaches fill up, ferry ports get crowded, and accommodation prices rise. June and September are often easier on the budget, while still offering warm conditions for swimming and sightseeing.

When comparing packages, look closely at the flight times and accommodation location. A hotel near the ferry port makes island transfers simpler, while a resort outside Split may require additional transport. Check what is included in the package before comparing prices, as transfer arrangements can make a difference to the overall cost.

## Make the Journey From Leeds Bradford Count

You don’t need to visit every Croatian island in one holiday. A few days in Split followed by stays on Hvar and Brač or Korčula gives you plenty to see without spending half the week in transit. Check the ferry timetable, allow time for the return journey and choose accommodation that suits the way you want to spend your days. Then you can give the beaches, towns and restaurants the time they deserve.
