## Why gaming monitors represent one of the best purchases you can make

![Gaming monitor and desk setup](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/L3PCSPz5pXLjMkNp9dibST-1200-80.jpg)

To get somewhat philosophical. As far as I'm concerned, we can categorize PC hardware into three distinct roles. The first, performance drivers, are your GPUs, RAM, SSDs, CPUs... you know, those things that generate the frames and determine your overall experience.

The second are the enablers: your interconnects, motherboards, cases, PSUs, cooling (admittedly the latter does sort of straddle the first group a bit as well).

And the last one is your experientials, basically what you use to engage with whatever it is you're doing.

It's that last group that's often overlooked in terms of real potential, and particularly how much joy and satisfaction an upgrade here can give you. Keyboards obviously grant that tactile feel and feedback every time you hit a key switch. The mouse then determines how smooth your engagement is, while headphones and speakers drive such an emotive auditory stimulus it's hard to measure.

Yet more importantly than all of those combined is the humble monitor. It's that that has the biggest impact on your PC gaming experience. Because, to use a PC in this capacity, you're going to be staring at a screen, and you're going to be doing that for 100% of the duration that you're using it. You might not type, your APM might be low, your mouse movement paused, you could be sat on the desktop doing nothing, but by god your eyes are going to be fixed on that panel.

It's why a lot of journalists get obsessive over high refresh rates and beautifully color-accurate QD OLED panels for desktop work. Because the reality is it makes every OS you can imagine feel so much smoother and more enjoyable to use, even outside of gaming.

And, typically, it does that without necessarily needing a massively OP graphics card (although obviously the latter does help). Nothing gives you a more premium feel than being sat in Excel on one of the best 34-inch Ultrawide OLED panels, that's for sure.

## OLED panels are now the cheapest they've ever been, and it gets even better

![Render of gaming desktop setup with Gigabyte monitor](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/QyCA2F4qrGoTMbjppk5X77-1200-80.png)

So why is now the best time to buy? Simply put, AI data centers don't need an abundance of monitors to operate, unlike the shipping containers full of DRAM, GPUs, and NAND, and that's a good thing for us, because production rates and development of this tech are continuing to improve rapidly, driving down prices.

We've seen some of the lowest price tags ever for OLED panels this year, across the myriad of Prime Days that Amazon has thrown our way seemingly every other month, with a whole host of 27-inch OLED 240 Hz monitors sitting around the $200-300 mark.

Why? Well, the brilliant thing here (aside from low demand from AI), is that there's not just one manufacturer producing the panels. Both Samsung Display and LG Display produce QD-OLED and WOLED gaming monitor panels, respectively. On top of that, we're actually getting two additional panel manufacturers joining the party as well, with TCL CSOT and BOE dipping their toes into the water too, either already producing them, or converting manufacturing lines over.

On top of that, production is increasing rapidly with the big two as well. And as we're seeing new, more exotic tech hit the market, in terms of 720 Hz refresh rates, RGB stripe QD-OLED, and Tandem OLED, these all drive down the prices of those older models, which are still exemplary to look at and use, particularly when gaming.

Combine that with a slew of secondary parties taking those panels and competing against each other for sales (Asus, MSI, AOC, ViewSonic and so on) and all of a sudden us consumers are finally on the receiving end of some good genuine capitalism for once.

## It's not all sunshine and cheap monitors

![Two Alienware gaming monitors against a sparkly background](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/GqQewp8AjUMKkGfUYMCC5Y-1200-80.jpg)

There is one slight caveat to all that, however, and it does once again revolve around DRAM componentry.

Unfortunately, we are seeing some impact from memory chip demand hit monitors as well (as they do use memory in the construction). KTC, AOC, and Dell have all seen price adjustments this year to name a few, attributed to, you guessed it, data center demand. So there's a chance the pricing might not fall as fast as we'd like. But it *is* falling.

## What should you be looking for?

![A graphics card in a gaming PC](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/DA2kKcQYs5cSNdhXBEywub-1200-80.jpg)

A lot of this is going to depend on your use case. Namely, what graphics card you have, what games you play, what you do day-to-day. If you're an RTS or MMORPG nerd like me, I highly recommend a 34-inch ultrawide, at 3440x1440.

It's just enough pixel density to keep you sated but not enough to murder your GPU. 100 Hz plus is a must, ideally a WOLED panel, but grab what you can afford (it also makes any productivity work a dream, unless you're editing 4K footage). Something like Alienware's legendary AW3425DW is a good fit.

Do you play FPS games, or competitive multiplayer titles? I'd highly recommend any of the myriad of 240 Hz 27-inchers. Acer's Predator X27U W1 is at an insanely low $270 already, meaning that may shift even further on the run up to the big day.

If you either already own an RTX 5090 or have just splurged solid second hand car money on one, 32-inch 4K QD-OLED is the way to go. Samsung's Odyssey G81SF is up there with the best of them, albeit at a four-figure price tag to match (although I have seen it float around the $700 mark at times).

Honestly though, I can't stress enough the advantage of a 34-inch ultrawide for productivity work. If you spot one at a low price, with solid reviews, that's the first thing I'd be looking to sink my teeth into.
