Former Premier League legend Fabrizio Ravanelli has won a High Court fight with the UK taxman over a £340,000 bill linked to his playing days 20 years ago.

Ravanelli, famed for his prematurely white hair which won him the nickname “white feather,” carved out a stellar career as a striker in Italy and later England, where he was one of the biggest stars in the early days of the Prem, clocking up a hat-trick against Liverpool on his debut for Middlesbrough in 1996.

The Italian international went on to make 50 appearances for the club, scoring 32 goals, before moving on to Marseille and Lazio, and then returning to the Premier League in the latter stages of his career with Derby County.

But he ended up fighting a High Court battle after some of his earnings - paid into an "employee benefit trust" (EBT) by Derby County - were hit with a massive tax bill when cashed in.

The trustees running the fund ended up handing HMRC £1.2m out of the £2.1m destined for the former star in "settlement" of any tax which was due on the money.

But the former footballer launched a High Court fight against the bill, arguing that he is due the money and that it should be paid back to him.

![Peter Schmeichel of Aston Villa argues with Fabrizio Ravanelli of Derby during the FA Barclaycard Premiership Match between Derby County and Aston Villa at Pride Par](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/24/12/1069457..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

After a hearing earlier this month, a judge has now ruled that HMRC considered his case wrongly and ordered that they reconsider whether he should get the cash.

Mr Justice Eardley said the taxman had refused to reopen the "settlement agreement" between HMRC and the trust on the basis of a wrong belief that the trust was liable for national insurance contributions (NICs) - as opposed to the player's employer, the now dissolved Derby County Ltd.

During the hearing earlier this month, the judge heard that the dispute arose because part of Mr Ravanelli's earnings at Derby County were paid into an employment benefit trust.

Although he was only at the club from 2001 to 2003, it paid a total of £1m into the trust between February 2002 and March 2006 - which due to investment gains was worth £2,136,310 by July 2021.

The issue of tax arose because, faced with the prospect of an impending inheritance tax charge due to employee benefit trust rules, the trustees decided to pay the money out.

The money to be paid out of the trust to the former footballer would be "part of his remuneration as an employee," the judge was told.

![Fabrizio Ravanelli celebrates scoring](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/24/12/1057620..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

After discussion with HMRC, a "settlement" was agreed by the trustee under which about £1.2m went from the trust to the taxman, with the rest going to Mr Ravanelli.

It led to the former player challenging the trustee's bill, arguing that any NICs due were the responsibility of his previous employer - the long dissolved club company - and that tax rules state that, "if the employer no longer exists, the employee will not have to pay any National Insurance contributions."

His lawyers said that, in those circumstances, HMRC acted wrongly in refusing to "reopen the settlement agreement" and should now pay him the £340,000 which was said to have represented NICs.

"The NICs were not owed by or on behalf of Mr Ravanelli," his barrister, Tim Johnston, said. "They remained liabilities of the employer, who had ceased to exist.

"According to HMRC’s own guidance, no sums should have been withheld in respect of NICs when the distribution from the EBT was made to Mr Ravanelli.

"Mr Ravanelli’s case is that, as a matter of tax law, NICs were never due. As a result, no amount for NICs should have been deducted from the distribution out of the EBT."

But HMRC barrister, Adam Tolley KC, said Mr Ravanelli's case that no NICs were payable because Derby County Ltd had been dissolved was "wrong as a matter of tax law" and a misunderstanding of the NICs legislation.

"When the trustee made the distribution, for the purposes of the relevant NICs legislation it did so as the deemed employer of the claimant and he received the distribution as the deemed employee of the trustee," he argued.

"As such, the trustee was liable to pay the NICs."

Giving judgment, Mr Justice Eardley said he preferred Mr Ravanelli's lawyers' analysis, with the result that HMRC's decision not to reopen the settlement agreement was "vitiated by a material error of law."

However, he said it would be "jumping the gun" to say that HMRC should now reopen the settlement or to hand Mr Ravanelli the money, as opposed to reconsidering his case based on a correct understanding of the law.

"I shall quash the decision refusing to reopen the settlement agreement and HMRC will need to remake that decision, this time without any reliance upon the erroneous view that, as a matter of substantive tax law, the trustee was liable to pay NICs," he said.

Ravanelli, who was not in court for the two-day hearing, works as a TV pundit and still plays in occasional legends' games, but missed the recent October 2026 Middlesbrough 150th anniversary game, which featured fellow Prem legend, Juninho, due to a leg injury.

He joined Derby County on a free transfer in 2001, but was reputedly one of the club’s highest-earners at a time when Derby were foundering financially - and was also relegated from the Premiership during his stint there.

He moved on to Scottish club, Dundee, after leaving Derby, later playing for his home town club, Perugia, in his final years before retiring professionally in 2005.

He subsequently switched to coaching, going on to help manage the Juventus youth team before taking up the post of head coach at French side, Ajaccio.
