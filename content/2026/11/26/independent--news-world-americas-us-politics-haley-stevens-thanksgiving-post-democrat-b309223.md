Rep. Haley Stevens' Thanksgiving greeting reignited criticism of the outgoing Michigan Democrat.

Stevens, 43, has been shunned by many in her party since she endorsed Republican Senate candidate Mike Rogers on the eve of the midterms elections.

Her surprise move came after a falling out — largely over Israel —with Abdul El-Sayed, the progressive Democratic nominee and her former primary rival. El-Sayed won the general election, becoming the first Muslim elected to the Senate.

“From our family to yours — happy Thanksgiving,” she wrote in a Wednesday X post. “Stick a fork in the turkey — and stick it to ‘em.”

The post included a photo of Stevens and her fiance, Jason Turkish, a Democratic donor who also endorsed Rogers and had accused El-Sayed of using “antisemitic language.”

![Rep. Haley Stevens' Thanksgiving greeting reignited criticism of the outgoing Michigan Democrat](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/26/14/28/GettyImages-2289134645.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“Grateful to live in a country where a landscaper’s daughter can serve four terms in Congress, and a legally blind guy can go to law school and spend his life helping people,” Stevens wrote. “A country big enough for every voice — where we can disagree, respect one another, and still share the same table.”

The post drew more than 1 million views and more than 1,000 comments, many of them mocking Stevens, who was seen this week with Turkish at a 5-star resort in Hawaii.

“This Thanksgiving, I’m thankful that you will not be in office come January,” one user wrote. Another replied: “Grateful to live in a country [where] you will get absolutely clowned on, forever.”

Many commenters likened Stevens' political future to a cooked bird.

“Stick a fork in your political career cause it’s done,” one wrote. Another added: “Imagine slaughtering your own political career worse than a turkey.”

Others mocked the post's design, saying it resembled an “in memoriam” message. “Why does it look like she used an obituary template?” one commenter wrote.

Some urged the outgoing Democrat to log off: “You can leave social media btw, no one is asking for you to be here anymore.”

Not everyone piled on. Some commenters welcomed the post and wished the 43-year-old congresswoman a happy Thanksgiving.

Since she endorsed Rogers, a decision that came after she said she no longer felt welcomed by El-Sayed’s campaign, some Democratic members of Congress have urged the party to distance itself from her.

“I hope she doesn't return to work on Tuesday,” California Rep. Lateefah Simon told *Axios* after the endorsement. Washington Rep. Emily Randall said she would support removing Stevens from the House Democratic caucus.

Stevens is leaving Congress when her term ends in January.
