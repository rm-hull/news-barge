**At 22, Halle has waived her right to anonymity to speak openly about the lasting impact of a sexual assault that occurred while she was unconscious and vulnerable during her early military career.**

Halle, a 22-year-old woman from Harrogate, has courageously chosen to share her experience of sexual assault in the military to help others who may be suffering in silence. The assault occurred when she was 19, shortly after beginning her career in the Army, and involved a colleague she had considered a friend. The attack took place while she was asleep and unconscious, leaving her unable to consent or defend herself.

The case resulted in a conviction in June 2026, with the defendant found guilty of assault by penetration and sentenced to three years and six months in prison, alongside a lifetime restraining order and registration as a sex offender. However, he was acquitted of rape.

Halle attended court to give evidence and read her Victim Personal Statement directly to the defendant, describing the profound and lasting effects the assault has had on her life. She recounted how the assault shattered her sense of safety and trust, particularly within the military environment where trust among colleagues is fundamental.

> **Halle said:**
>
> Over the past years, I have had to live with the consequences of what the defendant did to me. What makes this even harder to process is that this was someone I considered a friend, and the assault happened while I was asleep and completely vulnerable.
>
> I had no awareness of what was happening, no ability to consent, and no ability to protect myself or stop what was being done to me.
>
> The fact that I was unconscious and unable to defend myself has had a lasting impact on me and has affected the way I view my own safety and trust in others.

Halle described how her confidence and outlook changed completely after the assault, revealing that she now struggles to trust male friends and colleagues and experiences anxiety and suspicion during social interactions. The trauma has also significantly impacted her mental health, causing panic attacks and disturbed sleep, areas where she has sought therapy to aid her recovery.

> **Halle continues:**
>
> Sleep, which should be a place of rest and safety, became something I feared. I have undergone numerous forms of therapy in an attempt to process what happened and rebuild my life.
>
> The assault affected not only my personal life but also the career I had worked hard to build.
>
> My relationships have also suffered. I have struggled with trust, intimacy, and forming close relationships with others. The person I was before that night is not the same person I have been since.

Addressing the court, Halle expressed hope that the profound and lasting impact of the assault on her life was understood, demonstrating her desire to see justice and awareness raised.

North Yorkshire Police is supporting Halle’s decision to waive her anonymity as part of the 16 Days of Activism Against Gender-Based Violence campaign, highlighting the importance of victims’ voices in encouraging others to seek help.

> **Assistant Chief Constable Catherine Clarke said:**
>
> North Yorkshire Police is committed to working towards the Government’s pledge to halve violence against women and girls within a decade. This strategy builds upon our strong safeguarding foundations, operational excellence, multi-agency partnerships, and local innovation.
>
> While North Yorkshire remains one of the safest areas of the country, we remain committed to tackling VAWG-related crime and improving our service to victims.
>
> Thank you to Halle for her courage in sharing her story, helping to raise awareness of the devastating impact of sexual violence and encouraging others to find the strength to come forward and speak out.
