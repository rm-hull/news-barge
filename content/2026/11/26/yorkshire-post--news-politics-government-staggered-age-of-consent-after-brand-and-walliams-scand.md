# Government ‘actively looking at’ staggered age of consent after Brand and Walliams scandals

## Andy Burnham has confirmed the Government is “actively looking” at introducing a staggered age of consent, after the Russell Brand and David Walliams scandals.

This is part of a wider package of measures from the Prime Minister, which includes making it harder to silence victims through non-disclosure agreements (NDAs).

He also told the House of Commons that the Government is inviting survivors, parents and young people to take part in a “national conversation” on rape myths.

Brand’s conviction and imprisonment for rape and sexual assault and allegations against Mr Walliams have promoted conversations about power imbalances and consent.

This comes as TV star Georgia Harrison, a campaigner on violence against women and girls after being a victim of revenge porn, visited Parliament to urge MPs to support a Bill to extend compulsory relationships and sex education (RSE) up to the age of 18.

Labour MP Alistair Strathern is introducing this via a private member’s bill next week.

Relationships education is compulsory in primary schools in England and RSE is mandatory in secondary schools, but sixth form and FE colleges are not required to teach RSE.

Love Island star Ms Harrison said: “With the recent headlines raising serious questions about consent, power imbalances and abuse, it’s clear just how important these conversations are.

“Yet we’re still expecting young people to navigate boundaries, pornography and unhealthy relationships without always giving them the knowledge to do so.

“Having seen first-hand the devastating impact that abuse can have, I know how important it is that young people understand what healthy relationships should look like and how to recognise when something isn’t right.”

Walliams has been accused of sexual misconduct, including grooming teenage girls as young as 15 and abusing his position to pursue women, but he denies any wrongdoing.

Ms Harrison added: “Questions about sex, consent and relationships don’t stop at 16. So why should the lessons?”

Asked about recent high-profile cases in the press, Mr Strathern said: “My hope is that we can use the momentum of the last few weeks of horrific stories to help drive changes that we know will practically move the dial on setting up young people (for a) lifetime of success when it comes to healthy relationships and sex.”

This comes as the Government confirmed that it was “actively looking at” introducing the staggered age of consent.

This would limit 16 and 17-year-olds from having sex with people over an age from 21 to 25.

In Florida, for example, 16 and 17-year-olds can only consent to having sex with someone up to the age of 23.

Writing in The Guardian, Mr Burnham said: “This is a complex issue with different views, so we are inviting parents, young people, victim support services and the wider public to come forward to share their experiences and inform the process.”

Women and Equalities Minister Bridget Phillipson told Sky News: “What we want to do is to have a conversation about how we can look at that staggered age of consent for 16 and 17-year-olds in relationships with much older men.

![Bridget Phillipson](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/jpim-static/image/2026/11/12/00/cs-4a24a331-7385-43e5-9c7a-4ad10c95d34d.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


“There’s a question as to where you would draw the line, and that’s what we want to talk about.

“But also, how we can make sure that those in positions of power and trust aren’t able to exploit those positions, as we’ve seen in recent times, where men that seek to abuse women will often put themselves in powerful positions, whether that’s in the media, in business, in politics, in the church, and how we how we tackle that, and part of that is about the legal change that Government can bring, but it’s also about the cultural change.”

Ms Phillipson also suggested that young women should not be left alone with an adult at work.

“It cannot be acceptable, for example, to have young women in a closed room with an adult without supervision in a workplace environment,” she told Sky News.

“That is just completely counter to any kind of safeguarding or wellbeing practice you would expect.”

She pushed back at criticism she was “infantilising” people from former prime minister Liz Truss by claiming she was talking about children.

The Prime Minister’s official spokesman declined to say whether these comments were Government policy.

He said: “On the general measures, as both the minister and Prime Minister said, victims have suffered in silence, feeling like they cannot speak out because their perpetrator is too powerful.

“That must end. We must draw a line in the sand, he said, that powerful men are immune to facing justice because they think they can silence their victims.

“This is not only about making victims able to speak out about their own abuse, but also being able to challenge the system - and that is why we will also be looking to stagger the age of consent.”
