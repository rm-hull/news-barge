# Bank lending to smaller Yorkshire firms lifts in second half of the year

## Bank lending to smaller businesses in Yorkshire and the Humber increased between H2 2025 and H1 2026, according to a newly released tracker, with 10,240 loans and overdrafts approved or increased by the main high street banks.

The figure showed an 18 per cent increase on the previous year, with the value of these facilities reaching £1.4bn, an eight per cent increase, according to the British Business Bank’s Nations and Regions Tracker 2026.

A lower proportion of smaller businesses in Yorkshire and the Humber are using external finance than the UK overall figures, despite growth in bank lending and stable equity investment.

Across the region, 47 per cent of smaller businesses used external finance in the same period, below the UK average of 51 per cent and joint lowest nationally. A third also said they would be willing to use finance in the future to help their business grow, in line with the overall UK figures of 33 per cent.

Over the last decade, the number and value of equity deals has increased in Yorkshire and Humber has increased by 36 per cent and 88 per cent respectively. There has also been four times increase since 2017 in the number of unique venture capital investors per 100 high growth businesses in areas outside of London.

Cat Smith, director, Yorkshire and the Humber, Local Growth Team at the British Business Bank, said: “Yorkshire and the Humber has a strong and diverse smaller business community, with businesses across the region making an important contribution to the UK economy.

"Growing levels of bank lending and stable equity investment demonstrate the ambition of smaller businesses to invest in their future, while the region's strengths in areas such as digital technologies and advanced manufacturing provide a strong foundation for further growth.”

Equity investment has fared well in the latest 12-month period, with the region attracting £152m in announced smaller business equity investment between H2 2025 and H1 2026, representing one per cent of the UK total and broadly in line with the previous year.

The number of deals rose by 13 per cent to 83, accounting for five per cent of the UK total.

Based on the annual three-year average, activity was concentrated in West Yorkshire Combined Authority and South Yorkshire Mayoral Combined Authority areas, which together accounted for almost 80 per cent of the region’s deals.

However, raising successive rounds of capital is proving challenging for smaller Northern scale-ups. Among businesses across the North that raised their first venture capital round between 2016 and 2019, 59 per cent went on to raise a second round, compared with 71 per cent across the UK.

Across the Government’s Industrial Strategy [1]sectors, Digital and Technologies businesses accounted for the highest number of announced equity deals in Yorkshire and the Humber, with 36.

This was followed by Professional and Business Services with 22 and Life Sciences and Advanced Manufacturing with 15 each[2].

During 2025/26, the British Business Bank newly supported 2,700 businesses in Yorkshire and the Humber.
