Imagine finding a great Black Friday deal for that gadget you needed for ages, placing an order, getting the delivery tracking number, and then never receiving your parcel. This is what has already happened to thousands of shoppers worldwide, according to the latest NordVPN findings.

After analyzing victims' complaints found online, NordVPN’s Threat Intelligence research team was able to uncover a major delivery scam network that counts 54 fake courier companies, mostly run from China.

How the malicious delivery scheme works is simple. Once people place an order on a fake shop, one of the fake courier brands sends them a delivery tracking number. This makes the shops look like legitimate retailers so that, by the time shoppers realize their order isn't arriving, the online retailer stops replying, and they can’t get a refund.

## How the fake delivery factory works

The goal of this fake delivery factory, NordVPN explains, is to back up fake shops by providing the unfortunate shoppers with tracking numbers and fake parcel journeys so they can look like legitimate retailers.

This improves their chances of cashing in the payments from the card networks. It also helps scammers to win potential disputes over falsely 'delivered' goods.

"Tracking numbers are something we’ve all learned to rely on, and almost always they’re exactly what they seem," says Marijus Briedis, Chief Technology Officer at NordVPN. "This scam takes advantage of that trust. The shop pays for a delivery record for a parcel that was never sent. When nothing arrives, the shop stops replying, and the courier never existed."

NordVPN's researchers managed to find the main platform behind the scheme, which runs on a single server in Hong Kong. The same server was also linked to at least five of the 54 fake courier brands.

## How to stay safe

As shoppers are ready to get into the Black Friday frenzy over the weekend, Briedis shares some recommendations to avoid falling victim to similar fake shops and delivery scams:

* **Be careful with big discounts on social media.** Scam shops copy the names and look of well-known retailers to flood social media feeds with malicious ads. You should always check the brand’s official site directly instead of clicking the ad.
* **Check which courier the shop uses before you buy.** Trustworthy shops usually ship with well-known carriers and say so. Watch out for red flags like unfamiliar courier names, brand-new websites, missing physical addresses, and free email domains.
* **Pay with a method that offers buyer protection.** Credit cards and payment services with dispute options give you the best chance of getting your money back.
* **If the parcel is marked 'Delivered' but never arrived, act quickly.** Check with your local delivery service and open a dispute with your bank or payment provider as soon as it looks fishy. Include screenshots of the tracking history and the shop’s website.
