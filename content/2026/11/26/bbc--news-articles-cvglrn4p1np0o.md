**Andy Burnham was joined by former prime ministers at a memorial service for the late politician Ann Widdecombe.**

The Service of Remembrance at Westminster Cathedral was held on Thursday afternoon, with eulogies delivered and a choir performance.

Hundreds of guests attended, with David Cameron, Liz Truss, Boris Johnson and Rishi Sunak among them.

Bishop James Curry, the Auxiliary Bishop of Westminster, led the service after the Archbishop of Westminster, Richard Moth, was taken to hospital earlier.

Reform UK's chairman Lee Anderson, deputy leader Richard Tice and leader Nigel Farage were among those at the service

Moth, who is the head of the Catholic Church in England and Wales, underwent tests after he "felt unwell", Westminster Diocese said.

Reform UK leader Nigel Farage, former Conservative MP Philip Hammond, and Speaker of the House of Commons Sir Lindsay Hoyle also attended.

The Prime Minister was later seen leaving the cathedral with former prime ministers Rishi Sunak and Boris Johnson.

Widdecombe, a former Conservative minister and Reform UK's justice spokeswoman, was found dead at her home in Haytor on Dartmoor, Devon, on 9 July, aged 78.

Joshua Kerry, 28, of Byrley Road, Rotherham, South Yorkshire, was charged with her murder.

A trial before a High Court judge is scheduled for 8 June, and a plea and case management hearing on 29 January.

Widdecombe's family announced plans for a public memorial service following her death.

Widdecombe served as Conservative MP for Maidstone and the Weald in Kent for 23 years and was appointed to ministerial roles in Sir John Major's government in the 1990s.
