Fifa confirmed that hackers gained access to a “limited set of personal and business information” in a cyber-attack, amid heightened concern over digital vulnerabilities of leading organizations and institutions.

The global soccer governing body said it “condemns in the strongest possible terms the unlawful acquisition and publication of information, particularly personal data”, in a statement to the Associated Press on Thursday. “Such actions constitute a serious and unacceptable violation of privacy and can have significant consequences for the individuals concerned.”

Last week, a group calling itself the Handsome Boy Hacking Club posted a screenshot on X of what it claimed were Fifa files. The post has since then been removed after the account was suspended from X.

The Fifa cyber-attack is the latest high-profile digital disruption to hit a major organization in recent weeks.

Microsoft experienced a widespread outage earlier this month that caused disruption to millions of users, organizations and businesses across the globe. The company said the outage was a “technical issue” not related to cyber-attacks or AI agents.

The following day, the US experienced an unprecedented nationwide disruption to air travel that prompted a “ground stop” order to be issued by the US Federal ⁠Aviation Administration, which cited “communication issues” impacting air traffic control centers.

Markwayne Mullin, the homeland security secretary, later said in a news conference that preliminary investigations into the stoppage did not indicate that it was tied to a “nefarious attack”.

Three days later, customers reported service outages and difficulty accessing their online accounts at several major US banks and the money transfer service Zelle after a technical issue at JPMorgan Chase caused ripple effects throughout the financial system.

Such disruptions have intensified fear and concerns about the advancement of artificial intelligence, and the potential risks, even though there is no direct evidence that AI directly sparked the issues.

ESPN reported that unnamed sources said the Fifa hack accounted for less than 0.1% of data held by the organization, an that the breach was not a result of systems or technology failure on Fifa’s part, with hackers said to hav**e** gained access by impersonating staff.

As of now, it has not been shared when the cyber-attack took place, or the precise nature of the hacked information.

“FIFA is working closely with the relevant authorities, including law enforcement,” the organization said its statement sent to the Associated Press.

The cyber-attack comes as Gianni Infantino, Fifa’s president, is under scrutiny for a proposal to sell chunks of the World Cup to private investors.

Infantino admitted the project “has created divisions of a nature that are no longer in the interest of the objective”, amid widespread anger globally.

The plan has caused tensions within football around the world, leading Uefa to threaten a boycott of future Fifa tournaments if it was not shelved.

Uefa and other confederations have stepped up their search for candidates to run against Infantino in next year’s Fifa election, in which he had been expected to comfortably win a fourth term.
