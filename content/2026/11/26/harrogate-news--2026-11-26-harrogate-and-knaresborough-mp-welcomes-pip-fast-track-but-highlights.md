**Tom Gordon MP praises Government’s decision to fast-track Personal Independence Payment for people with severe conditions but warns of months of uncertainty amid conflicting reviews and calls for clearer government strategy.**

Tom Gordon, Liberal Democrat MP for Harrogate and Knaresborough, has welcomed the Government’s decision to fast-track Personal Independence Payment (PIP) support for individuals with the most serious and well-evidenced health conditions. Announced on 24 November 2026, the move aims to remove the need for a full assessment for this group and to end repeated award reviews for people with lifelong, progressive, or unlikely-to-improve conditions.

The changes follow the release of the final report from the Government’s review of PIP, led by Disability Minister Sir Stephen Timms, which recognised that the existing system is “no longer fit for purpose.” This review was co-produced with disabled people and organisations, gathering input from over 40,000 respondents.

However, Tom Gordon has expressed concerns that disabled people and carers in Harrogate, Knaresborough, and the wider North Yorkshire area face “months of uncertainty” due to conflicting recommendations from this review and a separate assessment by Alan Milburn focusing on young people and work.

The Milburn review suggests reducing disability support for 18- to 24-year-olds by £3.6 billion. This contrasts with Timms’ recommendations and has prompted concerns from the Liberal Democrats, who highlight the risk of worsening poverty for young disabled people. The Joseph Rowntree Foundation has reported that young people are already disproportionately affected by destitution.

> **Tom Gordon said:**
>
> Ending repeated, stressful reassessments for people whose conditions will never improve, and fast-tracking those with the most serious illnesses, is the right thing to do. Credit for that belongs to the disabled people who helped write this review.
>
> But disabled young people now face months of uncertainty, with Stephen Timms recommending one thing and Alan Milburn another. The Government must not waste time in setting out its approach.
>
> Families in Harrogate and Knaresborough who rely on PIP, and the carers for whom PIP is the gateway to Carer’s Allowance, deserve to know where they stand. Last year the Government’s own figures showed its original PIP plans would have pushed 250,000 people, including 50,000 children, into poverty. We cannot let that happen again by the back door.

The MP has called on the Government to publish a full poverty and destitution impact assessment of the Timms recommendations, including effects on Motability, Blue Badges, and Carer’s Allowance, prior to implementation. He emphasised the importance of involving disabled people in co-producing the assessment process and ensuring parliamentary scrutiny of forthcoming White Paper proposals.

Gordon also urged the Government to introduce an earnings taper to remove the “cliff-edge” punishment for carers increasing their paid work hours, and to expand on Charlie Mayfield’s review on supporting disabled and ill people into employment rather than relying principally on benefit cuts.

> **He further commented:**
>
> We need to manage the welfare bill after it soared thanks to the Conservative Party’s economic chaos. But the way to do that is to tackle the root causes: grow the economy to create jobs, bring down the cost of living, and fix the NHS and social care so health problems are treated early.
>
> That means guaranteeing everyone can see a GP within seven days, setting up mental health hubs for young people in every community, and giving disabled people who can work the support they need to do so.

Currently, about four million people claim PIP in England and Wales, with a cost estimated at £32 billion this financial year and projected to rise to £41.5 billion in 2030–31. PIP also serves as the gateway to other support schemes such as Carer’s Allowance, Motability, and Blue Badges.

The Liberal Democrat concerns reflect a wider debate about the adequacy of support for disabled people and carers amid rising costs and economic pressures. Harrogate and Knaresborough residents affected by PIP will be watching Government decisions closely in the months ahead.
