The Managing Director of York-based train operator LNER has reacted to the guilty verdict in the trial of a man who carried out a knife attack on one of its trains.

David Horne said: “We hope it helps bring some closure for all those involved in this horrific incident.”

He praised everyone caught up in the incident, including the member of train staff who was badly injured after trying to stop the attacker using a frying pan.

“Just over a year on” said Mr Horne: “Our thoughts very much remain with everyone affected by the shocking events of Saturday 1st November 2025.”

Anthony Williams, 33, has been found guilty of attempting to murder 14 people, including 10 on board the East Coast Main Line express to London.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/train-railway-stabbing-incident-lner-huntingdon-2-pa.webp)

Passengers and staff fought him off with anything they could get their hands on, including a toaster.

Some hid in toilets and bike rack areas before the driver was forced to make an unscheduled stop in Huntingdon, Cambridgeshire, where Williams was arrested.

CCTV on the train captured what happened (You can see that in the X post below from British Transport Police)

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/418259c1-9844-42ea-ba97-29db9c9a0d27.jpg)

As well as the train attacks, Williams, of Langford Road, Peterborough, was found guilty of attempting to murder four other people in separate incidents and will be sentenced at Cambridge Crown Court on 4 and 5 February.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/anthony-williams.png)

**LNER MD David Horne added:** “I would like to once again recognise the bravery and swift response shown by our colleagues, customers and the emergency services that night.

“The remarkable courage of our onboard crew who supported customers as the incident was ongoing, particularly Samir, who chose to step forward and protect those around him.

“Also, the quick and clear thinking shown by our driver, Andrew, and Network Rail signaller, Rob, which enabled the train to be brought swiftly into Huntingdon and the station colleagues who guided customers to safety as they left the train.

“I’d also like to thank British Transport Police for their diligent investigation and their determination to ensure justice was delivered.

“We are incredibly proud of the way everyone came together to support each other and the care, compassion and unity that has resulted from such harrowing circumstances.”

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/Samir_Zitouni__LNER_Customer_Experience_Host.webp)

CCTV footage played to the jury showed LNER staff member Samir Zitouni, a Customer Experience Host, use a frying pan to fend off Williams. His lungs were punctured and he needed four blood transfusions, chest drains and the assistance of a ventilator.

He said: “I want to express my deepest gratitude to the police, the NHS – particularly to Addenbrooke’s A&E doctors – and to the Ambulance crews on site last year.

“I appreciate everyone’s thoughts and prayers, including from my LNER colleagues and other railway workers, during a difficult time. Thank you.”

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/Andrew_Johnson__LNER_Train_Driver.webp)

**Andrew Johnson,** was driving the train and made a vital call to the signalling centre so it could be switched from the tracks for fast services to a platform line at Huntingdon. He knew that was the safest place for an evacuation.

He said: “Over the past year, I have been supported by my family, friends, colleagues and especially by my partner Abbie, and I am extremely grateful for their care and kindness throughout. I’m also grateful to everyone at LNER for their continued support.

“I’d like to thank those who were involved in the response on the night itself, particularly Rob Pape, the Network Rail signaller, who worked with me to get the train swiftly into Huntingdon.

“My thanks also go to the police and the medical teams who attended so quickly and provided vital care to those who had been injured and those that have supported since, including those involved in the investigation.

“As I said at the time, the heroes of the night were my colleagues onboard, particularly Samir whose actions were incredible.

“My thoughts remain with everyone who was injured and affected by this incident. I hope the conclusion of this case brings some measure of closure and allows us all to look ahead and move forward with our lives.”

**The story of what happened that night**

During trial, the court heard that on 31 October 2025, shortly before 7pm, Williams stabbed three people within minutes of each other at Henry Penn Walk in Peterborough – including a 14-year-old boy who was walking with his friend.

He then made a 10-minute walk to Ritzy Barbers where footage shows him walking in and shouting, brandishing a knife.

He later travelled to Stevenage, where he stole a pack of kitchen knives from an ASDA supermarket.

During the early hours of Saturday 1 November 2025, Williams used one of those knives to attack a 17-year-old boy on board a DLR train at Pontoon Dock station in east London, stabbing him in the face and hand. BTP officers attended the station within 10 minutes, but Williams had already left the scene.

Detectives launched an urgent investigation to identify and arrest the suspect, including a vast CCTV trawl and forensic enquiries.

Later that day they identified Williams as the suspect and attempted to arrest him at a recent address for him in north London at 6.48pm on Saturday 1 November.

Williams had been at an address in Peterborough, which he left at 6.15pm. At 7.26pm, he had boarded an LNER service travelling from Peterborough to London King’s Cross.

Eleven minutes after departing Peterborough, he produced a kitchen knife and began attacking passengers as he moved through the train.

Passengers fled to the front train carriage and tried to protect themselves, fighting Williams off with anything they could find, including frying pans and a toaster.

During the five-minute attack, ten people were stabbed by Williams.

The driver – who had nearly 20 years’ experience as a train driver – requested permission to divert the service and worked closely with the Network Rail signaller to stop the train at Huntingdon to allow police to get onto the train as soon as possible.

The first 999 calls to police were received at 7.40pm and armed police officers from Cambridgeshire Constabulary attended the station immediately to locate and detain Williams in the station’s car park within minutes.

Williams has been remanded in custody and will be sentenced at Cambridge Crown Court on 4 and 5 February.
