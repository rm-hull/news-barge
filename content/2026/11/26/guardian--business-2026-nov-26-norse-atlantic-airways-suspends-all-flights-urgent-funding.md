Norse Atlantic Airways has suspended all flights and said it will file for bankruptcy if it cannot secure short-term financing urgently.

The Norwegian airline, which operates long-haul flights around the world including from London Gatwick and the US to Europe and Asia, announced it was on the brink on Thursday morning, a day after warning that its finances were “critical”.

The airline, like many worldwide, is reeling from surging jet fuel prices that have sent costs skywards since the US-Israel war on Iran began in February, as well as hitting consumer confidence.

In a statement to the Oslo stock exchange, Norse said: “If a short-term financing solution is not secured urgently, it is the board’s ​intention to file for bankruptcy.”

It said it was still looking at options to secure short-term financing and was in discussion with investors regarding a possible takeover but said: “There can be no assurance that a viable solution will ​be found.”

Norse was founded in 2021 as it sought to step into the space vacated by Norwegian Air, whose attempts to fly budget long-haul were also abandoned as unviable.

Despite that, Norse revived the transatlantic routes, with a fleet of 12 Boeing 787 Dreamliners and ​many former Norwegian staff.

Norse said in the summer it was exploring a potential sale or merger, and set out on a cost-cutting programme called Project Falcon to save $50m (£38m), including axing about 75 jobs. It also moved its headquarters out of Norway to cheaper offices in Latvia, while the founder and chief executive, Bjørn Tore Larsen, left the airline.

It said it “sincerely apologises to its passengers for the inconvenience caused by this situation”.
