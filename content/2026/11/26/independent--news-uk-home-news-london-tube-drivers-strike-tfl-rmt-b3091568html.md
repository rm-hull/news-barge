Travel across the capital will be disrupted on Thursday as London Underground drivers start the first of a series of 24-hour strikes.

Members of the Rail, Maritime and Transport union (RMT) will walk out at midday following unsuccessful talks over a four-day working week.

Transport for London (TfL) advised passengers to check before travelling, with disruption expected prior to, during, and following the action.

Services are expected to run normally until around 10am on Thursday before Tube operations begin to scale back.

TfL stated that most routes will operate from midday, though all lines will face disruption.

There will be no service on the Circle or Piccadilly lines all day, nor on the Waterloo & City line after midday.

On Friday, operations will begin later than usual, with network-wide disruption until about noon.

Ongoing delays will continue until full service resumes around 6pm.

TfL said it had revised proposals for a voluntary four-day week for train drivers, with an option to remain on a five-day week with a guarantee of remaining at the same depot now part of the proposal.

![Ongoing delays will continue until full service resumes around 6pm on Friday](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/06/04/11/2278984758..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Aslef has backed the proposals and its members will be working normally.

Nick Dent, director of customer operations for London Underground, stressed that the proposals were voluntary and denied union claims that drivers were being asked to spend more time driving trains, increasing levels of fatigue.

He told the Press Association: “We have made some significant changes to our proposals and have asked the RMT to put these to their members.

“The proposals will lead to a more reliable service and will improve work/life balance.”

RMT general secretary Eddie Dempsey said: “This is not about improving work-life balance.

“It is about squeezing more work out of fewer drivers while giving management greater flexibility at the expense of our members.”

More 24-hour strikes are planned on December 17 and 19, January 4 and 6 and February 17, 23 and 25.

Ruth Duston, chief executive of London Heritage Quarter, which represents 7,000 businesses in London, including in Victoria, Westminster and Whitehall, said: “Businesses across the capital rely on the Tube to bring in hundreds of thousands of staff, customers and visitors every single day.

“This disruption will be hugely damaging for them during such an important trading period, and negotiations must resume to limit the economic impact on businesses across the capital.”
