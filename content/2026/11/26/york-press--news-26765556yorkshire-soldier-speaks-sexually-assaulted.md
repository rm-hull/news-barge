A North Yorkshire soldier has said she “felt betrayed” after being sexually assaulted by a colleague.

Halle was 19 when she was sexually assaulted after a night out by someone she considered to be a friend as she slept.

Halle, who has waived her legal right to anonymity, said she “never imagined that someone I trusted would take advantage of me while I was asleep and unable to protect myself”.

“I felt betrayed by someone who was supposed to be looking out for me,” said Halle, who is now 22 and did not want her surname to be used.

The defendant was jailed for three and a half years after being convicted of assault by penetration in June. He was also made subject to a lifetime restraining order and placed on the sex offender register.

The jury found the defendant not guilty of rape.

Halle said she decided to speak out to encourage other survivors of sexual violence to come forward and report it.

She chose to attend court in person to give evidence and read her victim personal statement directly to the defendant.

In the statement, she described the profound and lasting impact the assault has had on her life.

“I never imagined that someone I trusted would take advantage of me while I was asleep and unable to protect myself,” Halle said. “I felt betrayed by someone who was supposed to be looking out for me.

“Since the assault, I have struggled to trust male friends and male colleagues. I find myself constantly questioning people's intentions and expecting the worst, even when they have done nothing wrong.

“When male colleagues or friends speak to me, I often become anxious and suspicious, and I find it difficult not to assume that they may have ulterior motives. This is not how I was before the assault.”

Halle also said the assault had a “significant” impact on her mental health, resulting in panic attacks and sleepless nights.

Halle said she had undergone several forms of therapy but “recovery has not been straightforward”.

She added that the assault also “took away my self-respect and changed the way I viewed myself. For a long time, I felt like I had no control over my own body.

“Something deeply personal was taken from me without my knowledge or consent, and I was left to deal with the consequences.”

## Police chief praises Halle's bravery for speaking out

North Yorkshire Police’s assistant chief constable, Catherine Clarke, praised Halle for speaking out which she said would help “raise awareness of the devastating impact of sexual violence and encouraging others to find the strength to come forward and speak out”.

Ms Clarke said the police force is “committed to working towards the government's pledge to halve violence against women and girls within a decade”.

“We recognise that no single agency can tackle this issue alone and we will continue to invest in our safeguarding arrangements with key partners across North Yorkshire and the City of York,” she said.

Ms Clarke added: “In North Yorkshire Police we are very much aware that men and boys can be victims too.

“All victims can be confident that they will receive the same high quality standards we are committed to delivering for all victims.”
