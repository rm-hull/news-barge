Nearly three in five parents worry about meeting their young child’s needs due to impossible expectations, pressure to be perfect, or feeling judged, a survey has found.

More than three in four (76%) feel social media has increased anxiety around parenting, according to a report by early years charity Kindred Squared.

A survey, of more than 3,500 parents of children aged five and under in the UK, suggests that 70% believe that parenting decisions are more complicated today than in previous generations.

It found that 59% worry they are not able to respond effectively to their child’s needs because of impossible expectations, the pressure to be a “perfect parent”, or feeling judged by others.

The survey, carried out by Savanta between July and August, suggests that nearly half (48%) of parents believe it is harder to trust professional advice than it was for previous generations.

Parents who were surveyed said their own instincts were the biggest influence on their parenting decisions (39%) ahead of advice from professionals (23%).

Around one in five said they use AI tools such as ChatGPT (21%) and online parenting forums (20%) for parenting advice.

But only 12% named AI and 10% named forums as trusted sources.

One parent from London said: “I was sort of feeling this overwhelming pressure to be perfect. Like, you have to be perfect in everything.

“And I think social media influencers really get into your head and make you think that it’s possible to be that way, but in reality, it’s really not.”

Another parent from the West Midlands added: “Social media has an awful lot to answer for, full stop.

“It increases anxiety and turns even the most basic situation into a toxic bin fire.

“So it makes sense that something as tough as parenting is going to be affected.”

Felicity Gillespie, chief executive of Kindred Squared, said the isolation reported by parents, as well as feelings of being under pressure and a lack of trust in official advice, were “striking”.

She said: “I think this report clearly sets out that, for many parents, they feel that the task of raising children is tougher today than in previous generations.

“I hope these findings will inform policymakers at all levels who have a role in supporting parents and families because it’s quite clear that families with the youngest children need trustworthy, easy-to-access advice, the space to be the parents they want to be and, above all, we need to remove that overriding fear of being judged or being all alone.”
