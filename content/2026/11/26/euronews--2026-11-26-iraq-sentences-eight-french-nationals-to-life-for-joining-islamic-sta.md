## Two other French citizens have been sentenced to 15 years in prison, while the verdict has been postponed for 10 additional French nationals by the court.

Eight French nationals who joined Islamic State (IS) have been sentenced to life imprisonment, while two others have received 15-year prison terms. The Iraqi judiciary has postponed the announcement of the verdict for ten other French citizens.

Romain Garnier, who worked on IS radio operations, is among the French nationals sentenced to life.

Adrien Guihal, also known as Abou Oussama al-Faransi, is one of the defendants whose sentence will be announced at a later date. This man in his forties, originally from the Paris region (Seine-Saint-Denis), lent his voice to the audio recording of the Nice attack in which 86 people were killed on 14 July, 2016.

The same applies to Thomas Collange, whose wife is serving a ten-year prison sentence in France, and to Quentin Le Brun, who appeared in an IS propaganda video; for both of them the Iraqi judiciary has postponed the announcement of the verdict.

During the trial, most of the defendants pleaded guilty and expressed remorse. Some had asked to be repatriated to France.

France has repatriated hundreds of relatives of jihadists, mainly women and children held in Syrian camps.

The sentencing of the ten French nationals comes as Iraq continues to prosecute thousands of people suspected of having joined IS.

The Islamist group seized vast territories in Iraq and Syria in 2014 and was responsible for massacres, also forcing women and young girls into sexual slavery.

Many European citizens travelled to take part in IS's "jihad".

Last year, 47 French nationals were transferred from Syria to Iraq to be tried there.

In that group, 15 French nationals had already been sentenced to life imprisonment and another to 15 years in detention.
