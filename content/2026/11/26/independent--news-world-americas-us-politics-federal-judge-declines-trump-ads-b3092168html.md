A long-term ban on the Trump administration's taxpayer-funded political advertisements will not come from a New York courtroom, after a federal judge declined on Friday to extend restrictions on the spots.

U.S. District Judge Jennifer L. Rochon in New York found that the coalition suing over the ads, which includes the government watchdog group Common Cause, had not convincingly shown the advertisements were still causing them “imminent and irreparable harm" with November's midterm elections now over.

“The urgency of the earlier requests for temporary injunctive relief in this case centered around the November 3, 2026, midterm and state elections,” Rochon wrote in a court filing. “Those elections have now passed.”

The ruling leaves the ads' future in the hands of a different judge, who is overseeing a parallel case in Washington, D.C.

The outcome is an incremental victory for the Trump administration. In the Washington case, the Democratic National Committee has asked the court for a preliminary injunction to stop the administration from developing, distributing, or airing the advertisements.

A hearing on that request was held earlier this week, and the judge has not yet ruled. The TV spots at the center of Friday's ruling began airing in September, paid for at least in part through a $20 million Department of Homeland Security contract.

Some of the advertisements glorified the president with highlight reels of scenic speeches and callouts about tax cuts and support for law enforcement. One repurposed a clip used by Trump's 2024 presidential campaign, showing him walking down a hallway as his voice warns of a “final battle” against “globalists” and “warmongers.”

Some of the ads also ran on Trump administration social media channels and the White House website. The plaintiffs had argued the spots were illegal government-sponsored propaganda intended to promote the president's campaign message just weeks before Election Day.

The White House has defended the advertisements as public service announcements. Judges in both cases issued temporary restraining orders blocking some of the ads in the lead-up to the election. The temporary restraining order in the Democrats' case expires next week.

Bradley Girard, senior counsel at Democracy Forward, which represented the plaintiffs in the New York case, said while the team is disappointed, “we will continue to pursue this case on the merits to prevent this from happening in the future.”

The White House did not immediately respond to a request for comment.
