A “marauding” knifeman has been found guilty of the attempted murders of 14 people, including 10 whom he stabbed on a London-bound train that made an unscheduled stop in Huntingdon.

Anthony Williams, 33, stabbed a boy and attempted to stab two men in Peterborough on the evening of October 31 last year, before travelling to London by train, Cambridge Crown Court heard.

Once in the capital, he stabbed a boy on a DLR train in the early hours of November 1, before returning to Peterborough and spending much of the day at home.

On the evening of November 1, he walked from his home to Peterborough station, boarded a London-bound LNER service and stabbed nine passengers and a member of on-board staff, after the high-speed train had departed.

Teams at the Rail Operating Centre in York helped driver Andrew Johnson divert the train after the mass stabbing onboard allowing police to board.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21769340.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Anthony Williams who has been jailed *(Image: British Transport Police/Press Association)*

Passengers heard Williams talking about the devil, making comments like “the devil won’t win”, and he had a bottle of fortified tonic wine with him that he drank from.

The trial heard how on-board staff member Samir Zitouni tried to fend off Williams with a frying pan, and one passenger used a toaster, while others tried to block his way with trolleys and luggage.

Some sheltered in toilets, or in whatever safe areas they could find.

The driver stopped the train at Huntingdon with the help of a signaller, ahead of its next scheduled stop at Stevenage, and Williams was tasered and arrested in a car park at the station.

Jurors were told that Mr Zitouni was taken to intensive care in a life-threatening condition, having received stab wounds including three to the back.

He suffered “catastrophic blood loss” and required four blood transfusions.

The tip of the blade of Williams’s knife was close to the heart of passenger Sachin Balakrishnan at the time of the stabbing, jurors were told in agreed facts, and a wound to passenger Stephen Crean’s scalp was within 0.79cm of his brain.

Jurors were told Williams had asked Mr Crean: “Do you want to die?”

All survived their injuries.

Williams had admitted carrying out the attacks but denied that he intended to kill, and his trial was told that at the time he was suffering from an acute psychosis as a result of his schizophrenia.

But the defendant was found guilty of all 14 counts of attempted murder by a jury after 16 hours and 52 minutes of deliberations.

Williams, of Peterborough, was found guilty by majority verdicts of the attempted murders of a 14-year-old boy whom he stabbed, and of two men, Dawid Taborski, 26, and William Ogelby, 22, whom he tried to stab on the evening of October 31 in Peterborough.

He was also found guilty by a majority verdict of the attempted murder of Isaiah Idris, then 17 and now aged 18, whom he sliced on the face, on a DLR train that was approaching Pontoon Dock station in London in the early hours of November 1.

Williams was found guilty of the attempted murders of 10 people on the LNER train on November 1.

These were on-board staff member Mr Zitouni, 48, and nine passengers: Jonathan Gjoshe, 22, Sachin Balakrishnan, 29, David Presland, 73, Scott Bletcher, 50, Scott Green, 52, Kevin Deely, 58, Stephen Crean, 61, Raza Aslam, 44, and Michael Paffett, 34.

He was found guilty by majority verdicts of the attempted murders of Mr Deely, Mr Aslam and Mr Paffett.

He was found guilty by unanimous verdicts of the attempted murders of Mr Gjoshe, Mr Balakrishnan, Mr Presland, Mr Bletcher, Mr Green, Mr Crean and Mr Zitouni.

Williams did not give evidence at his trial and he appeared via video-link from Rampton Hospital, a high-security psychiatric hospital in Nottinghamshire, rather than in the dock at the court.

## Passengers fled 'in sheer terror'

Prosecutor Christopher Paxton KC earlier said that train passengers fled “in sheer terror” during the five-minute attack on the LNER train on the evening of November 1.

He said Williams had “posed a dangerous threat, marauding through towns and cities, on streets and trains alike, armed with a knife”.

He added: “He stabbed many, seeking to end their lives with deadly intention, striking out at them when they were defenceless and in fear.”

Going through psychiatric evidence, defence counsel Gillian Jones KC referred to the “delusional premise that his (Williams’s) life was at risk from a wide-ranging conspiracy by the devil”, and she suggested he did not intend to kill.

The judge had told jurors in his legal directions that a “person who is mentally ill, acting psychotically under a delusion, can still form an intent to kill”.

At a pre-trial hearing, Williams had pleaded guilty to wounding 12 of his victims with intent to cause really serious harm, and to the attempted wounding of Mr Taborski and Mr Ogelby.

Williams had also pleaded guilty, at the pre-trial hearing at Cambridge Crown Court, to three counts of possessing a knife in a public place.

Williams pleaded guilty to the common assault of a man, Jordan Morley, whom he spat at on a train approaching Biggleswade on November 1, and to the assault occasioning actual bodily harm of PC James Farrell whom he punched on the nose while Williams was in police custody on November 2.
