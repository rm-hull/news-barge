# Glossing over EU tuition fees demand will haunt Andy Burnham: The Yorkshire Post says

## Andy Burnham was unconvincing as he was pressed over by Kemi Badenoch on whether his new deal with the EU is dependent on UK university tuition fees for European students being reduced.

When details of the new ‘reset’ deal were announced on Friday including freer trade of food and drink and greater movement for young people, the Council of the European Union made clear that it would not sign off those arrangements until agreement had been reached on the still-disputed tuition fee issue.

The EU wants European students to be charged the same fees by British universities as UK students – leading Ms Badenoch to understandably question who would foot the bill for such a potential costly arrangement; either our universities or our taxpayers.

Mr Burnham attempted to paint the tuition fee issue as separate to the other agreed deals and claimed the fee reduction idea is an EU negotiating position which “won’t be put on the table” by the British as talks over the matter continue.

![Prime Minister Andy Burnham speaking during Prime Minister's Questions in the House of Commons.](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOjgzOThkMjE1LWY4YzYtNGMxOS04YmExLTFlNTBkMmZlODIyNzpjOTU5NTQxOS0wZDEwLTQwMjctYjFjMS1mNWU3N2RmMjBkNWE=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


But given the Council of the European Union won’t sign off on the other elements of the deal until a tuition fee arrangement is agreed, Ms Badenoch is clearly right that it is interlinked.

Mr Burnham accused the Conservative leader of looking “for the negative in everything” and claimed “she has not got the ability to see the bigger picture”.

But as things stand, the bigger picture is that unless Mr Burnham budges on tuition fees the rest of the deal he has been proclaiming will not be signed off by the EU. Telling Parliament that reducing fees for EU students is not on the table may well come back to haunt the PM.
