Several apartments in the formerly squatted building at Rigaer Straße 94 in Friedrichshain are scheduled to be cleared on December 15. A court spokesperson told the German Press Agency that notices were delivered to the affected residents last week.

The bailiff requested assistance from the police to clear the house. It’s expected that the police will be present in large numbers on the day of the eviction. The partially occupied building complex has around 30 apartments and has been the subject of legal debate for years.

According to court documents, the impending evictions affect nine people, against whom the landlord had filed lawsuits. Some of them have reached settlements with the building owner’s company in Great Britain. The court’s spokesperson added that five appeals are currently pending before the regional court. A decision on these appeals is expected in mid-January.

The community at Rigaer Straße 94 released a statement on Instagram relaying their dejection: “Being evicted from one’s own home is a reality for many people, even in the middle of winter, because rent is unaffordable, because you only earn crumbs from work. Because billions are poured into the military and war instead of social and educational purposes.”

Drawing on the eviction attempts in 2016 and 2021, which, according to the post, ended in “disaster for the cops”, the community living in the building once again has no intention of giving up easily. “Today, just as back then, one thing is clear – we will defend ourselves.”

Evictions and police operations at Rigaer Straße 94 have repeatedly led to clashes in the past. In previous years, there were incidents at and inside the building in which police officers were attacked with bottles and stones, and firefighters from the Berlin Fire Department were prevented from entering the premises.
