**Scotland's chief medical officer, Sir Gregor Smith, has resigned on mental health grounds.**

The 55-year-old, who has served in the position since December 2020, has faced questions over his role in the handling of avoidable deaths at NHS Grampian in recent weeks.

Smith said in a statement that he did not "currently enjoy the good mental health necessary" to continue in the job, adding that guiding the country through the Covid pandemic and its aftermath had "taken its toll".

First minister John Swinney thanked Smith for his service, adding that he had led the country through "one of the most demanding periods the Scottish health service has faced."

Smith's resignation comes after a surgeon, Dr Mohammed Habib, called for NHS Grampian to face a corporate homicide probe.

Habib leaked an unpublished review, carried out in 2022, which found 19 avoidable deaths among 45 patients in liver and pancreatic surgery at Aberdeen Royal Infirmary (ARI).

Smith and former health secretary Humza Yousaf were aware of issues at the time.

Health secretary Angela Constance has since commissioned an independent review into hepatobiliary surgery at the health board.

An oversight team has also been appointed following a separate report by Healthcare Improvement Scotland (HIS), which found "major deficiencies" in NHS Grampian's leadership and culture.

Smith said: "It has been a privilege to be the CMO of Scotland for the last six-and-a-half years, but it has become very clear to me that the nature and purpose of the role is not generally understood.

"Like many colleagues across the health system, those years of the pandemic and afterwards have taken their toll.

"Events over the last few days have shown me that I don't currently enjoy the good mental health necessary to continue with the exceptional challenges that this role can expose you to."

He added: "No-one in public life is unaccountable. I am confident, however, that the review initiated by the cabinet secretary will show that I provided my advice in relation to Grampian hepatobiliary service consistent with the role of the chief medical officer."

## Who is Sir Gregor Smith?

Smith spent 15 years as a GP in Larkhall before becoming medical director for primary care at the NHS Lanarkshire health board.

He became a senior medical officer within the Scottish government in 2012 and was appointed deputy chief medical officer three years later.

Smith was appointed interim chief medical officer in April 2020 after predecessor Catherine Calderwood resigned over the flouting of Covid regulations.

His appointment was made permanent in December of that year after becoming a familiar face appearing alongside Yousaf and then first minister, Nicola Sturgeon, at televised daily Covid briefings.

He was awarded a knighthood for his services to public health and the NHS in 2022.

Swinney said he "recognised and respected" Smith's decision to step down.

"Gregor became interim chief medical officer at the beginning of one of the most demanding periods the Scottish health service has faced," he said.

"He provided crucial senior clinical advice to government throughout the pandemic and then through the difficult recovery period that followed.

"I would like to thank him for his service to Scotland during Covid and beyond."

Health Secretary Angela Constance said: "In addition to his work during Covid, Gregor has been a strong advocate for person-centred healthcare, prevention and action to reduce health inequalities, drawing throughout on his experience as a practising GP.

"I thank Gregor for his commitment to public service and for his dedicated contribution to public health in Scotland."

Allegations over patient safety at Dr Gray's Hospital were published this week

BBC Scotland News became aware of new allegations from NHS Grampian on Wednesday.

Claimes published in The Times newspaper, external alleged a surgeon at Dr Gray's hospital in Elgin, Moray, attempted to take over a knee operation while he was recovering from a medical procedure and under the influence of drugs.

It also detailed a separate incident in which a surgeon had a physical altercation with a registrar who it is claimed suggested they were about to replace the wrong hip of a patient who was lying unconscious on the operating table.

The HIS report, published on 12 November, outlined serious shortcomings in management and leadership at NHS Grampian.

HIS medical director, Dr Simon Watson, described the report as "the most serious review" the healthcare watchdog had ever written, calling its findings "unprecedented".

Constance announced the independent review on Saturday. She said there was no "cover up" surrounding the 2022 report, adding that the review would be "100% transparent".

A spokesperson for Yousaf said he would answer questions about what he was briefed on and his follow-up actions at the upcoming independent review.

Reform UK's health spokeswoman, Helen McDade, said she expected "other resignations".

She added: "Gregor Smith clearly did not want to share information beyond the fact that he knew about the 2022 report, he appeared to be hiding things, but he may well be being a made a scapegoat."
