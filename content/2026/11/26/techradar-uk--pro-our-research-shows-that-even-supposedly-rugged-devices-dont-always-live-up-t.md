* **Lenovo report finds frontline technology is often struggling to fulfil promise**
* **Workers have encountered issues with outages, batteries and screens**
* **Report calls for tougher and more resilient technology**

Frontline workers are losing up to two working hours a week due to tablet or touchscreen failures, new research from Lenovo has claimed.

A survey of 1,000 UK workers in frontline industries such as energy, logistics, manufacturing, retail, construction and transportation found that technology issues were a major concern for many, particularly those working in demanding conditions and extreme weather.

Across 50 working weeks, the delays would add up to 92 hours a year — more than two standard working weeks.

## Tablet says no

The survey suggests frontline technology faces demands that go beyond those of a typical office - as nearly six in 10 respondents said they regularly work long shifts, while 57% work on the move and 52% need devices with extended battery life.

Device problems were common among those surveyed, with nearly half (44%) saying they had experienced batteries running out, 32% had struggled to use screens while wearing gloves, and 28% had difficulty using screens in the rain.

The effects also extended beyond simply lost time, as among respondents whose tablet or touchscreen device had failed or malfunctioned, 44% said it had delayed them completing tasks in the past 12 months, while 31% had been prevented from finishing a task altogether. In the same group, 41% reported an impact on customers or members of the public, and 34% said production or operations had been affected.

Workers also expressed concern about the conditions ahead, with almost two-thirds (64%) said more frequent or extreme weather could make it harder to do their jobs effectively - which rose to 77% among construction workers and 76% in energy.

The report raises questions about how devices are assessed before purchase, as two-thirds of those surveyed said technology described as durable or rugged had still struggled in their working conditions. Meanwhile, 82% said a device’s suitability for frontline work can only truly be judged in real-world conditions, and 80% believe frontline workers should have more input into technology decisions.

"Technology is the backbone of frontline operations, and when it fails, the consequences extend far beyond a broken device, disrupting productivity, delaying customers and adding pressure to workers," noted Ashley Rolfe, Chief Technologist at Lenovo UK&I.

"Our research shows that even supposedly rugged devices don't always live up to expectations, highlighting the need for technology that's proven in the toughest real-world conditions, not just in a laboratory."

"Because ultimately, the best technology is the technology you don't have to think about – it simply works, letting people get on with the job."
