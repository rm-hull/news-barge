Major donations from close associates of One Nation’s billionaire benefactor, Gina Rinehart, will be reviewed by the Australian Electoral Commission (AEC) amid concerns they were made on behalf of a third party.

The special minister of state, Don Farrell, said on Thursday that information about two $500,000 donations made to the Pauline Hanson-led party could be subject to further regulatory action if they are found not to comply with provisions in the Electoral Act.

In April, Hanson thanked two of Rinehart’s closest associates and senior executives for their “investment” in the party at the same time as she unveiled a private plane gifted to her by Rinehart.

The geologist Ian Plimer, who is an executive with Hancock Energy and known for his climate denialism, and Adam Giles, the head of Hancock Agriculture and former Northern Territory chief minister, each donated $500,000 to One Nation.

At the time, Hanson thanked “two wonderful patriotic Australians” for the combined $1m in contributions.

The money – and the use of the new Cirrus SR22 G7 plane – which can retail for about $2.1m, prompted Labor to accuse One Nation of being “a wholly owned subsidiary of Gina Rinehart”.

A question on notice about the donations from Plimer and Giles asked if the government had “any concerns that these donations were made on behalf of a third party”.

In answer, Farrell said the Electoral Act required relevant disclosures to be made within 2025-26 annual financial disclosure returns to the AEC, due earlier this month.

He said information about donations and in-kind support to political parties, including One Nation, would be published on the AEC’s transparency register on 1 February.

“The AEC will examine disclosures and determine whether further regulatory action is required,” Farrell said.

Guardian Australia has reported extensively on Hanson’s links to Rinehart, including her repeated failures to properly declare free flights on the mining magnate’s private jet.

Hancock Prospecting and One Nation were contacted for comment on Thursday.

The Coalition raised concerns with the federal government about whether Plimer and Giles had made the donations on behalf of a third party, asking if the source of the money would be investigated.

Crossbencher David Pocock also sought more information about the donations, using a parliamentary question on notice to ask if the AEC could investigate their source.

Guardian Australia reported in September that James Ashby, formerly Hanson’s chief of staff and now the party’s national director, drives a new Ford Ranger that was “personally” gifted to One Nation by Giles in April.

Hanson said the vehicle would help the party’s campaign efforts and “provides us the presence we need in the lead up to the next federal election”.

Hanson celebrated the defection of Liberal Leah Blyth to One Nation on Thursday, suggesting the senator would “build on One Nation’s expanding support” in South Australia.

The defection sparked recriminations from within the Coalition, even as Blyth struggled to justify her move to a party she has previously criticised – and as she fumbled key policy positions held by Hanson.

The Nationals leader, Matt Canavan, called Blyth “a turncoat” while the deputy Liberal leader, Jane Hume, said her former colleague “wasn’t a big contributor” to the Coalition.

In an interview with the Australian Financial Review on Thursday, Hanson insisted she was not controlled by Rinehart, pointing to her party’s opposition to the Australia-European Union free trade agreement.

Rinehart would benefit from the deal, including provisions to ease tariffs on beef and critical minerals.
