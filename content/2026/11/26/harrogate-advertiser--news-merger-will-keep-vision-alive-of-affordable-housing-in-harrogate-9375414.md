The merger of Harrogate Flower Fund Homes with Harrogate Housing Association has been described as a “positive step” for the affordable housing sector in a town with high property prices.

Due to increased legislation and regulatory requirements, the Boards of both associations felt that residents would be best served if the two officially joined, reducing duplication of work and saving money which could be invested into their homes.

Steven Brook, Chief Executive of Harrogate Housing Association, said: “The merger will see the number of homes that Harrogate Housing Association owns and manages increase to 342, with over 100 more in the pipeline.

“The change in ownership won’t mean any changes for Harrogate Flower Fund residents, who already have long-standing relationships with our team and told us in the consultation that they are very happy with the transition.

“What it will provide is increased stability at a time of increasing regulation within the housing sector.

“All our homes are in the Harrogate district, and we are committed to providing affordable homes for local people in an area with high property prices, as well as remaining local, approachable and focused in the delivery of our services.”

Harrogate Flower Fund Homes was established in 1982 by the Rotary Club of Harrogate and provides 33 affordable homes for older people - primarily one- and two-bedroom flats - close to local amenities and public transport links.

Harrogate Housing Association has been managing the homes since 2004, providing all of the day-to-day administration and maintenance.

John Wallace, Chair of the Harrogate Flower Fund Board and President of The Rotary Club of Harrogate , said: “Harrogate Housing Association shares this same ethos and values, and we feel certain they will keep the Rotary vision alive.

“In announcing this merger, I’d like to acknowledge the foresight of rotary members past and present in striving to deliver high-quality affordable homes and thriving communities for older people.”

Housing associations are not-for-profit organisations that provide, manage, and maintain affordable homes and support services, reinvesting all income back into housing and communities.

David Smith, Chair of Harrogate Housing Association, said: “The merger is a very positive step for us all, enabling us to maximise resources and deliver even more for our residents.

“I’d like to take this opportunity to welcome two of the Harrogate Flower Fund board members, Jo Allen and Abie Ansari, who are joining the Harrogate Housing Association board as part of the merger.”
