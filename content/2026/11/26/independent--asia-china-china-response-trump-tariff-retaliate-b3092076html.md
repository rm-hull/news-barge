China warned the US on Thursday that it “reserved the right” to retaliate against Donald Trump’s latest tariffs, unveiled as the American president remained fixated on his divisive economic policy.

China was one of several Asian economies targeted on Wednesday with a fresh round of levies on certain imports, a major step towards bringing back some of the sweeping global tariffs Mr Trump imposed last year, only to see the US Supreme Court strike them down in February.

China and Vietnam were hit with the steepest proposed tariffs, while Japan, South Korea, Indonesia, India, Malaysia, Singapore, Taiwan, Thailand, Bangladesh, and Cambodia also faced significant levies. The EU and Switzerland were targeted as well.

China, one of the largest trading partners of the US, faces 20 per cent tariffs over allegations of manufacturing overcapacity. The allegation is that the Asian economic giant is making far more industrial stuff than domestic and global demand can absorb. The levies could rise to as much as 40 per cent for China and 35 percent for Vietnam if Washington determines they aren’t complying with existing trade agreements.

“China urges the US to correct its wrong practices and return to the right track of resolving issues through dialogue and consultation,” commerce ministry spokesperson He Yadong said, adding that Beijing “reserves the right to take necessary measures and will resolutely safeguard its legitimate rights and interests”.

He urged the US to accelerate internal procedures to put a $60 billion tariff reduction arrangement into effect as soon as possible, adding that American and Chinese officials had kept communication over their trade truce.

A Chinese foreign ministry spokesperson accused the US of using claims of industrial overcapacity as a pretext for “political manipulation”.

“The so-called overcapacity is a false proposition and China opposes using it as a pretext for political manipulation,” he said.

“China and the US should work together to implement the important consensus reached between the two heads of state and maintain the momentum of stable development in bilateral economic and trade relations.”

The tariffs were proposed following an investigation led by Mr Trump’s trade negotiator Jamieson Greer into whether foreign trade practices violated existing agreements, or discriminated against American goods.

The fresh round of levies came after the Supreme Court ruled the president’s “Liberation Day” tariffs unlawful in February. The new duties are meant to match the rates agreed upon in trade deals struck since Mr Trump unleashed his trade war.

Under a trade agreement reached earlier this year, China was subject to a 10 per cent reciprocal tariff, in addition to a 10 per cent levy linked to fentanyl. The newly proposed tariff rate of 20 per cent could double if US officials determine that Beijing has failed to honour the terms of the earlier trade truce.

The latest tariff announcement threatened to strain relations between Washington and Beijing barely days after Mr Trump and Chinese president Xi Jinping held talks on the sidelines of the Apec summit in Shenzhen. The two leaders are expected to meet again in Miami next month.

![Vladimir Putin, Xi Jinping and Donald Trump in Shenzhen](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/19/17/2305282287..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The fragile trade truce between Washington and Beijing is set to expire on 10 January. It limits new US tariff increases to 20 percentage points on top of existing levies of 25 percent going back to Mr Trump’s first term. The latest proposal reaches that ceiling.

Under the latest tariff measures, Japan, Taiwan and South Korea face rates of 15 percent.

India faces a tariff of 25 percent, which may be lowered to 17 percent if a trade deal is done. Thailand, Bangladesh, and Cambodia face proposed rates of up to 18 percent. Indonesia and Malaysia are seeking to retain previously agreed rates of 19 percent. Vietnam remains in talks with Washington for a trade deal.
