## Bardella, who is vying to become France's next prime minister on a joint ticket with Marine Le Pen, faced allegations in September that he made conspiracist comments about Jewish people on Facebook in 2013.

Jordan Bardella, leader of France's far-right National Rally (RN), is facing renewed scrutiny, after Le Monde and Mediapart published further revelations on Thursday regarding antisemitic messages he allegedly wrote as a teenager.

Bardella, who is vying to become France's next prime minister on a joint ticket with Marine Le Pen, already faced allegations in September about alleged conspiracist comments he made about Jewish people on Facebook in 2013.

He has consistently denied doing these reports, decrying the media's "persecution" campaign on Thursday. He has already filed two legal complaints against Mediapart.

Taking to X, he wrote "for over two months, several media outlets have been investigating my childhood and adolescence, during which time I was a minor, between the ages of 13 and 17. These reports have extensively quoted statements that I formally deny having made."

Thursday's revelations include antisemitic comments Bardella made in private Facebook conversations with a high school acquaintance in the year he turned 18.

At the time, Bardella, now the president of the National Rally, was a teenager and a member of its predecessor, the National Front. The party was founded by Jean-Marie Le Pen, Le Pen's father, who repeatedly made Holocaust-denying remarks and was convicted several times for hate speech before his death in 2025.

Mediapart said it had seen these comments when it broke the story in September but had not published further excerpts to protect the identity of the outlet's source.

The acquaintance then agreed to share the unpublished remarks to show Bardella was lying after he rejected the initial accusations, the investigative outlet and Le Monde said.

In these, he allegedly wrote "it is Zionists who head banks" and seek a "new world order with Jerusalem as capital".

They said elements in the conversations seemed to confirm Bardella as being their author, including comments about his teachers as well as other details about his parents, and a stint teaching French to foreigners that he mentioned in his 2024 autobiography.

In recent years, the National Rally has courted Jewish voters by positioning itself as a bulwark against Islamic extremism in France, as Le Pen has made huge efforts to dissociate herself from her father's legacy.

Bardella has been instrumental in this effort, visiting Israel in 2025 — the first such trip by a French far-right leader — to demonstrate the National Rally's new credentials.

Researchers and critics of the party have dubbed this the "de-demonisation" of the party, after Le Pen expelled her father from the National Front in 2015 and rebranded it in 2018.
