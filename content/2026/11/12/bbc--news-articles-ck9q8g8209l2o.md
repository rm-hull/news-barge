Watch: Police arrests of two Iranians convicted of spying

**Two Iranian men have been convicted of spying for an Iranian intelligence service by carrying out surveillance on journalists and a lawyer in the UK.**

Mostafa Sepahvand, 41, and Farhad Javadi Manesh, 46, were convicted of "assisting a foreign intelligence service" at Woolwich Crown Court on Thursday.

They were tasked by Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) with monitoring the targets. Sepahvand intended to carry out a "violent attack" against at least one of them, while Manesh helped with planning.

A third man, Shapoor Qalehali Khani Noori, 57, was cleared of the same charge.

Sepahvand was in contact with two men with Iranian telephone numbers.

Police seized 534GB of data that showed Sapahvand and Manesh were given documents that included pictures of targets, their work addresses, the make and colour of their cars and the names of pets. They then used ChatGPT to carry out further research.

The pair, from west London, are asylum seekers who had been granted temporary leave to remain. Sepahvand arrived in the UK in a lorry in 2016, and Manesh arrived via small boat in 2019.

Sepahvand conducted surveillance and reconnaissance in preparation for inflicting violence on several "subjects" in the UK.

These included journalists Mojtaba Pourmohsen and Kambiz Hosseini, who worked for the London-based Persian broadcaster Iran international, Jonathan Turner, a founding member of UK Lawyers for Israel, as well as a media production professional.

Deputy Assistant Commissioner Vicki Evans, the senior national co-ordinator for Counter-Terrorism Policing, said it was the first time ChatGPT case search history had been presented in a state threat case.

She added: "It's something we're beginning to see more of as AI tools become more widely available and people become more aware of them."

It was clear from the evidence at the trial that at times Sepahvand felt he was being mistreated by his Iranian handlers, who had not paid him.

Evans said: "This is a trend we see in these types of cases where people get involved in work on behalf of foreign states and they're often promised money and lots of other things but they rarely receive what they've been offered."

Sepahvand had claimed he participated in the spying out of fear of the consequences of refusing but the prosecution described this as "entirely bogus".

In voice notes sent to an Iranian contact, he said he had been promised that a conviction for theft in Iran would be "sorted out" - which prosecutors said was part of his motivation for conducting the surveillance.

Sepahvand also asked for £150,000 in exchange for "finishing off" a target, stressing that he would "do it".

Frank Ferguson, of the Crown Prosecution Service's counter-terrorism division, said the two men had been promised rewards for sending intelligence back to contacts in Iran.

"Sepahvand himself was willing to go beyond surveillance to commit serious violence against their targets," he said.

"Thankfully, the defendants were stopped before any attack could take place.

"Cases like this show Iranian intelligence's willingness to use proxies to target the regime's critics abroad."

A spokesperson for Iran International said the verdicts would not "make the lives of our journalists any safer so long as the Islamic Republic of Iran continues its campaign of death threats and violence against them".

A statement continued: "Kambiz Hosseini and Mojtaba Pourmohsen and others remain under threat.

"We are thankful for the continued vigilance of the British police in protecting not only us but the many other Iranians targeted by Iran.

"Iran will no doubt continue its use of agents and proxies abroad in its campaign to terrorise its critics."

Sepahvand and Manesh will be sentenced at the Old Bailey on 17 December.
