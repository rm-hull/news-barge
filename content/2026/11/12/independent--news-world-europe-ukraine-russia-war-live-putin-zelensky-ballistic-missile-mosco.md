The bombing raid on the Omsk oil refinery continued for over eight hours on Wednesday, the *Kyiv* * Independent* reported.

Owned by Gazprom Neft, the refinery has the capacity to process about 22 million tonnes of crude a year, roughly 440,000 barrels a day, it said.

Omsk is a city in the vast Russian region of Siberia and lies about 2,500km (approximately 1,550 miles) from the Ukrainian border. Omsk governor Vitaly Khotsenko urged residents to remain indoors, the report said.

President Donald Trump’s envoys are in Moscow and will meet Russian president Vladimir Putin a day after peace talks in Abu Dhabi ended without any resolution.

## Ukrainian troops encountering ‘systemic’ Starlink disruptions, Zelenskyy says

President Volodymyr Zelensky said ​on Thursday that Ukrainian military units had recorded significant disruptions to the Starshield satellite communications service for several days, affecting the entire front line and Russian-occupied parts ⁠of Ukraine.

In a post on Telegram, Zelensky said that systemic restrictions were limiting the speed at which Starshield could be used to 161kmph (100 mph). Starshield is the military version of SpaceX's Starlink satellite communications service.

As ​a result ⁠of the restrictions, Ukrainian interceptors could not reach sufficient speed to down Russian jet-powered drones, with some ⁠hits already occurring, Zelensky said.

## Russia and Ukraine agree to biggest prisoner swap yet at Abu Dhabi peace summit

Negotiators from Russia and Ukraine have agreed to a major prisoner swap following two days of peace discussions in Abu Dhabi, though American mediators cautioned that deep divides remain over key matters such as territory.

Marking their first direct negotiations in nine months, both nations established an agreement in principle to exchange prisoners of war, with specific numbers expected to be finalised on Thursday.

US envoy Jared Kushner described the planned exchange as the "biggest humanitarian release of prisoners" during the four-and-a-half-year conflict, while Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskiy indicated that its overall scale would be "significant."

## Russia launches massive overnight strike on Ukrainian military factories

Russia's army said on Thursday it had carried out a massive overnight strike on military factories across Ukraine.

In Kyiv, the mayor said a Russian drone had struck an apartment block. Explosions were heard in the Ukrainian capital in the early hours of Thursday.

Kyiv mayor Vitali Klitschko said one woman had been reported killed ⁠and five people had been injured after ​a ⁠Russian drone struck a ‌nine-storey building in the city's Solomyanskyi district, setting it ablaze.

The Russian Defence Ministry said in a statement it had hit a rocket factory in Kyiv and a drone assembly workshop and missile fuel factory in the wider Kyiv region, as well as electricity substations in the western Zhytomyr region.

The Russian attacks had also damaged energy and civilian infrastructure in the north-western Rivne region, a local Ukrainian official said on Telegram.

## Ukraine captures first Indonesian prisoner of war fighting for Russia

Ukraine says it has captured its first Indonesian prisoner of war amid a reported surge in Russian recruitment of fighters from the southeast Asian country.

The 49-year-old man, originally from the Indonesian island of Java, was reportedly deployed to Ukraine's Zaporizhzhia region to take part in a Russian assault.

According to Ukraine's state-run *I Want to Live* project, the man managed to reach Ukrainian-held territory after his unit came under a drone attack, where he was subsequently captured by Ukrainian forces.

The capture comes amid Ukrainian claims that Russia has stepped up efforts to recruit Indonesian nationals to fight in its war.

## Ukraine's power supply on the brink as Russia intensifies attacks

Ukraine’s power supply is on the brink as Russia intensifies attacks on its energy grid.

Kyiv instituted a rolling schedule of power cuts in October, earlier this year than last winter, and they were already lasting longer, making homes darker and colder, the BBC reported.

Officials told the broadcaster they were concerned life could quickly become unsustainable for millions of Ukrainians if power supplies couldn’t be maintained for at least four hours per day. Some privately warn that scenario is already close, as some families are living with outages of up to 20 hours in Kyiv.

## Ukraine frontline mapped: The land in dispute as peace talks end without breakthrough

Negotiators for Russia and Ukraine emerged from a second day of US-brokered talks in Abu Dhabi on Wednesday confident about progress towards a long-awaited peace deal, despite lingering differences over territory.

Russia’s chief negotiator Vladimir Medinsky said that mutual understanding had been achieved on some issues and that more talks would be held, adding that the two sides had agreed on a large-scale prisoner swap.

Ukrainian negotiator David Arakhamia said that there was “good momentum” towards reaching a solution by the end of this year, but that Europe should also be represented in future discussions.

## When will the Kremlin talks take place?

The Kremlin talks are expected to take place in the first half of the day.

This will be the 10th time that Steve Witkoff has met Vladimir Putin in person.

The meeting will follow two days of trilateral peace talks in Abu Dhabi which finished on Wednesday with news of another planned major prisoner exchange but no public breakthrough in wider negotiations.

Kirill Dmitriev, Putin's special envoy, met Jared Kushner ​and Witkoff ⁠off their plane amid ‌wet snow and accompanied them to central Moscow ahead of their talks with Putin.

![](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/12/07/2026-11-12T055752Z_107583364_RC25MOA1WO3R_RTRMADP_3_UKRAINE-CRISIS-ATTACK.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

This week's Abu Dhabi talks were the first direct negotiations between Russia and Ukraine mediated by the US to try to end the 4-1/2-year-old war in nine months and, although Russian and Ukrainian negotiators reported some progress, the US side said differences remained on major issues, including territory.

Despite public optimism from US President Trump, there has been no sign of major compromises.

Kyiv has strongly rejected Putin's demands to surrender the whole of the eastern region of Donbas in return for peace, and with talks deadlocked on land, US mediators have tried to steer discussions towards postwar security guarantees and reconstruction.
