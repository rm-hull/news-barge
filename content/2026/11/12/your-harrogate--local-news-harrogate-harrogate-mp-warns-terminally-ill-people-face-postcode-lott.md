Tom Gordon MP has warned that terminally ill people could miss out on a £100 energy payment this winter depending on where they live.

The Government’s Household Energy Payment, announced in the Budget, will go to households in council tax bands A to D through the council tax system.

Some councils give terminally ill residents discretionary relief, reducing their council tax bill or clearing it altogether.

In a written answer to Mr Gordon, the MP for Harrogate and Knaresborough, ministers confirmed that people receiving Local Council Tax Support would get the £100 payment.

They gave no such guarantee for those receiving discretionary relief.

Ministers also left unanswered his question about how councils would reach eligible households without a council tax direct debit.

This includes terminally ill people whose bills are covered in full.

During Business Questions on Thursday, the Liberal Democrat MP asked the Leader of the House, Alan Campbell, to guarantee the payment for terminally ill people. Mr Campbell said he would take the point away.

The Government is giving councils in England £100 million to support households most affected by rising energy bills.

Mr Gordon is calling for the guidance on that fund to make clear that terminally ill people should receive support wherever they live.

Mr Gordon said:

> “Nobody facing a terminal illness should have to worry about whether they can afford to heat their home this winter, and whether they get help shouldn't depend on their postcode.
>
> “Council tax bands are a crude tool. They're not even good at taxing people fairly, let alone getting support to the people who need it most.
>
> “The Government can fix this with one line in its guidance to councils. I'll keep pressing until it does.”
