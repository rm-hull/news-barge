A Thirsk resident is seeking community support to establish a dedicated table tennis club in the town, providing opportunities for people of all ages to take part in the sport.

Leszek Chacel has launched an initiative to create Thirsk Table Tennis Club, with a vision for a permanent, affordable facility offering six to eight tables, coaching, junior development and opportunities to compete in organised leagues.

The idea has grown from Leszek’s experience of introducing his daughter, Victoria, to the sport. She has since developed into a competitive player and has represented North Yorkshire in a table tennis competition.

Despite living in Thirsk, the pair regularly travel between 10 and 30 miles to play at other clubs and take part in league competitions.

Leszek says they are not alone. Several local players are already registered with Table Tennis England and compete in organised leagues, but have to travel outside the town to play.

## A sport for all generations

One of the attractions of table tennis for Leszek is the range of ages it brings together. During her league experience, Victoria has played against an opponent aged over 80, while her youngest opponent was just 10.

Leszek believes the sport offers more than competition, providing opportunities for friendship, physical activity, concentration and learning across generations.

He hopes a club in Thirsk would allow children to discover the sport, give families an opportunity to play together and help older residents remain active.

## Looking for a suitable venue and funding

The initiative is still at an early stage, and no permanent venue or funding has yet been secured.

Leszek has contacted Table Tennis England, local authorities, community organisations and funding providers, including The National Lottery Community Fund. He has also approached a local school to explore possible cooperation and opportunities to introduce young people to the sport.

He is investigating potential premises and contacting local businesses to explore possible support for the project.

His longer-term ambitions include offering coaching, developing junior players, establishing teams to represent Thirsk in league competitions and organising tournaments attracting players from across North Yorkshire.

## How to get involved

Leszek is appealing to residents, families, businesses and organisations who may be interested in helping to establish the club.

He would particularly welcome hearing from:

* People and families interested in playing table tennis in Thirsk.
* Parents whose children would like to try the sport.
* Experienced players who could help develop the club and its competitive teams.
* Businesses willing to consider sponsorship or practical support.
* Anyone who knows of suitable premises that could accommodate a table tennis facility.
* People who may be able to help with funding applications, equipment or volunteering.
* [superchacel@googlemail.com](mailto:superchacel@googlemail.com)

The project does not yet have a permanent venue or a formal membership arrangement, but Leszek is keen to establish the level of local interest and build support before taking the next steps.

Anyone interested in playing, volunteering, supporting the project or helping to find a suitable venue can contact The Harrogate Informer, which will pass expressions of interest on to Leszek Chacel.

Leszek believes Thirsk deserves a place where people of different generations can develop their abilities, make friends, stay active and enjoy the sport.

His ambition is to give local people the opportunity to discover table tennis without having to travel outside the town.
