York is charging motorists premium parking rates throughout its Christmas Market period, including on days when the market itself is closed. With parking charges already rising in Harrogate, should North Yorkshire Council consider a similar approach to managing demand and generating income?

Parking charges have become an increasingly important issue for town centres, with councils facing pressure to balance the cost of operating car parks against the need to attract shoppers, visitors and businesses.

## How much more will motorists pay in York?

The following table shows the standard Sunday-to-Thursday on-street parking charges compared with the rates applied during the Christmas Market period at affected central locations.

| Parking duration | Normal rate | Christmas Market rate | Increase |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Up to 1 hour | £5.00 | £5.50 | 50p (10%) |
| 1–2 hours | £10.00 | £10.90 | 90p (9%) |
| 2–3 hours | £11.50 | £13.90 | £2.40 (21%) |
| Evening after 6pm | £4.00 | £4.80 | 80p (20%) |

The temporary rates apply from 12 November to 21 December 2026, including Tuesdays when the Christmas Market is closed. These figures relate to affected central on-street parking locations, not every car park in York.

Source: City of York Council parking information and its October 2026 parking variation notice.

In Harrogate, the issue raises a wider question: should parking be viewed primarily as a source of income for the council, or as an important part of keeping the town centre accessible and attractive?

## York takes a different approach during its Christmas Market

City of York Council is applying its premium Friday, Saturday and event parking rates throughout the Christmas Market period, running from 12 November to 21 December 2026.

This means motorists face the higher rates throughout the week, including on Tuesdays when the Christmas Market stalls are closed.

The council says the arrangements are intended to encourage visitors to use public transport during what it considers a peak period for traffic and visitor numbers.

However, the decision has attracted criticism from opposition councillors, who have questioned the fairness of charging premium rates on days when the market is not operating.

The argument is that higher parking charges could discourage people from visiting the city centre and spending money with local businesses.

## Why has York increased its parking charges?

The council’s approach is based on managing demand during a particularly busy period for the city.

By applying event parking rates throughout the market period, rather than just on the busiest individual days, the council aims to encourage visitors to consider alternatives to driving into the city centre.

Supporters of the policy argue that higher charges can help manage congestion and encourage public transport use. Critics question whether the policy risks making the city centre less accessible and placing an additional financial burden on visitors.

## Could higher parking charges work in Harrogate?

Harrogate has a different visitor economy to York, but the underlying question is similar.

The town relies on visitors, shoppers, hospitality businesses and people travelling into the centre for appointments and everyday services.

Increasing parking charges might encourage some motorists to use public transport, but it could also make Harrogate less attractive to people travelling from surrounding villages and communities where public transport options are limited.

There is also a question about whether higher charges actually reduce congestion or simply increase the cost of visiting the town.

North Yorkshire Council has previously justified parking tariff increases on the grounds of rising costs and investment in parking facilities. But whether motorists are seeing sufficient improvements in return is another matter.

## Should Harrogate increase parking charges?

Should Harrogate follow York’s example and use higher parking charges to manage demand during busy periods?

Or should North Yorkshire Council be doing everything possible to keep parking affordable and encourage people to spend money in the town centre?
