# Leeds dad left 'terrified' thinking daughter had been killed after pair knocked down on zebra crossing

## Edward feared for 10-year-old Lorna-Lily's life

A Leeds dad was "terrified" his daughter had died after the pair were knocked down on zebra crossing outside a primary school.

Edward Hardcastle and his 10-year-old daughter Lorna-Lily were using the crossing outside Lower Wortley Primary School when they were struck by a Skoda Yeti at 6.35pm on September 17. Edward had just picked Lorna-Lily up from a dance class and the pair were making their way home.

Edward, 43, suffered a fractured knee, a cut to his scalp and other minor injuries, but only his daughter's life was on his mind. Edward said: “What happened that day still haunts me, particularly seeing Lorna-Lily laying on the road. At that point, I didn’t even know if she was still alive. It was terrifying but I couldn’t even think about my own injuries, all I wanted to do was get to my little girl.

The pair were rushed to hospital by ambulance. Lorna-Lily was found to have a serious head injury and remained in hospital for around a week. She’s been left with cognitive issues, including headaches, memory problems and anxiety around cars.

Afterwards, Edward instructed expert serious injury lawyers at Irwin Mitchell to help them access the ongoing support they require. At a hearing at Leeds Magistrates Court on October 20, the driver was sentenced to 12 months community service and was also disqualified from driving for 12 months.

Edward, an HGV driver, was off work for around 11 weeks before going back on a phased return. Meanwhile, Lorna-Lily was absent from school for around three weeks.

Since the incident, Edward and Lorna-Lily’s mum Samantha, 45, have reported an increase in their daughter’s headaches and some concern around her memory, temper and patience. They also said she has difficulty expressing herself when excited and suffers from anxiety and nervousness, particularly around vehicles.

Edward said: “When we were told she had a head injury, we were devastated. She’ll always be our bright and funny Lorna-Lily but her life was changed in a split second through the actions of a careless driver and that is something I’ll never get over.

“She has a lot less patience now, gets angry more easily and even has nightmares at times. Her anxiety is also worse since the crash, especially when she sees cars. On the whole, however, she is doing well and we are incredibly proud of her. We’re also grateful for all the care and support that our daughter, and we as a family, have received.

“No sentence will ever make up for what we’ve been through, but by speaking out I hope I can make others realise the risks on the roads if you’re not careful.”
