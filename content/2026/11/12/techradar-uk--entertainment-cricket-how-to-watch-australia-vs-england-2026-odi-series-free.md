Nearly a year on from an embarrassing 4-1 Ashes defeat, England are back Down Under, this time for an extended white-ball tour that begins with a three-match ODI series.

The last bilateral ODI series between the two sides took place in 2024, when Australia toured England and emerged 3-2 winners. Since then, the teams have met just once, at the 2025 Champions Trophy, where Australia chased down 352 thanks to a magnificent century from Josh Inglis.

But with less than a year to go until the 2027 World Cup, both sides will be desperate to snap their losing streaks and rediscover some form. England struggled in the recently concluded tri-series involving Pakistan and Sri Lanka, managing just one win in four matches. Australia, meanwhile, are coming off a 2-1 ODI series defeat against South Africa.

Adding to the intrigue, recent revelations by former Australian batter Usman Khawaja about the Sandpapergate scandal have reopened old wounds within the Australian camp, putting several players under scrutiny. Fortunately for most of the current squad, only one player from the 2018 team is part of this series: stand-in captain Mitch Marsh. Pat Cummins, Mitchell Starc and Josh Hazlewood have all been rested.

Among the younger players, Mahli Beardman is an exciting addition and could make his international debut at just 21. Oliver Peake may continue his rise through the ranks, while Liam Scott could make his first appearance for Australia on home soil.

England, meanwhile, arrive with a little more momentum and are beginning to look like a settled unit under Harry Brook. Although Jacob Bethell is yet to be cleared of his knee injury and Josh Tongue is also unavailable, England have plenty of firepower at their disposal. Jofra Archer, whose absence was keenly felt in Pakistan, is back, alongside Gus Atkinson, Tom Banton, Sam Curran and Joe Root.
