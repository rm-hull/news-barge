Andy Burnham has stated his belief that a "win-win" agreement can be struck with the EU, which could be "more like the Brexit that people thought they were voting for in 2016".

The Prime Minister indicated that restarting discussions on future UK-EU ties has assisted progress on several pacts ahead of next week’s summit.

He also rejected an intervention from Lord David Cameron, who resigned from Downing Street in the wake of the UK vote to leave the EU, having led the Remain campaign after calling the referendum.

In Brussels next week, Mr Burnham is scheduled to meet European leaders to finalise treaties covering food and agriculture, emissions trading, and a youth mobility scheme, which could boost GDP by 0.4%.

Following this, the UK plans to "explore options" for a renewed long-term framework with the bloc.

Addressing the European Political Community (EPC) meeting in Ireland on Thursday, Mr Burnham said: "The requests are coming from the European side. We are putting our requests on the table too.

"I believe there is a good deal to be done that could be a win-win for Britain and Europe. I think it actually could be more like the Brexit that people thought they were voting for in 2016.

"I don’t think people thought they were going to get a Brexit as hard as the one that finally came through at the end of 2019. It was much harder than Theresa May’s deal when she was trying to come up with an arrangement.

"So let’s see where we get to in the next in the next seven days. But I think in the end, what we get could be good for Britain, but also closer to what people voted for at the referendum."

He did not dismiss offering reduced tuition rates to young Europeans within negotiations, adding: "There’s a lot of issues on the table as part of a deal and I’m certainly one that supports greater youth exchange between the UK and Europe."

Asked if reopening debate had aided or damaged the talks, Mr Burnham said: "I think it has helped, but there’s a long way to go."

He added that he would "fight now, over the days to come, to make sure we get this deal over the line".

Mr Burnham’s remarks in recent weeks have fuelled questions about the prospect of the UK returning to the EU or adopting its freedom of movement rules, but the Prime Minister has insisted voters would ultimately be “the boss” of any changes.

Lord Cameron said in an interview this week that starting a conversation about rejoining the bloc would be “very long and painful”, and that the UK’s economic problems over the last 10 years are not because of Brexit.

Mr Burnham said: “I think he is wrong to say that the low growth that we’ve had over the last decade is, quote, ‘nothing to do with Brexit.’

“I would say the evidence says otherwise, and indeed the Treasury analysis says otherwise, which is if we are able to come to a good working arrangement on emissions trading, on agri-food, on other things as well, that we can add growth to the British economy. So I think he is wrong to say that.

“It feels like his intervention is more about unity in the Conservative Party than it is about fighting for the British, the British interests.

“So just to finish, though, no one’s talking about rejoin. This is a deal that is about the next seven days.”
