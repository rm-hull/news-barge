More than 600 children from military families, from 29 North Yorkshire schools, met at Ripon Cathedral on Monday. Ripon Grammar musicians played every hymn.

More than 600 children and young people from military families filled Ripon Cathedral on Monday for North Yorkshire’s annual Festival of Remembrance for service children. They came from **29 schools** across the county.

The service on 9 November was the festival’s **14th**. North Yorkshire Council’s service children’s champion, Angela Campbell, organises it each year, the council said in its account of the day.

This year’s theme was **“belonging”**. The council says it reflected the shared experiences and connections that unite children from Forces families, despite the moves and separations military life can bring.

## What happened in the cathedral

The service remembered members of the Armed Forces who died in service. It also celebrated the children themselves.

The council picked out these moments:

* musicians from **Ripon Grammar School** accompanied every hymn
* the Service Children’s Community Choir sang “If You Hadn’t Been There” by Dolly Parton
* a **“You Belong Here”** exhibition showed self-portraits by**355 service pupils from 25 schools**, about one in ten of the county’s service children
* pupils from **Carnagill Primary School** wrote poems on the theme of belonging for the exhibition

The county’s Armed Forces champion, Cllr Kevin Foster, gave the choir **£450** from his councillor locality budget. Each of the council’s 90 councillors has £5,000 a year to spend on local causes. Cllr Foster served as a reservist for 30 years.

## What was said

Cllr Janet Sanderson, the council’s executive member for children and families, attended. She said: “Service children can face challenges as part of military family life, whether that’s moving schools, making new friends or coping with the deployment of loved ones.”

She added that the festival gives them “the opportunity to come together, reflect, celebrate their experiences and recognise that they belong to a wider community that understands and supports them.”

Cllr Foster said the service “celebrates the contribution that children connected to military families are making in the cause of our nation’s defence, global stability and peace.”

Cllr Alyson Baker, the council’s young people’s champion, said: “It was heartwarming to watch and listen to hundreds of school pupils from our county who came under one roof to take part in the poignant remembrance service.”

Guests included the Deputy Lord Lieutenant for North Yorkshire, Countess Peel, the Dean of Ripon, the Very Rev John Dobson, senior military representatives and the council’s chief executive, Richard Flinton.

## What it means for you

* **The festival is for children from Forces families in North Yorkshire schools.** It is organised by the council’s service children’s champion, and next year’s will be the 15th.
* **The cathedral was full of local talent.** Ripon Grammar School’s musicians played for the whole service, and pupils from Ripon Cathedral Primary School were among the hundreds taking part.
