Residents at Boroughbridge Manor marked Remembrance Day by remembering those who served in the armed forces and reflecting on the experiences of people affected by conflict.

The care home commemorated Remembrance Day on Sunday 8 November, joining the rest of the United Kingdom in observing a two-minute silence at 11am.

Residents wore poppies and watched the televised service from the Cenotaph. They also took part in activities including making knitted poppies, decorating a memorial cart and sharing memories with family and friends.

Jean, a resident at Boroughbridge Manor, said:

> **Jean, resident at Boroughbridge Manor:**
>
> It is very important that we keep the memories of those who fought alive and that we also think about those involved in conflicts around the world today.

Another resident, Hetty, remembers being called up during the war to work in the kitchens, helping to ensure soldiers were fed and watered.

Lynnette Sellers, General Manager at Boroughbridge Manor, said the occasion was particularly meaningful because many residents have personal connections to the military or have experienced conflict themselves.

> **Lynnette Sellers, General Manager at Boroughbridge Manor:**
>
> We have many residents with personal ties to the military and many who have experienced conflict either at home or abroad so it was important to us to mark Remembrance Day and honour the contribution of everyone involved in the World Wars and in subsequent conflicts.

Boroughbridge Manor is run by Barchester Healthcare and provides dementia care, residential care and respite care.
