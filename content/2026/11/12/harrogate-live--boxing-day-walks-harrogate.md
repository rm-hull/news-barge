We checked 10 Boxing Day walks near Harrogate, from the Stray to Fountains Abbey, for what is open on 26 December and where to park. Updated November 2026.

There are 10 Boxing Day walks in and around Harrogate for 2026, and this guide puts them in one place with what is open on Saturday 26 December, where to park and what it costs. Most people head for **the Stray**,** Valley Gardens and the Pinewoods**,** Knaresborough’s riverside**or, with a car,** Fountains Abbey**and** Brimham Rocks**. Every entry was checked against the owner’s own site on 12 November 2026.

Plan around the light. The sun sets at about 3.45pm in Harrogate on Boxing Day, Fountains takes its last visitors at 3.30pm and Harlow Carr at 3pm, so start anything longer than an hour by 1.30pm.

Plan around the transport too. Northern and LNER have both confirmed there will be no trains at all on Saturday 26 December, so the Harrogate line is shut for the day (our Christmas trains page has the full timetable). The first two sections are walkable from Harrogate and Knaresborough town centres; everything further out needs a car.

## In Harrogate, on foot

### 1. The Stray

Two hundred acres of open grass and tree-lined verges wrapped around the old town, and the walk closest to hand for most of the town. North Yorkshire Council’s Stray page says many of the paths across the main sections have suitable disabled access, and the land is protected by the Stray Act 1985, so there are no gates and no closing time.

A loop from West Park out to Christ Church and High Harrogate and back is an easy hour on surfaced paths. Parking on the grass or verges is not allowed; use the disc zone streets around it or the town’s car parks (our parking guide).

### 2. Valley Gardens and the Pinewoods

The classic Harrogate walk: through the 17-acre Valley Gardens, a Grade II listed park the council says is “open all year round”, then up into the 96 acres of the Pinewoods and along what locals call the mile walk or memorial walk to RHS Harlow Carr on Crag Lane. The Friends of Valley Gardens give the longer loop through the gardens, the Pinewoods and Birk Crag as about 3.5 miles.

![A path curving through Valley Gardens in Harrogate past autumn trees towards a bandstand-style shelter](https://harrogatelive.co.uk/img/boxing-day-walks-harrogate-1.webp)

The council’s Pinewoods page says tarmac paths link the wood to the town centre and most are fairly flat, but the lowest ground is often waterlogged in winter. The Valley Gardens have public toilets, including accessible ones; the Magnesia Well cafe’s opening times vary and it has not published Christmas hours.

![A surfaced footpath running between grass and tall trees in the Pinewoods, Harrogate](https://harrogatelive.co.uk/img/boxing-day-walks-harrogate-2.webp)

**Open** Valley Gardens open all year; the Pinewoods are open woodland**Price** free**Address** Valley Drive, Harrogate, HG1 2SZ (main gate at Cornwall Road and Royal Parade)**Parking** disc zone streets around the gardens; Pinewoods car park off Crag Lane**Toilets** in the Valley Gardens, including accessible toilets**Cafe** Magnesia Well cafe, 01423 525149, hours vary**Website** northyorks.gov.uk/leisure-tourism-and-culture/parks-play-areas-and-woodlands/parks-play-areas-and-woodlands/valley-gardens-harrogate**Good for** walking from town, dogs in the woods, a 3.5-mile loop via Birk Crag

### 3. RHS Garden Harlow Carr

The end point of the Pinewoods walk, and open on Boxing Day: the RHS visit page says the garden and garden centre close only on Christmas Day, and winter hours run 9.30am to 4pm with last entry at 3pm. It is a paid garden rather than a free walk, but walking in from the Pinewoods gets the car-free price of £12.90 for an adult instead of £18.40 on the day, with proof of car-free travel on arrival.

![A long straight path between planted borders and trees at RHS Harlow Carr, with stone lions at the top of the steps](https://harrogatelive.co.uk/img/boxing-day-walks-harrogate-3.webp)

Children aged 5 to 16 pay £6, under-5s are free, and RHS members get in free with a guest. Only assistance dogs are allowed.

The RHS lists Bettys at the garden as open daily 9am to 4.30pm; it gives no separate Christmas hours.

**Open** Saturday 26 December 9.30am to 4pm, last entry 3pm; closed Christmas Day**Price** adults £18.40 on the day, £12.90 car-free, from £13.60 booked online; children 5 to 16 £6; under-5s free**Address** Crag Lane, Beckwithshaw, Harrogate, HG3 1QB**Cafe** Bettys, daily 9am to 4.30pm**Website** rhs.org.uk/gardens/harlow-carr/plan-your-visit**Good for** winter garden, arriving on foot from the Pinewoods, assistance dogs only

## Knaresborough and the Nidd

### 4. Nidd Gorge, Bilton Banks

Forty-six hectares of ancient woodland on the southern bank of the Nidd between Harrogate and Knaresborough, owned by the Woodland Trust. Its wood page describes a well-surfaced forest track of about 0.7 miles from the car park down to the river, a footbridge about 300 metres from the car park, and a riverside path running a mile upstream to Bilton Beck.

![An earth footpath beside the River Nidd under bare winter trees in the Nidd Gorge](https://harrogatelive.co.uk/img/boxing-day-walks-harrogate-4.webp)

Look for the century-old yews of Bilton Banks and the ditches of Gates Hill Camp, an Iron Age hillfort 60 metres above the river. The trust warns it is steep in places with steps and boardwalks, and slippery when wet, which on 26 December it usually is.

The trust also publishes a longer Castle to Castle walk from Ripley to Knaresborough. Dogs are welcome.

There are no toilets; the nearest are at Conyngham Hall in Knaresborough.

**Open** public access woodland, no gates**Price** free**Address** B6165 Ripley Road, about 1.5 miles from Knaresborough High Street, just before Scotton Drive (grid ref SE328579)**Parking** free car park for about 10 cars**Toilets** none; nearest at Conyngham Hall car park, Knaresborough**Access** surfaced track from the car park; steep, stepped and slippery paths beyond**Website** woodlandtrust.org.uk/visiting-woods/woods/nidd-gorge**Good for** dogs, river walk, Castle to Castle route to Ripley

### 5. Knaresborough Castle grounds and the riverside

The castle grounds sit on a cliff above the Nidd looking straight down the gorge to the railway viaduct, and Culture North Yorkshire’s castle page says the grounds are open all year round. The museum inside closes from 25 December to 1 January, but the grounds do not.

![Knaresborough railway viaduct crossing the River Nidd, seen from the castle with trees and houses on the banks](https://harrogatelive.co.uk/img/boxing-day-walks-harrogate-5.webp)

From the castle, steps lead down to Waterside and the river path under the viaduct, which makes an easy hour’s loop back up through the town. Low Bridge is also where Knaresborough’s Boxing Day tug of war is pulled across the river between teams from the Half Moon and the Mother Shipton Inn, a fixture since the late 1960s; in 2025 the pull started at noon.

The 2026 details have not been published yet, and we will add them when they are. Free public toilets in Castle Yard car park open 8am to 8pm daily.

**Open** castle grounds open all year; museum closed 25 December to 1 January**Price** free**Address** Castle Yard, Knaresborough, HG5 8AS**Parking** Castle Yard car park**Toilets** Castle Yard car park, 8am to 8pm daily, step-free, accessible toilet and baby changing**Website** culturenorthyorkshire.co.uk/our-venues/knaresborough-castle**Good for** viaduct views, no car needed from Knaresborough, the Boxing Day tug of war

## Ripon and Fountains

### 6. Fountains Abbey and Studley Royal

The biggest Boxing Day walk in the district, and open: the National Trust’s calendar has the abbey and water garden open 9.30am to 3.30pm on Saturday 26 December, the visitor centre restaurant 9am to 4pm, and the shop 10.30am to 4.30pm; the whole estate is shut on 24 and 25 December. The Stray Ferret’s Seven Bridges Valley walk from the visitor centre follows the Skell for 3.8 miles.

![The ruins and tower of Fountains Abbey in low winter sunshine under a blue sky](https://harrogatelive.co.uk/img/boxing-day-walks-harrogate-6.webp)

Two things to know from the trust’s own notices: the Studley Tea-Room and the gate between the estate and the deer park stay shut until 2028, and the estate is closed on Fridays until February, which does not affect Boxing Day this year as it falls on a Saturday. The car parks lock 90 minutes after last admission.

There is more on the estate’s festive programme in our Fountains Abbey Christmas guide.

The Boxing Day tradition here is Ripon Cathedral’s St Stephen’s Day pilgrimage, which walks from the cathedral to the abbey “following in the footsteps of those who walked from Ripon and founded Fountains Abbey”, and ends with a short carol service in the cellarium. The cathedral lists it for 26 December 2026 from 10.15am to 12.45pm, and says entry to the abbey is free to non-members until noon, when the service starts.

**Open** Saturday 26 December, abbey and water garden 9.30am to 3.30pm; restaurant 9am to 4pm; car parks 9am to 5pm; closed 24 and 25 December**Price** adults £21, children 5 to 17 £10.50, family £52.50 (standard admission, no booking); free to non-members until noon on 26 December for the pilgrimage**Address** Fountains, Ripon, HG4 3DY**Parking** free at the visitor centre and West Gate; Studley Royal car park £7 for four hours, £10 a day**Website** nationaltrust.org.uk/visit/yorkshire/fountains-abbey-and-studley-royal-water-garden**Good for** the Boxing Day pilgrimage from Ripon, a long riverside walk, dogs on a lead

### 7. Ripon City Wetlands

Yorkshire Wildlife Trust’s reserve on former gravel workings beside the Ure and the Ripon Canal, opened in 2019. It is a flat there-and-back walk from the car park to the Riverside Lagoon screen and on to the Canal Reedbed Hide, not a loop.

The trust’s reserve page says the car park opens 9am to 4pm from November to February and that the reserve can be reached on foot along the canal towpath from dawn to dusk; it does not mention Christmas closures. The Wildlife Trusts say it is one of the best places locally for starling murmurations in autumn and winter, so it is worth timing a walk for the last hour of light.

The gravel paths are hard going for wheelchairs and buggies, and dogs are not allowed.

**Open** car park 9am to 4pm November to February; towpath access dawn to dusk**Price** free; non-members asked for a parking donation**Address** Boroughbridge Road, Ripon; or on foot from Renton's Bridge, Green Lane, Littlethorpe, HG4 3LR**Parking** reserve car park; out of hours on Boroughbridge Road before the bridge, or in Littlethorpe**Access** flat gravel paths, difficult for wheelchairs and buggies; two disabled bays reached with a RADAR key**Website** ywt.org.uk/node/3618**Good for** birdwatching, starling murmurations, a short flat walk, no dogs

## Out in Nidderdale

### 8. Brimham Rocks

The National Trust’s wind-carved gritstone outcrops on the moor above Summerbridge, free to enter and open on Boxing Day. The trust’s calendar has the countryside open from 9am to dusk on 26 December, with the kiosk, visitor centre and second-hand bookshop open 10am to 3.30pm; all are shut on 24 and 25 December.

![A balanced gritstone outcrop at Brimham Rocks overlooking wooded Nidderdale](https://harrogatelive.co.uk/img/boxing-day-walks-harrogate-7.webp)

Paths run between the rock formations from the car park, and the views over Nidderdale are the reason to go on a clear day. Parking is £7 for four hours or £10 a day for cars, paid at ticketless machines that take card and coin, or by the JustPark app; National Trust members and Blue Badge holders park free.

Phone signal can be poor, so download the app before you set off.

**Open** Saturday 26 December, countryside 9am to dusk; kiosk and visitor centre 10am to 3.30pm; closed 24 and 25 December**Price** free entry; parking £7 for four hours, £10 a day**Address** Brimham Moor Road, Summerbridge, HG3 4DW**Parking** NT car park, members and Blue Badge holders free**Cafe** kiosk, 10am to 3.30pm on 26 December**Website** nationaltrust.org.uk/visit/yorkshire/brimham-rocks**Good for** children scrambling, Nidderdale views, free entry

### 9. Swinsty and Fewston reservoirs

Two linked Yorkshire Water reservoirs in the Washburn Valley, about eight miles west of Harrogate, and the easy-going choice for a post-dinner walk. Yorkshire Water’s Swinsty walk is 3 miles, about two hours, on wide, well-surfaced paths with no steps, stiles or gates; its Fewston walk is 4 miles, and the two join up for a longer day.

![A picnic bench on the shore of Swinsty Reservoir with wooded hills across the water](https://harrogatelive.co.uk/img/boxing-day-walks-harrogate-8.webp)

The repair work on Swinsty’s southern embankment was due to finish at the end of September 2026. Yorkshire Water is trialling parking charges here: £1 an hour, £3 for six hours or £5 all day, paid by card only at the machines or by RingGo.

The reservoir page lists toilets, including an accessible one. Dogs on a lead.

**Open** open access paths**Price** free; parking £1 an hour, £5 all day, card or RingGo only**Address** Swinsty Moor and Fewston Reservoir car park, LS21 2NP; or Stack Point car park, HG3 1SU**Parking** both car parks have a 2.2m height barrier; Blue Badge annual permit free**Toilets** listed by Yorkshire Water, including an accessible toilet**Access** wide surfaced paths, no steps, stiles or gates**Website** yorkshirewater.com/things-to-do/walks/swinsty-walk**Good for** pushchairs, a flat 3-mile loop, dogs on a lead

### 10. Hackfall, Grewelthorpe

A wooded gorge on the Ure near Masham that William Aislabie, son of the man who built Studley Royal, turned into an 18th-century landscape of follies, grottos and waterfalls. It was felled for timber after 1932, and the Woodland Trust’s restoration won a Europa Nostra award in 2011.

![A ruined Gothic stone folly with pointed arched windows among trees at Hackfall](https://harrogatelive.co.uk/img/boxing-day-walks-harrogate-9.webp)

The trust’s Hackfall page suggests about two hours, with set walks from 30 minutes to three hours, and is plain about the ground: steep paths, steps, no wheelchair access and very muddy in the wet. Free car park on the left about half a mile before Grewelthorpe village.

No toilets on site; the nearest are in Masham, about two miles away.

**Open** woodland open access**Price** free**Address** near Grewelthorpe, Ripon, HG4 3BS**Parking** free Woodland Trust car park, half a mile before Grewelthorpe**Toilets** none on site; public toilets in Masham**Access** steep paths and steps, no wheelchair access, muddy in wet weather**Website** woodlandtrust.org.uk/visiting-woods/woods/hackfall**Good for** follies and waterfalls, a two-hour walk, walking boots

## Frequently asked questions

### Is Fountains Abbey open on Boxing Day?

Yes. The National Trust’s calendar has the abbey and water garden open from 9.30am to 3.30pm on Saturday 26 December 2026, with the visitor centre restaurant open 9am to 4pm and the car parks 9am to 5pm. It is shut on 24 and 25 December. Ripon Cathedral’s Boxing Day pilgrimage walks there that morning, and the cathedral says entry is free to non-members until the noon carol service.

### Is RHS Harlow Carr open on Boxing Day?

The RHS says the garden is closed only on Christmas Day, so on 26 December it keeps its winter hours of 9.30am to 4pm, with last entry at 3pm. Adults pay £18.40 on the day, or £12.90 if they arrive by bus, bike or on foot. Only assistance dogs are allowed.

### Are there trains or buses to Harrogate on Boxing Day?

No trains. Northern and LNER have both confirmed no trains at all on Saturday 26 December, so nothing runs on the Harrogate line. Walks in Harrogate and Knaresborough can be reached on foot from the town centres; Fountains, Brimham, Swinsty and Hackfall need a car.
