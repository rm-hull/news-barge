In Germany, the number of over-indebted people is steadily increasing. This is according to data from the Debt Atlas published by the credit agency Creditreform.

Berlin ranks fourth from last among the German states with an over-indebtedness rate of almost 13% – ​​ahead of Saxony-Anhalt, North Rhine-Westphalia, and Bremen. There was a slight increase of 8,000 cases, bringing the total to approximately 390,000.

Nationwide in Germany, the rate of over-indebted people rose to almost 11% by the beginning of October this year, according to the new Creditreform Debt Atlas. The atlas indicates that 7.36 million adults have debts exceeding their current income. This represents an increase of 293,000 people compared to 2025, or 4.1%.

Patrik-Ludwig Hantzsch, head of economic research at Creditreform, attributes the increase to several factors, including rising costs of living, energy, and housing. He also notes that unemployment is once again a concern.

According to the data, the number of affected individuals under 30 years of age has risen disproportionately across Germany, by almost 6% to around 1.17 million. That is 64,000 more people than before.
