Households in Wetherby area are being reminded to keep putting out their brown bins.

The message comes as Leeds City Council pauses its garden waste collections for the winter.

But Wetherby receives a combined food and garden waste collection and will continue to have their brown bins emptied throughout winter.

Councillor James Gibson, Leeds City Council’s executive member for environment, said: “For households in the Wetherby area who are part of the co-mingled food and garden waste collection trial, collections will continue throughout winter.”

Leeds City Council delivers the largest garden waste collection service by any council in the UK, annually collecting more than 31,000 tonnes of garden waste from 220,000 households in brown bins across the city.

The council is also developing plans to expand the service to additional households in the future, helping even more residents to recycle their garden waste.

The service is designed to assist households with gardens and to supplement home composting.

Each year, brown bin collections are paused during the winter months due to the dramatically reduced demand for garden waste disposal, with the service resuming in the following spring.

This year, the last round of collections will start from Monday 16 November with the final scheduled collection day being Saturday 28 November.

All Leeds residents who receive a brown bin garden waste collection can go to [https://www.leeds.gov.uk/bins-and-recycling/check-your-bin-day](https://www.leeds.gov.uk/bins-and-recycling/check-your-bin-day) to check their final collection date. Reminders can also be sent directly to smartphones by downloading the Leeds Bins app.

Over the winter garden waste can still be taken to any of the eight council-run local household waste recycling centres for free. Sites are open seven days a week, 8am-4pm. Check [https://www.leeds.gov.uk/bins-and-recycling/recycling-sites](https://www.leeds.gov.uk/bins-and-recycling/recycling-sites) for the nearest recycling centre.

All garden waste collected by the council throughout the year is composted locally and used in farming, horticulture and local land restoration projects.
