A York man helping about 100 people a day with a food bank run from his shed has said he is facing a very difficult situation as its future remains uncertain.

John McGall, the man behind the I Am Reusable Community Food Bank, said he had applied to extend his planning application after struggling to find a long-term base.

A total of 19 supporters have backed his bid to temporarily stay in Aldborough Way, including volunteer Brian Rushton who said more and more people relied on the food bank.

But three people have objected saying it is unsuitable for a quiet, residential street and often caused long queues and traffic problems.

Mr McGall’s bid to keep the food bank, near Leeman Road, open comes after it was first granted temporary planning permission to continuing operating at his property in 2024.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/john-mcgall-food-bank-2026-ldrs-2.webp)

The resident launched the site in 2020 during the coronavirus pandemic to help people who were isolating and struggling financially, according to his application.

Plans stated it had since grown to serve around 100 people a day and was supported by more than 60 volunteers every day from 10am to 5pm.

The food bank receives donations from around 15 supermarkets and local businesses who deliver goods from 8am to 10pm.

Mr McGall said he had been looking for a permanent home for the food bank over the last two years but his search had so far come to nought.

He added a purpose-run food bank also providing aid including employment and mental health support and cookery classes was needed in the long-term.

Mr McGall told the Local Democracy Reporting Service (LDRS): “I’d be quite worried if we had to shut because the cost of living’s going up with the war in Iran.

“We get homeless people, refugees, asylum seekers, families and professionals.

“I think some of the neighbours are fed up now because they thought we’d be gone in a couple of years.

“It’s been very difficult, if I’d have waited until November to sort this out I’d put my hands up but I have been trying to find somewhere, I wish I had an answer.

“This is a very important service for the people who rely on it, the ones who will suffer are the ones who use it.”

An objector living in the street said the food bank only previously been granted temporary planning permission because the area was unsuitable.

The objector said: “The land in association with the food bank is not considered appropriate in such a residential setting, given the harm identified in relation to visual appearance, parking and neighbour amenity.

“Nothing has changed since then and many of the immediate neighbours have waited patiently for this monstrosity to be removed.

“This is a residential area, not an industrial estate, and there are other, better  
organised food banks in York.”
