Ministers have not promised the £100 energy payment to terminally ill people on council tax relief. Harrogate MP Tom Gordon raised it in the Commons on 12 November.

Terminally ill people who pay little or no council tax could miss out on the government’s **£100 Household Energy Payment** this winter, Harrogate and Knaresborough MP Tom Gordon has warned, because ministers have not said whether households on discretionary council tax relief will get it.

The payment goes to households in council tax bands A to D and is paid through the council tax system. In North Yorkshire the council expects to start paying it from January 2027, and about 36,000 homes in the Harrogate and Knaresborough constituency are in the qualifying bands.

## What ministers have and have not promised

Some councils give terminally ill residents discretionary relief, so they pay little or no council tax. Other councils do not.

Answering written questions from Mr Gordon, the Liberal Democrat MP, ministers confirmed that two groups will get the payment:

* students who are exempt from council tax
* people receiving Local Council Tax Support

They gave no such guarantee for people on discretionary relief, terminally ill people among them.

Ministers also did not answer his question on how councils will reach eligible households that do not pay council tax by direct debit. Many terminally ill people on full relief pay nothing, so there is no direct debit for the £100 to be paid back through. North Yorkshire Council has already said that households paying by direct debit are likely to be paid more quickly, and that others will be invited to apply.

The answers confirmed that many tenants in houses in multiple occupation, and students in halls of residence, will not get the payment.

In its written answer on 3 November, the local government department said: “Halls of residence and purpose built student accommodation that fall outside the council tax system will not be eligible for the Household Energy Payment. However, the government recognises that some households which are not eligible for the Household Energy Payment may still need support.”

## The £100 million fund

The government is giving councils in England **£100 million** to help households most affected by rising energy bills. The written answer said: “Local authorities are best placed to identify and support vulnerable households in their area, and the government will issue guidance to local authorities on this in due course.”

Mr Gordon wants that guidance to make clear that terminally ill people must not miss out, wherever they live. North Yorkshire Council has not yet published how its share of the fund will work.

## “Take away what he has said”

At Business Questions on Thursday 12 November, Mr Gordon asked the Leader of the House, Alan Campbell, to guarantee that terminally ill people get the payment when it goes out this winter. Mr Campbell said he would “take away what [Tom] has said”. The exchange can be watched on Parliament Live, from 12.02pm.

Mr Gordon said:

“Nobody facing a terminal illness should have to worry about whether they can afford to heat their home this winter, and whether they get help shouldn’t depend on their postcode.

“Council tax bands are a crude tool. They’re not even good at taxing people fairly, let alone getting support to the people who need it most.

“The Government can fix this with one line in its guidance to councils. I’ll keep pressing until it does.”

## If you or someone you care for is affected

* **On full council tax relief and in band A to D:** the government has not yet said whether you qualify. Full eligibility criteria are still to be published.
* **Not paying by direct debit:** North Yorkshire Council says it will invite households to apply, and that you do not need to contact it now.
* **Missing out on the £100:** the council’s share of the £100 million fund is meant for households most affected by energy bills; it has not yet said how to apply.
