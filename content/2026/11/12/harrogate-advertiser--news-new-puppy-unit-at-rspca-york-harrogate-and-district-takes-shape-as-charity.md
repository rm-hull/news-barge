A specialist new puppy unit is taking shape at RSPCA York, Harrogate and District as the charity works towards creating a safe haven for some of the most vulnerable animals in its care.

The purpose-built facility will provide warm and secure accommodation for puppies and their mothers, giving them the specialist support they need from the moment they arrive.

Construction is now underway, with the new unit expected to open in January 2027 – marking a major milestone in the charity’s plans to improve animal welfare facilities for years to come.

The need for the new facility has been highlighted by a heartbreaking recent case involving four tiny puppies who arrived at the RSPCA York, Harrogate and District just two days old.

A member of the public brought the puppies to the charity in a cardboard box after their mother sadly died during birth, leaving them without the warmth and care they desperately needed.

Named James, Olivia, Luke and Jane, the puppies were cold, weak and extremely vulnerable.

The team at RSPCA York, Harrogate and District immediately stepped in, warming them up and beginning round-the-clock feeds every two hours – including overnight.

The puppies were later diagnosed with a bacterial infection, and one of the litter, Jane, became particularly unwell and required emergency veterinary treatment.

Thanks to the dedication of the charity’s team and support from Green Vets, all four puppies are now receiving intensive care.

Although their recovery remains uncertain, staff are doing everything possible to give them the best chance of survival.

Their story demonstrates exactly why the new puppy unit is so important.

Until the specialist facility opens, staff and volunteers are going above and beyond to support vulnerable animals, with some puppies cared for in foster homes so they can receive the constant attention they need.

When completed, the new puppy unit will provide a dedicated environment where puppies and their mothers can recover, grow and receive specialist care in a warm and safe setting.

Lucy Lowthian, Capital Campaign Lead at RSPCA York, Harrogate and District, said:

“Seeing work progressing on our new puppy unit is incredibly exciting.

“After years of planning, fundraising and hard work, we are finally beginning to see the facilities we have dreamed of taking shape.

“Knowing that this building could be ready to welcome puppies by January 2027 makes it all feel very real.

“But stories like these four tiny puppies remind us that this is about so much more than bricks and mortar.

“These are incredibly vulnerable little lives, and the care they need from the moment they arrive can make all the difference.

“Our team will always do everything possible to give every animal the best chance, but having the right environment and facilities is absolutely vital.

“The animals who come through our doors deserve the very best, and we need our community to help us give them that future.”

The puppy unit will cost around £300,000 to build, and RSPCA York, Harrogate and District is now appealing for help to raise the remaining £194,000 needed to complete the project.

The charity has already received a generous £100,000 contribution from Pets Foundation, helping to give the project a strong foundation.

Supporters have also raised almost £6,000 through virtual brick sales.

As an independently funded local charity, RSPCA York, Harrogate and District relies on the generosity of its supporters to continue its work.

Every donation, fundraising effort and virtual brick purchase will help bring the new puppy unit to life.

The facility forms part of the charity’s wider £5 million redevelopment plans.

The first phase also includes a new purpose-built cattery and a community and training hub, creating improved facilities for animals, volunteers and the wider community.

For almost 50 years, RSPCA York, Harrogate and District has provided a safe place for animals who have been abandoned, neglected, mistreated or left without anywhere else to go.

Each year, hundreds of cats and dogs receive veterinary care, rehabilitation and the chance to find loving new homes.

However, the charity’s existing buildings are increasingly struggling to meet modern animal welfare standards, making the redevelopment vital for the future of the Home.

Lucy added: “We are so pleased to see the puppy unit taking shape, but there is still work to do to reach our fundraising target for this building.

“Every contribution will help us create a warm, safe and specialist environment for puppies and their mothers.

“The puppy unit is one important part of our wider plans to transform RSPCA York, Harrogate and District and create facilities that will make a difference to animals for years to come.

“Please get in touch if you are a business that could support us, a community group looking for a cause to get behind, or someone who simply wants to help vulnerable puppies.

“Together, we can get this important building over the line.”
