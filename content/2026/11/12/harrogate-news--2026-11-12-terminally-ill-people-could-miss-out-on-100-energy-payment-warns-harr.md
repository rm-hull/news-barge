Terminally ill people could miss out on a £100 Government energy payment this winter because of differences in how councils administer council tax relief, Harrogate and Knaresborough MP Tom Gordon has warned.

The Liberal Democrat MP is calling on the Government to guarantee that people receiving discretionary council tax relief, including some terminally ill residents, are not excluded from the Household Energy Payment.

The payment was announced in the Autumn Budget and is intended to help households with rising energy costs. Households in council tax bands A to D in England are due to receive £100, with payments expected to begin from January 2027.

## Concerns over council tax relief

The payment is being administered through the council tax system, but Mr Gordon says the arrangements could leave some vulnerable residents uncertain about whether they qualify.

Some councils offer discretionary council tax relief to terminally ill residents, meaning they may pay little or no council tax. Other councils do not provide the same relief.

In response to a parliamentary question, ministers confirmed that students who are exempt from council tax and people receiving Local Council Tax Support will be eligible for the payment.

However, the Government has not given the same explicit assurance for people receiving discretionary council tax relief. Mr Gordon says this could create a postcode lottery, with eligibility depending on the approach taken by individual councils.

He has also raised concerns about how eligible residents who do not pay council tax by direct debit will receive the money. Some people receiving full council tax relief will have no direct debit arrangement through which a payment could be made.

## Government confirms some households will be excluded

Separate parliamentary answers have also clarified that some households will not qualify for the main payment.

In an answer to Mr Gordon on 10 November, Housing Minister Jim McMahon confirmed that the payment would be available to people living in properties in council tax bands A to D as their sole or main residence, provided the occupant is liable for council tax.

This means tenants in houses in multiple occupation (HMOs) whose landlords are responsible for the council tax will not qualify, even where tenants pay their own energy bills.

The Government has also confirmed that students living in halls of residence and purpose-built student accommodation outside the council tax system will not receive the payment.

In response to a further question from Mr Gordon, ministers said they had not estimated how many students in halls and purpose-built accommodation would be affected.

## £100 million fund for households missing out

The Government has announced a separate £100 million fund for local authorities in England to provide additional support to households most acutely affected by rising energy bills but not eligible for the main payment.

Ministers say local authorities are best placed to identify households in need of support and have said further guidance will be issued covering how the additional funding should be used.

However, Mr Gordon wants the guidance to make clear that terminally ill people receiving discretionary council tax relief must not be overlooked.

The wider Household Energy Payment forms part of a £2.6 billion Government scheme intended to provide £100 to eligible households across England. The Government estimates that up to 20 million households will receive the payment.

## MP raises concerns in Parliament

Mr Gordon raised the issue during Business Questions in the House of Commons on 12 November, urging the Leader of the House, Alan Campbell, to ensure terminally ill people receive the payment when it is distributed this winter.

Mr Campbell told him he would take away the points he had raised.

Mr Gordon said the Government could address the issue through its guidance to councils.

> **Tom Gordon, Liberal Democrat MP for Harrogate and Knaresborough:**
>
> Nobody facing a terminal illness should have to worry about whether they can afford to heat their home this winter, and whether they get help shouldn’t depend on their postcode.
>
> Council tax bands are a crude tool. They’re not even good at taxing people fairly, let alone getting support to the people who need it most.
>
> The Government can fix this with one line in its guidance to councils. I’ll keep pressing until it does.

## Questions remain over who will receive the payment

The Government has yet to publish its full operational guidance for councils, including the detailed arrangements for administering the payment and the additional discretionary fund.

That leaves questions over how people receiving discretionary council tax relief will be treated, how councils will identify eligible households without direct debit arrangements, and how the separate £100 million fund will be targeted.

For terminally ill residents and other vulnerable households, the distinction could determine whether they receive the additional help this winter.
