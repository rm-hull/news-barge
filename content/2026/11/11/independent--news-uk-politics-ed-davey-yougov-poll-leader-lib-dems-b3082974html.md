The majority of Britons do not recognise Britain’s longest serving party leader, Sir Ed Davey, even though he has led the Liberal Democrats for more than six years.

New YouGov polling has found only 37 per cent of voters recognised Sir Ed, raising further questions about the relevance of his party.

Respondents were showed photos of seven politicians, including leaders of five major nationwide parties, and asked to name them.

His recognisability was low even amongst Lib Dem voters, with only 49 per cent of the people who voted for the party in the 2024 general election being able to recognise Sir Ed from the photo.

Sir Ed - who has been at the helm of the Lib Dems since 2020 - recently survived a potential leadership challenge after reported discontent regarding the party’s lack of progress since the 2024 election, when it won a record 72 MPs.

![Sir Ed Davey is leader of the Lib Dems](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/13/12/2296576231..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The Liberal Democrats have made minimal polling progress since that vote, with a recent YouGov survey ranking the party at 12 per cent – around the same as its 2024 share.

Nigel Farage was the most widely recognised of the seven, with 89 per cent successfully identifying the Reform UK leader, whilst 83 per cent could recognise the prime minister, Andy Burnham.

Conservative leader Kemi Badenoch is recognised by two thirds of Britons, whilst Green Party leader Zack Polanski was recognised by 46 per cent of Britons, with this number rising to 64 per cent amongst 18-24-year-olds.

Just 28 per cent recognised chancellor John Healey, and 62 per cent said they didn't know who he was.

Lib Dem MP Charlie Maynard stepped down from his frontbench post last month, with reports indicating his resignation was designed to spark a vote of confidence in Sir Ed’s leadership.

Following an extended meeting of the parliamentary party in October, no ballot took place, allowing Sir Ed to stay in position.

In a survey released before the party’s conference in September, the Lib Dems were described as “weak” in YouGov polling by just more than half of those asked, and while the survey signalled the public largely sees the Lib Dems as moderate, 43 per cent said it is unclear what they stand for.

![Sir Ed - who has been at the helm of the Lib Dems since 2020 - recently survived a potential leadership challenge](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/09/20/12/eb8a098072ca9f055e1816f6c9bdfd38Y29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzg5OTg5MzQ4-2.85902485.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

It noted the Lib Dems have trailed other parties in voting intention polls for most of the last year and have seen “no real growth in support since the last general election”.

Deputy leader Daisy Cooper ruled out a future bid for the Liberal Democrat leadership and backed Sir Ed last month.

She insisted the party was going “from strength to strength” under Sir Ed despite reports of internal frustrations about its lack of progress since the 2024 election that saw it secure a record 72 MPs.

Asked about MPs losing faith in Sir Ed as the party lags behind Labour, the Tories and Reform UK in the polls, Ms Cooper told a press conference: “Ed is our most successful leader ever in the history of our party.

“And the last general election proved that we have been underestimated time and time and time again… for as long as we have first-past-the-post, the national polls do not translate into winning seats, and we have proven that under Ed Davey’s leadership we can transfer votes into seats like no other political party.”
