Planned industrial action by hospital consultants in England on 25 and 26 November has been paused after the British Medical Association (BMA) agreed to enter formal negotiations with the Government.

The announcement, made on Wednesday 11 November, means the planned two-day walkout will not currently go ahead.

The BMA said the decision followed meetings between its consultants’ committee and Health Secretary Yvette Cooper, with the union agreeing to suspend industrial action while discussions take place.

The dispute centres on consultant pay and working conditions. The BMA says consultants have experienced a 25% reduction in their pay in real terms over the past 17 years, alongside increasingly unsustainable workloads.

Dr Helen Neary and Dr Shanu Datta, co-chairs of the BMA consultants committee, said:

> **Dr Helen Neary and Dr Shanu Datta, BMA consultants committee co-chairs:**
>
> We welcome the opportunity to enter formal negotiations with the government and hope this marks a turning point in a dispute that has gone on for far too long.
>
> We’ve always said strikes do not need to go ahead as long as the government is willing to genuinely negotiate, and now is their chance to do so.
>
> We have received assurances that there are no constraints on what we discuss in these negotiations, and we look forward to hearing their proposals on both pay and working conditions.

The committee said the dispute was about more than pay, also raising concerns about the time, recognition and support consultants need to deliver patient care and avoid burnout.

The BMA warned that its willingness to pause industrial action would not continue indefinitely if negotiations failed to make progress.

The committee said further strike action could be called if discussions did not swiftly deliver on the issues at the heart of the dispute.

Health Secretary Yvette Cooper welcomed the decision, describing the discussions with the BMA as constructive and saying the Government would take forward work on reforms intended to give consultants a stronger voice, greater flexibility and more freedom to improve services.

**What this means for Harrogate patients**

Harrogate and District NHS Foundation Trust had published a notice on 10 November advising patients about planned consultant industrial action between 25 and 27 November.

That notice was issued before the BMA announced the pause.

Patients should follow any updated advice issued directly by the Trust. Anyone with an appointment should check communications from the hospital rather than assume that an appointment has been cancelled or changed.

The BMA’s decision pauses the planned November action while negotiations take place. It does not mean the underlying dispute has been resolved.
