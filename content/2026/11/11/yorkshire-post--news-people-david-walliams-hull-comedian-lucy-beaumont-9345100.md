# David Walliams: Hull comedian Lucy Beaumont reveals she was whistleblower in investigation

## Hull comedian Lucy Beaumont has revealed that she was one of the whistleblowers over the David Walliams allegations.

The 43-year-old said she raised concerns about Walliams after being told he was hosting teenage girls in his dressing room while working on a TV show and she was “worried” about whether he had behaved inappropriately.

The best-selling author and former Britain’s Got Talent judge, 55, was named as part of an investigation by The Guardian and ITV News on Tuesday, after he tried to obtain an injunction to prevent the reporting of allegations against him.

Walliams denies any wrongdoing. Beaumont discussed her response to the allegations involving Walliams’ while appearing on Sky News’ The Cathy Newman Show on Tuesday, saying she “wants to protect young women” in the TV industry.

“I don’t know Walliams, I haven’t worked with him but, bizarrely, it’s been a really strange day for me because, about three years ago, a young person – and it was their first job on TV – worked on a show he was on and the next show that they worked on was with me.

![David Walliams at the BFI London Film Festival](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/jpim-static/image/2026/11/11/14/cs-16787681-7e5c-4f33-bb86-aad46d37f853.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


“They took me aside and said, ‘I need to tell somebody something’ and confided in me.

“Some of (Walliams’) conduct that they were really worried about.”

Beaumont said the young person told her they had to “take young girls” who were “definitely” over 16 to Walliams’ dressing room, adding: “He felt they were vulnerable.”

She added: “At the time, he didn’t think there was a process, a way to let people know within the TV company what was happening and how he felt and I was really worried.”

The Hull-born star added: “I did as much as I could do privately.

“Whenever I’ve heard a rumour about anyone, I’ve done everything I can.

“There’s only so much you can do. I’m not the police. Nothing seems to be illegal.

“But I was really worried, and I was one of the whistleblowers. I was one of the people to let The Guardian know.”

![Lucy Beaumont was born and raised in Hull. Photo: Debbie Ellis](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOjBmNmMzYjNjLThhMDMtNGMzZS1iYTM0LWVmMWI4MWY3NTYxZToxZDIyYzFmMS00N2U3LTQ2OTctOTMzYi1kYjZhZDc4NDA3ZDg=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


Speaking about the TV industry, she added: “There’s no proper duty of care.

“There’s no social services that you deal with and it’s partly because the industry is very well-managed.”

She added: “These things slip through the net because, I think there will be now, but there hasn’t been a process that you can deal with.”

Beaumont said she had never worked with Walliams or witnessed any inappropriate behaviour from him but was “worried that these girls were young”.

![Comedian Lucy Beaumont. Photo: Debbie Ellis](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOmM4OWM4ZjkyLWM3ZmUtNDY4Yy04ZjVlLTcwOWFiYWM0YTJmMzo1ZDg0OTBiZi03MDNlLTRhMjEtODliZS0zOGY0NDhiNjg2ZjI=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


The comic said she reached out to an alleged victim who was “in a really bad way” and was worried about her well-being but said the woman “declined my offer of support”.

She said she did not know if the allegations were true but had wanted to help.

Beaumont said she believed speaking out about her concerns might cost her TV work: “The reason high-profile people are not saying anything is because they lose work, because you’re seen that you might be a trouble causer.

“It does need to be said that there needs to be some intervention.”

Beaumont started her career as a comedian in 2009 and is a vocal critic of misogyny, sexism, and inappropriate male behaviour on the UK comedy circuit and has also spoken about the impact this had on her career.

She has previously discussed “predatory” male behaviour in her industry and said people did not believe the women who were speaking up.
