Mischievous troll-like characters are taking over a popular York attraction this festive season.

Before an elf ever appeared on a shelf, an infamous rabble of Nordic troll-like characters used to monitor the behaviour of children to decide if they were worthy of receiving gifts at Yule – and this winter, they have decided to take up residence at JORVIK Viking Centre as part of this year’s Winter Adventure.

The Yule Lads tradition comes from Icelandic troll folklore, and comprise 13 brothers who visit children in the nights running up to Yule – the Norse winter festival.

Stories of the Yule Lads told around the winter fire were originally used to discourage children from heading into forests and mountains during the winter months when those places were enticing but particularly dangerous.

Over time, their reputation mellowed and visits by the Yule Lads were used to encourage children to behave well during all the excitement ahead of Christmas. Good children who leave a shoe on the windowsill are left a small gift, but naughty children instead receive a potato – a tradition that continues today.

“We’re both excited and nervous to be hosting the Yule Lads. They are our guests, so naturally we’re making them welcome, but they do have several centuries of mischief behind them,” said head of operations, Mark Jackson.

“With names like Spoon-Licker, Sausage-Swiper, Doorway-Sniffer and Meat-Hook, we’re not entirely sure what we’ll find when we unlock the doors each morning!”

The Yule Lads are a fun addition to this year’s Winter MisAdventure at JORVIK Viking Centre, running until 3 January 2027.

Continuing JORVIK’s winter tradition, the re-creation of Viking-age Coppergate has been transformed from Spring to winter, with a seasonal covering of snow all around the houses, the population wrapped up warmer against the cold, and icicles hanging from Bogar’s infamous toilet enclosure!

“We couldn’t squeeze all 13 into Coppergate at once, but we’ve found room for Meat-Hook, Pot-Licker, Stubby and Sheep-Stealer in the first instance,” adds Mark. “Whether they behave themselves is another matter entirely.”

Children visiting JORVIK during the Winter Misadventure can claim a free activity book, complete with games, puzzles and stories of how the Yule Lads caused chaos around the city.

JORVIK Viking Centre is open daily from 10am to 5pm (opening earlier from 9am from 19 December, and closed 24-26 December).

The attraction will close for a week from 4 January as work is undertaken on the Gallery ahead of the 2027 visitor season.

Tickets are £18.50 for adults, £12.50 for children and £15 for students on the door, with discounted rates of £17.50, £12 and £14 for prebooked visits (at least the day before). Prebooking is strongly recommended as JORVIK is regularly fully booked.
