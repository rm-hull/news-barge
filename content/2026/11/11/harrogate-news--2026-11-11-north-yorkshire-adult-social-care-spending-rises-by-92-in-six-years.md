Spending on adult social care in North Yorkshire has risen to more than £322 million a year, against a backdrop of growing national concerns about the organisation and long-term future of the care system.

Figures provided to the Harrogate Informer show that North Yorkshire’s net adult social care expenditure increased from approximately £168 million in 2019/20 to £322.3 million in 2025/26.

The figures represent an increase of around 92 per cent over six years.

The increase comes as Baroness Casey, chair of the Independent Commission on Adult Social Care, has warned that successive governments have been asleep at the wheel over the way England’s social care system is managed.

Research commissioned by the Casey Commission and carried out by the Nuffield Trust and the Institute for Government describes England as an international outlier, with no clear system stewardship for adult social care.

It compares England with countries including France, Germany and Japan, arguing that there is no single clear authority responsible for overseeing the system.

## North Yorkshire spending increases

The available figures show the progression in North Yorkshire’s net adult social care expenditure:

* 2019/20: approximately £168 million
* 2022/23: more than £200 million
* 2023/24: £253.9 million
* 2024/25: £303.9 million
* 2025/26: £322.3 million

The figures show a substantial increase in the council’s spending commitment, although they do not by themselves establish how much of the rise is attributable to increased demand, inflation, provider costs or changes in the level of care being delivered.

## National concerns over social care

The research published by the Independent Commission on Adult Social Care identifies several structural problems with England’s care system.

These include a sector dominated by private providers, differences in the support people receive depending on where they live, and significant variations in what people pay for care.

The research also highlights that people funding their own care pay, on average, 40 per cent more for the same service than people whose care is arranged by their local council.

England has more than 1.5 million social care workers across approximately 19,000 employers, but is the only country in the UK without a mandatory register of care workers.

> **Baroness Casey, chair of the Independent Commission on Adult Social Care:**
>
> Successive governments have been asleep at the wheel. In research we’re publishing today the lack of stewardship of social care comes through clearly. No one is in charge.
>
> Together, we can build a new settlement for care. One based on values I still believe this country shares: decency, contribution, and a duty to care for our neighbours, our loved ones and ourselves.

The Commission is expected to publish its final recommendations to Government in summer 2027.

Its work will consider what a National Care Service should look like, alongside recommendations on adult safeguarding and the treatment of conditions including dementia and motor neurone disease.
