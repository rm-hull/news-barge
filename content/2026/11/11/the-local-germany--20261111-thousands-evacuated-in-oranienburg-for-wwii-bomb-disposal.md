Around 6,300 people were ordered on Wednesday to leave the cordoned off area around a 250 kilogram unexploded bomb so it can be disposed of safely.

The bomb was discovered in the town, around 40 kilometres from Berlin, on Tuesday.

Checks to make sure that every person has left the zone were taking place on Wednesday morning, before work on disarming the bomb could begin. A drone had been deployed to survey the area.

Several roads have been closed in the town, in the Oberhavel district, and drivers have been urged to avoid the area.

The cordoned-off area includes Sachsenhausen railway station and its tracks. For this reason, regional services on the RE5 and RB12 lines between Oranienburg and Löwenberg are suspended until around 4pm. Deutsche Bahn is planning a rail replacement service for this period.

Tourists in Berlin and other areas who had planned to visit the former Sachsenhausen concentration camp – now a museum and memorial site – may be affected by the travel disruption.

The exclusion zone also includes schools, nurseries, sports facilities, the Schlosshafen with its associated caravan site, and parts of the Schlosspark. In addition, some shops on Bernauer Straße must remain closed. Bus services within Oranienburg are also restricted.

Officials said that from 1pm, the City Library on Schlossplatz will be available as a temporary drop-in centre for residents – however, it will not be possible to borrow books.

**Unexploded bombs a regular occurrence**

Bombs dating back to the Second World War are regularly discovered in Germany.

Careful operations involving highly skilled experts are needed to disarm the bombs safely.

They often result in large-scale evacuations as a precaution.

In June this year, around 6,500 people were evacuated from their homes in Potsdam after a 250 kg bomb was found. And in August, a number of people were asked to leave their homes in Berlin due to a bomb find.

Meanwhile, around 17,000 people were evacuated from Dresden in August 2025 for a similar defusal, and around 20,000 people were evacuated from central Cologne in June last year.
