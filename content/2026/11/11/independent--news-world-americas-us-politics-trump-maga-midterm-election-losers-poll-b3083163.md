Americans view President Donald Trump and the MAGA movement as the biggest losers of the midterm elections, according to a new poll.

In the latest *Politico* survey, which sampled 2,000 adults, 62 percent of respondents considered Trump the loser of the midterms, while 59 percent said the same of MAGA.

Another 51 percent viewed moderate Republicans as losers, followed by Elon Musk at 48 percent. Musk poured nearly $500 million into GOP campaigns, only to see Democrats notch victories in a string of red states and reclaim both the House and Senate.

Far fewer respondents counted working-class Americans (22 percent), moderate Democrats (14 percent) or progressive Democrats (10 percent) among the election’s losers.

Even Trump’s most ardent supporters offered a bleak assessment. Among self-identified MAGA Trump voters, 45 percent described the MAGA movement as the loser of the midterms, compared with 43 percent who named moderate Republicans and 42 percent who pointed to working-class Americans.

![American voters view President Donald Trump and his MAGA movement as the biggest losers of the midterm elections, according to a new poll](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/11/13/55/GettyImages-2303040364.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Fewer MAGA voters placed Trump himself in that category: 34 percent, the same share who named Musk.

The survey, conducted November 7-10, comes roughly a week after Election Day, when a blue wave swept the nation amid voter frustration over the cost of living, the Iran war and corruption.

As of Wednesday, Democrats are projected to win at least 54 Senate seats to Republicans’ 44 while easily claiming a majority in the House of Representatives. Democrats are expected to pick up 255 House seats to the GOP’s 167, with several races still undecided.

The new Congress will be sworn in in January. Democratic leaders have pledged to pursue an agenda focused on lowering costs, challenging Trump over the Iran war and tariffs, investigating corruption and moving to impeach Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth.

![Democrats are projected to win 54 Senate seats to Republicans’ 44, and 255 House seats to the GOP’s 167. Pictured: Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer poses for a photo with newly elected Democratic Senators](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/10/16/2304338111..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Trump has signaled a willingness to work with Democrats on some issues, telling *60 Minutes* that he would “love to do something on immigration.”

But his appointment of Stephen Miller, the architect of his hardline immigration agenda, as White House chief of staff signals the administration could take a more combative approach to dealings with Congress over the next two years.

Trump has also changed his tone on the elections in recent days.

After initially congratulating Democrats and blaming lackluster Republican candidates for his party’s losses, he questioned the integrity of the results on Monday, wondering aloud whether the elections were “honest.”
