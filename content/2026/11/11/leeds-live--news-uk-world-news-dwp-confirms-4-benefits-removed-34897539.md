# DWP confirms 4 benefits 'removed' from annual payment rates review

## The Department for Work and Pensions has confirmed the new benefit rates for April 2027 onwards in a Commons statement by Labour Party cabinet member Pat McFadden

The Department for Work and Pensions has announced that four benefit payments will be excluded from the annual uprating review.

The DWP has outlined the revised benefit rates due to come into effect from April 2027 onwards in a Commons statement delivered by Labour cabinet minister Pat McFadden. However, four specific rates - two relating to Universal Credit and two concerning Employment and Support Allowance (ESA) - have been left out, reports Birmingham Live.

Mr McFadden explained: "The Universal Credit Act 2025 removed the standard allowance and health elements of Universal Credit, as well as their Employment and Support Allowance equivalents, from my review. The Act delivered important changes to rebalance Universal Credit to tackle perverse incentives in the system. The UC Standard Allowance for a single person aged 25 or over will increase by around £219 a year."

That's approximately £71 more than if up-rated by inflation alone. These changes narrow the gap between the standard rate of Universal Credit and the health-related element, helping to strengthen incentives to move into and progress in work."

He continued: "The majority of these increases will apply across Great Britain, except for Personal Independence Payment and other benefits to help with additional needs arising from disability, and the rate of Carer's Allowance, which will only apply to England and Wales. In Scotland, these are devolved matters.

"All social security, including State Pensions, is a transferred matter in Northern Ireland. While not part of my formal up-rating review, I can confirm that Local Housing Allowance rates and the benefit cap will be maintained at their current levels and not increased for 2027-28.

"I will place the full list of proposed State Pension and benefit rates for 2027-28 in the Libraries of both Houses and on GOV.UK in due course."
