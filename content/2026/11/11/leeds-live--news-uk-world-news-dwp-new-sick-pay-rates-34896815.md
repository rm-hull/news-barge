# DWP new sick pay rates for millions pulling 'sickies' from April 2027

## The Department for Work and Pensions has confirmed new statutory sick pay rates coming into force from April 2027

The Department for Work and Pensions has announced updated statutory sick pay rates in changes affecting anyone taking time off due to illness. The DWP has outlined modifications coming into effect from April 2027.

From next spring onwards, the standard sick pay rate will increase from £123.25 to £127.55, according to the DWP's confirmation. Employees who are too unwell to work can receive up to £127.55 weekly, reports Birmingham Live.

Your employer provides this payment for a maximum period of 28 weeks. The statutory minimum cannot be reduced. However, you may receive a higher amount if your employer operates a sick pay scheme (or 'occupational scheme') - this information should be in your employment contract, under Labour Party government regulations.

According to the DWP: "You'll be paid SSP for all the full days you're off sick that you normally would have worked.. Your employer will use your average weekly earnings over an 8-week period to work out much you get. You still qualify if you started your job recently and have not yet received 8 weeks' pay. Ask your employer to find out more."

Regarding payment methods, the DWP explains: "SSP is paid by your employer in the same way as your normal wages, for example weekly or monthly. If you have more than one job, you may get SSP from each employer. Tax and National Insurance will be deducted.

"If you think you're not getting the right amount of SSP, talk to your employer. If you're still not happy, contact the HM Revenue and Customs (HMRC) enquiry line." To be eligible for Statutory Sick Pay (SSP), you need to be classified as an employee and have carried out some work for your employer, while also having been unwell for a minimum of one complete working day.

You're typically required to inform your employer of your inability to work before their specified deadline (or within seven days if no deadline has been established). Failing to notify your employer promptly could result in a reduction to your SSP entitlement.
