LNER has apologised and launched an investigation after disruption on the East Coast Main Line caused ‘absolute carnage’ at York Station yesterday (Tuesday).

As ***YorkMix*** reported, the rail company issued a ‘do not travel’ warning overhead line damage near Northallerton blocked the line.

At one point, hundreds of people were queueing out of York Station for the rail replacement bus services.

Similar scenes were replicated along the East Coast Main Line.

LNER put out a message saying: “Due to the severity of the disruption, we cannot guarantee that we will be able to get you to your destination.

“Trains that are able to run are also expected to be extremely busy. Rail replacement is in place however these are also expected to be extremely busy.”

One traveller posted on X at 5.31pm yesterday: “Absolute carnage at York, not one and I repeat one member of staff anywhere! Why? And where are the buses? Not one. 700 people here at least now.”

Another wrote: “Absolutely terrible service today. Supposed to travel from York to Edinburgh. Had to get two coaches to Newcastle which took almost three hours and then another train. Ridiculous.”

The overhead lines have now been fixed and services are running normally again today.

Today Gunnar Lindahl LNER joint operations director, said: “I want to apologise for the disruption you’ve experienced over the last 24 hours.

“Yesterday afternoon, we saw damage to our overhead electric wires in the Northallerton area, which meant that we weren’t able to pass trains safely through that area for a number of hours.

“That disruption continued into the evening and affected the whole East Coast Main Line and also the wider North East.

“And I want to acknowledge that for those of you who were trying to travel with us yesterday, it was incredibly difficult.

“Our engineers have worked really hard through the night, and we have safely restored the infrastructure this morning and are running normally.

“We’re also working to understand what the root cause of the incident was. Again, finally, I just want to apologise to you for the disruption caused.”
