# Certain DWP state pensioners aged 67 to 79 forced to pay back £200 payment

## Some state pensioners aged 67 to 79 may need to repay their £200 Winter Fuel Payment to HMRC if their total income exceeds £35,000

State pensioners between the ages of 67 and 79 may find themselves required to repay their Winter Fuel Payment to the Department for Work and Pensions ( DWP ) and HMRC. Those born on or before 27 June 1960 are eligible to receive between £100 and £300 towards their heating costs for the 2026 to 2027 winter period.

However, individuals whose total income exceeds £35,000 will see HMRC reclaim their Winter Fuel Payment. For those living alone or sharing accommodation with others who don't qualify for the payment, £200 will be provided if you were born between 28 September 1946 and 27 June 1960, placing you in the 67 to 79 age bracket, reports Birmingham Live.

The DWP explains: "If your total income is over £35,000, HM Revenue and Customs (HMRC) will take your Winter Fuel Payment back. Your partner's income does not count towards your total income. You can check if your income is over the threshold.

"You'll need to provide the total amount of income you expect to receive in the tax year before any deductions. HMRC will usually collect the amount you owe through your tax code. If you complete a Self Assessment tax return, you'll pay it through your tax bill instead.

"You can opt out of receiving Winter Fuel Payment if your income is over £35,000 or you do not want to receive it. Should HMRC alter your tax code to reclaim your Winter Fuel Payment, they will notify you via email or post.

"You're required to declare your Winter Fuel Payment on your tax return for each year you receive it. For those filing tax returns online, HMRC typically populates this figure automatically.

It appears as the 'Winter Fuel Payment charge' and forms part of your overall tax calculation. Those using Making Tax Digital cannot settle their Winter Fuel Payment debt when submitting their tax return through compatible software.

Following submission of your tax return, HMRC will send written confirmation detailing the amount owed and providing instructions on how and when to make payment through HMRC's online payment services. It's important to await contact from HMRC before attempting to process the payment.
