More than 600 service children from across North Yorkshire gathered at Ripon Cathedral for the county’s annual Festival of Remembrance.

The ceremony, held on Monday 9 November, brought together children and young people from 29 schools to remember members of the Armed Forces who have paid the ultimate sacrifice and celebrate the contribution of children from military families.

The event, now in its 14th year, was organised by North Yorkshire Council’s service children’s champion, Angela Campbell.

This year’s theme was **Belonging**, reflecting the shared experiences, resilience and connections that unite service children despite the challenges that can come with military life.

Children’s artwork featured prominently through a *You Belong Here* exhibition, displaying self-portraits created by 355 service pupils from 25 schools across North Yorkshire.

The exhibition represented around one in 10 of the county’s service child population and aimed to celebrate individuality while highlighting the common experiences that connect military children.

Pupils from Carnagill Primary School also contributed poems exploring the theme of belonging.

## Recognising the experiences of service children

North Yorkshire Council’s executive member for children and families, Cllr Janet Sanderson, attended the service.

> **Cllr Janet Sanderson, executive member for children and families:**
>
> These services are not only a poignant act of remembrance, but a beautiful celebration of the identity and achievements of the military children whose families make such an important contribution to our county.
>
> Service children can face challenges as part of military family life, whether that’s moving schools, making new friends or coping with the deployment of loved ones. This festival gives them the opportunity to come together, reflect, celebrate their experiences and recognise that they belong to a wider community that understands and supports them.

Highlights included musicians from Ripon Grammar School accompanying the hymns, while young members of the Service Children’s Community Choir performed a choral rendition of *If You Hadn’t Been There* by the late Dolly Parton.

North Yorkshire Council’s Armed Forces Champion, Cllr Kevin Foster, who served as a military reservist for 30 years, supported the choir with a £450 donation from his locality budget.

> **Cllr Kevin Foster, North Yorkshire Council’s Armed Forces Champion:**
>
> This service celebrates the contribution that children connected to military families are making in the cause of our nation’s defence, global stability and peace.
>
> It offers a space for reflection and pride recognising not only the sacrifices of military families but also the strength and contributions of service children themselves.

Among those attending were senior representatives from North Yorkshire Council, including chief executive Richard Flinton, military leaders, civic dignitaries and education partners. Guests also included Deputy Lord Lieutenant for North Yorkshire Countess Peel and the Dean of Ripon, the Very Rev John Dobson.

North Yorkshire Council’s Young People’s Champion, Cllr Alyson Baker, also attended.

> **Cllr Alyson Baker, North Yorkshire Council’s Young People’s Champion:**
>
> It was heartwarming to watch and listen to hundreds of school pupils from our county who came under one roof to take part in the poignant remembrance service.
>
> This special service marks a key highlight in the annual calendar, and I would sincerely like to thank everyone involved.
