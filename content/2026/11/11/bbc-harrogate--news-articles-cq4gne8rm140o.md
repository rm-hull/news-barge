**Services have been held across Yorkshire as communities came together to mark Armistice Day.**

A two-minute silence was held across the UK at 11:00 GMT, to commemorate the exact time an agreement to end the fighting of World War One as a prelude to peace negotiations began on 11 November 1918.

People paused at memorials, churches and civic ceremonies across Yorkshire to mark the 109th anniversary of the end of hostilities.

In North Yorkshire, council leader Carl Les said commemorations were still hugely significant, adding: "With conflicts still very evident across the world, it is as important today as ever to continue the tradition of commemorating all those who have lost their lives in wars."

A North Yorkshire Council spokesperson said the county had a long history associated with the armed forces and that there were "10,770 military personnel in the county - one of the largest numbers nationally".

![Crowds of people stood around graveyards outside. There is a wooden bench in the foreground. ](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/e6ad/live/472aa0d0-ddd3-11f1-9053-236ccb99630c.png)

Silence was observed at various services which took place across North Yorkshire

North Yorkshire is also home to Catterick Garrison, which is one of the largest military bases in the country.

Councillor Kevin Foster, the authority's armed forces champion, said the day was "a moment to reflect on the past".

"But it is also important for us to say thank you to all those who are involved in the military as they continue such a proud and important role in protecting the country," he added.

![A man dressed in a suite with military badges on his jacket and holding a military flag.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/800/cpsprodpb/2f2a/live/57ed5890-ddce-11f1-9053-236ccb99630c.png)

Bradford councillor Chris Howlett said it was a "very emotional day"

In Bradford, councillor Chris Howlett, the authority's armed forces champion, said that to see people turn out pay their respects "means so much".

He added: "We can't forget. We must continue remembering."

Howlett said that having served in the Army with the Prince of Wales's Own Regiment of Yorkshire, the day's events were "very personal" for him.

"My granddad was First World War, my dad the Second World War, and me and my brother were in the Army," he explained.

"My son also is still serving in the RAF, so today will be extremely emotional."

Leaders and dignitaries were joined by members of the public for a service at the Victoria Gardens War Memorial in Leeds, where wreaths were laid.

![Wreaths laid at the foot of the cenotaph in Leeds ](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/d27c/live/19615580-dde3-11f1-9cf4-b188db5b2d8c.jpg)

Wreaths were laid at the Victoria Gardens War Memorial in Leeds city centre

The Lord Mayor of Leeds, councillor Stephen Holroyd, laid a wreath alongside Lord-Lieutenant of West Yorkshire Prof Adeeba Malik and Mayor of West Yorkshire Tracy Brabin.

Councillor James Lewis, Leeds City Council leader, and the council's chief executive Ed Whiting also attended.

![Several poppy wreaths on the ground, one with a sign saying 'Lest we forget'.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/dea0/live/f0360050-ddc6-11f1-9053-236ccb99630c.png)

Wreaths were also laid during a service at Barker's Pool in Sheffield

In Sheffield, dozens of people - some accompanied by pets - turned out to pay their respects during a service at Barker's Pool, where wreaths were laid at the foot of the Sheffield War Memorial.

Veterans Gary Crossland and Alan Snowden were among those to attend.

Crossland, 68, who joined the Royal Navy in 1974, said while laying his wreath, he was remembering his father who also served in the navy.

"I was also thinking of the guys at sea that are deep down there lost at sea, not just English but other countries which have lost submariners and men and women," he said.

![Two small dogs with red poppy attachments round on collars round their necks. ](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/9119/live/f0494f50-ddc8-11f1-9053-236ccb99630c.png)

Watson and Bram, complete with poppies, were taken by owner Lenore to an Armistice Day service in Sheffield

Another attendee was Lenore, who served for 12 years in the The Royal Corps of Signals and who brought along her two dogs Watson and Bram.

"I've served three tours of Afghanistan and seen people injured," she said.

"I've seen people loaded onto aeroplanes to go back to their families with the Union Jack, or whatever country they were from.

"It's always very poignant, those memories, for me at this time of year."

At Parson Cross C of E Primary School in Sheffield, children lined up in the playground at 11:00 as a teacher played the Last Post.

Pupils, holding poppies, wreaths and posters they had made, observed two minutes' silence to remember those who have lost their lives during military service.
