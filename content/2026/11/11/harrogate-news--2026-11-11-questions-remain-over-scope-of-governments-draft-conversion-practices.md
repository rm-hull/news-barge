The Government’s Draft Conversion Practices Bill has raised questions about how the proposed legislation will distinguish abusive conversion practices from legitimate discussions about identity, family beliefs and professional support.

Among the issues requiring clarification are whether parents will be able to discuss their beliefs with their children, how teachers and religious leaders can offer advice, and where the boundaries will lie for therapists and other professionals supporting people exploring their identity.

Tom Gordon MP has added his views to the debate, expressing concern about the wording of the proposed legislation and the possibility of loopholes.

## Concerns over the definition of conversion practices

Gordon is concerned about the use of the term “abusive” conversion practices, arguing that it could imply that non-abusive forms of conversion practice exist.

He has raised the issue in Parliament, asking the Minister what steps are being taken to close potential loopholes in the proposed legislation.

Gordon also considers that parents and guardians should remain able to talk to their children, share their beliefs and values, and disagree with them. He considers these ordinary family discussions distinct from attempts to change or suppress someone’s identity.

## Counselling and professional support

Gordon has also addressed the implications for counselling and psychological support.

His position is that ethical, non-directive counselling and properly informed medical and psychological support should remain possible, allowing people to explore their identity without pressure towards a particular outcome.

He draws a distinction between this type of support and therapy explicitly designed to stop someone being LGBT+.

## Legislation still subject to development

Gordon acknowledges that the Draft Conversion Practices Bill remains subject to further development and parliamentary debate. He said this makes it difficult to give categorical answers about what would fall within its definitions in its current form.

The precise scope of the proposed legislation, including the protections for legitimate conversations and professional support, will depend on its final wording and the safeguards included as it progresses through Parliament.
