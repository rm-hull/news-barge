Thousands of incidents of fly-tipping in York have sparked a row amid fears the problem is growing and calls for action.

There were 3,788 incidents in 2025/6, up from 2,954 the year before – a near 25% increase.

Liberal Democrat Cllr Andrew Waller says the Labour administration on City of York Council needs to lay out what it’s doing to tackle the problem

Cllr Waller said he raised the issue with Labour’s Cllr Kent at a council meeting which received an update on public realm management last Tuesday (3 November).

His party said they would introduce community skips and tidy-up vans in fly-tipping hotspots to make it easier for people to get rid of their unwanted items.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/york-neighbourhood-caretakers-with-cllr-jenny-kent.webp)

Cllr Waller, who represents Westfield ward, said: “It is incredibly disappointing that Labour had nothing to say about the growing problem of fly-tipping in York.

“We asked some very straightforward questions at the meeting. What support is being provided to help residents dispose of their waste properly?

“Why aren’t we making greater use of CCTV in known hotspots? And why aren’t we building on successful approaches like the one at Acomb Morrisons?

“These are real problems affecting neighbourhoods across York, and residents deserve answers about what the council is doing to tackle them.”

Cllr Jenny Kent, the council’s environment spokesperson, said there was never an excuse for fly-tipping.

She said they have been looking at dealing with fly-tipping including through bulky waste collections for low-income households or those without a car.

She added the council’s tidy-up vans worked hard to respond to incidents quickly, including in Westfield.

Cllr Kent said: “Sadly, fly-tipping is on the rise nationally, it blights our environment, harms wildlife and spoils our streets.

“We don’t think community skips are the answer, as they don’t address the needs of those without a car, usually cited as the main reason people cannot visit the tip, and often result in additional fly-tipped waste that the council has to pay to remove.

“We reviewed the types of items being dumped in our streets and much of it is black bags of household rubbish left on verges and in back alleys.

“There’s no excuse for this when everyone has a fortnightly waste collection, I urge people to report others who they know or see committing this criminal offence.”
