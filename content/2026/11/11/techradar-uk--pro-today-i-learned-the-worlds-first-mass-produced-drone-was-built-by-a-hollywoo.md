The first military drone built in serious numbers didn't come from a defense contractor or tech giant - but from Reginald Denny, a British-born silent film actor who sold model aircraft on Hollywood Boulevard.

His Radioplane OQ-2 was the first mass-produced drone in the United States - and by the end of World War II, his company had delivered nearly 15,000 target drones to the US Army according to the Smithsonian's National Air and Space Museum.

## From film sets to model planes

Denny, whose real name was Reginald Leigh Dugmore, starred in nearly two hundred films over a half-century period, including both silent films and talkies. Apart from being a professional actor, he was also part of a Hollywood stunt pilot group called Thirteen Black Cats.

While he never saw aerial combat due to a training injury, Denny trained for the Royal Flying Corps in World War I as an observer and air gunner. His fascination for aircraft led him to open a hobby shop on Hollywood Boulevard in 1935, which he sold model airplanes and miniature engines.

In 1936, he developed his first radio-controlled prototype. The RP-1 had a nine-foot wingspan, ran on toy train motors, and used a simple rotary telephone dial to encode radio signals. He gave his first demonstration before the US Army in 1938.

After a few improvements, like swapping the homemade parts and replacing the telephone dial with a joystick, Denny got a commission from the Army in 1939 to build 53 of these aircrafts, which were assigned the name OQ-1.

## Targets that didn't mind being shot at

During the World War II period, the military was mostly using giant fabric tubes fastened on to tow planes for anti-aircraft target practice.

But this approach had too many restrictions for realistic combat training, like unrealistically slow flight speeds and the inability to perform evasive maneuvers like a real military aircraft.

Denny argued that a radio-controlled model plane would serve as a much better training dummy for target practice, while also de-risking the lives of military personnel responsible for piloting these tow planes, who operated under constant threat of friendly fire.

## First mass-produced military drone

After the initial OQ-1 order, Denny built an even more improved model which the Army designated OQ-2. Shortly after that, manufacturing was moved from Denny's home to a proper factory in Van Nuys, California.

Denny formed the Radioplane Company, which supplied a total of 14,891 target drones throughout the course of World War II. This included the heavily mass-produced OQ-3 and a custom version built for the US Navy, which was dubbed the TDD-1 (Target Drone Denny).

Denny ultimately sold his interest in the company in 1948.
