# 'Disgusted' residents open up on living in Leeds fly-tipping hotspot

## Residents spoke about fly-tippers dumping rubbish on the streets

“Disgusted” Leeds residents have spoken about living in a fly-tipping hotspot. Residents living in the Seaforth Road area, near Harehills Lane, have spoken of seeing new rubbish pile up in the streets every day.

They spoke about fly-tippers dumping rubbish on the streets and building refuse in their wheelie bins, so the bins can’t be collected.

Leeds City Council has said it has seized vehicles, issued fixed penalty notices and have seven prosecution cases progressing in the last month. The council also works with vulnerable adults to connect them with “appropriate support”.

Sam, 30, who is unemployed, believes the issue comes down to a lack of education in the neighbourhood. Sam said: “It’s disgusting. People think they can leave it on the side of the street for the council to pick up but I don’t think they know, that’s fly-tipping.

“The council should come round more often. I know they come around now and again, but that’s been there for about 5 days. It sets a bad example to young people, it makes them think they can litter and put stuff where they want.”

Wilma has lived in the area for about five years. She said new rubbish appears on the streets every day. Wilma said: “I feel disgusted. Coming from the Philippines, I didn’t expect it to be like this. I feel like people living here should be educated to respect the surrounding areas, as well.”

John, who has also lived in the area for about five years, spoke about flytippers targeting their bins. John said: “Currently, we’re have issues with the bins. Suddenly, someone just filled out bins with refuse, and they weren’t collected.”

Leeds City Council says it encourages residents to make fly-tipping reports takes action wherever possible. A council spokesperson said: “Our officers have carried out extensive work within the Harehills community and alongside the regular removal of fly-tipped waste, we continue to undertake targeted enforcement and partnership activity across Harehills to tackle environmental crime and improve neighbourhood conditions.

“Recent activity in the area has included the seizure of two vehicles linked to environmental offences, the issuing of two Fixed Penalty Notices for fly-tipping and seven prosecution cases progressing over the last month, covering a range of environmental offences.”

They added the council will use all legal powers at their disposal, and works closely with Serious Environmental Crime Team, police and the Environment Agency “to target what is often organized, criminal activity”. The council also works with Adult Social Care to help vulnerable adults.
