# Student who left woman in 'fear for her life' during Hyde Park rape learns fate

## Uvesh Vahora, 36, has been sentenced

An international student has been jailed for nine years after raping a woman in Hyde Park, Leeds.

Uvesh Vahora, 36, of no fixed abode, was sentenced at Leeds Crown Court on Wednesday, November 11. He was also ordered to register as a sex offender indefinitely.

He had been found guilty of rape on November 3 after a trial at the same court.

Vahora raped a woman at a property on Autumn Avenue in Hyde Park on the night of Thursday, April 9. He was living at the address at the time. The court heard he was in the country as a student.

The victim told friends what had happened and then contacted police. She was able to identify the property where the offence took place, and Vahora was arrested within around five hours. He has been held on remand since.

Speaking after the conviction, West Yorkshire Police Detective Inspector Tom Crosfill said: "Vahora has subjected the victim to a terrifying experience with her being in genuine fear for her life thinking that he was going to kill her. After disclosing the offence to friends, she contacted police with officers arresting Vahora within around five hours of this offence taking place.

"He has been held on remand since that time. In the meantime, those working on this investigation have gathered the evidence including victim and witness statements, CCTV and digital lines of enquiry that has led to Vahora’s conviction.

"I understand how incredibly difficult it can be to report rape and other sexual offences and I really hope that this conviction demonstrates how seriously an offence of this nature is treated from the outset. Our message to victims is that you will be listened to, you will be supported and we will do everything possible to bring perpetrators before the courts."

Following the sentencing, victim has released a statement via the police said. She said: "What I find particularly difficult is that I am the person who has been left carrying all of this. I am the person who has to live with the memories, the discomfort, the fear, the changes to my confidence and the damage to the way I see myself.

"I never chose any of this. I never chose to have my sense of safety, my trust in people or my relationship with my own body taken away.

"I hope that by speaking out, I can show others who have experienced something similar that their voices matter, that they deserve to be heard and believed, and that seeking justice is worthwhile. I hope anyone else in a similar position finds the strength to speak up and gets the justice they deserve."
