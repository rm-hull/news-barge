A motion to tackle congestion in York, by reopening certain roads, has failed to get approval at the city council meeting.

A motion from York Council’s Conservative group calling for a consultation on four streets in The Groves being opened up to cut queues has been rejected. There were a number of local residents who came to speak against it.

**Cllr Martin Rowley**, who put the motion forward, said councillors should set aside their political differences and back action to deal with congestion and pollution.** He told councillors:** “I have to emphasise, we are calling for consultation, not the roads to be open tomorrow.

“Consultation with all parties, residents, and agencies on opening these roads should be supported by all members with officers tasked to undertake genuine feasibility studies and accurate modelling to find a scheme that works.”

**Labour’s Cllr Dave Merrett**, who represents Guildhall which covers the area, said the measures would damage the environment and public health and affect residential parking.

He said: “Councillor Rowley’s motion has caused massive upset and concern. Even if one agreed with this motion, which I certainly don’t, fundamentally, it will not solve the biggest problem in the area, which is the regular queuing back from the car park in the hospital grounds.”

“It needs the problem at the hospital to be solved first and foremost.”

NHS York and Scarborough Trust officials said in May they were looking at ways to curb queues from York Hospital’s entrance back along Wigginton Road.

The Conservative motion stated prolonged gridlock in Monkgate, Wigginton Road and Lord Mayor’s Walk was causing unnecessary delays. It added exhaust fumes from idling cars was also harming air quality.

The motion proposed opening **Vyner Street** to one-way traffic from Wigginton Road to Haxby Road with a 20mph limit to curb congestion at the Crighton Avenue junction.

It also called for **Fountayne Avenue** to be opened one-way with a 20mph limit from Haxby Road to Wigginton Road to alleviate congestion heading out of York on the latter.

Opening **Penley’s Grove Street** and**Lowther Street** one-way with a 20mph limit to tackle queues in Monkgate and Lord Mayor’s Walk was also called for.

The motion stated previous decisions to close the streets to traffic and to widen footpaths and create a cycle lane in **Wigginton Road** had contributed to the gridlock.

It added other benefits could include a boost to trade for local businesses and curbing antisocial behaviour in the streets reopened to traffic.

Conservative Cllr Rowley said the proposals were common sense.

**Local Democracy Campaigner, Gwen Swinburn,** spoke against the idea: “Parts of the Groves have the worst health outcomes in York, and almost the entire neighbourhood is amongst the worst for living environment conditions.

“Since the LTN (Low Traffic Neighbourhood), our community has flourished. Residents have created gardens, maintained street planters, and organised clean-ups, making the estate cleaner, greener, and a welcoming place.

“Neighbours from all backgrounds mix and look out for one another. Door 84, growing churches, and community activities add to the sense of belonging.

“Our council estate is a community, not a bypass. Councillor Rowley, this ill-considered performative motion has caused real anger and distress amongst our residents.”

**Resident Andrea Robinson said:** “Before the changes were introduced, the Groves was used as a rat-run by commuters travelling across York. Those of us who lived here experienced the reality of that every day.

“Our streets are quieter, traffic levels have fallen, and importantly, the Groves feels much more like a residential community now.

“We should not be asked to give those benefits up simply to provide a more convenient route for people travelling through our community.”

**Another resident, Anne Parker, said:** “I have been resident on Viner Street almost all my life. It has been closed to through traffic for three decades, but I can still remember when it was open. It’s a narrow residential street with cars parked either way up in each side.

“Since the closure to through traffic, the residents have been able to walk, cycle, and move around more freely than safely than before, and this will threaten their quality of life.

“What I and councillors really agree on is that the problem of congestion in York needs to be addressed. However, a motion that wants to turn our residential streets into rat-runs is problematic and ill thought out and not appropriate.”

**Claire Ainsley said:** “The City of York Council did a great thing when it closed the Groves to traffic and it wasn’t an easy thing to do. It was quite controversial at the time. There was quite a lot of local opposition, but it was a brave thing to do.”

“The biggest issue is safety for children. We’ve got the big Park Grove Community School right in the middle. We’ve got St Wilfred, and we’ve got children crossing for the after-school clubs, and you would just have cars whooshing up that road, ignoring the safety bumps.

“And if you want to bring that back, I think that will be a hugely detrimental move.”
