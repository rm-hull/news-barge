# Leeds cocaine gang jailed after cops find £3.8m stash and £100k in cash

## A sixth man was jailed for a separate cocaine supply offence

Five people have been jailed following an investigation into a Leeds-based drug gang.

West Yorkshire Police said they recovered 45kg of cocaine with a potential street value of £3.8 million when they moved to arrest key members of the gang in June and August 2026. They also recovered 25kg of ketamine and more than £100,000 in cash.

The gang, based in Leeds, had been supplying cocaine to other organised criminal groups across the UK between January and August. The five men pleaded guilty to conspiracy to supply cocaine and were all sentenced at Leeds Crown Court on November 10.

Liam Johnson, 29 and of no fixed address, and Arwel Edmunds, of Cross Green Lane, Leeds, were each jailed for 11-years-and-three-months. Talib Khan, 40 and of Thornhill Street, Wakefield, and Peter Ward, of Sycamore Green, Pontefract, were each jailed for five-years-and-seven-months.

Mohammed Muneeb, 18 and of St Wilfrids Grove, Leeds, was sentenced to four-years-and-one-month.

Another man, Mark Beattie, 43 and of Layton Lane, Rawdon, was arrested as part of the same operation. He was sentenced to seven-years-and-six-months in jail for a separate conspiracy to supply cocaine in Huddersfield between March and May.

Restraint orders have also been put in place which total more than £1.6 million in relation to properties owned.

Detective Inspector Paul Hutchinson, who led the investigation, said: "This group were heavily involved in the organised supply of drugs, and the very significant quantities recovered during our investigation show the scale of their criminal enterprise and the profits they would have been making.

"The drugs trade can sometimes be seen as a victimless crime, but the reality is very different. It is the single biggest driver of serious crime in the UK and fuels violence and anti-social behaviour in our communities.

"Around half of all homicides, thefts and robberies are drug-related, and nearly two thirds of those arrested for domestic abuse test positive for drugs.

"Organised crime groups target some of the most vulnerable in society, exploiting them for financial gain, status and criminal influence, while causing significant harm to these individuals and our communities.

"West Yorkshire Police remains firmly committed to taking down those who involve themselves in the organised supply of drugs, and we hope the successful outcome of this investigation will provide some reassurance to the public."
