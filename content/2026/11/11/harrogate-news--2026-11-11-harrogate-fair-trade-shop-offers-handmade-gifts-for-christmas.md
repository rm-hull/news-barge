A Christmas shop tucked away inside St Peter’s Church in Harrogate is encouraging shoppers to discover its range of handmade decorations and ethical gifts ahead of the festive season.

Harrogate Fair Trade’s shop, located in the church porch on Cambridge Street, has been part of the town’s shopping community for around 37 years. Supported by 25 volunteers, it offers an alternative to mass-produced Christmas decorations, with products made by artisans from around the world.

The festive range includes illuminated paper stars handmade in India, colourful felt decorations from Nepal, recycled glass baubles, traditional paper decorations and hand-carved gifts.

The shop also stocks products made through MADE51, an initiative connecting refugee artisans with international markets, as well as Palestinian olive wood jewellery and a limited number of brass Christmas decorations depicting Harrogate’s Royal Pump Room.

## Handmade decorations that become family traditions

Vicky Wild, manager at Harrogate Fair Trade, said some customers return each year to add another decoration to their collection.

> **Vicky Wild, manager at Harrogate Fair Trade:**
>
> Our loyal shoppers come every Christmas to buy one new decoration. They still have the ones they bought from us years ago, and each one becomes part of their family’s Christmas traditions. That’s what we love about handmade decorations. They’re not designed to be thrown away when the festive season is over. They’re beautiful, individual pieces that people want to keep and enjoy for years.
>
> We sometimes describe ourselves as Harrogate’s hidden treasure because people are amazed when they discover us, and the crafted, beautiful objects we sell.

The charity is encouraging shoppers to make at least one of their Christmas purchases a Fair Trade gift this year. It is also highlighting gift ideas that can be put together for £20, including a reusable cotton bag, a handmade felt robin and Palestinian olive wood jewellery.

Vicky said the products represented the skills and livelihoods of the people who made them, with the charity working through UK suppliers who maintain longstanding relationships with artisans and producers overseas.

> **Vicky Wild, manager at Harrogate Fair Trade:**
>
> Every decoration or gift meaningfully represents someone’s craftsmanship, skills and livelihood. I often tell customers that I know the person who knows the person who made that. We work closely with our UK suppliers who are in regular contact with the artisans and producers overseas, and have longstanding relationships with them.

## Plans for a larger town centre shop

Harrogate Fair Trade is also developing plans for a larger town centre shop, supported by funding from the York and North Yorkshire Combined Authority.

Working with local partners including Zero Carbon Harrogate and Skills for Living, the charity hopes to create a new destination for ethical shopping, bringing together sustainable products, local artisans, refill facilities, workshops and community activities under one roof.

The vision is to support independent retail and contribute to the wider regeneration of Harrogate’s high street.

> **Vicky Wild, manager at Harrogate Fair Trade:**
>
> We’ve been part of Harrogate for nearly 40 years, and while we might be a hidden treasure today, our ambition is to become one of the town’s most distinctive independent shopping destinations. Christmas is a wonderful time for people to discover what we do.

Harrogate Fair Trade is located at St Peter’s Church Porch, Cambridge Street, Harrogate. The charity also operates an online shop.

Further information is available at www.harrogatefairtradeshop.co.uk.
