Remembrance Day commemorations have been held across North Yorkshire to honour those who have lost their lives in conflicts around the world.

Wreath-laying ceremonies took place at council buildings across the county on Wednesday, November 11, marking the anniversary of the Armistice that brought fighting in the First World War to an end in 1918.

North Yorkshire Council leader, Cllr Carl Les, said the commemorations remained as important today as ever, given the conflicts continuing around the world.

> **Cllr Carl Les, leader of North Yorkshire Council:**
>
> The annual Remembrance Day offers us a moment to reflect on all those who have given the ultimate sacrifice while serving their country.
>
> We are proud of the extremely strong links that North Yorkshire has to the Armed Forces, and those who are serving today should be given the utmost respect for all they do to protect and serve the nation.
>
> With each passing year, it would be easy for the commemorations to lessen in their significance from where they originated.
>
> But with the conflicts that are still very evident across the world, it is as important today as ever to continue the tradition of commemorating all of those who have lost their lives in wars.

A ceremony was held at County Hall in Northallerton, where a commemorative wreath was laid by the council’s chair, Cllr David Chance, followed by a two-minute silence at 11am.

Other commemorations took place at council bases across the county, including Scarborough Town Hall, Ryedale House in Malton, Selby Civic Centre, Mercury House in Richmond and Harrogate Civic Centre.

Cllr Chance said the occasion was also an opportunity to reflect on the impact of war on the families and loved ones of those who died.

> **Cllr David Chance, chair of North Yorkshire Council:**
>
> This is a moment for us all to remember those who have lost their lives in wars, and the profound impact that has on their loved ones.
>
> I am honoured to be representing the council as its chair this year and to have the opportunity to play a part in these commemorations in this role.

## North Yorkshire’s Armed Forces links

North Yorkshire has longstanding links with the Armed Forces and is home to 10,770 military personnel, one of the largest numbers nationally.

The county is also home to Catterick Garrison, one of the largest military bases in the country, and has among the highest numbers of veterans nationally.

According to figures from the 2021 Census, 29,831 former members of the Armed Forces were living in North Yorkshire.

The council’s Armed Forces champion, Cllr Kevin Foster, said Remembrance Day was also an opportunity to recognise those continuing to serve.

> **Cllr Kevin Foster, North Yorkshire Council’s Armed Forces champion:**
>
> Remembrance Day is a moment to reflect on the past. But it is also important for us to say thank you to all those who are involved in the military as they continue such a proud and important role in protecting the country.

Remembrance Day commemorates the Armistice which came into effect at 11am on November 11, 1918, bringing the fighting in the First World War to an end.

The following year, King George V asked the public to observe a two-minute silence at 11am on November 11, remembering the moment the guns fell silent.
