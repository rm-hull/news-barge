# DWP set to hand over £120 cash to people who are off work

## Extra DWP cash will benefit millions of people

The Department for Work and Pensions (DWP) is preparing to give a £120 cash injection to workers who need time off due to illness.

When you're too unwell to attend work, you're legally entitled to statutory sick pay (SSP) if you're employed. This represents the minimum amount employers are required to pay qualifying staff, and it kicks in from day one of your sickness absence.

SSP currently stands at £123.25 weekly, or 80% of your usual weekly wages - whichever is the lesser amount - and continues for up to 28 weeks. However, the payment is due to rise by 3.5% from April, providing qualifying workers with additional financial support during periods of illness.

The 3.5% increase mirrors the rise in the consumer prices index (CPI) inflation for the year ending September 2026 and will come into effect for SSP rates from April onwards. The rise will push the weekly amount from £123.25 to roughly £127.56 in the 2027 to 2028 tax year, representing a total increase of approximately £120.79 for someone claiming the benefit for the full 28-week period, reports the Express.

Announcing the increase in a review released after the Budget on 28 October, Minister of State Baroness Sherlock stated: "Other State Pension and benefit rates covered by my statutory review will be increased by 3.5%, in line with the increase in the consumer prices index in the year to September 2026.

"This includes most working-age benefits and other benefits for people below State Pension age; benefits to help with additional needs arising from disability; Statutory Payments including Statutory Sick Pay and Statutory Maternity Pay; and Additional State Pension. The Pension Credit Savings Credit maximum amount will also increase by 3.5%."

The April pay rise follows legislative changes to SSP regulations introduced earlier this year, which abolished the waiting period for sick pay and scrapped the minimum earnings threshold required to qualify for payment during illness. Following the amendments, SSP is now accessible to all qualifying employees irrespective of their income level, with payments commencing from day one of sickness rather than the previous fourth-day rule.

The old system entitled workers to receive SSP for a maximum of 28 weeks, covering all days they were absent from work due to illness - bar the initial three days. This meant eligible employees only began receiving sick pay from their fourth working day of absence. However, this three-day waiting window has now been eliminated, enabling workers to claim SSP immediately from their first day of illness.

In guidance released earlier this year, HMRC confirmed the SSP modifications, stating: "From 6 April 2026 the Employment Rights Act 2025 introduces two major changes to Statutory Sick Pay (SSP).

"Removal of the Lower Earnings Limit: All eligible employees will be entitled to SSP regardless of income. SSP will be paid at 80% of normal weekly earnings or the uprated weekly flat rate of £123.25 [£127.56 from April], whichever is lower. Removal of the Waiting Period: SSP will be paid from the first full day of sickness absence, not from day four.

"These changes make SSP more accessible and remove barriers for lower-paid employees, and will apply across the United Kingdom, including Northern Ireland.

"The relevant legislation will apply as to when the sickness absence took place. Absences starting before 6 April 2026 will follow the current system to determine eligibility and payment. Absences starting on or after 6 April 2026 will use the new rules, unless otherwise outlined in legislation."

Employees will receive SSP for every full day they are absent due to illness that they would ordinarily have worked, with employers calculating entitlement based on average weekly earnings across an eight-week period.

To be eligible for SSP, you must hold employee status, have carried out some work for your employer, and have been unwell for at least one full working day. You are generally required to notify your employer of your inability to work before their specified deadline - or within seven days should no such deadline have been set.

The DWP stated that the new "fairer rate" achieves the correct equilibrium between providing financial security to employees who become unwell and the burden placed on businesses, while maintaining the motivation for individuals to return to the workplace.
