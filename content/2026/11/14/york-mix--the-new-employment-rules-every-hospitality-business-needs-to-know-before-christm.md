P.J Staffing are here to help hospitality businesses for Christmas and beyond.

The Leeds-based recruitment agency, founded by Paul McCallum, shares the benefits of using a flexible staffing agency over Christmas.

P.J Staffing will also be holding a free webinar to help businesses understand the impact of the existing, October 2026 and incoming 2027 law changes as part of the Employment Rights Act 2025.

Originally from South Wales, Paul moved to Leeds over 15 years ago where he founded his company P.J Staffing – a recruitment agency specialising in events and hospitality, providing a flexible staffing solution to clients and flexible, temporary jobs to candidates.

***YorkMix*** spoke with Paul to find out more about recruiting for Christmas, and how P.J Staffing can help businesses with the Employment Law changes.

“Christmas time is a perfect time to start thinking about the flexibility of staffing that you may need,” said Paul. “If you don’t need to hire somebody permanent, you could use a staffing agency who does provide experienced and quality and reliable staffing.

“It can be a really cost-effective to use a flexible staffing agency over Christmas because if you don’t need permanent staff, but you actually just need an uplift in experienced staff on those busy festive nights, whether it’s bar, hospitality, or kitchen staff, then agencies like PJ Staffing, provide a flexible module, where you book what you need, and only pay for the labour provided generally.

“Whether that would be waiting staff, bar tenders, or your kitchen staff, with kitchen porters, an essential role, supporting your chefs in that kitchen to get those Christmas dinners out and happy customers.

“Our staff love it because they actually get to be a part of it, the Christmas parties and the festivities.”

Rachel, a supervisor with P.J Staffing said that working with the agency has “strengthened my communication and my management skills, and working with all sorts of people has really enriched what I do in my every day life.”

Rachel has worked Christmas for P.J Staffing before. “It’s quite exciting. The events that you go to, people are at that Christmas level, so everybody’s quite excited.

“I love the atmosphere, everybody’s always in a very good mood and it makes you feel super festive.”

Ethan, who’s 24, has worked with P.J Staffing for almost eight years, worked the summer season as cleaning supervisor at York Races. “I’ll be moving over to Stockeld Park in the winter as racing has finished now. They do a Santa’s Grotto, a panto, two restaurants, massive outdoor play areas and huge light displays – all of that has to be cleaned by us cleaners.

“Then when when the day opens and we have floods of families, adults, friends, and seeing their faces light up and get all warm from just enjoying themselves – so you take a lot of pride in it.”

“As the Christmas season approaches, and there’s going to be a lot of changes that are happening across the hospitality industry in regards to employment legislation,” said Paul.

There are 28 different legislation changes, some already in effect with 12 employment law changes as part of the Employment Rights Act 2025 come into force during October 2026 with the remaining during 2027. The Act received Royal Assent in 2025 but is being rolled out in stages through 2026 and 2027.

P.J Staffing have put together a free knowledge guide based Employment & Compliance document for Events and Hospitality in regard to the Employment Rights Act – detailing what’s in, what’s coming, what it means for events and hospitality and ensure you are completely aware of the changes. This free Knowledge Guide is available in full upon request at P.J Staffing’s website.

P.J Staffing are also hosting a **free educational webinar on Wednesday 25 November at 11am** all about the changes.

Paul has highlighted a few key factors for hospitality:

* Right to Work and Compliance Checks
  + “There are hospitality businesses across the UK that do use agencies, but the right to work and compliance checks now extend beyond just the agencies. It’s making sure clients are auditing people as well to make sure they’ve got the right to work checks, because the last thing you want is having somebody who’s illegally working and then getting a fine for 60,000 pounds.
  + “It’s encouraging not just hospitality but all businesses to do those checks or confirm with the agency what their policy is in regards to right to work and can they prove the right to work if you were to be audited by the Home Office or by one of the clients.”
* Tipping Policy
  + “With the tipping policy, it’s every employer now has to pass on 100% of the tips to everybody, including agency workers, to make sure it’s fair and transparent. It’s making sure that they’ve got a policy in place and they’ve consulted with their staff on that – if they do get checked and and they failed to do the consultation and agree with the staff, then they could be taken to tribunal.”
* Sexual Harrassment
  + “This is probably more an imminent one to make sure people have got a policy in place for this because you’ve got to take all reasonable steps to prevent it within hospitality, and any business. But if you look at hospitality, you’ve got all the Christmas parties coming up, and it’s making sure that you’ve got the right steps in place to to help prevent it from contractors, staff, delivery drivers, everyone.
  + “A failure to show all reasonable steps can also again trigger an investigation and a tribunal conversation uplift of 25%.”
* The Unfair Dismissal Qualifying Period
  + “The Unfair Dismissal qualifying period will reduce from two years to six months. So anybody currently in situ, when it drops to six months, would be eligible to claim unfair dismissal in a tribunal claim. Employees who started on or before the second of July, 2026, will already reach the threshold by 1 January. So anybody currently in situ when it gets to January, they could potentially, if they feel they’ve been unfairly dismissed, go to tribunal for six months service.”
* Guaranteed Hours Proposal
  + “The three things that are going to be part of this is the right to guaranteed hours, and then the right to reasonable notice of shifts and right to a payment of short notice cancellation.
  + “It’s quite a complex case for guaranteed hours, and I’m hoping the government comes back with a reasonable proposal for everybody.”

“I’ve worked in the Hospitality and recruitment industry for over 25 years, and I’ve never seen so many changes happen all within a short period of time,” said Paul. “I’m using a short period of time because it is over two years – and whilst that seems long, in perspective it’s not.

“It’s ensuring that we’re keeping on top of it as a business – as a business owner as well – and ensure that you’re educating the staff. Because if you don’t educate your staff, you put yourself at risk still. Not just permanent employees, but the agency staff as well.

“It’s an opportunity to help our clients to ensure that they are fully educated in what they’re doing. They may already have a HR company that they’re working, which was fantastic. But for those probably smaller independent businesses that potentially don’t have a HR department or HR contact, they’re putting themselves at risk if they don’t if they don’t comply with everything.”

The free webinar will cover all the law changes, which will be gone through in detail. Alongside Paul, there will be a HR, Legal and accountant representative present for different perspectives on how these changes could impact businesses, and how you can prevent it from impacting your business.

Joining Paul will be Sonehal Kooner at Van Nest Legal, Nick Hurt at ProgressA Ltd, and Jenna Taylor at Walker Taylor Accountancy.

“It’s very much going to be the aim to help people and also remind people as well, because everybody’s going into a very busy seasonal period, but this isn’t going away – you still have to ensure the changes are in place or have a plan to implement it before the new legislation comes into effect in 2027.

The incoming (and already in force) changes are already affecting events, hospitality businesses and staffing agencies like P.J Staffing.

“I’d say we’re seeing a shift at the moment for our Christmas staffing,” said Paul. “People are conscious of what’s happening, and they’re probably unsure of what they want to do yet, so we are trying to help and support Yorkshire businesses.

“Is there a risk of taking somebody on full time because of all the employment costs and the unfair dismissal coming in? Do they have the budget to take somebody on perm, or would it be better using a flexible staffing module like P.J Staffing, where they can dip in and out of our candidate pool as and when they need, especially as we go into Q1 of 2027, when hospitality businesses are not as busy.

“We are going to be all very busy over the next eight weeks , but bearing in mind there are a lot of legislation changes that are in force and will be coming into force, and we’re going to help people and remind people of these changes, no matter how uncomfortable it is.”
