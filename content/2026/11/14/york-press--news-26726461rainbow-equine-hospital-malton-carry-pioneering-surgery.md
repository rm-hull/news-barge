## North Yorkshire veterinary hospital to carry out pioneering new surgery

Jonathan Anderson of Rainbow Equine Hospital *(Image: Rainbow Equine Hospital)*

A veterinary hospital is set to become the first in the UK to carry out pioneering new equine neurosurgery.

Rainbow Equine Hospital in Malton will introduce a new treatment for horses diagnosed with wobbler syndrome, a condition caused by compression of the spinal cord in the neck.

The hospital has been performing corrective surgery for 16 years, but will now begin using 3D-printed titanium implants to improve outcomes and reduce recovery times.

Jonathan Anderson, clinical director and equine surgeon at Rainbow, said: "Rainbow Equine Hospital is excited to be leading the UK’s ability to perform the most advanced surgical techniques to treat wobbler syndrome and to be the first surgical team to bring this life-saving and life-changing technique to horses in the UK.

"We’re hugely grateful to VetPartners for providing us with the equipment that will enable us to do this."

Wobbler syndrome occurs when the spinal cord in a horse’s neck is compressed between two vertebrae, preventing vital nerve signals travelling from the brain to the limbs, and affected horses may trip, stumble or fall without warning.

Many affected horses are euthanised due to the risk of injury.

However, early diagnosis and intervention can improve the outlook for some animals.

Rainbow Equine Hospital is one of only a few centres worldwide offering corrective surgery for the condition, known as cervical arthrodesis.

This procedure fuses the affected vertebrae to relieve pressure on the spinal cord.

The hospital is now one of the first in the UK to use 3D-printed implants in the surgery.

Mr Anderson has spent 18 months training in the new technique, including shadowing surgeons in the United States.

He said: "Based on the positive outcomes of surgeries performed in the US and France, the new cage implant will create a stronger, more stable repair that will lower the risk of complications and could significantly cut recovery time.

"Through the existing surgical technique we are looking at a six to nine month return to ridden work following surgery and a rehabilitation programme, but the new implant has the potential to reduce this by several months.

"Many owners and even many vets may not be aware of what can currently be done to help wobblers, but we have the ability to save horses from euthanasia and there is a positive prognosis for many horses.

"The addition of this exciting technique will give new hope to owners whose horses have received a wobbler diagnosis and to their vets who want to do their very best for their patients and provide an alternative option to euthanasia."

The new procedure uses a 3D-printed titanium cage, fixed in place with custom-made titanium locking screws, to fuse the affected vertebrae.

This approach is expected to offer improved stability compared to the traditional method, which uses a perforated titanium cylinder.

Rainbow Equine Hospital is accredited by the Royal College of Veterinary Surgeons and provides 24-hour care from its specialist centre in Old Malton.

The hospital also accepts referrals from across the UK and Ireland.

With the introduction of the new technique and other recent advances such as foraminotomy surgery, Rainbow Equine Hospital has reinforced its reputation as a leader in equine neurosurgery.

The team also travels internationally to treat cases in countries where this level of expertise is not available.
