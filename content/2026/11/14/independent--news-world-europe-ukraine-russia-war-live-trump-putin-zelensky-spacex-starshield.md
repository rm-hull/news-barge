A Russian drone strike has hit a hospital in central Kyiv, injuring three patients and forcing the evacuation of 30 people. Seven people were rescued by emergency workers, and the hospital’s medical services were temporarily suspended as staff dealt with the damage.

President Volodymyr Zelensky said speed restrictions on Starshield remained in place along the entire frontline and in Russian-occupied parts of Ukraine, and they “limit our ability to intercept jet-powered Shaheds and counter other drones”.

Russia said on Friday it had intercepted nearly 1,200 Ukrainian drones overnight, in one of Kyiv’s largest reported attacks on Russian territory. Ukraine said it had struck a major oil refinery in the Volgograd region.

Russian attacks on Ukraine’s energy infrastructure, meanwhile, caused power cuts across 12 regions, according to deputy energy minister Artem Nekrasov.

The fighting has also reached the Baltic Sea, where Finland’s military confirmed that a Russian warship had been struck in the Gulf of Finland. Ukraine’s Security Service identified the vessel as the missile ship Naro-Fominsk.

## Zelensky says US floated Donbas withdrawal in exchange for Nato membership

US negotiators have asked whether Ukraine would withdraw from the eastern Donbas region in exchange for Nato membership, President Volodymyr Zelensky has said.

“The Americans raised a question: ‘What if Ukraine joins Nato? Would Ukraine withdraw from Donbas?’ This was the first time this had been raised,” Zelensky told reporters on Saturday.

![](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/14/09/2304267523..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Zelensky said Ukraine was not prepared to surrender its territory. However, he said he would put the question to a referendum if the country received a comprehensive security package, including security guarantees, a well-funded military, and funding for reconstruction

## Russia and Ukraine discuss major prisoner exchange involving up to 1,500 people each

Russia and Ukraine are working towards a large-scale prisoner exchange that could involve as many as 1,500 people on each side, Volodymyr Zelensky said.

The proposal was discussed during two days of US-mediated talks in Abu Dhabi, where Ukrainian negotiator Rustem Umerov and Russian representative Vladimir Medinsky reached an understanding on a potential swap, according to *The Kyiv Independent*.

Zelenskyy said the sides had discussed exchanging 1,000 or possibly 1,500 prisoners each, although the final numbers and date had yet to be agreed.

![](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/13/03/download..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

US envoy Jared Kushner, who took part in the diplomatic discussions, described the proposed swap as potentially “the largest prisoner exchange of the war”, saying it could reunite thousands of people with their families.

## Ukraine races to repair power plants as winter blackouts loom

Ukraine is racing to repair damaged power plants before winter after Russian missile and drone attacks forced three of the country’s major private energy company’s power stations to halt generation in November.

Workers at one damaged facility operated by DTEK, Ukraine’s largest private energy company, are clearing rubble by hand to reach buried turbines and boilers. “Right now, everyone has shovels and wheelbarrows as we try to reach the equipment,” a technician told Reuters.

![Workers clear debris at a thermal power plant of the largest Ukrainian private energy company DTEK, heavily damaged following Russian missile and drone strikes](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/14/03/2304415538..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

DTEK chief executive Maxim Timchenko warned that recovery could be slow. “So we do everything possible to make enough power generation capacity in Ukraine to get through the winter,” he said.

Power cuts have affected 12 Ukrainian regions, while some areas face blackouts lasting up to 18 hours a day.

## Ukraine says Starshield restrictions continue to hamper drone defences

Restrictions on the US Starshield satellite communications system remain in place along Ukraine’s frontline, hampering its military’s ability to intercept Russian drones, president Volodymyr Zelensky said on Friday.

“Unfortunately, the speed restrictions remain in place along the entire front line and in temporarily-occupied territories of Ukraine,” he said in his nightly video address. The restrictions, he said, “limit our ability to intercept jet-powered Shaheds and counter other drones”.

Zelensky said the curbs also sent Russia a signal that Washington might treat Ukrainian territory occupied by Russian forces differently. “We expect the speed to be restored,” he said.

Ukraine first reported restrictions on Starlink and its military-grade version Starshield in mid-October. Service was restored on 24 October, but Ukrainian forces reported renewed disruptions this week.

Neither SpaceX nor the US government accepted responsibility for the earlier restrictions, according to Reuters.

Meanwhile, Democratic US senators Jeanne Shaheen and Chris Coons have asked Elon Musk to preserve SpaceX’s records relating to the restrictions, giving the company until 30 November to confirm it has done so.

## Russian strikes on Zaporizhzhia injure 50, including eight children

Russian attacks on the southeastern Ukrainian city of Zaporizhzhia injured 50 people on Friday, including eight children. The strikes damaged residential buildings and killed a rescue worker, local authorities said.

Two projectiles fired from a Russian Tornado-S multiple-launch rocket system struck apartment buildings in the Kosmichnyi district, damaging flats across three floors and triggering fires, according to *The Kyiv Independent*.

![An apartment building damaged during a Russian airstrike in Zaporizhzhia](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/14/03/2026-11-13T125552Z_958949390_RC2XMOAJ9FDV_RTRMADP_3_UKRAINE-CRISIS-ATTACK-ZAPORIZHZHIA.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Ukraine’s State Emergency Service said rescuers freed 17 people trapped inside their apartments. A rescue worker was later killed when a concrete slab fell on him as he cleared debris, and a separate Russian attack killed a 17-year-old boy and injured at least four other people.

## Trump to leave Ukraine war off agenda at meeting with Putin and Xi

US president Donald Trump is not expected to raise Russia’s war in Ukraine when he meets Russian president Vladimir Putin and Chinese president Xi Jinping in China next Thursday, a White House official said on Friday.

The first trilateral meeting between the three leaders will instead focus on nuclear arms reductions, with Trump also expected to discuss the US-Israeli war against Iran. The leaders are due to meet on the sidelines of the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (APEC) summit in Shenzhen.

![Russian artist Alexey Sergienko unveils his new pop art painting dubbed Shenzhen, 26 in Saint Petersburg](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/14/02/2304231619..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“We’ve had many meetings recently with the Russians, but China is not really a part of that track, so it doesn’t seem appropriate to have the Ukraine discussion there,” the official said.

A senior Trump administration official said Washington had been holding separate discussions with Moscow on Ukraine, adding that China was not involved in those negotiations. Trump is also not expected to hold a separate bilateral meeting with Putin.

## Four killed in Ukrainian strike on Samara region industrial facility, governor says

The governor of Russia's Samara region said on Friday that Ukraine had struck an industrial facility there, killing four people.

In his statement on Russia's MAX messenger, governor Vyacheslav Fedorishchev did not name the facility struck, or the weapons used by Ukraine.

## EU foreign chief says Russia 'does not want anyone strong' at peace talks

The EU's foreign policy chief Kaja Kallas said on Friday Russia had no say over who represented the bloc in the Ukraine-Russia peace talks, adding she knew Moscow ded not want her there but that she spoke on behalf of all 27 member states rather than in a personal capacity.

"They don't want anybody strong on the other side because they want to push through their maximum demands", she said, adding that a ceasefire must come first as broader discussions about Europe's security architecture risked diverting attention.
