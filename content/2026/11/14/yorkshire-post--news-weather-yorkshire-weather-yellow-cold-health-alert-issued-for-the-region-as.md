# Yorkshire weather: Yellow cold health alert issued for the region as temperatures plummet

## The yellow cold health alert is in effect next week for Yorkshire and the Humber and the weather is likely to have minor impacts on health.

The yellow cold health alert is issued from 9pm on November 15 to 9am on November 19, 2026 across the Yorkshire region.

The yellow alert has a matrix score of 7 which means forecast weather is likely to have minor impacts on health and social care services, including:

* Increased use of healthcare services by vulnerable people
* Greater risk to life of vulnerable people

![A woman feeds the birds in the Museum Gardens in York as freezing fog and a hard frost covers the City.](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/jpim-static/image/2026/11/14/14/37/NLYP-14-11-2026-A-woman-feeds-the-birds-in-the-Museum-Gardens-in-York-as-freezing-fog-and-a-hard-fro.jpg?trim=151,52,87,55&width=640&quality=65)


Met Office chief forecaster, Chris Bulmer, said: “The UK forecast has moved to a colder phase with the daytime temperatures for most locations likely to stay in single figures.

“Overnight temperatures on Friday night will dip again to -4 or even -6 C in some locations, meaning another significant ground frost for many locations.”

UK Health Security Agency (UKHSA) has introduced the Weather Health Alerting system and low temperatures can result in increased use of health care services by vulnerable people.

There could be an increased risk to health for individuals aged 65 years and over, particularly those with pre-existing health conditions - including respiratory and cardiovascular diseases, as well as some other vulnerable groups such as those sleeping rough.

Consultant in public health at UKHSA, Paul Coleman, said: “This is the latest cold-health alert of the season, and we can expect more as we move further into winter.

“Cold weather can have a serious impact on the health of some people, including those aged 65 and over and those with pre-existing health conditions, so it is vital to check in on friends, family and neighbours who are most vulnerable.

“We’re also reminding people to take simple steps to stay warm and look after their health. If you can, try to heat the rooms you spend most of your time in, such as your living room or bedroom, and dress warmly to help you stay safe and well during colder weather.”
