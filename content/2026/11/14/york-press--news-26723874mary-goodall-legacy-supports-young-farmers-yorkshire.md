## Mary Goodall legacy helps young farming professionals in Yorkshire

The 2025 Goodall students receiving their graduation awards at the Great Yorkshire Show *(Image: Supplied)*

A farmer’s final gift is helping to shape the future of agriculture in Yorkshire.

The Goodall Agri-Development Pathway, a fully funded training programme for young professionals in the sector, was launched by the Yorkshire Agricultural Society (YAS) thanks to a legacy left by lifelong farmer Mary Goodall.

Designed to build skills, broaden experience, and prepare candidates for greater responsibility, the programme supports those aged 23 to 40 working in farming or related industries across the North of England.

Mary Goodall’s family visited the YAS headquarters at the Great Yorkshire Showground to share more about her life and to hear about her great legacy.

Her family said: "We are delighted that her legacy is supporting young people in agriculture through a programme that reflects her lifelong commitment to farming.

"The Pathway is an impressive example of how practical development and shared experience can help the sector’s future leaders to thrive.

"She was very forward-thinking, she was determined, and she had lots of friends in the farming community - she was very welcoming.

"She was a farmer all her life, and that's why this legacy is so special."

Launched in 2023, the scheme has already supported 22 alumni, with 12 more currently enrolled.

Up to 12 additional places are available for the 2027 cohort, and applications remain open until Friday, November 20, 2026.

David Tite, manager at the Yorkshire Agricultural Society, said: "We are incredibly grateful to Mary for her gift, which has enabled us to roll out a programme that develops the next generation of farmers through modules and a study trip in Europe.

"It’s a unique and life-changing programme and wonderful to see that her legacy lives on in the industry she loved so much."

The pathway includes eight in-person modules covering farm visits, conferences, expert speakers, and a European study tour.

Topics span teamwork and leadership, media training, business growth and direct sales, sustainable farming and diversification, farm administration and HR, and lean management.

Participants, known as Pathfinders, gain hands-on experience, industry insight, and the confidence needed to progress in their careers.

Graduates are recognised at a special awards ceremony held during the Great Yorkshire Show.

Applicants must be employed in farming or related sectors in the North of England and will undergo an interview process.

A £250 deposit is required, which is refunded upon completion of the programme.

The Yorkshire Agricultural Society, a registered charity, is deeply rooted in supporting and promoting Yorkshire’s farming, food, and rural communities.

Its work includes events, training, grants, scholarships, education, scientific research, and practical support, all aimed at strengthening the industry.

YAS is supported by its family of businesses, including Fodder, Yorkshire Event Centre, Pavilions of Harrogate, and the Harrogate Caravan Park, as well as flagship events such as the Great Yorkshire Show and Springtime Live.

In 2019, activities and events at the Great Yorkshire Showground contributed £73.7 million to the regional economy.

For more information about the Goodall Agri-Development Pathway, contact the YAS Charitable Activities team at [education@yas.co.uk.](mailto:education@yas.co.uk.)
