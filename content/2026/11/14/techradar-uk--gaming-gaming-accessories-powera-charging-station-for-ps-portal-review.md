## PowerA Charging Station for PlayStation Portal review

I’ve been using the PowerA Charging Station for PlayStation Portal for a few weeks now, and I can comfortably say that it exceeded my expectations right across the board.

Sure, it’s hardly the most exciting accessory around — it’s a dock that will bring your PS Portal back to life when it runs dry. But with eye-catching lighting, reliable performance, and a smart design, this charging station ended up impressing me a whole lot. So, is this PS Portal accessory actually worth its $37.99 / £19.99 / AU$59.95 asking price? Here’s my verdict.

I want to kick things off by talking about this thing’s design. I really like how the charging station mirrors the look of the PS5 and portal itself. Its white exterior and black detailing really help it fit seamlessly into your setup, meaning it doesn’t look like an eyesore on your shelf or TV unit. The base is also super sturdy and has some rubber feet to keep it in place, and its overall build quality is comparable to some of the best PS5 accessories.

![PowerA Charging Station for PS Portal with USB-C adapter](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/wSWwAt3e2W7QbdyKXDsEyf-1200-80.jpg)

Another aspect of the charging stand’s design that I like is the customizable lighting. There’s a discreet touch panel on the reverse side of the model that enables you to flick through three light modes: cycle, wave, and pulse.

These all look brilliant and reflect the ambient lighting of the PS5 console. You’re also able to adjust the brightness by holding down on the touch panel — which is highly responsive — plus there’s an option to switch off the lights entirely.

But how about performance? Well, the PS Portal itself is a little restricted in terms of charging speeds, supporting just 15W wired charging. But with that in mind, PowerA’s charging station puts on a good show. It got my PS Portal from 0% to 50% in just over 35 minutes, and from 0% to 100% in one hour and 45 minutes. That’s just as fast as I’d expected, and I found charging performance to be consistent across the whole testing period.

![Man pressing touch pad on PowerA Charging Station for PS Portal](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/c5fTfG2jKbH5p46Uny2zHg-1200-80.jpg)

One thing that is worth noting, however, is that there’s no charging brick included with the PowerA Charging Station. As a result, I had to use my own, but bear in mind that you’ll specifically need a brick with a USB-C port if you’re making use of the bundled USB-C charging cable.

However, when you have everything you need, the charging station is incredibly easy to use. Either plug it into your PS5 console or a wall charger, insert the magnetic USB-C adapter into your portal, place it onto the dock, and you’re good to go. I noticed that the magnetic USB-C adapter was a bit challenging to remove, but if you’re going to use the station regularly, you can just leave it in.

So is the PowerA Charging Station for PS Portal worth it? For me, absolutely. If you want a clean, easy way to charge your Portal, then it’s well worth picking up. At $37.99 / £19.99 / AU$59.95, it's an affordable solution, and both the build and charging quality hit the spot.

## Should I buy the PowerA Charging Station for PlayStation Portal?

## Buy it if…

**You want a clean charging solution** By using a charging cradle, you can avoid the awkwardness of cluttered wires. Just plug the dock into your PS5 console or an external wall charger, and you’re all set.**You want a great looking charging cradle**  
I love the way this charging station looks. It mirrors the aesthetic of the PS5 to perfection, and its adjustable lights are beautiful too — this will only make your gaming setup look better.

## Don’t buy it if…

**You need to charge on the go** If you’re on the move, this charging cradle won’t be the most convenient solution. Instead, I’d recommend picking up a high-quality wall charger, such as the option listed in the ‘also consider’ section.**If you use a thick case or skin on your Portal**  
If you’ve got a skin or case attached to your Portal, it may not slot into this charging cradle very nicely. As a result, I’d recommend just using a standard charger instead — ideally one that supports 15W wired charging.
