The redevelopment of the Brunswick Centre in Scarborough is looking to open in 2029 after an £18.5 million public funding package was agreed in principle by regional and local leaders.

The revised scheme, now known as SQ1, would transform the former shopping centre into a six-screen cinema, leisure, restaurant and retail complex in the heart of the town centre.

The project will move into its next phase after securing support from the York and North Yorkshire Combined Authority, North Yorkshire Council and the Pride in Place programme.

The package consists of £7 million in Combined Authority grant funding, £3.5 million from Pride in Place and an £8 million council loan.

However, there are still various obstacles despite the funding approvals.

Fresh planning consent will need to be approved as the previous one has lapsed. Construction funding will only be released once planning permission is secured, tenant commitments are in place, and SGI has secured commercial finance.

David Wells, development director at Scarborough Group International, said the revised scheme had been reworked to reflect changing market conditions.

“As the market has shifted, we’ve reworked the plans to make sure SQ1 delivers on acquiring the right occupiers, the right finance and a sound commercial footing, which benefits the public purse too. We’re now in discussions with a major national retailer and a cinema operator.”

The identities of the proposed retailer and cinema operator have not yet been disclosed publicly.

The project is currently expected to cost around £35 million, with SGI saying it will fund approximately half of that amount through its own investment and commercial borrowing.

The company said this includes money already spent acquiring the Brunswick Centre, meeting holding costs and carrying out design and planning work.

According to estimates, the completed scheme could support more than 400 direct jobs, create around 160 additional supply-chain roles and attract approximately 850,000 visits a year. Annual spending linked to the development has been estimated at up to £45 million.

David Skaith, Mayor of York and North Yorkshire Combined Authority, described the project as a key part of the new Scarborough Mayoral Development Zone.

“Transforming the Brunswick Centre into SQ1 will bring major retail, leisure, and a cinema back to the town centre, creating hundreds of new jobs and opportunities.”

SGI said design work is already underway and strip-out works are expected to begin in early 2027.
