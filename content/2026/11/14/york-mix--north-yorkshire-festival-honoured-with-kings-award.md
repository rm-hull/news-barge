The Ryedale Festival has been honoured with a King’s award.

It has been awarded The King’s Award for Voluntary Service, which is the highest honour given to volunteer groups in the UK – and recognises the extraordinary contribution of more than 100 volunteers who help bring music to communities across North and East Yorkshire.

The prestigious award, equivalent to an MBE, celebrates outstanding voluntary work that makes a lasting difference to local communities.

Established in 2002 to mark Queen Elizabeth II’s Golden Jubilee, it recognises groups whose dedication, generosity and commitment have an exceptional impact on the people and places they serve.

Volunteering has been at the heart of the Ryedale Festival throughout its history. Each year, volunteers give their time, energy and enthusiasm to help make the Festival possible, supporting everything from welcoming audiences and looking after artists to preparing venues, distributing publicity, selling tickets and helping events run smoothly.

Kate Hall, producer of the Ryedale Festival, said: “Our volunteers are such an important part of the Ryedale Festival.

“In so many ways they are the personality of the festival, adding warmth, enthusiasm, fun and good humour alongside a real commitment to making everything run to the highest standard.

“Our volunteer team spans generations, with some having been with us for many years and others joining for the first time, but all sharing a real pride in being part of the festival. There’s a great sense of friendship and teamwork and it’s fantastic to see their contribution recognised in this way.”

The festival is known for bringing internationally famous artists and live music to cities, towns and villages across North and East Yorkshire.

In 2026, the Festival presented 60 events in more than 35 venues, including nine new locations. Alongside its concert programme, the Festival creates opportunities for young people, from first encounters with live music and creative participation to work experience, professional development and a platform for some of the most promising young musicians of their generation.

Ed Anderson, chair of the Ryedale Festival, said: “This is a tremendous honour, and one that belongs to everyone who has given their time to the Ryedale Festival over the years.

“Our volunteers are at the heart of everything we do, bringing warmth, generosity and enthusiasm to the Festival. Their dedication helps us bring outstanding music to beautiful places across Yorkshire, welcome new audiences and create important opportunities for young people.

“We are immensely proud of them and delighted that their contribution has received this national recognition.”

The King’s Award for Voluntary Service is awarded for life. Recipients receive a certificate signed by His Majesty The King and a commemorative crystal, presented by the Lord-Lieutenant.
