# DWP urgent new Universal Credit warning as letters sent out

## The Department for Work and Pensions (DWP) has issued a crucial warning to all state pensioners

The Department for Work and Pensions ( DWP ) has issued an urgent alert to state pensioners who've been sent correspondence regarding Universal Credit. The department has updated its guidance for those who've reached State Pension age and been issued with a Migration Notice, clarifying how the reforms may impact their existing benefits, what financial assistance they could qualify for, and the necessary actions to maintain uninterrupted payments.

Pensioners receiving DWP correspondence directing them to transition to Universal Credit are being cautioned against disregarding the notification, as failure to meet the deadline could see their benefit payments cease entirely.

This development is part of the Government's broader welfare transformation initiative, which is systematically replacing older benefit schemes with Universal Credit. A number of legacy systems, including Tax Credits, Income-based Jobseeker's Allowance and Income Support, have already been discontinued, while Housing Benefit is similarly being phased out for the majority of fresh applications.

The transformation represents a key element of the Government's extensive strategy to discontinue legacy benefits and migrate recipients onto Universal Credit, reports the Express.

Previous support mechanisms, including Tax Credits, Income-based Jobseeker's Allowance and Income Support, have been superseded, while Housing Benefit is being progressively withdrawn in the vast majority of circumstances. Anyone receiving a Migration Notice must submit a Universal Credit claim by the deadline stated in their correspondence if they want to keep receiving financial support.

The guidance is applicable throughout England, Scotland and Wales, while Northern Ireland operates under different arrangements. For the majority of those issued with a Migration Notice, Housing Benefit will cease, regardless of whether they opt to transfer to Universal Credit.

Nevertheless, exemptions exist for people residing in supported or temporary accommodation, who can continue to have their Housing Benefit managed by their local authority.

Individuals whose Housing Benefit ends may be eligible to reclaim it within a three-month window if they failed to apply for Universal Credit, were deemed ineligible despite submitting an application before the deadline, or have since ceased receiving Universal Credit.

The DWP maintains that most claimants transitioning to Universal Credit will receive an equivalent amount to their previous benefits, or potentially more. This is facilitated through a mechanism called 'transitional protection'.
