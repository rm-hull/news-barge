# Council paying £6,000 a week to protect vacant Grade II-listed sports centre

## Keeping the vacant Richard Dunn sports centre building secure is costing Bradford taxpayers over £6,000 a week, it has been revealed.

The eye watering figure has led to one Conservative Councillor in Bradford criticising Historic England for suggesting the building be listed in 2022, a move that prevented its planned demolition.

But the national heritage body has pointed out that the decision to give a Grade II listing to the centre was ultimately made by the then Conservative Government.

Councillor Debbie Davies asked Bradford Council the cost of security on the huge site, and her question has just been answered by council bosses.

![Richard Dunn Centre 2024. Picture: 20th Century Society/Jonathan Taylor](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOmQzOWFjMzc5LWRjNTQtNGI4OS04NzI4LWIyODBlNDg4MTc0ZTphMDk0OWJkNS04ZWEzLTRlMTMtYjU0ZC04MTQwMDg3NGU2M2I=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


After a security audit of the site this spring, new measures to prevent trespass on the site are costing £6,017 a week – over £312,000 a year.

The 1970s leisure centre was shut in 2019 after the opening of the more modern Sedbergh Leisure Centre.

Initially the building was earmarked for demolition – a process that was held up by the Covid pandemic.

A few months before demolition was due to begin there was the shock announcement that the building had been awarded Grade II-listed status.

The request to list the building was made by the 20th Century Society, with Historic England recommending the listing be approved.

The body described the domed structure as a “bold and accomplished design for a 1970s local leisure.”

This listing put a halt to the plans to demolish the building, and market the site to developers.

The centre has remained empty since, although it has been used as a filming location for major movies such as 28 Years Later: The Bone Temple.

In her question to council bosses, Coun Davies also asked how “vandals and travellers” had been able to access the site earlier this year.

The response said: “Following a social media post and a break into Richard Dunns in late March 2026, a police security audit was completed, with a clear instruction to increase physical security presence on site.

“This was implemented with immediate effect and is currently costing £6,017 per week. Since the increase in physical security in April 2026, there has been no unauthorised access to the building, and one incident where travellers gained access, approximately 19 caravans, staying seven days and vacating without further incidents.

“Current security measures are under review with options being considered to increase other measures, and in turn reduce physical security.

“Any other security in place whilst the site is being used for filming, fun fairs or carparking is procured and paid for by those with a licence to occupy.”

After receiving the response, Coun Davies said: “The intervention of Historic England in 2022, which prevented the demolition of this building, which they describe as of “a bold and established design” has cost the residents of Bradford district a lot of money and left them cornered, with no end in sight.

“My colleagues and I do not share their description on this 1970s eyesore, which I suspect that His Majesty the King would describe as a carbuncle of a building.

“They should have placed more focus on the impracticality and unaffordable cost to local people of bringing the building back into use, than the thoughts of the 20th Century Society.

“If Historic England wish to digress from the purpose that the public associate them with, looking after buildings of genuine, serious value to the culture of the nation, they should fund the cost.

“It is unfair that during a cost of living crisis, people are funding the whims of these organisations to cling onto derelict buildings beyond economical repair with no purpose and absolutely no cultural value in the eyes of anyone not employed by them.

“The vast majority of Bradford district residents would shed no tears over the demolition of this building and much prefer an end to the nonsensical drain on their taxes.

“My Conservative group colleagues and I call on Historic England and the 20th Century Society, to fund the ongoing costs currently being footed by the taxpayer of a council in need of exceptional financial support from Government, for a building which has little if any prospect of ever being of any use to the residents of Bradford and beyond, until such time as anyone, including them can find a viable use for it.

“I challenge them to stand the costs whilst they find and secure a use for the building. It will not happen, because none exists and these organisations need to be held accountable, including financially.”

A spokesperson for Historic England said: “Historic England recommended the Richard Dunn Sports Centre for listing in 2022, following a careful assessment of its architectural and historic interest. The final decision to list the building at Grade II was made by the Department for Digital, Culture, Media and Sport.

“We recognise the concerns that have been expressed locally and remain committed to working constructively with Bradford Council and partners to explore realistic and sustainable options for the building’s future.”

A spokesperson for the 20th Century Society said: “We’d like to remind the councillor that the 20th Century Society and Historic England make recommendations on listing, but ultimately the decision to designate lies solely with the Minister. In the case of Richard Dunn, that was taken by the Secretary of State for Culture at the time, her erstwhile Conservative Party colleague, Nadine Dorries MP.

“What this fantastic building – and all of the many empty listed buildings across Britain – need is not political point scoring, but vision and commitment.

“In 2024 the society published ambitious proposals for ‘The Dunn’; we demonstrated how a world-class new indoor skate facility could be constructed under the big-top roof of the existing leisure centre.

"This was developed in collaboration with Skateboard GB (the national governing body), specialist architects, and in response to the wishes of Bradfordian’s who, when polled by the T&A, overwhelmingly responded that they wanted a skatepark on the site.

“Statistics confirmed that Bradford is one of the most poorly served cities in the country for skate and wheel facilities, so the opportunity for an imaginative reuse of the Richard Dunn Centre is clear.

“We stand ready and willing to work collaboratively and pragmatically with councillors of any political persuasion, to help find a viable new use for centre. No one wants to see an empty and shuttered facility.

“Rather than relitigating the past, we’d rather forge a better future for a place that’s woven into Bradford’s social and sporting history.”
