# Households set for DWP £25 payment as temperatures plummet this winter

## Brits could be eligible for a £25 Cold Weather Payment as temperatures take a plunge over the next few days

Britons could qualify for a £25 financial payment as temperatures plummet over the coming days. The Government provides Cold Weather Payments to households requiring assistance when the mercury falls below freezing during the winter period, helping towards heating bills.

To be eligible, temperatures across UK regions must be recorded or predicted to stay below 0C for seven consecutive days between November 1 and March 31. Claimants must also be in receipt of particular benefits, including pension credit, income support, income-based jobseeker's allowance and Universal Credit.

Those who meet the criteria will receive the payments automatically for each seven-day spell which satisfies the temperature requirements over the specified timeframe. Additional conditions also exist, with Britons residing in care homes typically not entitled to the financial support, while those in Scotland may alternatively qualify for the Scottish Government's fixed-rate Winter Heating Payment, valued at £62.

This comes as the UK enters a chillier spell of weather with temperatures anticipated to fall to -6C in certain regions this weekend. Friday delivered the first widespread frost of this autumn following the nation's coldest night of the season thus far on Thursday, when -6.6C was recorded in south Wales.

The UK Health Security Agency (UKHSA) has also announced its first yellow cold health alert of the season this week, affecting the north west and West Midlands from 9am on Thursday through to 12pm on Saturday, reports the Express. The agency warned that freezing conditions can result in increased pressure on healthcare services and heightened risks to vulnerable individuals' wellbeing.

Dr Anya Gopfert, consultant in public health at the UKHSA, said: "This is the first cold health alert of the winter, but we can expect more as we move further into the season. Cold weather can have a serious impact on the health of some people, including those aged 65 and over and those with pre-existing health conditions, so it is vital to check in on friends, family and neighbours who are most vulnerable."

The Department for Work and Pensions ( DWP ) explained: "If you're eligible to get Cold Weather Payments, you'll get £25 for each seven-day period of very cold weather between November 1, 2026, and March 31, 2027. You'll be able to check if your area is due a payment in November 2025.

"After each period of very cold weather in your area, you should get a payment within 14 working days. It's paid into the same bank or building society account as your benefit payments." The DWP is encouraging anyone who fails to receive a payment but thinks they're entitled to one to contact the Pension Service or Jobcentre Plus.
