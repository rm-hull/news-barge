Nidderdale National Landscape is recruiting volunteers for hour-long health walks starting in January and a 10-week nature programme. Training and kit are free.

Nidderdale National Landscape is looking for volunteers to help run free walks and outdoor sessions for people’s health, starting in January 2027.

The team behind the protected landscape west of Harrogate published the appeal on 4 November. It is part of a new programme, the Natural Health Service for North Yorkshire.

## Overview

**Volunteers are wanted for two things: hour-long “Breath of Fresh Air” walks from January 2027, and a 10-week nature and wellbeing programme running four times next year.** All training is free, outdoor kit is provided and petrol and out-of-pocket costs can be covered. To find out more, contact the programme’s coordinator, Ben Winthrop, on [ben.winthrop@northyorks.gov.uk](mailto:ben.winthrop@northyorks.gov.uk) or 07484 912072.

## The two kinds of session

**Breath of Fresh Air walks.** These are regular guided walks of about an hour, starting from different places across the National Landscape. They are meant to be accessible and low-barrier, for people who want to walk more for their health and for company. Trained staff lead them. Volunteers are asked to help on about two walks a month, on different days of the week, from January 2027.**The 10-week programme.** This is for people with mild to moderate mental or physical health conditions. The National Landscape names anxiety, depression, arthritis, diabetes and substance misuse as examples. Groups meet at different sites across Nidderdale for walking, mindfulness and guided activities with the conservation team. They are also encouraged to get confident using public transport and walking longer distances on their own.

The 10-week programmes run in February, May, August and October 2027. Sessions usually last from about 9.30am to 2.30pm, and there may be a dark sky session that runs into the evening.

## What volunteers do

A volunteer supports the leader on the day: chatting to the people taking part and helping things run smoothly. Every session is led by a qualified leader from the Nidderdale National Landscape team.

What the National Landscape asks of volunteers:

* for the 10-week programme, ideally one session a week for all ten weeks, and at least five weeks
* for the walks, ideally two a month
* training in first aid, health and safety, mental health awareness and safeguarding, which is provided free
* empathy, good listening and a commitment to including everyone

Volunteers get outdoor kit, and their petrol and out-of-pocket expenses can be paid.

## Why it is being set up

The programme is run with the region’s other protected landscapes: the North York Moors and Yorkshire Dales national parks and the Howardian Hills. It is meant to improve physical and mental health, cut social isolation and build more active communities.

The National Landscape says time in nature has been shown to reduce stress and anxiety, improve mood and lower blood pressure. It also says it can be a cheaper way of easing demand on the NHS. The programme will work with health, social care and community professionals, including GPs.

## What it means for you

If you walk in Nidderdale already and have time for a couple of walks a month, this is a way to use that, with the training paid for. The coordinator is offering an informal chat before anyone commits.

If you or someone you care for might benefit from the walks or the 10-week programme, the programme will be working with GPs and other health professionals. The National Landscape has not yet published how people can sign up as participants.
