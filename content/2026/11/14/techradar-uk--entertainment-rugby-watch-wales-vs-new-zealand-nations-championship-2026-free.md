Wales are hoping that their Nations Championship 2026 Round 5 clash with New Zealand will go down in rugby lore as their first triumph over the All Blacks in 73 years. The brutal reality after 34 defeats on the trot, however, is that it's more likely to be remembered for Beauden Barrett captaining the side for the first time and – if the Kiwis put a big enough score up – swinging the pendulum at the very top of the Southern Hemisphere standings.

Dave Rennie's men are unbeaten so far, but they're trailing South Africa on points difference. It's a yawning chasm too, with the Boks 67 points better off going into the penultimate round of the championship. The Kiwis' two remaining fixtures, however, are far more favourable than South Africa's.

Not only did Wales, sitting fifth in the Northern Hemisphere standings after two wins and two defeats, toil to victory against Japan last weekend, but they'll be without their two best players on the day at the Millennium Stadium. Aaron Wainwright has been ruled out with a calf issue, while Josh Adams, Wales' top try-scorer through the Nations Championship, has been overlooked entirely. Gabriel Hamer-Webb, winning just his second cap, takes his place on the wing.

Rennie has made a whopping 10 changes to the All Blacks lineup, with Richie Mo’unga stepping in for Damian McKenzie at fly-half, and Will Jordan, who leads the tournament try-scoring charts with six, getting the afternoon off. Fehi Fineanganofo comes in for his first New Zealand start.
