# Experts on 7 items missing from Andy Burnham's emergency WW3 stockpile list

## The Government's Be Ready campaign urges households to prepare for three days without essential services

The Government has launched a fresh initiative urging British households to brace themselves for up to three days without vital services. The Be Ready campaign, unveiled in October 2026, is calling on families to accumulate essential supplies ahead of possible crises, ranging from cyber incidents and electricity failures to severe weather conditions.

The official guidance lists bottled water, long-life food items, torches, batteries, blankets, a radio, cash, crucial paperwork and necessary medicines as must-haves. Yet specialists are highlighting a number of overlooked items that deserve a place in every emergency kit, from distress whistles to fundamental repair tools.

This recommendation arrives alongside fresh findings indicating many Britons haven't yet heeded governmental warnings, reports the Express. Research involving 2,087 adults throughout Great Britain, carried out by Find Out Now for The Grocer on 3 November, revealed that while 84% had heard of the Be Ready campaign, merely 23% had purchased extra essentials following its guidance.

A substantial 73% admitted they'd done nothing to build up their reserves. What other items ought households to include in their emergency provisions?

### 1. An emergency whistle.

Greg Potter, commercial sales manager at army uniform supplier Cadet Direct, suggests incorporating an emergency whistle into household preparations.

He cautioned that calling out for assistance can rapidly drain one's energy should someone become trapped, hurt or isolated from others during a crisis. He explained: "A small emergency whistle allows people to attract attention without using as much energy, making it a useful addition to any household emergency kit or evacuation bag."

### 2. Waterproof bags and pouches

Important possessions could be at risk from water damage, particularly during floods or evacuations. Potter suggests waterproof pouches and dry bags to safeguard identification documents, medication and smaller electronic items. He noted they are "worth adding to an emergency kit, especially if you could be at risk of flooding".

### 3. Duct tape

Tom Rogers, home expert at Manage At Home, reckons duct tape can serve multiple purposes for temporary fixes, including patching leaks and sealing damaged windows.

He explained: "Duct tape can temporarily seal a broken window, secure a loose fitting, or patch a leak - simple fixes that could prevent a small problem becoming a much bigger one during a storm or disruption."

### 4. A multi-tool or penknife

Rogers also advises keeping a basic multi-tool or penknife with your emergency provisions. Its adaptability could prove invaluable when shops and services aren't accessible, whether you're opening packages or tackling minor repairs.

### 5. Cord and basic repair supplies

Potter recommends including cord and a basic repair kit, which can assist with securing loose items, mending damaged bags and making temporary equipment fixes. These essentials require minimal storage space but could become especially valuable when supplies are hard to come by.

### 6. Sturdy footwear

In the chaos of an emergency, it's easy to overlook what you'll actually need to wear if you're forced to evacuate.

Potter suggests packing sturdy, comfortable footwear, which will help you traverse debris and uneven terrain while minimising discomfort and potential injuries.

### 7. A printed home insurance policy

Mark Finley, printing expert at Choice Stationery, cautions that families might find themselves unable to retrieve vital information should a cyberattack or extended power outage knock out online services.

He advises printing off a copy of your home insurance policy, which contains your insurer's contact information, policy details and emergency claims telephone number should your property sustain damage.

He also recommends keeping hard copies of bank contact information, mortgage or tenancy agreements, boiler warranties and pet documentation. He explained: "Paperwork rarely seems like a priority in emergency planning. But if something does go wrong, it could be one of the most useful things to have with you."
