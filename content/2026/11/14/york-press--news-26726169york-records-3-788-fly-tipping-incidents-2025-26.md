*York records 3,788 fly-tipping incidents in 2025/26 - readers have their say*

READERS have joined in a row over fly-tipping in York after it was revealed there had been 3,788 incidents in 2025/6.

Liberal Democrat Westfield ward councillor Andrew Waller said the Labour administration on City of York Council needed to say what it was doing to tackle the problem.

Cllr Jenny Kent, the council's environment spokesperson, said they had been looking at dealing with fly-tipping including through bulky waste collections for low-income households or those without a car.

It comes as council figures for 2025/6, the latest full year with figures available, were up from 2,954 the year prior.

Press readers have been commenting on the issue, with some putting forward some practical solutions.

Ellie Mosina posted: "Perhaps a tourist tax, kept locally, might help the council’s ability to tackle this sort of problem.

"I don’t think a couple of quid per person per night would have any detrimental impact on tourist numbers but would help the council to do a better job."

Joanna McGrath also had some positive suggestions on how to improve the situation.

"In countries in northern Europe they have bi annual 'kerb rubbish' days run by the council with the dates signposted well in advance.

"Every six months each 'ward' allows you to put out on the street the night before all the stuff the normal bin lorries won't take away and it's first come first served if you spot anything worth recycling.

"What's leftover the next afternoon the council take care of. It's a well organised and efficient way of dealing with the problem of too much stuff.

"These neighbourhoods are very densely populated apartment blocks etc - nobody has front driveways with room for skip hire so pragmatic solutions are needed."

A reader replied that this was a good idea, but would have to be rolled out across the whole of York for fairness. "Here in York they would only do that in certain areas which is unfair to the rest of us in the city who still have to pay to get someone to take ours away. If this was to happen it should be citywide."

Another commentator, Drago, said more fly-tipping was inevitable after recent changes at York's tips. "Who would have thought that reducing the number of public waste sites then making the rest more difficult to access would result in this?

"The council probably own many places they could utilise to make a very good sized household waste site with good access in and out without blocking a road and businesses access to their premises."

Another reader wrote: "This is negative politics. It’s designed to makes us all think there there is some easy solution to waste management that the council are withholding from us through spite.

"In reality, anyone with friends and relatives elsewhere in the UK will be aware that this is a national issue getting worse.

"The regular glum-faced photo ops from this councillor are purely designed to play politics rather than work cooperatively for constructive solutions for the city. Expect more of this as we move forward to the council elections. Canny residents will note positive councillors of all parties, not ones who play negative politics like this."

But Ellie Mosina also had some sympathy for the tricky task facing local authorities: "Given the enormous pressure under which local authorities operate and their statutory responsibilities it’s remarkable that things are as well run as they are."
