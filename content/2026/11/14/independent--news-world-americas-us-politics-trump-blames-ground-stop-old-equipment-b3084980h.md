President Donald Trump suggested Friday that aging equipment, or “old broken stuff,” may be behind a massive air traffic control outage that grounded flights across the U.S., as Democrats demand answers over the airport chaos.

Trump had not posted about the meltdown on Truth Social or made public remarks Saturday morning. Amid Friday’s nationwide ground stop, he instead posted about reviving the entertainment industry, CNN's Kaitlan Collins noted.

Fox News correspondent Peter Doocy said he spoke on the phone with Trump as the disruption unfolded, and that the president said the outage did not appear to be caused by a cyberattack or AI.

“He said instead this seems to be a situation where there’s a bunch of old broken stuff in the infrastructure that the FAA relies on to keep planes in the sky,” Doocy said during a live appearance on *The Ingraham Angle* from the White House.

“He said there’s a bunch of – some of this is like 55-year-old equipment that needs to keep working, keep running, and that they have worked on it, they’ve talked to Congress about replacing, it just hasn’t gotten replaced yet. So that is the first information that we have from the president.”

![President Donald Trump blamed aging FAA equipment for a nationwide flight outage that grounded planes as Democrats criticized the airport chaos](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/14/14/2026-11-14T110018Z_932004478_RC2LKOAEPGUW_RTRMADP_3_IRAN-CRISIS-TRUMP.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The president's comments came as Democrats called for a closer examination of the communications failure and the outdated technology underpinning the nation's air traffic control system.

Rep. Ted Lieu, a California Democrat, called the FAA's communications failure completely unacceptable, telling the *Los Angeles Times:* “I urge the FAA and the Trump administration to make sure this never happens again.”

Lieu also called for congressional hearings to determine what caused the breakdown and what needs to be done to prevent another nationwide disruption.

“What just happened is enormously damaging to trust in our air system, and our air system is a vital component of our national economy,” he said.

Sen. Tammy Duckworth, an Illinois Democrat and ranking member of the Senate Aviation, Space and Innovation Subcommittee, also called for a full investigation into the outage.

“The safety of our skies is built on redundancies,” she said in a statement. “Whatever led to the nationwide ground stop will warrant a thorough and complete investigation.”

![Secretary of Transportation Sean Duffy attributed the ground stop to aging technology that became overwhelmed with data, disrupting communications between pilots and air traffic controllers](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/14/15/2305027509..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Pete Buttigieg was in disbelief after watching a joint press conference with Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy and Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin, according to *The New York Times.*

“You can just write down the comic book asterisk swearing characters,” he told the newspaper. “I have questions.”

The FAA ordered the hours-long nationwide ground stop Friday after a communications failure disrupted 22 regional air traffic control centers. The order temporarily halted departing flights while allowing planes already in the air to land, triggering widespread delays as officials worked to restore normal operations.

Duffy later attributed the problem to aging technology that became overwhelmed with data, disrupting communications between pilots and air traffic controllers.

The failure involved decades-old copper-wire technology used to transmit voice and radar data between aircraft and air traffic control facilities.

Duffy said inconsistencies in the system made it difficult for controllers to reliably communicate with pilots, prompting the FAA to halt departures until the issue could be addressed.

Knock-on delays could last days, the agency warned.
