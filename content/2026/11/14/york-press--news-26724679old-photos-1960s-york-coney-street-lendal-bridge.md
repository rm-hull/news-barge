![1960: Cyclists outnumber motor vehicles as traffic makes its way across Lendal Bridge.](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/18797916.jpg?type=mds-article-962)

For some, these photographs will unlock memories of a York they knew well.

For others, they offer a glimpse of a city almost impossible to imagine today.

Taken in the early 1960s, the images capture familiar streets at a time when smoking chimneys, bustling roads, lost pubs and long-gone businesses were part of everyday life.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21710618.jpg?type=mds-article-620)1960: Smoke billows from chimneys across Holgate, creating a very different skyline from the one residents know today.

In the city centre, Border’s café and grocery shop could be found on Coney Street, while nearby Lendal Bridge was busy with traffic of a rather different kind.

![1960: Border's cafe and grocery shop pictured on Coney Street in the heart of York.](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21710617.jpg?type=mds-article-620)1960: Border's cafe and grocery shop pictured on Coney Street in the heart of York.

A photograph from 1960 shows cyclists streaming across the bridge and appearing to outnumber the motor vehicles around them.

There was royal excitement that year too, when the Queen Mother visited the Mansion House.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/18716900.jpg?type=mds-article-620)1960: The Queen Mother during a visit to York’s Mansion House.

But these pictures also capture a city on the brink of significant change.

St Saviourgate was photographed in 1961 before the Stonebow development reshaped the surrounding area.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/16178726.jpg?type=mds-article-620)1961: St Saviourgate pictured before the Stonebow development transformed this part of York.

By 1962, more of the old York was disappearing. Botterills Horse and Carriage Depository in Rougier Street was being torn down, its demolition capturing the shift from an age of horse-drawn transport towards the increasingly car-dominated city.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/16178731.jpg?type=mds-article-620)1962: Botterills Horse and Carriage Depository in Rougier Street is demolished as York enters a new era.

At Stonebow, cars can be seen parked on land where the Co-op and Stonebow House would later stand.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21710620.jpg?type=mds-article-620)1962: Cars parked at Stonebow on the site where the Co-op and Stonebow House would later stand.

And anyone walking along Coney Street today may struggle to picture one particularly bustling scene from 1962.

Long before pedestrianisation, shoppers stuck to the pavements as cars and lorries competed for space along the road down the middle of one of York’s best-known shopping streets.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21710619.jpg?type=mds-article-620)1962: Bustling Coney Street, when traffic still ran through the shopping street and pedestrians were confined to the pavement

The same year, the Plumbers Arms in Skeldergate was captured by the camera, preserving another piece of the city’s past. The pub features among The Press's extensive archive collection of photographs of 1960s York.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21710621.jpg?type=mds-article-620)1962 - The Plumbers Arms in Skeldergate.

Individually, the photographs are snapshots of ordinary life. Together, they tell a bigger story of a city beginning to change.

![1963 - H Bushell & Sons in Piccadilly.](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21710622.jpg?type=mds-article-620)1963 - H Bushell & Sons in Piccadilly.

More than 60 years later, York’s streets are still evolving, but how much of the city in these photographs do you remember?

Were you cycling across Lendal Bridge, shopping in Coney Street or drinking in the Plumbers Arms?
