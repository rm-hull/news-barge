# DWP new update for Universal Credit claimants overpaid in error

## Universal Credit claimants can be required to repay money overpaid due to DWP official errors

Universal Credit claimants can be forced to repay money they received due to errors made by the Department for Work and Pensions itself. Liberal Democrat MP Tom Gordon, who represents Harrogate and Knaresborough, has submitted two written parliamentary questions seeking clarity on the issue.

He requested information about how many Universal Credit overpayments resulting from official mistakes had been recovered over the past three years, along with their combined value. He also enquired about the number of applications to waive recovery of these overpayments that had been received and granted during the same timeframe.

Lilian Greenwood, Parliamentary Under-Secretary of State at the DWP, confirmed that official error overpayments are handled under the same recovery procedures as those caused by claimants themselves. She stated: "Official error overpayments within Universal Credit are treated the same as claimant error overpayments, as all overpayments are recoverable and we no longer track them separately for administrative purposes."

She referred Mr Gordon to the department's published fraud and error estimates and its annual report and accounts, which contain information about debt waivers. However, the response failed to provide specific figures for official error overpayments recovered during the three-year period requested.

Under Universal Credit rules, an overpayment can be reclaimed even when the claimant did nothing wrong and the error was entirely the department's fault - a stark contrast to some other benefits, where overpayments are automatically written off, reports the Express.

As outlined in the DWP's benefit overpayment recovery guidance, excess Universal Credit payments are recoverable regardless of whether they stemmed from a claimant error or an official mistake. There are several methods for recouping overpayments, including deductions from future payments or an agreed instalment plan.

That said, debts can be reduced or suspended based on the claimant's personal circumstances. For instance, repayments may be waived if paying them back would cause considerable financial hardship.

Waiver decisions can factor in a claimant's financial situation, the effect of recovery on their health or family, and the conduct of the department itself. The DWP may also consider how the overpayment originally came about. However, errors that are the DWP's fault do not automatically result in debts being written off. According to official guidance, waivers require compelling grounds, with each request judged on the claimant's individual circumstances.
