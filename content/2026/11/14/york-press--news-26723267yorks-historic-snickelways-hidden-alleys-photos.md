## Beyond Shambles: Hidden stories of York's famous snickelways

Lady Peckett's Yard by William Thackray

*York's historic snickelways and hidden alleys in photos by WILLIAM THACKRAY*

In early August the Press told the story of how as a ten-year-old I came to York on a school trip from Rotherham in 1955. And I was completely bowled over – “solid gone” with York just like Baloo was “solid gone” with the monkeys’ jazz music in “Jungle Book”!

Now a retired vicar in my eighties and living in Northallerton I’ve completed four photographic books about the history of York.

One of them is about The Snickelways of York, familiar to many York people through Mark Jones’s wonderful book of 1983.

Revisiting these alleys and short cuts was my tribute to him and his book.

I’m afraid I don’t sketch these days so my book only contains photos! But let me share 5 of them with you.

**1 Lady Peckett’s Yard**

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21708379.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Lady Peckett's Yard by William Thackray

The most atmospheric snickelway, named after Alice Peckett whose husband John was Lord Mayor 1701-02. The front of their lovely timber-framed house, Herbert House, is in Pavement and is now home to York Gin. As the picture shows, at one time it was Storey’s Cordwainer’s (a posh word for an upmarket shoemaker).

**2 Powell’s Yard** There is another very attractive little passageway – Powell’s Yard, off Goodramgate. To the left is a smart half-timbered house, now part of a shop. And ahead is a brick house with a curving staircase up to the back door. Quite different in appearance, but both from the 16th century.**3 The Shambles**

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21708381.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Shambles by William Thackray

No need to identify this snickelway! The Shambles was once chock-a-block with butchers’ shops but these days they’ve become Harry Potter shops and other assorted spooky emporiums.

**4 Pope’s Head Alley**

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21708382.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Pope's Head Alley by William Thackray

And here is York’s narrowest snickelway – Pope’s Head Alley. If ever you urgently need a shortcut from High Ousegate to Peter Lane, this is the one. But be warned – it’s just 31 inches across the narrowest point! It can also be quite dark but at least it has one of the handsome gas lamps installed in many snickelways.

**5 Pinder’s Court**

And finally here is York’s saddest snickelway – Pinder’s Court in what was Stonegate Arcade. With several shops it opened for business in the 1980s but closed down a few years later. It still has two locked gates on Stonegate but inside all is deserted and litter-strewn. But what’s this? A handsome but forlorn Victorian pillar-box with no collections anytime soon!
