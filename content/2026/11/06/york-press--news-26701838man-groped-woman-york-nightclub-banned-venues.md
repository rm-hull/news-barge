A man who groped a woman in a York nightclub has been banned from all licensed venues in the city centre for six months.

Keiron Barlow, 24, “lunged” at the woman then grabbed her breasts at Club Salvation in George Hudson Street, prosecutor Martin Butterworth said.

He said the woman had been out with a friend and recalled an “unknown” man, revealed to be Barlow, dancing with them earlier that night.

Mr Butterworth said the woman remembered Barlow being pushed away from the pair after trying to take one of their hands.

But the woman later returned to the dance floor and said Barlow appeared and “grabbed my breasts”, York Magistrates’ Court heard.

The woman then pushed Barlow away and slapped him, the hearing was told.

Barlow, of Burgess Avenue, Howden, denied sexually assaulting the woman but was convicted on October 8.

## 'I don’t feel like I can trust anyone like I did before' - victim

In a statement read in court, the woman said she struggled to trust people after she was assaulted by Barlow.

“After the incident I don’t feel like I can trust anyone like I did before,” she said.

She said she is now “constantly nervous” when “going into town” and avoids clubbing.

“I’m worried it will happen to me again,” she said.

Magistrates told Barlow he had been convicted of a serious offence but the matter could be dealt with by a community order.

They passed a 12-month community order and banned him from all licensed venues in the YO1 postcode for six months.

Barlow was also ordered to pay £964 in costs, including £200 in compensation for the victim.

Magistrates said Barlow would not be fitted with an electronic tag to monitor his whereabouts due to this being unsuitable because of his job.

It means Barlow will be responsible for staying out of licensed venues in York city centre, magistrates said.

They said Barlow may have to return to court to be resentenced if he is seen in any pub, club or bar in the city centre.

They also said Barlow must tell the probation service before changing his address.

Kristina Lancaster, defending, said Barlow had said the incident was an “accident” but accepts the impact it had on the victim.

She said Barlow has no previous convictions and has a good job which “remains secure”, despite his conviction.

She added that Barlow lives with his parents and is saving to buy a home of his own.

Barlow wore a dark blue suit to the hearing and spoke only to confirm his name and to say he understood the sentence.
