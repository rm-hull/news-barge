Construction has begun on a major electricity transmission project near Stornoway, marking a significant step towards connecting the Western Isles directly to mainland Great Britain’s power network.

SSEN Transmission has broken ground on its Lewis Hub at Arnish Moor, the first major construction milestone in the Western Isles Connection project.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21680115.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The ground breaking at Arnish Moor near Stornoway marks the project’s progression from initial site preparation into main construction. *(Image: Supplied)*

The hub will include a high-voltage direct current (HVDC) converter station and an AC substation, enabling up to 1.8GW of renewable electricity generation to feed into the national grid.

Highlands-based contractor RJ McLeod has been appointed to deliver the first major construction package. Balfour Beatty, Linxon, Hitachi Energy and NKT are also involved in the wider programme.

SSEN Transmission aims to spend £125 million with local businesses over the project's lifetime. More than 40 local businesses are already involved, with more than 200 jobs supported so far.

The scheme is also expected to create more than 70 apprenticeship and trainee opportunities, helping develop skills in the Western Isles.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21680114.jpg?type=mds-article-620)An indicative image of how the Lewis Hub will look once complete. *(Image: Supplied)*

The project is expected to support wider investment in local housing and infrastructure. New homes are planned at Melbost West and Stornoway Airport, while improvements to Arnish Road will also receive funding.

A £1.06 million community benefit fund is due to launch soon. Total community funding from transmission projects in the region is expected to exceed £5 million, subject to future project approvals.

Sandy Mactaggart, director of offshore delivery at SSEN Transmission, said: "Breaking ground on the Lewis Hub is a hugely significant moment for the project and for the Western Isles.

"A direct connection between the Western Isles and the main GB transmission network has been an ambition for decades.

"Through the Lewis Hub and wider Western Isles Connection, we are now turning that ambition into reality, unlocking the islands’ vast renewable energy potential and strengthening the electricity network for generations to come.

"I’d like to thank the local community for their support and engagement throughout the development of the project, and everyone who has taken the time to speak to us and share their views.

"We look forward to continuing that relationship as construction progresses."

Robert van Beek, managing director of RJ McLeod, said: "RJ McLeod is delighted to be working with SSEN Transmission to deliver the first phase of the Lewis Hub Western Isles Link, a project of national importance that will support both the energy transition and the UK's long-term energy security.

"For more than half a century, we have worked across the Western Isles, building strong and enduring relationships within local communities and with regional businesses.

"We look forward to working in partnership with them once again to successfully deliver this significant project for SSEN Transmission."

Eilidh MacKenzie, safety officer at SSEN Transmission, said: "I’ve always had a real passion for safety, so the opportunity to contribute to a major project like the Lewis Hub really appealed to me.

"It’s incredibly rewarding to help build a positive safety culture, make sure everyone goes home safe each day, and play a part in such an important project here in the Western Isles."
