With a pledge to “keep the fire burning”, the Forged event proved to be one of the hottest tickets in town.

For those who wanted to do something a little different on Bonfire Night, the Keighley & Worth Valley Railway was the place to be.

The recently-restored Grade II listed station at Keighley was the starting point for our adventure as we climbed aboard a steam train after dark.

With a blast of the engine’s whistle, we began our journey into the world of Forged, with the promise of mysterious railway characters, strange stories and a little audience participation.

![Action from the Forged event on the Keighley & Worth Valley Railway](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21677763.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Passengers arriving at Oxenhope during the Forged event on the Keighley & Worth Valley Railway *(Image: T&A)*

The main lights in the carriage were switched off, with illumination provided by subtle and atmospheric red lighting.

Sparks from the locomotive drifted past the window as our train climbed towards Ingrow.

With an air of mystery, an actor playing the role of a superstitious railway fitter informed passengers that we were heading for the Forge at the request of the Chief Engineer.

He knew not why, but all would become clear.

As the train chuffed along the Worth Valley, the lights from lineside cottages and mills twinkled in the darkness.

The Furnace Crew visited our carriage with spooky railway tales, some good humour and snippets of interesting local history.

Anticipation and excitement in our carriage grew – especially among the younger passengers – as we approached Oxenhope.

Arriving at the heritage railway’s southern terminus, we stepped onto the platform to be greeted by a stern-looking railwayman in a flat cap holding a fire staff and pointing the way.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21677766.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Action from the Forged event on the Keighley & Worth Valley Railway *(Image: T&A)*

As we entered the Forge – otherwise known as Oxenhope Carriage Shed – a smoky atmosphere set the scene.

Amid an industrial setting, there was time to grab some food and drink before the main show began.

A pint of Timothy Taylor’s Landlord proved the perfect antidote to the dry, hot conditions of the foundry!

Then, the sounds of machinery began to build, lights shifted through the darkness and mysterious figures appeared out of the steam.

The Chief Engineer walked up and down the room as the Forge began to awaken in stages.

The drama was ramped up when a performer sent shimmers of glowing sparks into the darkness.

Next came a spectacular display of flames, with fire-eating performances and impressive twirling of fire staffs.

In what was a truly immersive experience, we were stood between three performers who were stood on various stages.

![Action from the Forged event on the Keighley & Worth Valley Railway](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21677764.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Sparks fly in Oxenhope Carriage Shed during the Forged event on Bonfire Night *(Image: T&A)*

As the background music intensified, the show built towards a finale.

It was difficult to know which way to look as a magical conclusion brought together fire, sparks, steam, sound and light.

The things we’d been told on the train came in handy as the Chief Engineer asked questions in the hope that we could help him bring the Forge to life.

As we left the Forge to board our train back to Keighley, there were plenty of smiles to be seen on the faces of young and old alike.

The Keighley & Worth Valley Railway is on a roll right now, with the Forged event coming hot on the heels of last weekend’s sold-out Scare Train.

Soon, the railway will be transformed again when the eagerly-anticipated festive season begins.

Could the passionate volunteers who saved the line after its closure in the 1960s ever have imagined the extent to which it would become a line for all occasions? To draw a comparison with the Forged event, it was those dedicated railway enthusiasts who provided the initial spark and - in 2026 - the fire is still well and truly burning.
