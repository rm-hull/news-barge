Tom Gordon, MP for Harrogate and Knaresborough, has criticised Yorkshire Water after the company was downgraded to the lowest rating in the Environment Agency’s latest environmental performance assessment.

The assessment, published on 29 October, gave Yorkshire Water a one-star rating out of a possible four, classifying it as a poorly performing company. It had previously received two stars.

The Environment Agency’s assessment covers pollution, environmental compliance and other aspects of water company performance. Its report recorded 260 pollution incidents from Yorkshire Water’s sewerage assets in 2025, against 151 in the previous year.

The wider assessment recorded 335 pollution incidents from Yorkshire Water’s water supply and sewerage assets during 2025. The company also had 13 serious pollution incidents, placing it among the companies with the highest numbers of serious incidents.

Mr Gordon said the downgrade was no surprise, pointing to concerns about pollution in the River Nidd and his recent meeting with Surfers Against Sewage.

> **Tom Gordon MP said:**
>
> Nobody in Harrogate and Knaresborough is surprised. Just last week I met Surfers Against Sewage to talk about pollution in the Nidd. We have the same conversation every time, because nothing changes.
>
> The Conservatives broke this industry. For fourteen years they let water companies load up on debt, pay-out to shareholders and pump sewage into our rivers, while they cut the Environment Agency’s budget and voted against tougher limits on sewage dumping. They left behind a regulator with no teeth and an industry with no shame.
>
> Labour promised to clean up this mess, but more than two years on, the bosses who presided over this are still being paid handsomely. Ministers brought in a bonus ban and then stood by while payments are routed through offshore companies. That’s not tough regulation. It’s a loophole you could drive a tanker through.
>
> Residents are watching their bills climb while sewage flows into our rivers. It’s a slap in the face to every household in Yorkshire.
>
> Labour needs to stop tinkering and act. That means scrapping Ofwat now, not years down the line, turning water companies into public benefit companies that put the environment before shareholders, and making sure no boss of a one-star company takes home a penny more.

## Executive pay under scrutiny

The MP also questioned the remuneration of Yorkshire Water’s chief executive, Nicola Shaw.

Accounts for the 2025/26 financial year show Ms Shaw received a remuneration package of £732,000 from Yorkshire Water, alongside a further £660,000 from its parent company, Kelda Holdings.

Yorkshire Water has said the additional payment relates to work carried out for other companies within the Kelda group, including investor engagement and financial oversight. The company maintains that the payment is fixed, is not performance-related and is funded by shareholders rather than customer bills.

The payments have attracted scrutiny because of the company’s environmental record and rising customer bills.

## Chief executive to step down

On 30 October, the day after the Environment Agency announced its rating, Yorkshire Water confirmed that Nicola Shaw would step down as chief executive on 1 January 2027.

She is due to be replaced by Nicola Medalova, a former National Grid executive, who is joining the company as chief executive-designate ahead of the transition.

Yorkshire Water has acknowledged that its environmental performance needs to improve, saying it is focusing on preventing pollution incidents, improving the condition of its assets and upgrading infrastructure.
