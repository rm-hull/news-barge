*Glasgow-based SOLIS Group is using its own Asset Intelligence Centre, engineering expertise and new partnerships to make building management more sustainable and efficient*

Integrity, said the 20th century scholar and novelist C.S. Lewis, is doing the right thing even when no one is watching. It’s a maxim that has been adopted by SOLIS Group as one of its core values – accompanied by innovation, collaboration and sustainability.

Murdo McCaw head of facilities management at the Glasgow-based company, explains that the main sectors SOLIS serves are retail, industrial and social housing, plus the hospitality industry.

The group approach, he adds, enables clients to move from reactive maintenance to proactive asset management, improving reliability, extending equipment lifespan, ensuring compliance and supporting long-term sustainability goals.

The company was founded in 2020 and in the past 18 months has doubled its client portfolio and moved to new premises in Maryhill. Now with a staff of 22 it works with major national clients across a range of sectors that include Marks & Spencer, waste management firm Viridor, The Royal and Ancient Golf Club, Morrisons supermarkets and, locally, the Queen’s Cross Housing Association.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21678284.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Murdo McCaw head of facilities management at Solis *(Image: Supplied)*

With most of its business based in Scotland’s central belt, SOLIS is increasingly expanding across the UK with projects in locations such as Peterborough, Leicester, Birmingham and Croydon – and it has now established a presence in Manchester.

Essentially, McCaw says the group delivers bespoke engineering and facilities management solutions across a wide range of sectors. These combine its engineering expertise with intelligent technologies, including IoT (Internet of Things) enabled remote monitoring, predictive maintenance and data-driven analytics.

Its use of digital twin technology brings together several factors that include sensors and connected devices giving information about temperature, energy use, occupancy, air quality and equipment performance; data from heating, ventilation, air conditioning, lighting and security and other systems that use data analytics and AI.

This success hasn’t gone unnoticed: the company was named the most Client-Focused Facilities Management and Workforce Development Company in the Build Awards 2026 – UK, and among other recognitions was a finalist in the Environmental Award category of the Herald Scotland and Glasgow Chamber of Commerce Inspiring City Awards earlier this year.

Innovation, stresses McCaw is central to the group’s DNA and it has helped its customers to achieve energy saving performances that exceed 50%.

Last year the group announced a strategic partnership with Glasgow Caledonian University aiming to develop an advanced digital platform that would redefine building maintenance delivering smarter, more efficient and more sustainable outcomes.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21678288.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The company was named the most Client-Focused Facilities Management and Workforce Development Company in the Build Awards 2026 *(Image: KOKSIOWESTCOAST)*

The collaboration began after the university’s research team engaged with SOLIS through an innovation voucher supported by the West of Scotland KTP (Knowledge Transfer Partnerships) Centre and focusing on the design, deployment and maintenance of an Industrial Internet of Things predictive monitoring system to address clients’ potential security concerns, involving data services, integration, intelligence, and visualisation.

“We’ve been deploying remote monitoring since the company was founded and were looking for this to evolve into something bigger,” says McCaw. “Our USP is that we offer remote monitoring, with reactive maintenance in addition.

“We decided to build our own remote monitoring platform, and that has become our Asset Intelligence Centre,” says McCaw. “The AIC watches our clients’ plant rooms, heating systems and building conditions around the clock. Before a customer even picks up the phone, we already know there’s an issue. Often there’s an engineer on the way before anyone has noticed a problem, which saves tenants, customers and managers downtime and cost.”

SOLIS put its idea to the Knowledge Transfer Partnership and Innovate UK and secured significant funding from the latter to support the development of technology to transform the way building conditions and assets are monitored and managed across the social housing sector. “To us, GCU was the ideal candidate for us to partner with and we’ve taken on a PhD student, Atuahene Kwasi Barima, as our new West of Scotland KTP Centre Associate,” says McCaw.

“We have weekly meetings with academics at GCU and with the Knowledge Transfer Partnership and Innovate UK to ensure things are going smoothly as they should and so far, everything has been extremely successful.”

SOLIS believes that securing the Innovate UK funding is a major milestone for the group and McCaw says its technology will also help with compliance with the stringent and fast-evolving industry regulatory requirements, including HNTAS (Heat Network Technical Assurance) and Awaab’s Law.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21678283.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Solis Group *(Image: Supplied)*

The AIC gives landlords and heat network operators the evidence regulators are asking for: live flow temperatures, pressures and plant performance, plus early warnings of the conditions that lead to damp and mould and begin any needed repairs within set timescales.

“We can now provide greater visibility of building conditions, asset performance and potential risks, with earlier intervention and more proactive, compliant property management,” McCaw says. He again emphasises the group’s commitment to sustainability as not simply an objective but a fundamental part of how it operates. “Overall, we have a very robust ESG strategy and are aiming to be paperless by 2030, he adds.

“What gives SOLIS the edge in the competitive space of facilities management, especially concerning social housing, is that our unique technology ensures that buildings are compliant and energy usage costs are significantly reduced. And our customers know that with skilled engineers, our Asset Intelligence Centre and a 24/7 helpdesk, we deliver dependable solutions that keep their facilities safe and operating at their best.”
