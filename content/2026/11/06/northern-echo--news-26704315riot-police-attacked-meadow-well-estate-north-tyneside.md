The emergency services came under attack from bricks, missiles and fireworks in “pre-arranged” disorder linked to a false rumour about a house of multi-occupancy (HMO), police have said.

Trouble flared on the Meadow Well estate in North Tyneside, with Northumbria Police reporting anti-social behaviour and violent disorder.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21678052.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Riot police form a barrier as tensions escalate on the Meadow Well estate in North Shields, North Tyneside *(Image: North News & Pictures Ltd)*

It was understood a protest was raised about fears that a building would be used as an HMO but that was not correct and it was going to be a supermarket.

In a post shared on its social media channels, the force said officers were on the scene at Waterville Road area of the estate.

It said: “Emergency services in the area have been subjected to violent attacks including having bricks, missiles, and fireworks thrown at them.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21678053.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Riot police form a barrier as tensions escalate on the Meadow Well estate in North Shields, North Tyneside *(Image: North News & Pictures Ltd)*

“We believe the disorder has been pre-arranged and relates to misinformation in the community that the completed building is going to be used as a house of multi-occupancy (HMO) – which is not accurate.

“The site is planned for use as a supermarket.

“We are sharing this information to stop speculation in the community which may lead to further disorder.

“This behaviour is completely unacceptable, and we are working swiftly to identify those responsible and deal with them robustly.

“We simply will not tolerate this behaviour – and it doesn’t belong within our neighbourhoods.”

Anyone with information about the disorder should contact police.

The Meadow Well estate was the scene of riots in the early 1990s.
