Five men have been found guilty of conspiracy to murder involving a brutal nighttime machete attack in which a man had his hand hacked off.

Mohammed Zayad Khan also suffered multiple other injuries, including the partial amputation of his leg before being left for dead.

His nephew, Mohammed Usama Khan, suffered a slash wound to his neck.

Today at Bradford Crown Court Mohammed Waqqas Khan, 38, of Folkestone Street, Bradford; Rahees Khan, 43, of no fixed abode but formerly of Granton Street, Bradford; Hanzalah Saladin, 27, of no fixed abode, Bradford; Adam Takolia, 29, of no fixed abode, Bradford; and a fifth man who cannot be named for legal reasons, were found guilty of conspiracy to murder both victims by His Honour Judge Jonathan Rose.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21679780.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Mohammed Waqqas Khan *(Image: West Yorkshire Police)*

Rahees Khan and Alice Dobson, 31, of Nether Hall Road, Baildon, a former Police Community Support Officer with West Yorkshire Police, were also found guilty of perverting the course of justice by obtaining and disseminating confidential information held by the force.

The trial, which began in July, was paused in August when the jury was discharged following an allegation of jury tampering.

When the trial resumed Judge Rose took the decision to continue without a jury, with him alone deciding on the verdicts.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21679773.jpg?type=mds-article-620)ADAM TAKOLIA *(Image: West Yorkshire Police)*

Today, West Yorkshire Police confirmed that a man has been charged in relation to witness intimidation.

Amir Khan, 34, of Folkestone Street, was charged with witness intimidation.

He was remanded into custody and is next due in court on January 18 next year.

Two women aged 36 and 42 were also arrested on suspicion of witness intimidation.

Both have been bailed pending further enquiries.

In his opening to the trial in July, prosecution barrister Stephen Wood KC said the two victims, referred to as Zayad and Usama, were together in a VW Tiguan on Tickhill Street on the night of May 11 last year that was rammed without warning by a Mitsubishi Shogun.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21679786.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Rahees Khan *(Image: West Yorkshire Police)*

The Shogun then drove into the side of the Tiguan to block it between two other vehicles before the Tiguan was driven into by a BMW X3.

With their car trapped, Zayad and Usama got out and tried to flee but were chased, caught, and attacked by three men armed with machete-type bladed weapons.

Usama was struck by what appeared to have been a machete, which caused significant wounds to the back of his neck.

However, even though he was injured, he managed to get into a car driven by a friend, which then drove off.

Mr Wood told the jury: “Zayad, however, was not so lucky. He did not manage to escape.

“He tried to flee. He ran in the opposite direction [but] was pursued [and] surrounded by a group of males who relentlessly struck him with heavy bladed weapons.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21679788.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Hanzalah Saladin *(Image: West Yorkshire Police)*

“His head, his arms, and his legs were targeted.

“There was nothing that Zayad could do to protect himself from these blows.

“He sustained a wound to the top of his head, which resulted in an underlying skull fracture, but not before his left hand was completely severed.

“He also sustained a partial amputation of the left leg at knee level.

“I am sure that you will each understand from that description that he also suffered significant blood loss.

![Dobbs](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21679970.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Dobson *(Image: West Yorkshire Police)*

“You might agree with the prosecution when we observe that, when his assailants had finished with him, they left him for dead.”

“No-one lifted a finger to help him.”

Judge Rose has ordered pre-sentence reports for all guilty parties, which means sentencing will not take place until the New Year.

Judge Rose said he expects to pass “substantial” sentences for conspiracy to murder on February 8, 2027, but that Dobson’s sentencing for her “extremely serious” offence may not take place on the same date.

She was granted conditional bail but ordered to surrender her passport to obviate flight risk.

After the convictions, Detective Chief Superintendent Tanya Wilkins, of West Yorkshire Police, said: "This was a calculated, pre-planned, horrific attack on two men on the streets of Bradford where these men thought they could take the law into their own hands.

“Today ends a lengthy court process. The victims are still living with the horrendous impact of the attack today.

“The conviction of PCSO Dobson for perverting the course of justice represents a serious breach of trust placed in those who serve our communities.

“We make it very clear to all staff from the outset of their employment that they must only access information held on police systems for a legitimate policing purpose. The public quite rightly should expect that any sensitive and confidential information recorded about them is protected.

“Our officers and staff are not above the law, and this case is an example of us rightly holding PCSO Dobson to account for her actions following a thorough Counter Corruption investigation.

“PCSO Dobson remains suspended from duty and will now face a gross misconduct hearing.”
