A COLLEGE has opened new construction training facilities to support young people with hands-on skills and pathways into employment.

Shipley College in Saltaire held a ribbon-cutting ceremony to mark the official opening of the workshop, which will deliver practical, employment-focused training for 16 to 24-year-olds.

The new City & Guilds Construction Skills programme includes carpentry, joinery and broader construction training, with an emphasis on real-world experience and a conservation focus.

James Haigh, Construction & NEET Lead at Shipley College, said: "We've designed this training around what employers actually need.

"It isn't simply about teaching somebody how to use a saw or build a wall.

"It's about building confidence, developing professional attitudes and giving young people the practical skills they need to succeed in the workplace."

The first course begins the week of November 23, 2026, and is aimed at 16 to 18-year-olds not currently in education, employment or training.

They will study on Tuesdays and Thursdays, working towards Entry Level or Level 1 qualifications.

A second, 14-week programme for 19 to 24-year-olds will launch in January 2027.

The courses are designed to offer more than just technical skills, with an emphasis on employability and personal development.

Mr Haigh said: "Having worked in construction education and alongside conservation organisations for many years, I know how powerful hands-on learning can be.

"We've brought those experiences together to create something genuinely different for Bradford's young people.

"This is about giving them the confidence and foundations to build a future."

Shipley College has adapted existing horticultural space to accommodate practical construction training.

Martin Silverwood, principal and CEO of Shipley College, said: "This opening represents a significant step forward in how we respond to the needs of Bradford and the wider region.

"We know employers need people with practical skills, confidence and the right attitude to work.

"This is about opening doors, raising aspirations and making sure that young people across our district have access to opportunities that genuinely lead somewhere."

The launch was welcomed by local employers and Bradford Council.

Councillor Andrew Judson, deputy leader of Bradford Council and portfolio holder for economy, regeneration and planning, said: "Opening new, inclusive pathways into education, skills and employment is central to our ambitions for Bradford.

"We want every young person, whatever their starting point, to have the opportunity to develop their talents and build a successful future."
