A disgraced teacher who said he'd nearly put his “sausage into" a colleague was told off by shocked pupils over a lewd remark in class.

Students were left taken aback by assistant principal Sam Piggott's comments, telling him: "Sir, you cannot say that".

A teaching misconduct panel found Piggott made a string of “sexually explicit and misogynistic” comments, to the horror of fellow staff and students at North Shore Academy in Stockton.

He said in front of pupils that he had “a big one” while making a gesture towards his genitals.

And when a teacher nearly bumped into him, the now-33-year-old said in the earshot of pupils: "Sorry miss, I’ve just nearly put my sausage into you there."

A Teaching Regulation Agency misconduct panel found Piggott had shown a “deep-seated and wholly inappropriate attitude towards women” and banned him from the classroom.

The report said Piggott made the sausage remark while carrying sausages and mash after nearly bumping into a female teacher leading her class along a corridor.

The teacher told the panel her class was lined up behind her, with about five pupils probably within earshot.

She said the comment left her feeling “disgusted”, “objectified and embarrassed”.

The panel said he made a deliberate choice of words, with no plausible reason for using them without sexual intention. It described the comment as “demeaning and wholly inappropriate”.

When a pupil asked why he liked energy drinks, Piggott said he would have two at once as “it is like juggling two women”.

He then described the taste, saying: “I like how it drips down your throat”.

The panel heard Piggott made a hand gesture as he spoke and paused for a reaction.

Pupils were said to have been taken aback, with some responding: “Sir, you cannot say that”.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/13001120.jpg?type=mds-article-620)North Shore Academy

In another incident, Piggott was told a female teacher had left him a present for covering one of her lessons.

He replied that he hoped the present was a sex act.

He later returned to apologise, but the panel said the comment went “entirely beyond the parameters of any acceptable workplace banter” and was “objectifying, demeaning and a wholly inappropriate comment to make to colleagues”.

The panel found both comments were sexual in nature.

On another occasion, Piggott walked into a classroom while a colleague was handing out praise postcards to pupils.

After the teacher said he only had “big ones” left, Piggott replied: “I've got a big one”.

Piggott was said to made a gesture interpreted as a reference to his penis.

The panel called the remark a “clear and unacceptable sexual innuendo” in front of pupils and said it showed a “wholly unacceptable and entirely wrong way to behave”.

Piggott also told a colleague: “I cannot be trusted around drunk children”.

The panel did not find this comment was sexual in nature or that Piggott intended it to have sinister connotations.

However, it said the remark was negligent because it could obviously be interpreted that way and bring the teaching profession into disrepute.

Piggott joined North Shore Academy as a teaching assistant in 2018 and was promoted to assistant principal in January 2024.

He was suspended in March 2025, while the allegations were investigated.

The panel found comments made between June 2024 and March 2025 amounted to unacceptable professional conduct. It said Piggott had made sexist and misogynistic remarks in front of children he was supposed to guide as a role model.

It also raised concerns about his attitude towards female colleagues, saying he appeared to mistake friendship from junior female staff for sexual interest and pursue it at work.

The panel said there was no meaningful evidence he understood the harm or felt remorse, and feared he could repeat the behaviour if he returned to work.

It described him as a “crowd pleaser” desperate to be everyone’s friend who crossed boundaries to get a laugh.

Piggott has been banned from teaching indefinitely and cannot work in any school, sixth-form college, relevant youth accommodation or children’s home in England. He can apply for the ban to be reviewed from October 19, 2028.

A Northern Education Trust spokesperson said: “As soon as the serious allegations against Mr Piggott were raised, the academy immediately followed the trust’s established procedures to investigate the matter.

“The trust takes concerns of this nature very seriously and offered appropriate support to those impacted.

“Mr Piggott was dismissed from the trust at the conclusion of the investigation, and the matter was referred to the Teaching Regulation Agency. The trust is satisfied that the matter was managed robustly and in accordance with all internal procedures.”
