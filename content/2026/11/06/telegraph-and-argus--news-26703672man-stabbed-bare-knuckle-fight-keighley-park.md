A court has seen video footage of a shocking bare-knuckle fight between two men in a Keighley park that culminated in one of the pair being stabbed.

Knifeman Bilaal Saleem is now beginning three years’ imprisonment for malicious wounding and having an offensive weapon in public.

The court heard that the incident represented the third time he had stabbed someone.

The victim lost between half a litre and a litre of blood and required surgery in hospital.

The court heard that the incident on the afternoon of July 31 occurred during a bare-knuckle fight in 19th century Devonshire Park in Keighley between two men from warring families who wished to settle a dispute.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21676793.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Devonshire Park in Keighley *(Image: Google Maps)*

Saleem, 24, of Spencer Street, Keighley, was one of the estimated 40 spectators who were gathered in the park to watch the fight.

Mobile phone footage showed both fighters squaring up before one man punched his opponent to the ground twice.

Then, as members of the crowd moved forward, he stepped back to abide by a pre-arranged rule not to hit someone on the ground.

Off camera a voice can be heard shouting, “Back up, boys! Fair fight!”

Following the second knockdown, Saleem stepped forward.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21677023.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Saleem *(Image: West Yorkshire Police)*

Prosecutor Paul Mitchell said: “At this point the defendant can be seen emerging from the crowd of spectators, appearing to take a knife from a small crossbody bag.

“He leans forward and down and aims a short, stabbing motion towards [the winning fighter’s] leg before retreating with others in the crowd pulling him away.”

The victim was taken to hospital having suffered a 2cm long by 3.6cm deep wound which, said a doctor described as “a significant wound [with] a significant volume of blood loss.”

Saleem gave himself up on August 4 and was arrested.

He gave a “no comment” interview followed by a prepared statement in which he said he had attended the fight as a peacemaker and that he and his friends had been ambushed.

He denied attending the fight with a weapon or stabbing anyone.

He later pleaded guilty to malicious wounding and possession of a knife in public.

The court heard that Saleem had six previous convictions for 14 offences including for wounding with intent, possession of an offensive weapon, assault occasioning actual bodily harm, affray, and Class A drug supply.

Mr Mitchell said on two previous occasions Saleem had used a knife to stab a victim.

The incident in Devonshire Park, once a venue for brass band concerts, was the third.

Mitigating for Saleem, Clare Walsh said the short video of the incident represented “a snapshot” of what had happened but that the background to it involved the complainant who “had caused trouble for the defendant’s family in the past.”

She added: “It doesn’t excuse the defendant’s behaviour, but it gives an insight into some of the background to what was going on.”

She conceded that Saleem had “an unenviable record” and that there was an element of him “placing some blame on the complainant because of their family history” but asked the court to consider his level of maturity and remorse.

She described the incident as “a turning point”, that Saleem wished to move out of the area, and urged the judge to hand down a suspended sentence so that Saleem, who appeared via video link from HMP Leeds, could be afforded “an opportunity to prove himself”.

Sentencing Saleem to three years in jail for what he described as “an exceedingly unpleasant incident”, Mr Recorder Patrick Palmer said: “Not only do you have convictions for violence, but this was the third time in which you were to stab someone.”

He added that a pre-sentence report described Saleem as being of a high risk to other members of the public.

Recorder Palmer handed Saleem six months for the knife, to be served concurrently. He said Saleem would serve half of his sentence in custody minus any time spent on remand, with the remainder on licence.

Of the fight itself, he said: “Everybody there should be thoroughly ashamed of themselves for even being present at an occasion like that.”
