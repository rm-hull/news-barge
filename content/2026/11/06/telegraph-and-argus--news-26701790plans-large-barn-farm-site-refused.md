Planning officers have questioned the need for a large barn to be built on a farm that has no livestock and no machinery.

Bradford Council has this week refused “permitted development” for a 37.5m by 20m building at Low Fold Farm, Back Heights Road, Thornton.

The 11m tall barn would be built on the 5.6 hectare farm, and the planning application says it would be used to store “machinery used to harvest hay and the storage and hay bales after harvest.”

But planning officers raised numerous questions about the plans, submitted by Amar Hussain, and argued there was a lack of detail in the application.

They said: “Very little information of the farming enterprise has been provided.

“That which is known is that there is no livestock or machinery and the extent of the agricultural use comprises annual harrowing, weed management, cutting and conditioning of the grass and baling at harvest time. This work is done by a contractor.”

Officers claim there is already a large stone farm building on the site that would be adequate to meet this storage need.

Earlier this year, a planning application to convert this stone building into a large home was approved by the council.

That application claimed the building was no longer used for agriculture.

Referring to the new application, officers said: “The existing buildings appear to be more than adequate to manage the land in the way described.

“It has not been made clear why these buildings are not suitable for agricultural use.”
