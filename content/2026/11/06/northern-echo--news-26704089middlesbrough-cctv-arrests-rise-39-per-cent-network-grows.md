The number of arrests taking place in Middlesbrough involving CCTV has risen by 39per cent, councillors have been told.

An increase was also noticeable in the number of public space cameras around the borough, which increased by 131 in 2025, compared to 2024.

John Kirk, CCTV manager for Middlesbrough Council, spoke to members of the audit committee on Thursday, October 29, outlining the CCTV report for 2025.

He told councillors that at the end of last year, the system had 1,257 cameras, which incorporated a mix of hand tilting, zoom, static and thermal cameras, which cover the town centre, public spaces, car parks and “key council sites”.

He said the council also has 50 CCTV schemes across its buildings and assets, including automatic number plate recognition, body worn video, and trail cameras in gated alleyways – each with a named, trained scheme manager.

The control room operates 24/7, with a full-time Cleveland Police officer based in the team. In 2025, the team handled 10,235 requests and the average number of operators per shift rose from two to 2.4.

Mr Kirk said performance monitoring demonstrates that CCTV continues to operate “effectively”, with 99 per cent of cameras operationally available.

Along with a 12 per cent increase in the number of cameras, most metrics saw a rise in 2025, compared to 2024.

The number of CCTV recorded incidents rose from 6,276 to 7,957, however the number of footage reviews was down nine per cent.

Mr Kirk discussed a total 726 arrests supported by CCTV evidence, saying: “These figures highlight the system’s ongoing contribution to crime detection, investigation and public safety.”

As for improvements needed, Mr Kirk said some of the CCTV signage is ageing and will be replaced. Other priorities include more control room staffing; a Tees Valley Investment Zone bid which, if successful, would mean an extra town centre camera operator. Camera upgrades are also being explored at Albert Park, Riverside Park and Town Farm allotments.

Labour Councillor David Branson was pleased with the additional CCTV installations that had taken place in his Coulby Newham ward in the last few years, while Conservative Councillor David Coupe highlighted how his Stainton and Thornton patch is one of the only wards not to have any CCTV cameras, “despite years of trying”, and requested a camera in the centre of Stainton.
