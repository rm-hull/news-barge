Glasgow Clyde College has won two top honours at the Qualifications Scotland Excellence Awards 2026, recognising its inclusive approach to education and support for asylum-seeking young people.

The college was named Centre of the Year and also received the award for Promoting Inclusion at the ceremony, held at Qualifications Scotland’s Glasgow offices on Thursday, November 5.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21681022.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Castlebay team. *(Image: Supplied)*

The awards recognise individuals and organisations using qualifications to make education more inclusive, innovative and accessible. More than 260 nominations were received.

Glasgow Clyde College was recognised for its use of qualifications to create pathways into education, employment and further study for learners with different needs and ambitions.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21681020.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Robert Toft. *(Image: Supplied)*

Judges also commended its English for Speakers of Other Languages (ESOL) programme, described as the first in Scotland dedicated to unaccompanied asylum-seeking young people.

Castlebay School on the Isle of Barra won the Innovation award for its Nautilus Barra project, which uses the island as a “living classroom”. Pupils work with university academics on topics including marine science and drone technology.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21681021.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Alan Deans. *(Image: Supplied)*

In the individual categories, Alan Deans, nominated by the Glasgow Veterans Group, was named Qualifications Scotland Champion. He was recognised for more than 30 years of work using qualifications to support veterans and disadvantaged adults.

Robert Toth, nominated by Sanctuary Housing Association, received the Inspirational Learner award. After starting a new life in the UK, he has progressed into a deputy manager role in social care.

John Rafferty, principal of Glasgow Clyde College, said: "I’m over the moon and delighted that the college and staff have won these awards today.

"Qualifications Scotland has helped us deliver many types of qualifications and courses to suits lots of different learners no matter what their aspirations are.

"It means a lot to them, and these courses can be the difference in them getting a job, getting back on their feet, and achieving great things."

Monica McLeod, science teacher at Castlebay School, said: "We’ve been doing this project for over three years now and think it’s really important to give children on the island scientific opportunities.

"I’m really delighted to be recognised for it.

"We wanted to engage young people with their local environment and the impacts of climate change, as it can be easy to feel we can’t make a difference.

"The project makes them feel like they have the power to make that difference and then take that information back to their parents and grandparents."

Alan Deans, Qualifications Scotland Champion, said: "I’m immensely humbled to win this award.

"It’s a great honour and a privilege to work alongside veterans, working on employability skills, mental health, and self-esteem, among many other things.

"This award isn’t just for me; it’s for everyone else involved in the organisation too."

Robert Toth, Inspirational Learner, said: "I’m really excited to win – when they called my name I wasn’t expecting it.

"I started work as a carer when I moved to the UK and I just knew that to progress in the role I needed to keep studying.

"I’ve continued to move up and think the best way to progress in life is to keep learning."
