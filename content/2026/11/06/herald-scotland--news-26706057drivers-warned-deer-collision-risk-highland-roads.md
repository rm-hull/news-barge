Motorists have been warned to watch out for deer on roads at a series of collision hotspots across the Highlands.

NatureScot is working with Traffic Scotland and Transport Scotland to display warnings on fixed electronic variable message signs (VMS) at dawn and dusk, reminding drivers of the heightened risk of deer on the road.

Deer-vehicle collisions typically increase at this time of year as daylight hours shorten and red deer move to lower ground in search of food and shelter.

Red deer stags in particular may be attracted to richer feeding grounds as they recover from the rutting season.

A road safety campaign urging drivers to be alert for red deer on Highland roads has now been launched.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/16826054.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Signs will be placed on roads known to be deer hotspots *(Image: NatureScot)*

The warning messages will remain in place until the end of November on trunk roads across the main red deer range in the Highlands, as well as in Grampian and Argyll.

Affected routes include the A9, A99, A835, A87, A82, A83, A85 and A96.

This year, particular attention is being given to Lochend on the A82 near Loch Ness following reports of an increase in collisions involving red deer and vehicles.

Mobile variable message signs will be installed at the location throughout November to encourage drivers to slow down and remain alert for deer.

Speed monitoring will also be carried out for a week before and after the signs are in use to assess whether the warnings are having an impact on driver behaviour.

Dominic Sargent, NatureScot Deer Policy Officer, said: “Public safety is a key part of our wider work with partners to reduce deer impacts across Scotland, particularly in the areas where we know those impacts are highest.

“These warning messages are an important reminder to drivers to be ‘deer aware’, particularly at peak collision times around dawn and dusk, when deer are more likely to venture onto grass verges near roadsides as they browse and feed.

“Simply slowing down and being alert to the possibility of deer can really help to reduce the likelihood of collisions.”

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21679727.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Red deer, Forsinard Flows National Nature Reserve. *(Image: Lorne Gill/SNH)*

Angus Corby, Transport Scotland Landscape and Biodiversity Manager, said: “We continue to work closely with colleagues in NatureScot and our contractors to assess the extent and impact of deer-vehicle collisions across Scotland’s trunk roads, and to determine where mitigation is most needed.

“This work is a vital part of the overall management of the network for the reliability of these strategic transport routes and the safety of those using them.

“It is also important for the welfare of the wild deer, which are an intrinsic part of the landscape.”
