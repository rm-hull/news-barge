The £18.7 billion Scottish Mortgage Investment Trust has posted strong results and flagged the importance of its patient capital approach in enabling innovation and breakthroughs.

It highlighted Elon Musk’s SpaceX, artificial intelligence company Anthropic, pharmaceutical and biotechnology pioneer Moderna and drone delivery technology group Zipline as among standout holdings in its portfolio in the first half of its financial year.

The investment trust observed: “Moderna’s shares nearly tripled in a single day in August after its personalised cancer vaccine succeeded in a late-stage melanoma trial, a decade after development began.”

Scottish Mortgage, which is managed from Edinburgh by investment partnership Baillie Gifford, posted a 31.5% total return on net asset value for the six months to September 30.

This was nearly twice the 16% return on the FTSE All-World Index.

Scottish Mortgage highlighted its aim “to invest in the world’s most exceptional growth companies, whether public or private, anywhere in the world and patiently own them over long periods of time”.

It flagged its role in providing “long-term funding and support for companies and entrepreneurs building the future of our economy”.

Managers Tom Slater and Lawrence Burns, who are partners of Baillie Gifford, aim for Scottish Mortgage to achieve a greater return than the FTSE All-World Index, in sterling terms, over a five-year rolling period.

Mr Burns, who is deputy manager of Scottish Mortgage, said the past six months had shown how innovation can produce important breakthroughs.

In June, SpaceX went public, seven-and-a-half years after Scottish Mortgage first invested. In September, SpaceX’s Starship rocket reached orbit for the first time, more than three years after its first launch.

Scottish Mortgage said of SpaceX: “As launch costs fall, the managers expect new business applications to follow Starlink, from connecting mobile phones directly to satellites to providing computing capacity in orbit.”

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21680412.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Lawrence Burns said the past six months had shown how innovation can produce important breakthroughs. *(Image: Baillie Gifford)*

The investment trust noted it had reduced its holding in SpaceX “as lock-up restrictions allowed”, adding: “This reflected the holding’s growth to more than a quarter of the portfolio, not any loss of confidence in the company.”

Scottish Mortgage said the valuation of another of its holdings, Zipline, more than doubled following that company’s partnership with Uber, “after a decade spent developing drone delivery”, including deliveries of blood to hospitals in Rwanda.

Referring to Moderna, SpaceX and Zipline, Mr Burns said: “That these developments landed in the same six months was largely chance. The breakthroughs themselves were not; the people at these companies made them happen, through years of dedication and hard work.”

He added: “Nowhere has progress been faster than in artificial intelligence. The length of tasks an AI agent can complete on its own has been doubling roughly every four months. Businesses are adopting these tools at a pace with few precedents. Anthropic saw annualised revenue more than triple in three months, from $14 billion in February to $47 billion in May. The companies we speak with across the world are clear that they expect to spend more on AI and tell us that spending is earning a positive return. The path will be bumpy, but the long-term direction is clear."

Scottish Mortgage noted it “has been investing in private companies since 2012 to give shareholders low-cost access to the most exciting, transformational growth businesses that are staying private for longer”.

In the six months to September 30, the investment trust invested £268.7 million in private companies and held 53 private companies, representing 24.9% of total assets.

Scottish Mortgage added nine company holdings during the six-month period, three of them private as opposed to being listed on stock markets.

Detailing these private holdings, Scottish Mortgage said: “Hadrian uses software and robotics to run automated factories making precision parts for aerospace and defence. ICON uses robotic 3D printers to build homes and other structures, including barracks for the US military, with the aim of reducing construction time, labour and waste. Chai Discovery builds AI models to design new drugs, aiming to make drug discovery more like an engineering process than a slow, trial-and-error search.”

Scottish Mortgage said that, since announcing its enhanced buyback programme in March 2024, it has repurchased more than £4 billion of its shares.

It noted that, at September 30, the discount at which its shares were trading to net asset value was 7.9%, compared with 9.5% at March 31.
