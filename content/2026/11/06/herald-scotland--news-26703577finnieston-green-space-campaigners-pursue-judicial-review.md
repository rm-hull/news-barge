A charity fighting to save a rare green space in Finnieston from development have flats has said they will now be looking to pursue a judicial review after the window closed on it being called in by the Scottish Government.

A key document has been signed that means that it's no longer possible for the Government to intervene in the Glasgow City Council decision to approve the controversial development of 20 flats on the site of the Corunna Bowling Club in Finnieston.

Campaigners and politicians expressed their disappointment that it had not been called in.

As a result, said Dr David Johnson, a trustee of the Friends of St Vincent Crescent Conservation Area (FSVCCA), the community has no choice other than to "pursue a judicial review".

With that will come the challenge of raising thousands of pounds, on top of £2.9 million that the charity may also have to raise to buy the site.

Earlier this year, Glasgow City Council controversially agreed in principle to approve a development, subject to the developer Nixon Blue concluding a legally binding Section 75 planning agreement with the council.

That Section 75 has now been signed, and with that, said Dr Johnson, the possibility of a ministerial call-in "evaporates".

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21681243.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Corunna bowling club site in Finnieston, which is slated to be developed as flats *(Image: FSVCCA)*

What has happened with this small charity – which is now being forced to prove that it can raise £2.9 million in order to save the site – reveals, he says, some of the weaknesses in Scotland’s planning system, in spite of the powerful statement of National Planning Framework 4.

“There’s a national significance to this fight,” said Finnieston resident Jodie Troup, “because protected space should be protected – and if there’s no real protection, where does that leave us as a nation?”

Ms Troup is a member of FSVCCA which has been, she said, “shouting loud” in its attempts to persuade Scottish Government ministers to call in the planning decision made by Glasgow City Council to approve a development of flats on the Corunna Bowling Club site.

Almost ten years after the Finnieston site was first sold, eight years after locals began their fight, the charity is still pursuing community right-to-buy and a vision for the space that includes a community-run garden, food growing areas, nature-based play facilities and an inclusive indoor hub.

The battle has been taking place on multiple fronts. Seventy MSPs have been contacted and cross-party support has been obtained.

Scottish Green MSP Patrick Harvie even raised the issue in Parliament, saying, “as a result of the Scottish Government’s decision not to call in the planning application, the land is now massively overvalued compared with what it was previously valued at.

“The community has a right of first refusal, but exercising that right is unfeasible in the face of such a massive increase in the price tag, to more than 10 times the previous value, purely as a result of the planning permission.”

In December 2024, the bowling-green site was valued at £250,000 by the group’s chartered surveyor, but it is now valued at £2.9m.

For the charity, one of the biggest issues is the “loss of green space” in a part of Glasgow in which such space is in short supply.

“All of our councillors have requested a call-in,” said Dr Johnson. “Five of our MSPs have requested a call in and then as a local community, the charity has over 300 members living in the local area, in 250 flats.”

Among those supporting their cause is Pauline McNeill, Scottish Labour MSP for Glasgow Region, who said: “Protecting green spaces such as the Corunna Bowling Club is of paramount importance to local communities and the city of Glasgow.

“The case of the Corunna Bowling Club highlights issues that many communities across Scotland face around the future of conservation areas and the role of local people in decision-making.

“I believe it is a time for a review of planning law to protect against cases where local authorities go against their own development plans.”

She added: “The decision by the planning committee at Glasgow City Council to go against the Planning Officer’s recommendation and approve the proposed development of flats on the Corunna bowling club goes against the Council’s own Open Space Strategy within their City Development Plan.”

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21544936.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Pauline McNeill, MSP (right) called on Scottish ministers to call in the Corunna Bowling Club planning decision *(Image: Colin Mearns)*

Ms McNeill is among those who joined the call for a ministerial call-in of the development. She observed that it failed to happen “despite the fact that this planning application contradicts their own National Planning Framework 4 which encourages urban green space, community wealth and access to nature”.

The site, the charity points out, is protected space under City Development Plan Policy CDP 6 and is also within the St Vincent Crescent Conservation Area. Campaigners argue the green space is vital to the character and historic layout of the crescent.

“This is not just a Glasgow problem,” said Troup. “It’s a much wider problem, across the rest of Scotland. If we can’t protect this small bit – and if the Glasgow Planning Committee can just turn over and let developers build on land that has protections – that says something. And this is a development that has had push back from community, from MSPs, from the planning officer who suggested to refuse it. What’s the point of having robust planning rules in place if they’re not followed?”

Dr Johnson added: “There’s national planning legislation that covers the whole of Scotland, which is very strong and robust, but it’s only any good if there’s a willingness to enforce it. What’s the point of having national legislation if this isn’t going to be enforced?”

FSVCCA points to other campaigns, including the bid to save the Mount Florida Bowling Club site for the community, which campaigners have taken to judicial review; the fight over Edinburgh’s Roseburn Path; and the battle over Poet’s Neuk in St Andrews, where the first-of-its-kind ‘hostile’ right-to-buy in Scotland took place last month.

A judicial review over the Mount Florida site, seeking to overturn the approval for the development of flats at the site and create a precedent for future local campaigns, is currently being considered by a judge.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21681231.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Corunna bowling club site in Finnieston, which is slated to be developed as flats *(Image: FSVCCA)*

The Friends of St Vincent Crescent Conservation Area now look set to follow in the footsteps of Mount Florida.

This in itself will require substantial funds. The Mount Florida campaign, for instance, raised £10,000, much of it during a week at the beginning of the campaign and through a ‘hug-the-club’ stunt – plus a further £5000 as back up.

This has been a nine-year-long fight, beginning in 2017, when, after years of declining membership, the Corunna Bowling Club, on St Vincent Crescent in Finnieston, closed. The site was subsequently sold and has remained vacant.

In 2018, the first major attempt to redevelop the site, for 39 homes, was refused by Glasgow City Council.

Last year, a fourth development proposal was lodged by Nixon Blue and, in March this year, Glasgow City Council Planning Applications Committee went against its planning officers' recommendation and decided to approve the scheme, subject to completion of a Section 75 legal agreement.

It is a development that has been strongly opposed. There were 237 objections to the planning application, including opposition from councillors and four MSPs.

The community, meanwhile, still pursuing its right-to-buy, is currently expecting another decision from the Scottish Government on whether it has demonstrated that it can raise the £2.9m required and proceed with it.

“Unfortunately,” said Dr Johnson, “it seems that the legislation doesn’t like any variation on £2.9m. The legislation is such that we are stuck with it.”

“Once the decision notice is posted, the opportunity for a call-in to the Government evaporates instantly. It’s no longer possible. So our option would then be judicial review."

Much of the funding, they say, will be raised by crowdfunding. “But whether government land reform will be reassured that will be sufficient, we don’t know yet for sure. If there is a positive outcome for us we will have until March 6 next year to raise the money.”

Ms Troup pointed out other areas “derelict housing and brown spaces” that might have been more appropriate for the development of housing.

She said: “We appreciate there is a lack of housing. I know that Glasgow Council is trying to create more housing. But why these green spaces? Our area is already under Glasgow City Council’s threshold for how much green space per head. There are four or five other sites where flats are already planned or in the middle of being built in the area, that won’t provide green space.”

She also highlighted that Glasgow City Council is committed to providing 25% of affordable housing, but there is none in this development.

Depute Lord Provost of Glasgow Christy Mearns said: “It’s hugely disappointing that the Scottish Government did not call in the planning decision to build on the former Corunna bowling club. Not only is this site formally protected, it exists in an area with the lowest tree canopy per head of population and the community have worked tirelessly to protect it for positive community use.

"These were elements the planning committee failed to fully consider and now all protected green spaces risk being lost to developers seeking maximum profit at the expense of local people. The government won't allow communities an equal right of appeal, so they should ensure the current planning process works for communities."

A spokesperson for the Scottish Government said: "As this is a live planning application, it would not be appropriate for us to comment."

Glasgow City Council confirmed that the Section 75 had been signed and that "the planning application was recently approved at the Planning Applications Committee".
