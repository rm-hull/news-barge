Bradford College and Bradford Council have responded after concerns were raised about the withdrawal of an educational programme for 26 students.

Bradford College, on Great Horton Road, has said it can no longer deliver the Day 6 alternate provision, prompting concerns from parents of children who had no learning to return to after the school holidays.

Liz Leek, Deputy CEO, Quality & Curriculum at Bradford College, said: "Bradford College is a post-16 further education and skills provider.

“We have been supporting a small number of under-16 learners through a discretionary alternative provision arrangement known as Day 6 in partnership with Bradford Council.

“Following a review of our capacity and resources, we are unfortunately no longer able to continue delivering this provision.

“We recognise this change may be concerning for learners and families, as of Wednesday we understand the local authority have secured all Day 6 placements with alternative providers, helping to ensure continuity of education.

“We wish every one of these young people continued success in their studies and the very best for the future."

A Bradford Council spokesperson said: “Bradford Council is responsible for arranging education for children who have been permanently excluded from school and commissions Bradford College to deliver a number of these places.

“Bradford College informed the council at the end of the last half term that it had taken the decision to cease delivering this particular provision.

“While we recognise this unexpected change has caused understandable concern for children, young people and their families, once informed staff worked at pace to identify and secure alternative education arrangements for all affected pupils.

“Throughout this process Bradford Council has maintained regular communication with families, and the priority has been to ensure that every child continues to receive an appropriate education and the support they need.

“We appreciate that any change in educational placement can be unsettling, particularly for vulnerable children and those with additional needs, but we are confident that suitable educational provision is now in place for all affected pupils.”

A concerned parent, who had a son in the Day 6 programme, told the Telegraph & Argus: “I am writing to raise urgent concerns about 29 vulnerable students in Bradford who have been left facing an uncertain educational future after Bradford College reportedly withdrew from an alternative education programme with immediate effect.

The parent told the T&A this week: “They cancelled and the children had no school to go to.

“This programme was good as it was a way for the children to gain an extra qualification in life skills like food, barbering and business.”

Explaining how their son had secured a place, the parent added: “Last year, these 26 young people, including my son, were placed on a six-day education programme at Bradford College after finding themselves without a school place.

“The local authority had a responsibility to ensure they continued to receive an education, and this programme offered them a fresh opportunity at a time when they desperately needed support.

“Many of these young people are vulnerable. Some have Education, Health and Care Plans (EHCPs), others have special educational needs and disabilities (SEND), and many have experienced school suspensions and difficulties within mainstream education.

“For these children, the programme was more than just somewhere to attend lessons.

“It provided routine, stability, a supportive environment and an opportunity to rebuild their confidence in education.

“Unlike a traditional classroom setting, the smaller, more vocational approach suited many of their individual needs.

“Over the past year, these students have settled in, worked hard and begun building a future for themselves.

“Many have chosen vocational subjects such as barbering, motor mechanics and brickwork, giving them the opportunity to work towards qualifications that could open doors to further education, college courses and future employment.

“Then, in the middle of the school holidays, everything changed.

“Parents were contacted by the education board and informed that Bradford College had withdrawn from the programme with immediate effect.

“As a result, these young people have been told they will have no college to return to after the school holidays.

“These are 15- and 16-year-old students who are already partway through a crucial academic year, with GCSE examinations only months away.

“They have spent approximately a year and a half working towards a better future, only to have the educational pathway they had come to rely on pulled away from them at extremely short notice.

“The potential consequences are devastating.

“It is heartbreaking to see young people who had finally settled, found their confidence and begun engaging positively with education facing the prospect of starting all over again.

“Parents are left distressed and uncertain, while their children are left wondering what happens next.”
