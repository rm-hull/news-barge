A "creepy" drunk who tried to snatch a three-year-old girl from a dance class sending a hall into lockdown has been jailed.

Terrence Sarjantson walked up to two terrified young boys and told them to “get the girl” while they were playing football outside a dance class in Middlesbrough.

The 43-year-old then staggered towards the entrance after being confronted by an adult, who screamed for everyone to get inside before locking down the hall.

The Middlesbrough man then left and was tracked down nearby, where, when asked why he had tried to take the child, he responded: “Yes but I didn’t get her though”.

Durham Crown Court heard on Thursday (November 5) how Sarjantson could “not offer an explanation” behind the ordeal, but Judge Richard Bennett ruled that it was sexually motivated.

Saba Shan, prosecuting, described how CCTV captured the moment Sarjantson walked up to the boys, who had been playing football with the girl at around 8pm.

“The defendant approached the boys [and] said something to them before they went inside,” she said. “One of the boys goes to speak to the teacher.”

The court heard how the “panicked” youngsters told the teacher that they had been approached by a man.

An adult went outside and found the defendant, who “appeared intoxicated”, “slumped on the floor”, asking him twice what he wanted.

Ms Shan said: “He proceeded to walk towards the entrance to the centre. She shouted for everyone to get inside before she closed the doors.”

Sarjantson then left - but a mother whose child was at the class located the defendant nearby and asked him why he had tried to take the child.

He was shortly after arrested by police.

Ms Shan read out a victim impact statement from the mother of the young girl, who said the incident had “completely changed the way I feel about my own safety”.

“It is something that I can’t stop thinking about,” she said.

“I constantly find myself asking about the what ifs. These thoughts go around in my head repeatedly. The fact that she is so young makes it even harder for me to process.

“The impact has also affected my ability to feel safe in my own home. I find myself sitting awake until 3am or 4am in the morning, checking on her.

“Being in this area makes me feel unsafe. I don’t feel like the person I was before.”

The woman, who had been experiencing a tough time due to recent events in her life, including a cancer diagnosis, said she was “frightened, exhausted, and constantly on alert”.

Tom Bennett, mitigating, said Sarjantson carried out the incident “impulsively or spontaneously" as there was “no planning or any sophistication".

“It has clearly had an impact on the mother and having heard her victim's personal statement,” he said. “There is no evidence that there is an impact on the three year old child.

“I fully acknowledge that her mother feels in the way that she does - and any parent would no doubt feel that way.

“Whilst he does have a number of previous convictions, it is nothing of a sexual nature and there is nothing of this sort of nature of serious violence.”

Mr Bennett noted how Sarjantson has thinking deficiencies, attended specialist education, and has a difficult relationship with alcohol and drug misuse.

The court heard how the defendant was “heavily intoxicated” and had suffered a mental health episode earlier in the day.

Sarjantson has 25 previous convictions for 35 offences between 2002 and 2026, including theft, being drunk and disorderly, violence, battery, and assault of an emergency worker.

He initially pleaded not guilty to the latest offence but changed his plea at a hearing in September and accepted that the incident was sexually motivated.

Judge Richard Bennett said Sarjantson was “creepy and disturbing” and made the boys "understandably frightened” during the ordeal.

He said the class - made up largely of young girls - “went into lockdown” as they were “frightened about what you were going to do”.

He noted how Sarjantson told a “series of lies” after being arrested and had “selective memory”.

Judge Bennett jailed Sarjantson, of Carlow Street in Middlesbrough, for 22 months and handed him a Sexual Harm Prevention Order for ten years.

It means he cannot have any unsupervised contact with any female child under the age of 16 without the consent of an adult who knows of his background.
