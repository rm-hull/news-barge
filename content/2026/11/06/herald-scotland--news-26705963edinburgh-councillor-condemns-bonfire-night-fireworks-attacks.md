**A senior councillor says she is “angry and disappointed” following anti-social behaviour in pockets of the capital on Bonfire Night.**

While City of Edinburgh Council leader Jane Meagher said it was “encouraging” that the holiday was celebrated peacefully in most neighbourhoods, she hit out at “isolated incidents of disorder” caused by troublemakers “seemingly intent on causing fear and alarm in our communities”.

“We cannot and must not accept this type of mindless behaviour, in particular the targeting of our emergency services, and I hope those responsible are caught and brought to justice as soon as possible,” Councillor Meagher added.

Police officers were attacked with fireworks on Oxgangs Road in the south of the city, according to the Press Association.

One person, who did not want to be named, told the news agency: “They’re setting fire in the park and shooting fireworks at the police and firemen.”

No officers were injured during the disorder, which also saw bins knocked over and set on fire in the street.

There has been an overall reduction in Bonfire Night calls and disorder, but attacks on police and emergency services are still ](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21679572.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Councillor Jane Meagher and Chief Superintendent David Robertson of Police Scotland speaking to the media outside Fettes Police Station. *(Image: Danyel VanReenen/Press Association)*

“Officers attended and fireworks were set off in their direction, but no injuries have been reported,” a Police Scotland spokesperson said.

“Several small fires in bins and the park were extinguished by the Scottish Fire and Rescue Service."

Inquiries are ongoing.

Despite the ugly scenes in some areas of the city, Councillor Meagher said she was “pleased” that most residents enjoyed the celebrations at community-run events and that buses were able to run on-schedule.

She remarked: “Months of planning and close partnership working have gone into preparing for this period, and I’d like to thank our council teams, Police Scotland, the Scottish Fire and Rescue Service, Lothian Buses, community organisations and our other partners for all their efforts.

“Our approach has focused on prevention as well as enforcement, from Firework Control Zones to engaging with young people and providing positive alternative activities.”

This year, the council has banned the use of fireworks in 14 neighbourhoods across the capital; Corstorphine, Longstone, Sighthill/Broomhouse, The Murrays, Liberton Grange, Lochend, Stenhouse/Gaskell Street, West Pilton, Wester Hailes, Moredun, Seafield, Niddrie, Calton Hill and Balerno.

People who set off fireworks in those areas risk being charged with violating the terms of the Fireworks and Pyrotechnic Articles (Scotland) Act 2022, which means they could face fines of up to £5,000 and six months in prison.

“The organised events across the city also went well, offering families and communities the opportunity to come together,” added Councillor Meagher.

“I’m grateful to everyone who has played their part in making these efforts count.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21679586.jpg?type=mds-article-620)People watch a bonfire in the Wester Hailes area of Edinburgh. *(Image: Jane Barlow)*

“I’d like to reiterate my thanks to residents for acting responsibly, looking out for one another and helping to keep their neighbourhoods safe – and hope that they will remember Bonfire Night for all the right reasons.”

Firefighters received a total of 1,172 calls across Scotland between 6.30 and 11.30 pm on Thursday, responding to more than 767 incidents, including 335 bonfires.

Police also faced attacks in Knightswood and Castlemilk areas of Glasgow, Cumnock in Ayrshire, Larkhall in South Lanarkshire, and Blackridge, West Lothian.

“The communities of Scotland have overwhelmingly shown their commitment to enjoying both Halloween and Bonfire Night in the proper spirit,” said Police Scotland chief superintendent David Robertson.

“Nevertheless, we have seen, yet again, an unacceptable number of incidents where the police and our emergency service colleagues have been attacked with fireworks and other projectiles. This resulted in one of our officers and a colleague from the Scottish Ambulance Service sustaining minor injuries.”

Speaking at Fettes Police Station, Mr Robertson also addressed last night's disorder in Edinburgh.

He said: "It was localised. It was short-lived. So I do think that’s a positive.

“But nonetheless, that behaviour is completely unacceptable, and those who were involved will be identified and brought to justice.”
