An MP has called for funding from a scrapped leisure centre development to be invested into bringing a derelict community facility back to use.

The Greenwood Centre in Swain House, Bradford, was once a thriving community hub, but has been empty for years.

Since then it has become a target for vandalism and arson, with ambitious plans to bring it back into use failing to materialise.

## Squire Lane money

Now Bradford East MP Imran Hussain has called for Bradford Council, the owners of the building, to use funding from the cancelled Squire Lane leisure centre to redevelop the building.

The Squire Lane scheme was scrapped by the council’s new Reform Executive this summer.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21223752.jpg?type=mds-article-620)What the Squire Lane Leisure Centre would have looked like, had it not been scrapped *(Image: Bradford Council)*

At the time it was thought that over £14 million in Government funding pledged to the scheme would be lost.

But last week it emerged that Bradford would be able to retain the £14.6 million, and use it for other projects in the district.

## Modern youth and community hub idea

Mr Hussain believes some of this funding pot should be used for the Greenwood Centre.

He says the building has “become a persistent source of concern for local residents and the emergency services, with repeated incidents of anti-social behaviour and risks to public safety.”

Mr Hussain is calling for the centre to be transformed into a modern youth and community hub providing positive activities, educational and skills programmes, family support, community services, and health and wellbeing provision.

He has asked the council to fund an initial feasibility assessment covering the building’s structural condition, redevelopment costs, possible uses and future management.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/20249438.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Bradford East MP Imran Hussain *(Image: T&A)*

## 'Completely unacceptable'

Mr Hussain said: “Reform scrapped the Squire Lane Leisure Centre and then wrongly claimed that £14.6 million in government funding could not be spent elsewhere.

"They have now been forced into a humiliating U-turn.

“The Greenwood Centre is standing empty and deteriorating in the heart of Swain House while local young people are left without the facilities, support and positive opportunities they desperately need. That is completely unacceptable.

![The Greenwood Centre](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21229363.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The Greenwood Centre *(Image: T&A)*

“Regenerating the centre would bring a damaged community asset back into use and provide a safe, modern hub serving young people, families and residents across the area.

“Reform cannot continue making excuses while millions of pounds remain available and communities across Bradford East are crying out for investment.

“I have asked the council to fund the necessary feasibility work and meet with me urgently to take this proposal forward.

“The people of Bradford East deserve investment, not empty buildings, broken promises and more excuses.”

## What Reform has said

In response, a spokesperson for Reform said: “We warmly welcome that these monies will not be clawed back by national Government and that they remain available as a capital grant.

“As we have only learnt of these available monies very recently, we are not yet able to commit their use for any particular project across our district but, of course, will look to utilise them in future, to the benefit of Bradford residents.”

Although the centre is owned by Bradford Council, it is leased to community interest company Inspired Neighbourhoods on a long term basis.

Inspired Neighbourhoods announced plans to refurbish the building in 2024 but there has been little movement since.

This summer Bradford Council said it would be urging Inspired Neighbourhoods to surrender its lease.

The Telegraph & Argus has asked for an update on this process.
