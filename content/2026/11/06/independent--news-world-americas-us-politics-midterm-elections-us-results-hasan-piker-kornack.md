A successful midterm performance for Democrats highlighted an evolving battleground for news consumers, as digital livestreamers like left-leaning creator Hasan Piker draw audiences substantial enough to rival legacy media networks.

Broadcast television has historically anchored election-night coverage.

However, as viewers shift toward online personalities to parse major political events, mainstream networks are striving to rebrand their existing reporters for digital spaces.

This shift was evident Tuesday evening when Comcast's NBC News unveiled the "Kornacki Cam," an almost 11-hour digital broadcast starring data analyst Steve Kornacki.

Combining minute-by-minute election statistics with off-camera glimpses, the stream accumulated 29 million total views across platforms and maintained a peak of over 300,000 simultaneous viewers for nearly three hours.

Yet traditional outlets faced intense competition from unaffiliated creators whose political footprint has expanded rapidly over the past year.

Prominent among them was Piker, a self-described Marxist whose controversial statements, including declaring that "America deserved 9/11," have generated friction among Democrats.

![NBC News unveiled the "Kornacki Cam," an almost 11-hour digital broadcast starring data analyst Steve Kornacki](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/02/20/29/NBC-Steve-Kornack-Meet-the-Press.png?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&auto=webp)

Abdul El-Sayed requested that Piker temper his commentary after the streamer asserted that pro-Israel Jews ought to be worried about their personal safety, according to CNN reporting.

El-Sayed secured victory in Tuesday's Michigan Senate election, positioning him to become the first Muslim senator.

Data from analytics firm Streams Charts reveals that the 35-year-old streamer hit a peak of 241,783 concurrent viewers on Twitch during election night. Gathering 1.75 million total hours watched, Piker's broadcast stood as the second-most-viewed individual stream of the evening, trailing only NBC's "Kornacki Cam."

Those figures roughly tripled the audience for ABC News' YouTube stream and were five times larger than CBS News' digital offering.

Even so, standard television continued to command larger overall ratings, with Nielsen figures indicating primetime averages of 6.8 million viewers for NBC, 5.1 million for ABC, and roughly 4 million for Fox News.

![Hasan Piker with Abdul El-Sayed](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/08/09/20/download..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

"It's hard to pick a singular label like 2024's 'podcast election'. But if there was one channel that really gained steam this election cycle, it was livestreaming," noted Jasmine Enberg, co-founder of the creator economy startup Scalable.

Digital influencers previously altered political outreach during 2024 when Donald Trump won a second presidential term, illustrating how campaigns can leverage online figures to engage audiences outside mainstream media channels.

During these midterm elections, Republicans spent more than Democrats on conventional ad buys, yet figures such as El-Sayed and James Talarico relied on digital video to cultivate organic supporter networks.

Figures from Streams Charts indicate that political livestreams racked up 10.27 million hours of watch time, the equivalent of over 1,170 continuous years, across more than 2,500 distinct channels in the 30 days preceding the November 3 vote.

Audience numbers grew 4.5 times compared to the period before the 2022 midterms, with Piker generating almost 45 percent of the total viewing hours. Meanwhile, right-wing host Candace Owens reached a top concurrent audience of 70,000 viewers, according to the metrics.

## Piker’s growing political clout

In addition to supporting El-Sayed, Piker made appearances alongside Minnesota congresswoman Ilhan Omar and endorsed progressive candidates such as New York's Claire Valdez and Darializa Avila Chevalier, both of whom secured congressional seats on Tuesday.

Speaking to Reuters on Thursday, Piker confirmed he had received invitations to post-election events held by Democrats but opted not to go.

"I chose to stay at (my) home base here in Los Angeles and spent the night doing analysis because that is my primary focus. I'm not a campaign surrogate. I'm a political commentator. I'm not as affiliated with these campaigns as the media presents it."

![](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/06/19/11/07/IMG_7774.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

He further offered regrets regarding some of his earlier statements, specifically his past comments regarding the September 11 terrorist attacks.

The election coverage added roughly 40,000 new subscribers to his Twitch account, pushing his subscriber base past 95,000. California congressman Ro Khanna was among the guests who joined the stream, highlighting how Democratic lawmakers are attempting to connect with younger voters through digital creators.

That audience segment carries growing weight. Data from a 2025 Pew Research Center survey showed that 38 percent of Americans between the ages of 18 and 29 depend on social media influencers for news on a regular basis, compared to just 8 percent of adults aged 65 and above.

However, this expanding influence has invited criticism from political opponents; Republican candidate Mike Rogers incorporated Piker's previous 9/11 statements into attacks against El-Sayed during the Michigan Senate race.

Media analysts remain split on whether independent streamers can ever match the reach of major podcasters like Joe Rogan, whose episodes command millions of listeners and capture audiences that might otherwise ignore political news.

"It does seem clear that a lot of these streamers have become influential enough to become boogeymen for their opponents," said Max Willens, principal analyst at Emarketer.

"If you wanted to put a finer point on it and ask me, is Hasan maybe one of the leading voices and most influential public figures for the far left in the political system, that's probably true. But to say that he is in that kind of role for the capital-D Democratic Party, that's a bridge too far."
