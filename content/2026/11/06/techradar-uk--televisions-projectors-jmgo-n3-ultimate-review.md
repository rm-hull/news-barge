## JMGO N3 Ultimate: One-minute review

The JMGO N3 Ultimate is the new, top-of-the-line option from JMGO. It brings a lot of what the brand had already been packing into a new design with a motorized gimbal stand and adjustable optics that provide quick or finely controlled setup options.

The JMGO N3 Ultimate's projection can be splendid, thanks to bright RGB lasers that give the projector enough juice to hold up in even well-lit rooms. The N3 Ultimate has vivid color and crisp detail, too. Unfortunately, the brightness isn’t matched by effective dimming to offer better performance in dark scenes, which is perhaps its biggest setback. Its motion could also be smoother without so many artifacts, but it’s not terrible either.

All told, it’s a capable projector that could serve a lot of people well, as long as they’re not seeking out dark, home theater setups where contrast is as important as brightness. At its $2,999 / £2,499 list price, it’s competitively priced compared to the best projectors, but it’s been on sale for much less and becomes all the better a bargain.

## JMGO N3 Ultimate Review: Design and features

* **Built-in Google TV**
* **Motorized gimbal stand and optical lens adjustments**
* **1080p/240Hz VRR mode for gaming**

JMGO has been rocking the same design for a few years now, and the JMGO N3 Ultimate does little to shift that. It’s still a fairly compact, two-tone rounded box sitting aloft on a gimbal stand with a rotating base, making it easy to set up and point the projection where you want it.

This go around, JMGO has incorporated small motors for electronic control of the stand. By holding a dedicated button on the remote control and using gyro controls, you can adjust the tilt and rotation of the projector. It’s good for quick adjustment but not for fine tuning.

In an interesting twist, the N3 Ultimate is JMGO’s projector that least needed this technology, as the projector incorporates advanced optics with adjustable zoom and lens shift. The zoom can shift the projector from a 0.88:1 to a 1.7:1 throw ratio, giving it a ton of range. Meanwhile, the lens shift lets it move the projection up or down by 130% or sideways by 53%, so you really just need to point it perpendicular to your wall, and you should have no trouble shifting and squeezing the projection to fit without any keystone correction.

![A side view of the JMGO N3 Ultimate projector.](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/LVcfzyphnrQey2u7SC2x4S-1200-80.jpg)

Those manual controls are how you’ll get the best picture, but the JMGO N3 Ultimate has a host of automated tools that can quickly make adjustments for you, which may be convenient if you’re moving it often. It can analyze the projection area available to it, re-aiming, shrinking, and shifting the picture to fit. I found this using digital keystone even when it really wasn’t called for, though, so I wouldn’t count on it doing the best job.

One additional automatic tool that may be more helpful is the wall color adaptation. This uses the projector's camera and a series of test patterns to tweak colors so they appear better on your wall. You won’t need this if you use a screen, but it worked well on my beige walls.

Since the projector can move quite a bit in operation, it fits its connectors all at the base, rather than the rear of the projection unit itself. This can still get in the way of rotation, but makes vertical tilt a non-issue. The projector doesn’t have a great selection of ports, though, with just two HDMI 2.1 ports, one of which is eARC and probably should be kept for that purpose. There’s also a single USB-A port.

![A close up of the ports at the back of the JMGO N3 Ultimate projector.](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/Y6sdeyMRFfJb9vPUy7EkPS-1200-80.jpg)

Thankfully, you don’t need to use any of those HDMI ports for basic streaming. The projector has built-in Google TV, and it operates quickly on its 4GB of memory. Annoyingly, it pauses playback while navigating menus sometimes (sometimes it doesn’t), but it’s quite serviceable otherwise. It even gets 64GB of storage, which can be useful if you use lots of apps. Perhaps, more crucially, the projector connects over Wi-Fi 6, giving it faster and more stable connectivity to work with while streaming.

For cinephiles, the JMGO N3 Ultimate supports HDR10 and Dolby Vision. Meanwhile, for gamers the projector can shift from 4K60Hz over to 1080p/240Hz and even brings VRR for smooth, low-latency, responsive gaming. There are some trade-offs though. Enabling VRR hurt clarity no matter if I left the projector at 4K or swapped to 1080p. Even at 1080p, VRR took a small toll on clarity. Fine text exhibited artifacts that looked like chroma subsampling. In games, it wasn’t an issue, but navigating Windows on PC to select games to play, it was a nuisance.

![A view of the front of the JMGO N3 Ultimate projector.](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/NT4vw929GQg25JRUCJGRJS-1200-80.jpg)

Apart from its gyro controls, the Ultimate's remote is pretty basic. It has the standard Google TV controls and thankfully a pill-shaped volume control that’s easy to feel out in the dark. It includes shortcuts to Prime Video, Netflix, YouTube, and one app of your choice. Google TV Freeplay gets a separate shortcut at the top of the remote. Annoyingly, there’s no mute button for quick silencing and while the remote has motion-activated lighting behind some of its keys, it doesn't behind the shortcut buttons.

Since the JMGO N3 Ultimate is fairly portable, JGMO includes a foam carrying case that can fit it, the remote, and the power adapter all inside. It’s not exceedingly robust or impressive, but it’s not too bulky and should do a better job protecting the hardware than a typical backpack would.

* **Design & features score: 4.5 / 5**

## JMGO N3 Ultimate Review: Picture quality and performance

* **Wonderfully bright and colorful**
* **Black levels could be darker**
* **Crisp 4K visuals and responsive 1080p gaming**

The JMGO N3 Ultimate has brightness in spades, and that’s where it shines best. When it comes to darkness, it can struggle. It fails to sink into darkness where it needs to, and the raised grays are distracting to see, never mind the regularly present letterboxes. Even when scenes aren’t very dark, these shortcomings are clear. Neither Dolby Vision nor adaptive brightness features seem to do the trick.

Thankfully, as soon as more light is called for, the JMGO N3 Ultimate delivers. It pumped out over 5,200 lumens by my measurements, which is plenty for most scenarios and not too far off the rated 5,800 lumens (nor did it require an awful picture setting). Bright scenes are clear and vibrant. Shadows look stronger in contrast to the brightness and the brightness is even enough to give the JMGO N3 Ultimate a solid showing in a modestly lit room. This makes it a great match for casual viewing, news, and bright cartoons. That brightness makes it all the easier to go big without the image getting too dim.

Alongside the brightness, the color of the RGB laser projection is stunning, just as it’s been in other projectors I’ve tested with the tech. When watching *Supergirl*, the glow of the green sun has plenty of depth, and the red cape glows majestically whenever the sun hits it. When Kara’s laser vision rips through the Brigands, it’s properly dazzling. The world of Pandora in the* Avatar* series is also lushly presented.

Motion can be hit or miss. By default the projector wants to use unsightly interpolation that presents loads of artifacts and makes the projection feel like anything but high-fidelity. With the MEMC setting dropped to low, it doesn’t look so bad, avoiding distracting soap opera effect and serious artifacts while helping reduce some of the stutter that occurs when it’s disabled entirely.

One area RGB laser projectors have been improving is the DLP rainbow effect. I’m not terribly sensitive to it, but I can usually catch it if I’m looking for it. Lately, I’ve been spotting it less and less on high-end laser projectors, but I can still catch it on the JMGO N3 Ultimate. Thankfully, it’s subtle, and I find it’s largely limited to subtitles

The adjustable optics help ensure a sharp picture from wherever you want to project. Stretched across about an 150-inch section of my wall, 4K looked brilliant in my living room.

![A close up of the JMGO N3 Ultimate projector&#039;s remote control.](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/5gqKMoNeHfhmxFTQS8J6ZS-1200-80.jpg)

Outside of movies, the JMGO N3 Ultimate proves a great option for gaming. You want crisp detail from corner to corner in games, since you never know where something precious is hiding, and the optics help provide this. Latency is satisfyingly low at 4K but sinks to negligible levels at 1080p/240Hz. Mousing around my screen and zipping through levels in Hades 2, I didn’t feel a hint of latency in that mode.

The speakers on the JMGO N3 Ultimate aren’t bad, but they certainly aren’t an adequate match for the projection. They put out decent volume, helping ensure you’ve got enough sound to work with when you want to plop the projector down somewhere and start enjoying right away. They even provide some low-end, helping bump up effects in movies. But the quality of stereo sound won’t be very good unless you’re sitting perfectly in center with the projection.

* **Picture quality & performance score: 3.5 / 5**

## JMGO N3 Ultimate Review: Value

* **Can serve multiple purposes well**
* **Cheaper than many rivals while offering competitive brightness**

The JGMO N3 Ultimate is a capable projector and can work well for folks who aren’t committing to a pitch-black home theater where its weaker black levels will steal focus.

At $2,999 / £2,499, it’s still fairly competitive with what’s on the market, but at $2,349 / £2,119, it’s more of a steal. It can compete with large TVs on value while providing the flexibility to enjoy movies and TV in 4K or switch over to 1080p for high-speed gaming. The different realms it can cover all from a single product help boost its value.

* **Value score: 4.5 / 5**

## Buy it if...

**You don’t like turning out all the lights** The JMGO N3 Ultimate has brightness to spare, letting it handle dim and even well-lit rooms fairly well. If you don’t like viewing in the dark, this makes it a good match.**You want quick and easy flexibility** The JMGO N3 can work in a lot of different setups and it makes getting it all lined up easy. If you’re always moving your projector around, that can save you a load of time.**You want versatility**  
The JGMO N3 Ultimate can tackle 4K video, 1080p gaming, precise and careful optical setup, or quick and effortless setup. Its ability to step into different roles can be very convenient if you have a lot of different needs.

## Don't buy it if...

**You’re looking for OLED performance** Few projectors do a great job with their black levels, and even the best aren’t really on the level with OLEDs. The JMGO N3 Ultimate isn’t even close, with raised black levels that don’t really look great in dark scenes.**You have lots of input sources** With just two HDMI ports, the JMGO N3 Ultimate is a recipe for annoyance if you have a handful of consoles, a Blu-ray player and the like. Heck, it doesn’t even have a 3.5mm jack you can use to free up the eARC port.**You don’t move your projector ever**  
A big part of the package, and therefore what you pay for, is the flexibility of the gimbal and optics. If you’re not planning to take advantage of them, you can save some money by looking elsewhere.
