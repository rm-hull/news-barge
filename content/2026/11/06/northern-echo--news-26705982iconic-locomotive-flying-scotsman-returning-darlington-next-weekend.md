North East railway fans will have the chance to climb aboard one of Britain's most celebrated locomotives when the Flying Scotsman runs from Darlington next weekend.

Passengers can travel on the historic stream locomotive from Darlington station to Stanhope on the Weardale Railway, with journeys planned for November 13, 14 and 15.

Customers have been warned that tickets are selling fast, and are advised to book in advance to avoid missing out.

Two journeys will run each day, with departures at 8.40am and 1.40pm, returning to Darlington at 12.50pm and 5.45pm respectively.

The tour covers about 60 miles and includes three hours of travel and one hour at Stanhope.

Passengers will travel through Bishop Auckland, passing Hopetown Darlington, before continuing along the preserved Weardale Railway.

First Class seats cost £129 per person, while Traditional Class seats cost £79, or £20 for children.

Flying Scotsman is one of Britain's best-loved locomotives.

Built in 1923 by the London and North Eastern Railway, it became the first express service from London to Edinburgh in 1928, and reached a recorded speed of 100mph in 1934.

The locomotive was retired from regular commercial service in 1963, but was saved from scrapping by rail enthusiast Alan Pegler before being restored at Darlington’s North Road.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/20190584.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Flying Scotsman was last seen in Darlington in December 2025, when it passed through the town twice during a trip to Newcastle.

Flying Scotman appeared there for an open day in 1965, later touring the United States and setting a world non-stop steam record in Australia.

In 2004, it was bought after a National Railway Museum campaign raised £2.2m.

A £2m refit was then completed in 2016, allowing the engine to return to service.

It has since shared its time between mainline tours and displays at the National Railway Museum in York and Locomotion in Shildon.

Flying Scotsman was last seen in Darlington in December 2025, when it passed through the town twice during a UK Railtours trip to Newcastle.

It also featured at the Titans of Steam event at Hopetown during celebrations to mark 200 years of the Stockton and Darlington Railway.

Flying Scotman's upcoming return to Darlington is part of a wider return of the engine to the mainline, led by promoter Fox & Edwards Events, working with Northern Steam and the National Railway Museum, its custodian.

The November trips will support the continued operation of the locomotive, with organisers saying each booking helps maintain the national steam heritage icon.

Tickets are available through Fox & Edwards' website.
