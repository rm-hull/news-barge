Ireland says bomb threats 'likely ... a coordinated campaign of disruption'

The Irish government has warned against a “likely coordinated campaign of disruption” in a statement responding to the latest bomb threats against European flights after similar threats were issued against some Irish media organisations (11:22).

“The threats made this morning against media organisations here are very serious.

Hoax threats such as these cause fear, distress and disruption to people going about their daily lives and activities; they achieve no positive purpose whatsoever. It is not known yet who is responsible for these threats, whether it is individuals or a centralised or coordinated effort.

However, these threats come on the heels of the ongoing disruption to aviation around Europe caused by similar threats, and it seems likely that this is a coordinated campaign of disruption.”

It continued:

“The Gardaí, working in cooperation with the Defence Forces, and our cyber and aviation authorities, are working to establish who is responsible. The Gardaí and the Defence Forces are in ongoing liaison with their European security counterparts. Every effort will be made to identify those responsible and to hold them to account.

The main ⁠priority, however, is to ensure that people are kept safe, despite the unavoidable and unwelcome disruption that this may cause.

The Gardaí have been alive to the risk of disruptive threats such as these in the context of planning for EU Presidency events and are exercising the relevant protocols in response.”

And, on that note, we will be closing the blog for today.

At least 18 flights have been disrupted by another round of false bomb threats across Europe, bringing this week’s total to more than 50, amid growing concern about a potentially “coordinated campaign of disruption” (14:15, 16:53).

Prague has seen the largest number of alerts, nine (13:14), with issues reported also on flights to and from Brussels (13:26), Paris (12:03), Rome (11:35) and Sofia (12:58).

The number of bomb threats represents only a tiny fraction of all flights across Europe, with almost 27,000 flights scheduled today (13:31).

Separately, bomb threats were also issued against Bulgarian, Irish, and UK news organisations, disrupting broadcasts and prompting staff evacuations in Ireland (11:22).

Further threats were distributed to media organisations this afternoon, but could not be immediately verified as genuine (17:16).

Ireland has warned against a “likely … coordinated campaign of disruption,” as it condemned attacks causing “fear, distress and disruption to people going about their daily lives” (14:15).

'This is only the beginning,' group claiming responsibility for hoax threats says

A number of European media outlets – including news organisations in Bulgaria, Ireland, and the UK – have reported that a group claiming responsibility for this week’s threats sent an email to several outlets warning of further attacks on transport across Europe.

“This is only the beginning,” it reportedly said.

We have chosen not to reproduce the email in full, given its unsubstantiated claims.

Meloni warns civilian infrastructure appears to be 'under attack' after recent incidents

Italian prime minister Giorgia Meloni has warned that “what’s being attacked is civilian infrastructure,” as she highlighted the evolving nature of threats and how they change people’s thinking about defence and security which is “not just talking about weapons, but safeguarding citizens’ daily lives.”

Italian prime minister Giorgia Meloni pictured in Rome earlier this week. Photograph: Stefano Carofei/Sintesi/SIPA/Shutterstock

Meloni said that it wasn’t clear if the attacks on French blood banks, refrigerators and planes were connected, but they all highlighted the newly emerging threats and were “a cause for concern.”

“When hackers attempted to alter the temperature of the refrigerators that store blood for transfusions, that blood was secured by a system that costs money; when you pay to buy that system, do we consider it defence spending or healthcare spending? When they tell us that the resources committed to defence and security are money taken away from healthcare or hospitals, that’s nonsense, because … we need both,” she said.

Belgian government to have another go at finding compromise on budget

I promised you an update on the situation in Belgium, and it looks like King Philippe has successfully pushed the government to resume talks on the next year’s budget.

Prime minister Bart De Wever pictured during a plenary session of the Chamber at the Federal Parliament in Brussels yesterday. Photograph: Nicolas Maeterlinck/Belga/Shutterstock

Prime minister Bart De ​Wever offered to resign on Monday ‌following months of talks that ‌failed to yield an agreement on €10bn in spending cuts and tax increases ‌that he says are needed to stabilise Belgium’s finances.

After their second meeting today, the king’s office said talks would resume immediately as coalition partners seek ⁠to bridge remaining differences and reach an agreement to avert the prospect of early elections, Reuters reported.

The Royal Palace said that in conversations with the king this week all five parties in the government coalition had shown a willingness to compromise.

The king therefore tasked the prime minister with starting a new round of negotiations and with reporting back to the king on 16 November.

De Wever ‌did not speak to the press waiting outside the palace before and after his meeting with the king.

I keep monitoring the situation on European airports for you.

During an earlier disruption at Venice airport (11:33), a number of planes had to be diverted elsewhere: Brussels Airlines’s flight from Brussels and KLM from Amsterdam went to Trieste, whereas Air France’s service from Paris, Iberia’s service from Madrid, and Aegean’s from Athens went to Bologna.

Some of them have since completed their journeys flying back to Venice.

Dutch military police clears KLM flight from Prague

The Dutch military police has confirmed that it has checked a KLM flight to Amsterdam faced with a bomb threat and confirmed that no explosives were on board.

The statement did not identify the exact flight, but we know that a flight from Prague to the Netherlands was among the nine that saw bomb threats in Czech Republic, and local Dutch media said the affected flight was coming from the Czech capital, which means it was the flight KL1350.

While the number of bomb threats issued against European planes can sure be unsettling, it’s worth remembering it’s still a tiny, tiny, fraction of all flights taking place in Europe today.

Eurocontrol’s daily counter says there will be 26,709 flights in Europe today.

Hungary scrambles fighter jets to monitor three civilian flights, minister says

Hungary’s fighter jets have been scrambled three times this morning to escort civilian flights in Hungarian airspace after reported bomb threats, defence minister Romulusz Ruszin-Szendi said on Facebook.

He said the first alert came in response to a threat issued against a WizzAir flight from Brussels to Sofia (that’s the W64328 we reported on earlier at 12:58), and two more for flights from Prague to Cyprus and Bucharest (the latter one is the Tarom we reported on earlier at 12:16).

Looking at Prague’s departures board, the Cyprus flight will be Wizz Air’s W64600 to Larnaca.

The Prague airport has confirmed to the Guardian that nine flights have been affected by bomb threats so far today, one up from the latest update (12:26).

“The Police have taken the necessary security measures in response. Operations at Prague Airport remain unaffected,” a spokesperson said.

Some other planes appeared to be disrupted too, with AJet’s service from Istanbul eventually diverting to Vienna after making several circles around Prague – although there is no suggestion that it was affected by this morning’s threats.

At least 17 flights have been reportedly disrupted today, but we are getting less detail from airports and national authorities about false alerts today, making it more difficult to keep track of all planes affected.

It’s also interesting to see civil aviation authorities’ planes or helicopters flying near some of the affected airports today, including Prague and Bucharest, keeping a watchful eye on what’s going on.

Two planes land safely after alerts en route to Sofia

A Bulgarian transport ministry spokesperson told AFP alerts were also received for two flights to Sofia on Friday, and that both flights coming in from London Stansted Airport in Britain and from Brussels landed safely.

Looking at the airport’s arrivals board, that’d be W64328 from Brussels and FR1730 from London Stansted.

At least eight flights to and from Prague affected by bomb threats this morning
