A County Durham mum who suffered life-changing injuries in a crash at a problem junction has described the moment she was "propelled into another reality" on impact.

Emergency services were called to the B6279 at Kinninvie Crossroads shortly before 9am on October 13 after a BMW collided with an Audi before one of the vehicles crashed into a nearby home.

Audi driver Annette Kaye, from Middleton-in-Teesdale, was airlifted to James Cook University Hospital, in Middlesbrough, while a 35-year-old man was arrested on suspicion of causing serious injury by dangerous driving.

Ms Kaye, who spent a week in hospital, has been told it may take a year for her to fully recover and says more must be done to improve safety at the crossroads.

Recalling the incident, the 62-year-old said: "I remember the whole crash. I didn't see the other car, so it felt like I was being propelled into another reality.

"Everything went wild - turbulent, loud and smoky, which I now know was the airbags going off."

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21672901.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Audi driver Annette Kaye *(Image: ugc)*

The mum-of-two said: "I've got a fractured pelvis, they put two long screws in, and then my upper arm bone was snapped. They've put two metal plates around that to secure it.

"But I'm alive and that's the important thing.

"I think my insides have had a good shaking up - I've been incredibly lucky.

"I've been told to allow a year before I'm fully recovered if there aren't any ongoing issues.

"I think there were about 30 people from the emergency services there and I think they were all expecting the worst. My partner was told to get to the hospital quickly because they weren't sure which way I was going."

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21572667.jpg?type=mds-article-620) *(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)*

Ms Kaye, a therapist and artist, was in a wheelchair following the crash but is now walking with the aid of crutches.

She thanked all the emergency services and hospital staff following the incident but remains nervous about travelling by car and is unsure whether she will ever drive again.

"I've been in the car while my partner has been driving and I am a bit nervous," she said.

"I think it will be quite different for me getting back in a car.

"Ten months ago I bought my dream car, which was an Audi TT. I think it probably saved my life because I used to drive a Fiat 500 and I don't think I'd be here if I'd been in one of those. I really do feel like it saved me."

Ms Kaye accepts the junction meets legal requirements but says more safety measures are needed to prevent further serious crashes.

"There just needs to be more," she said.

"Legally speaking there's everything there that needs to be there, which is all very well and good, but people are getting hurt.

"And of course the woman in the house is understandably utterly traumatised by it.

Her call for action echoes those of homeowner Jen Horton, whose house suffered significant damage in the crash.

The County Durham grandmother previously said she fears somebody will eventually be killed at the crossroads after her home was struck by a vehicle for the third time in six years.

Former midwife Ms Horton urged Durham County Council to review the junction and said she avoids her dining room in case another vehicle crashes through the wall.

She said: "I'm angry.

"Is it going to take someone dying for them to do something?

"I don't use the dining room because I'm so scared we'll get hit again. When my granddaughter comes round I won't let her in that room. I latch the door so no one can go in."

Following the latest two-car crash, Kieron Moralee, Durham County Council’s traffic management section manager, said: “Our thoughts continue to be with Ms Kaye following the crash at Kinninvie. While the exact cause of the accident is still unknown, we do appreciate her concerns.

“We have previously implemented several improvements designed to slow traffic and improve safety at the junction, including stop signs, road markings and vehicle-activated warning signs. These measures have been installed well in advance of the junction to help ensure they are clearly visible to drivers.

“We will continue to monitor the area and consider whether any further measures are appropriate.”
