# Pharmacist says 55p cupboard food 'can ease aches and pains' in winter months

## A pharmacist says a 55p tin of chicken soup could help ease winter aches and pains

Finding yourself stiff and achy since the temperature has dropped? You're certainly not alone. A study conducted by Deep Relief has discovered that 90% of British respondents report their aches and discomfort worsen during colder spells, while 83% indicate that frosty, icy and snowy conditions heighten their pain levels.

Pharmacist Noel Wicks explained: "It's not just in people's heads. A study by the University of Manchester, known as Cloudy with a Chance of Pain, involving more than 13,000 participants, found that higher humidity, lower atmospheric pressure and stronger winds were all linked to increased pain."

The consequences extend far beyond mere occasional discomfort. Deep Relief's most recent Pain Census Report revealed that 41% of those surveyed find it difficult to rise from bed due to pain, while 22% endure back, muscle or joint pain on a regular basis, reports the Express.

Mr Wicks noted: "Aches and pains can make life incredibly uncomfortable and day-to-day activities become harder. Putting some simple steps in place can make all the difference."

### Pharmacist's guidance for alleviating winter aches and pains

**Embrace chicken soup:** Particular foods including those high in fat and sugar can promote inflammation, exacerbating pain. "On the flip side, an anti-inflammatory diet full of colourful fruits and vegetables and protein-rich foods such as chicken, eggs, tofu and fish, can help to improve markers of inflammation in the blood and tissues and help maintain healthy muscles and joints," said Mr Wicks.**A hearty chicken soup**- available for as little as 55p per tin - alongside stewed fruits and vegetable-rich casseroles are all excellent options for supporting sore muscles and joints.** Sip tea:**Remaining well-hydrated is essential for keeping joint tissue lubricated and muscles functioning correctly. That said, consuming cold water during the chillier months isn't particularly appealing. As an alternative, Mr Wicks recommended tea, including herbal infusions, as a pleasant means of maintaining adequate hydration.** Why cooling relief can help even in winter:** When the cold snap leaves you feeling rigid and uncomfortable, reaching for something cooling might appear counterintuitive. Yet the cooling sensation achieves more than simply making skin feel cold - it can also assist in diminishing the perception of pain.

Mr Wicks said: "While keeping warm and gently moving can help ease winter stiffness, targeted pain relief can be useful when back pain, muscle pain or rheumatism make everyday activities uncomfortable."

**Don't let cold weather stop you moving:** Warming up and cooling down around physical activity is particularly crucial during winter, as muscles and joints tend to feel considerably stiffer in cold conditions. "Plus, we spend more time sitting down in winter, so muscles might feel tighter," said Mr Wicks.

He added: "Dynamic (moving) stretches and mobility exercises are best before exercise to loosen joints and muscles. Think shoulder rolls, leg swings, arm circles and walking lunges. Static (still) stretches are best after exercise to avoid muscles from tightening up."

Even brief strolls and frequent activity intervals can help ward off the stiffness that accumulates during extended indoor spells.

### Is your pillow contributing to your discomfort?

Experiencing morning aches? Your sleeping posture might be the culprit. The study revealed that 33% of participants reported that inadequate sleeping positions worsen their discomfort.

Mr Wicks explained: "Poor pillows and uncomfortable mattresses can make us feel pain when we wake up. Check that your pillow supports your head and neck comfortably, without forcing them into an awkward position."

He also suggested taking a warm bath or doing gentle stretches before bedtime to aid relaxation.
