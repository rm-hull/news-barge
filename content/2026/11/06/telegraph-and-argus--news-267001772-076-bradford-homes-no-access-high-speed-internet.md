Over 2,000 homes in the Bradford district have no access to reliable high speed internet.

Council bosses were asked about the figure, as well as what was being done to ensure all people have good access, at a meeting last month.

Councillor Alun Griffiths (Lib Dem, Idle and Thackley) asked the council’s Executive: “Can the portfolio holder advise what percentage of Bradford residents are unable to access reliable high speed internet?

“What steps are being taken to improve this or if the figures are not available, to find out how big the problem is?”

The response read: “The latest Ofcom data shows that, as at January, 98.9 per cent of residential premises in Bradford can access superfast broadband of at least 30 Mbps, resulting in approximately 2,076 premises, unable to receive 30 Mbps.

“Bradford is broadly comparable with neighbouring West Yorkshire authorities.

"Gigabit-capable broadband is available to 96.3 per cent of Bradford residential premises, compared with 96.3 per cent in Leeds, 96.4 per cent in Kirklees, 90.0 per cent in Calderdale and 97.0 per cent in Wakefield.

“While the council does not build broadband networks, it works with government, the combined authority and telecommunications providers to identify gaps, support investment and improve connectivity.

“The focus is increasingly on the remaining hard-to-reach areas, including through the national Project Gigabit programme, which targets premises unlikely to be reached through commercial rollout.”
