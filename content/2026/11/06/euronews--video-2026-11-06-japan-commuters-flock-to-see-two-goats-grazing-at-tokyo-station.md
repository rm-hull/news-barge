One-year-old Nagi and two-year-old Pokke arrived in October at a 900-square-metre vacant plot in the bustling Shinjuku district, where they have been steadily **grazing on weeds** as part of an**environmentally friendly initiative** launched by**East Japan Railway (JR East)**.

The trial, which began on 9 October, offers an **alternative to conventional grass cutting and herbicides** and is expected to continue until around December, unless the animals finish the job sooner. Although JR East has previously experimented with goat grazing, this is its**first such project within Tokyo's central Yamanote railway loop**.

The unusual sight has **attracted schoolchildren, office workers and commuters**, many stopping to take photographs and videos. Footage shows the pair feeding, wandering around their enclosure and resting in a small shelter, seemingly unfazed by passing trains.

For local residents, the goats have brought an **unexpected moment of calm** to the daily rush. Tamae Noguchi, who works nearby, said watching them eat so enthusiastically lifted her spirits.

Signs ask visitors not to feed the animals, allowing **Tokyo's newest gardeners** to get on with their work.
