A Rosyth councillor has renewed his plea to Forth Ports to rethink their decision on a passenger ferry from West Fife.

SNP representative Brian Goodall put forward a motion at a meeting of the South and West Fife Area Committee after plans for the link to Europe were declared dead in the water in September.

The proposed service, which would have carried both freight and passengers, was blocked by Forth Ports who said the route would not be commercially viable without a long-term commitment from operators.

The motion notes the decision and calls on Forth Ports to “reconsider” and continue dialogue with the Scottish Government and ferry operator DFDS to explore “all possible options” to deliver a ferry link.

![Rosyth councillor Brian Goodall](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21408714.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Rosyth councillor Brian Goodall

Addressing the committee, Cllr Goodall said: “Most people will be aware this issue has moved on quite a bit since I submitted the motion and we had a statement from Forth Ports and they were doubling down on their decision against the ferry.

“At my party conference, I spoke to the chief executive of Forth Ports and he took ownership of the decision and said it was something that came to his table and he took the decision not to go ahead with that.

“What he did say at that point is they have decided against this in preference for increased freight at Grangemouth harbour which, for me, raised a concern.

“As part of the whole Greenport proposals, one of the elements was the suggestion there could be freight and possibly a passenger ferry service at Rosyth.”

Cllr Goodall said he had been offered a meeting with Forth Ports to discuss possible options for freight at Rosyth.

He continued: “It seems very clear that their preference for additional investment in relation to freight is Grangemouth.

“That has given me quite a bit of concern. If we cannot get a passenger ferry, the next best thing would be increasing freight activity at Rosyth.

“I feel it is still important and the committee was given opportunity for us to state our view and to continue to request a rethink from Forth Ports.

“No one in Rosyth who I have spoken to about this issue as anything but supportive about the ferry being re-established.

“There was discussion about whether Dunkirk was the right place to go to or if we should have been looking at somewhere closer to hand in terms of distance onto Continental Europe. But everyone wanted to see it happening from Rosyth.

“I am just happy to give the committee the opportunity to express the view that is almost unanimous view in the local community, certainly in Rosyth, that we want to see a continental ferry service.

“You have government funding in place and an operator who wants to see it happen. It is a shame a third party element of the project has stepped in and pulled the plug.”

Fellow Rosyth councillor Andy Jackson said he had been disappointed to hear Forth Ports had withdrawn their support.

He added: “The economic advantages to having a passenger ferry service to continental Europe would benefit not only the local economy and tourism because Rosyth and surrounding areas do have tourist attractions.

“It would heighten awareness of the area. I am very much in favour of increasing links with Europe and this would have environmental advantages to the local area as well.

“I would call on them to review it and listen to the people of Rosyth and all the stakeholders involved in it.”
