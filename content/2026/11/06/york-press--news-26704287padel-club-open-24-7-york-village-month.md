## 'First' for York with new club for fast-growing sport open 24 hours a day

Courthaus Padel will feature three indoor courts *(Image: Contributed)*

A new indoor sports venue is opening in York offering 24/7 access to one of the fastest growing UK sports.

Courthaus Padel will open on Saturday, November 28 in Common Lane, Dunnington, becoming York’s first always-open padel club, according to its owners.

It will feature three indoor courts and offer coaching, social nights, and league play.




![Courthaue Padel, Dunnington](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21678622.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Courthaus Padel launches on Saturday, November 28 with an open day *(Image: Contributed)*

Josh Turner, owner of Courthaus Padel, said: "We wanted to build the kind of club we’d want to play at ourselves: somewhere you can turn up and play whenever it suits you.

"Padel has really taken off in York, and we can’t wait to welcome everyone."

Padel is a fast-growing sport that combines elements of tennis and squash and is played in pairs.

According to the Lawn Tennis Association, it now has 1.5 million players in the UK.

Planning officers at City of York Council approved the change of use of a vacant former commercial/warehouse building and associated car park and Dunnington Parish Council said it was supportive of proposals that enhance village amenities and provide benefits to the local community, according to planning documents.

![Compass House, Dunnington](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21678626.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Courthaus Padel will occupy up three units at Compass House in Common Road, Dunnington *(Image: Kevin Glenton)*

As reported by The Press, a planning application for exposed courts submitted by Dunnington Sports Club on the same road were refused by council planners who said the new facilities did not justify the loss of land for existing playing fields and the relocation of existing football and cricket pitches, and also due to the impact on the Green Belt.

That application also heard from objectors who claimed noise from the courts would disturb people playing and watching bowls nearby and posed a potential injury hazard to cricketers.

An appeal against this City of York Council's decision has been raised.

In February, plans for padel courts in Clifton were refused - and a planning committee at North Yorkshire Council said noise concerns from players games prompted a councillor decision to turn down an application in Harrogate.

Planning officers at City of York Council said in respect of the Courthaus Padel application that the nearest residential properties were approximately 130 metres away on Common Road and Hessacarr Lane, adding that "considering the change of use will result in the creation of padel tennis facilities which are limited to indoors the impact upon residential amenity of neighbouring properties would therefore be negligible."

Courthaus Padel will offer coaching, court hire, and a membership scheme with discounted bookings and priority access.

The club will also host regular social sessions and league nights.

To mark the launch, the club will host a free open day from 10am on November 28.

Visitors can try out the courts with support from on-site coaches.

Equipment will be provided.
