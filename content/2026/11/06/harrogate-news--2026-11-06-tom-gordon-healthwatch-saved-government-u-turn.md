Tom Gordon, MP for Harrogate and Knaresborough, has welcomed the Government’s decision to retain Healthwatch after previously announcing plans to abolish the organisation.

Healthwatch is the independent champion for patients and care users in England, gathering feedback about health and social care services and ensuring that people’s experiences are heard by those responsible for providing them.

In July 2025, the Government announced that Healthwatch would be scrapped. The proposal prompted concerns about the future of independent scrutiny of NHS services and how patients would be represented.

Mr Gordon wrote to the then Health Secretary, Wes Streeting, in July 2025 and subsequently raised the issue in Parliament and with organisations in North Yorkshire.

Working with Healthwatch North Yorkshire, he highlighted the potential impact of abolition during parliamentary debates on eating disorders and Type 1 diabetes, questioning what would replace the organisation’s role in representing patients and holding services to account.

The Government confirmed on 28 October that Healthwatch would be retained.

> **Tom Gordon MP said:**
>
> Healthwatch has been saved, and that’s a huge win for patients in North Yorkshire and across the country.
>
> I’ve seen the brilliant work Healthwatch does here. They’re a vital champion for patients, making sure their voices are heard by the people running their care.
>
> I’ve fought to save Healthwatch from day one, and I want to thank Chris Brackley, former Chair of Healthwatch North Yorkshire, for bringing it to my attention last year. We worried it was a done deal, but this shows that you can stop bad decisions in their tracks.
>
> The Government quietly slipped this U-turn out under cover of the Budget, but I won’t let that take away from a well-deserved win for patients.

## Parliamentary campaign

Mr Gordon’s campaign included correspondence with the Department of Health and Social Care, parliamentary questions and debates, and meetings with Healthwatch representatives.

In September 2025, he raised concerns during a Westminster Hall debate on eating disorders, questioning how independent accountability of eating disorder services would be maintained if Healthwatch was abolished.

He also submitted written questions on 5 September 2025 asking how patient views and engagement would be independently represented following the proposed abolition, whether an impact assessment had been carried out, and whether funding would be protected for independent patient advocacy services.

Following a meeting with Healthwatch representatives in October 2025, he wrote to the Department of Health and Social Care calling for ring-fenced funding and guarantees that patients’ views would continue to be independently heard across the NHS.

He raised the issue again during a visit to Spa Surgery in February 2026 and in a parliamentary debate on Type 1 diabetes in March.

The Government’s decision to retain Healthwatch marks a reversal of the plans announced in July 2025.
