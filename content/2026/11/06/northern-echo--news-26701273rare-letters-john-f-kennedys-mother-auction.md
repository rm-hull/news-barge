A previously unknown collection of letters from the Kennedy family is set to go up for auction.

The archive, mainly comprising autograph and typed letters signed by Rose Kennedy, is expected to fetch between £2,000 and £3,000 at Tennants Auctioneers on November 25.

Rose Kennedy, mother to John F Kennedy and Robert F Kennedy, both of whom were assassinated, wrote most of these letters to her English friend and travel partner, Marie Bruce.

While the correspondence largely revolves around practical matters, it offers a glimpse into the bond between Rose and Marie.

Despite their relationship reportedly not being very close, these letters hint at a more intimate connection than previously thought, particularly those penned after the deaths of her son and husband.

A notable item in the collection is a printed note of thanks from Rose following Robert F Kennedy's death, with a handwritten update for Marie about the family.

The auction, named the Books, Maps & Manuscripts Sale, also features a range of antiquarian and collectable books.

Among them is a collection of historical and heraldic manuscripts from the 16th and 17th centuries related to Sir Henry St George and Sir Richard St George, estimated to fetch between £300 and £500.

The manuscripts include orders of proceedings to Parliament in 1614 and documents about the funerals of William Cecil.

A rare copy of Prelude by New Zealand writer and critic Katherine Mansfield, valued at £800-£1,200, is also up for grabs.

Written during a love affair in Paris, it was one of about 300 copies printed and only the third book published by the Hogarth Press.

Other notable items include a 1935 printing of Andreas Vesalius’s anatomical treatise Bruxellensis Icones Anatomicae, estimated at £1,500-£2,000.

The volume was produced by the New York Academy of Medicine and the University of Munich shortly after the rediscovery of the printing blocks in Munich; however, the blocks were later destroyed during the Second World War.

Another is a collection of glass slides of images taken by amateur photographer Harold Bryce Thomson, estimated at £300-£500.

The majority of the images were taken in and around Wensleydale and offer a fascinating insight into the rural community in the 1940s.

The archive comprises approximately 475 slides and includes insightful portraits, images of village life, landscapes, and beautifully lit depictions of the photographer’s family and friends.
