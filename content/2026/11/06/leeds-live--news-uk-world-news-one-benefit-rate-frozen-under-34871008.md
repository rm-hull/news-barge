# One benefit rate frozen under Andy Burnham with 200,000 people warned

## Charities including the Trussell Trust and Joseph Rowntree Foundation are warning that a freeze on Local Housing Allowance (LHA) rates could push 200,000 people into poverty

Andy Burnham's government's decision to freeze a key benefit has put hundreds of thousands of people at risk of poverty, experts have warned. Under the Labour administration, Local Housing Allowance (LHA) rates are set to remain frozen.

Local Housing Allowance (LHA) is disbursed either by your local council via Housing Benefit, or by the Department for Work and Pensions ( DWP ) and central government as the housing element of Universal Credit, reports Birmingham Live.

Organisations including the Trussell Trust, Generation Rent and the Joseph Rowntree Foundation are now cautioning that the freeze will "critically undermine" the government's commitment to eradicating rough sleeping. LHA has remained frozen since April 2024. The letter says: "You have been clear that tackling homelessness is a top priority."

"These two steps would bring your vision within reach by tackling the cost of rent and ensuring that housing support for those who need it actually covers the cost of a home."

Holly Williamson, chief executive of Generation Rent, described it as "hugely disappointing" that the government had maintained the LHA freeze. She went on to say: "When housing benefit doesn't cover the rent it forces families into impossible decisions, traps them in poverty and leaves them vulnerable to homelessness, with councils picking up the pieces.

"This decision also brings into question the government's goal of ending rough sleeping, as it will mean there are very few affordable long-term homes for people currently sleeping rough."

In response, a government spokesperson stated: "We're determined to turn the tide on poverty and ensure people can access secure, affordable homes. That's why we increased Local Housing Allowance rates in 2024 at a cost of around £7 billion over five years, while also investing over £40 billion in affordable and social housing and launched a £1 billion Crisis and Resilience Fund.

"Alongside this, our landmark Renters' Rights Act will protect tenants from unfair rent hikes and bidding wars, while we're building the homes this country needs to help tackle the root causes of housing affordability pressures."
