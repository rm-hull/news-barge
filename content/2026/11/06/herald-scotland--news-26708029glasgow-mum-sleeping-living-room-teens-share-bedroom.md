A mum sleeping in a living room while her two teenagers share the only bedroom in their social home is an example of the “massive problem” of overcrowding in Glasgow.

A council meeting heard the issue of overcrowding is getting worse due to the homelessness crisis as housing is allocated to those with nowhere to live first.

Councillor Robert Mooney who represents the Canal ward said he visited a lady whose son and daughter share the only bedroom in their Wheatley housing association home.

He said: “She is in a bed in the living room and she has health issues.”

Asking if overcrowding is getting worse, he told the city administration committee: “I can’t be the only councillor in the room who seems to have a significant increase in people coming to me regarding overcrowding.”

The Labour politician described it as “massive problem” and said the only option open to the mother was to swap her property with someone else.

In response council leader Ricky Bell said: “Yes it is getting worse. We have a housing crisis that is very well known. There are not enough houses.”

He said the council is “trying our absolute best to build more houses as quick as we can” but need additional Scottish funding.

Depute leader Ruairi Kelly said applications for housing involve a needs based assessment.

Pointing out the huge demand, he said the “fact that we have so many people in temporary accommodation and in homelessness means those that are overcrowded do not have the opportunity to get larger homes the same as what they might have five six or 10 years ago when there wasn’t the same level of need.”

The housing convenor added that “unfortunately those who are in an overcrowded home fall lower down the list than those in no home at all in B&B or temporary accommodation.”

He continued: “The only way we can address that is to deliver more homes.”

The meeting heard the number of homeless households living in B&Bs was sitting at about 2,100 down from 2,500 as a range of prevention work has been taking place.

The matter was discussed as councillor Kelly presented the Glasgow’s Strategic Housing Investment Plan (SHIP) for 2027 to 2032.

The council aims for 6,102 homes to be completed between those dates and it is estimated the city could receive potentially about £674.163 million as a Scottish Government grant to deliver affordable housing. A total of 4,845 homes would be available for social rent.
