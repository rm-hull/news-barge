Stockton Council’s cabinet has faced challenges and questions as it said its proposal to buy Billingham town centre for £13m was “not ideal but the only option”.

The proposed purchase was opened up for questions from councillors on Wednesday night (November 4), before cabinet deciding to buy Billingham town centre from its owner Sheet Anchor Evolve (SAE) for £13m.

Councillors gave varying views on the deal, from saying the first phase of a regeneration plan was a “no-brainer” to warning the Labour-led authority was “paying over the odds”.

![A bird's eye view of Billingham. Credit: Stockton Council. Free for LDRS use.](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21674613.jpg?type=mds-article-620)A bird's eye view of Billingham. Credit: Stockton Council. Free for LDRS use.

Labour members for Billingham started by welcoming the plan. Councillor Marc Besford, for Billingham North, said: “For too long residents have watched the town centre decline while successive private owners fail to invest.

“Doing nothing is not an option and local people deserve better. This is about taking back control of Billingham town centre and ensuring that decisions about its future are made in the interests of the people of Billingham.”

Billingham Central Cllr Ann McCoy said: “We have been waiting for several years with several different owners who have promised much and delivered very little. Following community engagement, it will give the people of Billingham the town centre they deserve.”

Billingham South Cllr Paul Weston said £20m government regeneration funding, £10m to come from the Tees Valley Combined Authority (TVCA) for a second phase and another possible £5m for housing was “free money”.

He said: “For me it’s a no-brainer. It is too big an opportunity to miss.”

Conservative Billingham West and Wolviston Cllr Marcus Vickers asked for assurances the deal would not collapse, as partial acquisition negotiations broke down with SAE walking away in November 2025.

He said he appreciated the council was “stuck between a rock and hard place”, but suggested SAE bought the town centre for £3m to £4m and asked how a deal giving it £10m profit for a town centre in a worse condition could be justified.

Council deputy leader and regeneration cabinet member Cllr Paul Rowling said he believed it was justified in regeneration and long-term benefit to residents, and not going ahead seriously risked losing the government funding.

He said: “There’s a deal on the table in front of us… This isn’t the ideal scenario, but we are where we are.

“Ultimately they’ve made it abundantly clear that it’s this price or no sale. It’s the only option. We don’t want to oversee decline.

“Is it my preferred option? No. Do I want to get results? Yes. Are there alternatives set out? No there aren’t.”

He said it was taxpayers’ money but the council reduced risks as it would no longer borrow £10m as previously planned. Chief financial officer Clare Harper explained the council would avoid having to repay £725,000 per year.

Cllr David Reynard, also for Billingham West and Wolviston, said a private company would run the town centre under council ownership and asked how that would differ to SAE keeping it.

Cllr Rowling answered: “I don’t have faith, quite frankly, that things will change under the current set-up. Short of ownership, we don’t have that control.”

![Councillor Paul Rowling from Stockton Council. Picture: LDR.](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21514357.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Councillor Paul Rowling from Stockton Council. Picture: LDR.

Conservative Eaglescliffe West Cllr Stephen Dodds asked how a £2.25m “premium” over a £10.75m market value presented to the council was value for money for taxpayers.

Conservative Hartburn Cllr Niall Innes asked: “Where’s the evidence to justify us paying above the odds? How’s this value for money when this cannot be guaranteed?”

Cllr Rowling said: “It’s taking control of the situation which justifies that. It’s not ideal but it’s the only option. They’ve been quite clear that if it’s not sold, it would just go to the parent company [M Core].

“They were abundantly clear it was £13m or nothing. If that was to happen, we would completely lose control.”

He said paying increased amounts was not unique and this situation was affected by SAE’s knowledge that the council had £20m.

Cllr Innes said the council had owned and sold the town centre twice before and failed to modernise, and asked what would be different this time.

Cllr Rowling pointed to successful recent regeneration in Stockton, Norton and Thornaby, and council investment in Billingham Forum and John Whitehead Park, adding: “I’m incredibly confident that we have a track record in doing this stuff well and I’ve got absolutely no doubts in my mind that this will be a success.”

Billingham-raised Cllr Ted Strike, independent member for Ingleby Barwick North, said: “I do believe the current owners are holding a gun against the head of the council. You’re in that difficult position because they’re saying ‘£13m or nothing’. I don’t envy you.

“What if the cost goes higher than the £30m we’ve acquired? Where will that money come from?”

Cllr Rowling said the council had made financial considerations, there was potential additional funding and cabinet was only deciding on a first phase, with fresh community engagement to come.

Norton North Cllr Tony Riordan said the Conservative group he led agreed something needed to be done with Billingham town centre but asked: “What happens when the costs inevitably rise? What financial safeguards are in place?

“The £10m TVCA commitment remains conditional on detailed business case approval, the long-term ownership strategy is unfinished and future phases so not have secure funding.

"If costs escalate or anticipated funding is not approved, will the Labour group guarantee that essential council services and local taxpayers will not be expected to pick up the bill?”

Iain Robinson, assistant director for town centre development, said there was an allowance for repairs, rental income and service charges could be used and risks had to be weighed up in the decision. He said pre-demolition surveys would inform future plans.

Cllr Rowling did not accept that things would fail, saying the TVCA funding was not a blank cheque but plans would be phased rather than rushing through a business plan.

He said he could not speculate, “foresee absolutely every outcome” or give a maximum figure of “financial exposure”, but the council had extensive financial and legal advice.

Cllr John McDermottroe, a Billingham town councillor and Reform UK’s Stockton chair, asked whether council negotiators ever walked away, to which Mr Robinson said: “Ultimately there was no position to walk away. We get to this decision and balance it up.”

The cabinet’s decision to approve the sale will not go before the full council, but questions can be asked about it.

Some questions could not be answered in the cabinet meeting as some information could not be made public, but the council’s executive scrutiny committee can look at the matter and can “call in” the decision for reconsideration.
