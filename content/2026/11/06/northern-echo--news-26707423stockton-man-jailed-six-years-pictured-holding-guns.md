A man who posed for a picture with a shotgun sent Snapchat messages saying he wanted to 'blast a few' and 'shoot them'.

A large black bag containing guns and ammunition was found "deliberately concealed and hidden away" in woodland on South Road in Norton last August, a short distance from Tyler Davidson’s home.

Police recovered three viable firearms - a Skorpian machine pistol, a sawn-off shotgun and a Sig Sauer pistol. Davidson's DNA was discovered on the magazine of the pistol.

The 30-year-old's phone was seized and a photo discovered of him holding a shotgun and a Carlos pistol.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21681640.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Tyler Davidson, 30, of Rochester Road in Stockton, was sentenced today (Friday, November 6) *(Image: CLEVELAND POLICE)*

Teesside Crown Court heard the shotgun appeared to match one found in the bag.

Further analysis of his phone found Snapchat messages suggesting he had access to guns and saying, “I want to shoot them”, “I’m going to blast a few tomorrow” and “if he was round here, I would shoot the face of (sic) him”.

A number of other bags, backpacks and cartridges were also found during the woodland search, which saw a footpath cordoned off for four days.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/19782199.jpg?type=mds-article-620) *(Image: TERRY BLACKBURN)*

Davidson, 30, of Rochester Road in Stockton, pleaded guilty to two counts of possessing a firearm of length and possessing ammunition for a firearm without a certificate.

He was jailed for six years on Friday (November 6).

Cleveland Police Temporary Detective Superintendent Stuart Hodgson, who oversaw the operation, said: “I must acknowledge the discovery and subsequent recovery of three viable firearms that had been deliberately concealed and hidden away in woodland.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/19782283.jpg?type=mds-article-620) *(Image: TERRY BLACKBURN)*

“This is significant in our commitment to protect members of the public and our communities from the serious violence and harm caused by firearms and often their association with serious and organised crime.

Mr Hodgson added: “Information and intelligence are key to being able to remove firearms from the hands of dangerous criminals.

“I would take this opportunity to encourage anyone who has information to come forward as soon as possible.”

Information relating to firearms can be reported to Cleveland Police by calling 101, through the force's website or anonymously through Crimestoppers.
