The highest-performing secondary schools across the North East have been revealed in the latest Department for Education GCSE performance tables.

New provisional figures for the 2025/26 academic year show how state-funded mainstream secondary schools across County Durham, Darlington, Tyne and Wear and Teesside performed using the Government's Attainment 8 measure.

Emmanuel College in Gateshead emerged at the top of the regional rankings, recording an Attainment 8 score of 59.8.

The school finished ahead of Kings Priory School in North Tyneside, which achieved 57.6, while Teesdale School and Sixth Form in Barnard Castle completed the top three with 57.0.

St Leonard's Catholic School in Durham was fourth with 56.5, narrowly ahead of Durham Johnston Comprehensive School, which achieved 56.3.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21673480.jpg?type=mds-article-620)A secondary school *(Image: PA MEDIA)*

Three schools shared sixth place on scores of 55.0: Gosforth Academy in Newcastle, Marden High School in North Tyneside and The Academy at Shotton Hall in County Durham.

All Saints Church of England Academy in Stockton was next with 54.8, followed by Tanfield School in County Durham with 54.4.

The figures reveal that County Durham has five schools among the ten highest-scoring establishments in the region.

Elsewhere, Cardinal Hume Catholic School in Gateshead recorded 54.3, while Ingleby Manor Free School in Stockton and St Mary's Catholic School in Newcastle each achieved 54.1.

Whitley Bay High School recorded 54.0, while St Aidan's Catholic Academy in Sunderland achieved 53.6.

In Darlington, Carmel College was the borough's highest-scoring school, recording 52.7, ahead of Hummersknott Academy on 50.7.

Across Teesside, All Saints Church of England Academy recorded the highest score among mainstream secondary schools, followed by Ingleby Manor Free School and Egglescliffe School, which achieved 51.6.

Rye Hills Academy in Redcar and Cleveland recorded 51.2, while Outwood Academy Bydales achieved 50.4.

The figures also show differences between schools within the same local authority areas, although the results represent average attainment rather than a direct measure of teaching quality or pupils' progress.

Attainment 8 measures pupils' average achievement across eight qualification slots, including English, mathematics, three English Baccalaureate subjects and three further approved qualifications.

The measure gives double weighting to English and mathematics, provided the relevant entry requirements are met.

A higher score indicates stronger average attainment across the qualifications included in the measure, but it does not necessarily mean a school provides a better overall education than one with a lower score.

The regional rankings include mainstream academies, maintained secondary schools, free schools, a city technology college and alternative mainstream provision such as university technical colleges.

Independent schools and special schools have been excluded from the main rankings because their pupil intakes and educational provision can differ substantially.

The figures are provisional for 2025/26 and remain subject to revision.
