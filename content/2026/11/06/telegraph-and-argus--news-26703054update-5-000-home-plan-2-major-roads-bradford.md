The regeneration of a huge chunk of a city centre will create 5,000 homes, attract new business and “overcome some of Bradford’s biggest barriers of economic growth”.

But the Southern Gateway development will rely on millions of pounds of private investment, and one councillor has warned that public spending on the scheme must not “spiral out of control.”

## Where will it be?

The 126-hectare site includes much of the land between Manchester Road and Leeds Road.

It will eventually also include the new rail station being built at the interchange and the planned Mass Transit system that will link Bradford to Leeds.

## How much will it cost?

Members of the council’s Executive were given a report on the project at a meeting on Tuesday.

With the Government confirming last month that the station rebuild would go ahead on the current Bradford Interchange site, the Southern Gateway plans were now in a strong position to move forward.

![An artist's impression of the planned Southern Gateway](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21675850.jpg?type=mds-article-620)An artist's impression of the planned Southern Gateway *(Image: Bradford Council)*

The council will set aside £1.46 million over the next two years to help develop the plans for the Southern Gateway and new station.

This includes the cost of new scheme managers, legal costs, design works and investigating funding models.

Over five years, that cost is expected to rise to £3.35 million.

## What does the Southern Gateway offer?

The report to this week’s Executive meeting said the confirmation of the new station provided a “once-in-a-generation opportunity to stimulate and support transformational growth and regeneration of the Southern Gateway.

“National and regional regeneration policy and funding is increasingly focused on ‘densification’ of such core locations around transport hubs, as a way of creating critical mass and viability in development and investment, and to underpin local economic success.

“The Southern Gateway offers significant potential to accommodate new homes, employment space, commercial activity, public realm and supporting infrastructure, while strengthening connections between the city centre and surrounding communities.”

As well as the new housing and business space, the masterplan will include “the social infrastructure a growing city centre population will need.”

## Why it is important

David Shepherd, Director for Place, said the Southern Gateway, along with the new station, would: “unlock multi million pounds worth of funding to overcome some of Bradford’s biggest barriers to economic growth and change the fortunes for Bradford residents, who face some of the UK’s worst deprivation challenges.

“Rail investment alone will not deliver the benefits, this is why the Southern Gateway programme is so important.

“It will unlock development and private sector investment, support new homes and commercial growth, creating jobs and introducing a thriving, high quality gateway into the city.”

He said progressing with the Southern Gateway masterplans would put Bradford in the “strongest possible position to leverage further investment.”

![An artist's impression of the planned Southern Gateway](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/20939613.jpg?type=mds-article-620)An artist's impression of the planned Southern Gateway *(Image: Bradford Council)*

## Fears over costs rising

Councillor Rebecca Poulsen welcomed the scheme, but raised concerns that costs could rise if not properly monitored.

She said: “There is a history of projects spiralling out of control, and in the past we’ve seen money from hard working taxpayers going to fund projects like this.

"We need to make sure we’re ruthlessly looking at the cost.

“This scheme doesn’t just need public money – it needs private sector investment.”

Councillor Matt Edwards (Green, Holme Wood and Bierley) said: “This report doesn’t give reference to the numbers of housing.

"Will it include affordable and social housing, and how many homes does the council expect to be built here?”

He pointed out that the Southern Gateway is not included as a potential housing site in the council’s latest draft of the Local Plan, saying: “5,000 homes on bornfield land next to a rail station are far more sustainable than plans for 1,500 homes on the Green Belt, for example, in my ward.”

## Council fires back

Richard Hollinson, Assistant Director for Planning, Transportation & Highways, said: “At this stage the masterplan work is still quite conceptual.

"It indicates in the region of around 5,000 homes on previously developed land.

"The amount of affordable housing will be determined at a later stage.

"Any development in that zone would be subject to planning, and we would expect a level of affordable housing to come out of that.

“The Southern gateway is in the local plan, it is not a specific housing allocation, but we are factoring in the 5,000 homes as part of the brownfield windfall part of the housing allowance for the local plan.

"So those figures are in the local plan, that means less Green Belt loss in the district as a result.

“But the level of evidence behind these 5,000 homes is less clear at this point in time.”

He said further work on the plans would help the council prove that this level of housing could come forward in a “sustainable, regeneration led way in this location, and not at the expense of other locations.”
