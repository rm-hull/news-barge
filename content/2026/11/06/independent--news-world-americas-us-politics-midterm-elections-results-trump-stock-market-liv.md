As Democrats continue to add to their “blue tsunami” of midterms wins, President Donald Trump has pointed the finger of blame at the opposition and at the American public for Thursday’s stock market plunge, which has since begun to ease.

The Nasdaq Composite dipped 5.52 percent while the S&P 500 dropped 4.36 percent in response to this week’s resounding election results.

“The Stock Market suffered one of the Worst Days in History based on the results of the Election,” Trump wrote on Truth Social. “Remember, it wasn’t Republicans, it was Dumocrats.” In a follow-up, he told the electorate: “You only have yourselves to blame!”

Stocks began to recover Friday morning, with the S&P 500 edging up 0.70 percent and the Nasdaq gaining 1.28 percent after Thursday’s sell-off.

With votes still being counted in the Alaska and Mississippi Senate races and beyond, Democrats currently hold a 54 to 44 majority in the Senate and are projected to win 253 seats in the House to the GOP’s 157.

The results come amid a civil war brewing within the Trump administration, with insiders scrambling to assign blame, the president reportedly sounding out advisors on the potential firing of Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth and Secretary of State Marco Rubio reportedly on “resignation watch.”

## Republican lawmaker says there's 'a lot of questions about Hegseth'

Rep. James Comer, the Kentucky Republican who chairs the House Oversight Committee, offered no assurance of support for Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth on Friday.

Democrats have said they will push to impeach Hegseth, and administration officials are reportedly considering ousting him.

“What do you want to see done with Pete Hegseth?” a NewsNation host asked Comer.

“That’s gonna be a decision that President Trump’s going to have to make,” Comer replied. “But I can tell you with a high level of confidence that there’s a lot of questions about Hegseth. We have no idea what the strategy is moving forward in Iran. No one’s happy about Iran, and I think the Republican voters are more upset about what’s going on in Iran than the Democrat voters.”

## Hegseth not even under consideration for Senate role as his possible administration escape hatch closes: report

Prior to the midterms, it was suggested that Pete Hegseth might make a play for Marsha Blackburn’s Tennessee Senate seat, assuming it became vacant when she won her race to become the Volunteer State’s next governor, as a means of escaping angry Democrats on Capitol Hill.

Blackburn did indeed win the governor’s manion, as it happens, but Hegseth is reportedly not in contention to be her replacement after all, leaving him trapped inside the Pentagon – unless Trump fires him.

## El-Sayed has already found something he wants to work with Trump on

Senator-elect Abdul El-Sayed has already found something he wants to work with President Donald Trump on – cutting aid to Israel.

The Michigan Democrat, who has been an outspoken critic of Israeli leadership during the war in Gaza, which he calls a genocide, backed Trump’s idea to cut $4 billion in aid to Israel by the end of the year, saying that he would “love to work with him on doing that.”

“I don’t think we should be continuing to subsidize a foreign military that has done the worst things with our money,” El-Sayed told the hosts of MS NOW’s *Morning Joe* Thursday.

“In fact, I don’t know that we should be continuing to sell weapons to a country that is, again, using our weapons to do awful things.”

## Texas went so blue Talarico got more early votes than Paxton got in total

Ruby red Texas went so blue during the GOP’s crushing midterm defeat that Democrat James Talarico got more early votes than Republican Ken Paxton got in total.

Talarico defeated Paxton with more than 5.4 million votes in a blue tsunami that hit their state, as well as other reliably Republican states such as Iowa and Kansas.

Paxton got 4,249,373 total votes, including 3,232,667 early votes, according to data from Texas Secretary of State Robert S Howden’s office.
