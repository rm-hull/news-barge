Firefighters on Teesside were attacked as they faced a 70 per cent surge in Bonfire Night incidents.

Cleveland Fire Brigade said its crews attended more than 100 fires since November 3, including 83 on Bonfire Night - up from 49 last year.

Many involved preventable, deliberately started rubbish fires, the brigade said.

Firefighters also faced violence while responding to emergencies.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21679431.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The charred remains after a bonfire *(Image: CLEVELAND FIRE BRIGADE)*

The fire service said: "Sadly, our crews have also faced violence while responding to these emergencies and working to keep people safe.

"Our firefighters wear body-worn cameras, and any assault or abuse directed towards emergency workers will be reported to police.

"We will support action being taken against anyone who attacks our crews.

"We are asking parents, carers and the wider community to speak with children and young people about the consequences of attacking emergency workers or deliberately starting fires."

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21679430.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Disposed fireworks *(Image: CLEVELAND FIRE BRIGADE)*

It warned that rubbish fires put lives at risk, damage communities and keep firefighters from other emergencies.
