Irvine Welsh has revealed he is to pause writing new books to focus on film and TV projects.

The best-selling author suggested he had become tired of travelling around the world to promote his latest novels.

Welsh, who has published four books in the space of four years, revealed he had “loads” of screen projects he wanted to get off the ground.

He was speaking at an event in his native Edinburgh to promote his latest novel, Can Nothing Save Us?, a thriller focusing on three generations of a wealthy family in modern-day Los Angeles that examines America’s gun culture, which is currently in development to be turned into a TV drama.

Welsh, who was taking part in a Topping & Company in-conversation event with the poet Len Pennie, told how the idea for his new novel had emerged from abandoned plans for a comedy series about an American “superstar DJ”, which Calvin Harris, Will Smith and Jay-Z had all been attached to.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21266218.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Irvine Welsh launched a new musical adaptation of Trainspotting earlier this year. *(Image: Ian West/PA Wire)*

Welsh’s most recent screen work, with regular collaborator Dean Cavanagh, has included the drama series Crime, an adaptation of his novels on troubled Edinburgh detective Ray Lennox, and Creation Stories, a feature film about the life of Glasgow-born music industry mogul Alan McGee.

Welsh, who turned 68 in September, told the audience that the success of Trainspotting, his debut novel, had effectively allowed him to “retire” in his early thirties.

He said: “To me, one of the best things you can do in your life is make your hobbies pay. Writing is something I would do whether I got paid for it or not.

“I was fortunate enough to be able to get paid for my very first book. I was kind of able to retire when I was 32. I have basically been indulging my hobby since then.

“One of the great things about writing is that it, if you have a different theme and a different set of ideas, it gives you a chance to reinvent the process. Every book I have written has been done slightly differently to the last one.

“I used to work through the night, but I don't do that so much now. I used to do that with the earlier books, but I’m a bit more disciplined now.

“I'm up early and doing stuff early in the morning when my brain is kind of more awake.

“The great beauty of it is that you get to pick your own hours, not just when you work, but how you work.”

Can Nothing Save Us? is the 16th novel to be published by Welsh, who has published three new novels featuring his Trainspotting characters over the last decade - The Blade Artist, Dead Men’s Trousers and Men in Love, which was launched last year, and two Ray Lennox novels, The Long Knives and Resolution, which was published in 2024.

Welsh said: “I went a bit nuts during lockdown. I just kept writing. There was nothing else to do, I knocked out all these books.

“It’s been great fun, but you forget that you have got to promote them and they come out in all these different languages.

“I have been jumping around all over the world for what feels like forever, but certainly for the last five years.

“I want to take a couple of years off from publishing books, and just focus on TV and film stuff. I’ve got loads of different projects.

“The great thing about film and TV is that if you get something made nobody gives a f\*\*\* about you. Nobody wants to talk to the writer. They want to talk to the actors, basically. If they can’t get them, they will talk to the director. If they can’t get the director, they will grudgingly talk to the writer. You get an easy ride.

“You get to do your thing, write your screenplay and then just vanish, basically. You can then get on with some more stuff.

"When you write a book, once it is published that is when the work really starts.”

Welsh also spoke at the event about the growing influence of artificial intelligence on human beings, which he described as "very extractive and very manipulative."

He said: "It has kind of gone into everything and everywhere. We don't even know we are using it sometimes. I don't think it is doing us any good.

"We really have to start thinking again and we have to start reading again.

"One of the things I am really keen to do is to get people reading again, particularly young men. They have stopped reading, basically.

"Reading is an exercise in empathy. You are putting yourself in the positions of other people, other cultures, other viewpoints and other morals.

"If you lose that, you lose a massive chunk and essence of your humanity. We have to read. We have to stay educated. We have to keep our minds moving . We cannot do that if we outsource everything to a machine. We are just going to turn to vegetables, basically."
