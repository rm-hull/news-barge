A specialist electrical commissioning firm is marking five years in business with plans for major growth at home and abroad.

Northern HV, based in Hartlepool and founded by directors Chris Thomas, 36, and Phil Bromby, 44, has grown from a home-office operation into an international turnkey project delivery specialist.

The company serves a range of sectors including renewables, data infrastructure, and transmission substations.

Mr Thomas said: "It is fantastic what we have set up, but it wouldn’t be where it is if we didn’t have the people.

"Sometimes we are so busy, we don’t realise how big we have become.

"The team works, and we know we can rely on the people with us.

"We try to make sure we keep the business heading in the right direction."

Northern HV is now headquartered at the Hartlepool Innovation Centre and has expanded into multiple units at Enterprise Court.

The directors plan to convert one of these units into a training academy to support the development of new engineers.

Staff numbers have grown steadily, from two people in the first year to 13 employees and contract managers, with the company regularly deploying between 30 and 50 specialists.

Its fast-response call-out team operates 365 days a year under a framework agreement with a major UK energy company.

The company has also established a branch in Sweden.

Mr Bromby said: "We want to help create the right culture that suits us.

"Ultimately, everyone’s opinion counts.

"Our goal now is to keep building the team, growing the work, getting a bigger place and consolidating the units."

The business began after the directors, who met offshore and shared a passion for cars, embarked on a European road trip.

Mr Thomas said: "We set up as Northern HV, got some European contractual work, and started to do some SAP (Senior Authorised Person) work, commissioning and testing.

"Through various contacts, we got some work in Mexico on drilling shops, switchgear, and circuit break testing.

“We came back, continued to mould into SAP and test work, and touched base with another company in Denmark on a wind farm project. It all escalated from there."
