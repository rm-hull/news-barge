Small boats like this one in Gravelines have mainly launched from France in recent years

**Dover MP Mike Tapp says smugglers will keep adapting after it was discovered that a boat stopped in the Channel with 38 migrants on board had launched from the Netherlands.**

Prosecutors have confirmed the vessel that was found floating with no engine near Cadzand on Saturday had entered the water from Dutch territory.

This year crossings have already been attempted along a wider stretch of coast from Belgium to Normandy.

The UK government said that overall crossings were down significantly this year.

"As launch disruptions grow, the criminal gangs will adapt and a part of that will be finding new launch locations and methods," said Tapp.

"That means our operational efforts must also be agile.

"We have seen a significant reduction in crossings this year, but we still have a long way to go."

![A man with blonde hair and a short beard, and wearing a white shirt, is standing in front of a sign - it is white with a large red cross on it and the words, in black, Rode Kruis](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/153e/live/9f9d8a90-d9c6-11f1-a00c-673f8daaffd9.jpg)

Bastiaan van Blokland from the Netherlands Red Cross said the crossing was "unusual"

Dutch Deputy Prime Minister Bart van den Brink, who is also minister of asylum and migration, said it was "highly disturbing" to see this happening in Dutch waters.

Speaking to RTL he said: "This problem was situated around Calais for a long time and moved up north in the direction of Belgium.

"But now for the first time in the Netherlands we see this occurring."

He said after the incident the relevant Dutch authorities would be brought together to "see what we can learn from this".

Bastiaan van Blokland, from the Netherlands Red Cross, said the crossing was "unusual" and "didn't look safe at all", adding "we don't see these things often, if at all".

"The situation definitely was dire, 38 people on a boat with legs dangling out of the boat, in the cold weather, without an engine," he said.

"We were surprised that this situation occurred, we are not very familiar with these kind of movements and we will be alert for the future."

Two men are still being questioned on suspicion of people smuggling, police said.

## 'New methods'

Small boat smuggling attempts have increased from 2018 and beaches around areas of Calais and Dunkirk in Northern France have tended to be the traditional launch points.

Earlier this year, the BBC reported on a sudden rise in small boats setting off further up the coast from Belgium.

Federal Belgian police suggested it had been due to French enforcement activity.

Police officials in the Netherlands described the small boat rescue as rare.

However, there have been a small number of cases in recent years where smugglers have been caught trying to use yachts to smuggle migrants into the UK from the Dutch coast.

The UK government said this year had seen the lowest number of summer arrivals - a period when journeys often rise in part due to the warmer weather - since 2020.

Last week crossings resumed after three weeks of stormy weather.

Former director general of the UK Border Force Tony Smith said it was "clear that the human smugglers operating in Europe continue to try new routes to bring illegal entrants to the UK".

"In recent months we have seen launches from the Normandy beaches, Belgium and now as far afield as the Netherlands rather than across the short straits to Dover under the original model," he said.

"It is important that the Border Security Command adapt its operations to cope with this ever expanding challenge."

The government said in October that the home secretary had signed a deal with the Belgian interior minister enabling police in both countries to exchange intelligence more quickly to disrupt and dismantle organised criminal networks, including those involved in migrant smuggling.

Nearly 1,050 French officers are now supporting UK-France efforts to stop crossings, with an additional new surge team bolstering operations on Western beaches, according to officials.

A Home Office spokesperson said: "Joint efforts with international partners have prevented over 50,000 crossing attempts and seized 1,100 boats and engines since the election.

"But we must go further. That's why we are boosting the number of officers on the ground in France and removing the pull factors drawing people here through illegal and dangerous routes."
