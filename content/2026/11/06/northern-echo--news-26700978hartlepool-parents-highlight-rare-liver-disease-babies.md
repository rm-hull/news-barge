A couple is taking on a 100-mile walking challenge to raise awareness of a rare liver disease in babies.

Amy and Ben Meredith, from Hartlepool, are walking throughout November to support Liver UK and highlight the signs of biliary atresia.

Born in August, Ella began showing the worrying symptoms at just nine days old.

Amy said: "I couldn’t get through to my midwife, so I called 111 for advice, and they advised me to take her to paediatric A&E.

"So, we took her to North Tees Hospital, and as I was changing her nappy after a blood test, a senior doctor noticed that her stool colour was very pale – kind of clay coloured.

"We had never seen this before, but even if we had, I’m not sure if we’d have raised it as a concern.

"The doctors explained that pale stools in newborn babies can be a sign of biliary atresia, a rare and serious liver disease, and said it was lucky we had brought Ella in when we did, as it needs to be treated as early as possible.

"We were disturbed, however, to realise that, as new parents, we were completely unaware of the symptoms of this life-threatening condition.

"We thought we’d like to raise some awareness of this condition as well as funds for Liver UK, the charity that supports families who are affected."

Ella’s tests ultimately came back negative, and she has since recovered fully.

Michelle Wilkins, head of children’s and families services at Liver UK, said: "We’re delighted to hear that baby Ella is well and very grateful for what Amy and Ben are doing to raise awareness of liver disease in babies.

"If biliary atresia is detected early, surgery can take place, which can delay the need for a liver transplant for many years.

"Our Yellow Alert campaign is all about identifying liver disease in babies, and we have a wealth of information and free resources available to parents and health professionals."
