For almost five years, David and Mollie Somerville have spent an hour every Friday standing outside the headquarters of one of Britain's largest local government pension funds, hoping to persuade it to move away from fossil fuel investments.

The couple have held a weekly vigil outside the West Yorkshire Pension Fund (WYPF) offices in Bradford since 2021, protesting against investments in oil and gas firms including Shell and BP. Despite spending around 250 Fridays outside the same building, they say they have no plans to stop.

## **Five years of weekly protests**

Every Friday at midday, the couple are joined by supporters outside WYPF's Bradford headquarters.

Mollie said: "We started the vigil nearly five years ago and we come every Friday from 12 o'clock till one o'clock. We do it every Friday for one hour, never missing a Friday."

She added: "The reason we do it consistently on a Friday, at the same time, is that everybody knows that we're going to be here. Supporters come. If they can't come one week, they know that we'll be here the next week, and so do the pension fund."

The campaign began after David's brother took part in similar demonstrations outside Greater Manchester's pension fund.

David said: "Bradford is where the headquarters of West Yorkshire Pension Fund are, where our local authorities, and quite a lot of other local authorities, have their pensions."

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21637778.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Banners demanding the end to fossil fuel investment face the office entrance. *(Image: T&A)*

## Concern over fossil fuel investments

WYPF is one of the largest Local Government Pension Scheme funds in England and Wales, managing around £20bn of assets on behalf of current and former local authority employees.

The Somervilles say their concern centres on the fund's continued investment in major fossil fuel companies.

David said: "The main thing is that we're trying to get them to get out of fossil fuels, oil, gas, coal, and we've been trying for years. A few words have changed but they haven't changed."

He added: "They still have half a billion in Shell, half a billion in BP, and those aren't the only ones, but they're the main ones."

David argued that the long-term effects of climate change outweigh any short-term financial returns generated for pension holders.

"They think it makes money for the pensioners. That's what they think, and it is legal. They're not doing anything illegal, but the trouble is they're not thinking of the long term."

## 'Climate disaster' warnings

David believes climate change is already having visible consequences.

He said: "Fossil fuels are the main cause of carbon dioxide in the atmosphere, which is causing not climate change, but climate disaster. It's happening much faster than scientists thought even five years ago."

David added that future impacts could be felt through food shortages and rising costs.

He said: "The next change to come will probably be noticed on supermarket shelves because farmers haven't been able to grow crops either to feed cattle or to feed people."

## Progress or just words?

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21637775.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Pension fund staff encounter the silent vigil as they leave the office at lunchtime. *(Image: T&A)*

The couple say they have noticed a change in the pension fund's public messaging over the years, including greater acknowledgement of climate risks.

David said: "They have replied to emails and some of their reports over the years have acknowledged that climate change is happening. They've said they will not invest in any new projects.

"There's no definite progress. It's words more than anything else. It's not real progress.

"They are beginning to change the tone of their reports, but we can't point to something and say, 'This has actually changed now'."

## 'We're like mosquitos'

The couple describe their campaign as one of persistence rather than confrontation.

Mollie said: "They know that we're here, a little bit like a mosquito buzzing round their heads. Whether we make a change at all, we don't know, but we're going to keep going until we do."

She believes their presence has had some impact.

Mollie said: "There have been small movements. The fact that they are writing things about climate on the website and in reports shows that they are being rattled."

## Fund points to climate commitments amid divestment campaign

The Telegraph & Argus approached the West Yorkshire Pension Fund seeking comment on the campaign, its investment policies and the issues raised by protesters, but received no response.

The fund has previously highlighted its climate commitments, including signing the 2024 Global Investor Statement to Governments on the Climate Crisis.

The statement calls on governments to accelerate the transition away from fossil fuels, expand renewable energy generation and support efforts to achieve net-zero emissions by 2050.

It also backs plans to phase out unabated fossil fuel use in line with international efforts to limit global warming to 1.5C.

## No plans to stop

Asked how long they intend to continue standing outside the building every Friday, Mollie said the answer was simple.

She said: "The main thing is that we're trying to get them to get out of fossil fuels, oil, gas, coal, and we've been trying for years."

Until then, David and Mollie say they will continue their now-familiar weekly vigil outside the pension fund's Bradford headquarters, hoping that persistence will eventually bring change.
