**Friday's top story: Immigrant shopkeepers in Halle strike against far-right**

Immigrant shopkeepers in the eastern German city of Halle, fearful for their future after the virulently anti-immigrant AfD party scored a landslide victory in recent regional elections, staged a one-day strike Thursday.

"Because of the AfD and the racism -- well, I just can't stay here in Halle," Mohamad-Samer, who works at a fast-food restaurant that shut as part of the demonstration and did not want to give his last name, told AFP.

"And everyone else -- all the Syrians, all the foreigners -- they cannot stay here either," said the 29-year-old who has lived in the city for three years.

Barber shops, restaurants and small retail shops -- about 30 businesses in total -- all joined the demonstration, pulling down their shutters and marking off their businesses with red-and-white tape.

The coordinated action, dubbed "Halle Without Us?", was aimed at showing how vital immigrants are for the life of this mid-sized city in the eastern state of Saxony-Anhalt.

The far-right Alternative for Germany (AfD), which campaigned on pledges of mass deportations and touted the slogan "Remigration now!", won nearly 44 percent of the vote in September's state election.

Following the "Gen Z regelt" protests when tens of thousands of mostly young people demonstrated in cities across Germany last weekend, another day of action is already set for November 20th.

According to a recent DeutschlandTrend survey by ARD, the protest actions are overwhelmingly supported by the German population.

Almost three-quarters of Germans understand the concerns of the demonstrators, and 82 percent of people in Germany said they think that politicians do not take the concerns of young people seriously enough.

Nine out of ten respondents said participation in registered demonstrations is a justified means of asserting one's political point of view, but a good two-thirds of people were against road blockades.

Ninety-five percent of people reject violence against people or property as a political tool.

The survey polled 1,297 people from Monday to Wednesday of this week.

People in Germany are living longer, but life expectancy still varies considerably by region and gender, according to new research by the Federal Institute for Population Research.

Baden-Württemberg records the highest figures, with women expected to live an average of 84.6 years and men 80.6 years. Men in Bavaria also reached the 80-year mark for the first time in 2025.

The life expectancy gender gap is widest in eastern Germany. In Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania, women live 6.4 years longer than men on average, compared with a 3.7-year difference in Saarland.

Life expectancy rose for men in every state compared with 2024 and now exceeds pre-pandemic levels nationwide. The picture for women is less positive. Figures fell in Schleswig-Holstein, Bremen, Brandenburg and Thuringia, where the decline reached six months.

Women in seven states remain below their 2019 life-expectancy levels.

Access to healthcare also matters, including the number of doctors and the distance residents must travel to hospitals and specialists.**Study warns AI expansion could increase water stress in Germany**

The expansion of data centers triggered by the AI boom threatens to put a massive burden on the regional water supply in Germany, according to a short study by the Oeko-Institut.

The study found that local server farms used around 13 billion litres of water for cooling in 2025.

By 2030, this demand could grow to up to 27 billion litres per year through new mega data centres - which corresponds to the drinking water consumption of a large city with around 580,000 inhabitants.

The new calculations are in stark contrast to assessments presented by industry representatives. The industry association Bitkom had recently classified the water demand as largely unproblematic.

The Oeko-Institut, however, calculates a weighted average value of 0.67 litres of water per kilowatt hour of IT electricity consumption.

Today's annual consumption of 13 billion litres already corresponds to the entire water content of Berlin's Wannsee.

Planned large-scale projects are particularly taxing: For a single 100-megawatt data center, the experts estimate the annual water requirement at 200 to 930 million litres, depending on the cooling technology, which is equivalent to the drinking water needs of a small town with up to 20,000 people.

Bayern also completed a domestic double but lost a dramatic Champions League semi-final to Paris Saint-Germain.**Bosch to cut 750 more jobs at Abstatt site**

Bosch plans to cut another 750 jobs at its Abstatt site in Baden-Württemberg by the end of 2029 as pressure on Germany’s automotive industry intensifies.

The reductions will affect the supplier’s mobility division and will be implemented as socially responsibly as possible, the company confirmed to *Handelsblatt*.

Bosch said growing competition from Chinese rivals and the transfer of some system-development work to China were driving the decision.

Finance executive Mohammed Abraham said the company needed to adjust its structures and costs to secure the site’s long-term future. Abstatt will nevertheless remain a development centre.

The announcement follows plans revealed in September to cut 900 jobs – half the workforce – at Bosch’s Nuremberg site.

Together, the two measures add 1,650 positions to the company’s planned German reductions, taking job cuts in its mobility division to 23,650.
