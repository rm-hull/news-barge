More than 40 creatives from across the Cairngorms National Park have taken part in a globally renowned photography project aimed at celebrating their contribution to culture and community.

Members of the Cairngorms Creative Collective joined Inside Out, the international participatory art project founded by French artist JR, by creating a striking installation featuring black-and-white portraits of photographers, makers, writers, performers and other creative practitioners who live and work across the National Park.

The portraits of 42 creatives were brought together in a large-scale group image on the plateau of Cairngorm Mountain, creating what organisers described as an iconic visual statement about the strength and diversity of the region’s creative community.

The Cairngorms Creative Collective brings together artists and creative practitioners from across the National Park, using collaboration to promote creativity, cultural heritage and connections to the landscape while supporting a new generation of creative talent in the region.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21679416.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The Cairngorms Creative Collective's Inside Out portraits on Cairngorm mountain's plateau, Cairngorms National Park. *(Image: Hannah Bailey)*

The project places the Cairngorms on the international map of Inside Out, which has involved more than 700,000 people in 154 countries and territories around the world.

The Collective’s involvement forms part of the Cairngorms 2030 programme, which is supported by The National Lottery Heritage Fund.

Photographer and project producer Hannah Bailey said the installation was designed to highlight both the individual talents of local creatives and the importance of the wider network that supports them.

She said: “We hiked up to the Cairngorm plateau to lay out forty-two portraits of local creatives who live and work in the National Park. We wanted to show that they're here, east and west of this summit, as skilled individuals and as a strong community.

“Creatives in rural areas are so often undervalued or overlooked, which is why being part of a global project like Inside Out matters. It puts the spotlight on the Cairngorms Creative Collective, the network that brings all these faces together as one force, supporting creativity in the park for generations to come.”

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21679415.jpg?type=mds-article-620)More than 40 creatives from across the Cairngorms National Park took part in the international photography project. *(Image: Hannah Bailey)*

The project was included in the Collective’s first two-year activity plan and was seen as an opportunity to raise awareness of the organisation while adding its voice to an international artistic movement.

As well as joining the global project, members wanted to champion the concept of dùthchas – a Gaelic word encompassing ideas of kinship, heritage and the deep connections between people, nature and place.

Jess Greaves, director of the Cairngorms Creative Collective, said the initiative offered a powerful introduction to both the National Park and an international audience.

She said: “Taking part in Inside Out felt like the right way to introduce the Cairngorms Creative Collective to the National Park and to the world.

“We wanted to raise awareness of the collective as a geographic group of artists and to use our collective voice to celebrate what we feel passionate about: dùthchas, community and this extraordinary landscape we call home.”
