Fans of Richard Hammond's latest TV series about a classic car company have reacted to the latest episodes by saying how much they miss the family which co-founded the business.

The former Top Gear and The Grand Tour presenter is back with a new series of Richard Hammond's Workshop, which follows life at his classic car restoration business, The Smallest Cog. The workshop is based at the Rotherwas industrial estate in Hereford.

The sixth series began on Quest on October 27 and features the 56-year-old presenter, who attended Ripon Grammar School and Harrogate College, attempting to turn his Herefordshire workshop into an internationally recognised name.

Fans of the series have already reacted to the latest episodes, with some saying how much they miss members of the Greenhouse family who previously appeared in episodes which are also available on Discovery+.

![](https://www.oxfordmail.co.uk/resources/images/21669774.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The Greenhouse family with Richard Hammond in 2021 *(Image: Contributed)*

One viewer posted: "I watched the whole of series 6 last night on HBO Max and yeah I agree with other comments, that the Greenhouses leaving the The Smallest Cog isn’t the same without them being there now."

Another added: "Not the same without the Greenhouses."

Mr Hammond founded the business in 2021 after mechanics Neil Greenhouse, Anthony Greenhouse, and Andrew Greenhouse - who had worked on his vehicles for years - were at risk of losing their own workshop.

The new episodes promise some of the team's "most ambitious and intricate restorations yet", alongside the realities of expanding the business and recruiting new staff.

A synopsis for the series said: "The Herefordshire workshop tackles its most ambitious and intricate restorations yet, while grappling with business expansion and the challenges of recruiting new staff.

"From high-profile projects to quirky classics, expect Hammond’s trademark chaos as he balances passion with hard-nosed decisions."

Mr Hammond said earlier the new series is "all about recruiting new skills", to the business, with a new business development manager, Roy, joining the team, who was featured in a trailer for the new series.

He added: "I want to grow the business; I want a hub. I want a little roadhouse where people can come and see the cars being worked on, maybe do some car storage, maybe even consign some cars for sale. I don't know, celebrate the things that I love. But also, we need cars to work on.

"It is a real workshop. One of the issues is people sometimes think we're like the repair shop, and you bring your car along and get 50 grands' worth of work done for free. I'd have to pay for that, and I can't."

Earlier this year, it emerged that the car workshop was at one point struggling under debts of about £700,000, according to The Sun newspaper.

Mr Hammond reportedly injected nearly £600,000 of his own money into the garage venture, where the team chrome-plated Jeremy Clarkson’s vintage tractor.

It later emerged via documents posted with Companies House that Mr Hammond, who is believed to have earned a fortune of £37m, is the majority shareholder in The Smallest Cog.
