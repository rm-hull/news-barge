Colom leads Republican incumbent by 1,800 votes in nail-biting US Senate race in Mississippi

Democrat Scott Colom, a district attorney, is leading Republican incumbentCindy Hyde-Smith by a little more than 1,800 votes in the shockingly competitive US Senate race in Mississippi.

As of now 96% of the vote has been counted, but the Associated Press has yet to call the contest. Mississippi law that allows mail-in ballots to be counted as long as they are postmarked by, and received within five business days of the election. This means we might not get the final result until early next week.

If Colom wins, it would be an astonishing defeat of a senator who is serving her first full-term, in a state where a Democrat hasn’t won a US Senate seat since 1982. Colom would also be the first Black senator from Mississippi since the Reconstruction Era, after the Civil War. Trump won the Magnolia state by 22 points in 2024.

Democrat Scott Weiner has been elected to represent California’s 11th Congressional District.

The Associated Press called the race at 11:25am ET this morning. He will fill the seat held by Representative Nancy Pelosi, who is retiring from Congress at the end of the year.

Lee Zeldin, the administrator of the US Environmental Protection Agency, has come out in support of Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, amid threats of impeachment from some congressional lawmakers.

“Pete Hegseth is a ROCK SOLID patriot” Zeldin said on Friday morning. Hegseth, he claimee, has “selflessly lived his life willing to sacrifice everything in defense of the rest of us.”

“Our nation is made stronger by Pete’s continued service” his statement added.

It comes as Hegseth appears likely to face a congressional impeachment effort in the coming weeks following the midterm elections, as lawmakers on both sides of the aisle have increasingly criticized his leadership decisions, including his decision to purge top generals, and his handling of the Iran war.

In a post on social media this week, Republican Representative Thomas Massiesaid: “There is one contingency in which I won’t reintroduce the articles of impeachment: Hegseth resigns.”

On Thursday, Trump defended Hegseth and said that he was doing a “fantastic job”. Hegseth also released a video this week dismissing the impeachment effort.

Another Trump loyalist resigns from justice department as Democrats prepare oversight

Jason Reding Quiñones, a fierce ally of Donald Trump and the US attorney for the Southern District of Florida, has resigned from the justice department.

He announced his departure on social media, thanking Donald Trump for his “trust”. The outgoing prosecutor notably launched an investigation into intelligence officials who launched an inquiry into how Russia boosted Trump in the 2016 presidential election. This despite the fact that ex-FBI and intelligence officials were investigated and effectively exonerated by two special counsels and a Republican-led Senate panel.

Quiñones’ departure comes just a day after Todd Blanche, the attorney general, announced that Harmeet Dhillon, the justice department lawyer who led the Civil Rights division, had resigned. Dhillon was the first high-ranking justice department official to leave after the midterm elections in which Democrats took control of Congress. Democrats are expected to use that power to aggressively investigate executive agencies, including the justice department.

Federal judge temporarily halts building work on Trump’s triumphal arch

Michael Sainato

A federal judge in the early hours of Friday blocked any construction work from beginning on Donald Trump’s towering triumphal arch for 14 days, a major setback for one of the president’s most prominent projects in his vision to remake the nation’s capital.

The temporary restraining order was issued by US district judge Tanya Chutkan in a Washington DC court that has regularly proved to be a thorn in the US president’s side, such as in previous cases involving election subversion and the January 6 insurrection by Trump supporters.

The new ruling followed a series of steps taken on Thursday by the government, including the final approval of the 250ft-high (76 meter) arch by the National Capital Planning Commission in an 8-4 vote after a nine-hour hearing where dozens of witnesses opposed the construction.

The arch is one of several projects around Washington that Trump has undertaken as he remakes the city, from renovating one of its storied public golf courses to threatening to demolish the John F Kennedy Center for the Performing Arts. Those projects could soon face greater scrutiny after the Democrats gained control of Congress.

Flávio Bolsonaro, the far-right politician who lost Brazil’s presidential election last month, has insisted his defeat is not “the end for Brazil” as he battles to maintain political relevance after fleeing to the US.

Bolsonaro absconded early last week shortly after losing the 25 October election to the leftwing incumbent Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva by more than 4m votes.

On Thursday night Bolsonaro made his first major public appearance since arriving in the US, speaking at a rightwing gathering at Donald Trump’s Mar-a-Lago property where he claimed “great things” were still to come.

“I know this wasn’t the end for Brazil and I know it wasn’t the end for America,” Bolsonaro, 45, told a gala dinner at the Conservative Political Action Conference (CPAC)’s four-day “gathering of visionaries” event, where the former British prime minister Liz Truss is also due to speak.

“Even when what seems like the end isn’t what we’d hoped for, we must thank God and have even more faith that this isn’t yet the end,” he added. “No one shuts the door that he opens – and the door he shuts, no one opens.”

Earlier, in his first post-election interview, Bolsonaro made a series of unsubstantiated claims about election fraud and insisted he received “help from absolutely no one” to flee.

“It was all me,” Bolsonaro told the New York Times, contradicting reports that he was helped by the Trump adviser Jason Miller and Santiago Caputo, a close aide to Argentina’s rightwing president, Javier Milei.

He said he would “take to the grave” the full story of how he had fled Brazil, where he faces federal police investigations for money laundering and corruption. Bolsonaro is a member of congress who has held elected office for more than 20 years but he will lose parliamentary immunity early next year when his term ends, meaning he could then be prosecuted.

European stock markets have fallen sharply as a rout in US tech stocks spread across the Atlantic, after the Democrats’ midterms victory sparked fears of a slowdown in the AI industry.

A sell-off on Wall Street on Thursday was followed by sharp falls in Asian stocks markets on Friday morning. The investor sentiment then reached London, where the market is poised for its worst day since March.

Asian markets fell sharply on Friday morning. Photograph: Ritchie B Tongo/EPA

The FTSE 100 index, which tracks blue-chip shares listed in London, was down 207 points, or 1.9%, at 10,575 points in mid-morning trading.

Germany’s Dax index fell by 1.9% in morning trading, France’s Cac 40 dropped by 1.5% and Spain’s Ibex lost 1.1%.

Susannah Streeter, the chief investment strategist at Wealth Club, said: “The tech sell-off has intensified, with the pain of the brutal sell-off on Wall Street carried straight through to Asia and knocking sentiment in Europe.

“Big worries are colliding – the build-out of datacentres looks set to slow, which risks shredding the spending spree underpinning the boom, right at the time that investors have become super nervous about returns on this massive investment.”

Tuesday night’s explosive rout of the Republican party was supercharged by the fury of Hispanic Americans who turned out in record numbers in key battlegrounds to protest the Trump administration’s anti-immigrant assault on their communities.

“Trump went against us,” said Benny Melendez from Weslaco, Texas. “He made us the target of ICE operations, so we decided to punish him by voting against his party. That’s what happened.”

Melendez, the owner of a small housebuilding company in south Texas, voted for Donald Trump in the presidential election two years ago, attracted by his promise to secure the Mexican border and improve the economy. But on Tuesday, he cast his vote for the Democrats in a display of pure anger.

Attendees listen to speakers at the Voto Latino rally in Weslaco, Texas, for Democratic candidates Gina Hinojosa for governor, James Talarico for the US Senate, and Bobby Pulido for the US House on 25 October ahead of the midterm elections. Photograph: Michael Gonzalez/AP

Two of his cousins and up to 15 of his building workers have been picked up and deported to Mexico by ICE. When Melendez voted for Trump in 2024 he expected the immigration raids would focus on those with criminal records, he said, not his relatives and employees.

“They’re taking our neighbors. They’re taking our friends. They’re they’re taking our workers, and they’re taking our family members. This is an attack against our community.”

Melendez’s rage, replicated in the millions across Texas, Florida and other critical swing states, itself tells a story. It speaks to how Trump first wooed, and then alienated, the backing of many Latino voters, effectively shooting himself in the foot.

Welcome to our live coverage of the US midterm elections – here are some of the latest key developments.

A number of closely watched contests in the midterms are still to be called, including the final two US Senate races – in Mississippi and Alaska. The Democratic candidates are hoping to oust Republican incumbents to bolster their new majority in Congress. Right now, Democrats have a 106-seat majority in Congress as we await final results.

Democrats flipped another eight House seats, strengthening their majority in the lower chamber. Democrats are now leading Republicans 253 to 157, with 25 races left to call, as a number of GOP representatives felt the weight of the blue wave that has swept their party out of power. Republicans held on to two House seats in the west as congressman Mike Kennedy won re-election in Utah and Amanda McKinney won in Washington.

Democratic socialist Nithya Raman was elected to serve as the mayor of Los Angeles, eking out a narrow win over incumbent Karen Bass, in a major shake-up in local politics that reflects the growing dissatisfaction with the direction of the city. The progressive urban planner had served on LA’s city council since 2020.

Los Angeles mayor-elect Nithya Raman (centre). Photograph: Evelyn Hockstein/Reuters
