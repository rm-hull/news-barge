A Barnard Castle pub said to be among the UK's "most haunted" has captured attention online thanks to tales of a ghostly child, a praying monk and a shadowy figure said to appear in guests' rooms.

The Old Well Inn on The Bank has earned a reputation as one of Teesdale's most intriguing buildings, with paranormal stories stretching back years and continuing to fascinate visitors today.

Guests have reportedly experienced unexplained disturbances in one of the inn's bedrooms, where the spirit of a young boy is said to linger.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21675569.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Historic photograph of the Old Well Inn in Barnard Castle, a pub renowned for its ghost stories, hidden well and centuries of history. *(Image: NORTHERN ECHO)*

Others have described waking during the night to find a dark shadowy figure standing nearby or climbing onto the bed.

Down in the cellar, the stories continue.

Some visitors claim to have spotted a ghostly monk beneath the building, while others have reported cold spots, strange shadows and an overwhelming feeling they are not alone.

Whether fact, folklore or imagination, the accounts have helped cement the inn's reputation as one of the region's most talked-about "haunted" locations.

Yet the pub's real history is no less remarkable.

Long before it became the Old Well Inn, the building traded as The Ship Hotel, despite being hundreds of miles from the nearest coastline.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21675339.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Historic image of the Old Well Inn in Barnard Castle, showing the building and its former frontage in the early 20th century. *(Image: NORTHERN ECHO)*

Later, following the arrival of the railway in Barnard Castle during the 1850s, it became known as the Railway Hotel.

It was during its days as The Ship that one of Teesdale's most shocking unsolved crimes unfolded.

On New Year's Eve 1812, 18-year-old housemaid Hannah Latham spent the evening at the inn before setting off through the snow towards Brignall, where she worked.

She never arrived.

The following morning her body was discovered near Startforth with her throat cut.

Despite an investigation led by local magistrate John Bacon Sawrey Morritt of Rokeby Hall, nobody was ever charged.

More than 200 years later, the murder remains unsolved.

But the building's greatest mystery may lie underground.

Divers explored it in 1969 and again in 2018, descending around 25ft into the darkness without reaching the bottom.

The cave-like chamber remains one of Barnard Castle's most unusual hidden features and has only added to speculation surrounding the building.

Whether visitors believe in ghosts or not, the Old Well Inn remains a place where local legend, unsolved history and underground secrets combine to create one of County Durham's most intriguing pubs.
