Democrats continue to add to their blue tsunami as President Donald Trump — correctly — pointed the finger at them for a stock market plunge.

“The Stock Market suffered one of the Worst Days in History based on the results of the Election. Such a sad thing to watch!” Trump wrote on Truth Social.

Several Democrats were projected to win House races, including Luz Rivas in California, Sam Forstag in Montana and Shannon Taylor in Virginia.

These wins bring the total number of Democrats projected to win House seats to 247, compared with 147 Republicans. In the Senate, the current tally is 54 Democrats and 44 Republicans. Democrats will control both chambers when the new Congress convenes in January.

The results come as a civil war is brewing inside the Trump administration, with insiders scrambling to assign blame.

Trump has reportedly called advisors asking whether Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth should be axed. Other key players caught in the turmoil include Vice President JD Vance and White House Chief of Staff Susie Wiles.

Meanwhile, Secretary of State Marco Rubio is on “resignation watch,” following the GOP’s disastrous midterm performance, sources familiar with the matter told *The Independent*. The White House denied the report.

## Texas went so blue Talarico got more early votes than Paxton got in total

Ruby red Texas went so blue during the GOP’s crushing midterm defeat that Democrat James Talarico got more early votes than Republican Ken Paxton got in total.

Talarico defeated Paxton with more than 5.4 million votes in a blue tsunami that hit their state and other reliably Republican states such as Iowa and Kansas.

Paxton got 4,249,373 total votes, including 3,232,667 early votes, according to data from Texas Secretary of State Robert S. Howden’s office, last updated late Thursday afternoon.

Talarico got nearly 150,000 more votes from Texans who cast their ballots before Election Day than Paxton’s total vote tally. The Democrat got a total of 5,444,982 votes, including 4,397,474 early votes.

## DOJ on hunt for Supreme Court abortion ruling leak

The feds have not given up trying to find the identity of the person who leaked a 2022 draft of the Supreme Court ruling that overturned Roe v. Wade.

The U.S. Attorney’s Office for the District of Columbia, headed by Trump appointee Jeanine Pirro, is managing the investigation. Pirro, working with career prosecutors, assigned senior prosecutor Carlton Davis to the matter. Davis previously handled local fraud and violence prosecutions, as well as an unsuccessful effort to indict six Democratic lawmakers who had advised military personnel about refusing unlawful orders.

The Justice Department has accelerated its inquiry in recent weeks, The New York Times reported. Prosecutors have issued grand jury subpoenas for testimony and requested information from people employed by the high court when the draft opinion was disclosed.
