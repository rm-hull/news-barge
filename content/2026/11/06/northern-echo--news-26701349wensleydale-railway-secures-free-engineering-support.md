A HERITAGE railway is on track for major improvements thanks to pro bono support from a rail engineering company.

Wensleydale Railway, a volunteer-led heritage line, has received free track design services from Essex-based Track Engineering Construction Services (TECS) Ltd.

Three TECS staff visited the railway between October 12 and 14 to survey Leeming Bar yard and the Bedale station curve.

John Seaton, land surveyor at TECS, said: "It was a great place to work and interesting to learn more about the Wensleydale Railway."

The company has offered to complete the necessary track design work for the Leeming Bar yard remodelling and the Bedale station track relay.

Mat Powroznyk, senior track design engineer at TECS, said: "I'll be assisting with the designs for a reconfigured layout at Leeming Bar yard and a renewal of the track through Bedale station, where a sharp curve alongside the platform makes for an interesting challenge.

"After 15 years as a track designer, it's genuinely rewarding to put that experience towards a heritage line that depends on people giving their time and skills.

"It's a reminder of why I enjoy this profession."

Wensleydale Railway, which operates heritage diesel services between Scruton and Leyburn, is one of the longest heritage lines in the UK at 22 miles.

The improvements will support its goal of restoring passenger services to Redmire by 2028.

Jon Trueman, industry partner manager at Wensleydale Railway, said: "This collaboration between the Wensleydale Railway and TECS Ltd will support vital projects to improve safety and efficiency at our Leeming Bar engineering base, and to ensure that the tightly-curved platform interface at Bedale remains resilient and fit to use.

"We're very grateful to TECS Ltd for their support."

The five-mile section between Leyburn and Redmire has been closed to passenger trains since 2020 due to deteriorating track and infrastructure.
