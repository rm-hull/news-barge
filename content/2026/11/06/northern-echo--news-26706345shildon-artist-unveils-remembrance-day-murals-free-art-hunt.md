A County Durham artist who transforms vandalised walls into colourful community artwork is turning his attention to Remembrance Day with new murals and a free art hunt for residents.

Craig Comerford, known as I Am Risk, started painting murals in Shildon after spotting what he called “unsavoury graffiti”.

His aim was to cover abusive writing and make people in his community smile, and quickly became a popular figure, with residents searching for his artwork across Shildon.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21680075.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Craig Comerford's mural on Hancock & Ainsley pharmacy, Shildon Main Street. *(Image: Craig Comerford)*

Mr Comerford also began giving away free artwork, which drew further attention from residents. In October, he hid magpie paintings around the town, advertising on social media that Shildon residents could take home whichever ones they found.

Mr Comerford has now announced another art giveaway alongside two new murals in honour of Remembrance Day.

His murals depict the local War Memorials, the first on Hancock & Ainsley pharmacy on Shildon's Main Street, the second near New Shildon War Memorial on Redworth Road.

The Remembrance Day murals drew praise from Shildon residents, with one describing it as "absolutely class," and another writing: "I love this. You're so talented."

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21681228.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Comerford's new mural on Redworth Road, Shildon. *(Image: Craig Comerford)*

Mr Comerford has permission for every wall he paints, helping him avoid disputes with businesses. He added that while other artists use free work to promote sales, he wanted to use his murals and giveaways to connect with the community.

Tomorrow (Saturday, November 7), Mr Comerford will be hiding six paintings, two on canvas and four on cardboard, around Shildon, before posting clues and pictures to encourage residents to look for them.

He asks that if residents like the art, they take it home, otherwise they should leave the paintings for others to find.

Residents reacted positively to the announcement on social media, describing the art hunt as “lovely and thoughtful” and “brilliant."

What began as an effort to brighten up Shildon has transformed Mr Comerford into a popular local artist, with residents keen on his regular town-wide treasure hunts.

With new Remembrance murals unveiled and another free art hunt planned, residents will once again be keeping their eyes peeled for the latest creations from one of the town's most recognisable artists.

More information, and updates about Craig Comerford's artwork, can be found on his Facebook page, I Am Risk.
