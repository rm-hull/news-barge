Newbattle Golf Club is launching an appeal to around 130 other golf clubs for support as it attempts to buy the land on which its course sits. The aim is to raise up to £40,000 towards professional and legal costs, with other clubs being asked to donate four-balls or similar prizes for a silent auction that is expected to be held before the end of the year.

The Midlothian club has already made an informal offer below the current £700,000 asking price, at a level it believes it can afford without overstretching its finances, and is awaiting responses from two prospective lenders. No offer has yet been accepted and there is no guarantee a purchase will ultimately proceed.

Captain Andy Pratt, a Newbattle member for 25 years, joined the club's management council after retiring in 2020 because he wanted to “give something back”. After spells as handicap convener and vice-captain, he is now helping to lead the attempt to secure the club’s future.

![Around the Greens logo](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/18712832.jpg?type=mds-article-620) *(Image: Damian Shields)*

**Newbattle has leased its course for generations. Why are you now trying to buy it?**

We have leased the land from the same family, the Marquesses of Lothian, for about 130 years, essentially since the club was founded. Last year, the then current Marquess died and the estate passed to his brother, who decided to sell parcels of land, one of which was Newbattle Golf Course.

We decided we would like to buy the course to secure the club’s future and avoid paying rent. I was also unsettled by the prospect of having a new landlord, because we would have no idea what they might want to do in the future.

**Is the club in immediate danger if somebody else buys the land?**

No. We are protected by our existing lease, which runs until around September or October 2038. That gives us another dozen years or so, but beyond that we would be in the unknown.

We have been working hard over the past year to establish whether buying the land is viable. We have amended our constitution and worked with the local council to make sure the land was not earmarked for development.

![SANYO DIGITAL CAMERA](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21676234.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The parkland course at Newbattle Golf Club, which the club is seeking to buy to secure its long-term future *(Image: Newbattle Golf Club)*

**The course was originally on the market for £1.1 million. Where are negotiations now?**

We thought that was obviously too expensive and didn't represent the value of the course. Anyone buying it would only receive our rent, which is £30,000 a year plus VAT, so it would have been a very poor return on an investment of about £1.3m.

The price came down to £700,000 and we have been talking to the selling agents about a price that will work for both parties.

**Why are you turning to other golf clubs for support?**

The aim is to cover our professional fees. Buying land is not cheap, and we do not have a large amount of money sitting in the accounts. The purchase could involve unexpected costs of perhaps £30,000 or £40,000, although we do not yet know the exact figure because we do not know the final sale price.

We are asking other golf clubs for help because that is what we would do for another club. There is a sense of camaraderie among golf clubs in Scotland.

**How close are you to knowing whether a deal is possible?**

Our funders are considering our request right now. Once we have written confirmation of an agreed line of credit, we can follow up our informal approach with a formal offer.

![Newbattle Golf Club](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21676249.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Newbattle Golf Club’s parkland course, with the clubhouse visible beyond the fairway *(Image: Newbattle Golf Club)*

**What would owning the course actually change for Newbattle?**

Obviously, if we take out a mortgage, we will have repayments. But once the mortgage period ends, we would have neither mortgage payments nor rent. That would free up cash flow for improvements to the golf course.

For example, we would love to install an irrigation system, but we cannot currently afford it. Our fees are set at a competitive level compared with similar clubs, and after rent, staff costs and other outgoings there is not currently sufficient money left for us to contemplate capital-intensive projects.

**So what is your ideal long-term outcome?**

Once the mortgage is paid off, we would no longer have that £3,000-a-month rent. That money could be invested directly in the clubhouse, car park or course, improving the facilities for members and visitors.

It would be great to refurbish the clubhouse, which was built around 1935 or 1936 and needs some attention, including work on the roof and new windows. We would also be able to invest in our course to make sure it is as good as it can be.
