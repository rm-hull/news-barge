Claims York households will face big council tax rises due to a £32.3m financial black hole have been branded scaremongering ahead of talks with the Government to resolve the issue.

York Council's executive has backed starting talks with ministers about options it has to deal with the budget gap forecast to face the authority by 2030.

Cllr Katie Lomas, the authority's Labour finance spokesperson, said York's lower than average council tax rates had put them in a difficult position but speculation about future increases was unhelpful.

Liberal Democrat opposition leader Cllr Nigel Ayre said Government assistance to avoid effective bankruptcy would not come without large tax hikes which his group estimated could be around £240.

It follows warnings over the council's finances as work gets underway on the authority's budget for 2027/8 which is set to be decided in February.

A report to the executive stated the council was set to see some of its most significant financial challenges to date and addressing them would be extremely difficult for residents.

It added a combination of rising costs, below average local council tax rates and national funding reforms set to make York the lowest-funded local authority per head were to blame.

Officials stated budgets could either be balanced through cuts which could see some services stop entirely or support such as Government loans could be sought to buy time for changes.

The executive's decision on Tuesday, November 3 authorised council officials to approach the Government to begin discussions about the options which are available to the authority.

Council finance lead Debbie Mitchell told Tuesday's meeting enhanced cost control measures were already in place across the authority as work continues to manage pressures.

The chief finance officer added work included trying to make the council as efficient as possible, looking at generating income, reviewing spending on major projects and seeking external funding.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/19773943.jpg?type=mds-article-620)City of York Council officials are set for talks with the Government about the authority's finances *(Image: Newsquest)*

Labour Executive Member Cllr Lomas said they were working to try and avoid short-term solutions to the problem while trying to balance delivering services with traversing a difficult financial landscape.

She added Liberal Democrats were indulging in fantasy maths when claiming that Band D homes faced £240 increases in yearly council tax bills.

The executive member for finance said: "I'd urge residents not to buy into any scaremongering but listen to what we're doing, they are trying to understand - will they be spending £240 on council tax?

"Absolutely not, it's meaningless nonsense.

"In 2023 we inherited a council in crisis and approaches to budgets were all about short-termism and winning elections, I make no apologies for not just focusing on the here and now but doing the right thing for generations to come.

"We're facing a really tricky budget this year and I think it would be disingenuous to go to the public with a range of options because we're not in that situation, we're having to make difficult decisions."

Liberal Democrat leader Cllr Ayre said Labour needed to put the city before its own political future and not put off difficult financial choices until after next May's local elections.

The opposition leader added honesty was needed about what talks with the Government and potential Exceptional Financial Support (EFS) would entail for future budgets.

EFS refers to temporary measures the Government allows councils to take and support to avoid issuing a Section 114 notice, the mechanism authorities use to effectively declare bankruptcy.

Cllr Ayre said: "Labour both nationally and locally have effectively bankrupted the city, York will need EFS to avoid a Section 114 notice.

"We need EFS and it needs it now, saving £32m in two years is simply not possible and EFS doesn't come without big council tax increases, promising no increases above five per cent next year is disingenuous.

"You may or may not be able to fudge the budget with loans but the quid pro quo for that will be council tax increases in future years.

"I've asked you to start a conversion with residents for months and instead you have delayed, obfuscated, buried the reality of this and totally lost control of the budget."
