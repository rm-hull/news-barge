# DWP new standard Pension Credit rate confirmed with minimum amount guaranteed

## The Department for Work and Pensions (DWP) has confirmed an increase for Pension Credit claimants

The Department for Work and Pensions (DWP) has announced an uplift for Pension Credit recipients - bringing weekly payments to £247 from April. The confirmation from the DWP and the Labour Party government follows the Autumn Budget from John Healey.

DWP minister Pat McFadden revealed: "The standard minimum guarantee in Pension Credit will also increase by 3.9% in line with the increase in average weekly earnings. From April, it will be £247.30 a week for a single pensioner and £377.40 a week for a couple, ensuring the incomes of the poorest pensioners are protected."

Pension Credit provides additional financial support to help with living expenses if you're above State Pension age and receiving a modest income. The benefit can also assist with accommodation costs including ground rent or service charges, reports Birmingham Live.

Additional support may be available if you're a carer, have a severe disability, or care for a child or young person. Pension Credit operates independently from your State Pension. You may qualify for Pension Credit regardless of whether you have alternative income streams, savings or are a homeowner.

Eligibility applies if both you and your partner have reached State Pension age, or alternatively if one of you receives Housing Benefit for those over State Pension age. When submitting an application for Pension Credit, your income undergoes assessment.

For those with a partner, incomes are evaluated jointly. Income encompasses your DWP State Pension, additional pensions, wages from employment and self-employment, and the majority of social security benefits, such as Carer's Allowance.

However, not all benefits count towards your income calculation. For instance, the following are excluded: Adult Disability Payment, Attendance Allowance, Child Benefit and Christmas Bonus, alongside Council Tax Reduction and Disability Living Allowance.

Further exemptions include Housing Benefit, Pension Age Disability Payment, Personal Independence Payment, Scottish Adult Disability Living Allowance and social fund payments such as Winter Fuel Payment.
