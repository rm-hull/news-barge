A rogue trader who took more than £70,000 from customers for work he never carried out has been put behind bars for two years.

Durham Crown Court heard Kye Ranson would visit customers’ properties and provide quotes for work before asking for a deposit, usually amounting to 50 per cent of the quoted price.

However, after customers handed over the money, the work was not carried out.

Ranson instead gave a series of excuses, including bad weather, problems with his car or supplies, family emergencies and fitters not being available.

The court heard Ranson was the sole trader of KR Creative Design Solutions, with Durham County Council becoming aware of reports of unfair business practices involving him and the firm from August 2025.

When Ranson did return to customers’ homes, the court was told he would delay matters further.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21681749.jpg?type=mds-article-620)An image Ranson sent to a customer of work he planned to carry out. *(Image: Durham County Council.)*

Customers who asked for their money back were not refunded, with Ranson suggesting he had already paid for bespoke materials which could not be refunded.

In total, customers lost £70,202, with the highest single loss standing at £24,525. Some were able to recover money through their banks.

The 43-year-old, of Meadow Road in Lemington, Newcastle, took payments from eight different customers before being taken to court by Durham County Council.

He pleaded guilty under the Fraud Act 2006 to one offence of knowingly participating in a fraudulent business between May 2024 and June 2025.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21681932.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Durham Crown Court *(Image: THE NORTHERN ECHO)*

Victim impact statements described the effect his actions had on customers.

One said: “This has had an adverse effect on my mental health. We had saved so hard to get the money together and I felt so low. I was on long term sick for over three months.

“Luckily, I have a supportive workplace. Kye knew this and I felt he played on it. I felt broken.

“I moved out due to having no kitchen and the living environment. I was upset that he had pulled the wool over my eyes.

“I felt like we had a good relationship with Kye, and I trusted him. It put me in a bad state of mind, and I got obsessed with checking my emails and messages.”

Another customer said: “The situation turned our world upside down and it was difficult living in the environment and waiting for deliveries to come which didn't arrive.

“It has made me feel sad, depressed and angry. He made us feel sorry for him and we trusted him, we saw no red flags.”

A third added: “It has been a bad experience, I feel a lack of trust in tradesmen, and I feel we’ve been living a substandard way of life due to this matter.”

In mitigation, the court heard Ranson was suffering with his mental health at the time following separation from his wife.

The judge was also asked to take into account his good character and genuine remorse.

Sentencing Ranson to two years in prison, the judge said he had “strung them along with excuse after excuse with no intention of doing the work.”

Owen Cleugh, Durham County Council’s acting head of community protection, said: “Mr Ranson took thousands of pounds from people who trusted him to carry out work which they had agreed with him.
