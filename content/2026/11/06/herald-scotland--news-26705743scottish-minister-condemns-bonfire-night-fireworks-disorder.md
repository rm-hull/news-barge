A Scottish Government minister has condemned fireworks-related disorder after emergency workers were attacked and a police vehicle was set alight during Bonfire Night.

Seven people were arrested following incidents across Scotland on Thursday evening, with a police officer and ambulance worker suffering minor injuries in Glasgow.

Education Secretary Mairi McAllan said attacks on emergency services and members of the public were "completely unacceptable".

Speaking to BBC Radio Scotland on Friday morning, she said police and emergency services had been well prepared and that most fireworks displays had passed without problems.

Speaking on the radio this morning, the minister said: “Police Scotland were well prepared. Our emergency services were well prepared. The vast majority of displays went ahead without any problems, and that’s to be welcomed.

“But nonetheless, I would want to be absolutely unequivocal. Totally unacceptable that any disorder related to fireworks should be ongoing.”

Her comments come after Community Safety Minister Kirsten Oswald faced questions at Holyrood on Thursday about further restrictions on fireworks.

Oswald said ministers were continuing to examine provisions of legislation passed in 2022, following questions from Green MSP Holly Bruce about the introduction of licensing requirements.

Police Scotland said it received 1,024 fireworks-related calls between Halloween and Bonfire Night, compared with 833 last year, although fewer incidents required a specialist public-order response.

Police and an ambulance were responding to a report of an injured man in Greenside Street when one police officer and an ambulance staff member sustained minor injuries after both crews were attacked with fireworks and a police vehicle was set alight and seriously damaged.

A 16-year-old boy was arrested, police said.

Firefighters were also forced to withdraw as they faced anti-social behaviour while attending to bin fires in the south of Edinburgh, while police were targeted with fireworks in other areas of the city.

Fireworks were also thrown at members of the public in Clydebank and there were attacks on fire crews in the Knightswood and Castlemilk areas of Glasgow, Cumnock in Ayrshire, Larkhall in South Lanarkshire, and Blackridge, West Lothian.

The incidents came amid a large police operation to deal with disorder on Bonfire Night, known as Operation Amberlight.

Police Scotland said officers and ambulance crews were called to reports of an injured male on Greenside Street in Glasgow shortly before 8pm.

A spokesperson for the force said: “During this time both crews were targeted with fireworks resulting in a police vehicle being set alight and seriously damaged. One of the ambulance staff sustained a minor injury.

“Operation Amberlight specialist resources were deployed, and one male has been arrested.

“Inquiries are ongoing and police maintain a presence in the area.”
