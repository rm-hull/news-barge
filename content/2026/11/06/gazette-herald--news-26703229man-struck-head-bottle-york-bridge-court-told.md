A man was struck on the head with a glass bottle on a York bridge in broad daylight, a court has heard.

Ouse Bridge was busy with pedestrians when David Shaffi launched an attack on the man on April 25, 2025, prosecutor Beth Richardson said.

Ms Richardson said Shaffi, 40, held a bottle to the man’s head before striking him with it.

She said Shaffi then grabbed the man and hit him again on the head followed by a third blow to the stomach. He proceeded to throw the bottle in his direction.

An acquaintance tried to intervene before the attack but was pushed to the floor, York Crown Court was told.

The court heard the victim was left unconscious and was taken to hospital with cuts to his head, swelling to his ear and grazes to his hip.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21676125.jpg?type=mds-article-620)David Shaffi, left, during attack on Ouse Bridge in York on April 25, 2025 *(Image: YappApp)*

Shaffi was arrested by police in a pub in Micklegate later that afternoon.

He told police he carried out the assault in response to the man attacking him the previous night, the court heard.

Shaffi, of Station Road, Castleford, was later charged and pleaded guilty to Section 20 wounding.

For Shaffi, Rhianydd Clements said he struggled with a dependency on alcohol and had been drinking before the attack.

The court heard Shaffi has 28 previous convictions for 59 offences including domestic abuse.

Ms Clements said alcohol misuse underpinned Shaffi’s offending.

Turning to the attack on Ouse Bridge, the defence barrister said: “He was struggling to control his emotions after a dispute with the complainant.”

She said Shaffi had expressed remorse and regret for his actions and had made efforts to contact a detox premises to stop drinking.

Ms Clements said Shaffi, who also has depression and anxiety, had been clean from drugs and alcohol in the weeks prior to his sentencing hearing on Thursday (November 5).

She said Shaffi is “very keen” to engage with alcohol treatment services.

“He wants to get his life back on track,” Ms Clements said, adding: “He wants to change his life, in his words, while he still has time ... He doesn’t want to be an embarrassment to his family anymore.”

She said Shaffi is planning to get a job as a labourer.

“He is seeing this as a turning point in his life,” Ms Clements said.

## 'It’s time this type of offending stopped' - judge

Judge Simon Hickey said the incident on April 25 resulted in Shaffi’s most serious offence for violence to date.

But he said any jail sentence would likely be short, given current laws requiring offenders to serve 40 per cent of their sentence before being eligible for release.

He said it would be more effective to address the alcohol dependency, adding that it was “clear the background is alcohol” to Shaffi’s offending.

The judge jailed Shaffi for 18 months suspended for two years.

He also ordered that Shaffi completes an offending behaviour course.

“I’ll take a chance with you, Mr Shaffi ... You’re 40 – it’s time this type of offending stopped,” Judge Hickey said before passing sentence.

“The ball's in your court,” the judge told Shaffi as he was released from the dock.
