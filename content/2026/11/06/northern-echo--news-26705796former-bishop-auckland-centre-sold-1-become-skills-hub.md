A former community centre in Bishop Auckland is set for a new lease of life after being sold for just £1 and earmarked for transformation into an employment and skills hub.

Social enterprise Bridge Creative has bought the former Henknowle Community Centre and unveiled plans to create a 5,000 sq ft facility supporting people with learning disabilities, autistic people and the wider community.

Called Bridgeworks, the centre will sit opposite Bridge Creative's existing base, The COSE, offering vocational training, work experience and community activities under one roof.

Closed for more than a year, the building will become a hub for practical skills, qualifications and routes into paid work.

Training will cover catering and hospitality, creative production, events, logistics, warehousing, facilities management and digital skills.

The project will help young people with learning disabilities and autistic young people move from education into work.

Ben Tinkler, founder and managing director of Bridge Creative, said: "It's fantastic to be bringing this community building back into use, and we're really excited about what we can create here, not just for the people we support, but for the whole local community.

"There's such a huge need locally for these kinds of opportunities, particularly for young people with a learning disability and autistic young people who often face significant barriers when leaving education and moving into employment, but also for the local community who have been without a community centre for over a year now.

"We want Bridgeworks to provide that nurturing environment where people can explore their interests, develop practical skills, build confidence and gain real experience of work, with the right support around them as they take those next steps.

"But it's also about changing mindsets. We want to open doors to a much wider range of careers and demonstrate what people can achieve when they're given the right opportunities."

The site will include a commercial training kitchen and community café, giving people hands-on experience while creating a social space for residents.

Bridge Creative will expand its design-and-build workshop into the building and hope to take on larger commissions for events, festivals and cultural projects.

The site will offer space and services to businesses, creating jobs and work experience opportunities.

Community groups and organisations will be able to use the facility for activities and events.

The organisation has released concept images for the site, with refurbishment due to take place in stages throughout 2027.

Trades4Care CIC will help to paint and decorate the building, giving young adults practical experience in the trades while restoring the former community centre.

Bridge Creative plans an open day as work progresses and is inviting local businesses, employers, colleges and community organisations to help shape Bridgeworks.

Bridge Creative also works with Billy's Bus, the popular doggy daycare transport service across County Durham.
