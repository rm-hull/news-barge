THE music of Howard Goodall is known to millions from beloved TV shows such as Blackadder, The Vicar of Dibley and Red Dwarf.

His compositions have been part of British television life for decades. But next week audiences will have the opportunity to discover a very different side of his music when Bradford Festival Choral Society performs his deeply moving Eternal Light: A Requiem.

In a special video message the acclaimed composer has recorded ahead of the special concert, Goodall pays a warm tribute to Bradford’s oldest choir as it celebrates 170 years of music-making in the city.

The historic choir will perform Eternal Light: A Requiem at Bradford Grammar School on Saturday, November 14.

Founded in 1856, BFCS is one of the country’s longest-established choral societies. But rather than marking its anniversary with music from the past, the choir has chosen a programme featuring three living British composers.

Goodall was particularly impressed by that decision.

“I’m also very touched and impressed that though you’re 170 years old, you are doing three pieces by living composers at this concert,” he said.

He described the move as a “wonderfully forward-looking, youthful approach to what a choral society may be in the 21st century,” adding: “And I applaud and pay tribute to you for that.”

In his message, Goodall also praised the Society’s commitment to artistic excellence and contribution to the community, reflecting on the extraordinary power of singing together.

He spoke about the rewards of learning challenging new music and the sense of achievement that comes from being part of a choir.

The composer also revealed that Eternal Light, first performed in 2008, has now had more than 800 live performances around the world.

Written as a work of consolation for the living, rather than a traditional Requiem for the dead, it has developed a particularly personal significance for audiences experiencing grief and loss.

Instead of focusing solely on prayers for the dead, Goodall conceived Eternal Light as a work of comfort for the living - combining the traditional Latin Requiem with English poetry in music of warmth and humanity.

Goodall reflected on the many messages he has received from people describing what the music means to them, observing that every performer and audience member takes something different from a live performance.

Taking place just days after Remembrance Sunday and Armistice Day, the Bradford concert explores themes of loss, remembrance, consolation, peace and hope.

Alongside Eternal Light, the programme includes Cecilia McDowall’s Ave maris stella, dedicated to peace and originally composed for an Armistice Day performance, and Tarik O’Regan’s Triptych, which brings a different musical perspective to themes of memory, grief and hope.

The Choral Society will be joined by professional soloists and strings from the Yorkshire Symphony Orchestra, conducted by Musical Director Thomas Leech.

The concert forms part of Bradford Festival Choral Society’s 170th anniversary season. The choir is celebrating its long place in Bradford’s cultural life while continuing to introduce audiences to music written for the present time.

Thomas Leech says that, despite the choir’s long history, the evening is very much about looking forward - and welcoming people who may never have experienced a live choral concert before.

“One of the wonderful things about Howard Goodall’s music is how directly it communicates,” says Thomas.

“Eternal Light is full of warmth and humanity, and although it deals with loss and remembrance, it’s ultimately a work of consolation and hope.

“With around 130 voices and live orchestral strings, there will be moments of real power, but also beautiful stillness.

“We would love people who haven’t experienced a live choral concert before to come and discover just how moving it can be.”

\* The concert takes place at Price Hall, Bradford Grammar School, on Saturday, November 14 at 7.30pm.

Tickets cost £20 standard, £18 for over-60s and £5 for students, under-30s and people on low incomes. Under-18s can attend free with an accompanying adult.
