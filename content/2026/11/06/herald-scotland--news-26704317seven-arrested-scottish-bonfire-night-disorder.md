Seven people were arrested amid Bonfire Night disorder which saw emergency services targeted with fireworks and a police vehicle set alight in Glasgow.

Police and an ambulance were responding to a report of an injured man on the city's Greenside Street when one police officer and an ambulance staff member sustained minor injuries after both crews were attacked with fireworks.

A police vehicle was set alight and seriously damaged.

Meanwhile, firefighters were forced to withdraw as they faced anti-social behaviour while attending to bin fires in the south of Edinburgh, and police were targeted with fireworks in other areas of the city.

Scottish Fire and Rescue said that it had received more than 1,100 emergency calls during the night, and that crews faced six reported attacks.

Between 5.30pm and 11.30pm a total of 1,172 calls were made to the SFRS, and firefighters attended more than 767 incidents, including 335 bonfires.

Fireworks were also thrown at members of the public in Clydebank and there were attacks on fire crews in the Knightswood and Castlemilk areas of Glasgow, Cumnock in Ayrshire, Larkhall in South Lanarkshire, and Blackridge, West Lothian.

The incidents came amid a large police operation to deal with disorder on Bonfire Night, known as Operation Amberlight, which saw Police Scotland’s largest deployment of officers since the visit of US President Donald Trump in 2025.

Police Scotland said officers and ambulance crews were called to reports of an injured male on Greenside Street in Glasgow shortly before 8pm.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21677874.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Police Scotland deployed up to 1,700 officers as part of Operation Moonbeam *(Image: PA)*

A spokesperson for the force said: “During this time both crews were targeted with fireworks resulting in a police vehicle being set alight and seriously damaged. One of the ambulance staff sustained a minor injury.

“Operation Amberlight specialist resources were deployed, and one male has been arrested.

“Inquiries are ongoing and police maintain a presence in the area.”

In Oxgangs and around Colinton Mains in Edinburgh, bins were knocked over and set on fire in the road on Thursday evening.

There was a large police presence in the area, as trouble involving groups of young people with fireworks was reported.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21677873.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Police officers gathered for a pre-Bonfire Night briefing at Fettes Station in Edinburgh *(Image: PA)*

Locals stated that groups of youths were launching fireworks at emergency services as they attempted to deal with the disorder.

One person, who did not want to be named, told the Press Association: “They’re setting fire in the park and shooting fireworks at the police and firemen.”

A Police Scotland spokesperson said: “Around 5.30pm on Thursday, November 5 2026, we received a report of a group of youths creating a blockage in the road on Oxgangs Road North, Edinburgh.

“Officers attended and fireworks were set off in their direction, but no injuries have been reported.”

Police also went to the Kirkton area of Dundee following reports of a significant group of individuals gathering and setting illegal fires.

As part of its Bonfire Night policing operation, Police Scotland called in assistance from forces in England and Wales, as it mounted the biggest deployment of officers since the visit of Mr Trump last year.

The November 5 celebrations coincided with a major European football clash and a possible protest in Glasgow, and officers from as far afield as Avon and Somerset, Hampshire, Hertfordshire and South Wales were expected to be on the streets of Scotland.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21677902.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Police walk Besiktas fans to the ground ahead of the UEFA Europa League, league stage match at Celtic Park, Glasgow. *(Image: PA)*

Police said a “proportionate policing response” was in place for the Celtic vs Besiktas Europa League match which passed without serious incident while no resources were required in relation to the possible protest.

Chief Superintendent David Robertson said: “While we have seen a rise in the overall number of calls relating to fireworks incidents between Halloween and Bonfire Night 2026, when compared to last year, I am encouraged by the reduction in serious incidents requiring us to mobilise specialist resources during this time.

“The communities of Scotland have overwhelmingly shown their commitment to enjoying both Halloween and Bonfire Night in the proper spirit.

“Nevertheless, we have seen, yet again, an unacceptable number of incidents where the police and our emergency service colleagues have been attacked with fireworks and other projectiles. This resulted in one of our officers and a colleague from the Scottish Ambulance Service sustaining minor injuries."

Deputy Assistant Chief Officer Ian McMeekin, SFRS Head of Prevention, recognised the contribution made by communities, partners and emergency responders.

He said: "This was one of the busiest nights of the year for our firefighters and Operations Control, and I would like to thank everyone who played a part in helping to keep their communities safe.

"In the weeks leading up to Bonfire Night, we worked closely with local authorities, Police Scotland, schools, youth organisations and community groups across Scotland to raise awareness of the risks associated with fireworks, unsafe bonfires and antisocial behaviour.

"While our crews are always ready to respond when needed, we'd much rather see people stay safe and avoid needing our help in the first place."

DACO McMeekin added: "The night itself was incredibly busy. Crews attended hundreds of bonfire night related incidents as well as normal incidents such as road traffic collisions.

"Our exceptional Operations Control staff fielded hundreds of calls from the public and partners, while co-ordinating our response to dozens of ongoing incidents at a time.

"Our dedicated firefighters across the country responded to incidents in often challenging circumstances to protect communities. The support of our partners including Police Scotland was essential in ensuring we could attend incidents safely."
