The performance of secondary schools across County Durham in the latest GCSE results has been revealed in new Department for Education (DfE) figures.

The provisional figures for the 2025/26 academic year show how schools across the county performed using the Government's Attainment 8 measure, which tracks pupils' average achievement across eight GCSE qualification slots.

Teesdale School and Sixth Form emerged as the county's highest-performing mainstream secondary school, recording an Attainment 8 score of 57.0.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21344826.jpg?type=mds-article-620)A GCSE exam *(Image: Rui Vieira/PA WIRE)*

The Barnard Castle school finished narrowly ahead of St Leonard's Catholic School in Durham, which achieved 56.5, while Durham Johnston Comprehensive School completed the top three with 56.3.

The Academy at Shotton Hall, in Peterlee, was fourth with 55.0, followed by Tanfield School, which recorded 54.4.

Sedgefield Community College also featured among the county's highest-performing schools, achieving 52.4, ahead of Wolsingham School on 52.0.

Whitworth Park Academy, Easington Academy and Woodham Academy completed the top ten, with scores of 51.3, 51.0 and 50.5 respectively.

In total, 11 mainstream secondary schools in County Durham recorded Attainment 8 scores above 50, with Hermitage Academy achieving 50.1.

Elsewhere, Framwellgate School Durham recorded 48.9, Belmont Community School achieved 48.6 and Seaham High School scored 47.8.

Consett Academy achieved 47.2, while Park View School recorded 46.8.

Further down the table, North Durham Academy recorded 39.9, Ferryhill School achieved 39.7 and Bishop Barrington Academy and Durham Academy each scored 39.0.

Greenfield Academy recorded 37.7, while UTC South Durham achieved 33.4.

The figures are based on average Attainment 8 scores, with higher scores indicating stronger overall GCSE attainment across the qualifications included in the measure.

Attainment 8 takes account of pupils' results in English and mathematics, three subjects included in the English Baccalaureate, and three further approved qualifications.

The scores are not percentages or GCSE pass rates, and they do not measure pupils' progress from their starting points.

The Department for Education's provisional data also includes independent schools, special schools and further education institutions, although these have been kept separate from the main mainstream secondary school rankings because of differences in their pupil intakes and educational provision.

The figures provide a snapshot of GCSE attainment across County Durham in the 2025/26 academic year and remain subject to revision.
