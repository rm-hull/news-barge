Changes planned for City of York Council will not affect local house prices or school catchment areas, the authority’s leader has said.

Cllr Claire Douglas said the claims about the effect of ward boundary changes following an independent review were categorically untrue.

The Labour council’s leader added council wards only related to where people would vote in local elections.

It comes after the Local Government Boundary Commission for England (LGBCE) unveiled its final recommendations for the council’s wards in September.

Proposals drawn up following a review last year would see the number of councillors in York increase by one to 48 and its wards go from 21 to 23.

A new single-member Leeman Road ward would be created to cover the area where the York Central development, which includes at least 2,500 homes, is set to be built.

The changes are set to come into effect in time for York’s next local elections in May, subject to their approval by Parliament.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/cllr-claire-douglas-city-of-york-council.webp)

In her report to the council’s full meeting on Thursday, November 12, Cllr Douglas said most residents would see changes to the shape of their electoral wards and their names in some cases.

She added reviews typically took place every five to 10 years to ensure there are enough councillors to provide equal representation.

The council leader said: “Unfortunately, I have seen evidence of residents being told, falsely, that these changes will impact their house prices and school catchment areas.

“This is categorically untrue, the ward you live in is purely related to how and where you vote in local elections and designed to make sure that each person and community in our city is adequately represented.”
