Jennifer Balcarras, 48, and her 20-year-old daughter, Selah Reed, were killed near Bella Coola on British Columbia's central coast, and conservation officials are trying to catch the grizzly bear believed to be responsible.

The deaths happened only 3 to 4 kilometers (1.8 to 2.4 miles) from where a grizzly attacked a group of schoolchildren and teachers last year as they walked on a trail.

Eleven were injured, two of them critically, in the Nov. 20, 2025, attack. Jeff Tire, an inspector with the Conservation Officer Service, said that the aggressive bear was never captured, but officials were able to collect its DNA. Officials have set new traps in hopes of catching the bear involved in last weekend’s attack and learning whether it was the same bear from a year ago.

![This photo provided by Matthew Balcarras on Thursday, Nov. 5, 2026, shows Selah Reed, left, and her mother, Jenny Balcarras](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/06/05/Canada_Bear_Attack_37_95.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

They also are not sure whether more than one bear was involved. The alarm was first raised when Reed missed a routine Saturday night call with her sister.

By the next morning, no one had heard from Reed or her mother, Jenny Balcarras, and the family asked the Royal Canadian Mounted Police to check on their home in Bella Coola.

Balcarras' car was gone and the doors were locked. They found the car at a trailhead up the road, but Balcarras and Reed were nowhere to be found. Family members across Canada and the U.S. started mobilizing.

Jenny Balcarras' brother, Matt, headed north from Vancouver Island, and his brother left from Calgary. They met and drove through the night but got a call late Monday that their sister had been killed, likely by a grizzly. When they arrived at the trailhead Tuesday morning, ready to join the search for Reed, her remains had also been found.

The search was now a tragic recovery effort.

![](https://static.independent.co.uk/2025/11/21/8/19/Bella-Coola-Vancouver-Grizzly-Bear-Attack-Canada-Map.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Terry Webber, chief administrative officer for the Nuxalk Nation, said the killings were triggering for the families who were traumatized by last year’s attack. “Our community is still healing,” he said, adding that they’ve imposed a curfew and are providing mental health services. “Our priority is to ensure they’re safe and ensure our community is supported.”

The delay in finding the remains of the two women was complicated by difficult terrain, even though it was close to the trailhead, and the grizzly bear's behavior. Searchers found Jenny Balcarras first, then her daughter.

More resources and a cadaver dog helped the search, Matt Balcarras said.

Searchers found his niece's remains in a bear cache — a place where bears stash food, sometimes by digging a small hole and covering it with branches and vegetation to keep other animals and birds away. “Even though it was within 100 or so meters (330 feet), locating remains in a cache is difficult,” he said.

By the time officials located his niece, they could identify her only by her clothing, he said. His sister was in a similar condition, but they knew who she was thanks to several tiny tattoos, he said

![A grizzly bear walks through grasslands on May 23, 2026 in Yellowstone National Park, Wyoming](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/05/29/16/2277921327..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“There's no obvious body to identify,” he said. “The coroner’s investigation will include DNA and dental work. But there is no doubt that the remains that were found are theirs.”

Grizzly bears are common in the area surrounding the Bella Coola community, officials said. Conservation officials maintain a hotline where people can report problem bears, but no calls had been received recently, Tyre said during a media briefing. Balcarras, an art therapist, had moved to Bella Coola during the summer.

She had previously lived in Alaska, where her children were born, and was “the central organizing person in our family,” Matt Balcarras said. “In classic, strong single-mother style, she scraped together a career and a life for herself and her kids,” Balcarras said. She was small in stature but strong in character and physical abilities, he said.

She was comfortable living among the wildlife, he said. “She lived in the Bush when her kids were babies," he said. “She and my niece were not naive or foolish or uninformed. They’ve lived in these areas and have been around animals of countless types for years and years and decades.”

The bear believed to have killed the two women has not been caught, and officials are using DNA to establish whether it is the same grizzly involved in last year's attack on the school group.
