Two County Durham men have been punished after fraudulently obtaining almost £10,000 through false council tax claims.

Scott Wheatley, 44, and Martin Fairlamb, 48, both of Edderacres Walk, Wingate, made false claims to Durham County Council to benefit from discounts on their council tax bills.

Wheatley claimed a 25 per cent Single Person’s Discount for more than six years, having previously claimed a 100 per cent Student Exemption for almost two years.

In April 2021, he told the council he had always lived alone and submitted a form requesting the single person discount.

However, an investigation by the council found independent evidence from multiple sources showing Fairlamb had been living with Wheatley between 2012 and 2021.

Peterlee Magistrates’ Court heard that in December 2022, Fairlamb falsely claimed he had moved into a separate property on Albert Street in Shildon.

He told the council he had occupied the property since August that year, providing a landlord letter signed by Wheatley and an agreement listing Wheatley as the landlord and Fairlamb as the tenant.

The tenancy agreement, dated June 2022, was supposedly witnessed by Wheatley’s mother and listed her address.

However, council records showed she did not live at the address until August 2022.

The court heard the documents were used to support a false account of Fairlamb’s occupation and were made, supplied and used by both men to continue the fraud.

This allowed Fairlamb to falsely benefit from a Single Person’s Discount until April 2024 while continuing to live with Wheatley at Edderacres Walk.

Fairlamb also applied for a Council Tax Reduction on the basis he was the sole adult occupier, but this was not processed because his earnings were too high.

Evidence gathered by the council included marriage and family records, employer records, insurance policies, credit and retail accounts and utility consumption data.

During interview, Wheatley accepted Fairlamb stayed with him “more often than not”, but sought to minimise the permanence of the arrangement.

Fairlamb maintained he had initially lived at the Albert Street property with Wheatley’s parents before becoming its sole occupier.

However, when asked how he could have lived there without using any gas or electricity, he was unable to provide an explanation.

When challenged with evidence, he ended the interview and declined to answer further questions.

Following its investigation, the council reassessed council tax payments at the relevant properties and removed Wheatley’s Student Disregard and Single Person Discount.

The council found it had overpaid £9,743.29 across the addresses as a result of the falsely claimed discounts.

In mitigation, magistrates heard Wheatley had not been aware he was not entitled to the benefit and had continued rolling it over, although he accepted it was his responsibility to know.

The court was also told he was the main carer for Fairlamb and a dependent.

Magistrates fined Fairlamb £200 under the Fraud Act 2006 and ordered him to pay £325 in costs and an £80 victim surcharge.

Wheatley was handed a 12-month community order requiring him to complete 150 hours of unpaid work.
