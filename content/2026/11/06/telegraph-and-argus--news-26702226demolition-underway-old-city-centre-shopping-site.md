Demolition is well underway at an old city centre shopping centre.

Oastler Shopping Centre, on John Street in Bradford, previously housed dozens of independent traders selling food, clothing, household goods and books.

The demolition is the first phase of a major regeneration scheme that will bring 1,000 homes, business units and green spaces to the city centre.

## How is the demolition progressing?

The former Bazaar building is almost cleared, with Shaw House and the multi-storey car park beside Oastler Market next to be demolished.

As previously reported, Councillor Andrew Judson, Bradford Council's Executive Member for Economy, Regeneration and Planning, said: "These sites have played an important role in Bradford's history and we are now preparing them for a new future at the heart of our city centre."

## City Village details

The first phase of City Village will deliver 97 homes on the upper Oastler site and council-owned car parks across Westgate.

Incommunities will manage the homes, which will be offered through shared ownership, affordable housing and social rent.

![Drone shots show how the demolition works are progressing.](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21674699.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Drone shots show how the demolition works are progressing. *(Image: T&A)*

Later phases will bring larger apartment blocks to the former Oastler Market and Kirkgate Shopping Centre sites.

Their ground floors will include shops, cafes and bars.

## Where has the money come from?

The project is backed by ECF, a partnership between Homes England’s National Housing Bank, L&G and Muse.

Funding includes £30 million from Homes England’s Brownfield, Infrastructure and Land Fund and £13.2 million from the West Yorkshire Combined Authority.

## 'New era' for Bradford

Bradford Council leader Stephen Place said: "This is the start of a new era for Bradford.

"We were a city built on textiles and wool, now we’re moving to industries like advanced manufacturing and space technology."

Cllr Place acknowledged that many people would feel the loss of Oastler Market and Kirkgate, but stressed the need for progress.

The City Village site is surrounded by vacant buildings, including a former NHS unit.

Cllr Place said: "We’ve got all these wonderful buildings.

"We’re open for business.

"There is the political will to push Bradford forward.

"If you are thinking of opening a business, a bistro, a café, a comedy club, a jazz lounge, come and see us at Bradford Council and we’ll do the best we can to work with you."

## When will the demolition be complete?

Martin Smith of Metropolitan Demolition outlined the next steps.

He said: "Once we’ve finished demolition of the Bazaar we’ll move on to the Shaw building.

"Simultaneously we’ll start knocking down the car park of the Oastler building."

Demolition will begin in the middle of the car park and move towards Rawson Road, then continue on its Westgate side before reaching the former Morrisons and the main Oastler building.

Work is expected to be completed by spring.
