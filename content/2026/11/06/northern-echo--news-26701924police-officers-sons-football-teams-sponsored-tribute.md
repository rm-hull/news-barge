A former Cleveland Police officer has helped keep the memory of PC Matthew Blades alive by sponsoring the football teams played for by his two sons following the tragic A66 crash.

Paul Cooper, founder of the Pocket Sergeant app, has funded training jackets for two Seaton Carew FC youth teams after learning that both of PC Matthew "Matty" Blades' sons play for the club.

PC Blades and colleague PC Tom Clough died following a crash on the A66 in August, prompting an outpouring of support from across the policing community and beyond.

Pocket Sergeant previously donated £1,000 to a fundraiser launched following the tragedy.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21674399.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Paul Cooper, founder of Pocket Sergeant *(Image: CHESTER COOPER)*

Mr Cooper said he was inspired to do more after seeing a Facebook post from Seaton Carew FC and learning of the role Matty had been due to play at the club.

He said: "I saw Seaton Carew FC's Facebook post on August 23 and read that both of Matty's boys played for the club.

"Matty had been due to start coaching the Under-7s, the team his youngest son played for.

"That struck a chord with me.

"I was lucky enough to coach my own son, Chester, from Under-7s for several years.

"I know how much that time together meant to me and it was heartbreaking to think that Matty and his son never got to share that experience."

Recognising the financial pressures faced by grassroots football clubs, Mr Cooper approached Seaton Carew FC about sponsoring training jackets for both of Matty's sons' teams and their coaches.

The jackets feature a special tribute on the sleeve reading "Matty 13" - a reference to the officer's surname and his playing number.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21674397.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The jackets have PC Matthew Blades' nickname and playing number *(Image: CHESTER COOPER)*

Mr Cooper said the tribute was created in consultation with Matty's family and the club.

He said: "It means a lot to me that we've been able to do something for both teams in Matty's memory.

"I hope that when someone on the touchline asks what 'Matty 13' means, it gives people a chance to talk about him and share a little about who he was.

"Above all, I wanted his family, the club and his colleagues to know we're thinking of them and that Matty is still very much remembered."

In a Facebook post announcing the initiative, Mr Cooper described meeting Matty's wife Sarah and the couple's two sons.

He wrote: "Following the tragic loss of Cleveland Police officer PC Matty Blades, we wanted to do something thoughtful for his two boys and the football teams that meant so much to him.

"Matty was due to begin coaching Seaton Carew FC U7 Seahawks this season, where his youngest son plays.

"Sadly, he never got that opportunity.
