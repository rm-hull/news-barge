A North East MP has called for police to get stronger powers and more resources to tackle the growing use of illegal off-road bikes in antisocial behaviour and organised crime.

Darlington MP Lola McEvoy urged the Government to give officers new powers to tackle illegal electric-bike and off-road motorcycle use across the region.

The Labour MP said battery-powered bikes had become "the getaway vehicle of choice" for lawbreakers and warned about riders wearing balaclavas to hide their identities.

North East MPs attended the debate, along with Julie Francis, whose mother Gloria Stephenson was killed in Sunderland last year after being struck by an electric motorbike.

Ms McEvoy told MPs: "Since I was elected with this Labour Government in July 2024, it has become increasingly clear that battery-powered bikes are the getaway vehicle of choice for those who do not respect the rule of law."

She paid tribute to Julie Francis, who was watching from the public gallery, and told MPs: "Her mother, Gloria, was killed in Sunderland by a teenager illegally riding an off-road bike. Julie is as determined as I am to get more resources, and to get justice for Gloria, herself and the family."

Ms McEvoy said North East police forces struggled to pursue offenders.

She told the debate: "The police's problem is that they do not have the resources to send out a police helicopter and a full squad to chase down those people every time antisocial behaviour is reported."

The MP said off-road bikes were widely used in crime and called for more neighbourhood policing in hotspots, plus powers to seize and destroy vehicles.

She added: "The use of balaclavas to evade detection is intimidating. The fact that the bikes are not licensed, plated or chipped means that the riders have a getaway vehicle."

Policing minister Sarah Jones acknowledged MPs’ concerns and said illegal bikes were becoming a growing problem nationwide.

She said: "I hope that those who have lost loved ones or been injured can be reassured that we are looking very carefully at this challenge in the Home Office and across Government."

Ms Jones said ministers were considering legal changes, better officer training and support for police forces carrying out large-scale illegal-bike seizures.

She added that ministers were weighing short-term measures, longer-term legal reforms and ways for forces to share tactics that work.

She said: "The message to all our fair, law-abiding citizens should be, 'You are abiding by the law, and we are grateful for that. These other people will not get away with what they are doing'."
