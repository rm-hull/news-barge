Economy Secretary Stephen Flynn has been told to "get on top of his department" after saying whistleblower allegations about conditions at the Dalzell steelworks were matters for the company, not ministers, despite millions in taxpayer support for the beleaguered firm behind the plant.

As The Herald revealed in August, Lib Dem MSP Willie Rennie wrote to the Economy Secretary after being approached by a source at the plant in Motherwell.

They told him Sanjeev Gupta's company was buying steel slab from Kazakhstan rather than from British steelmakers, that the plant had been "ransacked" with "nearly every piece of copper" stolen, and that £7 million owed to the Scottish Government had been "transferred elsewhere" rather than spent on the plant.

He was also told there was a shortage of fitters and electricians and that "the furnaces that heat the slabs are on their knees due to lack of maintenance and investment".

The money was lent by Scottish Enterprise to Liberty Steel Dalzell as working capital, under a facility agreed in 2017 after ministers bought the Dalzell and Clydebridge mills from Tata Steel for £1 and immediately sold them on to Mr Gupta's Liberty House.

![Sanjeev Gupta is currently under investigation by the Serious Fraud Office](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/6071765.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Sanjeev Gupta is currently under investigation by the Serious Fraud Office

There was also an allegation that pensions had not been paid.

The Herald confirmed last month that employer and employee pension contributions for workers have, in fact, not been paid since July. Aviva told The Herald it cannot currently accept further payments but did not say why.

The Scottish Government is currently pursuing GFG Alliance through the courts.

Ministers have asked a judge for an interim interdict to prevent breaches of the terms of the controversial Lochaber Guarantee Reimbursement Agreement, which underpins their 2016 guarantee of the smelter's power purchase.

Dalzell has not filed accounts for five years, and Mr Gupta is facing prosecution by Companies House over the failure, as well as a long-running investigation by the Serious Fraud Office.

On Kazakhstan, Mr Flynn wrote: "The sourcing of raw materials used at Dalzell is a matter for the company."

The furnaces, the shortage of tradespeople and the copper theft received the same treatment. In each reply the Economy Secretary said the condition of the plant and the availability of staff were "matters for the company".

On pensions, Mr Flynn went a little further. He wrote: "The payment of employers' pension contributions is the responsibility of the company. I recognise that this issue is of great concern to the workforce and I am supportive of GFG's efforts to find a solution to allow pension contributions to be made and understand that the pensions regulator has been engaged by GFG Group."

On the £7m, he wrote: "The Scottish Government has not loaned money to Liberty Steel. Scottish Enterprise made a working capital loan to Liberty Steel in 2017 and Scottish Enterprise is responsible for monitoring that transaction."

Mr Flynn describes GFG Alliance as the owner of "important businesses in Scotland that serve strategic industrial markets" and argues that ministers had little choice but to act.

He wrote: "Without Scottish Government intervention these operations were set to fail with the loss of industrial capacity and vital employment in Lanarkshire and Lochaber."

He said that, to protect jobs and taxpayers' interests, the government maintains regular dialogue with GFG and that his officials operate "robust monitoring and risk management processes, supported by legal and commercial advisors".

The Economy Secretary also told Mr Rennie that officials engage regularly with local management at Dalzell, although employment terms remain the responsibility of the company.

Mr Flynn said: "The GFG Alliance as custodians of these important businesses — both at Dalzell and in Lochaber — must live up to its obligations and ensure all payments due to staff either through wages or pension contributions are met."

He added: "This government will continue to take all necessary steps to enforce our own legal rights under the Lochaber guarantee and to advocate for the workers of Alvance and Liberty Steel."

Mr Rennie said the letter amounted to a refusal to answer, and, as a result, "taxpayers will assume the worst".

“Stephen Flynn was given a chance to dispute the reports of unpaid loans, supplies being solicited from Kazakhstan because British suppliers will not take the risk and pensions going unmet that I raised with him," the Lib Dem said.

"He has not done so. As a result taxpayers will assume the worst.

“I have to admit that in 15 years of being an MSP, I cannot remember receiving a letter from a Cabinet Secretary who seems so truculent at being asked to account for money that his government has spent and the dubious characters it has partnered with."

![The Dalzell steelworks at Motherwell](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/19949512.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The Dalzell steelworks at Motherwell *(Image: Kirsty Anderson)*

He added: “The original decisions to hand these businesses over to Sanjeev Gupta may not have been Stephen Flynn’s but they are now his responsibility. I appreciate that Scottish Enterprise are the ones who are administering the actual loans but responsibility for Scotland’s enterprise agencies is literally one of the first bullet points in his job description.

“It does not fill me with confidence that he is now saying that this is all a matter for the company.

"There are tens, if not hundreds, of millions of Scottish taxpayers’ money potentially at risk here. He should get on top of his department and speak with Dalzell, Scottish Enterprise and the workers who are being hung out to dry as a matter of urgency.”

The Scottish Government was approached for comment.

Last month, creditors to Mr Gupta's Liberty Steel Newport, the first of his UK steel plants, appointed liquidators to oversee a wind-down of the business.

The Welsh steelworks, which he bought in 2013, employs about 180 people.
