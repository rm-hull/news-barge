Amanda Owen's ambitious restoration of a historic Yorkshire Dales farmhouse is set to continue after Channel 4 confirmed two further series of Our Farm Next Door.

Their progress has been documented in the More4 series Our Farm Next Door: Amanda, Clive and Kids, which follows the challenges of bringing the neglected building back into use.

The fourth series reaches a major milestone as Amanda and her daughters prepare for their first overnight stay at the farmhouse, with furniture collected by Amanda over the years finally being moved inside.

But the restoration is far from over.

Channel 4 has announced that Leeds-based Wise Owl Films will produce a further 10 episodes across series five and six, documenting the property's continuing transformation from a house into a home.

The new commission follows the success of the programme, which the broadcaster says has been More4's highest-rated show for three years.

## How has Anty John's changed?

When Our Farm Next Door began in 2024, the family faced the enormous challenge of bringing a neglected historic property back into use.

Amanda, former husband Clive Owen and their nine children have worked alongside local builders and tradespeople to restore the farmhouse while retaining its character.

The renovation has involved major building work, repairs and improvements to the interior, with previous episodes documenting the practical challenges of making the property suitable for family life.

The third and fourth series have increasingly focused on the inside of the building, including painting, flooring and creating individual rooms.

The fourth-series finale, scheduled for November 2, sees the family moving furniture into Anty John's as the house finally begins to take shape.

The episode also features Amanda and her daughters preparing for their first sleepover at the property.

According to the programme synopsis, it will be the first time anyone has slept there in many decades.

## What happens next to Anty John's?

Channel 4 has confirmed that the renovation will continue in series five and six, with the family pressing ahead with their plans for the property.

Series five will begin immediately after the first overnight stay and follow the Owens from early summer 2026 through to spring 2027.

The new episodes will also explore the history of Anty John's, with Amanda investigating the life of former owner Anthony Clarkson, a 19th-century Swaledale mapmaker.

Clarkson's diaries are expected to reveal further parallels between his life and the Owen family's experiences almost two centuries later.

Meanwhile, life at Ravenseat will continue to provide an important part of the programme.

Edith, Violet and their sisters will reopen the family's cream tea van, while Sidney and Miles take on more farming responsibilities.

James Knight, creative director at Wise Owl Films, said Amanda's unusual approach to the renovation was part of the programme's appeal.

He said: "Amanda admits she has never seen a renovation show on TV, which might help explain why we think Our Farm Next Door is unlike any other series on television."

He added that the combination of farming, family life, the building's history and the slow pace of restoration meant there was always something happening.

The programme's renewal means viewers will be able to follow the project beyond the milestone of the family's first sleepover.

While Anty John's is increasingly taking shape as a family home, Channel 4 has not confirmed when the restoration will be completed or whether the family will move there permanently.

Series five and six have been commissioned as two five-part series for More4, with broadcast dates yet to be announced.
