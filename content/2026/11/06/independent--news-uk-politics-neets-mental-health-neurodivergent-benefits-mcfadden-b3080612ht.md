Young people with mental health challenges or neurodiversity are better off working than on benefits, the Work and Pensions Secretary has said, as he promised major welfare overhauls in 2027.

Employment is "part of the answer" for the 45% of young people not in employment, education or training (Neets) who have mental health conditions or are neurodiverse, Pat McFadden explained.

The senior Government minister made the claim when asked by ITV News why Neet numbers grew from 21% in 2014.

Mr McFadden told the broadcaster: "There’s been a big change in recent years. In the last five or six years, maybe the last 10 years, we’ve had a really big increase in young people saying that they are unfit for work, very often because of mental health or neurodiverse conditions.

"I’m concerned about that because I think that while there’s greater prevalence of these conditions, I don’t think it should lead to a life of inactivity and a life on benefits."

![McFadden (middle) has promised a major welfare overhaul in 2027](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/09/07/22/01M1YA3DAP0XA8Q7WFTYBHVTY6.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

He was speaking on a fact-finding trip to Denmark, where the Fleksjob scheme enables people with disabilities or health conditions to remain employed on reduced hours or with adjustments instead of taking sick leave.

Employers pay wages, topped up by local government subsidies.

Mr McFadden added: “I think for many people, work is part of the answer. Work is good for you and the people that I’ve met here in Denmark who benefited from the Fleksjob scheme, they are living examples of that.

“Every single person I met here in Denmark, who’ve been helped to work through the Fleksjob scheme that they operate in this country, said not only had it given them money to earn, but they felt better mentally by coming to work.”

Mr McFadden said the Government would not “lift and shift” the scheme to use in the UK, but that there are “lessons to be learned from the success of what’s happened in Denmark” for Britain.

ITV also put to the minister that it would take a long time to reduce the costs of the growing welfare bill through a flexi-job scheme: Denmark has seen its disability employment rate rise from 40% to 60% over a decade.

![Burnham (middle) said the UK must “invest in success” rather than “failure through the benefits system”](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/07/28/12/cd23946df0cafc1b84574e656424af29Y29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzg1MzIyODUx-2.85437412.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Asked what more immediate steps are being taken, Mr McFadden said: “We are acting now – we’ve changed the universal credit rules. We’re acting on fraud and error. We’ve changed the rules in the Motability scheme.

“So, there are things that we’re doing now. We’ve got two really important reports coming out in the next few weeks from Alan Milburn and Stephen Timms on different parts of the benefits system.”

He suggested the Government’s response to those two reviews would be to bring forward a new law in Parliament.

Mr McFadden added: “When they come out, we will respond to them. I expect us to legislate next year, so we’ll be taking action in the near future in response to those reports.”

Last month, Prime Minister Andy Burnham said the UK must “invest in success” rather than “failure through the benefits system” as he set out his commitment to end the youth unemployment crisis.

The prime minister described the number of young people not in employment, education or training (Neet) as an “economic catastrophe and a moral failure”.

Ministers are expected to lay out their response to the Timms review into disability benefits reform, and Milburn review into getting more Neets into work in the coming weeks.

Milburn’s review into youth employment will recommend that the UK should identify and monitor young people at risk of economic inactivity to prevent them slipping out of the workforce.

The former Labour health secretary will say that the government must set up locally-led youth services in every community in England, when he delivers his recommendations this month, in a bid to stop teenagers from falling through the cracks.

It comes as the government grappled with how to keep the ballooning welfare bill under control.

The Office for Budget Responsibility forecasts it will rise to £431 billion by the 2031-32 financial year, largely driven by health-related and pensioner benefits.

More than half of all welfare spending on the Government’s books goes towards pensioners, including the state pension and other benefits.
