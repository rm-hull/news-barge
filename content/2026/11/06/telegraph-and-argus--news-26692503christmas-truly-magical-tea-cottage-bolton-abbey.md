ROB and Charles Stephenson Hooper moved from the US to run a 200-year-old tearoom on Bolton Abbey Estate. We spoke to Rob about their business.

Q: When was The Tea Cottage established? Who was behind it and how did it come into being?

A: The buildings that now make up The Tea Cottage were originally residential crofters’ cottages and remained so until 1977, when they were converted into a café. People have therefore been coming here for tea for almost 50 years. Over those years it became something of a Bolton Abbey institution and was even one of Victoria Wood’s favourite places to visit.

We took over The Tea Cottage in March 2025. We carried out a complete decorative refit and made significant improvements to the facilities, while being very careful not to lose the history and character that made the cottages special in the first place. We like to think of ourselves as the latest custodians of somewhere that had a long history before us, and we want to preserve that while giving it a fresh lease of life.

Q: Describe the location - what do customers say about it?

A: One of the lovely things about The Tea Cottage is that you don’t really know what is waiting for you until you walk through the gate. From the village it looks like a small traditional crofter’s cottage, which is exactly what it once was. But once you come down the pathway and around the cottage, the whole place suddenly opens out onto our beautiful patio, with a stunning panoramic view across the Bolton Abbey Estate and Bolton Priory.

There really is nothing better than sitting outside on the patio with a cup of tea - or perhaps a glass of wine - and one of our freshly made meals, looking out across the estate. That is exactly where our slogan ‘Brew with a View’ comes from.

In summer the patio becomes a huge part of the café, but the view is beautiful throughout the year. One of the pleasures of being open 364 days a year is watching the landscape change completely through the seasons.

Q: Describe the interior and facilities.

When we took over, we completely redecorated and refurbished The Tea Cottage and improved the facilities. We wanted to raise the standard considerably while making sure that a historic Yorkshire cottage still felt like a historic Yorkshire cottage, rather than turning it into a generic modern café.

The Tea Cottage is also much larger than it first appears from the village. Upstairs we have a stunning events space with exposed beams and around 200 years of history. It is a wonderful setting for private parties, celebrations, group bookings and afternoon teas.

Q: What sort of customer experience do you offer?

A: We pride ourselves on offering a premium customer experience, but for us premium does not mean formal or stuffy. It means looking after people properly.

When you arrive, you are greeted by one of our friendly members of staff, who will find you the best seat we can - whether that is somewhere cosy inside or, when the weather allows, a table on the patio overlooking the estate. We want customers to feel welcomed and looked after, rather than simply processed through a café.

We take reservations, which means people making a special trip to Bolton Abbey can know that they have a table waiting for them. We also take advance reservations for afternoon teas, parties and events.

We are proudly dog-friendly too. Dogs are a huge part of life at Bolton Abbey and are genuinely welcome at The Tea Cottage rather than merely tolerated.

Q: What sort of food and drink do you serve?

A: We serve food throughout the day. Every day begins with a hearty full breakfast menu from 10am until noon, followed by an extensive lunchtime menu from noon until 3pm.

Everything is homemade. Our lunchtime menu includes dishes such as homemade lasagnas and fishcakes, freshly prepared salads, sandwiches and lighter lunches, alongside traditional favourites. Throughout autumn and winter we make delicious homemade soups, which are particularly welcome after a long walk around Bolton Abbey on a cold Yorkshire day.

We change our main menu twice a year, with separate spring/summer and autumn/winter menus. We keep the favourites that regular customers expect, while making sure the food reflects the season - lighter dishes and fresh salads during the warmer months and warming soups and heartier homemade dishes as the weather gets colder.

We try to source as many products and ingredients locally as possible. During the summer we also sell locally sourced ice cream, including ice cream for dogs.

We are the only fully licensed café here and serve a range of beers, wines and cocktails alongside our teas, coffees and soft drinks. So customers can stop for coffee and cake, enjoy a proper lunch with a glass of wine, or make a celebration or afternoon tea even more special.

Q: What are your specialities?

A: Our homemade scones are something we are particularly proud of. They have won first-place ribbons at numerous local and county fairs, so there is a little bit of competitive history behind them as well as a great deal of baking.

They are naturally an important part of our afternoon teas, which are another speciality of The Tea Cottage and are particularly popular for birthdays, anniversaries and celebrations.

Our homemade lasagnas and fishcakes are consistently popular, as are our breakfasts. During autumn and winter our homemade soups really come into their own, while in summer the locally sourced ice cream is extremely popular – although some days we suspect the dog ice cream may be greeted even more enthusiastically than the human version.

Q: When are you open?

A: We are open seven days a week, 364 days a year, closing only on Christmas Day. Our official opening hours are 10am until 4pm, with breakfast served from 10am to noon and lunch from noon until 3pm.

There is also a slightly unofficial Tea Cottage rule: if you happen to arrive around nine in the morning and the coffee machine is already up and running, there is a reasonable chance we will take pity on you and make you one.

Q: Do you host parties, functions or events?

A: Yes. We take reservations for parties, events, groups and special occasions, which are typically held upstairs in our beautiful events space. The exposed beams and 200-year-old history give it enormous character and make it feel very different from a conventional function room.

We also take reservations for afternoon teas and can cater for celebrations such as birthdays and anniversaries.

Q: What do you do at Christmas?

A: Christmas is one of the most exciting times of the year and we really go to town with it. Every year we erect a large real Christmas tree on the patio, positioned so that visitors can take a photograph beside it with the Bolton Abbey Estate and Bolton Priory in the background. It has become one of the most photographed sights at Bolton Abbey during the Christmas season, and we love seeing families, couples – and quite a few dogs – stopping to have their photographs taken there.

Throughout December we offer special Christmas-themed afternoon teas. At weekends they become even more special, with live harp music performed by Tea Cottage co-owner Charles Hooper while guests enjoy their afternoon tea.

This year, on Christmas Eve, we are going one step further with a special Christmas afternoon tea accompanied by live harp music and sing-along Christmas carols. We want it to feel like a proper Christmas occasion rather than simply going out for something to eat.

Our Christmas afternoon teas, weekend afternoon teas with live harp music and the special Christmas Eve event are all reservation only.

There is something particularly magical about The Tea Cottage at that time of year: sitting inside a historic Yorkshire cottage, enjoying Christmas afternoon tea and live harp music, while outside there is a real Christmas tree with Bolton Priory beyond it.

Q: is there anything else you would like to say about the business?

A: For us, The Tea Cottage is about making the most of an extraordinary place while giving people the level of food, service and hospitality that makes them want to come back.

We know we are fortunate to have one of the most beautiful settings in Yorkshire, but we never want to rely on the view alone. We want someone to come in because they are visiting Bolton Abbey and then return because they remember the welcome, the homemade food, our staff and how they felt while they were here.

From the village we may look like a little old crofter’s cottage. Come down the pathway, though, and you discover everything else - the café, the historic upstairs events space, the patio and that extraordinary view.

That really is The Tea Cottage: Brew with a View.
