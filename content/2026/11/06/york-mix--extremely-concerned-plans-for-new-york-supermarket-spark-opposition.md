Plans to create a large Tesco supermarket on part of the B&Q site in York have attracted opposition.

As we revealed last month, plans are in to alter, subdivide, and extend the B&Q store off Hull Road to make room for the supermarket.

According to that application, the development will bring a major food store close to thousands of residents, and create 150 new jobs.

One of those to oppose the plans is Graham Kennedy, managing director of Inner Space Stations on Hull Road.

In a letter objecting to the development, he writes: “We are extremely concerned about the potential consequences of introducing a major supermarket at this location, not only for our own business but also for neighbouring residents, other independent retailers and the wider local community.”

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/b-and-q-hull-road-york-pd.webp)

His principal concern is the extra traffic generated by the supermarket. “We believe that the increase in traffic will create another gridlocked exit out of the city.

“It will further diminish the city centre for shoppers resulting in empty shops and job losses.”

Mr Kennedy also says the number of allocated parking spaces would be inadequate, and the proposed toucan crossing “will have a significant negative impact on traffic in the area”.

A neighbour living next to the site has also objected: “I strongly object to Tesco’s opening as a 24-hour supermarket,” she writes. “Many people adjacent to the site have children and they should not be subjected to this noise pollution.”

She also has concerns about the traffic: “Does the council really want to make Hull Road into the same commuter nightmare that exists on Fulford Road? The council must carefully consider whether the surrounding roads and junctions can safely accommodate the additional traffic generated by this development.”

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/b-and-q-tesco-hull-road-plan-2-pd.webp)

But the planned new Tesco has received support too. One resident writes: “I think having a Tesco on the B&Q site would bring so much convenience to living in the area.

“At the moment, we have to drive to Monks Cross or Clifton Moor, both at least ten minutes away, which isn’t ideal if you just want to pop out for a few bits.”

Another writes: “A Tesco store will be of benefit to all the area. Even with a bit more traffic it will be easier than attempting to access Monks Cross or Clifton Moor, especially with the new  
development at Monks Cross.”
