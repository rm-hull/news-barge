Charlie MacGregor, founder of The Social Hub, talks about the “deeply personal milestone” of bringing the model “home” to Glasgow.

A “self-confessed optimist”, Mr MacGregor believes “society’s biggest challenges become far easier to solve when different minds sit in the same room, listen to each other, and share a common table”.

**Job title**: Founder and chief executive officer** What is your business called?**The Social Hub** Where is it based?**Our headquarters are in Amsterdam, but my roots are Scottish. Bringing our model home to Glasgow - with an eye on Edinburgh next - has been a deeply personal milestone.** What does it produce/do?**We are fundamentally in the business of connection. People often ask if we are a hotel, student accommodation, or a co-working space. The answer is “yes”, and so much more. We build multi-use, hybrid spaces where diverse people collide, collaborate, and thrive under one roof. Where modern cities often isolate, we bring people back together.** To whom does it sell?**To anyone seeking community. It’s a mix of global travellers, students taking their first big step into the world, entrepreneurs scaling ideas, and locals stopping by for a coffee or an event. The magic happens in the spontaneous intersections between those worlds.** How many employees?**Over 1,200 dynamic team members across Europe.** What attracted you to your current role?** My path began alongside my father, who pioneered purpose-built student housing in Edinburgh. I quickly realised that students deserved far more than just four walls and a desk.

We launched what started as The Student Hotel in the Netherlands, and the concept evolved organically. We saw that local entrepreneurs needed space to create, so we introduced co-working. Restaurants, event spaces and wellness gyms followed. Before long, I realised we weren't just running accommodation - we were building living, breathing social ecosystems.

I am a self-confessed optimist. I truly believe society’s biggest challenges become far easier to solve when different minds sit in the same room, listen to each other, and share a common table, and we saw this diverse community buzzed!

**What were you doing before?** I left school at 15. I didn’t do so well there but I loved working. I worked in garages, as a trainee salesman, then I worked on construction sites, and picked up real-world perspectives along the way before entering the property world. That journey taught me one thing early on: infrastructure is just the hardware; human connection is the software.**Read more**

**What do you least enjoy?** Bureaucracy is anchored in the phrase, “We’ve always done it this way.” Cities are evolving far too fast for legacy mindsets. While we must respect and protect our heritage, we have to make room for radical fresh thinking, sustainable regeneration, and bold design that anticipates the next 50 years, rather than just guarding the past.**What do you consider to be the main successes of the business?** Building a commercially thriving, B Corp-certified European business is something I’m proud of, but I’m far prouder of the human and social impact within our walls.

Independent research showed our Glasgow hub generated over 10 times the social value of a traditional hotel, nearly £10 million in its very first year, while initiatives like our Better Society Academy bring together policymakers, change-makers, and future leaders (including First Minister John Swinney in Glasgow) to tackle systemic global challenges.

Through the TSH Talent Foundation, we commit 1% of our annual revenue directly to supporting high-potential young people who lack traditional access or opportunity. That balance of purpose and commercial scale is what true success looks like to me.

**What are your ambitions for the firm?** To redefine hospitality entirely. The hotel of tomorrow cannot merely sell a bed; it must actively enrich the surrounding city. As we expand across Europe, our ambition is for every Social Hub to serve as a vital piece of civic infrastructure - a destination defined by the community that grows around it.**What are the challenges facing the sector and market, and what could be done to address these?** Beyond standard industry headwinds, the core challenge lies in how we think about urban development. Cities are battling isolation, housing crunches, and shifting high streets, yet developers continue erecting single-use, rigid structures.

The future belongs to dynamic, mixed-use environments that remain vibrant from morning to night. Urban planning needs to ask a bolder question: "What lasting value does this space contribute to the city over the next half-century?"

**What single thing would most help?** Institutional courage. The world’s greatest cities are those brave enough to test new ideas. It’s about finding the confidence to honour history while actively designing for tomorrow.

**What is the most valuable lesson you have learned?** Purpose and profit are not mutually exclusive - they are accelerators of one another. For decades, business assumed you had to choose between doing good or driving bottom-line growth. When you build something that genuinely enriches lives, commercial success naturally follows.**What was your best moment?** Returning to Scotland after building across Europe and seeing people instantly embrace the vision in Glasgow. Watching thousands of individuals from completely different walks of life interact effortlessly inside the hub never fails to inspire me.

On a personal level, seeing the real-world footprint of Movement On The Ground - a charity I co-founded in 2015 to transform refugee camps into safe, dignified, self-sustaining communities - remains one of the most humbling experiences of my life. Very proud that this is still going strong with projects in Gaza and Syria as well as working with the Dutch Government to introduce our model of camps, which we think should be “campUSes” across the country.

**What has been your most challenging moment?** Navigating major economic shifts, global crises, and rapid international scaling. Like any founder, there are moments where you wonder if you’ve taken on too much. But pressure sharpens strategy - our most meaningful innovations have always been born out of moments where we were forced to rethink the rules entirely.**How do you relax?** I’m not great at standing still! I find my energy through outdoors, travel, and quality time with my kids. Burning Man has also become an essential annual reset. It’s a powerful reminder of what humans can create when guided by radical generosity, creativity, and sheer playfulness.**What phrase or quotation has inspired you the most?**“Here and Now”. It’s a simple compass, but it shapes how I think about life and being connected. This is something I have brought into hospitality, community-building, and leadership.** What is the best book you have ever read?**My favourite book is probably “The Forty Rules of Love” by Elif Shafak - all about love. I’m also a huge fan of Humankind: A Hopeful History and Moral Ambition by Rutger Bregman. Together, they offer a compelling reminder that humans are inherently wired for good - and challenge us to direct our talents toward meaningful solutions. That ethos sits at the very heart of The Social Hub.** Where do you find yourself most at ease?**Wandering through one of our hubs on a busy afternoon. Seeing a student collaborating with a start-up founder, a local grabbing coffee, and a guest checking in - that’s where the vision comes alive.** If you weren't in your current role, what job would you most fancy?**Full-time humanitarian work. My work with Movement On The Ground has taught me more about resilience, dignity, and human potential than any boardroom ever could.** What countries have you most enjoyed travelling to?** Italy holds a very special place for me - cities like Rome, Florence, and Bologna effortlessly weave ancient heritage into everyday modern life (which is precisely why we’ve built hubs there!).

That said, nowhere matches the warmth of Scotland. Even after decades living in the Netherlands, stepping back onto Scottish soil always feels like home.

**How are you finding Glasgow as a location for The Social Hub?** It has exceeded every benchmark. Glasgow possesses a rare mix of raw creative energy, authenticity, and openness. The city immediately understood our model - universities, civic leaders, businesses, and locals embraced the hub as a shared public asset. It has quickly become one of our strongest-performing locations in Europe.**What is the split of guests at The Social Hub in Glasgow?** It changes with the season, but on any given day, you’ll find international students, digital nomads, local creators, tech founders, and hotel guests mingling together. That rich mix is the secret sauce.**How do you feel Glasgow city centre is faring?** Glasgow is undergoing a transformation, and I am exceptionally optimistic about its trajectory. The foundations are world-class: top-tier universities, a thriving cultural scene, and an unmatched sense of warmth. When civic leadership, local enterprise, and communities collaborate boldly, real urban regeneration happens – not just in physical structures, but in collective confidence and opportunity.
