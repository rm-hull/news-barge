A Darlington toy exchange that helped hundreds of families is looking for a new home with added security after its old stall began to be misused.

Macauley MacDonald and his partner, Nuha Alom, are seeking a new base for their toy exchange, which has been based in the indoor market since November last year.

The scheme saw hundreds of toys donated and exchanged at an unmanned stall in the covered market, helping families and promoting reuse.

Darlington Market provided the space for free, but the couple are now looking for a new home after the arrangement ended because it could not accommodate the stall’s extra security requirements.

Macauley said he had been told by other market stall owners that the stall was being misused and attracting thieves.

The couple have contacted organisations across the town since losing their market base, but have so far found no new venue, and are in a race against time to get a location set up for Christmas.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21664749.jpg?type=mds-article-620) *(Image: DARLINGTON TOY EXCHANGE)*

Macauley said: "They can no longer help us but we absolutely appreciate all of the help they gave us in the past.

"However, all good things come to an end.

"It's just unfortunate that we are struggling to find a new venue in town.

"I've spoken to someone at the council who has put out some feelers to see what's happening.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21670861.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Macauley MacDonald and Nuha Alom. *(Image: MACAULEY MACDONALD)*

"I've spoken to the shopping centres in town but they say they're at full capacity or they already have people queuing up for the big units, or the spare ones need refurbishment so they're unsuitable.

"Essentially, everyone I have asked has been unable to help but they are still trying to support us.

"Queen Street and Cornmill want to be donation stations for us.

"They want to help us in any way they can, just unfortunately there isn't any space."

Residents have rallied around the project despite the setback, Macauley said.

"The public are trying to help us and everyone's full of ideas but unfortunately nothing has come from it," he said.

Macauley started the Toy Exchange after becoming frustrated by the cost of toys for his young son.

He said: "I've got a little boy and he'd just turned two at the time.

"We were struggling to find good quality toys and everything was so expensive.

"I just thought children need a bit of a break.

"They don't get much for free.

"They get school and dentistry for free but that's not very fun is it.

"I thought why isn't there anything like this?

"So I set it up myself."

Organisers fear their Christmas Toy Exchange will be cancelled if they cannot find a suitable venue.

The pair are appealing to businesses, landlords and organisations with town-centre space to help keep the project running this Christmas.

A spokesperson for Darlington Market said: "We were delighted to support the Free Toy Exchange by providing a free unit for such a noble cause over the Christmas period last year, and we were pleased to extend this arrangement through to October this year.

"However, as the initiative grew, Macauley required a larger space with enhanced security, which we unfortunately could not accommodate.

"We now need to refurbish and smarten up the area to prepare it for a concerted commercial lettings campaign that will support the continued use of this building.

"We hope they can find a more suitable location while we continue to regrow Darlington Market and navigate through a difficult patch for the High Street, and hopefully we can support them again when we are in a better position to do so."
