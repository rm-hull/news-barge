An NHS trust which provides mental health care in York has been told to improve after a watchdog raised concern over its leadership.

The Care Quality Commission (CQC) said it did not have sufficient assurance that risks were being addressed consistently and effectively at the Leeds and York Partnership NHS Foundation Trust.

CQC also raised concern over there being 27 “fire related incidents” at the trust between April 2024 and March 2025, meaning it was the fifth highest trust in the country for fire related incidents that year.

There was a further 24 fire related incidents at the trust from April and December 2025, the watchdog said.

CQC rated the trust’s leadership as “requires improvement” for how well-led it is following an inspection from April 14 to 16.

The trust’s director of nursing and professions, Nichola Sanderson, said it was “disappointed” with the rating but accepts CQC’s findings and has “already taken significant action” to address the watchdog’s concerns.

CQC said the trust provides services to autistic people, people with a learning disability and people with mental health needs across Leeds and York. The trust employs more than 3,000 staff and delivers care from 66 different sites, including Mill Lodge off Huntington Road.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/20579417.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Mill Lodge, a children’s mental health ward run by the Leeds and York Partnership NHS Foundation Trust, off Huntington Road in York *(Image: Google)*

CQC said the trust was in early discussions around a merger with a local community trust at the time of the inspection. It said the trust and community trust already share the same chief executive officer.

During the inspection, CQC’s inspectors said they found the trust was in breach of regulation relating to good management.

They said trust leaders “were visible” but feedback from partners about their experiences with them “were mixed”.

“Some described the board and senior leaders as strong and credible, whilst others expressed concerns regarding the trust's focus and responsiveness to the needs of people using services.”

CQC’s inspectors also found most staff were confident that their feedback, suggestions and concerns would be welcomed and lead to improvements.

But they said: “Data showed more improvements were needed in relation to reports of bullying and harassment.”

CQC’s deputy director of mental health in the north, Craig Howarth, said systems at the trust “weren’t always effective at delivering high-quality care or ensuring staff and leaders understood the issues”.

“There were also areas requiring further improvement with fire safety, people being placed out of area, as well as safeguarding processes. The trust needed to work more effectively to manage and reduce these risks at a faster pace.”

Inspectors also found the pace at which some improvements were being made “didn’t always provide sufficient assurance that risks were being addressed consistently and effectively”, Mr Howarth said.

He added: “We have told leaders at the trust where improvements are needed, and we will continue to monitor it closely to ensure this happens.”

## 'Work to do' at trust but it's 'moving in the right direction', says director

Ms Sanderson said the trust knows “there is more work to do” but is “confident that we are moving in the right direction”.

“We remain committed to continuous improvement, working openly and constructively with the CQC, and ensuring that the people who use our services receive the highest possible standards of care.”

Since the inspection, Ms Sanderson said the trust has “strengthened our governance arrangements and delivered significant improvements across service quality and patient safety”.

“This has been subject to additional scrutiny through a system-led rapid quality review involving NHS partners and the West Yorkshire Integrated Care Board.

“The review considered the actions taken by the trust and improvements made since the assessment, and concluded that sufficient progress had been achieved for the quality review process to be formally closed.

“We also welcome recognition in the report of leadership colleagues across the trust who ‘embody the culture and values of their workforce’ and that we have an open culture where staff feel able to raise concerns, report incidents and suggest improvements.”

Ms Sanderson added that she was “incredibly proud of our staff, whose compassion, dedication and commitment to our service users was observed by the inspection team and is recognised throughout the report”.
