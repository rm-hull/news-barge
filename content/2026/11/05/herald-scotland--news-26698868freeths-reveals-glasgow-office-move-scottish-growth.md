*The law firm has revealed a major office move and flagged its growth ambitions.*

*This follows rapid expansion in Scotland.*

Law firm Freeths has highlighted its growth ambitions in Scotland and revealed plans to move its enlarged Glasgow team to the Aurora building in Bothwell Street.

The firm declared the expansion “marks the next phase of Freeths’ growth in Scotland”, having built its Glasgow office from a two-lawyer launch team to 23 in little more than three years.

It revealed it had increased its Glasgow office revenue by 43% in the year to March.

Freeths opened its Glasgow office in July 2023, led by Paul Ockrim and Nick Taylor. Mr Ockrim and Mr Taylor, partners of the firm and joint heads of Freeths’ Glasgow office, were previously at Addleshaw Goddard. They were real estate partners at HBJ Gateley, which merged with Addleshaw Goddard in 2017.

Shortly after Freeths' Glasgow office launch, Mr Taylor and Mr Ockrim were joined by Allison Campbell, who also came from Addleshaw Goddard and had worked with them as a trainee and then junior lawyer.

Mr Taylor said: “We started in a 6,000 sq ft office which was kindly donated by Andrew Mickel and Craig Ormond of Mac and Mic (Mactaggart & Mickel) with two laptops and a photocopier and a set of carpet bowls to make use of the remaining 5,900 sq ft.”

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21670471.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Paul Ockrim and Nick Taylor joined from Addleshaw Goddard. *(Image: Supplied)*

The Glasgow team now comprises 21 lawyers and two support team members.

Freeths is moving to the Aurora building in February next year.

This year alone has seen the arrival of partners Heather Gibson, Alastair Frood and Richard Lockhart in the law firm’s Glasgow office.

Noting it is building a “full-service offering in the Scottish market”, Freeths observed it has expanded its “capabilities across corporate, dispute resolution, projects and infrastructure, real estate, restructuring and insolvency, banking and finance, tax and commercial law”.

The firm said of the impending move to the Aurora building: “This move will give the growing team more space to work together and meet clients. It also supports Freeths’ longer-term plans to continue building its business in Scotland.”

Freeths noted the investment follows the firm securing its first Scottish practice rankings in The Legal 500 UK 2027. The Glasgow team has entered “tier 4” for insolvency and corporate recovery and has been named a “firm to watch” in the commercial property: Edinburgh and Glasgow category, Freeths observed.

The firm declared its expansion in Scotland “sees three leading figures in the Scottish legal market reunited” as it flagged its recruitment of Heather Gibson, Amy McVey and Louise Mahon.

Mr Taylor noted the trio had worked together at Scottish law firm Brodies earlier in their careers.

Freeths said of Ms Gibson, Ms McVey and Ms Mahon: "Specialists in tax, banking and corporate law respectively, the three were all attracted to Freeths by the opportunity to work together again and build their practices.”

Mr Taylor said: “All of them are great hires – very capable lawyers with excellent reputations.”

Ms McVey joined Freeths from Burges Salmon in October 2024. Ms Mahon joined late last year from Morton Fraser MacRoberts.

Ms Gibson joined in September from Addleshaw Goddard.

Mr Lockhart joined Freeths in February, having previously been at Scottish law firm Burness Paull.

Mr Frood moved to Freeths from Eversheds Sutherland in June.

Freeths said: “The recent arrival of Alastair Frood, a highly respected senior litigator from Eversheds, further strengthens Freeths’ position as a serious contender in the Scottish legal market.”

Mr Ockrim said: “We opened in Glasgow to build a serious, long-term presence in Scotland. The response from clients and the calibre of people joining us have reinforced that ambition. Aurora will give us the space to keep building. It is a long-term investment in our people, our clients and our place in the Scottish market.”

Freeths said “further recognition for the Scottish office has been gained in Chambers UK 2027”.

Referring to commercial dispute resolution partner Jane Dickers, it declared: “Jane Dickers has entered the litigation rankings at band 4.”

Freeths added: “Heather Gibson has been recognised as up and coming for tax, and Amy McVey retains her up and coming ranking for restructuring and insolvency.”

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21670455.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Paul Ockrim and Nick Taylor set up the Glasgow office. *(Image: Supplied)*

Mr Taylor said: “The growth has come from listening to clients, building the team around what they need and speaking to people we know that are of the absolute highest quality. We now have greater depth across several core areas, while keeping the close, collaborative approach that has shaped the office from the start. At a personal level, it is fantastic to be able to work with former colleagues who have been able to share in our vision to build a new offering in the market.

“Our new home and the latest Legal 500 and Chambers recognition show the progress the team is making. We have strong foundations in place and plenty more we want to achieve.”

Karen Winterbottom, head of location, facilities and services at Freeths, said: “The Glasgow team has changed considerably since the office opened, so it needs a workplace that reflects that. Aurora gives colleagues more room to connect and provides a welcoming place for clients. It also gives the team the flexibility it needs as the business continues to develop.”

Freeths said its Scottish expansion forms part of continued investment across its national office network. The firm reported record revenue of £187.6 million for the year to March 31, an increase of more than 12%. Profit rose by 8.2% to £64.3m.
