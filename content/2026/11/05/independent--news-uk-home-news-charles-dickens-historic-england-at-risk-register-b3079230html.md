A writing chalet where author Charles Dickens wrote some of his acclaimed novels is among several historic sites added to Historic England’s latest heritage at risk register.

The two-storey Swiss chalet, which once stood in Dickens’ garden at Gads Hill Place in Higham before it was moved to the gardens of Eastgate House in Rochester, Kent, in the 1960s, was flagged due to concerns about its decaying timbers and structural problems.

There are close to 5,000 buildings, places of worship, archaeological sites, parks, gardens, conservation areas, battlefields and protected wreck sites on the register, which shows they are at risk of neglect, decay or inappropriate change.

New entries include Dickens’ hut, which was originally a Christmas gift to the author in 1864, serving as a quiet spot where he was able to work on several novels including *Our Mutual Friend, The Uncommercial Traveller*and his final unfinished work* The Mystery of Edwin Drood*.

But now the Grade I-listed building is in poor condition, with timber decay and structural instability preventing public access and leaving it in need of repair.

![Charles Dickens' writing chalet is one of the historic sites to be added to the latest heritage at risk register](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/04/16/01M6KFDE8CKH6FZT62CXTYQWME.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Medway Council has secured a £240,000 grant from the National Lottery Heritage Fund, in addition to £40,000 of council funding and £5,000 from the Dickens Fellowship, to develop plans to restore the building and create a new garden setting.

Grade I-listed Hall’s Croft in Stratford-upon-Avon, Warwickshire, has also been added to the list due to the major conservation challenges it faces.

At Hall’s Croft, where William Shakespeare’s daughter Susanna lived with her husband Dr John Hall, funding is needed to save the 17th-century building’s exterior, including repairing damage caused by a vehicle hitting the front of the structure.

It has already benefited from a £1 million donation from playwright Ken Ludwig, but more funds are needed for the next phase of work, Historic England said.

Other sites added to the heritage at risk register include Hastings Castle in East Sussex – one of the first three castles built by William the Conqueror and depicted in the Bayeux Tapestry, which has become vulnerable in the face of its exposed setting and coastal erosion.

![Hall's Croft, Old Town, Stratford-upon-Avon, Warwickshire](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/04/16/01M6KFCQDCDHK0A9W4CE2758YV.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

A large area of wildlife-damaged moorland in Fylingdales, North Yorkshire, where a blaze last year caused damage to 20 scheduled monuments, six listed buildings and natural landscapes including important peatlands, has also been added to the list.

In better news, a number of historic buildings and sites have been taken off the list as a result of being saved from neglect and decay, including the Jenner Hut, in the garden of The Chantry, Berkeley, Gloucestershire, where Edward Jenner carried out the first smallpox vaccinations in 1796.

Funding from the National Lottery Heritage Fund has meant work has been carried out to save the hut, christened by Dr Jenner as the “Temple of Vaccinia” and seen as the birthplace of vaccination and immunology.

The historic Newlyn Harbour in Cornwall, Loftus conservation area in Cleveland and Redcar, the Grade II\*-listed Severn Wharf Building – part of the Ironbridge Gorge Museum in Shropshire – and Wolterton Hall Park and Gardens landscape in Norfolk have also all been saved.

![Dr Jenner's Hut in the Garden of the Chantry is one of the historic sites that has been taken off the heritage at risk register](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/04/16/01M6KFCMH63M7P9WEAQ3XJ5FNB.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Heritage minister Baroness Twycross said: “Every part of the UK is bursting with history, so it is vital we protect the heritage sites at the heart of communities which help tell the stories of our nation.

“That’s why our £1.5 billion Arts Everywhere Fund is so important, supporting efforts to save more than 1,000 arts venues, museums, libraries, and heritage buildings across England.”

She said it was fantastic to see Newlyn Harbour removed from the list having received a grant from the Heritage at Risk Capital Fund.

“However, this year’s register is an important reminder of the pressures facing our historic environment, from ageing buildings and structural deterioration to the impacts of extreme weather.

I want us to build on our progress to ensure our heritage is protected for generations to come,” she said.
