# DWP £490 boost for millions of state pensioners under 76 confirmed

## The Department for Work and Pensions (DWP) has confirmed a £490 boost for state pensioners on the new, full state pension

The Department for Work and Pensions (DWP) has announced a £490 increase for state pensioners receiving the new, full state pension.

The DWP new state pension applies to men born after 1951 and women born after 1953. This means that by next year, recipients will be aged 76 and below, reports Birmingham Live. The announcement follows Chancellor John Healey's Autumn Budget delivered last week.

Pat McFadden, a Labour Party cabinet member, explained: "I have concluded my statutory annual review of State Pension and benefit rates under the Social Security Administration Act 1992. The new rates will apply in the tax year 2027-28 and will mainly come into effect from 12 April 2027.

"I am pleased to announce that the basic and full rate of the new State Pensions will be increased by 3.9%, in line with the increase in average weekly earnings in the year to May-July 2026. This delivers on our Triple Lock commitment throughout this Parliament to increase these rates in line with the highest of growth in prices, growth in earnings or 2.5%.

"From April, the full annual rate of the new State Pension will increase by around £490. The full annual rate of the basic State Pension will increase by around £375." The increase arrives as the triple lock guarantee remains in place until 2030, after which adjustments will be made. Tom Stevenson, from Fidelity International, said: "This was not a Budget that we will remember for long.

"The financial markets have given the Chancellor a relatively easy ride. Investors have been reassured by the absence of any unpleasant surprises, but no-one has been left excited by the prospects for much stronger growth. The new Chancellor has passed his first test.

"He is fiscally credible. But the longer-term challenges remain. Britain still has high borrowing costs, not much room for manoeuvre and a disappointing growth outlook. But nothing was broken today."

Patrick Thomson, head of research analysis and policy at Standard Life Centre for the Future of Retirement comments: "Confirmation in the budget documents that the State Pension will increase by 3.9% next April will be welcome news for millions of pensioners, but it also means the full new State Pension will rise to £13,036.40, overtaking the frozen £12,570 Personal Allowance.

"However, the government have announced a new Pensions Tax Allowance to address this so those on the low-income pensioners aren't pulled into paying tax simply because their payments has increased." The boost comes as the triple lock remains until 2030, when it will then be adjusted.

Tom Stevenson, from Fidelity International, said: "Those with total income between £12,570 and £13,037 will receive the full Pensions Tax Allowance, worth up to £94 in the first year, while it will then taper away at £1 for every £3 of additional income, meaning some support available up to an income of £13,319.

"This avoids a cliff edge, and the automatic application should make the process easier for people in this income bracket. The policy has been estimated to cost £45m in the first year which is small scale in the scheme of government finances but will be tangible benefit for those affected.

"However, with the State Pension continuing to rise against a frozen Personal Allowance, even relatively small differences in retirement income could lead to different tax outcomes, adding another layer of complexity to the system."
