A woman who drove with a workman on top of her bonnet after a dispute over a parking space avoided jail today.

Tracey Kelly, 57, ploughed into Frank Cochrane in Glasgow's Partick on November 14, 2025.

The pair had earlier exchanged verbals amid claims Mr Cochrane was parked too close to Kelly's car.

Mr Cochrane went on to stand in front of Kelly's vehicle before she drove off and took her victim along with her.

She went on to do an emergency stop which resulted in Mr Cochrane hitting the ground and suffering injuries.

Kelly pleaded guilty at Glasgow Sheriff Court to the assault to Mr Cochrane's severe injury, impairment and danger of life.

Sheriff Michael Hanlon today tagged Kelly for 12 months, keeping her indoors between 7pm and 7am. The tag will be weekend only for the last three months of its duration.

Kelly, of Clydebank, West Dunbartonshire, was also put under supervision for 12 months and disqualified from driving for 27 months.

The court previously heard that Mr Cochrane was working at a property on Hayburn Crescent and his van outside.

Kelly - who was behind the wheel of a Renault Captur - was parked behind Mr Cochrane's van.

Prosecutor Katie Malcolm said: "Kelly got in her vehicle and intended to drive away, however, due to Mr Cochrane's van being parked close to her, she found it difficult to drive out the space.

"This caused an argument between them and Mr Cochrane walked away to continue his work but returned shortly after when the argument continued."

Kelly drove away but Mr Cochrane stood in front of her car to stop her from leaving.

Miss Malcolm stated: "Kelly drove her vehicle which cause it to strike Mr Cochrane on the body.

"Mr Cochrane grabbed hold of the bonnet and Kelly accelerated on the road.

"Mr Cochrane remained on the bonnet as Kelly drove her vehicle.

"She did an emergency stop at the end of the road and Mr Cochrane was thrown from the bonnet onto the road."

Kelly drove way and police as well as an ambulance attended the scene.

Mr Cochrane was taken to hospital and was found to be unable to put weight on his right foot.

There was swelling to his right heel and around his ankle.

It was found that there was a deformity to his heel and his right ankle was put in a cast.

He was given crutches to use for nine weeks and was required to attend hospital visits. Mr Cochrane was also unable to work.

Mobile phone footage of the incident was shown to the court.
