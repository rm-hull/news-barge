A former Durham University lecturer, who went on to become one of the world's leading experts in international law, has been elected as a judge at the International Court of Justice (ICJ).

Professor Dapo Akande taught at Durham Law School from 2000 to 2004, and received an honorary doctorate in civil law from Durham University last July.

He will serve a nine-year term from 2027 to 2036, and is set to be the UK's first ICJ judge since 2017 after receiving support from a majority of United Nations member states in the election.

Akande will play an independent role in the court’s work to uphold international law and resolve disputes between countries.

53-year-old Akande was born and raised in Nigeria, and is now Chichele Professor of Public International Law, and a Fellow of All Souls College, at the University of Oxford.

He is also an elected member of the United Nations International Law Commission.

Professor Akande said: “I am deeply honoured to be elected to the International Court of Justice.

“I am incredibly grateful to all the states that have put their faith in me. I pledge to serve with independence and to deciding cases on the basis of a faithful and impartial application of the law.”

He added: “I look forward to contributing to the ICJ’s vital work in helping to resolve disputes by peaceful means, alongside the other colleagues elected today.”

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21673977.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Akande's ICJ election returns a United Kingdom judge to the court after an absence of almost a decade. *(Image: Wayamo Foundation)*

Akande originally qualified as a lawyer in Nigeria, and has nearly three decades of experience as an international law scholar and practitioner in cases before international courts and tribunals, including the International Court of Justice itself.

He has also advised governments and international organisations, including the United Nations and the African Union, with his work involving states from all five United Nations regional groups.

Professor Akande’s links with Durham stretch back decades, with his grandfather attending Fourah Bay College in Sierra Leone, at a time when its qualifications were awarded by Durham University.

He joined Durham Law School as a lecturer in law in 2000 and remained there until 2004, before moving to teach at Oxford.

Durham University awarded Professor Akande an Honorary Doctor of Civil Law in July 2025, recognising his contribution to international law and his long connection with the university.

At the ceremony, his oration described him as “an eminent scholar, practitioner, and global authority in public international law."

Sedgefield-born actor Mark Gatiss was also among those awarded by Durham University last summer, receiving an honorary doctorate for his contribution to the arts.

Professor Shaun Pattinson, of Durham Law School, said Professor Akande had made "extensive" contributions to international law, and congratulated the British-Nigerian lawyer for his election, praising him for his popular scholarly blog EJIL:Talk!

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21673966.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Professor Shaun Pattinson (right) of Durham Law School, said Professor Akande had made "extensive" contributions to international law, and congratulated the British-Nigerian lawyer for his election. *(Image: Durham University)*

Pattison said he considered Akande's blog "his most significant professional achievement," being read across the global legal community, and hailed him as a "world-class scholar."

Akande's ICJ election returns a United Kingdom judge to the court after an absence of almost a decade. His campaign was supported by the Prime Minister, UK ministers and diplomats working around the world.

Prime Minister Andy Burnham celebrated the decision as a "clear signal to our partners that the UK is back on the world stage," adding that it signified the country was "ready to play our part in ensuring global stability and upholding the rule of law.”

The Prime Minister added: “Professor Akande will bring integrity, legal rigour and independent expertise to the International Court of Justice and I am incredibly proud that he is representing the UK.”

Foreign Secretary Ed Miliband similarly praised the former Durham University lecturer as "impressive," adding that the government was "proud to support his campaign.”

He said: “I am confident that his legal expertise, independence and commitment to international law will make him an asset to the Court and the international community.”

Professor Akande will begin his term in 2027 and serve until 2036, marking a significant milestone for both the UK's law community, and for Durham University, which helped launch Akande's career from the North East to the world's highest court.
