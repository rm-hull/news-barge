Mayor David Skaith has welcomed new legislation that could allow York and North Yorkshire to introduce an overnight visitor levy, describing the proposed charge as a potential “game changer” for the region.

However, the plans have prompted questions from Harrogate’s hospitality sector, with the general manager of the Crown Hotel warning that businesses need a meaningful say in how the levy operates and where the money raised is spent.

The Visitor Levy Bill was introduced in Parliament on 5 November and would give regional mayors powers to introduce a charge on overnight accommodation.

Mayor David Skaith has committed to introducing a levy for York and North Yorkshire, with the intention of using the revenue to support investment in the visitor economy, including more regular bus services, improved public spaces and town centres, and attracting further visitors and investment.

> **David Skaith, Mayor of York and North Yorkshire, said:**
>
> We’re home to historic towns and cities, two of the UK’s best National Parks and a coastline brimming with character.
>
> There’s something for everyone, and we welcome more than 40 million visitors every year. That’s why an Overnight Visitor Levy will be a total gamechanger for our county.
>
> Investing in York and North Yorkshire’s cities, towns and villages to make them a better place to visit, and to live in, is at the heart of why I will be introducing a Levy in our county.

The Mayor is among ten regional mayors who have committed to keeping any visitor levy at 5% or below.

The proposed percentage-based approach is intended to protect lower-cost accommodation, with cheaper stays attracting smaller charges.

Under the legislation, areas wishing to introduce a levy will first need to set out proposals covering rates, thresholds, exemptions and how the money will be spent. An impact assessment will also be required, followed by a local consultation to allow residents and businesses to have their say.

National exemptions are proposed for student accommodation, care homes, shelters and stays of more than 28 days. The Mayor and York and North Yorkshire Combined Authority would also have the ability to introduce further exemptions.

## Government says money will stay local

The Government has presented the legislation as part of its wider programme of fiscal devolution, giving local leaders greater powers to raise and invest money in their areas.

> **Angela Rayner, Secretary of State for Housing, Communities and Local Government, said:**
>
> Millions of people visit our wonderful towns and cities each year, and it’s right that our communities should be able to feel the benefit of that footfall.
>
> Every pound raised through these levies will stay in the local area. Whether that’s better transport, thriving high streets, or more attractions that keep visitors coming back.
>
> This is about giving mayors and local leaders the choice to invest back into what matters most to local people, while protecting lower-cost stays and making sure businesses have their say.

## Crown Hotel boss calls for assurances for Harrogate

Dan Siddle, General Manager of the Crown Hotel in Harrogate, said there was a clear need for continued investment in the visitor economy, but warned that the proposed levy needed careful consideration.

He called on the Mayor to engage with local businesses to ensure the charge was not perceived simply as another tax on the hospitality sector.

> **Dan Siddle, General Manager of the Crown Hotel, said:**
>
> There is no question that Harrogate and the wider region need continued investment in the visitor economy, but the introduction of this levy needs very careful consideration, and I would implore the Mayor to engage with local businesses so the levy isn’t perceived simply as another tax on the hospitality sector.

Mr Siddle pointed to Harrogate’s previous experience with a proposed Accommodation Business Improvement District (BID), which would have created a ring-fenced, locally controlled fund focused on growing overnight stays and strengthening the town as a destination.

That proposal ultimately failed to secure the necessary support from hotel owners, brands and operators.

He said that experience should not be overlooked as discussions move towards a mayoral visitor levy.

He also raised concerns about how much control local businesses would have over the money, pointing to the experience of established BIDs in Manchester and Liverpool.

Mr Siddle argued that there was an important distinction between businesses deciding the level of a charge and how the proceeds are invested, and a levy determined and controlled at regional level.

> **Dan Siddle said:**
>
> For Harrogate, the fundamental question has to be whether money generated by our visitor economy will be demonstrably reinvested back into the destination that generated it.
>
> Harrogate could potentially be a significant contributor to a York and North Yorkshire-wide levy, yet there needs to be confidence that the town and its hospitality businesses receive a proportionate benefit rather than simply providing additional funding for much broader regional priorities.

He also highlighted the financial pressures already facing hotels, including increases in employment costs, business rates and other operating expenses.

An additional charge would also bring administrative and collection responsibilities for accommodation providers.

Mr Siddle said businesses needed meaningful engagement, clear evidence of the economic impact and transparency about where the money would go and how its success would be measured.

> **Dan Siddle said:**
>
> Before progressing, there therefore needs to be meaningful engagement with the businesses expected to collect it, clear evidence of the economic impact, and absolute transparency around where the money will go and how its success will be measured.

The legislation would bring England closer to arrangements already operating in other destinations, including Edinburgh, Manchester, Paris and Barcelona.

For Harrogate, the debate is likely to centre on how a regional visitor levy would translate into tangible benefits for the town’s tourism and hospitality businesses, and how local businesses will be involved in decisions about the money raised.
