The reason for many of the complaints being upheld against Middlesbrough Council has been branded as “stupid”.

The description comes against a backdrop of falling grievances against the council, as the 2024/25 figure of 1,378 fell drastically to 823 in 2025/26. There has also been a fall in the percentage of upheld complaints.

Councillors on Middlesbrough’s audit committee heard about complaints directed at the council between April 2025 and March 2026.

Ann-Marie Johnstone, head of the chief executive’s department, highlighted the different types of complaints and an increase in the number of compliments that the council had received.

She said the council tends to focus on the rate of “upheld complaints” and conceded that the timeliness of dealing with complaints had become “a bit of a challenge”.

Ms Johnstone highlighted a rise in the use of AI, which has not only increased the volume of grumbles, but the complexity of complaints that the council receives – she mentioned a recent complaint which was 100 pages in length, which in turn puts a strain on council resources.

Labour Councillor Lewis Young pointed out a line in the report which said the majority of complaints were upheld due to the service areas not responding within the time requirements, rather than due to failures within the service area itself.

He branded this as a “fairly stupid reason” for the council to have complaints upheld.

He highlighted the sheer contrast in complaint response times from different departments. In 2025/26 in environmental issues, 82 per cent of complaints received responses on time, compared to just 28.8 per cent for education and children’s services, describing the latter as an “appalling response rate”.

Ms Johnstone responded that a number of actions are being taken, including recruiting an additional officer, and raising the issue with managers where there is a “concerning trend”.

She said there are now monthly meetings in place with children’s services, specifically focussed on the matter of complaints.

Fellow Labour Councillor David Branson meanwhile was looking at comparisons to neighbouring councils within the Tees Valley, which measured the number of complaints to the Local Government Ombudsman.

He remarked that Middlesbrough had the “worst performance” out of all five local councils. Cllr Branson queried if this was down to not having the same amount of money to spend on dealing with problems, or due to having more problems.

During the meeting, held on Thursday, October 29, Ms Johnstone directed Cllr Branson to the number of upheld decisions, per 100,000 residents. Under this metric, Middlesbrough is middle-ranking, coming third out of the five local authorities.

As for planned actions in 2026/27, papers added: “The council remains in the process of implementing a new complaints management system, which is expected to increase automation and improve the speed and ease of complaints management. Implementation has been significantly delayed, but the system is expected to be introduced during 2026/27 following a technical review from ICT.”
