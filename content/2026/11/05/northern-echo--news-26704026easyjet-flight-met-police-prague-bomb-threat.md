An easyJet flight from Newcastle Airport was met by police in Prague on Thursday morning after an anonymous bomb threat prompted a major security response and disruption to flights across Europe.

At least a dozen aircraft were reportedly diverted or investigated after receiving threats, with several flights arriving in the Czech capital among those affected.

Passengers travelling from Newcastle to Prague safely disembarked after their aircraft landed and were directed to a remote stand while security checks were carried out.

Prague Airport said an anonymous warning had been received relating to a number of incoming flights.

A spokesperson said: “This morning, an anonymous threat was received concerning the possible presence of explosive devices on board selected flights arriving from Dublin, Glasgow, Athens and Newcastle.

“In accordance with the Airport Emergency Plan, the Security Operations Command Center was activated under the direction of the Police of the Czech Republic.

“The aircraft landed safely in Prague and were directed to remote stands. Following their arrival, the Police of the Czech Republic conducted security searches of the aircraft for explosives, all with negative results.

“All passengers safely disembarked from the aircraft and received their baggage once all necessary security measures had been completed.

“The situation had no significant impact on other airport operations.

“The threat is currently under police investigation."

An easyJet spokesperson said: “An easyJet flight from Newcastle was met by police on arrival in Prague as a precautionary measure due to a security related matter.

“Passengers on both flights disembarked normally.
