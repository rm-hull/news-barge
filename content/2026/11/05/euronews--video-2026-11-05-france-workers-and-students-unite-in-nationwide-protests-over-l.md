France was gripped by a **nationwide day of strikes and demonstrations** on Thursday 5 November, as trade unions mobilised workers, students and other protesters to demand**higher wages, tax justice and better public services**.

By 1 pm, the Interior Ministry had counted **270,000 demonstrators across 180 marches** outside Paris, while the CGT reported more than 250,000 participants in 73 cities. Around**70,000 police officers and gendarmes were deployed nationwide**, 81 secondary schools were blockaded and** 15.81% of teachers joined the strike**.

In Marseille, thousands marched from Les Réformés towards Place Castellane, waving union flags and lighting flares as police monitored the procession. **Seventeen metro stations were closed**.

In Nantes, **around 30,000 people took to the streets** according to organisers, compared with a police estimate of 24,000, after unions secured permission to march through the city centre.

In Paris, tensions erupted outside Lycée Honoré-de-Balzac in the 17th arrondissement, where **police used tear gas to disperse students** protesting a lack of resources for education.

Footage showed a firework exploding above the crowd, cardboard burning in a bin outside the school gates and officers moving in to clear the area. Police also **dismantled another student blockade** on Rue de Rivoli. The capital's**main union march set off from Place de la République at 2 pm**, following a route imposed by authorities towards Place de la Nation.

The mobilisation marked **another major test of the autumn protest movement**, bringing together workers, students and public-sector employees amid** growing demands for improved pay and working conditions**.
