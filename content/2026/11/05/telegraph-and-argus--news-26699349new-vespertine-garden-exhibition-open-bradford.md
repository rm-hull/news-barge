A GLOWING twilight landscape filled with UV-lit sculptures is set to transport visitors into the hidden world of Bradford’s urban wildlife.

Vespertine Garden, an immersive new exhibition, will open at South Square Centre in Thornton on Friday, November 6, 2026, featuring sculptural plants and creatures brought to life with the help of more than 1,000 schoolchildren and community groups from across the district.

The artwork has been created in collaboration with professional artists Amy and Emma-Jo Bairstow, who led workshops with children and community members to explore themes of biodiversity and the natural environment.

Ryan Hughes, director at South Square Centre, said: "Vespertine Garden reflects our commitment to making contemporary visual arts accessible to local communities and creating opportunities for people to work alongside professional artists on ambitious creative projects.

"What's been particularly special about this project is seeing children and young people become part of the creative process.

"Through the workshops, they've been exploring the wildlife that lives alongside us, thinking about how we can protect it and using their imaginations to help bring this exhibition to life."

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21670785.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Local community group, the Wildlife Boys, enjoy a sound walk *(Image: Matt Radcliffe Photography)*

The exhibition explores the wildlife that emerges at dusk, from bats and moths to hedgehogs and foxes, and highlights the need to protect the ecosystems that surround us.

Visitors will step into a dreamlike twilight garden illuminated by UV lighting and accompanied by an original soundscape inspired by the local environment.

Mr Hughes said: "This is the first exhibition I'm overseeing as Director and I’m so excited to share the finished installation with our community.

"We hope it will be a magical experience for visitors of all ages, but also one that encourages people to look a little more closely at the natural world around them and think about how we can care for it."

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21670786.jpg?type=mds-article-620)School children creating sculptures for the exhibition *(Image: Matt Radcliffe Photography)*

Vespertine Garden brings together Amy and Emma-Jo Bairstow, sound artist Fran Bundey and filmmaker Matt Radcliffe in a multidisciplinary collaboration that combines sculpture, light, video and sound.

The installation uses recycled materials, including paper and cardboard, as part of its environmental message.

Funded by Arts Council England and Bradford Culture Company, the exhibition was inspired by Neon Garden, previously presented by Art Works at Barnsley Civic Centre.

The public is invited to experience Vespertine Garden at a special opening evening on Friday, November 6, 2026.

Entry to the Main Gallery will be via timed slots, and advance booking is recommended.

For more information and to book tickets, visit southsquarecentre.co.uk.
