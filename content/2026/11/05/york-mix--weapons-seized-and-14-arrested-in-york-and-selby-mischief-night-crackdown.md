Fourteen people were arrested and a number of weapons and drugs seized as police staged a Mischief Night crackdown in York and Selby.

In the latest North Yorkshire Police Operation Tornado blitz, drug dealing and shop crime were the targets.

The operation coincided with Mischief Night, 4 November. Long associated with harmless pranks, “in recent years it has also provided opportunities for a minority of people to commit anti-social behaviour, criminal damage and low-level disorder,” police said.

Officers carried out enhanced patrols across York and Selby, focusing on known anti-social behaviour hotspots.

With shop theft offences typically increasing as the festive season approaches, officers also conducted patrols at out-of-town retail parks using plain-clothed officers and unmarked vehicles.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/police-operation-tornado-mischief-night-4-nov-2026-nyp-2.webp)

The operation was aimed at deterring offenders, gathering intelligence and supporting retailers.

A 19-year-old woman and a 32-year-old man were arrested in a York hotel room during the operation. Police recovered around 100 wraps of suspected crack cocaine and around £1,000 in cash.

The pair have been charged with supplying class A drugs, possession of an offensive weapon in a private place and possession of criminal property.

During the Operation Tornado day of action, officers:

* Made 14 arrests across the York and Selby area
* Executed four warrants, leading to the recovery of cannabis, suspected Class A drugs and weapons
* Recovered about £150,000 worth of stolen property from a lock-up in West Yorkshire as part of an investigation into the theft of an HGV trailer from Selby
* Arrested three of the area’s most wanted offenders who were wanted for recall to prison
* Seized two knives, four swords and an illegally-owned air rifle.

## New police e-bikes

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/police-operation-tornado-mischief-night-4-nov-2026-nyp-3.webp)

North Yorkshire Police also trialled their new e-bikes during the day.

Capable of speeds of up to 15.5mph, the electric bikes “give neighbourhood policing teams greater flexibility to patrol communities, engage with the public and respond more quickly across busy areas”, police say.

Chief Constable Tim Forber joined officers as the bikes were deployed for their first operational day in York city centre.

Chief Constable Forber said: “By combining high-visibility patrols, proactive operations, educational engagement and targeted enforcement activity, we are taking action against those who seek to cause harm while also working to prevent problems before they occur.

“Mischief Night should not be used as an excuse for criminality or anti-social behaviour. Through operations like this, we are sending a clear message that we will take action against those who make life a misery for others, while continuing to support and protect our communities.

“While Operation Tornado provides an opportunity to bring together a wide range of policing activity into a dedicated day of action, it mirrors the work our officers and staff carry out every day across North Yorkshire to prevent crime, target offenders and keep people safe.”
