**Campaigners have blamed a “small band of dedicated whingers” for the council's decision to shelve plans to extend the capital’s tram network to Granton.**

Looking for Growth (LFG), a London-headquartered lobby group with branches in Edinburgh and Glasgow, has urged councillors to reverse the decision, which they say will negatively impact residents in the north of the city.

“People in Granton have been waiting two decades for their tram to arrive,” said Alan Grant, a member of the local LFG chapter.

“Lately, a small but influential group of noisy NIMBYs has threatened the future of Granton’s tram,” he added.

“Most objections concerned the Roseburn Path, which they claimed would be put at risk. Often omitted from their criticism was the fact that the path was a railway line until the 1960s and that it was always intended to transport people around the city again.

“Its use as a walkway was meant to be temporary, and restoring a transport connection would fulfil its original purpose.”

![man](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21674883.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Alan Grant is a member of LFG Edinburgh. *(Image: Supplied)*

A council-run consultation published in June found that 68 per cent of respondents opposed building the tram line along the popular green corridor.

However, market research revealed that 23 per cent of city residents opposed the Roseburn Path route, while 43 per cent supported it.

Speaking to The Herald earlier this year, campaigner Euan Baxter said: “These results show overwhelming opposition to the council’s proposal to take a tram down the Roseburn Path.

“We are now calling on the administration to listen to residents and drop any plan to use the Roseburn Path for a tram line before any more public money is wasted.”

At a meeting of the Transport and Environment Committee in September, councillors took a similar approach, agreeing to put the northern tram line on ice while focusing efforts on a southern spur towards the Edinburgh BioQuarter.

The SNP, Labour, and Scottish Greens joined forces to support the proposals, while the Lib Dems, who wanted the use of the Roseburn Path to be explicitly ruled out, voted against the motion, as did the Conservatives, who opposed any public money being spent on the tram extension.

Speaking about the potential for a city-wide tram network, Mr Grant said: “Edinburgh is one of Europe’s fastest growing cities, and it’s easy to see why.

“There’s so much to love here. Our next step is obvious: a tram route running all the way from the north of the city to the south.

“Granton has seen considerable development and investment lately, including new homes and places of business.

![The Roseburn Path is a green haven](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/19499060.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The Roseburn Path is popular with cyclists and walkers. *(Image: Gordon Terris)*

“But it’s unfair and unreasonable to expect people to live there while remaining reliant on an overcrowded and stretched bus network.”

“Building the tram would improve access to jobs, education, and opportunities across the city.”

The group has recruited Edinburgh North and Leith MP Tracy Gilbert to their cause, as well as national politicians like Dominic Cummings and Morgan McSweeney.

“The tram to the north is the key to unlocking growth for communities who need it most,” Ms Gilbert said earlier this week.

“Politicians should make decisions that benefit all our citizens, particularly the most left behind.”

“We look around and see a malaise that has characterised a whole generation. In the modern UK, getting a data centre, tram line, or nuclear power plant built seems to take ages, cost a fortune,” said Mr Grant.

“Everything, it seems, must be subjected to myopic and trivial NIMBYism, complaints, negativity, and nebbish busybodies whose favourite word is ‘no’.

“We want to change that,” he added. “We believe growth is the answer and that Britain can grow its way out of the sad state those who govern have got us into.”

“Whether it’s a cleaner street or a major infrastructure project, the principle is the same. We should be making our communities better places to live, work, and invest in, rather than finding endless excuses for doing nothing.”
