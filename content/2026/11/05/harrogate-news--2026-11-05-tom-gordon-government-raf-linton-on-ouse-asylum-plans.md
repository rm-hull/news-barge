Tom Gordon, MP for Harrogate and Knaresborough, is to continue pressing the Government over proposals to accommodate up to 1,200 asylum seekers at RAF Linton-on-Ouse, saying he is not satisfied with the response given by Asylum and Immigration Minister Anna Turley in the House of Commons.

Mr Gordon raised concerns during an exchange with the Minister on 3 November about the suitability of the former RAF base, including local infrastructure, public transport and access to healthcare.

Following the exchange, Mr Gordon’s office confirmed that he did not consider the Minister’s response satisfactory and believed insufficient weight was being given to the opposition expressed by North Yorkshire Council and the Mayor of York and North Yorkshire.

He intends to continue raising the matter with the Government, working alongside other MPs, including Liberal Democrat MP Calum Miller, who has similar proposals affecting his constituency.

Mr Gordon also maintains that the Government should publish the evidence behind its decision to reopen the site, together with the full costs of bringing it back into operation.

## Concerns over the suitability of the site

The proposed use of RAF Linton-on-Ouse has attracted opposition from residents and the Linton-on-Ouse Action Group, which argues that the location is unsuitable for accommodation on the proposed scale.

The group says the village has approximately 600 adult residents and 100 children, meaning accommodation for 1,200 people would represent a substantial addition to the local population.

During the Commons exchange, Mr Gordon raised concerns about limited public transport, access to healthcare and the capacity of water, sewerage, drainage and electricity infrastructure.

He also said that much of the infrastructure had been removed following the abandonment of an earlier Home Office proposal for the site in 2022.

## Disagreement over how close the site is to the village

During the exchange, Anna Turley challenged Mr Gordon’s description of the base’s relationship with the village.

According to the Linton-on-Ouse Action Group, the Minister said the asylum seekers would not be housed in the community, but on a specific, self-contained site away from the community.

The Action Group disputes that characterisation. It says the main entrance to the former RAF base, which it understands will be used by asylum seekers entering and leaving the site, is approximately 22 steps from the first family home on a nearby residential estate.

The group argues that the site is therefore effectively within the village rather than separated from it.

## Action Group questions the projected costs

The Linton-on-Ouse Action Group is also calling for the Government to publish the full financial assessment behind the proposal.

In a statement issued following the parliamentary exchange, the group questioned the estimated operating costs and called for transparency over the cost per person per night.

It cites an estimated range of £527 to £853 per person per night, compared with what it describes as an average hotel accommodation cost of £140.

These figures are estimates presented by the Action Group and have not been independently verified for this report.

The group has also published an updated cost briefing, including an October 2026 addendum addressing what it says are increased costs arising from the limited operational time window.

## Calls for wider consultation and government scrutiny

The Action Group has set out a number of demands, including publication of the infrastructure assessments, financial calculations and operational costs associated with reopening the base.

It is also calling for scrutiny of the potential demands on healthcare, policing and fire services, and for engagement with the local community before any final decision is made.

The group has separately called on Andy Burnham to honour what it says was a public commitment made on 27 September to listen to all stakeholders and support cross-party consultation.

It has also highlighted that Sir Alec Shelbrooke, MP for Wetherby and Easingwold, has written to the Home Office about the proposal and is awaiting a response.

## Pressure on Government to publish the evidence

Mr Gordon’s decision to continue pressing the Government means the issue is likely to remain on the parliamentary agenda.

His focus is on obtaining the evidence behind the choice of Linton-on-Ouse, understanding the full financial implications and ensuring that the objections raised by local authorities and the Mayor are properly considered.

The Government has yet to satisfy those seeking a fuller explanation of the site’s suitability and the costs involved.

For residents of Linton-on-Ouse and the surrounding area, the central questions remain whether the site’s infrastructure can support accommodation on this scale, what additional pressure would be placed on local services and whether the financial case for reopening the base has been properly established.

Mr Gordon says he intends to continue seeking answers from the Government, working with MPs who share concerns about asylum accommodation proposals in their own constituencies.
