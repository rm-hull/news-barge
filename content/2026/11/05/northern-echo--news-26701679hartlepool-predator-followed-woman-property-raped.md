A North East rapist who ignored a woman's repeated pleas to stop will spend the next nine years behind bars.

Jack Crannage, 27, of Hartlepool, attacked the woman after following her into a property.

The victim repeatedly told Crannage "no", but he removed her clothing and raped her, a court heard.

Following the attack, the woman managed to escape through a window and contacted police.

Crannage was arrested by officers on the same day and taken into custody for questioning.

After a three-day trial, he was found guilty of rape and appeared before Teesside Crown Court on November 2, where he was sentenced to nine years behind bars.

The court also imposed an indefinite restraining order.

Detective Constable Gareth Davies, from Cleveland Police's Protecting Vulnerable People team, praised the victim's courage throughout the investigation and trial.

He said: "Crannage took advantage of the woman and raped her despite saying no to him.

"She was so frightened, that afterwards she escaped through a window where she managed to seek safety with a friend until police arrived.

"Despite causing her pain, humiliation and leaving her deeply affected by his actions she has remained brave and courageous throughout the entire criminal process including the ordeal of a trial.

"I want to take this opportunity to recognise her courage and know that she was listened to and supported."

Anyone who has experienced rape or sexual abuse can report it to Cleveland Police by calling 101, or 999 in an emergency.

Support is also available from specialist services including Teesside Sexual Assault Referral Centre (SARC), which provides 24-hour crisis intervention and support regardless of whether an offence has been reported to police.

Victims and survivors can also seek help from organisations including A Way Out, Barnardo's and Arch, while those affected by child sexual abuse or exploitation can access support through the IAmReady portal.
