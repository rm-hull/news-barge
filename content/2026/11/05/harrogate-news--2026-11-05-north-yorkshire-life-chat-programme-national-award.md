A North Yorkshire Council service that helps people overcome barriers to employment, learning and improved wellbeing has received a national award.

The council’s adult learning service won the Healthier Futures Award at the Get the Nation Learning Awards 2026 on Wednesday 4 November for its Life Chat programme.

The programme offers one-to-one conversations to help residents identify their strengths, consider their ambitions and find ways to address challenges affecting their ability to work or access learning.

In its first five months, Life Chats reached 1,098 people. Of these, 166 progressed into learning and 169 began their journey back into employment.

The service works with health services, Talking Therapies, Jobcentre Plus and community organisations to support people experiencing issues including poor mental health, social isolation and long-term economic inactivity.

## Support to return to work

One person to benefit from the programme is Amelia Davies, 51, from Reeth, who was looking for work that could fit around caring for her two children, both of whom have additional needs.

After attending a Life Chat session, she said she felt more confident about finding suitable employment. She subsequently secured a flexible work placement with a local business.

> **Amelia Davies said:**
>
> It was really refreshing to talk to someone who didn’t know me. I have a child with a disability and additional needs, as well as another with special educational needs, and there are times when it can be challenging.
>
> I didn’t want to burden family and friends with my worries about finding work, so being able to speak openly with someone independent was invaluable.
>
> After the chat I felt really optimistic. I got really good advice and felt hopeful that I would find something I could do part-time, which I did.

The programme also supported Trevor Knight, from Northallerton, who was looking for a new job after experiencing difficulties commuting to his previous role in Durham.

Following a period of personal difficulty after his mother’s death, he contacted Life Chat for help with job applications. He received one-to-one support from a skills and engagement officer at Northallerton Library and help to develop an action plan.

He has since secured a position as a business support officer with North Yorkshire Council’s planning service.

> **Trevor Knight said:**
>
> I saw Life Chat online and thought I would reach out as I was struggling to apply for jobs without a laptop and when I was applying for roles, I wasn’t being shortlisted, so I wanted some help around that.
>
> I would definitely recommend it. I found it really helpful for somebody to point me in the right direction.

## National recognition

North Yorkshire Council’s executive member for education, learning and skills, Cllr Annabel Wilkinson, said the award recognised the work of the adult learning service and its impact on residents.

> **Cllr Annabel Wilkinson said:**
>
> This award is a fantastic recognition of the life-changing difference that Adult Learning North Yorkshire is making to residents across our county.
>
> By helping learners develop new skills, build confidence and make positive changes to their health and wellbeing, we’re supporting people to create healthier, happier and more fulfilling futures.
>
> It is wonderful to see the hard work of learners, tutors and the wider team recognised at a national level and I am delighted that North Yorkshire is leading the way in showing what adult learning can achieve.

Life Chats form part of the York and North Yorkshire Combined Authority’s Work and Skills Interchange, which connects residents and employers with employment, health and skills support.

The programme is supported by the Combined Authority using funding from the Department for Work and Pensions’ Get Britain Working Economic Inactivity Trailblazer.

Mayor of York and North Yorkshire, David Skaith, said the authority was proud to have funded the programme and highlighted the difference it had made to residents.

More information about Adult Learning North Yorkshire and Life Chats is available from the North Yorkshire Council website.
