Textiles have long been associated with domestic life and traditional craft, but a major new exhibition at York Art Gallery will explore how women artists have used fabric and other tactile materials to challenge those expectations.

**Interwoven: Stories of Women, Textiles and Art** opens on 5 February 2027, bringing together works from the gallery’s own collection with loans from artists including Anna Perach, Lubaina Himid and Magdalena Abakanowicz.

The exhibition will examine the use of textile materials in contemporary art, bringing together artists whose work challenges conventional ideas about the status of textile art and its association with domesticity.

Alongside Interwoven, the gallery will host **Relative Ties: Mabel Nicholson, Nancy Nicholson, EQ Nicholson and Louisa Creed**, an exhibition touring from the Women’s Art Collection at Murray Edwards College, University of Cambridge.

## Three generations of women artists

Relative Ties follows the artistic work of four women from the same family across more than 100 years. It includes works on paper, fabric, rugs, interior design, wallpaper, letterpress design and paintings.

The exhibition will also mark a return to York for Nancy and EQ Nicholson, who received some of their first recognition at the gallery’s 1988 exhibition, The Nicholsons: A Story of Four People and Their Designs.

One of the works on display will be a painting by Mabel Nicholson, *Nancy in the Tartan Frock*, dating from around 1910. Previously thought to have been lost, the painting was discovered in a private collection and will be shown publicly for the first time in more than a century.

The subject is Mabel Nicholson’s daughter, Nancy, adding another connection to the family story explored by the exhibition.

## Exploring women’s creative lives

Livia Turnbull, Curator of Contemporary Art at York Art Gallery, said the exhibitions would provide both contemporary and historical perspectives on women’s artistic practice.

> **Livia Turnbull, Curator of Contemporary Art at York Art Gallery, said:**
>
> Interwoven has been an absolute pleasure to curate so far, working with artists who create striking, bold, and ambitious artworks which explore and challenge the status of textile art, and its association with domesticity.
>
> We are so pleased to be working with the Women’s Art Collection, to bring the Relative Ties exhibition to the north of England. It tells an important story of the shifting expectations placed on the Nicholson women and how family shaped their creative output.
>
> Hosting Relative Ties gives historical context and depth to the contemporary artworks that will be on display in York Art Gallery’s exhibition Interwoven.
>
> The complementary exhibits bring out strong threads of female strength and creativity across generations and mediums in an exciting and insightful exploration of craft and art. The exhibition is an opportunity to give space to emerging and established artists bringing another perspective on contemporary art and material practices.

Harriet Loffler, Director of the Women’s Art Collection, said bringing Relative Ties to York was particularly significant because of the gallery’s previous connection with the Nicholson family.

> **Harriet Loffler, Director of the Women’s Art Collection, said:**
>
> I am thrilled that the Relative Ties exhibition will tour to York Art Gallery. This represents a homecoming as the gallery hosted the landmark 1988 exhibition showcasing the remarkable work of the artist sisters-in-law Nancy Nicholson and EQ Nicholson.
>
> The connection is strengthened further by the fact that Louisa Creed, EQ’s daughter, has lived in York for decades and is a key figure in the artistic and creative life of the city.
>
> We hope visitors will flock to see this remarkable exhibition that considers what we inherit from our mothers, what happens when we collaborate with our siblings and what gets passed on to our daughters.

## Exhibition dates and admission

Both exhibitions will be on display at York Art Gallery from **5 February to 6 June 2027** and are included in general admission.

York Art Gallery is open Wednesday to Sunday, 10am to 5pm.
