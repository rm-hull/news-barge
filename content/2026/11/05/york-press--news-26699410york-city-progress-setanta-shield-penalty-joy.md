IN today's series of subscriber-only nostalgia deep dives, we look back at a time when York City secured a dramatic penalty shootout win over Mansfield Town to progress in the Setanta Shield.

On this day in 2008, York City ended their recent run of poor form by securing a dramatic penalty shoot-out victory over Mansfield Town in the Setanta Shield.

The Minstermen had struggled to find the net in their previous three matches, but Mark Greaves’ deflected header in the fourth minute of stoppage time ensured they would not go scoreless again.

This win marked a significant moment for City boss Colin Walker, as it allowed him to finally overcome his predecessor, Billy McEwan, after three unsuccessful attempts against the Stags earlier in the season.

York City’s attacking display was notably improved, registering 11 shots on target, in stark contrast to their previous two matches, where they had only managed to force one save from the opposition goalkeeper.

Although new striker Bruce Dyer had exited the pitch before the shoot-out, his presence had a notable impact on the game.

The former million-pound man showcased his strength and composure, helping to set the tone for a more assertive attacking performance from the Minstermen.

Steven Hogg and Andy McWilliams, both making their first starts of the season, also played crucial roles in the match.

Despite Mansfield resting key players for the third round tie, they still fielded a strong side, featuring one-time York loan target Emile Sinclair.

Sinclair opened the scoring just 42 seconds into the second-half, capitalising on a lapse in the City defence to poke the ball past goalkeeper Michael Ingham.

York had earlier started the match with intent, as Ben Purkiss sprinted down the right flank, delivering a cross that Onome Sodje headed wide just 17 seconds into the game.

Dyer tested Mansfield goalkeeper Paddy Gamble with a low shot after powering past Stags defender Alan O’Hare, while Ingham remained alert to watch a dangerous free-kick from Alex Jeannin bend wide.

On 11 minutes, David McGurk nearly found the net, but his close-range effort was cleared off the line following a corner from Purkiss.

As the first-half progressed, Hogg took a long-range shot that challenged Gamble, while Hurren squandered an opportunity by heading against the roof of the David Longhurst Stand.

McWilliams came close to scoring just before half-time, but his volley from a deep cross by Purkiss struck the crossbar.

However, Mansfield took the lead early in the second-half, with Sinclair racing clear and beating Ingham to Aaron O'Connor's through ball, poking it into the bottom corner of the net.

The Minstermen were unfortunate not to equalise on 67 minutes, as Jeannin cleared an acrobatic overhead kick by Sodje off the line, followed by another clearance for Adam Boyes’ follow-up effort.

Ingham made a crucial save in the third minute of stoppage time, denying Sinclair's powerful run into the penalty box.

The City goalkeeper's presence during Hogg’s last-gasp corner unsettled the Mansfield defence, leading to Greaves’ late header that secured the draw.

Boyes almost won the tie before penalties, but his shot went wide from ten yards.

The penalty shoot-out began with Craig Farrell missing his attempt, while Stallard's shot hit the woodwork.

However, the Minstermen regained composure as teenage Boyes scored confidently, and Ingham saved Jeannin's weak effort.

Sodje and D'Laryea both converted their penalties, while Hogg scored firmly into the roof of the net.

Rusk ultimately sealed the victory by scoring and sending City into the next round of the competition.

Also on this day, York have been involved in a number of FA Cup ties.

They enter the competition this Saturday when hosting Northampton Town at the LNER Community Stadium, but have had mixed results in the FA Cup in recent years on this day.

In 2022, City headed to a Shrewsbury Town side two leagues above them - and gave their hosts quite the scare.

Jordan Shipley had fired the Salop ahead after just two minutes, and Luke Leahy netted from the spot in first-half stoppage time.

Just two minutes later though, Manny Duku, who had given away the penalty, went from zero to hero as he halved the deficit.

Shrewsbury survived some late pressure from National League York to advance to the next round of the FA Cup.

On this day in 2012, there was also disappointment when City hosted AFC Wimbledon in the FA Cup.

A 1-1 draw, thanks to goals from Jamie Reed and Wimbledon's Charlie Strutton, meant York had to embark on a 450 mile midweek trip for a replay.

There have also been a host of league fixtures on this day in past years.

Just last year, City welcomed Gateshead to the LNER Community Stadium and eased to a 4-0 win after goals from Josh Stones, Joe Felix, Alex Hunt and Ollie Pearce.

In 2017, strikes from Jon Parkin and Dan Parslow meant Joe Guest's goal for Curzon Ashton wasn't enough to cause an upset at Bootham Crescent.

Finally, in 2011 City and then-Blue Square Premier leaders Wrexham locked horns at Bootham Crescent, and played out a goalless draw.

Away from York City, Selby Town were left surprised after former Prime Minister Boris Johnson sent the club a video message saying: "Go the Robins and here's to another 100 glorious years!"

It was a time where Selby were celebrating their centurion year, with former commercial manager, and now chairman, Dave Haddock commenting: "There is a lady who lives locally in Selby called Judith who is well known throughout the town.

"She has her own video blog which she shares online through Selby Facebook groups, and I saw that she was wanting shout-out requests for a video she was making.

"I thought it’d be amusing to have Judith shout out my friend's name on her Hallowe'en video blog, and after she did so, my friend Simon said he would repay me by getting someone bigger and better to give me a shout out.

"Then yesterday morning he sends me a link to a video from former X-Factor star - Wagner who does indeed give me and the club a shout out.

"I shared the video via Selby Town’s social media and like myself, other people loved it as Wagner was such a funny (and very random!) person to be talking about the club.

"Later that morning I saw that our local Selby MP Nigel Adams had liked and shared the video on Twitter, and I joked to Simon that Nigel might do a video next or he might ask Boris to do one.

"Just a few short hours later I started to receive notifications about a social media post...and sure enough Nigel had recorded us a shout out video from him and Prime Minister Boris Johnson!

"Now politics aside, it doesn’t matter who you vote for or what you think of any politicians - getting a message from the Prime Minister of the country wishing us well was amazing!

"We’ve gone from Judith working in a Selby Supermarket to the Prime Minster of the UK via Wagner formerly of the X-Factor, so now the question is, who next?"
