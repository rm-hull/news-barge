A proposal for Stockton Council to buy Billingham town centre for £13m has been approved by leading councillors.

Stockton Council’s cabinet held a special meeting last night (November 4) to decide on the proposed purchase of Billingham town centre from its current owners, Sheet Anchor Evolve.

Leaders hope the decision will mark an end to years of uncertainty and underinvestment from a string of private owners, halt the town centre’s decline and pave the way for future regeneration.

Councillor Paul Rowling, the council’s deputy leader and cabinet member for regeneration and resources, has said there were both risks and opportunities of buying the town centre, but highlighted an overarching concern that without “taking back control” of the area, nothing would change.

![Councillor Paul Rowling from Stockton Council. Picture: LDR.](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21514357.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Councillor Paul Rowling from Stockton Council. Picture: LDR.

He said the town centre was “quite run down” and government funding which would be used to buy it needed to be spent by March 2028, “so this is very much the only game in town if we want to do it”.

He told the cabinet meeting tonight the plans would “open a new chapter” for the town. He said: “We can either take back control for the people of Billingham or do nothing and allow Billingham town centre to continue to decline.”

The council’s report said “all-encompassing” public sector intervention was needed to “reinvigorate and rejuvenate the town centre”, improve its appearance, bring in more people and investment, reconnect residents to it and instil pride in the town.

It said the council considered factors including income, yield, costs, risks, consequences, implications of delay, legal advice and value for money in meeting Sheet Anchor Evolve’s £13m asking price, £2.25m over a £10.75m general market pricing given to the council.

The report says: “The estimated overall cost of acquiring Billingham town centre and undertaking a programme of securing vacant possession for demolition, repair and regeneration works is approximately £30m.”

It says the council wants to buy the town centre from SAE and “develop a scheme to rationalise and shore up retail provision and develop other uses for the town centre”, using £20m of local regeneration funding from the government for the purchase, first phase of works, demolitions and urgent repair and maintenance.

"It proposes to draw up a full business case and use another £10m of Tees Valley Combined Authority (TVCA) funding for a second phase of regeneration.

The council plans to start demolitions with Queensway South, the former library and clinic site, move tenants, begin “a programme of community engagement to refresh the vision for the regeneration of Billingham town centre”, and set up a Billingham Regeneration Board in the first phase, and bring a proposed regeneration scheme to cabinet later.

“The future uses of the cleared sites will be informed by public and stakeholder engagement and will respond to market and investment interest. This will be shaped by a revised masterplan,” says the report, with further plans proposed for 2027-8.

“There is no clear alternative achievable route to delivering regeneration,” adds the report. It says if the town centre were sold to an alternative buyer or transferred, “the likely outcome is a continued lack of investment and continued decline”, risking the £20m regeneration money being returned unspent.

The cabinet agreed to pass all 15 recommendations.
