A report has revealed substantial year-on-year increases in the issuing of child protection plans in Middlesbrough and Redcar and Cleveland.

At the end of 2025/26 there were 467 children in Middlesbrough subject to a plan, an increase of 26 per cent from 12 months earlier.

The equivalent rate in March 2026 per 10,000 population was more than three times the rate across the country.

In Redcar and Cleveland there were 251 children on a child protection plan, an increase on the previous year of 32.9 per cent.

Here the rate per 10,000 population was more than twice that in England.

Child protection plans are formal agreements put in place by local authority children’s services and other professionals when a child is deemed at risk of, or has suffered, significant harm.

They aim to keep children safe, support their health and development, and help parents or carers to make positive changes.

The figures were contained in an annual report by the South Tees Safeguarding Children Partnership (STSCP), a collaboration between Middlesbrough and Redcar and Cleveland councils, Cleveland Police and the North East Integrated Care Board, known as safeguarding statutory partners.

The partnership oversees multi-agency safeguarding arrangements and involves other relevant organisations and agencies, such as public health, local hospital trusts and the probation service.

The report also said more requests for support on child welfare issues were being received – more than 2,000 a month in Middlesbrough, a 23 per cent increase, and more than a thousand in Redcar and Cleveland, a 9.9 per cent increase.

Danielle Swainston, Redcar and Cleveland Council’s executive director for children and young families, who chairs the STSCP, said domestic abuse, substance misuse, parental mental health and child exploitation were all factors in the rising demand being experienced.

She said: “Partners are working together to implement the government’s social care reform called ‘Families First’.

“This requires areas to develop a family help offer with focuses on supporting families early and also using multi agency child protection teams to introduce a specialist team to work with families to enable children to safely stay at home.

“Early findings in pilot areas across the country show that this approach can reduce the need for crisis intervention.

“We will be monitoring our progress to understand whether this new approach reduces the number of families needing child protection support.”

In a foreword to the report, Mrs Swainston wrote: “This has been a year of significant development and learning for the partnership.

“Against a backdrop of increasing demand for children’s services, high levels of deprivation and growing complexity in safeguarding need, partners have continued to work collaboratively to strengthen our arrangements and improve the quality and effectiveness of our response to children and families.

“Our priorities of neglect, exploitation, empowering young people and strengthening partnership working have remained at the heart of our activity.

“We have launched and embedded the South Tees Neglect Strategy, delivering extensive practitioner engagement and training, improving awareness of cumulative neglect and supporting earlier intervention for children and families.

“We have also continued to strengthen our response to child exploitation through the development of a Tees-wide Harm Outside the Home approach, creating greater consistency, shared learning and improved multi-agency practice across the region.”

She added: “By working together, sharing responsibility and maintaining relentless focus on improving outcomes, we can continue to build a safeguarding system that keeps children safe and helps them thrive.”

The report said Middlesbrough and Redcar and Cleveland local authorities had “high aspirations” for children and young people, but both areas had “significant challenges” to address.

Key drivers in terms of demand for children’s social care included deprivation, poor housing, and high levels of unemployment.

According to the Department for Education, Middlesbrough has the highest level of need in this regard in the country, with Redcar and Cleveland placed fifth.

Meanwhile, local area child vulnerability profiles produced by the Children’s Commissioner show that 574 children across South Tees aged 0 to 17 years (of which 220 are aged 0 to 4) are estimated to live in households where issues with alcohol/substance misuse, domestic abuse and mental health problems are all present.

These factors known as the ‘trio of vulnerability’ have been shown, when combined, to put children noticeably at greater risk of immediate harm, as well as having a detrimental impact on later life outcomes.
