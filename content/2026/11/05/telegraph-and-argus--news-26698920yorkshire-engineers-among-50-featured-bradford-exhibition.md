A PHOTOGRAPHIC exhibition celebrating the engineers changing the world is launching in Bradford.

The National Science and Media Museum will mark National Engineering Day on November 4 with the opening of 50for50: Portraits of engineers, a free exhibition commissioned by the Royal Academy of Engineering.

The exhibition features powerful portraits taken by photojournalist Jude Vidal and showcases 50 engineers whose work has shaped modern life. It highlights breakthroughs in fields such as telecommunications, satellites, wind energy, artificial intelligence, healthcare and computing.

Sir Demis Hassabis, a pioneer in artificial intelligence, is among those featured, alongside Alice Delahunty, president of National Grid Transmission, Brompton Bicycle CEO Will Butler-Adams and nuclear engineer Dame Sue Ion.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21670563.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Alice Delahunty *(Image: Supplied)*

Several Yorkshire-based engineers are also included, such as Professor Catherine Noakes, an expert in airborne disease transmission from the University of Leeds.

Professor Noakes played a key advisory role during the Covid-19 pandemic as a member of the UK Government’s Scientific Advisory Group for Emergencies (SAGE), helping to shape national policy on ventilation and infection control.

Professor Sarah Hainsworth, Pro-Vice-Chancellor (Research) at Durham University, also features in the exhibition.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21670548.jpg?type=mds-article-620)NSMM Foyer Display 50 For 50 Portraits of Engineers *(Image: Supplied)*

She went to school in Baildon and then Richmond in North Yorkshire and played a key role in the team that examined the remains discovered beneath a car park in Leicester in 2012 and helped identify them as those of King Richard III.

Photographer Jude Vidal said: "The engineers I've had the privilege of photographing are some of the most remarkable people I've ever encountered.

"Doing relentless, world-shifting work, and doing it so quietly, so modestly.

"Their stories deserve to be told.

"The world needs the kind of hope that lives inside those stories.

"And young people need to see themselves in these portraits, to understand how big, how expansive, how genuinely exciting engineering is as a life."

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21670564.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Luke Bisby *(Image: Supplied)*

The exhibition is part of the Royal Academy of Engineering’s 50th anniversary celebrations and aims to inspire the next generation to consider engineering as a career.

It also emphasises the vital role engineering plays in addressing global challenges and improving everyday life.

To mark the opening, Professor Noakes, Professor Hainsworth and photographer Jude Vidal will visit the museum on November 4 between 10am and 11am for interviews and photographs.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21670550.jpg?type=mds-article-620)NSMM Foyer Display 50 For 50 Portraits of Engineers *(Image: Supplied)*

The exhibition is located in the museum’s foyer and is free to visit.

It is part of a UK-wide tour designed to highlight the impact and importance of engineering.

For more information, visit the Science and Media Museum website.
