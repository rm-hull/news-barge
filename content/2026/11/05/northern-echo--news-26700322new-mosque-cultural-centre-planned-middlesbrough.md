Vacant land in Middlesbrough could soon house a mosque, if plans are given the green light.

The Muslim Federation of Cleveland has requested permission to construct a mosque and cultural centre – along with associated car parking and landscaping – on vacant land, where they used to worship, before the previous building was torn down.

The most recent use of the Park Road North site is listed as the Abu Bakr Mosque and Cultural Centre, with a new building of the same name planned for the plot.

Plans seek to create 26 on-site car parking spaces as part of the proposal, along with 18 cycle spaces.

Papers submitted to Middlesbrough Council’s planning authority detail how the mosque and cultural centre has long been a “backbone of the local community, providing help and support to all members of the community regardless of faith”.

It is confirmed that the new mosque will be entirely privately funded, with papers adding: “The potential boost to the regeneration of the area cannot be understated.”

![The vacant land on Park Road North, Middlesbrough. Credit: Daniel Hodgson. Free for LDRS use.](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21672302.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The vacant land on Park Road North, Middlesbrough. Credit: Daniel Hodgson. Free for LDRS use.

The site is a “prominent” plot on the corner of Park Road North, with the original buildings (nos. 7 and 9) previously demolished, and the site cleared “some years ago”.

Prior to being torn down, no. 9 was a “home” for the mosque “for many years”. The application is described as the culmination of a “long and extensive consultation” which has lasted more than a decade, including two planning applications – one of these was approved in 2012 and one refused in 2023.

Proposals have been “considerably reduced” in scale, in order to address the council’s concerns relating to parking, visual impact and effect on the Albert Park and Linthorpe Road Conservation Area, papers outline.

The building will now be 2.5 storeys in height, rather than the previously proposed four storeys.

An additional off-site car parking area has also been factored into the plans. 20 spaces have been secured at Ali’s Supermarket on Linthorpe Road, for use by worshippers attending Friday Prayers, which will be split into two sessions.

The proposed building is described as “beautiful and elegant” and papers assert that it will not have any detrimental effects on either highway safety or the amenity of nearby residents.

Planning documents add: “The simple contemporary design will provide a striking landmark on this prominent corner.

It will complement the grandeur of the Victorian villas and the leafy environment of Albert Park which give the Conservation Area its character.”

The mosque that was agreed in 2012, but never built, is described as “much larger” than the proposal that currently sits with the council. The latest application has not yet been allocated to a council officer to consider.
