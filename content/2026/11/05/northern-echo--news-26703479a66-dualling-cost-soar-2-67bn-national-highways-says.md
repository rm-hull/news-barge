The cost of the long-awaited A66 dualling scheme linking North Yorkshire and Cumbria could more than double to £2.67bn, just a week after the Government confirmed a nine-year timetable for the upgrade.

The figure, disclosed by National Highways at the Highways UK conference, is more than twice the project's original £1.3bn estimate.

But the roads agency said no final decisions had been made and the full programme was still to be announced.

The potential increase raises fresh questions over the cost of one of northern England’s biggest road projects, already hit by years of delays and uncertainty.

![A single carriageway stretch of the A66](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21352664.jpg?type=mds-article-620)A single carriageway stretch of the A66 *(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)*

A National Highways spokesperson said: “We will set out our full programme through our Strategic Business Plan and Delivery Plan in due course, which will reflect the outcome of the Government’s consultation on changes to RIS3. No final decisions have yet been made.”

The disclosure came just over a week after the Government said in its Autumn Budget papers that main construction work would begin in November 2026.

But the papers also revealed that the project would take nine years, rather than the five previously expected.

The revised timetable has already drawn criticism from Richmond and Northallerton MP Rishi Sunak, who called the delays “frustrating”, as previously reported by The Northern Echo.

The Budget documents, published on Wednesday, October 28, said: "Giving the green light for main construction works to begin on the A66 next month, delivering a continuous 50-mile dual carriageway between the M6 and A1(M), strengthening connectivity between the North-East and Cumbria.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21676537.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The A66 at Scotch Corner *(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)*

The scheme will upgrade the remaining single-carriageway sections of the A66 between Scotch Corner in North Yorkshire and Penrith in Cumbria.

It will create a continuous 50-mile dual carriageway linking the A1(M) and M6.

Campaigners have long demanded improvements over concerns about road safety, congestion and poor connectivity.

At least 12 people have died on the A66 since 2023, fuelling fears over the safety of the existing route.

The previous Conservative government approved the upgrade plans, but they were paused after Labour won the 2024 general election.

The Government reaffirmed its commitment to the scheme in July 2025, when preparatory work began along the route.

The potential increase will add to questions over the scheme’s delivery, although National Highways stressed that £2.67bn is not the final figure.
