Firefighters have reminded people how to dispose of fireworks safely ahead of Bonfire Night celebrations across the North East.

Bonfire Night is tomorrow (November 5), with a range of organised displays planned across the region from November 4 through next weekend and beyond.

Hindu festival Diwali also falls on November 8, meaning fireworks may be used across the North East for several weeks.

Residents have been urged to attend organised firework displays instead of home celebrations, but those who insist on holding private displays have been warned that fireworks can still contain hot material or unexploded composition after appearing to finish.

Mishandling fireworks can cause burns, fires and injuries to waste workers.

Here is how to dispose of used, unused and defective fireworks safely.

County Durham and Darlington Fire and Rescue Service (CDDFRS) urged residents on social media to dispose of fireworks safely and warned that bins are often targeted on Bonfire Night.

They wrote: "We'd also like to encourage residents to bring their bins in to reduce the risk of them being damaged, stolen, or used to fuel fires."

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/20157151.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Residents are advised to never return immediately to relight a defective firework, as it may ignite later. *(Image: Pixabay)*

CDDFRS said used fireworks should be allowed to cool for a minimum of 30 minutes, ideally until cooled completely.

Residents should then place the fireworks in a bucket or large container and cover them completely with cold water.

They should remain submerged for at least 24 hours, or longer if possible.

After soaking, excess water should be drained away. The fireworks should then be placed in two heavy-duty plastic bags and put in a general waste bin.

Bag fireworks only when they are completely cold and safe.

Fireworks must not be placed in recycling bins, dismantled or burned on a bonfire.

Residents should not put them beside a bin, use another household’s bin or take them to an unapproved site.

CDDFRS advises that unused fireworks should also be soaked and bagged.

Fireworks contain explosive materials and heavy metals, so careless disposal can start fires in waste vehicles, pollute soil and harm the environment.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21575983.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Residents have been urged to attend organised firework displays instead of home celebrations, but those who insist on holding private displays have been warned that fireworks can still contain hot material or unexploded composition after appearing to finish. *(Image: Cleveland Fire)*

CDDFRS attended 91 callouts in County Durham and Darlington on Bonfire Night in 2025, prompting warnings about the risks linked to fireworks and bonfires at home.

For boxes or large quantities of unused fireworks, contact County Durham and Darlington Fire and Rescue Service, which may arrange collection by a licensed waste carrier.

Defective or misfired fireworks need to be handled differently.

Everyone should keep away from a failed firework, and wait for the period of time stated by the manufacturer.

Never return immediately to relight it, as it may ignite later.

Residents should contact the retailer, local council or fire service for advice about defective fireworks, as instructions may differ between types.

CDDFRS also advised people to buy fireworks only from licensed sellers and check for the UKCA or CE mark. Fireworks should be used outdoors in a clear, open space, with instructions followed carefully.
