One in seven parents and carers across Yorkshire and the Humber have considered cancelling Christmas because of financial pressures, according to research commissioned by charity Family Action.

The research found that 15% of parents and carers surveyed had considered cancelling Christmas because they could not afford it. A further 9% said they would be unable to afford presents for their children this year.

The findings also highlight the emotional impact of rising festive costs:

* 67% said they felt they had failed their children if they could not provide the Christmas presents they asked for.
* 32% thought their children would be worried about the cost of Christmas and family finances.
* 54% said Christmas felt less magical because of financial pressure.

Family Action is encouraging individuals, businesses and organisations to support its annual Toy Appeal, which provides gifts to families experiencing financial difficulties.

Last year, hundreds of families across Yorkshire and the Humber received gifts through the appeal.

> **One parent who received support said:**
>
> The Toy Appeal really helps us out as things are very, very tight this year so we can wrap the gift for him and say it’s from us.

Another parent described how their child had enjoyed a book and teddy received through the appeal, explaining that they would not otherwise have been able to afford the items after being made redundant.

> **Sir David Holmes CBE, Chief Executive of Family Action, said:**
>
> Our new research shows the painful reality facing many families this Christmas. Parents want so much to give their children joy, but many are being forced to make choices no family should have to make. A donated gift can mean far more than a toy — it can mean relief, dignity and a moment of Christmas magic.

People can find out more about the Toy Appeal or make a donation through the Family Action website.

## About the research

The research was conducted by Censuswide between 23 June and 13 July 2026, surveying 2,011 parents and carers aged 16 and over with children under 18. At least 100 respondents were surveyed in each UK region.

The Yorkshire and Humber findings are regional figures and do not represent Harrogate specifically.
