## The next Bishy Road? Ambitious plan to transform York neighbourhoods 'in need'

A performer during the Bishy Road Street Party - plans have been unveiled to help turn Fishergate and Fulford into the next Bishy Road. Image: Newsquest

*Fulford and Fishergate plan aims to create York's next Bishy Road, reports MAXINE GORDON*

AN ambitious project to turn an 'in need' part of York into the next Bishy Road is underway, The Press can exclusively reveal.

The team behind the successful AcombFest are now turning their attention to Fishergate and Fulford in a mission to put local businesses and creative talents on the map and drive footfall to this corridor of the city.

Jeff Clark, of Art of Protest Projects, is one of the leaders behind the venture and told The Press: "We are commercial do-gooders.

"We looked at Fulford and Fishergate and it looked like a place in need."

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21670166.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Jeff Clark, of The Art of Protes, who has revealed a project to revamp Fishergate and Fulford in York. Image: Kevin Glenton

He said the stretch between Fishergate and Fulford was long, with many local places to eat, drink, and shop, but did not appear to be connected to each other.

"We looked to see what we could do to support them," he said.

After speaking to locals in the area, and with funding from various sources including the York & North Yorkshire Combined Authority, Jeff and co-creators Black Hat Studios, have launched the Fulford Journey.

The project will create an online directory of local businesses and artistic talent to raise the profile of the area and also run a three-day 'festival' next year in a similar vein to the successful AcombFest in July.

Jeff said the "weekend" of fun would be based at various key locations down the Fishergate and Fulford Road corridor, hopefully including venues such as The Plough and Jorvine, the Middle bar and Fulford Arms and The Lighthorseman pub.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21670167.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The Lighthorseman in Fishergate, York. Photo by Maxine Gordon

The team have already created a similar business directory for Bishy Road.

As part of the venture, a series of six artworks will be installed in the Fishergate and Fulford Road areas too. An art fair is also planned.

"We want to create a journey in which everyone can be a part of," said Jeff. "It's an opportunity for the place to flourish and grow."

Asked whether Fishergate and Fulford Road could be as successful as Bishy Road – celebrated for its wealth of independent business and community spirit – Jeff said: "100 per cent."

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21670171.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The Plough Pub, Fulford Picture Frank Dwyer.

He added: "It's a ten-year vision. The reason why Bishy Road is so successful is that the business community acts as one unit – it is the same in Micklegate and Fossgate.

"When you begin to support one another these things happen."

He added: "It's all about taking York and letting it step up.

"If you go to Manchester, Leeds or London, it is made up from the character of its wards and boroughs. We need to find these places in York and support them and visit these places."

Jeff said the idea for the project came after a night out in Fulford with the Art of Protest team.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21670168.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The Middle bar owner John Sunman - a lively new venue in Fulford Road. Photo -NQ

He said: "We started at Jorvine then walked down and had a drink in lots of places and we were well fed too.

"We really enjoyed socialising in the location but felt a connection was lacking. It needs connecting."

Jeff is inviting people to get involved in the project through a series of public events. The next one will take place on Tuesday, November 24 at 7pm at the Fulford Arms when he will also be hosting a spray paint workshop. Jeff says he is reaching out to local churches too, pointing out that every church in Acomb took part in AcombFest, hosting events in the premises.
