Thousands of Police Scotland officers trained to use Tasers have just 641 weapons between them, The Herald can reveal.

There are currently 2,126 specially trained officers to use Tasers in Scotland, meaning there is roughly one weapon for every three officers trained to carry one.

In a stark contrast from south of the border, West Yorkshire Police – with a population half of Scotland – has 620 Tasers for 916 trained officers, just 21 fewer weapons than are available across the whole of Scotland.

Of Police Scotland’s 16,402 officers, almost nine in 10 are not trained to use Tasers.

Police leaders have warned the figures are “alarmingly low”, arguing officers are going into violent and unpredictable situations without the protection of Tasers.

Tasers are considered to be effective in de-escalating dangerous situations for the public and officers by issuing an electrical discharge and often acting as a deterrent.

The figures were revealed by the Scottish Government in a written parliamentary question to Tory MSP Stephen Kerr.

Mr Kerr, the justice spokesman for his party, said officers were being left at the “mercy of criminals”.

He has backed calls from the Scottish Police Federation (SPF) to widen the rollout of Tasers to all officers on the frontline.

David Kennedy, general secretary of the SPF, told The Herald the current levels were “completely inadequate”.

“It means officers who have undertaken the training may still attend potentially dangerous incidents without immediate access to this vital piece of non-lethal safety equipment,” he said.

“More concerning is that around 87% of the workforce are not trained to use Tasers at all. Police officers are increasingly dealing with violence, weapons and unpredictable situations, yet the overwhelming majority do not have access to this additional protection.”

He added: “One Taser for every three trained officers is not a meaningful or credible approach to officer safety.”

The SPF has urged the Scottish Government to ensure every officer has access to the equipment to “protect themselves and the public”.

Mr Kennedy referenced the 2022 Skye shootings as a primary reason why all officers require Tasers after local officers had to wait for an armed response unit to travel from Inverness.

Police Scotland said there is 24-hour coverage to an officer trained in using Tasers, but Chief Constable Jo Farrell has said a further rollout should be considered in the future.

Scotland’s Chief Superintendent Rob Hay, who is President of the Association of Scottish Police Superintendents (ASPS), also called for additional investment.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/18097576.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Chief Constable Jo Farrell. *(Image: PA)*

He said: “Against a backdrop of 3 years of increases in assaults on police officers, the number of just 641 Tasers available to officers across the country is alarmingly low.”

If Tasers are not available to staff, it increases the likelihood of having to remit to other tactics and equipment, such as the baton, which could have higher injury rates, according to the Chief Superintendent.

Mr Kerr said: “Savage SNP cuts are leaving hardworking officers dangerously ill-equipped to challenge violent criminals.

“It is scandalous that officers are having to share tasers or that such a minority of the overstretched force are even trained to use them.

“Neil Gray needs to act urgently and ensure officers have the means to protect themselves from dangerous offenders and act accordingly, which means equipping and training them all with tasers.

“Currently the inaction from the SNP is leaving them at the mercy of criminals.”

Meanwhile, Police Scotland said it has increased the number of specially trained officers from 500 to more than 2,000 since 2021.

Chief Superintendent Stevie Dolan, of Police Scotland’s operational support division, said: “We have a duty to protect life and ensure the safety of our officers, staff and the public. The use of Taser gives officers an additional tactical option.”

He added: “Trained officers are spread across operational policing shifts and specialist units to ensure 24-hour coverage across the country. We currently have 641 operational Tasers for use by these officers.”

He said “significant investment” was being made to transition to an updated Taser device.

“Tasers protect the public and emergency service workers from violent individuals and the mere presence of the device is often enough to de-escalate situations,” Mr Dolan said.

“It is also highly effective in situations where people may not only harm officers but may be intent on harming themselves.”

A Scottish Government spokesman said: "The provision of equipment, including Tasers, is a matter for the Chief Constable, and we note that Police Scotland has advised that the number of Tasers for deployment is under review and the number of trained officers has quadrupled."
