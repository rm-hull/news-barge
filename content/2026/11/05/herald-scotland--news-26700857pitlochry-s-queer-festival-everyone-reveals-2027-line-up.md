It was the star-studded event which brightened up the depths of winter in a Scottish town when it was launched earlier this year.

Now Pitlochry Festival Theatre has revealed plans for the return of the “queer festival for everyone” which will take over the venue for four days in January.

The Traitors star Stephen Libby, American author Armistead Maupin, chef Yotam Ottolenghi, broadcaster Banjo Beale and comic Rosie Jones are expected to be among the main attractions at the 2027 edition of “Out in the Hills”.

Theatre-makers Johnny McKnight and Robert Softley Gale, actor and comedian Greg McHugh, crime writer Val McDermid, trad music star Malin Lewis, broadcaster Gemma Cairney and author Chitra Ramaswamy are among the Scottish cultural figures confirmed for next year’s event.

The festival was billed as “a weekend of queer joy” when it was first announced by the theatre’s artistic director, Perthshire-born Alan Cumming.

The 2027 edition, which will run from January 28-31, will feature late-night ceilidh and disco events, live music sessions, a pop-up sauna, a “kilted yoga” class and an inflatable curling rink.

Storytelling, dance, theatre and poetry events are also part of the Out in the Hills line-up, which has been programmed for a second year by Glasgow-based playwright Lewis Hetherington.

When Out in the Hills was first announced by the theatre just over a year ago it was described as a festival which would invite audiences to “find new ways to look at the world and each other”.

Hetherington said: “We were absolutely thrilled to bits with the response to the first festival.

“We knew we were taking a risk doing a festival in the middle of January.

“But what is brilliant about the Alan Cumming-era in Pitlochry is his total give-it-a-go attitude. We all felt quite empowered and emboldened.

“We had a lot of feedback that January is actually a really good time to have a festival. January can feel like a bit of a slog for lots of reasons. Queer people are also more likely to be estranged from their family. Christmas can be a hard time for so many reasons.

“A lot of people told us how nice it was to have a big, joyful, sparkly festival to look forward to in the cold and dark winter months.”

The inaugural edition featured sold-out appearances from broadcaster Graham Norton, TV screenwriter and producer Russell T Davies, and award-winning actor Sir Ian McKellen.

Hetherington said: “We knew there were some really well-known names in the programme and thought that people would maybe just pop in and see the people they knew.

“We worked really hard to make sure the programme was action-packed and that the whole building was animated in different ways.

“That really seems to have paid off as we had overwhelming feedback that people loved coming to the festival for the whole weekend. We also found that people came into the building for one event and suddenly signed up for a whole bunch of other things on the same day.”

The first edition of Out in the Hills also featured appearances from Zander Murray, Scotland’s first openly gay professional footballer, “Hebridean Baker” Coinneach MacLeod, former Scots Makar Jackie Kay, playwright Jo Clifford, percussionist Evelyn Glennie and former SNP MP Mhairi Black.

Hetherington said: “One of our big intentions last year was to get lots of different people across the threshold.

“A lot of people contacted me directly to say how emotional they found the whole festival.

“At the end of the first day, people were asking about the next festival.

“The theatre had always set out with the intention for Out in the Hills to be a permanent fixture.

“We obviously had to see how the first festival went, but it landed so well and doing it in January really seemed to work. Everyone in the theatre has been really on board with the festival.”

The 2027 festival will see Greg McHugh, star of Gary: Tank Commander, Fresh Meat and Only Child, host an in-conversation event with Stephen Libby, who was the joint winner, with Rachel Duffy, of the UK version of The Traitors earlier this year.

Armistead Maupin will be interviewed at next year’s Out in the Hills by Scottish author Zoe Strachan a year after being forced to pull out of planned appearance after suffering broken ribs and a shoulder injury after falling down a flight of stairs at home.

Johnny McKnight, who co-wrote The High Life musical with Alan Cumming and Forbes Masson, will be speaking to Rosie Jones and Robert Softley Gale about their recent collaboration on a stage show exploring disabled people’s experiences of sex.

The festival will also be staging a reading of extracts from a new play commissioned by Softley Gale’s theatre company Birds of Paradise, which explores the true-life story of a disability rights movement which existed in the early days of the Nazi Party.

McKnight will be speaking to Banjo Beale and his husband Rohan Christopher about the TV show which follows the pair’s efforts to transform a derelict mansion on the Isle of Ulva into a new boutique hotel.

Christopher Hampton will discuss Scottish Ballet’s new film adaptation of Romeo and Juliet before a special festival screening.

Val McDermid will be looking back on her 40 years as a crime writer, since the publication of debut novel Report for Murder, with former Scottish Labour Party leader Kezia Dugdale.

Aberdeenshire writers Mae Diansangu and Shane Strachan will be in-conversation about queerness, language, poetry and the Scots language.

Matt Hyndman, who appeared alongside Stephen Libby in The Traitors, will be discussing the new hiking club he has created in an event on queer people and outdoor activities, which will also feature snowboarder Lauren MacCallum.

Piper and fiddler Malin Lewis will be hosting the Friday night “Big Gay Ceilidh” with cabaret star Chris Wilson, while Saturday’s late night Disco in the Hills will feature drag king Loose Willis and Jamie Rea, the deaf and visual artist who starred in the hit stage show The Singer, which KT Tunstall wrote songs and music for, earlier this year.

The festival will also feature a specially-curated exhibition of objects chosen by young people across Scotland and Drag Me Up sessions, which will give audiences the chance to have a “rummage through” a dressing-up box at the theatre.

Hetherington said: “When Alan and I first talked about the festival, we created the brief for it together and came up with the idea of it being a queer festival for everyone.

“I really hold that in my mind. I really do want there to be something for everyone in our programme. I love that challenge that I have set myself and I think that’s what makes Out in the Hills really feel like a festival.”
