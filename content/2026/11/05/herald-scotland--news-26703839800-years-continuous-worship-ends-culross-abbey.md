A service of closure has been held to officially mark the end of Culross Abbey's purpose as a Church of Scotland after over 800 years of worship.

Located in the historic village of Culross in Fife, the Abbey was founded by Malcolm, earl of Fife in 1217 to 1218 as a daughter house of the Cistercian monastery at Kinloss. The abbey church was built soon after, with work continuing into the 1300s.

Culross is said to have been the site of an early Christian community led by St Serf and of which St Kentigern (Mungo) was a member.

The Abbey was put up for sale by the Church of Scotland in August last year due to a shrinking congregation, high maintenance costs, and an ongoing portfolio reduction.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/19961282.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Inside Culross Abbey. *(Image: Gordon Terris/Herald&Times)*

Marketed via the Church of Scotland's Properties Listing for offers over £35,000, the sale included consent for the space to be adapted for alternative community use - such as a museum, art gallery, library, or day nursery.

The Herald understands that the Abbey remains under offer with its sale pending completion. An expectation also exists that the building will be retained for community use, despite no longer holding regular services.

A service of closure was held at the Abbey on Tuesday, November 3.

According to reports, Lord Bruce, whose family has long historic ties to the local parish and Kirk, announced that he plans to purchase the building and preserve it for continued, occasional worship, alongside new heritage and cultural events.

Joining locals and community members in attending the service were members of the University of St Andrews Chaplaincy.

In a post on social media on Wednesday, November 3, the Chaplaincy wrote: "At Culross Abbey Church in west Fife last night for a moving service marking its closure as a Church of Scotland. With monastic roots, this has been a place of worship for over 800 years.

"There will be worship in other places in a new, larger parish, but many there had a deep sense of sadness at the ending of a long era.

"I was heartened however by words from Lord Bruce, whose family have long connections with parish and Kirk, who said he was purchasing the building for continued worship alongside heritage and cultural events."
