A former police officer has been barred from policing after engaging in "targeted and sexualised behaviour" towards a female colleague.

Former Durham Police Constable Scott Campbell was found guilty of gross misconduct following a four-day hearing at The Glow Centre in Newton Aycliffe, held from October 12 to 15.

The eight allegations concerned Campbell's behaviour towards two female colleagues between March and September 2023.

The panel found his behaviour towards one woman was "unwanted, sexualised, targeted and intentional".

It ruled his actions "violated her dignity and created an intimidating, hostile, degrading, humiliating or offensive environment".

Campbell was also found to have failed to 'exercise self-control and failed to treat the woman with courtesy and respect'.

A separate incident involving a second female colleague was also found to be inappropriate, although the panel did not establish a breach of the Standards of Professional Behaviour in relation to her.

All eight factual allegations were found proved.

The panel ruled Campbell had breached the Standards of Professional Behaviour relating to Authority, Respect and Courtesy and Discreditable Conduct.

The proven breaches were assessed as gross misconduct.

Campbell would have been dismissed without notice had he still been a serving officer.

His name will now be added to the College of Policing Barred List, preventing him from returning to policing.

In reaching its decision, the panel considered the targeted, intentional and persistent nature of the conduct and the harm caused to public confidence in policing.

It also considered Campbell's service record and personal mitigation presented on his behalf.

The hearing was chaired by Assistant Chief Constable Richard Allen, alongside independent panel members Stan Irwin and Jake Miller. Legally qualified person Peter Cadman assisted the panel.
