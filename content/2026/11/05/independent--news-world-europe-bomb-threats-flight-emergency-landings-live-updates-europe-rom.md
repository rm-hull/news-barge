Two UK airports have received hoax bomb threats on Wednesday, according to a report, after several flights across Europe were grounded following similar threats.

The threats at Heathrow and Birmingham airports were resolved quickly with no impact to flights, The Times reported. Heathrow did not deem the hoax threats to be credible.

It comes after bomb threats forced more emergency flight landings across Europe on Thursday morning.

Reports of bomb alerts against flights across the region are still coming in as of 11am on Thursday, with two Milan-bound flights landing safely following threats.

Flights from London, Dublin, Madrid, Copenhagen and Helsinki, were also among those that made emergency landings in France, Bulgaria and Belgium.

Aer Lingus flight EI402 from Dublin to Rome landed safely at Evreux airport, 100 km west of Paris, after being diverted as a security precaution, while a flight from London emergency landed in Plovdiv, local media said.

It comes after a series of bomb threats earlier this week caused similar emergency landings, prompting serious concerns over the threat of information warfare during a week that has also seen cyber attacks on supermarket fridges and blood banks in Europe.

## At least 21 flights impacted by bomb threats

At least 21 flights have been impacted by bomb threats across Europe today, following several hoaxes earlier in the week.

This is the full list of airlines that were targeted on Thursday:

* An Aegean Airlines flight from Thessaloniki was affected by a bomb alert that came after the plane had landed in Milan Malpensa.

* An Aer Lingus flight from Dublin to Rome was diverted to the Evreux air base in northern France following a bomb alert.

* Spain's Guardia Civil said that three bomb threats received by email affected six Air Europa flights at Madrid-Barajas airport, including four Iberia flights from Rome and Milan and two Air Europa departures to Paris.

* An ⁠Austrian Airlines flight from Tel Aviv to Vienna diverted to Sofia.

* An Air Serbia plane received bomb threat and landed safely at its destination.

* Two easyJet flights, ⁠one from Newcastle and one from Glasgow, were met by police on arrival in Prague as a precaution ​over a “security-related ⁠matter”.

* A threat was made against a Finnair flight from Helsinki to Rome. At the same time, a Jettime-operated flight from Helsinki landed safely in Vienna after receiving a bomb threat.

* A LOT flight from Warsaw landed safely in Brussels following a bomb alert.

* A Warsaw-Paris flight was stopped while taxiing and directed to a designated parking stand after a report of a potential security threat.

* Italian border police were carrying out "bomb checks" on a Ryanair flight from the northern Portuguese city of Porto. Another three Ryanair aircraft received bomb threats and made landings at Bulgarian airports on Thursday.

* Wizz ⁠Air confirmed that a flight from Milan Malpensa to Bucharest Otopeni received a bomb threat but, already on final approach, ⁠landed safely without diverting.

## Three flights affected by hoax bomb threats in Madrid

Three flights were affected by hoax bomb threats at Madrid's airport on Thursday morning, Spanish authorities said.

They added that security forces had ruled out any risk.

Airlines across Europe received a series of bomb threats on Thursday morning for the second time this week, forcing many to make unplanned or escorted landings, although none of the threats immediately proved to be genuine.

## UK government responds to threats at Heathrow and Birmingham airports

The UK government has responded to the threats reported at Heathrow and Birmingham airports.

“Threats reported at Heathrow and Birmingham in the early hours of this morning have been resolved,” the spokesperson said.

“It’s right that authorities treat any potential threats seriously — the UK has one of the most robust aviation security systems in the world, with well-tested processes for dealing with any threats, as well as malicious hoaxes, like those we’ve seen this week.”

## Two UK airports 'targeted by bomb threats' - report

Heathrow and Birmingham airports both received hoax bomb threats on Wednesday, after dozens of flights were affected across Europe, according to a report.

Heathrow did not deem the hoax threats to be credible.

Metropolitan Police are continuing to monitor the situation, the outlet reported.

## The Latest: Flights diverted in Europe over bomb threats

Flights from across Europe were diverted or forced to make emergency landings on Thursday because of bomb threats, authorities said. So far inspections have not turned up hazardous or suspicious materials.

The incidents followed hoax bomb threats earlier this week that prompted searches, diversions and, in one case, a military escort.

More than a dozen flights were targeted, included those from Copenhagen, Dublin, Helsinki, Madrid, Milan and Warsaw.

## UK government says threats have been 'resolved'

Threats made to UK flights on Thursday have now been resolved, according to a statement by the UK government.

A UK government spokesperson said: “Threats made to UK flights today have been resolved.

“It's right that authorities treat any potential threat seriously - the UK has one of the most robust aviation security systems in the world, with well-tested processes for dealing with any threats, as well as malicious hoaxes, like those we've seen this week.

“The Met’s Aviation Policing Command is keeping the situation under review and liaising with the relevant authorities."”

## No issues identified in inspections after three bomb threats, says bulgaria

The inspections of three Ryanair aircraft that received bomb threats and landed at Bulgarian airports earlier on Thursday did not identify any issues, the country’s transport ministry has said.

"Upon landing, the necessary inspections were carried out in accordance with the applicable procedures and no issues were identified," Bulgaria's transport ministry told Reuters.

Airport operations at both Sofia and Plovdiv airports have returned to normal, it said.
