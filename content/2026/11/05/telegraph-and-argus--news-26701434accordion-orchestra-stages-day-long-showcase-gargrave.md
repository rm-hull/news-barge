ACCORDIONISTS from across the UK gathered for a day of music, community, and charity at Gargrave Village Hall.

The event marked Craven Accordion Orchestra’s third annual Accordion Showcase and Celebration Day, and raised £2,000 for the Motor Neurone Disease Association (MNDA).

Originally launched in 2024 to celebrate the orchestra’s 30th anniversary, the showcase has since grown into a national gathering of accordionists, supporters, and music lovers.

"One of Craven Accordion Orchestra's aims is to promote and progress the accordion, challenging public perceptions of this very versatile instrument," said Harry Hinchcliffe, accordion teacher and founder of the orchestra.

"That goal was certainly acheived during this concert - many audience members who hadn't seen the orchestra perform before left the venue amazed at just what the instrument could do, how good it can sound, and proclaiming "well, i wasnt expecting that! When's the next one,"

The day began with a junior festival featuring 46 young musicians from the Craven School of Music.

Children as young as five took part in the non-competitive showcase, performing for adjudicator Phillip Henderson, who offered supportive feedback.

The afternoon programme featured adult performances from TNG, Craven Accordion Orchestra’s mixed-ability group, as well as soloists and ensembles from across the UK.

Highlights included Robin Johnstone-Hyland from Scotland, who demonstrated Italian Polverini accordions, and Alan Shute from Squeezebox Marketplace.

Helen Rich from Manchester, the Burton Accordion Group, and Carlisle-based duet Julie Best and Jean Corrighan also took to the stage.

As day turned to evening, the village hall was transformed for a sold-out, movie-themed concert by the Craven Accordion Orchestra, which recently won the Open Amateur Orchestra Category at the International Trophée Mondial de L’Accordéon.

The concert featured a vibrant programme of film music under the baton of conductor Sarah Evans.

The orchestra performed a medley of tunes from The Sound of Music, Jeff Wayne’s "Eve of the War" from War of the Worlds, themes from Top Gun and The Dam Busters, and Rodrigo’s "Concerto de Aranjuez" from Brassed Off.

Clips from each film were played on a screen behind the performers, adding to the cinematic atmosphere.

Mr Hinchcliffe added: "A very successful, fun day was had by accordionists of all ages, and a total of around £2,000 was raised for the orchestra's chosen charity of the Motor Neurone Disease Association."

The orchestra also expressed thanks to North Yorkshire County Council for sponsoring the event.

The orchestra's next public performance will take place at Cliffe Castle on Sunday, December 20, at 2pm.
