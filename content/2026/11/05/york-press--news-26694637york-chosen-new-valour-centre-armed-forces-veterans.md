## 'We must never forget' - York chosen for new centre to support veterans and families

Rachael Maskell MP has welcomed York being identified for a Valour Centre to ensure veterans are fully supported. *(Image: Newsquest)*

“Have you forgotten yet?...Look up, and swear by the green of the spring that you'll never forget.... For the world’s events have rumbled on …”.

Aftermath by Seigfried Sassoon, one of the many war poems I studied for my English Literature exam. I often carry their bitter words in my mind, as we debate defence.

It has never been easy for those who have left the battlefield.

![York Central MP Rachael Maskell](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/17073030.jpg?type=mds-article-620)York Central MP Rachael Maskell says York has been chosen for a new VALOUR Centre for Armed Forces veterans. *(Image: Newsquest)*

The scars of war leave their mark, and only those who ran across the landing beaches, ducked the sniper fire or laid their friend to rest will ever understand their depth.

It is why our Government is determined to ensure greater safety of our Armed Forces and our country.

Too often we have heard tales of equipment shortages and old kit.

It is why we have to restock, replenish and rapidly adapt to the changing world of AI, drones and technology.

But ultimately, we have to recognise the human story above all; all those who have served down the years, must have the very best services available to them.

Whether like Ken Cooke, who we are so proud of in York, D-Day veteran who served in the Second World War, or those who embarked on tours in Northern Ireland, the Falkland’s, Iraq, Afghanistan, peace keeping operations or used their skill in conflict prevention and strategic leadership, 11 November gives us all an opportunity to remember the sacrifices made and service given.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21664871.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Ken Cooke, pictured in September 2026, signing copies of his book Every Day is an Adventure in St Lawrence Parish Church in Lawrence Street, on the same day as receiving a scroll formerly honouring the Freedom of the City recently conferred upon him. *(Image: (Image: David Harrison))*

This year, building on the Armed Forces Covenant, I am pleased that York has been identified for a VALOUR Centre in my constituency.

Developed by those who have served, VALOUR is the programme that ensures that our veterans are fully supported.

It joins up services, it enables access and it ensures that the respect that is paid when in uniform, continues for the rest of a soldier, sailor or airman or airwoman’s life.

Thanks to the tireless work of the Royal British Legion in York, throughout the year, much progress has already been made in developing services with local agencies.

Having the VALOUR Centre takes this to the next level.

It is a place for veterans to get all the support they need, alongside their families.

At each centre, you’ll find a space where you can talk things through, ask questions and take the next step in a way that feels right for you, with people who understand military life and are ready to help.

This is about taking care of those who have gone ahead for us, while at the same time Government are preparing for a new investment programme to meet our NATO commitments.

Of course, we all long for the risks to diminish and for the fighting to stop.

The world is a volatile and unstable place.

If we invest in defence, diplomacy and development, we can address the needs of today and tomorrow.

War does not sit easy with me, nor should it any of us, but with the outstanding provision of our Armed Forces, the excellence of our diplomatic service and our understanding of the power of local delivery of aid we can still the storms and seek peace.

It was an honour to attend the Royal British Legion commemoration at the Joseph Rowntree Theatre last Sunday, an event that always leaves me in a place of reflection of my duties as your Member of Parliament.

Next Sunday, I will lay my wreath on behalf of all my constituents, each with their own stories and memories.

For most of us they are those now handed down, but for some they are recent and they are raw.

This week I will also place my cross in the Parliamentary Memorial Garden in Westminster, and as I do, will take time to reflect once more on the sacrifices made by all who serve and their families.
