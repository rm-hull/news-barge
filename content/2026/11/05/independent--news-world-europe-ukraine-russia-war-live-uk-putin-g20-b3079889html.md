## Canada refuses to play Russian ice hockey teams in protest against Moscow’s war in Ukraine

Canada has joined a growing group of countries refusing to play Russian ice hockey teams in protest against Moscow’s war in Ukraine.

Canadian prime minister Mark Carney said Russian players would not be allowed to compete at next month’s world junior championship in Canada.

He said: “While Russia and Belarus are supporting this illegal, unjustified, horrific war in Ukraine, as long as that’s going on, there will be none of those athletes competing for their countries in Canada.”

The move follows a Court of Arbitration for Sport (CAS) decision to lift the ban on Russian participation in International Ice Hockey Federation events, arguing that safety concerns “were not supported by sufficient evidence”.

The ban had been in place since Russia’s full-scale invasion of Ukraine in February 2022.

Finland, Denmark, Sweden, Norway, Latvia and the Czech Republic have also opposed playing Russian teams.

Ukrainian foreign minister Andrii Sybiha thanked European countries for “refusing to share the ice with Russia”, adding: “If CAS lacks a moral compass, let’s show that hockey still has values”.

“Refuse to play against Russia and deny visas to its players. Keep Russia off the ice! A war of aggression and hockey are incompatible,” he said.

## British volunteers cite donor fatigue as they say financial support for Ukraine is declining

Public willingness to financially support Ukraine is declining after nearly four years of war, with British volunteers citing donor fatigue and other global crises.

Graeme Reavley, a 69-year-old from Northumberland has made 21 trips to Ukraine since Russia’s 2022 invasion, delivering 50 vehicles through his independent aid organisation, which has raised around £150,000.

“It’s now dragged on for four years, the war. There are a lot of other issues in the world requiring people’s support,” he told the BBC.

While he says raising money has become increasingly difficult, donations of humanitarian supplies such as clothing, sleeping bags and fire extinguishers have increased.

British paramedic science lecturer Kate Snowdon who recently delivered a vehicle and medical supplies to Lviv warned that ordinary Ukrainians risk being forgotten as public attention shifts elsewhere, urging people to continue supporting the country.

“There’s a real concern that everyday people are going to be forgotten in all of this,” she said.

## Drone attacks in Black Sea pose new challenges to Bulgarian border police

Bulgarian border police crews are responding to drone attacks on commercial ships as Russia’s war in Ukraine brings growing danger to shipping off the Black Sea coasts of NATO members Bulgaria and Romania.

Over 24 days in October, nine cargo ships and tankers were struck by aerial or naval drones near the Bulgarian and Romanian coastlines.

At least 30 sailors on commercial ships were confirmed dead or reported missing.

![A border police officer guards the shipping tank container stranded on rocks after being washed ashore by rough seas at the rock formation known as The Ships on November 3, 2026 near Sinemorets, Bulgaria](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/05/09/2303290782..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The attacks have added dangerous rescue missions to the duties of Bulgarian border police, who also combat unauthorized migration and cross-border crime.During one recent operation, a border police patrol helped the crew of the Chelsea 3, a Ukraine-operated cargo ship sailing under a Maltese flag, after it was hit by three drones.

All 15 crew members were rescued from the damaged vessel despite strong winds and high waves, said Nikolay Monev, who commands the border police base in Sozopol on Bulgaria’s southern Black Sea coast.

“We are capable of an immediate response. Our vessels serve as the first line of response to such situations, including incidents involving drones,” Monev said.

The Sozopol base, established in 2013 with EU funding, keeps between two and five vessels on active duty at all times.
