As people prepare for Bonfire Night events across the North East, firefighters are reminding families that sparklers are not harmless toys.

Handheld fireworks can burn at temperatures of more than 2,000°C, County Durham and Darlington Fire and Rescue Service (CDDFRS) said, prompting safety chiefs to issue guidance aimed at reducing the risk of burns and injuries.

Bonfire Night has arrived, with organised displays planned across the region from tonight (November 5), until next weekend and beyond.

While sparklers have become a low-cost, easy staple among those celebrating Bonfire Night, many people are unaware of the potential danger they pose.

Last year, NHS England noted a 35 per cent rise in hospital admissions for firework and sparkler-related injuries, recording 161 hospital admissions for such injuries over the year.

Many of these cases stemmed from improper use of fireworks and sparklers, and caused life‑changing harm, especially in children.

Families have been advised by CDDFRS to attend organised Bonfire Night displays, but those planning to celebrate at home have been urged to be aware of the laws around fireworks and bonfires.

Those attending organised displays should also check whether sparklers are allowed, as many events do not permit them.

### How can people use sparklers safely?

CDDFRS said handheld sparklers should be treated with the same care as any other firework, as they can burn hotter than a blowtorch and pose a serious injury risk if not handled properly.

Children under five must not be given sparklers at all, CDDFRS said, while children over five should be supervised by a responsible adult at all times.

The service also advised all users to wear gloves and a thick jacket when handling sparklers, holding them at arm's length,

Users should only ever hold one sparkler at once, and be aware of where the hot end is at all times.

Sparklers should not waved around, especially when close to other people.

Used sparklers must be placed straight away in a bucket of cold water, or sand, to best mitigate fire risk.

They should remain there for several hours, or until they have completely cooled, before being thrown away.

The British Red Cross said waving sparklers around, and hearing them crackle away, is "all good fun... until someone burns themselves.”

### What should people do if a sparkler burn occurs?

The British Red Cross warned that used sparklers can remain scalding hot even after they appear to have gone out, and added that if a burn occurs, cold running water remains the best treatment.

Anyone who suffers a burn should cool it under cold running water for at least 20 minutes, as soon as possible.

This can reduce pain and swelling, as well as the risk of scarring.

After the burn has been cooled, it should be covered with cling film or a clean plastic bag, the Red Cross said.

This helps keep the area clean and will not stick to the injury, while reducing pain by keeping air away from the skin.

999 should be called if any burn may need urgent medical treatment.

Medical advice should always be sought for a baby or child who has been burned, the Red Cross added.

Anyone who cannot make the call should ask somebody else to do so.

If a person is burned through a glove or other clothing, the item should not be removed if it is stuck to the burn, as trying to pull the fabric from the burn could cause further skin damage.

Instead, cold running water should be applied through the glove for at least 20 minutes, followed by urgent medical treatment.

A glove that is not stuck to the burn can be removed carefully, followed by cold running water.

If running water is not immediately available, the Red Cross said any cold liquid, including juice, beer or milk, can be used as a last resort to cool the area as quickly as possible, but cold running water should be used as soon as it becomes available.

If clothing catches fire, residents should follow the 'stop, drop and roll' instruction: they should stop whatever they are doing, drop to the ground, cover their face with their hands, and roll over and over until the flames are extinguished.

Full firework first aid advice can be found through the British Red Cross.
