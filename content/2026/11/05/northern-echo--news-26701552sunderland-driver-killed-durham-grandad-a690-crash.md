A dangerous driver was doing more than double the speed limit seconds before he crashed into another car, killing a much-loved grandad.

It is estimated James Waddell was going at 72mph in a 30mph zone when his red Subaru Forester crashed into a blue Volkswagen Tiguan driven by Paul Andrew Fulcher.

The 53-year-old from Durham, known as Andrew, suffered serious injuries and was taken to Sunderland Royal Hospital.

Despite the efforts of medical staff, he died on April 25 from injuries sustained in the collision, 11 days after the crash on the A690, at the junction with Ettrick Grove.

Police found Waddell had been driving at excessive speed moments before the crash.

Officers also discovered the Subaru had defective brakes.

The 21-year-old, of King Edward Street, Sunderland, admitted causing death by dangerous driving.

At Newcastle Crown Court on Friday (October 30), Waddell was sentenced to seven years and six months in prison.

He was also banned from driving for 11 years and must pass an extended driving test before getting back behind the wheel.

Inspector Steve Clare, of Northumbria Police's Serious Collision Investigation Unit, said: "Firstly, we would like to pay tribute to Andrew's family and loved ones for the strength, patience and composure they have shown throughout these proceedings.

"Waddell's actions were incredibly reckless and dangerous and have resulted in a beloved dad and grandad tragically losing his life.

"No outcome at court will ever fill the void felt by Andrew's family in losing him, however I hope the fact Waddell is now behind bars brings them a sense of closure as they look to move forward.

"Andrew's death could have been avoided altogether, and we're committed to cracking down on drivers who flout the law and put people at risk.

"Our message is clear, as a driver of any vehicle, if you drive dangerously and your actions result in someone's death, then expect to be prosecuted and go to prison."

Andrew's family previously paid tribute to him, saying: “Our hearts are shattered by the loss of Andrew Fulcher - a loving husband, son, dad, and grandad.

“He was our rock, our protector, and the soul of our family. He wasn’t just loved by us, but by hundreds of friends who will feel his absence deeply.
