Hubert Aiwanger, the Bavarian Minister of Economic Affairs and leader of the Free Voters party, has died. Berlin police confirmed this on Wednesday evening. He was found lifeless in a Berlin hotel. Aiwanger was 55 years old.

He was discovered in his hotel room near Friedrichstrasse station in the early evening. “For investigative reasons, the specific circumstances of the discovery cannot yet be disclosed,” said a spokesperson for the Berlin public prosecutor’s office.

Earlier, Péter Vida, Brandenburg’s state chairman of BVB/Free Voters, had also confirmed corresponding media reports on Wednesday evening. Later, Bavaria’s Minister-President Markus Söder (CSU) also reacted to the politician’s death.

Berlin police and prosecutors announced that a death investigation has been launched. The aim is to “determine the specific cause and circumstances of death and to rule out any possible foul play,” they stated. An rbb reporter reported that a large police presence was at the hotel on Wednesday evening.

The Free Voters’ Berlin office opened on Wednesday. Aiwanger was absent, as Péter Vida confirmed. The reason given was that the 55-year-old was unwell and had therefore remained at his hotel in Berlin-Mitte. Subsequent calls to his mobile phone went unanswered.

On Wednesday evening, Bavarian Minister-President Markus Söder (CSU) reacted to the politician’s death. “We are all deeply shocked. Our thoughts are first and foremost with his family and his children.” He called Aiwanger’s death “an absolute tragedy.”
