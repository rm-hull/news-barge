THE owners of a garden centre west of Skipton say they are losing business because of poorly thought out 'road closed' signs.

Bev and Paul Keyworth, of Gledstone Gardens, West Marton, say the signs should be accompanied by another sign saying 'business open as usual' but rarely are.

The latest closure took place on Tuesday when Gledstone Road, off the A59, to the Keyworth's business was closed for the day for work to take place by Openreach.

Mrs Keyworth said fortunately, she had seen a customer who was in the process of turning round on the A59 and was able to tell them they were open.

She said: "It happens time and time again without any consideration or notification.

"We found out on Tuesday from a lady that lives down in the village that saw the 'road ahead closed' sign going out.

"This week it was Openreach, sometime it's Highways, sometimes it's some other utility company and no one takes responsibility for the correct signage being put out."

"They effectively shut us down for days on end sometimes, when we are actually accessible, they just need to let our customers know we are open and accessible by putting out the correct signage."

Skipton MP Sir Julian Smith has asked North Yorkshire Council to review how businesses are notified and consulted about road closures after concerns were raised with him by several firms.

He has said: "Signs must clearly indicate when businesses remain open and accessible, so customers are not discouraged from visiting.

"This is particularly important in rural areas that rely on visitor trade, where unclear signage can have a significant impact."

Mrs Keyworth added: "With advance notification and correct signage for customers, we would be able to manage the situation much better, including scheduling deliveries.

We understand works are necessary, we just want notification when possible - we realise there are emergency closures - but Tuesday's was not one.

We want to see the 'Business Open as Usual' sign along with each 'Road Closed' sign."

North Yorkshire Council says: "For its own planned highway works, the council will always seek to ensure that advanced notification letters are issued to affected homes and businesses at least seven days in advance, along with erecting 'Businesses Open as Usual' signs.

"For utility works, there is nothing in the legislation requiring this to happen, but the council works to ensure that as much notice is given as possible."

The council says it also issues press releases and social media updates about roadworks likely to cause major disruption.
