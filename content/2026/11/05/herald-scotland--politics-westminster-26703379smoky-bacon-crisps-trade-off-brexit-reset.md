The prospect of a ban on smoky bacon crisps didn't really bother me too much. They're rotten. But then, when a pal suggested Taylor's (formerly Mackie's) haggis and black pepper crisps were at risk, I was livid.

I'll only give Brussels my haggis crisps when they pry them from my cold, dead hands.

But my friend wasn't quite right. Haggis crisps are going nowhere. They'd only be a bother to the EU if Taylor's went out of their mind and decided to rebrand them as heart, lungs, liver and black pepper flavour. And even then, it'd only be the word liver that'd be an issue.

Across the EU, Regulation 2026/1739, formally signed into law by the European Parliament and Council, legally restricts the word "meat" and 31 specific cuts and culinary terms solely to genuine animal products.

The prohibited list covers everything from chicken, beef, and pork to steak, ribs, goose, goat, T-bone, liver and, of course, bacon.

It followed intense lobbying by European livestock farmers struggling against thin margins, rising costs, and a supermarket squeeze, who wanted action on what they described as "cultural hijacking".

These groups argued that venture capital-backed veggie food firms were unfairly trading on their traditions and marketing power by selling bacon and steak made of plants.

Yet the final text of the meat law reveals a chaotic political compromise. Agricultural hardliners initially wanted to outlaw terms describing what you might call meaty formats.

So things like "burgers" and "sausages" would have been for beef and pork only, and the humble veggie burger would become a "veggie disc" and vegan sausages hearty "plant tubes".

Ultimately, "burger" and "sausage" were spared and the axe fell on biology and butchery.

Green groups have accused the Commission of hypocrisy, pointing out that while Brussels continues to spend millions urging citizens to adopt plant-rich diets to cut agricultural emissions under its Farm to Fork strategy, it has now saddled low-carbon alternatives with punishing marketing hurdles.

Could those punishing marketing hurdles now be coming for your lunchtime meal deal?

Because the regulations apply across the entire food supply chain rather than just to faux-meat substitutes, long-standing pantry staples have been caught up. Which is why ministers are now being asked about the smoky bacon row.

As part of the UK government's new veterinary and sanitary and phytosanitary (SPS) agreement with Brussels, London has promised regulatory alignment on food safety, animal welfare, and labelling. In exchange, the border bureaucracy that has hobbled trade since Brexit gets wiped away. British producers get to resume exporting fresh sausages, uncooked burgers, and live shellfish to the continent without a mountain of red tape.

The prize of this deal is substantial in Scotland, which accounts for nearly a third of all UK food and drink exports. Scottish Government economists estimate the sector lost up to £2.8 billion in agrifood exports to the EU between 2021 and 2025 alone, with fresh salmon, shellfish, Scotch beef, and lamb battered by physical border checks and export health certificates.

Is it worth it? Angela Eagle, the Environment Secretary, thinks so. She certainly wasn't keen on the question when asked about it on Times Radio by Stig Abel on Thursday.

"We've had EU straight bananas and now we're talking about banning smoky bacon crisps. I think you should get a life."

"This is actually about ensuring that we can do business with our closest neighbours. We've got huge potential growth in our economy if we allow our farmers and food producers to be able to export into Europe again without all the huge uncertainty and the costs of frictions that Brexit brought us. This will be a deal that improves that situation."

Snack makers still have until August 2029 to lobby Brussels for an exemption, but if that fails, they will have to choose to either add real pig extract, making the crisps more expensive, shorter-lived, and off-limits to vegetarians, or rebrand.

The big question, of course, is what do you call a smoky bacon crisp if not smoky bacon?
