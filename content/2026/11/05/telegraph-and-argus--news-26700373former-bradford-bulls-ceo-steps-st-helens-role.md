FORMER Bradford Bulls Super League winner and chief executive Abi Ekoku has sensationally left his role at St Helens after just over six months.

He was only appointed as CEO there in April, but he has now stepped down for family reasons.

Before arriving at Saints, he held the same position at the RFL, on an interim basis.

"We sincerely thank Abi for his contributions to the club during what has been a very challenging year," Saints chairman Eamonn McManus said.

"In particular, we appreciate the personal sacrifices made by him due to his family residing in London.

"We wish him every success for the future, and he remains very much a friend of the club."

Current Saints board member Joe Nicholl will take over the role from Ekoku.

Ekoku has a fascinating back story, having initially been a discus thrower.

He was so good at that, he won gold at the 1992 UK Championships, shortly before moving into rugby league with London Crusaders.

It was around that time that his younger brother Efan, got his big break in the Premier League with Norwich City.

Back to rugby league, and the older Ekoku was at Halifax Blue Sox when Super League began in 1996.

But he moved to Bulls ahead of the 1997 season, helping them win their maiden Super League title that year.

He also played in their Challenge Cup final defeat to St Helens that May.

The winger retired from playing in 1998, but he has had several administrative roles in the game since.

And surely the most successful of those was at Bradford.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21672417.jpg?type=mds-article-620)A smartly-dressed Abi Ekoku pictured at a Bradford Bulls forum in 2002 during his time as CEO with the club. *(Image: NQ Staff)*

He became the club’s CEO in 2000 and helped oversee the 2001 Super League title triumph, two Challenge Cup successes and World Club Championship glory in 2002.

Ekoku left the club at the end of April 2003, just days after the second of those Challenge Cup victories, over Leeds at the Millennium Stadium in Cardiff.

He spent nearly two decades out of the game altogether, before returning last year to work at the RFL.
