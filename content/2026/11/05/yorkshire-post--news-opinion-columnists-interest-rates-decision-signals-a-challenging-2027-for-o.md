# Interest rates decision signals a challenging 2027 for our finances: The Yorkshire Post says

## Over the last three years, politicians in the UK have trumpeted falling and stabilised interest rates as a signal that the economy is moving in the right direction and inflation is under control – even if it often hasn’t felt like that to the ordinary man and woman on the street.

But if it wasn’t already clear from prices at the petrol pumps and in the shops recently, Donald Trump’s ill-fated decision to launch a war against Iran has pushed the global economy into an even more challenging direction.

The ongoing impact on the UK has been highlighted not only by confirmation of the Bank of England’s well-trailed decision to increase interest rates for the first time since August 2023 from 3.75 per cent to 4 per cent but by the bleak economic predictions which accompanies the decision.

Governor Andrew Bailey has said it is unlikely to be the last such increase unless the Middle East conflict is resolved, with projections that inflation will reach five per cent next year following substantial rises in energy bills in the coming months.

There are expectations that regulator Ofgem will increase its price cap for household bills by almost 25 per cent to an average annual bill of £2,150 in January. The Bank’s report indicated that higher energy costs could feed further into supply chains by next year, as some firms see their hedged energy prices unwind.

It predicted that British households will also face more expensive food, with food inflation expected to rise as high as 4.8 per cent next year.

There is some hope the results in the US midterm elections increase the likelihood of a resolution to the Middle East conflict but as things stand, next year looks like it will be a challenging one for everyone’s finances.
