A man who repeatedly stabbed his housemate has been detained under the Mental Health Act.

Kurt Pearn was diagnosed with paranoid schizophrenia after his arrest on March 7, Teesside Crown Court heard.

The 35-year-old had facial burns from pouring hot water over himself when he was arrested.

Prosecutor Paul Newcombe said: “The defendant and the complainant lived together in Consett.

“On this day, Pearn woke the man, spoke incoherently to him, and then stabbed him from behind with a wooden-handled steak knife.

“The victim called 999 saying he had been stabbed multiple times and I don’t know where he has gone.”

Police went to the Oak Terrace address and found the victim with stab wounds and Pearn with facial burns from pouring boiling water over himself days earlier, Mr Newcombe said.

He added: “When he was arrested, the defendant continued to speak in an incoherent manner.”

The victim was stabbed six times – four times to the arms and twice to the torso – and was taken to hospital for treatment for a laceration to the liver.

The victim refused surgery and later stopped co-operating with police, Mr Newcombe said.

Pearn, of Weavers Croft, Crook, admitted wounding after his arrest in March.

His lawyer, Chris Baker, said two psychiatric reports found that Pearn had paranoid schizophrenia.

Recorder Tony Hawks detained Pearn under the Mental Health Act.

He said: “You are a man with a serious mental health issue and you are currently detained in hospital.

“I have considered the psychiatric reports and the experts’ view is that you suffer from paranoid schizophrenia and recommended a hospital order as an appropriate sentence.”
