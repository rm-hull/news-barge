A GLOBAL climate phenomenon is expected to bring heavy and intense rain to York this winter which will impact local rivers, according to a council official.

Steve Wragg, City of York Council's highways lead, said this year's winter was expected to be wetter locally due to the El Niño forecast for later this year.

The council official, whose brief includes responding to floods, added York's defences were good but they could not eliminate risks entirely and residents would be kept informed of any issues.

It comes as a significant El Niño is likely to occur towards the end of this year and could be the strongest on record, according to the Government.

The phenomenon, caused by unusually warm surface temperatures in the Pacific Ocean, typically increases the likelihood of more extreme weather particularly in Asia, South America and parts of Africa.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/20273828.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Flood water covering New Walk from Skeldergate Bridge to Millennium Bridge *(Image: Kevin Glenton)*

UK weather tends to be less directly affected but the strength of this year's El Niño means its impact on Europe remains uncertain.

Other effects could include an increase in costs for food due to crop damage and disruptions to harvests and rising energy prices as global competition for supplies intensifies.

Council head of highways asset management Mr Wragg said York had built up a good network of defences following the floods of 2007 and 2015.

But he added officials would be watching what happens as winter approaches and the global climate phenomenon's effects begin to be felt.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21671927.jpg?type=mds-article-620)City of York Council Head of Highways Asset Management Steve Wragg *(Image: LDRS)*

The official told the Local Democracy Reporting Service (LDRS): "We will help manage flood risk, the long-range forecasts are for more storms this winter, El Niño's expected to bring heavy and intense rain.

"The weather is expected to be wetter than last year, we will see the effects of that in the Ouse and the Foss, we'll be monitoring that 24/7.

"Any residents who are concerned should check the council's and Environment Agency's online resources and if in doubt contact one of us and we'll reassure people where possible.

"We have a really good network of flood defences and level of protection in the city but that doesn't remove risks completely.

"We'll ensure that we issue flood warnings in a timely manner when we need to."
