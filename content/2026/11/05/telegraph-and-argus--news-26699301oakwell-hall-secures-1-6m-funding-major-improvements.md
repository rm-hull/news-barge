A historic Elizabethan hall will close for major conservation works supported by more than £1.6 million in funding.

Oakwell Hall, a Grade I listed building in Kirklees, will be shut to visitors from Monday, November 30, 2026, with works beginning in early 2027 and the hall expected to reopen in spring 2028.

The improvements are being supported by more than £1.6 million from Arts Council England’s Museum Estate and Development (MEND) Fund, along with investment from Kirklees Council.

Councillor Pip Harvey, cabinet member for place, culture and volunteering, said: "Oakwell Hall is such an important part of who we are in Kirklees.

"It’s part of our heritage, our history and our identity, and I’m incredibly proud that we’re investing in it.

"It’s about looking after these special places, making sure they’re here for future generations and giving the opportunity to enjoy and be part of the history that makes Kirklees so special."

The programme includes roof repairs, improved rainwater drainage, conservation of historic windows and interiors, and the installation of an accessible ground-floor toilet.

These works represent the most significant investment in Oakwell Hall in many years.

Although the hall will be closed throughout 2027, the visitor centre, car park, and parts of the grounds will remain open to the public, including the upper sections of the walled garden.

Visitors can also continue to explore the hall’s history through its virtual tour available on the Bloomberg Connects app.

The timing of the works is particularly significant, as 2028 marks 100 years of Oakwell Hall being in public ownership.

Cllr Harvey said: "Importantly, there will still be plenty of opportunities to enjoy the wider site while the hall is closed.

"The visitor centre, car park, and parts of the gardens and grounds will remain open, so people can continue to visit and enjoy this beautiful place as the work progresses.

"We would also like to thank Arts Council for providing funding and support for this milestone project."

Rebecca Ball, Area Director, North, Arts Council England, said: "Our museums, libraries and arts organisations have the power to change lives, they give us access to new worlds, teach us about our heritage and give us new perspectives on life.

"I’m so pleased that we’ll be supporting so many of our cultural organisations in the North with this capital investment, which will ensure that they can continue to deliver incredible arts and culture to their communities."
