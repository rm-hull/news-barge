The new owner of a snooker centre says they were not told there was no planning permission in place when they bought the business.

A retrospective planning application to convert Unit 10A at Beckside Business Centre into a snooker centre has been submitted to Bradford Council.

The business is currently called ASQ Snooker, and the application acknowledges that the business has operated without planning permission for eight years.

## What the new owner has said

Submitted by Qasim Siddique, the application adds: “I acquired the business in October 2025, taking over an active, fully operational enterprise.

"The landlord facilitated the sale of the business to me without disclosing that formal planning permission for leisure use was not in place, under the reasonable belief on my part that all statutory consents were satisfied.

“I was only first notified in June 2026 that the unit lacked formal planning permission.

“Having already invested heavily in purchasing and fitting out the business, absorbing these unexpected planning costs has placed a significant financial strain on me.

"Nevertheless, I have proactively funded and submitted this application in good faith to ensure full planning compliance with Bradford Council.

“Over £100,000 has been invested into high-quality internal fit-outs, acoustic insulation, safety infrastructure, and professional sports equipment.

“The business has consistently paid business rates to Bradford Council, contributing directly to the local economy.”

A decision on the application is expected before the end of the year.
