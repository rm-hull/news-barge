Ten historic buildings and sites across the North East have been added to a national “at risk” register, while four others have been saved and had their futures secured.

Historic England has revealed its Heritage at Risk Register for 2026, highlighting places threatened by neglect, decay or inappropriate development.

Among the latest additions is one of Seaham’s most historic buildings, the Londonderry Institute, which has stood in the County Durham town for more than 170 years.

![AT RISK: Londonderry Institute Seaham, County Durham](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21672339.jpg?type=mds-article-620)AT RISK: Londonderry Institute Seaham, County Durham *(Image: © Historic England Archive)*The Grade II\* listed building was designed by Thomas Oliver and opened in 1855 as a Literary and Scientific Institute.

It became an important social hub as Seaham expanded during the 19th century alongside the mining industry under the Londonderry family.

However, its interior has been heavily altered over the years, and the building has now fallen into a state of disrepair.

There are plans to breathe new life into the institute and restore it as a grand hall with a heritage and museum element.

Proposals could include historical exhibits alongside immersive digital content exploring Seaham’s history and its mining past.

![AT RISK: Londonderry Institute Seaham, County Durham](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21672338.jpg?type=mds-article-620)AT RISK: Londonderry Institute Seaham, County Durham *(Image: © Historic England Archive)*

The project could also explore the town’s links to the Londonderry family, Lord Byron and his daughter Ada Lovelace.

The Londonderry Institute is one of ten sites across the North East which Historic England says are at risk of neglect, decay or inappropriate development.

Christ Church, on Carlisle Street in Felling, Gateshead, has also been added to the register.

![AT RISK: Christchurch, Felling, Gateshead, Tyne & Wear](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21672328.jpg?type=mds-article-620)AT RISK: Christchurch, Felling, Gateshead, Tyne & Wear *(Image: © Historic England Archive)*

The Grade II listed church dates from 1866 and remains a community hub, hosting a food bank, Welcome Wednesdays and sessions for children during the school holidays.

![AT RISK: Christchurch, Felling, Gateshead, Tyne & Wear](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21672330.jpg?type=mds-article-620)AT RISK: Christchurch, Felling, Gateshead, Tyne & Wear *(Image: © Historic England Archive)*

But problems with its condition have restricted what can be offered from the building.

Lead theft and attempts to steal replacement materials have damaged the roof and allowed water to get inside, according to Historic England.

While ten sites have joined the register this year, four others in the North East have been removed after their futures were secured.

They include Loftus Conservation Area in Redcar and Cleveland, which has undergone regeneration following concerns over the loss of historic shopfronts and inappropriate alterations.

![SAVED: Loftus Conservation Area, North Yorkshire](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21672337.jpg?type=mds-article-620)SAVED: Loftus Conservation Area, North Yorkshire *(Image: © Historic England Archive)*

Work has included transforming the Congregational Church into Loftus Studios, an artists’ space, while Temperance Square has been created as a training and employment hub with garden space.

Claudia Kenyatta CBE and Emma Squire CBE, co-chief executives of Historic England, said: “Our historic buildings and places help tell the story of where we live and contribute to the character and identity of local communities.

“Each year, the Heritage at Risk Register showcases the positive impact that restoring and reusing these special places can have.

![SAVED: Loftus Conservation Area, North Yorkshire](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21672333.jpg?type=mds-article-620)SAVED: Loftus Conservation Area, North Yorkshire *(Image: © Historic England Archive)*

“The most effective way to protect our buildings is to bring them back into use as places that create connection and enjoyment for local people.

“The sites saved and removed from the Register this year show what can be achieved when partners and communities work together to deliver positive, lasting change.

“By working together, we can protect our heritage for generations to come.”

Historic England spent £1.06 million in grants supporting repairs and work at seven sites on the Heritage at Risk Register across the North East during 2025/26.

Heritage Minister Baroness Twycross said: “Every part of the UK is bursting with history, so it is vital we protect the heritage sites at the heart of communities which help tell the stories of our nation.

![SAVED: Loftus Conservation Area, North Yorkshire](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21672334.jpg?type=mds-article-620)SAVED: Loftus Conservation Area, North Yorkshire *(Image: © Historic England Archive)*“That’s why our £1.5 billion Arts Everywhere Fund is so important, supporting efforts to save more than 1,000 arts venues, museums, libraries, and heritage buildings across England.**Read more**
