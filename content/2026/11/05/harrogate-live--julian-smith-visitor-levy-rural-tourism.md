Sir Julian Smith says an uncapped overnight visitor levy would hurt rural hospitality, while ministers say it is a local choice. Updated November 2026.

Sir Julian Smith, the MP for Skipton and Ripon, has called the Government’s overnight visitor levy “a mad plan” and “another hammer blow on rural hospitality businesses”. He spoke out on Thursday 5 November, the day after the Visitor Levy (England) Bill had its first reading in the Commons.

The Bill would let strategic authorities charge a new local tax on stays in residential accommodation. Sir Julian’s objection rests on three written questions he put to ministers, and on what he says their answers failed to provide.

## One answer to three questions

He tabled the questions on 9 October, and they asked:

* what the levy would mean for businesses and the rural economy in North Yorkshire, the county that includes Harrogate, Knaresborough and Ripon
* what the impact would be on rural areas more broadly
* whether rural hospitality businesses, accommodation providers and tourist boards had been consulted to the same extent as those in urban areas

Jim McMahon replied for the Ministry of Housing, Communities and Local Government on 19 October. The parliamentary record shows the same wording given to all three.

It says the Government published its consultation response on 10 September, and that it “heard from a broad range of stakeholders, including representatives from rural areas”.

“The choice of whether to introduce a levy will rest with strategic authorities, following consultation in their area,” the answer continues. “The impacts of the levy will be determined by local decisions, and any levy will need to reflect local circumstances, including the implications for businesses, visitor destinations and rural economies.”

Strategic authorities “will be expected to consult with businesses and communities, and to assess the likely effects of their proposals before implementation”, it adds.

The answer gives no assessment specific to North Yorkshire or to rural businesses, and it does not say directly whether rural and urban groups were consulted equally.

## ‘An uncapped tax’

“These answers will do little to reassure businesses across North Yorkshire and other rural areas,” Sir Julian said. “This is a mad plan by the Government and the local Labour Mayor and is another hammer blow on rural hospitality businesses.”

His main worry is how high the charge could go.

“What is particularly worrying is that this would be an uncapped tax,” he said. “There is nothing in the Government’s approach that appears to prevent local authorities from continually increasing the levy over time, creating uncertainty for businesses and making rural destinations less competitive.”

He also pointed to the jobs he says depend on visitors.

“Tourism supports thousands of jobs across our area, from hotels and B&Bs to pubs, restaurants and visitor attractions,” he said. “The Government should be backing these businesses, not creating new barriers that risk putting visitors off and damaging local economies.”

Hospitality was also part of his complaint about the October Budget, when he criticised the lack of a VAT cut for pubs, hotels and restaurants.

## What it means for you

Nothing changes yet for anyone booking a room or running a B&B. The Bill has had only its first reading, and its second reading is the next stage.

Neither Sir Julian’s statement nor the ministerial answers give a rate or a start date for North Yorkshire. On the Government’s account, any levy here would be decided locally and would follow a local consultation, which would be the point for businesses and residents to respond.

Sir Julian’s questions and the answers are on the UK Parliament website. His full statement is on his own website.
