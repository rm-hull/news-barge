Utility companies should pay more for the permits required when highway routes are dug up for roadworks, a councillor says.

Ormesby councillor Glyn Nightingale requested figures from Redcar and Cleveland Council and quoted one payment for permission amounting to £150.

Local authorities issue permits to utility companies carrying out work on council-owned highways and other land usually to cover an agreed duration.

Charges levied are set by the Department of Transport within a statutory framework.

Cllr Nightingale told a meeting: “I thought at first it was a typo and there should have been two extra zeros included.

![Cllr Glyn Nightingale, Ormesby ward. Credit: TVCA. Free for LDRS use.](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21674854.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Cllr Glyn Nightingale, Ormesby ward. Credit: TVCA. Free for LDRS use.

“These are multi-million pound companies, we are not charging them very much for the disruption they cause, and I think we should.”

He said there were “social costs” and costs to third parties when roadworks caused disruption to people going about their business.

Cllr Nightingale said: “There’s often disruption to traffic flows and devastation to roads and footpaths which then have to be resurfaced.

“Then there is the resulting mess that is left when they [utility companies] are finished.”

The councillor cited one recent example of a roadworks scheme in Ormesby which left residents having difficulty getting in and out of their drives.

He said higher permit charges would recompense the council and also mean extra cash could go into its coffers.

Responding to a question from Cllr Nightingale on the subject, Councillor Carl Quartermain, the cabinet member for highways and transport, said there was no scope for the council to make additional charges.

He said: “The council will continue to work with the utility companies through the permit scheme to minimise disruption to residents by co-ordinating works, challenging unnecessary occupation of the highway and ensuring companies meet their obligations in terms of communication and traffic management.”
