**A Scottish Green MSP has reiterated calls for a ban on the sale of fireworks, two years after a baby red panda died at Edinburgh Zoo on Bonfire Night.**

Maggie Chapman said that the death of Roxie, who was found to have choked on her vomit as fireworks were set off near the zoo, was a “heart-breaking reminder” of the “devastating impact” they can have on animals.

Ms Chapman said: “Bonfire Night should be about bringing people together, not causing fear and distress.

![Roxie the Red Panda](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/18763794.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Roxie the red panda died on Bonfire Night in 2024. *(Image: RZSS)*

“We know that pets, wildlife and many people suffer from the noise every year,” she added.

“There is no justification for the widespread misuse of fireworks in our communities. Scottish Greens want to see an end to private firework sales.

“Safe, organised displays can still bring people together, but we must do more to protect people and animals from unnecessary harm.

“We can celebrate without causing suffering.”

However, community safety minister Kirsten Oswald told STV that the Scottish Government does not have the power to enact a blanket ban on firework sales.

She said: “Within the powers we do have, we’ve introduced a ban on buying fireworks for, or providing fireworks to, under-18s, and made attacks on emergency workers using fireworks an aggravating factor that courts can take into account when sentencing offenders.

“We have also provided local authorities with powers to designate firework control zones, which ban the use of fireworks in local areas.”

Since the death of Roxie, Edinburgh Zoo has campaigned for firework control zones in the capital, successfully securing a ban for the area around the zoo in Corstorphine.

Speaking to The Herald earlier this year, Royal Zoological Society of Scotland chief executive David Field said that the new rules meant Roxie had not died in vain.

“That one period in Edinburgh, it just went crazy,” he remembered. It went crazy, crazy, crazy.

“The impact was that Roxie died that night,” Mr Field said. “We attribute it to the extreme amount of fireworks that were around the area.

“Fireworks are amazing and they are wonderful. But they don't need to be loud and banging. After Roxie’s death, we've just received an immense amount of support.

“When we approached the police and Edinburgh City Council the response was: ‘Thank you, we need evidence, we need justification [for a ban].’ And this was a very high-profile case.

“In a strange way, it's the power of animals to change things for good. So Roxie will be remembered in that way.”

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21187970.jpg?type=mds-article-620)David Field is the chief executive of RZSS. *(Image: Eva Milne)*

Mr Field suggested using drone displays as a noise-free alternative to traditional fireworks.

“I think we saw a more mature and reasonable attitude across Edinburgh, even outside of the exclusion zones,” he said of last year’s Bonfire Night.

“I believe there was a lot better and more responsible use of fireworks.”

This year, the use of fireworks has been banned in 14 neighbourhoods across the capital; Corstorphine, Longstone, Sighthill/Broomhouse, The Murrays, Liberton Grange, Lochend, Stenhouse/Gaskell Street, West Pilton, Wester Hailes, Moredun, Seafield, Niddrie, Calton Hill and Balerno.

People who set off fireworks in those areas risk being charged with violating the terms of the Fireworks and Pyrotechnic Articles (Scotland) Act 2022. This means they could face fines of up to £5,000 and six months in prison.

Speaking ahead of this year's celebrations, council leader Jane Meagher said the city would not allow the “dangerous actions of a minority” to ruin the evening for all.

“Preparations are well underway, from Firework Control Zones and targeted enforcement to education, youth activities and practical work to tackle hazards in our neighbourhoods,” the Labour councillor said.

“We are doing everything we can to help keep Edinburgh safe, and we’re asking residents to play their part too.”

Councillor Meagher added: “We all have a role to play in keeping our communities safe. Please think about the impact your actions can have on others, follow the safety advice, and report dangerous behaviour when you see it.”
