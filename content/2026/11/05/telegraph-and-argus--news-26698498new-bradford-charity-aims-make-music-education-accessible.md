A NEW charity is working to break down barriers to music education.

Friends of Bradford Music & Arts Service, established in 2023, has now become a registered charity, enabling it to apply for additional funding and expand its support for children and young people across the district.

The charity was created by parents, carers, and supporters to help raise funds so more children can learn an instrument, join music groups, and take part in performances.

Suzie Crookes, chair of Friends of Bradford Music & Arts Service, said: "We're absolutely delighted to have achieved charity status.

"It's taken a lot of hard work from a fantastic team of volunteers, so this is a real milestone for us.

"We've seen first-hand the difference music can make to young people.

"It helps children build confidence, learn new skills, and make friends.

"Becoming a registered charity opens up new funding opportunities and will help us support even more children and young people across the district.

"Whether it's helping a family with the cost of an instrument, funding equipment, or supporting a young person to take part in musical activities, we want to make sure that cost is less of a barrier to getting involved.

"We're incredibly grateful to everyone who has supported us so far and we'd love to hear from anyone who would like to get involved.

"Whether through volunteering, fundraising, sponsorship, or donations, every contribution will help create more opportunities for local young people."

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21669772.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Students of Bradford Music & Arts Service visiting the tuck shop run by volunteers from the Friends of Bradford Music & Arts Service *(Image: Supplied)*

While many already benefit from Bradford’s Music & Arts Service, costs can be a challenge for some families.

The charity aims to help cover expenses such as instrument hire, equipment, lessons, travel, and participation in ensembles.

Carl White, head of Bradford Music & Arts Service, said: "This is a fantastic achievement for the Friends group and shows the commitment of the parents and volunteers involved.

"The charity will allow us to access funding that isn't available to us as a local authority service, helping us provide extra support and opportunities for children and young people.

"We're excited about what we can achieve together and the difference it could make in the years ahead."

Councillor Sally Birch, executive member for children and families at Bradford Council, said: "I'd like to congratulate everyone involved in Friends of Bradford Music & Arts Service on this fantastic achievement.

"The dedication shown by the parents, volunteers, and supporters behind this charity is inspiring."
