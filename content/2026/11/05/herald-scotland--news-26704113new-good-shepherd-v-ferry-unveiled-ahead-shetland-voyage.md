**The Fair Isle's long-awaited new ferry has been unveiled at a Whitby shipyard, as the £45.4 million project to replace the island's ageing lifeline vessel nears completion.**

Tarpaulins were removed this week from the scaffolding surrounding the Good Shepherd V at a boatyard in Whitby, revealing the vessel for the first time since construction began last year.

Built by Parkol Marine Engineering, the 24-metre monohull ferry has reached a stage where its hull and superstructure are largely complete and painted. The vessel is expected to be lifted into the River Esk in the coming weeks before final fitting-out work, systems testing and sea trials.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21677490.jpg?type=mds-article-620) *(Image: SIC/Paul Armstrong)*The Good Shepherd V is being built to replace the Good Shepherd IV, which has provided a vital connection between Fair Isle and the**Shetland** mainland since 1986.

In addition to carrying passengers, the ferry is a lifeline service for the island's residents, transporting freight, supplies, and larger goods that cannot be moved by air.

Work is continuing alongside the vessel's construction on harbour infrastructure in both Fair Isle and Shetland. Installation of a winch house and cradle for the Fair Isle boat noust is due to continue over the coming months, while major works at Grutness, including an extended pier and new breakwater, are nearing completion.

The overall project is estimated to cost £45.4m, with £18.6m funded by Shetland Islands Council and £26.8m provided through the UK Government's Levelling Up Fund. The council awarded the £5.6m contract to build the new ferry to Parkol Marine Engineering in June 2025.

Subject to weather conditions, the vessel is expected to sail from Whitby and arrive in Shetland before Christmas.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21677491.jpg?type=mds-article-620) *(Image: SIC/Paul Armstrong)*

Moraig Lyall, chair of Shetland Islands Council's environment and transport committee, said: "This is a key milestone for the project. After months under wraps, it's good to see the new vessel unveiled before the final stages of work take place.

"The Good Shepherd V will be such an important asset for the social and economic future of Fair Isle. I look forward to seeing more progress in the coming weeks before she leaves Whitby on her way to Shetland."
