South Tyneside residents still have time to share their views on proposed powers aiming to tackle the use of face coverings worn during incidents of antisocial behaviour or crime.

Around 300 people have already taken part in a public consultation aimed at informing South Tyneside Council’s plan to tackle people using face coverings to conceal their identity when engaging in crime or antisocial behaviour.

The local authority is proposing giving ‘authorised officers’ new powers where face coverings, such as balaclavas, are used in certain circumstances.

If introduced, the targeted action would form part of a Public Spaces Protection Order (PSPO), which is a legal power that allows councils to ban or restrict activities in a certain area.

The consultation has been asking people whether they believe there is a need for the proposed power and if it could improve feelings of safety and confidence.

![South Shields Town Hall LDRS](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21641059.jpg?type=mds-article-620)South Shields Town Hall LDRS

South Tyneside Council has previously stressed that the measure would not be a ban and would not affect face coverings worn for legitimate religious, health or work reasons.

The PSPO consultation was raised during a recent meeting of the council’s our place and environment scrutiny committee on November 3, 2026, at South Shields Town Hall.

James Maughan, head of public protection at the council, said there had already been around 300 responses to the consultation and urged members of the public to have their say before the consultation closes later this month.

He added that local authorities were taking a regional approach to the issue and that enforcement of the PSPO would “fall primarily, if not exclusively, to colleagues in the police”.

It was also noted that offences under the face coverings PSPO, if implemented, could be dealt with by a fixed penalty notice or more formal legal action for persistent breaches.

Councillor Karen Pittuck, the council’s cabinet member for highways, neighbourhoods and community safety at South Tyneside Council, previously stressed the proposals were focused on “specific situations”.

This includes situations where a “face covering may be used to conceal someone’s identity while committing crime or antisocial behaviour, or causing intimidation or distress”.

Cllr Pittuck added: “It would allow authorised council and police officers to require someone to remove the face covering.

“It is important to be absolutely clear that this is not a proposal to ban face coverings. It would not affect people wearing them for religious, health or work reasons.

“We want to hear a broad range of views, including any concerns people may have about how the proposed power could be used or enforced.”

Chief Superintendent Darren Adams, Northumbria Police’s antisocial behaviour lead, previously recognised that the use of face coverings to conceal someone’s identity to commit crime, take part in antisocial behaviour or cause fear and intimidation, was a concern for communities.

He added the force was “supportive of our local authority partners opening conversations in relation to potential restrictions.”

The council consultation on the face coverings PSPO will close at 5pm on Sunday, November 22.

You can have your say at: www.southtyneside.gov.uk/face-coverings.
