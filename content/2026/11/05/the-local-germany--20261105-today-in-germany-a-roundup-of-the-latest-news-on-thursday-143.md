**Thursday's top story:Bavarian deputy leader Hubert Aiwanger found dead in Berlin hotel**

Hubert Aiwanger, Bavaria’s deputy state leader and economy minister, has been found dead in a hotel room in central Berlin, police have said.

The 55-year-old politician was discovered on Wednesday in the Mitte district. Police and prosecutors have opened an investigation to establish the cause and circumstances of his death and rule out third-party involvement. No further details have been released.

Aiwanger had travelled to Berlin for the opening of an office belonging to his Free Voters party but reportedly missed the event after feeling unwell.

Bavarian premier Markus Söder described his death as an “absolute tragedy” and said his thoughts were with Aiwanger’s family and children.

Aiwanger had led the Free Voters in Bavaria since 2006. Under his leadership, the party entered the Bavarian government in 2018 as the junior coalition partner to Söder’s Christian Social Union.

He attracted national controversy in 2023 over an antisemitic pamphlet dating back to his school years. Aiwanger denied writing it but acknowledged carrying copies and later apologised. He refused calls to resign.

Aiwanger lived with his partner and had two children.

**Germany hopes for progress in ending conflicts after US election**

Germany's foreign minister expressed hope Wednesday that the "very clear" Democrat victory in the US midterm election might bring new initiatives to end conflicts in the Middle East and Ukraine.

"With their majority in both chambers of Congress, the Democratic Party will likely seek to shift the political course in the United States," Foreign Minister Johann Wadephul said a day after US voters dealt President Donald Trump's Republicans a historic defeat.

Through close cooperation between the United States and Europe, "there may be a new opportunity to launch a fresh initiative" to resolve conflicts, he said.

Wadephul said he was "quite optimistic" that US aid for Ukraine – which has been slashed under Trump – would be the subject of "more discussion" by US lawmakers from both parties.

The minister also noted that Trump's tariffs and the US war against Iran both appeared to play a role in the election result by driving up the cost of living.

Wadephul suggested that election results in the United States and elsewhere underscore that it is "our task" to bolster the political centre in times "characterised by the erosion of political norms and international rules, as well as by polarisation and populism".

**Property giant Vonovia 'concerned' over Berlin left's housing plans**

Europe's leading corporate landlord Vonovia said Wednesday it was "concerned" at plans by Berlin's possible incoming far-left led city administration to socialise apartments to tackle the city's housing crisis.

*Die Linke* (The Left) party won an election in the city-state in September and is now in talks with two other parties about forming a state government.

One of *Die Linke's*flagship promises in the campaign was to socialise some 240,000 apartments held by large corporate landlords.**READ ALSO: Germany’s interior ministry puts forward plan to block housing socialisation in Berlin**

"Of course, we are concerned about plans to nationalise housing owned by private companies," Vonovia CEO Luka Mucic told reporters Wednesday during a conference call on the company's third-quarter results.

He said such a move in Berlin would be the "wrong path" and "highly legally questionable", arguing that only an increase in construction could supply the 1.4 million homes needed to plug Germany's housing gap.

Landlords would compensated to the tune of 16 billion euros, averaging 100,000 euros per apartment, well below market prices.

Vonovia itself would stand to receive an estimated 12 billion euros. The group says its holdings in Berlin are worth around 20 billion euros, meaning *Die Linke's*plans would represent a significant loss.**Germany’s climate credibility wanes ahead of COP31**

Germany’s reputation as a climate leader is coming under growing pressure ahead of the COP31 summit in Antalya, Turkey as scientists and environmental groups accuse Berlin of breaking promises and pursuing contradictory policies.

Chancellor Friedrich Merz has described climate change as a threat to life and promoted green investment as an economic opportunity. Yet critics point to plans for new gas-fired power stations, slower renewable-energy expansion and efforts to weaken parts of the EU Green Deal.

Germany’s Expert Council on Climate Change expects the country to miss its target of cutting emissions by 65 percent by 2030. Campaigners also criticise falling international climate finance. Germany had pledged €6 billion annually for developing countries, but its contribution dropped from €6.1 billion in 2024 to €4.7 billion in 2025.

The government blames financial constraints and says loans and private investment will help bridge the gap.

Germany is expected to advocate fossil-fuel reductions and climate funding at COP31. Merz’s decision not to attend the leaders’ meeting has nevertheless reinforced doubts about Berlin’s commitment to international climate diplomacy.

**German children's book author Janosch dead at 95**

The German children's book author and illustrator Janosch, whose works delighted generations of children in the country, has died at the age of 95, his literary agent confirmed to AFP on Wednesday.

Horst Eckert, writing under the pen name "Janosch", created a number of iconic characters that remain instantly recognisable to Germans of all ages, including the *Tigerente* (or "Tiger Duck"), a striped wooden toy duck on wheels pulled along by a string that appeared in numerous books.

![Janosch pictured with illustrations of a bear and a tiger](https://apiwp.thelocal.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/665624112.jpg)

His children's book "*Oh, wie schön ist Panama*" ("The Trip to Panama") – which follows the journey of two friends, Little Bear and Little Tiger, with Tiger Duck in tow – was published in 1978 and went on to become a children's classic in Germany.

According to his agency, his works have been translated into more than 30 languages and have received many awards.

His agent and publisher, Bernhard Feil, told AFP that he died on Tuesday, citing the author's wife.

German President Frank-Walter Steinmeier hailed the writer's "unbridled imagination" and said that "our world has become a little poorer" with his passing.

"For countless children, for countless adults who live with children, and for everyone who has not forgotten how to marvel and wonder, Janosch has been – and remains – a companion through life like few other authors or illustrators," Steinmeier said.
