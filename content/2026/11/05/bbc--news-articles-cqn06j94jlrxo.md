!["Crushing midterm rebuke to Trump," reads the headline on the front page of the Financial Times.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/5c31/live/18e53100-d89b-11f1-a701-fddedf244c16.jpg)

Almost all the front pages carry the results of the US midterm elections, where President Donald Trump's Republican Party is suffering heavy defeats and will lose control of Congress. "Crushing midterm rebuke to Trump" is the Financial Times' verdict, embedding a tally of the results so far - which show the Democrats winning majorities in the Senate and House of Representatives. "Anger over cost of living spurs Democratic blue wave" the paper says, providing its business-focused audience a taste of the market reaction by reporting "oil prices tumble and tech hyperscalers' shares surge".

!["Vance turns on Trump," reads the headline on the front page of the Daily Telegraph.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/a3f7/live/11c994b0-d89b-11f1-b441-2b33b063138f.jpg)

The Daily Telegraph focuses on the blame-shifting, saying Vice President JD Vance "turns on Trump" and "blames Iran war" after the "Republicans' humiliating midterm election defeat". It quotes the "defiant" president after he said: I'll do it all again", according to the paper. The Telegraph also notes some of the Democrats' breakthroughs on the "record-breaking" night, including its victory in Kansas which it explains "had not elected a Democrat to the Senate since 1932".

!["Trump faces backlash as Democrats make history," reads the headline on the front page of the Times.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/88a5/live/143239d0-d8a7-11f1-93e1-3b657b665bcf.jpg)

"Trump faces backlash as Democrats make history" says the headline in the Times - placing a photo of James Talarico, the successful Senate Democratic candidate in Texas, placing a hand across his chest in front of his supporters at his victory rally, celebrating a feat not seen "for nearly 40 years". It says the "president's opponents" have "captured seven Senate seats in Iowa, Kansas, Maine, Ohio, North Carolina, South Carolina and, in the biggest prize of the night, in Texas".

!["Vance blames Trump's war for crushing defeat," reads the headline on the front page of the i Paper.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/6ec2/live/1d128700-d89b-11f1-b441-2b33b063138f.jpg)

"Vance blames Trump's war for crushing defeat" continues the i Paper. "Blue wave sweeps America - washing away Maga candidates", the paper concludes, although it adds comments from the White House: "Trump is still your President".

!["Maga turns on Trump," reads the headline on the front page of the Daily Mail.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/7b94/live/5f9082c0-d89c-11f1-a701-fddedf244c16.jpg)

"The daggers were out for Donald Trump" says the Daily Mail, writing: "JD Vance and even Don Jr blame President's Iran War debacle for poll wipeout". The Mail also highlights a piece from its veteran columnist Richard Littlejohn under the headline "A Republican friend tells me: "Trump's crazy, he's capable of anything now - even starting WWIII".

!["Trumped," is the single-word headline on the front page of the Guardian – splashed over a woman holding up her arms in joyous praise.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/7502/live/3b47df20-d8a7-11f1-93e1-3b657b665bcf.jpg)

"Trumped" is the Guardian's single-word headline. Americans "woke up to a radically altered political landscape", the paper says, as a "new era on Capitol Hill dawned".

!["Lame Donald Duck: Trump in limbo as he loses Senate and House," reads the headline on the front page of the Metro.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/f875/live/154c79e0-d89b-11f1-93e1-3b657b665bcf.jpg)

"Lame Donald Duck" says the Metro, teasing the president using the "lame duck" refrain that appears on multiple front pages, crediting the insult to the victory speech of "Democrat big-hitter Jon Ossoff", who won his Senate race in Georgia, who the paper nominates as "a likely 2028 presidential challenger".

!["Trump dumped," reads the headline on the front page of the Independent.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/6323/live/7d546750-d89b-11f1-a701-fddedf244c16.jpg)

"Trump dumped" says the Independent. "American voters rebuke president over Iran war and hand Congress to Democrats in historic election upset," it reports.

!["Trump faces final curtain: And now the end is near," reads the headline on the front page of the Daily Star.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/6582/live/221e7d30-d89b-11f1-a701-fddedf244c16.jpg)

The lyrics sung by Frank Sinatra in My Way endure on the front page of the Daily Star, which says that Trump "faces final curtain", concluding "and now the end is near". The Star also shows the president holding a microphone with a speech bubble proclaiming: "I did it Maga way".

!["Trump's election disaster: Reborn in the U.S.A." reads the headline on the front page of the Daily Mirror.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/e1aa/live/29a07c70-d89b-11f1-93e1-3b657b665bcf.jpg)

The Daily Mirror revives Bruce Springstein with: "Reborn in the U.S.A." writing that "Americans stood up to their bullying president and said no to hatred and lies".

!["Markle 'filth statsh no3'," reads the headline on the front page of the Sun.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/3368/live/15bbcec0-d8a1-11f1-93e1-3b657b665bcf.jpg)

Switching focus from the midterms in the US, the Sun tops with a report on Thomas Markle Snr, the father of the Duchess of Sussex. "Markle filth stash no3" says its headline, writing that the FBI is to "search Hollywood lock-ups" as they "investigate a potential third cache of child abuse images". Markle has strongly denied possessing any such images.

!["You are not listening!" reads the headline on the front page of the Daily Express.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/82cc/live/69048370-d89b-11f1-93e1-3b657b665bcf.jpg)

Finally, residents of Linton-on-Ouse in north Yorkshire, joined by the shadow home secretary Chris Philip, stare down the barrel of a lens on the front page of the Daily Express, under the headline: "You are not listening!", in relation to what the paper terms a "plan to dump more than 1,200 migrants into a community of just 600". It comes after a government minister says "the planned sites to house asylum seekers will be 'away from communities'," according to the paper.

The fallout from the US midterm elections features on most of the morning's front pages, including the Financial Times, external, which says Americans delivered a "crushing rebuke" to Donald Trump, after a campaign dominated by the spiralling cost of living. The Daily Mail, external agrees, arguing voters passed a "damning verdict" on the president because they were "enraged" by high inflation and rising fuel prices.

The Times, external says Mr Trump has entered a "lame duck period" after a series of "history-making moments", including the election of America's first Muslim senator. According to the Daily Mirror, external, Abdul El-Sayed won in Michigan "despite a vile smear campaign" after voters grew sick of the president's "bullying" and "narcissism".

With the headline, "Vance Turns On Trump", the Daily Telegraph, external highlights how the US vice president has blamed the Iran war for the Republicans' humiliation. It says JD Vance is now "desperately trying to distance himself" from the conflict, in what's described as a "first move towards a possible 2028 White House run". The Guardian, external warns that Donald Trump is "psychologically ill-equipped" to deal with the spotlight that is leaving him to focus on potential candidates. But the paper suggests that such a shift could "eat away" at what it calls the president's "cult-like dominance" of the Republican party.

The Sun, external says the FBI is to search lock-ups in Hollywood, rented by the father of the Duchess of Sussex, as part of an investigation into allegations of child sexual abuse. The paper says Thomas Markle's son was interviewed by agents after he informed them about the two storage units. Markle has called the claims against him "sick lies".

The Daily Express, external features the concerns of residents in the North Yorkshire village of Linton-on-Ouse, where the government plans to house more than 1,200 asylum seekers on a former RAF base. Writing in the paper, the shadow home secretary, Chris Philp, says anyone who thinks families will carry on as normal when hundreds of men are camped at the end of their road has never had to walk a teenage daughter home in the dark. Ministers say the site is designed to be as self-sufficient as possible to reduce the impact on the local community.
