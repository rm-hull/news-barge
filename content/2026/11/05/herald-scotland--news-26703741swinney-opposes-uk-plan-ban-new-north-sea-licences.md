John Swinney has said the Scottish Government does not support the UK Government's bid to ban new oil and gas licences in the North Sea.

The First Minister told MSPs that fossil fuels would be needed “for years to come”, and that SNP MP will vote against Andy Burnham’s Energy Independence Bill unless the ban is removed.

The move marks a further shift away from the position set out by Nicola Sturgeon in 2023, when the Scottish Government’s draft energy strategy included a presumption against new oil and gas exploration.

The draft legislation announced earlier this week would remove the North Sea Transition Authority's power to grant new licences, in line with Labour's manifesto pledge.

Existing fields could still produce extra oil from within their current boundaries or nearby areas.

The UK Government says that could yield around 60 million barrels and support hundreds of jobs. The Bill would also scrap gas standing charges and speed up grid connections. It does not affect the Rosebank decision.

Energy Secretary Miatta Fahnbulleh said the bill would help "build a clean energy future which people feel in their pockets, households and communities, ensuring support reaches those who need it most".

Asked by Reform UK's Scottish leader Malcolm Offord about the Labour proposals, Mr Swinney said: "The Energy Independence Bill that was introduced by the United Kingdom Government brings together a number of measures that the Scottish Government supports.

"But we do not support the provisions of that Bill in relation to the position on the granting of new licences for the exploration of new oil and gas fields, for the simple reason that any particular applications need to be considered in relation to their impact on our carbon reduction targets and given the fact that we are going to be using oil and gas for some years to come."

Following First Minister's Questions, a spokesman for Mr Swinney said the SNP would try to get the ban removed as the bill makes its way through the Commons.

He said: "We will seek to get those provisions removed from the bill that goes through Parliament. But if those provisions are in the bill, when it goes to its final vote, we can't support the bill."

The spokesman said the SNP backed other measures in the bill on fuel poverty and renewables, but that the licensing provisions amounted to "a complete ban" which "goes against" the case-by-case approach the First Minister has set out.

In what was ultimately one of her final acts as First Minister, Nicola Sturgeon published the Scottish Government's draft energy strategy, which set out a presumption against new oil and gas exploration.

While the final version of that strategy has yet to materialise, ahead of May's election the First Minister signalled a shift in his party's position, saying energy security should be considered in any new decisions on further exploration, though he stopped short of backing Rosebank and Jackdaw.

Earlier this month, delegates at the SNP conference in Aberdeen backed a motion brought by energy minister Stephen Gethins, which supported domestic energy production as "clearly preferable to importation".

Mr Swinney's spokesman said a ban and a presumption against were "probably not the exact same thing."

At FMQs, Mr Swinney was challenged from both sides. Mr Offord accused him of a U-turn, and said: "It is madness to shut down the North Sea. So let me ask the First Minister again. Why has he not fought tooth and nail to keep the North Sea open? It's Scotland's oil."

Scottish Conservative leader Russell Findlay pressed him for the second week running on whether he supports the Rosebank field west of Shetland.

Mr Swinney said again that each project must be assessed for its emissions impact, and accused Mr Findlay of asking the same questions as Mr Offord, saying: "It's the merger of the Conservative Party and Reform."

Mr Findlay said: "We back new drilling. We'd extract every valuable drop of North Sea oil and gas for Scottish jobs, lower bills and energy security."

From the other direction, Scottish Greens co-leader Gillian Mackay called the First Minister's comments a "shocking U-turn".

She said: "It is a huge departure from only a few years ago when the Scottish Government was committed to a presumption against oil and gas. When even the UK Government is taking a stronger position, it shows how far the SNP has drifted. It is a shameful retreat and a slap in the face for future generations."

Oxfam Scotland head Katherine May said: "The First Minister knows there is no such thing as a climate-compatible new oil and gas field. These tests are increasingly being used to avoid admitting that reality."
