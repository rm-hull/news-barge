Rishi Sunak has officially reopened a historic bridge in North Yorkshire following extensive restoration work.

The Long Bridge in Barton has undergone a major refurbishment funded by North Yorkshire Council and carried out by contractor PBS.

Repairs included work to the arches, resurfacing of the footbridge, and the installation of a new wooden parapet.

Mr Sunak, MP for Richmond and Northallerton, said: "It was a real pleasure to officially reopen this bridge and see this important part of Barton's history restored for residents to enjoy.

"The number of people who came along to see the bridge reopened shows just how much it means to the village.

"It's clearly a much-loved landmark, and I'm pleased that the work has been carried out to preserve it for future generations.

"I want to thank North Yorkshire Council, Barton Parish Council, PBS Construction and everyone involved in carrying out the refurbishment for their hard work in restoring the bridge."

Located in Marygate, the bridge crosses Barton Beck and is a central feature of the village.

It was granted Grade II listed status in May 2025 in recognition of its historic and architectural value.

The restoration has preserved the integrity of its stonework and brick arches.

Following the reopening, Mr Sunak joined residents for a coffee morning at Barton Village Hall, organised by the parish council.

Frank Walsh, chairman of Barton Parish Council, said: "The community of Barton was proud to welcome Rishi Sunak to reopen the bridge following its extensive restoration by North Yorkshire Council and principal contractor PBS, under the direction of bridge engineer Abrar Ahmed.

"The bridge has been at the centre of many village activities and celebrations over the years and is a treasured village landmark.

"We are grateful to Rishi and North Yorkshire Council for their support."
