# Police warn someone could be killed after youths throw fireworks at ambulances and officers

## Police have issued a stark warning after youths were caught throwing fireworks at emergency vehicles and members of the public, amid fears someone could be seriously injured or killed.

Officers reported incidents involving fireworks being thrown at police cars, ambulances and members of the public over the Halloween period.

A number of youths were dispersed by officers and returned to their parents, with investigations into their actions now underway.

Police have condemned the behaviour as reckless and warned that it could result in serious injuries or even fatalities.

As Bonfire Night celebrations continue, parents are being urged to speak to their children about the dangers of fireworks and the consequences of using them irresponsibly.

![A number of youths were dispersed by officers and returned to their parents, with investigations into their actions now underway](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/jpim-static/image/2026/11/05/16/09/screenshot-2026-11-05-160021.png?trim=26,0,26,0&width=640&quality=65)


Neighbourhood policing teams and public order officers will be carrying out additional patrols alongside partner agencies this weekend to help keep communities safe.

Police control room staff are also preparing for an increase in emergency calls, with the public urged to avoid unnecessary calls so emergency services can respond to those most in need.

Superintendent Sarah Gilmour, who is leading the force's efforts during the Dark Nights period, said: “Bonfire Night should be enjoyable for everyone, and the appalling actions of those who are intent on causing harm should not ruin everyone’s fun.

“We have a zero-tolerance approach to anyone who poses a threat to life, property, or public safety - including our officers, and for those under 18, parents will also face consequences.”

Police have reminded residents that it is illegal to set off fireworks in public places and warned against using them in ways that could endanger innocent bystanders.

Officers are urging families to take responsibility and help prevent further dangerous incidents during the Bonfire Night period.
