**The flood barrier system in Renfrew, designed to protect the town against a one-in-200-year flood event, has "proven to be fundamentally flawed", according to the leader of Renfrewshire Council.** Storm Boelo struck on October 24 and 25, during which the River Clyde reached its highest recorded level since digital records began in 1998. The flooding affected 13 businesses in**Renfrew** and caused extensive damage to the southern plant room controlling the £117 million**Renfrew** Bridge,**leaving the swing bridge inoperable for months** while investigations and repairs continue.

The council's flood barriers, which are part of the £10m North **Renfrew** Flood Protection Scheme, were not deployed.**Read More: Renfrewshire Council rejects independent review of Storm Boelo flood**

Renfrewshire Council delivered the North Renfrew Flood Prevention Scheme, which includes the flood barriers at Ferry Road and Fishers Road, and completed it in 2016 as part of the wider Metropolitan Glasgow Strategic Drainage Partnership (MGSDP) programme.

The £10m North Renfrew Flood Prevention Scheme (NRFPS) comprises a 3m-high embankment and a new underground pumping station at the Mill Burn at Fingal Road to protect more than 300 homes and businesses in North Renfrew.

At a meeting of the council's Infrastructure, Land and Environment Board, on Wednesday, a report revealed that the Scottish Environment Protection Agency (SEPA) issued no flood warning before the storm, and following a meeting of the Infrastructure, Land and Environment Board, on Wednesday, The Herald reported that the SNP-led administration was accused of "marking its own homework".

Opposition spokesperson, councillor Ben Smith, proposed an amendment calling for an external person to oversee the upcoming review. The board's **SNP** convenor did not accept the amendment, prompting a vote. Nine administration councillors voted against the proposal, while six opposition councillors backed it.

Following the vote, Labour Cllr Smith criticised the decision, arguing that residents would expect greater independence given the scale of disruption caused by the storm.

Leader of Renfrewshire Council, Iain Nicolson, said Cllr Smith's claims were "patently untrue" and added that the flood alert system has "proven to be fundamentally flawed".

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21672290.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Workers were pictured working at the barriers last week *(Image: Newsquest)*He said: “The review agreed at board involves multiple external partners independent of the Council such as Scottish Environment Protection Agency, the UK MET office,**Scottish Government** and various utility companies who are all involved in the West Resilience Partnership who co-ordinate our emergency responses.

"Cllr Smith statement that it’s the Council marking its own homework is a direct attack on council officers and patently untrue. The Renfrew Bridge is still under contractual obligations and covered by an existing warranty scheme.

"The Council will undertake a comprehensive review into the circumstances of the flooding in Renfrew which will be done in partnership with the Scottish Environment Protection Agency (SEPA) and other stakeholders to understand why the actual level of the River Clyde differed so significantly from forecast levels."

"As part of the review, we will consider commissioning independent expertise in flood risk, hydrology and coastal modelling. We can then look to identify any improvements we can make as a council to strengthen our own arrangements.

"We as an SNP administration are focussing our efforts on getting the Renfrew Bridge open as quickly as possible and bringing in changes as quick as possible to the Renfrew barrier alert system which after all was introduced by a previous Labour Council in 2013 and has proven to be fundamentally flawed.”

The review is expected to examine the council's response to Storm Boelo, which caused significant flooding across parts of Renfrewshire and damaged the recently opened Renfrew Bridge.

Officials say the flood barriers were not activated because established procedures, based on previous flood events, risk assessments and operational experience, did not indicate intervention was necessary. The council notes that since 2016, 45 amber and red weather warnings have affected the Strathclyde area without any flood alerts reaching the activation threshold.

Speaking at the completion of the works in 2016, Councillor Eddie Devine, convener of Renfrewshire Council’s Environment Policy Board, said: “This new scheme will provide a high level of protection for homes and businesses in the north of Renfrew for the next 200 years.

“It will mean an end to residents’ worries and the considerable resources that have to be spent on sandbagging and clearing up the streets when flooding occurs.”

The council said it had offered support and assistance to all affected businesses it could contact, including waste removal services. Ten of the 13 businesses are understood to have resumed trading.

The report also stresses that problems at Renfrew Bridge were unrelated to decisions over flood barrier deployment.

The bridge, which opened in May 2025, remains within its two-year defects period. Contractors and the council's insurers are investigating whether a construction defect contributed to the significant water ingress that damaged operational equipment.
