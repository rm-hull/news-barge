**Aberdeen producer and DJ Vagrant Real Estate has won the 2026 Scottish Album of the Year (SAY) Award for*Neither Collar Nor Crown*.**

The album, which brings together 25 Scottish artists from across hip-hop, folk, spoken word and Gaelic music traditions, was named the winner at a ceremony in Dundee's Caird Hall on Thursday night.

Vagrant Real Estate, the recording name of Nick Cronin, won the £20,000 top prize, beating a shortlist that included Barry Can't Swim, Brìghde Chaimbeul, Hen Hoose Collective, Swim School and VLURE.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21677625.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Produced by the Scottish Music Industry Association (SMIA), The SAY Award celebrated its 15th year at Thursday's ceremony by recognising Scottish music past, present and future. *(Image: Ryan Johnston / ryanjohnstonco)*Released on St Andrew's Day last year,* Neither Collar Nor Crown* combines English and Gaelic lyricism with traditional folk influences, rap, hip-hop and choral arrangements. Contributors include rapper Bemz, folk singer Iona Fyfe and MC Jackill.

The SAY Award, organised by the Scottish Music Industry Association (SMIA), is marking its 15th year and has distributed more than £462,000 in prize money to Scottish artists since launching in 2012.

Robert Kilpatrick, chief executive and creative director of the SMIA, described the winning record as "an unapologetically Scottish record" and "a masterpiece in both collaboration and storytelling".

He said: "Bringing together 25 Scottish artists, it's an unapologetically Scottish record that pushes the boundaries of Scottish hip-hop while drawing deeply from our shared cultural and musical heritage.

"It's visceral, powerful and a masterpiece in both collaboration and storytelling."

The Dundee ceremony also saw electronic music pioneers Boards of Canada honoured with the Modern Scottish Classic Award for their influential 1998 debut album *Music Has The Right To Children*.

The record joins a list of previous winners including Frightened Rabbit's *The Midnight Organ Fight*, Paolo Nutini's* These Streets*and KT Tunstall's* Eye to the Telescope*.

Meanwhile, singer-songwriter Nikhita was named winner of the Sound of Young Scotland Award, which supports emerging artists with funding to create a debut album.

The award package is worth up to £10,000 and includes grants for studio hire and session musicians, as well as a vinyl pressing run for the finished record.

The ceremony, held at Dundee's Caird Hall for a second consecutive year, featured performances from previous SAY Award winners and nominees, including last year's Scottish Album of the Year winner Kai Reesu and former Sound of Young Scotland recipient Alice Faye.
