Vulnerable children are being put at risk of exploitation and abuse by being placed in unregulated homes, including in Airbnb properties.

The warning comes as Durham police and crime commissioner Joy Allen renewed calls for urgent action after becoming aware of unregulated accommodation being used for children in County Durham.

She said some children have been sent to accommodation run by providers from outside the area, including rented properties and Airbnbs.

Ms Allen said: “I will not stop fighting for those children who need our protection most.

“As Police and Crime Commissioner, I see first-hand the devastating consequences when vulnerable children are not adequately safeguarded. Too often, this can lead to exploitation, abuse and lifelong trauma.”

![Durham Police and Crime Commissioner Joy Allen](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/20209900.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Durham Police and Crime Commissioner Joy Allen *(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)*

She wants stronger checks on accommodation and staff, greater powers for Ofsted to investigate and close illegal homes and more foster carers and regulated children’s homes - and has raised her concerns with Government ministers, Ofsted, the Children’s Commissioner, councils and MPs.

She added: “Children who have already experienced instability and adversity deserve safety, care and support, not environments that leave them exposed to further risk.”

Darlington Borough Council said the safety of children in its care was of “utmost importance” and it remained in regular conversations with Ofsted.

A spokesperson said: "We recognise the concerns raised by the Police and Crime Commissioner and support efforts to strengthen the safeguarding of vulnerable children.

"Where placement decisions are required, these are made following careful assessment, taking account of the child's needs, risks and circumstances, and are kept under regular review.

"Like councils across the country, we continue to face challenges arising from a national shortage of suitable placements.

"We are working with regional partners to increase the availability of high-quality foster care and regulated residential provision to ensure children receive the care, stability and support they need."

Last year, Durham County Council voted to declare a crisis in children's social care and Deputy council leader Darren Grimes said it was the "most urgent threat to the well-being of our children and the financial stability" of the county.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/19720831.jpg?type=mds-article-620)(Left to right) Council Leader Andrew Husband, Deputy Leader Darren Grimes, and cllr Joe Quinn. *(Image: Stuart Boulton)*

Rachel Farnham, Durham County Council’s director of children and young people’s services, said: “We take our responsibility to identify and secure registered provisions for all children in care very seriously.

"We share the Police and Crime Commissioner’s belief that unregistered placements are unacceptable.

"However, in the most exceptional of circumstances, when every alternative has been fully explored and exhausted, they can also be unavoidable."

She said Ofsted had recently highlighted the “intense scrutiny and detailed risk analysis” carried out by the council to keep children safe, as well as its work to increase the number of regulated placements available.

“Our Beacon Hub and supporting solutions service, meanwhile, is helping families to rebuild relationships when there is a risk of them breaking down," she continued.

“The 12 North East councils have also recently been successful in a bid to become one of the Government’s eight Regional Care Cooperatives. This will allow us to go even further to tackle some of these challenges.”

The Government has pointed to £2.4bn being invested in its Families First Partnership programme and plans to create another 10,000 foster placements.

Ofsted is also setting up a dedicated team to tackle the use of unregistered children’s homes nationally.
