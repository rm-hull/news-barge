A MAJOR new exhibition exploring the work of women artists is set to open in York - featuring a piece of art which was recently found in a private collection.

York Art Gallery will launch Interwoven: Stories of Women, Textiles and Art on February 5, 2027, running until June 6, 2027.

The exhibition will feature contemporary textile art from the gallery’s own collection as well as loans from artists including Anna Perach, Lubaina Himid, and Magdalena Abakanowicz.

Livia Turnbull, curator of contemporary art at York Art Gallery, said: "Interwoven has been an absolute pleasure to curate so far, working with artists who create striking, bold, and ambitious artworks which explore and challenge the status of textile art and its association with domesticity.

"We are so pleased to be working with the Women’s Art Collection, to bring the Relative Ties exhibition to the north of England.

"It tells an important story of the shifting expectations placed on the Nicholson women and how family shaped their creative output.

"Hosting Relative Ties gives historical context and depth to the contemporary artworks that will be on display in York Art Gallery’s exhibition Interwoven."

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21675474.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Anna Perach, Olimpia (2025) © Anna Perach. Courtesy Richard Saltoun Gallery, London, Rome and New York *(Image: Provided)*

The exhibition will also include work from three generations of the same family: Mabel Nicholson, Nancy Nicholson, EQ Nicholson, and Louisa Creed - whose work will visit on tour from the Women’s Art Collection at Murray Edwards College, University of Cambridge.

'Relative Ties' traces the creative legacy of three generations of women from the same family, spanning more than 100 years of artistic work across paper, fabric, rugs, interior design, wallpaper, letterpress design, and painting.

Harriet Loffler, director of the Women’s Art Collection, said: "I am thrilled that the Relative Ties exhibition will tour to York Art Gallery.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21675465.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Flying Carpet Hollybush Garden Photo Andy Keate *(Image: Provided)*

"This represents a homecoming as the gallery hosted the landmark 1988 exhibition showcasing the remarkable work of the artist sisters-in-law Nancy Nicholson and EQ Nicholson.

"The connection is strengthened further by the fact that Louisa Creed, EQ’s daughter, has lived in York for decades and is a key figure in the artistic and creative life of the city.

"We hope visitors will flock to see this remarkable exhibition that considers what we inherit from our mothers, what happens when we collaborate with our siblings, and what gets passed on to our daughters."

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21675473.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Katarzyna Perlak, 'Mother Tongue, Woven Skin' (2025). Installation view, Liverpool Biennial *(Image: Provided)*

Nancy and EQ Nicholson were previously recognised at York Art Gallery in the 1988 exhibition The Nicholsons: A Story of Four People and Their Designs, with this show opening nearly 40 years later.

The exhibition will also mark the rediscovery of a long-lost work by Mabel Nicholson, titled Nancy in the Tartan Frock, c.1910 which was recently found in a private collection.

It will return to public view more than a century after it was last shown.

The exhibitions will be included in general admission to York Art Gallery.
