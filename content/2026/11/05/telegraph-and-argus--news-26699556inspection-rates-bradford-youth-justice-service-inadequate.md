Serious failings have been uncovered in the service responsible for supporting some of the most vulnerable children in Bradford.

The Bradford Youth Justice Service (YJS), which supervises children aged 10 to 17 with complex needs—including those in care—was rated 'inadequate' following a reactive inspection by His Majesty’s Inspectorate of Probation.

Martin Jones, chief inspector of probation, said: "We found a service with a genuine desire to improve outcomes for children, with leaders and staff working to embed child-first principles.

"However, risks to children and others were not analysed consistently, responsive planning was weak, and management oversight was significantly variable, leaving too many children with unmet safety and wellbeing needs."

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21671404.jpg?type=mds-article-620)An image of a teenage boy *(Image: UGC)*

Inspectors identified "significant weaknesses in the ability of the YJS to keep children and communities safe" and found that governance and partnership arrangements had not provided enough oversight or challenge to improve frontline practice.

The report said this required urgent attention.

Partnership working was described as ineffective, with agencies often working in isolation and prioritising their own objectives instead of providing a coordinated response for children in the youth justice system.

Despite these concerns, the report acknowledged that staff at Bradford YJS were dedicated to supporting children and demonstrated strong values and knowledge.

However, workforce pressures—including staff vacancies, high sickness absence, and gaps in partner provision—had led to excessive workloads and inconsistent practice.

Mr Jones said: "The challenge for Bradford YJS is to ensure, at pace, that governance, partnership activity, and management oversight are focused on improving the quality of assessing, planning and delivery, particularly in relation to children’s safety and the safety of others.

"A whole-system response from all partners will be required for the YJS be able to achieve consistently positive outcomes for children while also keeping them, and communities, safe."

The inspectorate made nine recommendations, including five for the Bradford YJS management board.

These include taking collective ownership of performance and implementing a robust assurance framework to drive rapid improvements in work to keep children and communities safe.

Four of the recommendations are directed at the Youth Justice Service itself, including the need to improve the quality and depth of assessments, planning and delivery to better safeguard children and the public.

Fieldwork for the inspection was carried out over two weeks in June 2026.

It included a review of cases involving children subject to bail or remand, court disposals and out-of-court resolutions, as well as interviews with case managers.

Ruth Terry, chair of the Bradford Youth Justice Partnership Board, said: "The Board and all the statutory partners collectively own the changes that are needed.

"Through stronger oversight, consistent partner contributions, and a continued focus on performance, we will deliver the rapid progress required to strengthen practice and keep children and communities safe.

"The report identified positive foundations on which to build, including feedback from parents and carers about respectful relationships with staff and interventions that had helped children make meaningful changes in their lives.

"Inspectors also found that leaders and staff were working to embed child-first, restorative, and trauma-informed principles throughout the Youth Justice Service.

"However, we recognise that these strengths are not yet reflected consistently in the quality of practice experienced by every child.

"We accept the findings of the inspection and are already working with partners to deliver a coordinated response to the recommendations, with a clear focus on achieving sustainable and measurable change."

Charlotte Ramsden OBE, chief executive of Bradford Children and Families Trust, said: "Keeping children safe, helping them make positive changes, and protecting victims and communities remains our primary focus.

"This is a disappointing report, and we take the findings seriously.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/16113291.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Charlotte Ramsden OBE, Chief Executive, Bradford Children and Families Trust *(Image: Bradford Council)*

"This was a partnership inspection and a partnership improvement plan is already in place, along with specific plans for the youth justice service itself.

"Work is underway to strengthen multi-agency working, quality assurance, analysis, and management oversight, supported by additional investment in staffing and workforce development.

"We are determined to learn from these findings, work at pace with all our statutory partners, and deliver the changes needed to strengthen practice, keep children safe, reduce reoffending, and create safer communities."

Councillor Sally Birch, Bradford Council's portfolio holder for children and families, said: "We accept the findings from His Majesty’s Inspectorate of Probation’s report into Bradford’s youth justice services.

"Making Bradford a great place to grow up is a top priority for us so it is vital that we have effective services in place that keep our children and our communities safe and that also support the victims of crime.

"It is clear that we have work to do to achieve this priority.

"Where the inspection found services have not been good enough, we have already put plans in place so they improve quickly, and we will work with all the statutory partners to make sure that they do.

“We know there is a lot more to do, and we have clear plans in place to make sure we continue this at pace.

"We recognise that this important area for the children and families of our district must deliver better experiences and outcomes for them.”
