Plans for what is expected to become the UK's largest lithium extraction facility have taken another step forward after a £500,000 funding boost for the County Durham project.

Weardale Lithium secured the grant from Business Durham’s Eastgate Regeneration and Infrastructure Catalyst Fund to develop its proposed facility at the former Eastgate cement works near Stanhope.

The funding will be used to carry out preparatory work at the brownfield site, which received unanimous planning approval earlier this year.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21673342.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Aerial view of the former Eastgate cement works near Stanhope, the brownfield site where Weardale Lithium plans to develop a major lithium extraction facility. *(Image: Weardale Lithium)*

When fully operational, the facility is expected to produce at least 12,000 tonnes of battery-grade lithium carbonate annually and create about 125 jobs.

The project aims to bring back into use a site that has lain dormant since the closure of the Eastgate cement works more than 20 years ago.

Weardale councillor John Shuttleworth has long campaigned for new investment at the former cement works site after its closure in 2002 resulted in the loss of 147 jobs.

He said: "I have wanted something on there since it closed. It has taken almost 25 years and something is better than nothing.

"If it brings young families to the area it will be even better and any jobs it brings to the area are most welcome."

The development forms part of a wider push to establish County Durham as a centre for lithium production and processing, supporting growing demand from the electric vehicle and battery manufacturing sectors.

Councillors backed the plans in February, calling the scheme a major opportunity for Weardale.

At the time, committee members highlighted the jobs, investment and long-term economic benefits it could bring to the area.

The latest announcement provides fresh momentum for the project and comes as interest continues to grow in Weardale's lithium resources.

Elsewhere in the dale, Northern Lithium has demonstrated the commercial potential of extracting lithium from local brines.

In 2023, the company said it had produced battery-grade lithium carbonate at industrial scale during trials with technology partner Evove.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21673321.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Lithium carbonate produced from Weardale brines during industrial-scale trials by Northern Lithium and technology partner Evove, highlighting the area's potential to supply critical minerals for electric vehicle batteries. *(Image: Press release)*

Together, the projects have raised hopes that Weardale could play an important role in securing a domestic supply of lithium, a critical mineral used in rechargeable batteries.

Councillor Kyle Genner, Durham County Council's cabinet member for neighbourhoods, environment and police relations, welcomed the investment.

He said: "The Eastgate Regeneration and Infrastructure Catalyst Fund has been earmarked for the former cement works for almost two decades, so it is great to see it finally being used to bring this site back to economic life.

"This new facility will not only give this disused site new purpose, but create secure, local job opportunities for the people of Weardale and the wider county.

"We are therefore extremely pleased to support this scheme which will help provide impetus for this burgeoning new industry at the heart of Weardale."

Weardale Lithium chief executive Stewart Dickson said the funding would let the company move to the next stage of development.

He said: "The redevelopment of this brownfield site represents a significant opportunity to bring new industrial activity and skilled employment to Weardale, while contributing to the UK's ambition to develop a secure domestic supply of critical minerals.

"The North East already has a strong automotive and battery manufacturing sector, and our ambition is to establish lithium production in County Durham as an important part of that supply chain."
