Scottish stage and screen stars Alan Cumming and Shirley Henderson are to take a hit Pitlochry Festival Theatre musical to New York.

A History of Paper, which the actors performed in at the Perthshire venue this summer, has been confirmed for a 13-week “Off-Broadway” run in 2027.

Written by the late playwright Oliver Emmanuel and composer Gareth Williams, A History of Paper started life in 2017 as a Radio 4 play before a work-in-progress show at Dundee Rep two years later, returning to the venue in September after its sold-out Pitlochry run.

Cumming and Henderson, who first performed together in children’s drama series Shadow of the Stone in 1987, will be reunited on stage when the musical opens at the Stage 42 theatre on 42nd Street in February.

A History of Paper, which is billed as “a musical about joy, grief and all the little things that make up a life”, charts the love story of a couple through the paper artefacts of their relationship.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21675251.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Alan Cumming, Gavin Whitworth and Shirley Henderson starred in the Scottish musical A History of Paper at Pitlochry Festival Theatre this year. *(Image: Tommy Ga-Ken Wan)*

Cumming saw A History of Paper at the Traverse Theatre in Edinburgh, which co-produced an award-winning initial run of the musical with Dundee Rep at the 2024 Fringe, weeks before he was unveiled as Pitlochry Festival Theatre’s new artistic director.

Cumming joined forces with Dundee Rep to commission a revival of the show as part of his first season of shows in Pitlochry, and persuaded Henderson to star opposite him.

The Herald’s theatre, Neil Cooper, described this year’s revival as “a living monument that preserves the emotional paper trails that chart the magic moments of people’s lives”.

A History of Paper will be the first production from either Pitlochry Festival Theatre or Dundee Rep to secure an Off-Broadway transfer when it runs from February 12-May 16 next year.

Cumming, who moved to New York to pursue his stage and screen career in the 1990s, said he was thrilled to be able to take the show to his “adopted home” in the United States.

Cumming said: “This is one of these rare shows that transcends its own story and manages to touch an audience in a really visceral and beautiful way. And it utterly belongs in New York City.

“Since taking over as artistic director at Pitlochry Festival Theatre, it has been my plan (and dream) to start a theatrical exchange channel between Manhattan and our theatre in the hills.

“This is the first show to do so and I couldn’t be happier to be doing it alongside Shirley, who I first worked with over forty years ago.”

Henderson will be making her New York stage debut in A History of Paper.

She said: “When Alan whispered backstage to me that we might be taking our show A History of Paper to New York, my heart skipped a beat.

“In that moment, I felt excited, a little scared, but happy to be holding hands with Alan as we take this beautiful piece of writing with sweet music on the next part of its journey.

“I never imagined it was possible to have the opportunity to step onto the New York stage. I am glad it’s with my lovely friend Alan Cumming.”

Andrew Panton, Dundee Rep’s artistic director, said: “Creating this show with Alan, Shirley and our amazing creative team has been a dream.

“Some stories ask us to lean a little closer. A History of Paper is one of them – the story of one relationship quietly becoming about all of us: the people who shape our lives, the memories we hold on to, and the ordinary moments we come to treasure most.

“The work I’ve seen in New York has long inspired me to make theatre. To see work developed at Dundee Rep reach international audiences is a powerful reminder of why we invest in new work and the artists who create it. I couldn’t be more excited to bring our beautiful show to New York City audiences.”
