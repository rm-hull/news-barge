A County Durham teenager who successfully campaigned to save a project supporting people experiencing homelessness has received national recognition for his efforts.

Deaken Towle, 13, from Shildon, was presented with a British Citizen Youth Award (BCyA) Medal of Honour at the Palace of Westminster, celebrating his commitment to volunteering and community work with Shildon Alive.

The award recognises young people aged 18 and under who make a positive difference in their communities, and comes as the latest accolade for Deaken, who was previously named Young Person of the Year at The Northern Echo's County Durham Together Awards.

Now in his second year volunteering with Shildon Alive, Deaken has spent countless hours supporting the charity's work helping people facing hardship through its community hub, food bank and advice services.

However, it was his determination to revive the charity's "Hand Up" project that helped earn him national recognition.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21670200.jpg?type=mds-article-620)County Durham teenager Deaken Towle receives his British Citizen Youth Award Medal of Honour at the Palace of Westminster in recognition of his work supporting people experiencing homelessness through Shildon Alive. *(Image: Tani-Dee Robinson)*

The scheme provides essential items including toiletries, hygiene products and weather protection for people experiencing homelessness.

When funding for the project came to an end, Deaken decided to take action.

In September last year, he wrote to Durham County Council asking for support.

His letter led to an invitation to address councillors, where he outlined the importance of the project and the wider impact of homelessness across County Durham.

His campaign was successful and funding was secured to continue the initiative.

Speaking about the project, Deaken said he felt it was important to "carry it on".

"What motivates me is seeing all the people suffering and being helpful to the community," he said.

The teenager added that volunteering had changed him "in every way" and said one of his ambitions was to encourage more young people to get involved.

His work has since attracted the attention of Durham County Council's homelessness service, which visited Shildon Alive to discuss the project and ways of supporting homelessness services locally.

During the visit, the charity donated 15 Hand Up bags to aid its work.

Away from the project, Deaken has also taken part in sponsored sleep-outs organised by homelessness charity Cornerstone and has helped collect used crisp packets to be transformed into blankets and washbags for people in need.

The sleep-outs remain among his favourite memories from volunteering.

Speaking after receiving the award, Deaken said: "Receiving a BCyA medal means a lot to me, and the awards ceremony is a day I'll always remember.

"Working with Shildon Alive and Cornerstone, I hope I've helped more people understand homelessness and encouraged them to support those in need.

"We can't always solve every problem, but we can do our best to help make a difference. I love helping my community and aspire to keep doing more to help others in the future."

Deaken's mother, Tani-Dee Robinson, said his passion for helping others was clear to see.

"He often comes to me saying, 'I've got this idea'," she said.

Reflecting on the ceremony, she described the experience as "absolutely phenomenal".

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21670158.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Award-winning Shildon teenager Deaken Towle, 13, who was recognised at the Palace of Westminster for his work supporting people experiencing homelessness through Shildon Alive. *(Image: Kate Knight)*

For Deaken, the evening brought another unexpected reason to celebrate after he learned he had been promoted to Lance Corporal with the Cadets.

"It was the best experience," he said. "To share the event with other people around my age was heartwarming."

Lauren Robinson, who works closely with Deaken through Shildon Alive, said his contribution to the charity had been invaluable.

"Deaken's kindness, enthusiasm and willingness to help others shines through in everything he does," she said.

"He has supported our community shop, food bank and local people experiencing difficult circumstances.

"His involvement with the Hand Up project has helped us extend our support beyond our usual services, showing how young people can make a meaningful difference in their community.

"His work demonstrates the power of young volunteers to inspire positive change."

The award was presented by CBBC presenter Ben Shires in front of Deaken's family, fellow recipients and BCyA patron Dame Mary Perkins.

Dame Mary, founder of Specsavers, praised the teenager's achievements, describing him as "a true community champion whose kindness, commitment and dedication have made a lasting difference to the lives of others".

She added: "They embody the very best of citizenship and show how one person can make a real difference."

Mike Faulkner, director of the British Citizen Youth Awards, said it had been "a fantastic evening" celebrating inspiring young people from across the UK and recognising their outstanding contributions to their communities.
