A cricket club has apologised after a hedgehog was found trapped in loose netting left on its ground, prompting a review of safety procedures.

The hedgehog, now named Picard, was discovered by members of the public at the weekend after becoming entangled in netting at Otley Cricket Club's facilities.

After cutting him free, they took him to Otley-based Prickly Pigs Hedgehog Rescue, where he was found to be suffering from hypothermia and was tired, hungry and thirsty.

A spokesperson for the rescue said Picard was now recovering after staff removed the remaining netting wrapped around his neck and legs.

![](https://www.ilkleygazette.co.uk/resources/images/21675790.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The netting which had to cut from Picard *(Image: Prickly Pigs Hedgehog Rescue)*

## Rescue's warning

The rescue used the incident to remind organisations and residents of the dangers posed by unsecured netting.

In a social media post, a spokesperson said: “This isn’t the first and won’t be the last if nothing is done.

“It doesn’t take long just to either pack away netting or pin it up after it’s been used. Hedgehogs are now an endangered animal in the UK with their status on The Red List being ‘vulnerable to extinction’.”

## What the cricket club has said

Responding to the incident, Otley Cricket Club issued a public apology and outlined steps it has taken to prevent a repeat.

A club spokesperson said: “Earlier this week, a little hedgehog named Picard was found trapped in loose netting left on our ground.

"He was cold, exhausted, and dehydrated, but thanks to kind members of the public and the wonderful team at Prickly Pigs Hedgehog Rescue he is now getting the care he needs.

“It was an easy oversight at the end of the season, but an oversight with dangerous consequences. With hedgehogs now listed as vulnerable to extinction in the UK, we know good intentions aren't enough.

“We have taken immediate action: all our netting is tied high off the ground for winter, and we have updated our protocols so players, coaches, and staff ensure nets are packed away or pinned up after every single session.”

The club said it was sharing details of the incident to encourage other sports organisations to check their own facilities.

“We are sharing our mistake openly because we want other sports clubs, cricket grounds, and football facilities to check theirs today,” the spokesperson added.

“It only takes two minutes to walk the pitch, lift a trailing net off the grass, or pack it away. It costs nothing, but it could save a life.”
