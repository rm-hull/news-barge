TG Jones is set to close its store at Teesside Park in the new year.

The national chain, formerly WHSmith, revealed it would be closing its store at the shopping park last week when a closing down sale was launched.

It means that TG Jones will soon no longer have a presence in Stockton, with the nearest branch being Middlesbrough’s Cleveland Centre.

Bright yellow signs now surround the windows of the store stating that “everything must go” .

Bosses say the closure comes amid work to "secure the long-term future” following the implementation of a court-approved restructuring of the business.

The store is expected to shut on January 9, following the closing down sale.

A spokesperson said: “As we work to secure the long-term future of TGJones, we have confirmed that our Teesside Park shop will close.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21670416.jpg?type=mds-article-620)TG Jones at Teesside Park *(Image: CONTRIBUTOR)*

“We know this will be disappointing news for our colleagues, customers and the local community.

“Our focus now is on supporting those affected and continuing to serve customers throughout the closure period.

“We would like to thank all of our colleagues for their commitment and the contribution they have made to TG Jones."

TG Jones is expected to close around 150 stores after securing approval for its restructure in a bid to rescue the business from insolvency.

The company has suffered from long-term sales decline due to high inflation, increased online shopping, lower consumer spending and rising labour costs, the court heard.

The rebranding from WH Smith to TG Jones also negatively impacted sales, with the original brand name still remaining for some airport and train station stores.

TG Jones will also soon close at the Cornmill Shopping Centre in Darlington on November 28.

Teesside Park, which is currently celebrating its 35th anniversary, is currently operating at full occupancy following the recent openings of Office and Oliver Bonas.

The two brands took over from Charles Clinkard and Pro Cook respectively, which both pulled down the shutters earlier this year before the units were revamped and reopened.
