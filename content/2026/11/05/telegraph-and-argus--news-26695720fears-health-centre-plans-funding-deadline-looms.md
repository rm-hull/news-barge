It is hoped that a “critically important” scheme will still go ahead, despite a funding deadline rapidly approaching.

Plans to build a new Health and Wellbeing Centre on North Street in Keighley have been in the pipeline for years, and over £3 million in Towns Fund cash has been pledged to the project.

The centre, built on the former Keighley College site, will host numerous NHS services, and has been tied with the rebuild of Airedale Hospital.

## Towns fund cash has to be spent soon

The project will get £3,400,000 from the Towns Fund, a pot of cash awarded to Keighley by the Conservative Government in 2019.

But Towns Fund cash has to be spent by March 2028, and although the health centre has obtained planning permission, there has been little update on the scheme in recent months.

Last week, members of Bradford Council’s Regeneration and Environment Scrutiny Committee were given updates on the Keighley and Shipley Towns Fund schemes, and officers were asked for an update on the health centre.

## Red rating for health centre

A report to members listed projects and their progress, assigning them “traffic light” colours to show the risks that the projects may not proceed.

While most of the Keighley projects have either been completed or are “green,” the health centre has a red rating – raising concern the money might not be spent before the March 2028 deadline.

The report said: “The Keighley Health and Wellbeing Centre remains a strategically important project with the objective to deliver improved access to health and wellbeing services from a central town centre location, while supporting wider regeneration objectives and helping to increase activity and footfall within Keighley.

“Airedale NHS Foundation Trust remain committed to the scheme and is continuing to work collaboratively to develop a delivery approach that aligns with the funding requirements and envelope currently available.”

## Scheme is 'vital' to Keighley

Councillor Amjad Zaman (Lab, Keighley Central) said the scheme was vital to the town as it would provide services that residents currently have to attend Airedale Hospital for.

He said: “This is right in the centre of Keighley. If you go to Airedale Hospital sometimes you face a 14-hour wait alongside people who are screaming and crying.

“I know of parents who won’t take their children to A&E because the hospital can’t see them fast enough.

“Whatever you can do to get a health facility into Keighley, you will get our full support.”

## Time scale questioned

Cllr Ibrar Ali (Bradford Moor, Bradford Independent Group) questioned the time scales.

He said: “This is quite a large project, and we are only 18 months from when it is meant to be completed. What are the risks of this scheme collapsing?”

Adam Brannen, Assistant Director for Regeneration & Economy, said the funding would come from both the Towns Fund and the NHS’ New Hospital Programme fund.

He told members there may be some flexibility that would allow the scheme to keep its funding if the 2028 deadline passes – as long as the plans are at an advanced enough stage.

Urging officers to ensure the development happens, Cllr Zaman said: “This is a really critical project for the people of Keighley, Haworth, even Bingley.”
