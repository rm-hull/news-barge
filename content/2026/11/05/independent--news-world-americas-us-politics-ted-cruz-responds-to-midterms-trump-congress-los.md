Texas Senator Ted Cruz delivered a scathing assessment of his party’s sweeping defeats following major Democratic victories across the United States, describing the losses as a "blue tsunami" after Republicans were ousted from nearly every major office across his home state.

He pointed to elevated gas prices, a rise in home and automobile repossessions, alongside record numbers of weekly plasma donations, as clear indicators that citizens across the country were struggling financially.

"That is very real pain from real Americans, and too many Republican politicians seem like they didn’t understand or they didn’t care," Cruz added.

Cruz launched into the economic crisis many Americans are facing on a daily basis.

"2026 was not simply a blue wave. It was a blue tsunami. It was an a-- kicking," Cruz said on an episode of his podcast, Verdict with Ted Cruz, released Wednesday. "It was not simply a bad 2006 midterm election. It was a Watergate-level repudiation."

His commentary came as Democrats ended more than three decades of Republican rule in a state long considered out of reach for the party.

James Talarico claimed a U.S. Senate seat against scandal-plagued Republican nominee Ken Paxton, while Gina Hinojosa ousted three-term Governor Greg Abbott. Meanwhile, Democrat Vikki Goodwin defeated Lieutenant Governor Dan Patrick, and Democrat Nathan Johnson won the attorney general’s race.

Democrats in Texas also won statewide seats overseeing land, agriculture, and energy regulation, along with judicial offices, while successfully flipping control of the state’s House of Representatives.

"Nowhere are the consequences of this election greater than Texas," Cruz said. "We need to listen and respond. We need to champion economic policies that make a real difference in working people’s lives and we need to deal with the consequences."

The election results represented a dramatic reversal in a state that President Donald Trump won by approximately 14 percentage points in 2024, where Republicans had held every statewide position since 1994.

The shift formed part of a widespread nationwide surge for Democrats, signaling a broad rejection of MAGA and a rebuke of policies under Trump, including tariffs and the war in Iran, which voters blame for driving up cost-of-living expenses.

"Food is too high, gas is too high, diesel is too high, insurance is too high, life is too high," Cruz said. "Too many Republican politicians seem like they didn’t understand or they didn’t care."

The 55-year-old senator has represented Texas in the US Senate since 2013 and is not scheduled for reelection until 2030.

A former state solicitor general and longtime Trump ally, Cruz previously survived two high-profile Democratic challenges that generated hopes of a Democratic realignment in Texas. He defeated Beto O’Rourke by nearly 3 points in 2018 before defeating Colin Allred in 2024 by over 8 points, despite polling indicating a tight race.

"I am the only statewide elected Republican who remains in Texas," Cruz said. "That’s not something I ever thought I would say."
