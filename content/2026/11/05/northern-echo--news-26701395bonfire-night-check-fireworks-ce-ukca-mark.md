Police and firefighters are urging firework buyers to check for safety marks before Bonfire Night, warning that uncertified products can be dangerous.

Thousands across the North East are preparing to celebrate Bonfire Night tonight, November 5.

Residents are being encouraged to attend one of the organised events happening across the region over the coming weeks, where trained operators handle the fireworks and safety measures are in place.

These displays reduce the need for members of the public to store, light and manage fireworks themselves.

Durham Constabulary have said anyone holding a display at home should follow the safety instructions, store fireworks carefully and buy them from a licensed retailer with a CE or UKCA mark.

Fireworks should be stored and used as directed, away from people, flames and other sources of heat.

Fireworks should be disposed of according to firefighters' advice, which is to soak the used firework in water for 24 hours after letting it cool, before disposal.

Any legitimate fireworks should hold either a CE or UKCA mark, which show that fireworks have been tested against recognised safety standards.

The CE mark shows a product meets European safety, health and environmental requirements, while UKCA means it meets UK safety standards.

Both marks are valid, and businesses can sell fireworks carrying either one.

For fireworks, certification covers how they are made, their stability and how safely they work.

A certified firework should work as expected when lit, without putting users or nearby people at unnecessary risk.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21673679.jpg?type=mds-article-620)If no CE or UKCA mark can be found on the box, buyers should avoid the product, regardless of its price or how it is being sold. *(Image: European Union/UK Government)*

Uncertified fireworks have not undergone the same testing, so they may not burn, launch or burst as intended.

Unmarked or unofficially imported fireworks are more likely to misfire, topple or behave unpredictably.

The mark is usually printed on the outer packaging. Buyers should also look for the firework category, such as F1, F2 or F3, the required safety distance and clear instructions in English.

If no CE or UKCA mark can be found on the box, buyers should avoid the product, regardless of its price or how it is being sold. Packaging and cost alone should not be used to decide whether a firework is safe.

Using a licensed retailer is the first step when buying fireworks for a private display.

Selling uncertified fireworks is against the law, and using illegal fireworks could bring a fine of up to £5,000 or six months in prison, Durham Constabulary has warned.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21640212.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Durham and Darlington Fire and Rescue Service station manager Kev Bell said fireworks should only be bought from a licenced retailer, and must bear the CE or UKCA markings. *(Image: CDDFRS)*

Durham and Darlington Fire and Rescue Service station manager Kev Bell said: "The best way to protect your loved ones this Bonfire Night is by attending an organised fireworks display."

"Whereas having fireworks or bonfires at home increases the risk of people suffering injuries such as burns and fires breaking out, which can put lives and property at risk.

“We cannot stress enough how important it is that only an adult handles fireworks and that these are bought from a licensed retailer, bearing the CE or UKCA mark on the box.”

Parents have also been warned about the risks of sparklers, which should never be given to children under five because they can cause serious burns.

Northumbria Police and partner agencies recently seized thousands of pounds’ worth of illegal fireworks in North Shields ahead of Bonfire Night.

Paul Russell, Tyne and Wear Fire and Rescue Service’s area manager for community safety, said illegal fireworks put the public at risk.

He added: "By purchasing only from licensed traders, people can help protect themselves, their families and their communities."

Buyers should check the safety marks and instructions before leaving the shop.

If the safety information is missing or unclear, the product should not be used.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21663577.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Northumbria police and partner agencies recently seized thousands of pounds’ worth of illegal fireworks in North Shields ahead of Bonfire Night. *(Image: Northumbria Police)*

Durham Constabulary has advised residents to report concerns about fireworks sales to their local authority’s Trading Standards department.

Attending an organised display remains the advice from fire and rescue services for people who want to enjoy fireworks while reducing the risks linked to a display at home.
