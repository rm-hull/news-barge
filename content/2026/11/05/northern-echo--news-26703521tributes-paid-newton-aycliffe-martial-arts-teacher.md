A County Durham man who mentored hundreds of martial arts students has been remembered as “the most gentle soul” after he died aged 71.

Sean Dooney, from Newton Aycliffe, died on October 31 after a brief illness.

Sean spent the past 15 years overseas, first in Gibraltar and later in Portugal, teaching martial arts, practising yoga and helping others, but remained a well-known name across the town.

![Sean Dooney](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21676710.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Sean Dooney *(Image: LEE DOONEY)*

In 2016, he met Lee, who would later become his wife.

The couple married in 2023 and went on to work together teaching martial arts and wellness classes.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21676709.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Lee and Sean Dooney *(Image: LEE DOONEY)*

Lee Reubens-Dooney, 72, said her husband dedicated his life to helping others.

She said: "He was the most gentle soul. He always had time for everybody else.

"He was a guide, a teacher, a master of martial arts, an advocate for treating people like you want to be treated.

"He was always laughing and mischievous. He loved life and lived it. He practised yoga and Irish stick fighting and Muay Thai every day."

Lee met Sean after a friend recommended one of his yoga classes. The pair were friends for several years before becoming a couple marrying three years ago.

Together, they ran classes in Irish stick fighting, a traditional martial art.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21676729.jpg?type=mds-article-620)PICTURED L-R ARE COACH SEAN DOONEY JNR. ,CRAIG MOIR, LEE MAKIN, SIMON MOIR, MARK MAKIN,SARAH MAKIN, KIRSTY MOIR AND LOUISE JOSS. 11/02/1997 *(Image: MICHAEL PECKETT)*

Lee said Sean never lost his passion for teaching.

"Sean never stopped working," she said.

"We worked together every day.

"People need help and Sean was always ready to help. He was always willing to listen.

"He was able to nourish people and they remember it.

"The outpouring of love, honour, affection and gratitude is almost more than I can bear in a wonderful, horrible way."

Gary McAllister, who runs Phoenix Thai Boxing in Shildon, had known Sean for around four decades.

![Thai boxers Lee Smith, Gary McAllister, Sean Dooney, Nick Hands and Dave Berriman pictured in Spennymoor 2004](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21676731.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Thai boxers Lee Smith, Gary McAllister, Sean Dooney, Nick Hands and Dave Berriman pictured in Spennymoor 2004 *(Image: PROVIDED)*

Sean trained Gary in Thai boxing at Newton Aycliffe Youth Centre in the late 1980s, sparking a lifelong passion.

Gary, 56, said Sean was his inspiration and motivation.

"We took a journey through Thai boxing and what a journey it was," he said.

"I was never a fighter as a child and didn't even know how to fight. I was obsessed with Thai boxing and Sean saw that.

"Sean trained me and gave me the skills and focus I needed. He always made me believe in myself.

"I was committed to the sport and to Sean as my teacher.

"Sean gave me my passion for the sport.

"He was a super intelligent man. He could hold a great conversation."
