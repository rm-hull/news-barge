BRADFORD City supporter Georgia Thornton has just won a SkyBet Fan Spotlight award.

And given the 20-year-old has been raising money for the Bradford Burns Unit for nearly half of her life thanks to her Bradford City Ribbons campaigning, it could not be more deserved.

The T&A caught up with the Lancaster University student, who hopes she will always be able to carry on producing her now-famous claret and amber ribbons, pin badges and hair bows.

As of this June, her efforts have raised nearly £28,000 in total for the organisation officially known as the Plastic Surgery and Burns Research Unit, which was set up following the 1985 Valley Parade Fire Disaster.

Thornton told us: “It started initially back in 2017, and it was for my nan's funeral, where I made claret and amber ribbons for people to wear, because she was a massive Bradford City fan.

“But at the end of it, people had left donations there, so I decided to give it those to the Burns Unit.

“I thought that's what my nan would have wanted, being a massive Bradford City fan and being there on the day (of the disaster)

“It got recognised by the club after I donated and it's just gone from there.”

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21666833.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Georgia Thornton pictured with some of last season's Bantams squad. *(Image: UGC)*

Nearly a whole decade later, Thornton proudly continues with her fundraising efforts, despite being a busy woman.

Asked how glad she is to have kept Bradford City Ribbons going for all these years, she said: “It's really good.

“It keeps money still going to the Burns Unit and there's the little badges that everyone can wear and everyone at the club now knows what it represents, so it's a really good thing.

“I do criminology at university, so the idea is to become a detective I think.

“But I'll keep going with Bradford City Ribbons for as long as I can, until I don't have time to do it anymore.

“Hopefully that day doesn't come and I can always keep doing it.”

Thornton has a brilliant support network behind her too, with the club and her family backing her fundraising efforts.

She said: “It's nice for it to be recognised and when the club put it out there as well, that just means everyone knows about it more.

“I get so many more people buying the products because the club are backing the campaign as well.

“My family’s support definitely means a lot to me too and I don't think I'd be able to do it without them.

“Going to the matches and everything, whenever I'm at the memorial game, they're always there walking around with me.”

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21666831.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Georgia Thornton giving an interview pitchside at Valley Parade, where she goes regularly despite being based in Morecambe. *(Image: UGC)*

Thornton, whose father’s side of the family all hail from Bradford, was there on Saturday too, but it was a rare shocker for City at Valley Parade as they crashed to a 3-0 defeat against derby rivals Huddersfield.

It ended the Bantams’ 12-game unbeaten run in all competitions, and Thornton has her fingers crossed it was only a blip.

She said: “Hopefully, because we definitely played well in parts.

“I did an interview at half time actually, and I said I was hopeful we’d bring it back because we were definitely the better side in the first half at least.”
