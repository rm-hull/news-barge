A coroner has criticised Army safety procedures and training failings after a young County Durham officer was killed when he was run over by a reversing vehicle during an exercise.

Second Lieutenant Max George, 26, was knocked down and run over by a Warrior tank during Exercise Combat Spirit in June 2022.

At Wiltshire and Swindon Coroner's Court, area coroner Ian Singleton ruled that Lt George died from multiple traumatic injuries and recorded his death as an accident.

But he found a series of failings that contributed to the tragedy.

The coroner highlighted four failings: using the vehicle to disembark troops when it was not designed for that purpose, poor maintenance of its faulty door alarm, inadequate training for the student commander, and too few suitable vehicles and personnel on the exercise.

The inquest heard that 5 Rifles soldiers were taking part in Exercise Combat Spirit when Lt George was ordered out to take command on the ground as troops moved towards a target.

Lieutenant Colonel Graham Watson, who led the Army's inquiry, said a smoke grenade was deployed as the Warrior stopped and Lt George began climbing out.

Lt George was delayed freeing his equipment before reaching back inside for his weapon, the hearing heard.

The student commander then believed Lt George had moved clear.

Col Watson said: "Believing Lt George was clear the student commander commanded the driver to reverse. Lt George was still at the rear reaching for his weapon. He was knocked to the ground."

Between 38 and 43 seconds passed between the vehicle stopping and the collision, the inquest heard.

Witnesses shouted, waved and sounded a horn to warn those inside the Warrior, but could not prevent the collision.

The coroner said the commander thought Lt George was clear after hearing a "clunk" and losing sight of him through the periscopes.

"The Warrior reversed before Max had moved clear, knocking him to the ground and running over him," Mr Singleton said.

The hearing examined the failure of a safety alarm meant to alert the driver when the Warrior's rear doors remained open.

The alarm system had been faulty since 2018, the inquest heard.

The officer told the inquest that several faults had been recorded, including a snapped rear-door alarm cable and a faulty locking mechanism.

The hearing also heard Lt George's vehicle was an FV-511 Warrior, which was not normally used to disembark troops or for close-combat operations.

Col Watson said most of the student commander's training had been on the more common FV-510 variant.

Lt George's family paid an emotional tribute to the former Durham School pupil, describing him as a "caring, devoted" son and brother.

His mother, Vivienne, said: "We are struck by just how adored Max is by everybody he came across.

"Max had effortless leadership qualities, and he truly was a star.

"His potential was enormous, and he was an exceptionally talented, yet humble man.

Lt George was Durham School's former Head of School and excelled academically throughout his education.

Victoria Neale, of Pepperells Solicitors, which represented the family, welcomed the findings after the inquest.

She said the hearing had exposed "many faults and failings", including faulty equipment, its use for the wrong purpose, and failures in vital safety systems and training.

"The Ministry of Defence has confirmed that changes have since been made in response to the findings of its investigations," she said.

"Whilst nothing can undo the family's loss, it is hoped that these measures, if implemented and adhered to, will reduce the risk of a similar tragedy occurring in the future."

Lt George's family said they remained immensely proud of his achievements and service but described his death as devastating for everyone who knew him.

Brigadier Giles Malec, head of the Army Safety Group, said: “First and foremost, our thoughts and sympathies remain with Second Lieutenant Max George’s family and friends at this very sad and difficult time.

“Max served his country with pride and is sorely missed by his loved ones and military colleagues.

“We recognise the seriousness of the failures which led to Max’s tragic death and remain committed to ensuring that the lessons identified are properly embedded, that risks are reduced, and outstanding recommendations continue to be progressed.

“We thank Max’s family for their composure and strength during this process, and we acknowledge the professionalism of all those involved in examining the circumstances surrounding Max’s death.
