CAB drivers across Bradford are calling for urgent action after a spate of attacks on vehicles.

Stones and other objects have been thrown at private hire vehicles in recent weeks, causing serious damage and allegedly injuring at least one passenger.

According to drivers, the incidents have left them fearing for their safety.

### 'What safety are drivers guaranteed?'

Ilyas Hussain, who runs Power Carz in Eccleshill, said: "We are getting big stones thrown at cars, especially the windows.

"It's been going on for about a month, mostly down Harrogate Road.

"There has been a lot of damages to our cars.

"Last week, we had two cars smashed up, but nothing seems to have been done."

Mr Hussain said concerns had been raised with police but drivers remained worried.

He said: "Harrogate Road is a big main road that goes to Leeds Bradford Airport.

"As cab drivers, we can't avoid it.

"What safety are drivers guaranteed?"

### One passenger allegedly injured in attack

He said the attacks were also affecting passengers, particularly elderly people who depend on cabs to complete day-to-day tasks.

Mr Hussain said: "I have a lot of elderly customers and they can’t even go to the shops themselves.

"We help them out as much as we can."

![A stretch of Harrogate Road (A658) where the attacks seem to be concentrated](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21674228.jpg?type=mds-article-620)A stretch of Harrogate Road (A658) where Mr Hussain said the attacks are concentrated *(Image: Google Maps)*

He said one customer had been injured in an incident and claimed some drivers had stopped reporting attacks due to lack of action.

Mr Hussain said: "A lot of drivers have stopped reporting it because they aren’t doing anything about it.

"One of our customers was injured."

Mr Hussain added: "I’ve been driving for 26 years and never seen it as bad as this."

### 'Who's going to work when you run the risk of getting your car smashed up?'

He added that drivers have been left with difficult decisions about whether to continue working.

Mr Hussain said: "These drivers are self-employed.

"But who’s going to work when you run the risk of getting your car smashed up?"

He said a plan had now been put in place to address the problem.

Mr Hussain said: "We have been told that there are plans in place to tackle the issue.

"I just hope it gets sorted."

### Reports of 'eggs and stones' thrown at taxis

Another private hire firm, Local Cars, based in Wibsey, reported a rise in similar incidents.

In a post on social media post, the company said: "Over the past few weeks on evenings, we have had numerous reports of children waiting on side streets, hiding behind parked cars and along roadsides, throwing eggs and even stones at passing vehicles."

![Local Cars, based in Wibsey, has reported eggs and stones being thrown at vehicles](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21674531.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Local Cars, based in Wibsey, has reported eggs and stones being thrown at vehicles *(Image: T&A)*

The company highlighted the financial impact on drivers.

The post continued: "One of our drivers had their windscreen smashed, costing £430 to repair.

"Another driver had their vehicle door dented, with repairs costing £350.

"These are not small amounts of money.

"These drivers work hard to earn a living, and these unexpected costs come directly out of their pockets."

### 'Behind every cab is a driver trying to earn an honest living'

Local Cars urged parents to talk to their children about the dangers of such actions.

The post said: "We are asking all parents to please take just two minutes to sit down with their children and have a conversation before they leave the house.

"Ask them one simple question:

"How would you feel if someone threw a stone at a cab carrying a member of your family and that person was seriously injured?"

The company warned that what might seem like a prank could have devastating consequences.

The post said: "What might seem like two minutes of laughter, a bit of fun or a joke with friends could have life-changing consequences for someone else.

"Behind every cab is a driver trying to earn an honest living.

"There could also be a family, children or vulnerable passengers sitting in the back."

### 'It is dangerous, costly and potentially life-threatening'

The firm said it had contacted police but was also appealing to parents for support.

The post said: "Please speak to your children.

"Explain to them that this is not harmless fun.

"It is dangerous, costly and potentially life-threatening."

The T&A has reached out to West Yorkshire Police for comment.
