A BAND has been named among the Tees Valley Artists of the Year for the first time.

Hartlepool indie five-piece Marketplace joins dance artist Anthony Lo Giudice, writer Carmen Marcus, visual artist Stuart Langley, and singer-songwriter Jodie Nicholson as part of the 2026/27 cohort.

The Artists of the Year programme, now in its third year, provides nearly £30,000 in funding and business support to help artists advance their careers.

Tees Valley Mayor Ben Houchen said: "We’ve had some brilliant successes from our Artists of the Year programme and it’s great to see how deep our pool of talent runs.

"Having this backing means our talents from Teesside, Darlington and Hartlepool have the tools to succeed and can take on the world.

"I wish them all the best of luck, and I look forward to hearing about their progress this year and in the years to come."

The year-long scheme runs from October 2026 to September 2027 and offers each artist a tailored package including investment equivalent to one year of the UK Real Living Wage, a professional development budget, and mentoring.

Recipients also receive business support, fundraising guidance, PR assistance, and access to industry networks.

The new cohort includes:

* **Marketplace:** The first band to be named Tees Valley Artists of the Year, Marketplace is made up of Evie Rhodes (vocals/guitar), Joel O’Beirne (vocals/bass), Jack Purcell (guitar), Matthew Short (synth/vocals), and Ross Dixon (drums).

Their upcoming EP, due in early 2027, has already attracted support from BBC Radio 1, Radio X, and BBC Introducing.

* **Anthony Lo Giudice:** A dance artist from Hemlington whose work blends contemporary dance, theatre, and visual storytelling.

Through his company, Anthony Lo Giudice Dance Theatre, he creates performances that reach audiences across the UK and Europe.

* **Carmen Marcus:** Based in Redcar and Cleveland, Ms Marcus is a writer whose debut novel *How Saints Die*and award-winning play* And the Earth Opened Up Under Her*draw on myth, folklore, and realism.* **Stuart Langley:** From Hartlepool, Mr Langley is a visual artist known for public art, installations, and sculptures.

His recent work includes *hold me and show me you love me*, a large heart sculpture made from donated mobile phones.* **Jodie Nicholson:** A songwriter and producer from Hurworth, Ms Nicholson creates chamber-pop and synth-driven alt-pop.**READ MORE: County Durham brass band on secret recording for John Lewis ad's Sam Fender track**

Following her self-released debut album Golden Hour in 2019, she worked with producer Tim Bran on breakthrough single Move, earning a place on the BBC Radio 6 Music playlist and support from BBC Radio 1.

The programme is part of the Tees Valley Combined Authority’s commitment to developing the region’s creative and cultural industries.
