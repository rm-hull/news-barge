A total of 14 arrests have been made and weapons seized during a police crackdown on crime and anti-social behaviour.

The action took place across York and Selby yesterday (Wednesday, November 4) as part of North Yorkshire Police’s monthly Operation Tornado, to coincide with Mischief Night.

The operation included extra patrols, warrants, retail crime operations and school engagement.

Four warrants were executed, and officers recovered cannabis, suspected Class A drugs, and weapons.

A lock-up in West Yorkshire containing around £150,000 of suspected stolen property was uncovered as part of an investigation into the theft of an HGV trailer from Selby.

Three of the area’s most wanted offenders were also arrested, each wanted for recall to prison.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21673964.jpg?type=mds-article-620)CC Forber with the new e-bikes in York *(Image: North Yorkshire Police)*

A 32-year-old man and a 19-year-old woman were detained in a hotel room where officers found around 100 wraps of suspected Class A drugs, about £1,000 in cash and two knives.

Both have been charged with being concerned in the supply of Class A drugs (crack cocaine), possession with intent to supply Class A drugs (crack cocaine), possession of an offensive weapon in a private place and possession of criminal property.

They are due to appear in court.

In total, officers seized two knives, four swords and an illegally owned air rifle during the operation.

Chief Constable Tim Forber of North Yorkshire Police said: "Operation Tornado is about much more than enforcement.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21673958.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Police out on the road in North Yorkshire *(Image: North Yorkshire Police)*

"It is about preventing crime, tackling anti-social behaviour and ensuring people feel safe in their communities.

"By combining high-visibility patrols, proactive operations, educational engagement and targeted enforcement activity, we are taking action against those who seek to cause harm while also working to prevent problems before they occur."

Mischief Night, traditionally observed on November 4, has become associated in recent years with anti-social behaviour, criminal damage and low-level disorder.

North Yorkshire Police carried out enhanced patrols across known hotspots, forming part of wider plans around Halloween and Bonfire Night.

Mr Forber said: "Mischief Night should not be used as an excuse for criminality or anti-social behaviour.

"Through operations like this, we are sending a clear message that we will take action against those who make life a misery for others, while continuing to support and protect our communities."

"While Operation Tornado provides an opportunity to bring together a wide range of policing activity into a dedicated day of action, it mirrors the work our officers and staff carry out every day across North Yorkshire to prevent crime, target offenders and keep people safe."

The operation also included retail crime patrols at out-of-town retail parks, using plain-clothed officers and unmarked vehicles to deter theft and gather intelligence.

Officers visited Vale of York Academy and delivered talks to around 150 students on personal safety, hate crime and the dangers of illegal vapes.
