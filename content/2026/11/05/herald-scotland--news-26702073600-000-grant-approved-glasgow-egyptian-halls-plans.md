Funding of more than half a million pounds will be provided to a developer to help take forward plans that could save one of Glasgow's most vulnerable A-listed buildings.

Glasgow City Council is to provide a grant of up to £600,000 to support development proposals for the A-listed Egyptian Halls on Union Street.

The building, one of the finest works of renowned architect Alexander "Greek" Thomson, has been in disrepair for many years, with its upper floors lying vacant and derelict.

It has been on the national Buildings at Risk Register since 1990.

The funding will assist Ediston, the preferred developer, in further developing its proposals to bring the building back to life and remove it from beneath the tarpaulin cover that has shielded it from public view for many years.

Ediston aims to convert the upper floors into a hotel with the first and second ground floors having leisure uses

In February last year, the council approved an investigation into the potential use of Compulsory Purchase Order (CPO) powers in relation to the Egyptian Halls building.

In August 2025, an open call was issued to establish whether there was a credible development proposal capable of supporting a CPO process.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/17096942.jpg?type=mds-article-620)

Of the three submissions received, Ediston's achieved the highest score and emerged as the preferred option.

Ediston's proposals identified the need for public funding alongside private investment to make the repurposing of the building viable.

The developer has argued that a combination of public and private sector support will be required to unlock the site's redevelopment potential.

In February 2026, a council committee authorised officers to enter into negotiations with Ediston on the terms of a back-to-back compulsory purchase order (CPO) agreement.

Councillors also agreed that work should continue to assess the potential use of CPO powers as part of efforts to bring the building back into productive use.

As part of that process, Ediston must carry out further technical and professional work, including detailed property and engineering surveys, to develop its initial proposals and provide evidence to support any future consideration of compulsory purchase powers.

The developer will use the newly approved grant funding to undertake this work, helping to refine the preferred redevelopment option and inform ongoing negotiations with the council over the back-to-back CPO agreement.

Funding for the project was approved by councillors on Thursday and will be drawn from the council's £10 million Bringing Property and Land Back Into Productive Use budget.

The strategic capital investment programme is designed to support regeneration projects involving council-owned assets, privately owned properties and wider development opportunities where there is a clear public benefit and alignment with council priorities.

Councillor Ruairi Kelly, Depute Leader and Convener for Housing, Development and Built Heritage at Glasgow City Council, said: "This award is another key stage in the work to bring back to life an outstanding example of Glasgow's built heritage.

"The funding will help to support the development of an exciting proposal to return what is a long-term vacant and deteriorating heritage building back to full and productive use.

"The redevelopment and repurposing of fantastic historic buildings such as this is a key part of our strategy to create even more attractive and remarkable places in our city centre."
