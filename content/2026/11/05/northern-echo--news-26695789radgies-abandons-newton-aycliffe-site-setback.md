A Darlington takeaway has abandoned plans for a new branch in Newton Aycliffe despite starting work after realising it would need planning permission.

Radgie's owner Ishy Miah had signed a lease and begun fitting out the former hairdresser's unit in the Woodham area but hadn't realised he would need planning permission for change of use.

Instead of applying to the council, he decided to abandon the project and says he will now look for a new site in Newton Aycliffe.

Ishy described the decision as "heart-breaking" after weeks of work preparing the premises.

Durham County Council has confirmed that no planning application was ever submitted.

Mr Miah, 24, said the business had been hoping to open in Newton Aycliffe for some time and secured the keys to the premises around a month ago.

He said he believed the property could be adapted for Radgie's without significant difficulty.

However, as work got underway, it became clear the process would be more complicated than first anticipated, with planning permission required before the unit could be used as a takeaway.

He said: "In hindsight, I should have done more research. Once I realised what was involved, it made sense to stop before we'd committed too much."

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21666440.jpg?type=mds-article-620)A loaded fries meal from Radgies, as the takeaway chain continues plans to open in Newton Aycliffe *(Image: NORTHERN ECHO)*

Despite the setback, he says Radgie's remains fully committed to opening in the town and has already begun searching for alternative premises.

He has viewed a number of potential sites in both Newton Aycliffe and nearby Shildon as it looks to revive its expansion plans.

"We are 100 per cent going to open another branch," Mr Miah said.

"I'll make sure I do more background work first this time, but I can promise we will be coming to Aycliffe soon."

The business reassured customers in its social media announcement that it has no intention of abandoning its plans for the town.

Radgies only opened on Victoria Road in Darlington in February but moved to Blackwellgate in the town centre in June.

Ishy learnt the trade working at his dad's long-established venue Café Spice before opening the street food-style venue.
