Stockton Council’s £13m purchase of Billingham town centre has been described as “only option to unlock progress”.

The Labour-led authority’s cabinet met last night (Wednesday, November 4) to decide whether or not to buy the declining town centre. They confirmed the current town centre owner Sheet Anchor Evolve (SAE) would not budge on its £13m asking price.

With the clock ticking towards a March 2028 deadline to use £20m of government regeneration money, the cabinet decided it would pay the £13m – £2.25m over its own valuation of the site – and kick off a first phase of regeneration.

![A bird's eye view of Billingham. Credit: Stockton Council. Free for LDRS use.](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21674613.jpg?type=mds-article-620)A bird's eye view of Billingham. Credit: Stockton Council. Free for LDRS use.

Councillor Paul Rowling, the council’s deputy leader and cabinet member for regeneration and resources, said private sector owners had failed to invest in the town centre and the sale would “open a new chapter and begin the process to create a new, vibrant and sustainable town centre”. He put forward the case for sale, which he described as “the only option to unlock progress”.

He said: “We have a chance to take back control and give the platform to the people of Billingham to create meaningful change, to begin the journey towards a regenerated town centre that they can be proud of.

“We can either take back control and deliver for the people of Billingham or do nothing and allow Billingham town centre to continue to decline.”

![Councillor Paul Rowling from Stockton Council. Picture: LDR.](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21514357.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Councillor Paul Rowling from Stockton Council. Picture: LDR.

He said he shared residents’ frustrations and scepticism, but the sale was vital to restore local pride, support businesses, make the area safer and attract investment.

He added: “Put starkly, I believe that public intervention is the only way to stop decline. The evidence clearly shows the failure of the private sector.

“Billingham town centre can no longer be left behind. Taking back control will allow significant improvements. We should not be hesitant to intervene when needed.”

He said the funding was there, from £20m government money and £10m from the Tees Valley Combined Authority (TVCA) following a business case, without affecting council finances as it would no longer borrow £10m as previously planned, adding: “This is a prudent approach.

“The reality is that the current owner will not sell for less than £13m. Attempts were made to negotiate a lower price, however this was met with a clear negative response.

“We are unapologetically ambitious for our borough and now is the time to invest in Billingham. There are also no other development regeneration plans for alternative schemes across Billingham. There is no alternative and the time is now for action.”

He said buying only part of the town centre was not an option, with SAE pulling out of those negotiations last November. And he argued a compulsory purchase order would risk leaving the government money unspent by the 2028 deadline.

Council officers said cabinet needed to weigh significant regeneration opportunities and benefits against significant risks and consider affordability and value for money.

Iain Robinson, the council’s assistant director for town centre development, said: “The owner’s consistently maintained its position on price. It is not what we’d prefer to acquire the centre for.

“Acquisition provides the strongest platform for delivering long-term regeneration.”

Chief financial officer Clare Harper said with Chartered Institute of Public Finance and Accountancy (CIPFA) urged caution and highlighted “significant financial concerns”. However she said the council had strengthened the proposal and lessened risks.

Ged Morton, director of corporate services, said price was a key issue along with the consequences of not going ahead, and future decisions would come back to cabinet, saying: “This isn’t the end of it, this is the beginning of a decision that leads to other things.”

Cabinet members supported the sale. Cllr Nigel Cooke said it was “the only real way forward”.

Cllr Clare Besford, a Billingham resident for 45 years, urged colleagues to be ambitious and bold, saying it was a significant moment for the town which would transform it and bring jobs, investment and confidence.

![Councillor Clare Besford, cabinet member for children and young people at Stockton Council.](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21674914.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Councillor Clare Besford, cabinet member for children and young people at Stockton Council.

She said: “I’ve spent years wondering whether we would ever get this over the line.

“For too many years the future of own town centre has been shaped by circumstances beyond our control. Tonight we have the opportunity to change that. It presents a once-in-a-generation opportnity to reshape the future of Billingham town centre for our residents, businesses and future generations.

“Investment in Billingham town centre delivers significant economic and social benefits. Doing nothing is not a viable option, nor is simply tinkering around the edges.

“Tonight we have a choice. We can accept the status quo and continue to watch opportunities pass us by, or we can take back control of our town centre, invest in its future and give Billingham the chance to thrive again.”

![Councillor Pauline Beall from Stockton Council. Picture: LDRS.](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21674915.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Councillor Pauline Beall from Stockton Council. Picture: LDRS.

Cllr Pauline Beall said it was one of the hardest decisions to make, adding: “It is to make sure we give the people of Billingham what they deserve. We will make bold decisions, we don’t shy away, and this is one I won’t shy away from.”

Council leader Cllr Lisa Evans concluded: “There is no alternative. To let Evolve keep hold of that centre any longer is the wrong decision.”

The cabinet agreed 15 recommendations for the first phase to acquire the town centre freehold, prepare to move tenants, demolish the Queensway and former library and healthcare site, make urgent repairs, create a “development platform”, speak to the public and draw up a new masterplan with a Billingham Regeneration Board.

Cllr Rowling said local people would be listened to.

“Regeneration must be done alongside people, not imposed on them,” he said.
