A woman poked a police officer in the eye while trying to stop her partner being arrested, a court heard.

Lucy Peart said she tried to stand between the officers and her partner but could not remember assaulting either of them.

The 42-year-old had little recollection of her behaviour after suffering a seizure, Newton Aycliffe Magistrates’ Court heard.

Prosecutor Rebecca Rowlands said Peart intervened as the two officers dealt with her partner, who pushed one of them to the ground.

As the officer lay on the ground, Peart poked her in the eye, causing immediate pain and temporary impairment.

Peart also kicked a second officer in the leg as officers dealt with the pair.

The injured officer said she suffered cuts, bruises and abrasions, became emotionally overwhelmed and feared the incident would stay with her.

Peart, of Pearson Street, Spennymoor, admitted assaulting two police officers in a rear alley off Fenwick Street on July 16.

Her solicitor, Georgia Snowdon, said Peart had been diagnosed with a neurological disorder triggered by stress, anxiety and being touched by others.

She said: “She has a vague recollection of approaching the officers and putting herself between them and her partner.

“She has little recollection of what she has done after that but she doesn’t dispute what happened.

“She is remorseful and she didn’t want to behave that way.”

Bench chairman David Thompson fined Peart £200, ordered her to pay £200 compensation and £165 court costs.

The court heard that the man involved had already been sentenced to pay a total of £675 in fines, compensation and costs.
