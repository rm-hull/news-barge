John Swinney spent just 75 minutes in meetings with officials discussing the restructuring of Scotland’s councils before announcing plans to overhaul local government.

The First Minister met senior civil servants twice on the issue – branded the biggest shake-up of public services in decades – before the reforms were unveiled in September’s Programme for Government.

The Scottish Government wants to drastically shake-up local government by scrapping the existing 32 councils and replacing them with six to 10 regional authorities.

A further 120 to 160 community bodies have been proposed, but councillors and political opponents are among those raising concerns about the lack of information on the shake-up.

Prior to the Programme for Government, Mr Swinney and officials discussed the proposals in two meetings, lasting 45 minutes and 30 minutes respectively.

Two further meetings were held after the announcement, taking the total time the First Minister has spent in meetings with officials on the restructuring plans to two and a half hours.

The figures were obtained through freedom of information requests by Scottish Labour, which has accused the SNP-led Scottish Government of “making it up as they go along”.

Joe Fagan, the finance and local government spokesman for Scottish Labour, accused the First Minister of developing plans with “limited” preparation.

He said: “John Swinney plans to break up more than 30 years of local government organisation in this country and appears to have done so in meetings barely lasting two and a half hours total.

“The public aren’t going to have confidence in proposals dreamt up in less time than it takes to watch Harry Potter.

“What we need now is for the First Minister to set out a clear plan for reform, explain how it will improve services and demonstrate that councils and communities will have a meaningful say in decisions that affect them.

“Instead, it feels like ministers are making it up as they go along.

“Local government is too important to be reshaped on the back of such limited preparation.”

Mr Swinney announced the largest-scale shake-up of the public sector on September 1 this year.

He said ministers were considering reducing the size of councils to increase sustainability as the Scottish Government looks to plug a £4.5 billion gap in the public finances.

Precise details were set out by Deputy First Minister Jenny Gilruth later that month.

The revelation comes days after Councillor Steven Heddle, vice-president of Cosla – the local government body – urged the Scottish Government to slow down on its council overhaul.

A three-month long consultation process is already underway, but local government chiefs do not believe that is adequate time to properly scrutinise the reform plans.

Writing for The Herald, Mr Heddle said councils had already made £5bn of savings since 2011, stating: “When the government says that collectively Scotland’s budget requires £4.7bn in savings, councils feel they have more than delivered their share already.”

“Let’s not hurry into a costly reorganisation that will throw up more questions than answers for our communities and workforce,” Mr Heddle added.

It is estimated that the Scottish Government’s wider restructuring programme could put up to 20,000 public sector jobs at risk across Scotland.

Trade unions, including Unison, Unite and the GMB, have warned there are unanswered questions on where the axe will fall when councils are restructured.

Earlier this week, The Herald also revealed the Scottish Government’s plan to cut the number of territorial health boards from 14 to two was launched without a business case, while the government’s own risk assessment highlighted the danger that reform is delivered inconsistently, leading to delays, higher costs and organisational instability.

The First Minister met with Cosla leaders on Wednesday afternoon where he pledged powers will not be centralised to the Scottish Government, adding more powers would be available for councils.

A spokesperson for Deputy First Minister Jenny Gilruth said: “The Labour Prime Minister spent Friday negotiating with John Swinney for more powers for a reformed local government structure. The leader of Scottish Labour on COSLA spent this afternoon with the First Minister negotiating the detail of that reform. The only person who doesn’t seem to have got the memo that the current structure of local government is going to change is Joe Fagan.”
