Israeli soldiers stood guard near olive groves in the occupied West Bank village of **al-Mughayyir** on Thursday as Palestinian families gathered what they could during one of the**few days they are permitted to harvest their crops**. Footage showed** troops firing tear gas** near the olive pickers, with smoke drifting through the trees as a boy threw a canister aside.

Home to around 3,000 people, the village lies north-east of Ramallah and has some 4,300 hectares of land, yet residents say they can **access barely 100 hectares**. Reaching their groves requires** prior Israeli military approval**, with access restricted to a handful of days and subject to age limits and bans on equipment. Villagers say such brief visits are not enough to maintain their trees, which need year-round care.

Al-Mughayyir has faced mounting pressure from nearby Israeli settler outposts. **Six residents have been killed in 2026**, its eastern entrance has been** closed by the army for more than two years**, and** around 12,000 olive trees were uprooted** from its eastern plain in 2025.

Across the West Bank, the UN has recorded **377 settler attacks** causing casualties or property damage since September,**the highest figure since records began in 2006**. The incidents have left one Palestinian dead, more than 200 injured and** 3,850 trees damaged**.

The olive harvest provides a **vital source of income for an estimated 80,000 to 100,000 Palestinian families**. Israeli settlements in the occupied West Bank are** considered illegal under international law** by most countries.
