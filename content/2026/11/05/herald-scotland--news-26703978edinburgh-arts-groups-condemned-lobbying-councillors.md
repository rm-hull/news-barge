Arts organisations in Edinburgh city centre have been criticised for "lobbying" councillors for more financial support.

The Scottish Greens have claimed that large-scale cultural organisations have too much influence on spending decisions in the city.

One of the party’s senior elected councillors has suggested the Scottish capital is wrong to promote itself as a “city of culture” because of the way the arts are currently supported in the city.

**Read more:**

Dan Heap, the party's culture spokesperson in Edinburgh, said “larger organisations” had often been funded at the expense of “smaller organisations” by putting pressure on decision-makers.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21677354.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Dan Heap is the culture spokesperson for the Scottish Greens on Edinburgh City Council. *(Image: Supplied)*

His criticisms have emerged ahead of the 80th anniversary of Edinburgh's festivals in 2027.

He highlighted recent claims by some campaigners that around 91 per cent of the council’s support for the arts is allocated to organisations based in the city centre, and described Edinburgh’s existing cultural landscape as “very, very unequal”.

The council is currently reviewing how arts and culture organisations will be supported in future ahead of applications opening for a new long-term programme in the spring. The overall pot available is expected to increase from the current figure of £4.5m a year to £6.5m from 2018 thanks to the introduction of Edinburgh’s new visitor levy.

Cllr Heap, who did not name any organisations, has spoken out weeks after councillors agreed to use part of the income being generated by Edinburgh’s new visitor levy to pay for a one-year 25 per cent increase in funding for festivals, events and venues which have long-term agreements with the city council.

The move, which was initially opposed by Labour and Green councillors, will benefit many of the city's best-known cultural institutions, including the Edinburgh International Festival and Fringe, the Royal Lyceum, King's, Traverse and Festival theatres, Dance Base, the Filmhouse cinema, the Queen's Hall, the Stills Gallery and Edinburgh Printmakers.

Umbrella body Festivals Edinburgh, which works with 11 of Edinburgh’s long-running events, had earlier warned the council that the city’s worldwide “brand and appeal” was at risk of “erosion” unless prolonged underinvestment in its annual events was tackled.

A report for the council’s culture committee painted a picture of “fewer premieres and commissions, less experimentation and innovation, and reduced national and international activity”.

The Festivals Edinburgh report called for the council to take steps to protect “a golden asset for the city, the people who live here and the businesses that operate here”.

Cllr Heap’s comments echo those of Margaret Graham, the Labour-run council's culture convener, who suggested that Edinburgh needed to take a "more measured approach" to cultural events in the city centre.

Speaking before Labour agreed to support the 25 per cent increase for all its “strategic cultural partners”, Cllr Graham said culture was “only one of the pegs” that the city’s city international is hung on, as she praised Edinburgh’s football clubs, Six Nations fixtures at Murrayfield, the city’s universities and its finance sector.

She suggested some city centre residents were left “traumatised” by the impact of some events, adding: “Community cohesion has taken quite hit due to accommodating visitor numbers. As a council, we have been browbeaten to an extent.”

Cllr Heap was speaking at a public meeting called by the Scottish Greens to address fears of a growing “crisis” in Edinburgh’s cultural sector.

More than 250 artists, organisations and politicians attended the gathering, which was instigated in the wake of news of the looming closure of St Margaret’s House, a long-running cultural hub in the Meadowbank area, plans to demolish part of the Summerhall arts complex to make way for residential accommodation, student flats and a boutique hotel, and controversial course closures at Edinburgh College of Art.

Cllr Heap, who represents the Sighthill and Gorgie areas, told the public meeting that he was in favour of Edinburgh developing a series of “council-owned art hubs”.

He said: “We like to call ourselves, and the council certainly does, a city of culture.

“I think sometimes that's true, but it's not nearly as true sometimes as we like to think if you look at who has access to culture, who doesn't, which parts of the cities get the funding and which don't, which type of organisations get the funding and which don't. It is very, very unequal.

“We do have a lot of inequality. The larger organisations have the lobbying strength and do manage to persuade those who pull the financial levers to get funding, often at the expense of smaller organisations.

“We also have issues around the geographical spread of culture. I think this has improved a little bit, but not nearly enough.

“I represent a part of the city which has very little cultural infrastructure.

“If you look at the funding decisions we've just made, over Green objections, a lot of that funding went to city centre-based organisations.

“We heard recently from smaller cultural organisations that something like 91 per cent of all the cultural funding that we give out goes to city centre-based organisations.”

Speaking after the vote to give organisations a 25 per cent funding increase in the next financial year, Cllr Graham said the council needed to find “a fairer and more sustainable way of doing business” when it came to the arts sector.

Cllr Graham said the council was aiming to have new long-term funding agreements in place for cultural organisations from April 2028.

She told The Herald: “Officers have so far met with more than 80 organisations, both current and potential future grant recipients, and further engagement is scheduled over the coming weeks to ensure we have a clear understanding of the needs of the sector.

“This engagement will inform the recommended grant funding criteria, which will be reported to the March meeting of the culture, as agreed by the council in September, to allow for the fund to open up for applications shortly thereafter.”
