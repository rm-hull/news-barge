THIS feature follows on from last week’s article about Bingley Drill Hall and monuments raised to remember Bingley soldiers in the Boer War and the First and Second World Wars.

It gives background to the ongoing commitment of the Bingley community in remembering those who gave their lives in serving their country.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21675041.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Remembrance Day in Bingley 2014

Bingley Royal British Legion (1944) Branch

The branch, which closed in 2019, had a rich 75-year history deeply intertwined with the civic identity and welfare of Bingley.

Part of the national British Legion Network, the branch was formed during World War II to look after local service personnel. Latterly it operated as a volunteer charity branch managing the annual Poppy Appeal and being a major organiser of Bingley’s Remembrance Day Event.

Our thanks go to the vision of the many volunteers who over the years gave their time and energy to proactively maintain community involvement. This included involving scouts, guides, local schoolchildren and a range of local organisations in the annual Remembrance Day Parade.

In 2007 the Bingley Detachment of the Army Cadet Force became affiliated to the Branch, promoting a closer liaison with the younger generation and greater awareness of each other’s activities.

The Branch was sadly eventually closed when replacement of dedicated, retiring long serving committee members became difficult. Because the branch could no longer maintain an active leadership group its operations ceased.

The Bingley Remembrance Group

The Remembrance Group was formed to fill a community need after the closure of the British Legion Branch, which was no longer able to single handedly manage commemoration events.

To prevent Bingley from losing its public tribute traditions, the Remembrance Group collaboratively work in close partnership with Bingley Town Council, local schools, churches, and community organizations like the Rotary Club and the Women’s Institute (WI)

What they do

The primary focus of the group is in preserving the town’s historical memory through organising major community events:

Co-ordination, fundraising for, and organising the annual Remembrance Day Parade and Service, marching from the Town Square to the War Memorial in Myrtle Park and observing a two-minute silence. This year it will be held on Sunday, November 8. The event is attended by around 1,500 residents each year.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21675038.jpg?type=mds-article-620)A display of poppies outside Bingley Pool in 2018.

Organising public landmark events:

May 2025 was the 80th Anniversary of Victory in Europe and a Beacon Lighting Ceremony was held at 9pm in the Market Square. Beacon lighting had taken place at Druids Altar to celebrate VE Day in May 1945.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21675040.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The VE Day Beacon Lighting in Bingley, 2025.

In May 2026, and now an annual event, Bingley 1940s Day was held in the town centre and included an RAF Dakota flypast. Bingley was transformed into a 1940s celebration as crowds gathered to enjoy a day of vintage charm, classic vehicles and wartime nostalgia.

June 2026 was the 82nd anniversary of D-Day, and Bingley residents remembered with gratitude and respect the courage and sacrifice of all those who took part in the Normandy landings on June 6, 1944.

The Remembrance Group is proud to be part of a network working together to ensure that community spirit, involvement and engagement in Bingley continues to be a feature of Town events.

A key part of Remembrance Day is the role played by the Bingley Detachment Cadets and Bingley Sea Cadets.

Bingley Detachment Army Cadet Force

The Detachment are appealing for local volunteers to help support its growing number of cadets. They continue to attract strong interest from young people across the local area and are now seeking additional adult volunteers to help deliver training, activities and events.

No previous military or cadet experience is required. The Cadet force is looking for reliable, enthusiastic adults with good organisational skills and a willingness to support and inspire young people. Full training and ongoing support are provided, and Volunteers can become involved in leadership training, fieldcraft and adventure activities, sports and community events.

Detachment Commander Sergeant Mark Garbus said: “Volunteering with the Army Cadet Force is incredibly rewarding. Our cadets develop confidence, resilience and life skills and our volunteers play a vital role in making that happen.

“You don’t need a military background, just a commitment to supporting young people and being part of a great team.”

Ideally, volunteers would be available for two evenings each week and one weekend activity approximately every three months.

Anyone interested in learning more can visit www.army cadets.com and go to the adult volunteers section.
