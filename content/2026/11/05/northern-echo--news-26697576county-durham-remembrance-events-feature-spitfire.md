County Durham will mark Remembrance with a reproduction Spitfire, striking poppy installations and an art exhibition.

Durham County Council is staging events at its venues and in communities on Remembrance Sunday, November 8, and Remembrance Day, November 11.

Visitors to Durham City on Remembrance Sunday will have the opportunity to get up close to a reproduction Supermarine Spitfire, which will be on display in Millennium Place from 9am to 2pm.

First built in the 1930s, the Supermarine Spitfire helped defeat Luftwaffe attacks during the 1940 Battle of Britain, in which 1,542 aircrew were killed and 422 wounded.

The display will coincide with the City of Durham Parish Council’s annual Remembrance Day Parade.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21668703.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The Story illuminated re *(Image: DURHAM COUNTY COUNCIL)*

A powerful exhibition about aerial battles will also be held at The Story, the council’s culture, heritage and registration venue near Durham City.

The free exhibition, featuring work by Seaham-born artist and historian Denis Harry Fox, runs from 10am to 4pm on both days of Remembrance weekend.

The Story’s Search and Discover space will also offer an origami make-and-take activity and colouring sheets for families from Saturday, November 7, to Wednesday, November 11.

Durham County Council will also light The Story red from Saturday, November 7, to Wednesday, November 11.

Cllr Robert Potts, Durham County Council’s Armed Forces Champion, said: “It’s so important to remember the brave servicemen and women who fought to defend the freedoms and way of life we enjoy today. Remembrance Day and Remembrance Sunday provide a chance for us all to reflect on the sacrifices they made and honour their memory.

“It’s also an opportunity to pay tribute to those who kept the home fires burning, and to think about those whose lives were disrupted by displacement, separation and loss. Behind every uniform was a family affected by war, and every community felt the impact of sacrifice.

“I hope people will take the opportunity to see the reproduction Spitfire and to pay their respects at the many remembrance events taking place in communities across the county.

“We will remember them.”

![The Story illuminated re](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21668704.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The Story illuminated re *(Image: DURHAM COUNTY COUNCIL)*

The Story and its sister venue, The Light, at Aykley Heads, are also supporting Durham Markets’ A Community Remembers project.

Earlier this year, Durham Markets called on knitters and crocheters to help create 16,386 woollen poppies to represent every Durham Light Infantry (DLI) soldier who lost their life during the First and Second World Wars.

The poppies now form a canopy suspended from the Market Hall entrance in Durham City to commemorate Remembrance Day.

Before Remembrance Day, The Story will hold school workshops where children use the poppies to create commemorative wreaths.

Some pupils will lay the wreaths in front of the Wakenshaw Gun at The Story before the national two-minute silence at 11am on Wednesday, November 11.

The Light has teamed up with artist Betty Barnet Brown, visitors and Durham Chorister School to create a hanging installation from its poppies.

‘For Tomorrow they gave their Today’ will be displayed in The Light’s front windows from Wednesday, November 4.
