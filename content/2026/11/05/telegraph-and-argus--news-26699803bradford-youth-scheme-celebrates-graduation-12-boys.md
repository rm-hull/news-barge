A YOUTH programme is helping to build brighter, more resilient futures for young people in Bradford.

Twelve boys have graduated from the Bradford Youth Empowerment Intervention (BYEI), a community-led programme supported by Muslim Aid and delivered in partnership with Step-In Educational Services.

The early intervention scheme focuses on strengthening mental wellbeing and preventing future involvement with the criminal justice system.

Nisar Hussain, manager at Step-In Educational Services, said: "We were incredibly proud to celebrate this milestone alongside them and their families.

"Graduation may mark the completion of this stage of the programme, but we hope the confidence, skills and relationships they have developed will stay with them as they continue to shape their futures."

The programme combines physical activity, mentorship, life-skills training and emotional wellbeing support, using boxing and fitness to teach discipline, resilience and teamwork.

Lucy Rae, UK programmes manager at Muslim Aid, said: "The graduation ceremony was about recognising these young people for their commitment, their potential and the progress they have made.

"Every one of these boys has strengths and ambitions, and programmes like BYEI give young people the space, guidance and encouragement to recognise those qualities in themselves."

It came as Muslim Aid marks Men's Mental Health Awareness Month through its Resilient Minds campaign, which highlights the importance of accessible, preventative and community-based approaches to mental wellbeing.
