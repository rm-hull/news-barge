# Tory hypocrisy to claim reversing Brexit would be a 'disaster': Yorkshire Post Letters

## Peter Brown, Cleckheaton.

Tory chair and Thirsk MP Kevin Hollinrake claims reopening the Brexit debate would be “a disaster for business” because Yorkshire’s farmers and manufacturers need certainty (Labour MPs push back at Burnham on rejoining EU, The Yorkshire Post, October 27).

Well, that’s hypocrisy from a party that gave us four prime ministers in six years, prolonged Brexit wrangling, a coup by its Eurosceptic fringe, and Liz Truss’s disastrous economic experiment.

Of course businesses need predictability, but Mr Hollinrake cites trade rules, regulation and costs as sources of uncertainty – precisely the issues a serious discussion on closer European ties should address. Calling it “relitigating the past” avoids weighing the benefits of change against the costs of standing still.

Does he really believe Yorkshire’s businesses prefer the status quo to better European trading arrangements?

Nobody should pretend changing our EU relationship involves no uncertainty, but refusing to consider change is no strategy for investment and growth.

Yorkshire’s firms need a credible route to better trading conditions – not the certainty that their disadvantages against EU single market competitors will be further baked in.

If Andy Burnham delivers that through closer EU ties, he can bring concerned “Red Wall” Labour MPs and their constituents on side too.

He might even win over a few of the nearly one-third (29 per cent) of Conservative voters who support rejoining the European Union (YouGov, Sept 24–25). Approaching three-quarters (71 per cent) of Tories want a closer relationship with the EU – not the troubled one Mr Hollinrake’s party left behind.
