A RIBBLE Valley phone box at the centre of Britain has been granted Grade Two listed status.

The kiosk in Dunsop Bridge was officially opened in June 1992 by explorer Sir Ranulph Fiennes, who made the first call from the kiosk to Ordnance Survey.

It was built as the 100,000th public telephone kiosk in the country, and its location was defined by Ordnance Survey as the geographic centre of Great Britain.

Jayne Rimmer, north region listing team leader at Historic England, said: "This KX100 is historically significant as it was installed as a landmark on account of its location and being the 100,000th kiosk to be opened in the country with some unique design features.

"It also makes an important contribution to our understanding of the historic development of the telecommunications industry in England before the introduction, and subsequent widespread use, of mobile phones.

![](https://www.cravenherald.co.uk/resources/images/21673269.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Inside the now listed payphone *(Image: Historic England/Alan Bull)*

"As part of the last generation of the public telephone boxes, it is a nationally significant kiosk, and we are delighted it has been given Grade Two listed status and protection."

The KX100 model, designed by industrial designer Ronald David Carter, was aimed to be wheelchair-friendly and robust.

![](https://www.cravenherald.co.uk/resources/images/21673320.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The Dunsop Bridge kiosk has been listed *(Image: Historic England/Alan Bull)*

It succeeded the iconic K2 and K6 red telephone boxes and featured a bespoke design by Wolff Olins, with key places in Britain radiating out from the BT logo and Dunsop Bridge.

Oli Marshall, campaigns director of Twentieth Century Society, said: "We’re absolutely delighted that the KX100 kiosk at Dunsop Bridge has been designated Grade II, as Twentieth Century Society’s famous telephone boxes campaign comes full circle.

"There’s no small irony that it was the arrival of the KX100 that first spurred the Society into action back in 1985: once the slayer of traditional red telephone boxes that we fought vigorously to repel, now itself being given heritage protection. What goes around comes around!"

He added: "While widely unloved and underappreciated in comparison to the classic red telephone boxes, the 'ugly duckling' KX100 model has developed a cult following in recent years."

Councillor Simon Hore, leader of Ribble Valley Borough Council, welcomed the listing.

Cllr Hore said: "This is just another reason to visit the small but wonderful Dunsop Bridge, which is at the geographic centre of Britain and on part of the King’s Duchy of Lancaster estate.

"Local residents, as well as the ducks that are regular 'visitors' to the village green where the kiosk is located, will be very proud to hear it’s been granted this elevated status as a landmark with a difference.

"The phone box has a special place in local people’s hearts."
