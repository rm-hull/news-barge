A man who strangled a woman until she was unconscious has been jailed for more than two years.

Bradford Crown Court heard how 46-year-old Anthony Roberts from Illingworth had been traumatised by the death of his mother in a house fire when he was 15, and that he drank on the anniversary of the incident.

He had been drinking and taking drugs on July 22 this year.

Prosecutor Jayne Beckett said Roberts and the victim, who were in a relationship, were at a barbeque together where Roberts consumed cocaine and alcohol.

An argument began which escalated when they went home.

Three days later, after visiting friends and going to a pub, the woman pushed Roberts to the face.

When they returned home and she went to get ready for bed, he followed her and placed his hands around her neck whilst she lay on the bed.

Mrs Beckett said: “He gripped her tightly while shouting at her. The victim struggled to breathe and can only recall the defendant being angry and red-faced.

“The victim passed out.

“Eventually the victim regained consciousness and went downstairs where she located the defendant. He then placed his hands on her shoulders and pushed her into a wall causing her head to split open on impact.”

The police were called and Roberts was arrested.

He claimed that the woman had assaulted him and that he had pushed her off.

The woman went to hospital where she was treated for a 1.5-inch wound to the back of her head. She also had marks around her neck.

In a statement read to the court on her behalf the victim said she had been left “completely traumatised” by the attack and had struggled to come to terms with what happened to her.

She said she felt constantly unsafe, was frequently on edge, paranoid, and that the incident had had “a profound impact” on every aspect of her life.

Roberts pleaded guilty to intentional strangulation and assault occasioning actual bodily harm.

The court heard that Roberts had previous convictions for assault, drugs offences, and criminal damage.

Mitigating, barrister Verity Barnes said Roberts had been “out of any major trouble” for almost 20 years and that he had nothing on his record for violence or domestic offending.

She said he did not accept that anything had happened on July 22, but he did accept entirely what had happened over the evening of July 24 into July 25.

She added: “He recognises what he did, he recognises that it was wrong, and that he needs to be punished.”

She said Roberts felt “disgusted and appalled” with himself, and that he was sorry.

She said he had been suffering with his mental health for 30 years ever since the death of his mother in a fire and had turned to drink and drugs to cope.

She said the incident happened around the anniversary of his mother’s death, when he took more drugs and drank more heavily.

Mr Recorder Tony Watkin sentenced Roberts to 27 months’ imprisonment for intentional strangulation plus six months, to run concurrently, for ABH.

He is also subject to a 10-year restraining order banning him from any contact with the woman that he strangled.

Recorder Watkin told Roberts: “One can only imagine the absolute fear that [your attack] would have instilled in [the victim] as you did that, seemingly out of control to the point where she could not breathe and lost consciousness.

“It is a very vivid image of the level of force and anger that you were projecting onto her at the time.”
