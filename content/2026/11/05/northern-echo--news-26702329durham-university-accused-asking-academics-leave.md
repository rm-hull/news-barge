Durham University has been accused of asking academics to leave so it can spend money elsewhere as it launches a voluntary severance scheme.

The university is giving academic staff the opportunity to apply to leave their jobs voluntarily, with the scheme opening on Monday (November 9), and closing on December 7.

Durham University said it was in a "secure financial position" and stressed the scheme was not a redundancy programme.

It said there were no targets for savings or staff cuts and there were no plans to reduce academic staff numbers when the scheme ends.

The university said voluntary severance would give staff an opportunity to consider their future while helping it create "sustained financial capacity to invest in priorities" linked to its strategy.

However, Durham University and College Union (UCU) questioned why academics were being asked to leave if there were no plans to reduce staff numbers.

A Durham UCU spokesperson said: "The University says it is in a secure financial position and will record an improved operating surplus for last year. So why is it asking academic staff to leave?

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/16906411.jpg?type=mds-article-620) *(Image: Northern Echo)*

"By its own account, this scheme has no savings target, and there are no plans to reduce academic staff numbers.

"Its stated purpose is to create 'sustained financial capacity to invest in priorities'. In other words, staff are being invited to go so that money can be spent elsewhere."

The union said it was the third year in a row that Durham staff had faced voluntary severance and claimed last year’s scheme resulted in around one in 20 colleagues leaving.

Last year the university announced it would cut staff costs by £10m, impacting about 200 non-academic positions with a further £10m reduction also proposed for 2026 - this would include a £5m reduction in academic staff costs and £5m in non-academic staff.

The spokesperson added: "If academic numbers will not fall, the University must explain how departing colleagues will be replaced.

"If they are not replaced, the work will fall on those who remain, just as it did last time."

UCU said remaining staff were already working longer hours and carrying heavier workloads.

The union welcomed the university’s commitment that the scheme would be voluntary, adding: "No one should feel pushed into leaving, least of all in the weeks before Christmas."

Durham University said applications would be considered based on teaching, research and wider operational needs, including the impact on workloads.

It also said it remained committed to retaining all academic disciplines and did not intend to close departments.

In response to The UCU, Durham University said: "It is important that the University returns a surplus, so that we can continue to invest in core priorities: in research, education, student experience, and supporting infrastructure.

“Over the last few years, Durham University has addressed the acute financial pressures affecting UK higher education through proactive and prudent financial management.

“This scheme is part of that ongoing process, because, put simply, costs are projected to rise more rapidly than income. We will continue to maintain Durham University's sound financial position.

“We monitor and manage University operations closely and responsibly to ensure that all staff have a manageable workload - continually reviewing what activity must be delivered, and what can be paused or stopped.

“In 2025, we declined many applications for voluntary severance because certain work had to continue.

"It is important that the University returns a surplus, so that we can continue to invest in core priorities: in research, education, student experience, and supporting infrastructure."

## Durham University statement in full:

A spokesperson for the university said: "Durham University is opening a voluntary severance scheme (VSS) for academic staff.

"Durham is in a secure financial position. We are a QS World Top 100 and UK top 10 university with regional, national and global impact.

"We continue to tightly manage our finances to maintain a strong academic environment for future generations.

"Voluntary severance gives academic colleagues an opportunity to consider their future plans. It also helps the University to create sustained financial capacity to invest in priorities aligned to our strategy.

"Decisions will be made following consideration of academic, research and operational needs, including academic workload implications.

"This scheme has been communicated widely to our academic staff and to our recognised trade unions. It opens on Monday 9 November and closes on Monday 7 December."
