# Major plans for the future of Kirkstall Abbey unveiled

## You can have your say on the plans

Major plans have been unveiled for the site of Kirkstall Abbey.

An ambitious vision for the future of much-loved Abbey House Museum has been revealed. Innovative proposals for the Kirkstall attraction would see the Grade II listed museum transformed into an immersive family-friendly visitor experience.

The plans for the site have been drawn up as part of a Reimagining Project supported by Leeds City Council and the Department for Culture, Media and Sport (DCMS)’s museum renewal fund, with the aim of taking the first step towards a brighter and more sustainable future for the attraction as it approaches its centenary year.

The concept designs include a series of dynamic proposals to create a more interactive visitor experience, with a focus on intergenerational play for people of all ages to enjoy together. The proposals include uplifting the museum’s traditional streets to enhance their immersive nature and include magical reveals that will let the public experience history through hands-on activities.

Collections celebrating Leeds will be at the heart of the museum, drawing inspiration from the popular toys and games, archaeology and social history collections, with ambitions to create flexible display spaces which are able to change regularly. The plans root the museum in its place, exploring the building’s centuries-old roots, including past residents and their links to Leeds, as well as its original role of being the gatehouse of Kirkstall Abbey.

The proposals mark a bold new direction for Abbey House Museum, which was put forward for potential closure in 2024 as part of measures to help Leeds City Council address unprecedented budget pressures. The proposals for the museum’s future have been drawn up after consultation and research including sessions with staff, local history groups and families. Yorkshire based architect firm Bauman Lyons (Leeds) and exhibition design consultancy Creative Core (Halifax) were appointed to work on this exploratory phase after a competitive open tender.

Further communication will now take place with staff, key stakeholders and the community before a development application for external funding will be submitted for further development work. An exhibition is also currently being planned for 2027 which will celebrate the museum’s centenary.

Councillor Salma Arif, Leeds City Council’s executive member for economy, said: “This is an exciting moment for the future of Abbey House Museum and one which we hope will be the beginning of a bold new direction for a special place which has been part of the Kirkstall community for centuries. During the public consultation on the museum’s future, what really came across was the passion and determination people have to see the site succeed, and we want to channel that enthusiasm into creating an attraction which can continue to welcome visitors for many years to come.”

Opened to the public in 1927, the building now occupied as Abbey House Museum originally served as Kirkstall Abbey’s gatehouse until the dissolution of the monasteries. It became a farmhouse and then a home for the owners of the Kirkstall Forge before opening as a museum.
