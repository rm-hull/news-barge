Thousands of pounds worth of tools have been stolen from a hospice during a break-in.

The theft took place at Sue Ryder Manorlands Hospice in Oxenhope on Wednesday when thieves forced entry into a maintenance workshop and gardeners' shed, taking power tools, hand tools, plumbing supplies, screws and gardening equipment.

The cost of replacing the stolen equipment is estimated to be more than £7,000, not including personal tools belonging to staff.

The break-in has been reported to West Yorkshire Police.

## 'They've taken anything they could get their hands on'

John Skilton, site supervisor at Sue Ryder Manorlands Hospice, said: "When we came in on Wednesday morning, we found that they’d cut the locks off and forced the doors open.

"They’ve taken all the power tools, all our hand tools, our stock of screws and plumbing supplies.

"They’ve taken anything they could get their hands on.

"We’ve done an inventory and the replacement cost of everything is around £7,000, and that's not including the personal tools belonging to staff."

## 'We can't even do some of the basic jobs we need to do'

The break-in has left the hospice’s maintenance team without the equipment it needs to carry out work on the site.

Mr Skilton said: "We weren't even able to repair the damage at first because we didn't have the tools to do it.

"We had to find more tools before we could carry on with the work.

"We can't even do some of the basic jobs we need to do."

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21675247.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Thieves stole thousands of pounds worth of tools *(Image: Tom Oakley)*

He explained that the buildings have since been secured.

The hospice has asked the public to make financial donations to help cover the costs, rather than make donations of tools, so the maintenance team can get back to carrying out repairs and work.

## 'It's been upsetting for everyone here'

Adam Brunkskill, regional fundraising team manager at Sue Ryder Manorlands Hospice, said: "It’s been upsetting for everyone here to discover what has happened.

"Every penny we spend on replacing tools and repairing the damage is money that we would much rather be putting towards caring for patients and supporting their families.

"We rely on donations to fund our care, so this kind of unexpected expense puts extra pressure on an already stretched budget.

"We’d be incredibly grateful to anyone who is able to make a donation towards the cost of replacing the stolen equipment.

"Financial donations will help us buy the tools and supplies we need to get the maintenance team back up and running."

## What has been stolen?

Mr Skilton said the loss of personal tools is especially hard for tradespeople, as many have built up their collections over many years.

He said: "Those tools are like a suit of clothes to a tradesperson.

"You’ve had them all your life, you’ve added to them and you know exactly what you need.

"It’s a big thing to lose them."

![Locks on the gardener's shed and maintenance workshop had been tampered with](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21675246.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Locks on the gardeners' shed and maintenance workshop had been tampered with *(Image: Tom Oakley)*

Mr Skilton added: "Sue Ryder Manorlands Hospice is such a well-loved place in the local community, and people will be appalled that this has happened here.

"We’re just trying to concentrate on getting back up and running at the moment.

"We need to buy the tools again so we can get on with the work that needs doing."

## What have West Yorkshire Police said?

A spokesperson for West Yorkshire Police said: "Police were called to reports of an overnight burglary at a hospice on Keighley Road, Oxenhope at around 9.00am on Wednesday 4 November.

"Enquires remain ongoing."

Anyone who may have information about the incident is urged to contact police via Live Chat at www.westyorkshire.police.uk/livechat quoting reference 13260626640.
