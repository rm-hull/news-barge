Two of Durham's most familiar faces will switch on the city's Christmas lights this year after being chosen as local heroes by the public.

Former Durham City police inspector Dave Clarke and City of Durham Parish Clerk Adam Shanley will take centre stage at Durham Christmas Sparkle on November 29 alongside Durham Mayor Councillor Amanda Hopgood.

The pair were selected following a public nomination process organised by Durham BID, which asked residents to put forward people who have made a difference to life in the city.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21675080.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Crowds gather in Durham city centre for a past Christmas lights switch-on event. *(Image: Stuart Boulton)*

While both are well known in Durham, the award recognises years of work behind the scenes supporting community groups, charities, businesses and residents.

Mr Shanley, a prominent figure in civic life in Durham, received a string of nominations praising his commitment to the city and the enthusiasm he brings to local projects and events.

One resident described him as a "genuinely lovely guy with bags of sparkle", while others credited him with helping promote Durham as a place to live, work and visit.

Mr Clarke, meanwhile, was recognised for his long record of public service.

The former police inspector now leads Durham Cats and Dogs Home and works extensively with community and charitable causes.

Last year, he was awarded the Honorary Freedom of the Parish of the City of Durham in recognition of his contribution to making the city safer and supporting local people.

His work has ranged from helping vulnerable residents and homeless people to caring for stray animals and bringing festive cheer to children in hospital through appearances as Father Christmas.

The pair’s friendship is also well known in Durham, with Mr Shanley once describing Mr Clarke as having an "incredible capacity to do good in this world".

Reacting to the news, Mr Shanley struck a typically light-hearted note.

Thousands of people are expected to attend Durham Christmas Sparkle later this month when the pair join civic leaders on stage to switch on the festive lights and officially launch the city's Christmas celebrations.
