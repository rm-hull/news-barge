Plans for two new homes in a County Durham village have been approved despite neighbours warning that extra cars could make a narrow track unsafe.

Durham County Council has given permission for the homes on land behind Meadowside, South Side, Shadforth.

One will have three bedrooms and the other five.

Ten objections were made, with much of the concern focused on plans for cars to reach the homes along a narrow track used by walkers, cyclists, horse riders and farm vehicles.

One neighbour said the route was "not suitable for general traffic" and warned that extra vehicles could cause "increased danger to walkers, cyclists, dog walkers, alpaca walkers, and children who frequently play there".

Another objector said allowing cars to reach the homes this way would "add to the potential conflict between vehicle and pedestrian users".

Concerns were also raised about heavy building vehicles damaging the track and the nearby village green during construction.

Neighbours questioned why a different entrance, which formed part of earlier plans for the site, could not be used instead.

One described that route as a "perfectly reasonable access point".

The new development will have space for up to eight cars.

Council officers accepted the previously approved entrance would be better but said the new route would not cause enough problems to turn down the plans.
