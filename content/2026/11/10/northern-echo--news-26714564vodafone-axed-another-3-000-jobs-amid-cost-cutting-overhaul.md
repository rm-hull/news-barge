Vodafone has cut 3,000 roles across its UK and European division in the first half of the year.

The company did not say how many of the jobs were lost in the UK, where it is headquartered.

Vodafone said it removed 200 million euros (£165.4 million) in costs across its European business, including 50 million euros (£41 million) of savings in the UK.

The UK savings followed its 2025 merger with Three UK to create VodafoneThree.

Roles were also affected in shared services operations, which provide support to the division from centres in India and eastern Europe.

Vodafone separately announced another 2,300 job cuts in Germany as it seeks to improve performance there.

The company is aiming to cut more than 2 billion euros (£1.64 billion) in annual costs across Europe and shared operations by 2030.

Its half-year results showed it had raised its annual earnings forecast to between 13.2 billion and 13.4 billion euros (£10.8 billion to £11 billion).

That implies organic growth of about 6% to 8%.

The previous forecast for underlying earnings was between 13 billion and 13.3 billion euros (£10.7 billion to £10.9 billion), following an increase in July.

Vodafone reported a statutory pre-tax loss of 373 million euros (£309 million) in the six months to September 30, compared with a profit of 2.1 billion euros (£1.7 billion) a year earlier.

The loss was due in part to a 4.85 billion euro (£3.98 billion) writedown in Germany and other impairment charges.

Margherita Della Valle, Vodafone group chief executive, said: “I am very happy about our overall group performance, but Germany is not yet where it needs to be.

“We have a clear action plan in progress, which is structurally simplifying our German operations and improving customer satisfaction across all of our services.”

Ms Della Valle has led a cost-cutting drive since taking the top job in 2023.

The company said 11,000 roles had been cut between then and the end of September, while annual costs had been reduced by 700 million euros (£580 million) so far.
