The people and traditions that have helped shape Darlington are at the heart of a new exhibition at Hopetown.

Connected Communities: Stories and Cultural Practices in Darlington brings together residents’ voices, memories and creative work to explore identity, culture and a sense of belonging.

Professor Azadeh Fatehrad, project lead and Professor of Art and Public Policy at Teesside University, said: “Connected Communities is about recognising that the identity of a place is shaped not only by its history and buildings, but also by the people who live there, the traditions they carry and the relationships they create.”

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21687282.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Stories of home, identity and belonging unite in a new exhibition *(Image: Supplied)*

"Through this project, we have worked with communities to explore how storytelling, creativity and shared cultural experiences can bring people together.

"The exhibition celebrates these contributions and invites us to think about belonging as something we build collectively."

The exhibition, which will run until January 3, 2027, highlights the cultural experiences of refugees, migrants and local residents.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21687283.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Stories of home, identity and belonging unite in a new exhibition *(Image: Supplied)*

It explores how everyday traditions, memories and creative activities can foster connection and a sense of home.

Darlington Council leader Councillor Stephen Harker said: "This exhibition is a reflection of the diverse people and communities that make Darlington such a great place to live, work and visit.

"Our rich history is made up of the experiences of residents from many different backgrounds, and this exhibition is a chance to find out more about them and celebrate the positive contribution they make to our borough."

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21687286.jpg?type=mds-article-620)New Hopetown exhibition brings Darlington’s community stories to life. *(Image: Supplied)*

The project brings together film, textiles, storytelling and other creative outputs developed in collaboration with the community.

It is the result of a year-long participatory arts and research programme led by Professor Fatehrad with support from cultural and community partners across Darlington.

Workshops, storytelling sessions and cultural exchanges have formed the basis of the project, bringing together people from different backgrounds to share and reflect on their experiences.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21687285.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Film, textiles and storytelling celebrate Darlington’s diverse communities. *(Image: Supplied)*

The exhibition focuses on themes of home, migration, friendship and cultural exchange.

It aims to show how collective cultural participation can strengthen social connections.

By hosting the exhibition at Hopetown, organisers hope to draw a link between Darlington’s railway and industrial history and the contemporary experiences of its people.

Creative Darlington manager Stephen Wiper said: "Our vision is that Darlington will be a place in 2026 and beyond where culture enriches lives, involves people and is central to identity and prosperity.

"The Creative Darlington programme budget support for the Connected Communities project, alongside support from other sources, has enabled people to enjoy and share creative activity together."

It will remain on display until early 2027.
