When it comes to pop culture, arguably no other topic is dominating the headlines quite like You Can See Everything.

That's right, the documentary about notorious Silicon Valley fraudster Elizabeth Holmes has become one of the most talked-about films of the year.

Directed by Nathan Fielder and Lance Oppenheim, the A24 documentary first hit cinemas in the US on October 16th, resulting in packed-out theatres. It is now making its way to international audiences.

Unfortunately, film fans in Germany will have to wait a little longer. But don't panic: it's on its way.

The official release date of the film in Germany is December 3rd. However, there are some showings ahead of this date in late November – but some are already sold out.

Here's how you can watch it in English in cinemas in Germany soon.

*Note that many showings are classed as OmU (original with German subtitles) or OV (original version). If you see DF, that means the film is dubbed in German.*

**READ ALSO: How to watch movies in your preferred language in German cinemasBerlin**

The York Kinogruppe, which has several cinemas around Berlin and one in Munich, already has a number of preview showings of the film scheduled from November 19th and November 26th. Try your luck for a ticket – but keep in mind that demand is strong and you might have to wait a little longer.

You can also buy tickets for screenings in early December.

Other cinemas showing the film include: UCI Luxe East Side Gallery, B-ware Ladenkino and Cineplex Titania.

**Hamburg** Cinemas in the northern city of Hamburg showing the documentary (as well as some previews) include the Savoy Filmtheater, the Abaton Kino, the UCI Kino Hamburg Mundsburg and Studio Kino.**Munich** If you're in the Munich area head to City Kinos, which is showing a couple of previews, as well as Museum Lichtspiele and Leopold Kino.**Frankfurt** It is more difficult to find previews of You Can See Everything in Germany's financial capital but the Arthouse Kinos Frankfurt is showing the film in its Harmonie cinema on December 3rd.**Cologne**

Those in the west should try and book tickets at the Cinedom for preview showings. Other cinemas with the documentary on the schedule include the Metropolis and Odeon.

And for those of you who just can't wait, you could do what some other movie fans have been doing and cross the border to Poland where it was released on November 6th.

Happy viewing!
