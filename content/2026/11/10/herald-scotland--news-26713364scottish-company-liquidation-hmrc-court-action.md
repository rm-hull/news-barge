A liquidator has been appointed to a Scottish construction trade firm.

It comes after an HMRC court action against the Stirling-based company.

The most recent accounts for Stewart Joinery Contracts Limited showed an average of 12 people employed by the company and liabilities of £176,616.

A petition was presented to Stirling Sheriff Court by His Majesty’s Revenue and Customs in July seeking a liquidator and to wind up the business.

![Construction worker working with house wooden roof against sunset sky](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21576597.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Liquidator appointed to Scottish construction trade company *(Image: Getty Images)*

Now Paul Dounis, of Opus Restructuring, has been appointed liquidator following an interim appointment in September.

Accounts for 2024 showed fixed assets of £13,205, set against £16,506 the year before.

Debtors amounted to £131,443 in 2024, compared with £115,446 in the period prior, with cash at bank and in hand £45,065, set against £110,993.

Creditors with the amount due within one year amounted to £298,482, compared with £220,421 the year before, and those with the amount due after one year was £76,847, set against £100,933.

A spokesperson for HMRC said it does not comment on individual cases for legal reasons, but added: "We take a supportive approach to dealing with customers who have tax debts and only file winding-up petitions once we’ve exhausted all other options, in order to protect taxpayers’ money."

It comes after there was a slight increase in the number of compulsory liquidations across the UK, according to the most recent figures.

In Scotland in September, there were 117 company insolvencies registered, 14% higher than the number for the same month last year.

The total number of company insolvencies included 68 creditors’ voluntary liquidations, 38 compulsory liquidations, 10 administrations and one company voluntary arrangement.

There were 73 company insolvencies registered in Scotland in August, 23% lower than the number in August 2025. The total number of company insolvencies was made up of 30 creditors’ voluntary liquidations, 35 compulsory liquidations and eight administrations.

South of the Border in September, there were 341 compulsory liquidations, 1,420 creditors’ voluntary liquidations, 102 administrations and 19 company voluntary arrangements.

The total company insolvencies in August in England and Wales was made up 314 compulsory liquidations, 1,431 creditors’ voluntary liquidations, 182 administrations and 19 company voluntary arrangements.

Molly Monks, insolvency specialist at Parker Walsh, said that “when creditors take matters to court, directors can have far fewer options left on the table”.
