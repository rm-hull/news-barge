A CANCER survivor whose son also battled the disease and became a double Paralympic gold medallist has taken part in a charity fashion show to support the charity who 'changed the lives' of her family.

Allison Whiteley, who is 61 and from Northallerton, has walked the catwalk at a Yorkshire Cancer Research event in York last Wednesday (November 4) which aimed saw 15 people from across the region taking to the catwalk in outfits sourced from the charity’s shops.

Each model represented the statistic that someone in Yorkshire is diagnosed with cancer every 15 minutes, with everyone involved affected by cancer - through either diagnosis or a loved ones journey, with some people (including Allison) falling into both categories.

Her son Laurence was diagnosed with osteosarcoma, a rare and aggressive bone cancer, 20 years ago at the age of 14.

Allison said: "Laurence was very sporty. He was a keen swimmer and a champion triathlete. Then suddenly, he was having chemotherapy, losing his hair and unable to walk.

"The treatment was so hard on his body. At once stage, we were told he only had a 30% chance of survival. You feel completely helpless as a parent. It felt like the end of the world."

Laurence went on to make a remarkable recovery, eventually learning to walk again and finding a new passion in rowing where he won gold for ParalympicsGB at both the Rio and Tokyo games.

Last year, Allison was diagnosed with breast cancer following a routine mammogram.

She said: "I had initially considered ignoring the screening invite because I was fit and active. We'd already had such a terrible experience with Laurence that I think I convinced myself it wouldn't happen to me, or at least not at my age.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/5565213.jpg?type=mds-article-620)HOME: Paralympic gold medalist Laurence Whiteley is given a hero's welcome as he returns toTees Rowing Club, Stockton. Picture: SARAH CALDECOTT.

"Hearing I had cancer brought everything back from my son's diagnosis."

She underwent a mastectomy and reconstructive surgery earlier this year, and recent scans have shown no evidence of cancer.

Alison added: "Nobody really prepares you for how different you might feel after surgery. I didn't want to lose my breast and it's taken time to adjust.

"There have been moments when I've felt self-conscious, but I've learned to focus on everything my body has carried me through.

"I'm still getting used to the changes, but I'm in a much more positive place than I was a year ago."

As part of her recovery, she took part in the Yorkshire Cancer Research fashion show at the charity’s new superstore, where 15 models took to the runway in outfits sourced from the charity’s shops.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21676483.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The Yorkshire Cancer Research fashion show shone a spotlight on 15 supporters - one for every minute a person is diagnosed with cancer in Yorkshire *(Image: Jonathan Pow)*

She said: "After cancer affected our family twice, I'm ready to embrace new challenges and do what I can to support the work of Yorkshire Cancer Research.

"I wanted to show that life continues after cancer and that it's possible to feel confident again."

"Research has changed the lives of our family. It helped Laurence survive a rare cancer diagnosis and go on to become a Paralympic champion, and it helped ensure my cancer was detected early and treated.

"I want more people across Yorkshire to have same opportunities of life after cancer."
