A £2.5m programme has been unveiled to help people across the Tees Valley access skilled jobs, strengthen grassroots charities and improve the environment.

Launched by NZT Power and the Northern Endurance Partnership (NEP), the multi-year initiative aims to deliver lasting benefits through employment and education opportunities, community projects and improvements to natural spaces.

Ian Hunter, managing director of NZT Power, said: "Teesside is an historic industrial heartland, and NZT Power is part of its next chapter.

"Our priority is ensuring the benefits of this project are felt right here on our doorstep, from today through to the next generation.

"This £2.5 million plan is a big part of that, helping to build skills, careers and stronger communities across the Tees Valley."

The programme is based on a local needs assessment and input from more than 40 regional organisations.

It will fund support in four areas: education, routes into work, community resilience, and sustainability and biodiversity.

Schools will get hands-on STEM workshops, careers guidance and teacher training.

Partners include the High Tide Foundation, Spark Tees Valley and Redcar & Cleveland Voluntary Development Agency.

Secure Roots, a new school programme delivered with Spark Aspirations, was launched alongside the plan.

Secure Roots will run in 50 Tees Valley primary schools, covering mental health, financial literacy, first aid and anti-social behaviour.

It aims to build resilience and vital life skills in young children.

All primary schools in the area will get free access to Spark’s digital careers platform, connecting children with local role models and career options.

The programme will support skills through apprenticeship levy gifting, funded technical training and recruitment opportunities in NZT Power’s supply chain.

Rich Denny, managing director of NEP, said: "Creating the infrastructure needed to achieve net zero is about more than engineering projects.

"It's about investing in people, supporting communities and building a legacy that benefits future generations.

"Through this funding, we can help address local priorities by creating opportunities for people across Tees Valley to develop new skills, explore careers and share in the long-term benefits these projects will bring."

The plan also includes community resilience projects developed with Business in the Community.

They will support people facing food poverty with FareShare North East, upgrade community hubs and help tackle debt, mental health problems and digital poverty.

Staff will volunteer and provide in-kind support through the programme.

Environmental funding will support habitat restoration and public engagement through the Tees Valley Nature Partnership and the Access to Nature scheme at Coatham Marsh.

Claire Wordsworth, stakeholder and partnerships manager at NZT Power, said: "We have taken time to listen to local organisations and understand where investment can make the greatest difference.

"This plan has been shaped by what communities have told us they need, and by evidence of where some of the greatest challenges and opportunities lie.

"Our ambition is not simply to fund individual projects, but to build long-term partnerships that help strengthen communities, create opportunities and leave a lasting legacy.

"Secure Roots is a great example of that approach, supporting children early in their lives with the confidence, skills and aspirations that can make a real difference to their futures."

The new investment comes on top of £2m already spent socially by NZT Power and NEP.

Past commitments include the Tees Valley Net Zero Industry Scholarship Programme and regional apprenticeship schemes.

The programme’s results will be monitored throughout to check it meets community needs and delivers real results.

The projects are expected to create or support more than 3,000 jobs.
