A County Durham garden centre boss has defended charging families to see Santa's reindeer after furious customers branded the move "scandalous".

Richardson's Garden Centre in Hawthorn, near Seaham, has found itself at the centre of a festive row after introducing a £2 admission fee for its popular reindeer attraction for the first time.

The move sparked an online backlash from customers who questioned paying for a Christmas tradition that had been free for years.

One customer argued the reindeer already generated income for the family-run business by attracting visitors who would then spend money in the café, restaurant and shop.

They said: "Sometimes an attraction doesn't need to make money directly because it brings customers through the door who spend money elsewhere.

"I'm not saying the reindeer don't cost money to look after, but charging £2 per person could also put people off visiting altogether. I don't think people are being entitled by questioning a charge that wasn't there for the previous years."

Others feared the introduction of a charge would make it harder for families to continue a festive tradition many have enjoyed for decades.

One commenter suggested a booking system could have controlled visitor numbers, while another said the charge had reduced how much they spent elsewhere during their visit.

They said: "I went last week, didn't realise you had to pay until I got there.

"Normally visit the café but after paying to see the reindeer and buying food for them I didn't bother getting a coffee."

![One of the reindeer at Richardson's Garden Centre near Dalton Park peers over a fence with its tongue sticking out, while other members of the herd stand in a straw-covered enclosure behind it.](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21690024.jpg?type=mds-article-620)A curious reindeer sticks its tongue out at visitors at Richardson's Garden Centre, where a new £2 admission fee has sparked debate among some families. *(Image: George Richardson)*

But for owner George Richardson, the backlash misses a bigger picture.

The 55-year-old said he was "a bit surprised and a little angered" by the criticism, claiming people had overlooked rising reindeer costs and this year's major investment.

"There was a degree of entitlement from people," he said.

"It's like people thought that because it had been free for so many years it should always be free.

"I think more people would've been up in arms if we hadn't done the reindeer at all."

Mr Richardson says the reality behind the attraction is far removed from the simple reindeer pen many visitors see.

The garden centre maintains a herd of about 150 reindeer, with smaller groups rotating through the public display throughout the festive season.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21690046.jpg?type=mds-article-620)A reindeer from Richardson's Garden Centre's 150-strong herd grazes in a field. The business says rising care costs contributed to the introduction of a £2 visitor fee this Christmas. *(Image: George Richardson)*

Mr Richardson said caring for them comes at a considerable cost.

Feed alone costs about £500 a week, while a recent order of straw came to £5,000 before veterinary bills and other welfare costs are factored in.

He said: "For years we've provided the reindeer for free, with customers just having to pay £1 for reindeer feed.

"The problem is the cost of maintaining the reindeer has gone astronomical."

But the Garden Centre owner insists visitors are no longer simply paying to see the reindeer.

This year, the family-run business has invested more than £20,000 expanding its Christmas offering, transforming what was once predominantly a reindeer attraction and Christmas tree sales area into a much larger festive experience.

Visitors are now greeted by singing snowmen, festive displays, animatronics and interactive features designed to create a more immersive Christmas atmosphere.

Mr Richardson believes that part of the story has been lost amid the criticism.

"What has been misunderstood is that this year has been a real increase in what we are offering," he said.

"In past years, we've kind of just had reindeer in a pen and then sold some Christmas trees around it.

"This year we've spent over £20,000 on Christmas decorations and animatronics. It's all just an attempt to really embrace the Christmassy feel."

The fee was also introduced in response to soaring visitor numbers.

According to Mr Richardson, last year's attraction became so busy that some families struggled to get close to the animals, prompting the garden centre to look at ways of managing demand and improving the overall experience.

The display is now permanently staffed, allowing visitor numbers to be monitored and entry controlled. Children under one are admitted free of charge.

Despite the online row, Mr Richardson is confident families will continue to support the attraction in the run-up to Christmas.

After a slower start to the festive season than expected, visitor numbers have already begun to pick up, with a strong turnout last weekend helping to ease concerns.
