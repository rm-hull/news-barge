New images have revealed the ongoing transformation of the site a former office block Edinburgh’s first new city centre concert hall for more than 100 years.

The project to create a 1000-capacity venue in the heart of the capital’s New Town is said to have reached a “major construction milestone” with the completion of excavation work to create its 10-metre-deep basement.

The “scale and outline” of the Dunard Centre is said to have been revealed by the completion of a “complex phase” of the £162 million venue, which will take shape near St Andrew Square over the next three years.

Dressing rooms, backstage facilities and a lower ground floor bar will all be installed in the space that has been excavated in recent months.

The latest phase of work on the project, which was rescued just over a year ago by the Scottish Government when it committed an additional £20m, has seen the installation of “temporary props” to support the basement walls.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21693055.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Dunard Centre Jo Buckley has described progress on the new Edinburgh concert hall as 'really thrilling'. *(Image: Ian Georgeson)*

Chief executive Jo Buckley said the latest work offered “thrilling” glimpses of the new venue, which is expected to be completed by the end of 2029.

The new venue is expected to “rise from the ground” early next year when the building’s steelwork begins to take shape.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21693052.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Excavation work has been completed on the site of Edinburgh's new Dunard Centre concert hall. *(Image: Ian Georgeson)*

The next phases of the construction project include concrete works in the ground and lower basements of the venue, as well as the installation of the new building’s “structural cores”.

The Dunard Centre will provide a new home for the Edinburgh International Festival and the Scottish Chamber Orchestra.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21693048.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Work on the new Dunard Centre concert hall in Edinburgh city centre is expected to be completed by 2029. *(Image: Multivista)*

The first purpose-built concert venue to be created since the Usher Hall opened in 1914, the Dunard Centre will be designed to accommodate classical, pop, rock, folk, jazz and electronica concerts, as well as spoken word events.

The concert hall project was first announced more than a decade ago by Impact Scotland, a new charitable trust created by American arts philanthropist Dr Carol Colburn Grigor, after it reached agreement with the Royal Bank of Scotland to secure a site behind its historic 18th century Dundas House building on St Andrew Square.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/20114889.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Work on the Dunard Centre concert hall in Edinburgh city centre is expected to be completed by the end of 2029. *(Image: David Chipperfield Architects)*

However, the project was dogged by delays and funding problems until a final contract was signed with construction giant Balfour Beatty last October.

Initial plans to replace a five-storey RBS office block dating back to the 1960s with the new venue were backed by the city council in 2019, when the venue was expected to cost £45 million to create.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21693070.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Work is underway on the 10-metre deep basement areas of Edinburgh's new Dunard Centre concert hall near St Andrew Square. *(Image: Ian Georgeson)*

By then, the Scottish and UK governments had already agreed to commit £10m each to the project, with the city council contributing £5m.

However, the project was held up by the Covid pandemic and a protracted legal dispute with the developers of the neighbouring St James Quarter development, which was only resolved after the concert hall plans were scaled back to reduce the impact of the venue on views from a new luxury hotel.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21693139.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The Dunard Centre will be Edinburgh's first purpose-built new concert hall for more than a century. *(Image: David Chipperfield Architects)*

Demolition work on the former RBS offices got underway at the start of 2023 but the gap site lay empty for around 18 months as work went on behind the scenes to bridge the growing funding gap.

Under the rescue deal announced by Impact Scotland last October, the Scottish Government’s additional £20m commitment is being matched by Dr Colburn Grigor.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21693083.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Balfour Beatty project director Martin McGough and Dunard Centre chief executive Jo Buckley inside the basement area of the new Edinburgh concert hall. *(Image: Ian Georgeson)*

The start of excavation works was announced in June when the “full footprint” of the venue was revealed for the first time.

Around 16,000 cubic metres of material is said to have since been removed from the site – the equivalent of around 2750 grand pianos.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21693140.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Classical, pop, rock, folk, jazz, spoken word and electronica events are planned to be staged at the new Dunard Centre concert hall in Edinburgh city centre. *(Image: David Chipperfield Architects)*

A spokesperson for the Dunard Centre said: “The work marks the end of a complex phase of the project, revealing the scale and outline of Edinburgh’s first purpose-built concert hall in over 100 years.

“Temporary props supporting the basement walls are now in place, while concrete works are progressing to form the ground and lower basement levels.

“The basement will house essential operational infrastructure for the venue, alongside dressing rooms, a lower ground floor bar and a range of backstage and support facilities that will help bring the future venue to life.

“The next phase of construction will see the installation of the building's structural cores, which will house staircases and service shafts while providing critical support for the structure above.

“Steelwork is expected to begin at the start of next year, marking the next visible stage as the venue begins to rise from the ground.”

Ms Buckley said: “It's great to be past what can often be a particularly challenging phase in any construction project, digging into volcanic rock laid down thousands of years ago.

“To be able to see the full shape and outline of our future venue – from the footprint of the box office to the curve of the auditorium and even the first hints at stairwells – is really thrilling.

"Not only can we now appreciate the scale and depth of the venue, we can start to picture how it will actually be used - where audiences will arrive, how people will flow through the building, and how the new public pathways that hug our walls will connect our venue with the restaurants, bars, hotels and shops in this vibrant corner of the city.

“Most importantly, it helps us imagine what this place will become: a venue buzzing with life, people, music and culture.”

Martin McGough, project director at Balfour Beatty, said: “Completing the main excavation marks a significant milestone for the Dunard Centre and is testament to the expertise and dedication of our project team and specialist supply chain partners.

“With works to form the basement structure now well underway, we look forward to building on this progress as the Dunard Centre begins to rise above the ground in the new year.”
