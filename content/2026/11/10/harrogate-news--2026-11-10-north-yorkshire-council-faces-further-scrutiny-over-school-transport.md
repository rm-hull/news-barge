North Yorkshire Council is proposing further changes to its home-to-school transport policy following a review of the system introduced in September 2024.

The recommendations will be considered by councillors at a full council meeting on Wednesday 18 November, following a six-week consultation which attracted 588 responses.

The council has highlighted strong levels of support among consultation respondents for several of the proposed changes. However, the figures represent the views of those who took part and do not necessarily reflect the views of families across the county as a whole.

The revised policy, introduced in September 2024, changed the main eligibility criteria for free home-to-school transport. Under the policy, eligibility is generally based on a child’s nearest school with available places, rather than simply their catchment school.

The council said the changes were introduced to make the service financially sustainable, fair to families and consistent with government guidance. A post-implementation review was promised after a full year of operation.

## Consultation results

More than 450 of the 588 people who responded to the consultation were parents and carers.

The council reported that 85 per cent of respondents supported updates to the mapping data, while nearly 80 per cent backed clearer guidance on school entrance points and eligibility.

More than 86 per cent agreed that discretionary measures should be considered where two schools share the same drop-off point for council-run transport.

Proposals to clarify eligibility where routes beyond the statutory walking distance are considered unsafe received nearly 83 per cent support.

Changes to the way distances between children’s homes and schools are measured received support from nearly 78 per cent of respondents. Meanwhile, 60 per cent agreed that the proposed revisions would make the policy clearer and easier to understand.

## Rural routes among the proposals

The consultation also considered proposals to remove five routes from the council’s mapping system because of the potential for severe weather and challenging driving conditions.

The routes are Fleet Moss Road, Keld to Kirkby Stephen, Buttertubs Pass, The Stang and Kidstone Pass. Almost 84 per cent of respondents supported the proposal.

A further route, between Halton Gill and Settle, was highlighted by members of the public and will also be considered by councillors.

The implications of these proposals for families, particularly those living in rural areas, will form part of the decisions being considered at the full council meeting.

## Concerns over the impact on families

Concerns have also been raised about the wider consequences of the transport policy for families, with criticism that decisions about children’s education and wellbeing have increasingly become financial decisions for parents.

Arnold Warneken, chair of the North Yorkshire Green Party, said the consultation did not adequately address the practical difficulties created by the existing policy.

He argued that families had been placed in situations where they faced choosing between sending siblings to different schools, selecting schools that might not be appropriate for their children’s needs, or taking on additional journeys at their own expense.

Warneken also highlighted the potential financial hardship for families and the environmental consequences of increased car journeys.

He said the proposed changes remained inadequate and criticised the length of time before they would take effect, as well as the absence of retrospective reassessment or compensation for families affected by the current arrangements.

In his response, Warneken described the policy as having been driven by financial considerations rather than the interests of children, families and communities. He also questioned the resources spent dealing with what he described as repeated problems with the system.

He called on the council’s Executive Member to demonstrate a willingness to change direction, saying that it was not too late to replace what he described as chaos with courage and compassion.

## When would changes take effect?

The council’s executive considered the remaining 17 recommendations from the policy review on 18 September. Ten were approved, while seven were noted because they related to arrangements already included in the current policy.

North Yorkshire Council provides home-to-school transport for around 11,000 eligible pupils. The service costs more than £54 million each year, according to the council.

Any changes approved by councillors will not take effect before September 2027 and will apply to children starting Reception and Year 7 in September 2028.

Parents and carers applying for school places this academic year for children starting Reception in September 2027 are being advised that the existing policy remains in place until decisions have been made.

The full council will consider the recommendations on 18 November 2026.
