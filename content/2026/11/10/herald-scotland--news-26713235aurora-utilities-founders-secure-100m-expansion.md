Two Scottish founders have secured a further £100 million equity commitment as they expand their business.

The latest fundraising takes the total equity commitments secured by the founders and their team for the business to £250m since 2024, when they attracted around £150m of backing.

The Aurora Utilities business created by the two founders supports onshore wind farms and other renewable energy projects, battery energy storage systems, data centre developments, and heavy transport electrification.

Its projects include the electricity connection for Scotland’s first megawatt heavy goods vehicle (HGV) charging hub, as well as the adoption and operation of new grid connections for bus operator Stagecoach to power high-capacity electric vehicle (EV) charging infrastructure in Arbroath, Ardrossan, Dundee, Kilmarnock and St Andrews.

Aurora Utilities, which is chaired by former SSE finance director Gregor Alexander and has grown from two to 55 people since launch, connects residential and commercial infrastructure projects across Great Britain to the electricity network. It is an Ofgem-regulated, independent electricity networks business.

The two founders driving the business and its growth are chief executive Simon Reilly, originally from Laurencekirk, and chief commercial officer Jamie McAinsh, from Perth.

The pair previously worked together for more than three years at mua Group, the independent multi-utility network operator established as a joint venture between SGN and J. Murphy & Sons.

Mr McAinsh still lives in Perth, splitting his time between there and Aurora’s London headquarters.

Mr Alexander chairs SSEN Transmission and is a non-executive director of NEOS Networks, a UK business-to-business telecoms provider which is a joint venture between Perth-based energy giant SSE and Infracapital Partners.

The £100m equity commitment has been secured from I Squared Capital, Allianz Global Investors, Swedish pension fund Kåpan and M&G Investments.

Recognising their shared belief in the transition away from oil and gas towards greater electrification, Mr Reilly and Mr McAinsh felt a partnership would allow them to play a direct role in delivering the infrastructure needed to support the UK’s decarbonisation.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21692074.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Simon Reilly of Aurora Energy and Damian Darragh of I Squared Capital. *(Image: Supplied)*

Drawing on their experience in gas and multi-utility businesses, they saw an opportunity to establish an electricity network operator built around that transition “from the outset”.

Flagging its “next phase of growth”, in the wake of securing the additional £100m equity commitment, Aurora Utilities said: “The additional capital marks an important milestone in Aurora’s continued expansion across Great Britain, accelerating the delivery of critical energy infrastructure projects to meet growing demand driven by electrification, grid modernisation and the transition to net zero.”

Aurora Utilities declared that, since securing its initial investment, it has established itself as “one of the fastest-growing IDNOs (independent distribution network operators) in the UK”, expanding its customer base and “delivering electricity infrastructure across a diverse range of sectors”.

The company said that the latest funding would “support continued recruitment”, including “further strengthening Aurora’s in-house operations, engineering and land rights capabilities”.

Aurora Utilities added: “The additional commitment underscores the continued confidence from leading institutional infrastructure investors in Aurora’s long-term growth strategy, operational delivery and role in supporting the UK’s future energy system. It also highlights the increasingly important role of independent network operators in facilitating investment in the UK’s electricity infrastructure and supporting the transition to a more electrified economy.”

Aurora Utilities noted that, since entering the market, it had expanded its presence across the energy infrastructure sector, supporting major housing developers, large-scale data centres, commercial and industrial projects, megawatt charging hubs for HGVs, public transport electrification programmes, battery storage, and renewable energy projects.

It added: “This growing footprint includes networks that charge one in four electric buses in London, alongside connections supporting more than 2.36 TWh (terawatt-hours) of solar generation and battery energy storage, equivalent to the annual electricity needs of almost one million UK homes.”

The wider senior team at Aurora Utilities includes chief financial officer Nairn Black, from Dundee, and delivery director Mark Sneddon, from Glenrothes. Mr Sneddon oversees the technical delivery of new grid connections.

Mr Reilly said: “This latest investment reflects the strength of our business, the capability within our team and the scale of opportunity emerging across the UK energy infrastructure sector.

“Demand for resilient electricity infrastructure continues to accelerate as the UK transitions towards a more electrified economy. With the support of I Squared Capital, Allianz Global Investors, Kåpan and M&G Investments, we are well positioned to continue scaling the business and delivering for our customers.”

Damian Darragh, fund partner at I Squared Capital, said: “Aurora has delivered impressive growth since our initial investment, establishing a strong platform in a sector that is fundamental to the UK’s energy transition and economic competitiveness. We look forward to continuing to support Simon and the team as they further scale the business across the UK.”

Anish Majmudar, head of infrastructure and real assets at M&G Investments, said: “The electrification of the UK economy is creating a significant need for investment in the infrastructure that connects new sources of demand and generation to the grid. Aurora is helping address that need across areas including transport, renewable energy, battery storage and data centres.

“We have been impressed by the quality of the team, the progress the business has made and its ability to compete for increasingly complex connections. This investment gives us the opportunity to support the next stage of Aurora’s growth while investing in infrastructure that is critical to the UK’s energy transition.”

Aurora Utilities said: “The business has developed a strong position in a market benefiting from long-term structural growth, underpinned by the electrification of transport, increasing power requirements from data centres and industrial users, residential housing developments, the deployment of renewable energy and battery storage, and the wider modernisation of the UK's electricity networks.”
