Seven people have found themselves in court for breaching Durham city's booze ban.

All seven were convicted of breaching the city’s Public Space Protection Order after ignoring warnings to stop drinking, police said.

The order, which came into force in October 2024, restricts public drinking, begging and taking drugs and urinating in public in Durham city centre and is enforced by police and neighbourhood wardens.

**Those prosecuted were:**

* Jason Bainbridge, 56, of no fixed abode, who was found guilty of breaching the PSPO and remanded in custody.
* Jason Lythe, 37, of no fixed abode, was found guilty and released on conditional bail.
* John Bostock, 47, of Trimdon Station, who was found guilty and released on conditional bail.
* Debbie Traynor, 44, of no fixed abode, who was found guilty and sentenced to 12 weeks in prison, suspended for 12 months.
* Thomas Pye, 36, of no fixed abode, who was found guilty and fined £50.
* Lindsey Smith, 32, of Chester-le-Street, who was found guilty and received a six-month conditional discharge.
* Robert Carr, 41, of no fixed abode, who was found guilty of breaching the PSPO and fined £80 for resisting arrest.

Police said people who breach the order are given a chance to comply, but face enforcement action if they ignore warnings.

That can lead to arrest, prosecution and a criminal conviction.

Durham Police warned that anyone who keeps drinking in the designated area after being told to stop could face enforcement action and be taken to court.
