A Bradford off licence with links to criminal behaviour cannot be run by a man currently living in Blackpool – councillors have decided.

The Bradford Licensing Panel refused to transfer the alcohol licence of Lynxes on Sunbridge Road to Rebin Shaswar Hamasalih at a meeting on Monday.

They were told that while Mr Hamasalih was willing to move to Bradford for the £250 a week job, he would initially have to carry out his premises supervisor duties from Blackpool – including viewing the store’s CCTV remotely from his home in the seaside town until he makes the move to West Yorkshire.

The panel were discussing an application to transfer the licence for Lynxes to Mr Hamasalih from the current licence holder Mohammed Jihad Jamal.

## Alcohol licence stripped earlier this year

Mr Jamal was stripped of his licence to sell alcohol at a hearing earlier this year after the panel heard that raids on the store in April uncovered hundreds of packets of counterfeit cigarettes.

![Counterfeit cigarettes found in the Sunbridge Road branch of Lynxes](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21035047.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Counterfeit cigarettes found in the Sunbridge Road branch of Lynxes *(Image: West Yorkshire Trading Standards)*

He had told the panel that he was in Iran at the time of the raid, and had no idea that his staff had been selling illegal products.

He has appealed the decision to revoke his licence, and the appeal will be held at Bradford Magistrates Court on Wednesday.

## Application to transfer licence to Blackpool man

Since the revocation he applied to have the licence transferred to one of his employees who was present during the raid, but withdrew that application after West Yorkshire Police objected.

An application to transfer the licence to Mr Hamasalih was then submitted.

The application claimed he was an independent person with no links to the previous running of the store.

But police once again objected. They said when speaking to staff at a recent visit to the store, no one knew of Mr Hamasalih, and said Mr Jamal remained “the boss.”

In a letter to the panel police said: “The police concern is whether these applications represent the genuinely independent change in management and control, or whether the previous management arrangements remain substantially in place.”

At the meeting Mr Hamasalih was represented by barrister Vincent Blake-Barnard, who said: “The applicant is aware convincing the panel will be an uphill struggle.

"My role is to convince you that this application is not merely a fiction, but represents a real change in how the organisation is run.”

## Blackpool man did not turn up

Mr Hamasalih did not attend Monday’s meeting.

Asked to explain his absence, Mr Blake-Barnard said he lived in Blackpool and likely “didn’t want to incur the expense” of travelling to Bradford to make his case to the panel.

Mr Blake-Barnard added: “In an ideal world he would be here to put the committees concerns to rest.”

He pointed out that Mr Hamasalih had previously been granted a licence by Blackpool Council, showing another authority had found him to be a fit and proper person.

Referring to police concerns that no staff knew of Mr Hamasalih, he said Mr Jamal was waiting until the decision was made before informing his employees of the new DPS.

## £250 a week

Mr Jamal was present at the meeting, and said he was the owner, and still ran the shop – Mr Hamasalih’s role would be to make sure the shop stuck to its licence.

The panel were told that when Mr Hamasalih takes on the role, he will receive £250 a week, with Mr Blake-Barnard saying: “As soon as the role is guaranteed he will be moving to the area from Blackpool.

“There may be a short interim period of a few weeks where he has to monitor the store CCTV from his home (Blackpool), but he would make weekly visits to the shop.”

Councillor Ian Evans (Reform, Queensbury) pointed out that this would be less than minimum wage to run a shop.

Mr Blake-Barnard pointed out that a DPS doesn’t have to be at the premises the whole time.

Cllr Evans said: “So he’ll move to Bradford for a £250 a week job?”

Mr Blake-Barnard said: “We’ve not indicated that it would be his only job. He is currently unemployed, this is his next step back into work.”

PC Tony Brown told the panel that there were numerous inconsistencies with the application, and concerns remained over Mr Jamal’s continued role at the top of the business.

He said: “It is said that Mr Hamasalih is currently unemployed, but he is currently listed as the DPS for 51-53 Topping Street in Blackpool, and he has held that licence since September.

“I would ask the panel to consider if the £250 per week payment indicates the amount of managerial oversite he will have.”

PC Brown pointed out that business that Mr Hamasalih runs in Blackpool opens from 8am to 11pm, and Lynxes opens from 7am to 2am.

He said: “He will be running two shops, two hours away from each other. Would he be able to attend the shop if an incident happened?”

The panel refused to transfer the licence.
