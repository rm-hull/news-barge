While public discussions around congressional impeachment usually center on a sitting president, the constitutional mechanism extends to a broader array of federal leadership.

The same provision that allows Congress to impeach a president — Article II, Section 4 — also gives it the authority to impeach and remove federal officials, including Cabinet secretaries:

“The President, Vice President and all civil Officers of the United States, shall be removed from Office on Impeachment for, and Conviction of, Treason, Bribery, or other high Crimes and Misdemeanors.”

Cabinet secretaries are included in the part about “all civil Officers of the United States.”

There's no strict definition of “high crimes and misdemeanors,” and it isn't necessarily akin to violation of a criminal statute or court conviction. Historically, for presidents and other officials, impeachment has been used to address abuses of official power and breaches of public trust.

## Is impeaching Cabinet officers the same as it is for presidents?

The process is very similar.

![Pete Hegseth takes questions from reporters during a news conference at the Pentagon on November 10, 2026 in Arlington, Virginia](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/10/21/2304464390..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Cases originate in the House of Representatives, with the introduction of a resolution or a committee investigating allegations. The House investigates alleged misconduct and decides whether to formally charge an official through articles of impeachment.

If that happens, and a simple majority of the House votes to impeach, then the Senate holds a trial.

For presidents, how that trial is conducted is explicitly set out in the Constitution, with the chief justice of the U.S. Supreme Court presiding.

It's not as clear for Cabinet secretaries as to who oversees the proceedings.

## If a Cabinet official is impeached, is he removed from office?

No. It takes a conviction to do that.

Just like for presidents, saying that someone has been impeached is basically a formal accusation, not a conviction. A secretary who has been impeached by the House can continue serving while the Senate considers the charges.

## How much of Congress has to agree for a Cabinet secretary to be removed?

It takes a simple majority in the House to technically impeach a Cabinet secretary. But for a conviction in the Senate, it takes a two-thirds vote, a significantly higher threshold.

## How common is impeachment for Cabinet secretaries?

Overall, it's rare.

William W. Belknap, who served as war secretary for President Ulysses S. Grant, was the first Cabinet secretary to be impeached. He resigned hours before the House voted to impeach him in 1876 on allegations he accepted bribes in a trading post scandal.

He was subsequently acquitted by the Senate.

The second Cabinet secretary to be impeached was Alejandro Mayorkas, who served as Homeland Security Secretary in the Biden administration. The House narrowly voted to impeach Mayorkas in February 2024. The Senate dismissed the case two months later, saying it didn't meet constitutional standards, and Mayorkas continued to serve until President Joe Biden left office.
