The Prime Minister has committed to ensuring concerns raised in The Northern Echo’s manifesto are “taken seriously”.

Andy Burnham said the Echo has “rightly kept a spotlight on issues that matter deeply to people across the North East” as he praised our six-point manifesto for the region.

It includes calling on him to compensate victims of the Kirklevington scandal, making devolution work, and saving high streets from decline.

A public inquiry into the County Durham and Darlington breast cancer scandal, committing to Maya's Law, and speeding up the Tees, Esk and Wear Valley (TEWV) mental health trust public inquiry make up the list of six priorities.

Mr Burnham promised to deliver two points in August - devolution and saving high streets - and vowed to get back to the Echo on others.

And this week, he said he is committed to ensuring concerns are taken seriously.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21679866.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Prime Minister Andy Burnham *(Image: Peter Byrne / PA)*

The vow came as he spoke to the Echo about the Kirklevington scandal and the tragic story of victim Paul Overton, who died before he could get justice for the abuse he suffered as a boy.

He was one of hundreds subjected to horrific daily abuse behind bars between the 1960s and 1990s - and not one former officer has been brought to justice.

He said: “The accounts of abuse at Kirklevington are deeply disturbing, and I know how long victims and their families have been waiting for answers.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21342486.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The Northern Echo manifesto *(Image: THE NORTHERN ECHO)*

“Paul Overton’s story is a powerful reminder of the lasting impact this has had on people’s lives. His family, and every person affected, deserve to have their voices heard.

“The Northern Echo has rightly kept a spotlight on issues that matter deeply to people across the North East, and I am committed to ensuring the concerns raised in its manifesto are taken seriously.”

## **Key priorities**

When Mr Burnham took over from Keir Starmer, he said he would speak up for all aspects of the UK - and our manifesto calls on him to stick to that promise.

In September, the Government announced the public inquiry into TEWV would finally get underway, chaired by retired judge John Potter, who said his first priority was to “meet and listen to those affected”.

The date of the inquiry’s first public hearing has not been announced.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/20817016.jpg?type=mds-article-620)A Tees Esk and Wear Valley NHS Trust sign *(Image: Sarah Caldecott)*

Construction work is also continuing on Darlington's Treasury hub, which was dubbed No 11 North by Chancellor John Healey earlier this year.

It is hoped that the Darlington Economic Campus will be an example of making devolution happen - with Mr Healey saying his bid to “make growth in every postcode” was already “well underway” in the town.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21631811.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The new Darlington Treasury hub *(Image: TVCA)*

Other issues in the manifesto include saving high streets, with Mr Burnham previously telling the Echo were "absolutely going to get more support”.

We are also calling for a public inquiry into the breast cancer scandal, where around 1,500 patient records remain under review while victims continue to wait for answers.

An independent review last November found “systemic failures”, “missed warnings” and “ignored red flags” in the trust’s breast services. It said 51 women were harmed.

The Echo has also joined the family of murdered Shotton Colliery toddler Maya Chappell to call for Maya's Law.

The campaign calls for a Child Risk Disclosure Scheme, mandatory information-sharing between police, health and social care, strengthened multi-agency protocols and new powers for professionals to raise alerts.
