One of County Durham's oldest social clubs has undergone a dramatic transformation after being saved from closure.

Shildon Social Club, formerly Old Shildon Working Men's club, reopened its doors on Shildon's Main Street in September, after months of renovations from owners Newday Group.

The club was facing financial difficulty towards the end of 2025, secretary Phillip Blackett said, with staff fearing that the venue would be forced to close permanently.

The refurbished social club is now attracting new customers while keeping its community spirit which has lasted generations. The building is believed to be at least 130 years old, although Mr Blackett says no one is sure exactly when it was built.

Mr Blackett said the club had been popular with Shildon residents for decades, but its declining condition had left the historic venue facing closure.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21625855.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Secretary Philip Blackett said he is still "in disbelief" that the club is still running. *(Image: Northern Echo)*

## ‘We were just stuck in a hole’

Mr Blackett, 70, said he and his friends have been involved with the club since they were teenagers, making permanent closure devastating to contemplate.

He said: “By late last year, we were just stuck in a hole. We couldn't get out of it. The roof was absolutely pouring, and the ladies' toilets needed work.

"We were constantly patching things up just to keep going.”

The club had a number of functions booked, he added, which meant repairs had to work around day-to-day operations.

Mr Blackett said he had warned friends and staff that the club was unlikely to survive before inviting the community to discuss the club's future at a general meeting in January.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21625879.jpg?type=mds-article-620)There is some debate about the exact date of construction, but it is agreed that Old Shildon Working Men's Club is at least 130 years old. *(Image: Northern Echo)*

"It wasn't just us," Mr Blackett p said. "What I felt for, more than anything else, was the boxing club upstairs.

"If we had to close... it was just wrenching at me thinking of that, and then the staff."

A solution arrived when the club’s fruit-machine engineer suggested contacting Newday Group director John Taylor.

Newday Group owns and runs several pubs and social clubs across the North East.

Mr Blackett explained that after feeling "totally fed up," he decided to make the call to Mr Taylor in January.

Mr Taylor returned his call within an hour, offered to inspect the Shildon club with his son, and later attended the January meeting to discuss taking it over.

Newday Group took over the club and began renovations that same month.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21625838.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Before and after: Shildon Social Club's seating area *(Image: Philip Blackett / Northern Echo)*

The venue stayed open for as long as was possible, but closed temporarily in July while more work was carried out.

Mr Blackett said Taylor was "fantastic" and supported the club throughout the work. He added: "He was great with the staff, paid all the stuff, paid all the bills.

"He backed us all the way."

Most of the work was finished when the club reopened in September; furniture has since been added to the function room, while soundproofing continues.

The revamp brought a new bar, seating, beer garden and stage with live-performance equipment, plus pool tables, self-scoring dartboards and 75-inch televisions.

Walls were also removed to create a more open layout.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21625854.jpg?type=mds-article-620) *(Image: Northern Echo)*

The club now has a function room, snooker room and a darts room with Sunderland memorabilia displayed around the walls, including a signed boot from Jobe Bellingham.

It was renamed Shildon Social Club before reopening, a change Mr Blackett said he had wanted for years.

Before the July closure, John Taylor said: “We are really excited to refurbish the club for its members and wider community.

“The people of Shildon have supported the club for a very long time and it was the right time to refurbish it for generations to come.”

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21625849.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The renovations have brought a wave of new and returning customers to the club, Philip Blackett said. *(Image: Northern Echo)*

Mr Taylor also committed to improving the Old Shildon Boxing Club’s facilities upstairs.

## ‘The transformation was unbelievable’

Mr Blackett said: “What they've done is unbelievable. I'm absolutely over the moon with what they've done.”

Bar staff member Caitlyn Mulley has worked at the social club since before the renovations. She said she was nervous about losing her job when closure appeared possible, but that it was "worth it in the end".

“The transformation was unbelievable," Caitlyn added, "it was very grey before, I like that there's more colour.

"And there's more people coming in which keeps me busy!”

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21625837.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Before and after: Shildon Social Club's Bar *(Image: Philip Blackett / Northern Echo)*

The redevelopment opened Shildon Social Club to new customers who had not visited before, Mr Blackett said, but the changes had not removed the club’s identity.

He said the club’s "spirit is very much preserved", with new and returning customers enjoying the atmosphere.

Amid widespread social-club closures across the North East, Mr Blackett said he was "so proud" Shildon’s club had been saved for the wider community as well as himself.
