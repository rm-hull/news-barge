Three business centres in Sunderland are being targeted for further growth under plans to attract more businesses, support expansion and create high-quality jobs for residents.

Sunderland City Council is working with the North East Business & Innovation Centre (BIC) to unlock the potential of three business hubs: Evolve, Washington and Sunderland Software Centre.

Councillor James Jackson, cabinet member for business, housing and regeneration, said: "Our ambition is to create an economy where businesses can start, grow and succeed, creating high-quality jobs and opportunities for people across Sunderland.

"Our business centres have an important role to play in that.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21687837.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Sunderland Software Centre in Sunderland city centre *(Image: Supplied)*

"They're important assets for the city, providing the space and environment for businesses to establish themselves, innovate and expand.

"We want to make sure we're maximising their potential, both in terms of attracting businesses into the space available and supporting those businesses to flourish.

"The BIC have an exceptional track record in supporting enterprise and innovation.

"Their expertise, networks and experience will help us take these centres to the next level, maximising their economic impact in the most cost-effective way."

The council appointed the BIC to run the centres, attract new tenants, support growing firms and turn them into stronger business and jobs hubs.

The three sites are home to around 83 businesses supporting 456 jobs.

The BIC’s contract to manage the centres began in May after a competitive tender process.

Since taking over, the BIC has tightened administration and finances, drawn up plans to improve digital connectivity and started upgrading meeting rooms.

The BIC has also refreshed marketing to raise the centres’ profile.

Evolve Business Centre at Rainton Bridge is 87 per cent occupied, compared with 74 per cent at Washington Business Centre in Turbine Business Park.

Sunderland Software Centre has the most room for new tenants.

Several businesses have moved in or expanded across the three sites over the past year.

Technology consultancy Plexus Innovation moved into Evolve in March.

Specialist communications agency Ignifi moved its 25-strong team to Sunderland Software Centre in August.

The Software Centre also welcomed branding agency Mondays Design and Civia Health, which opened its flagship clinical research and community health facility in February.

At Washington Business Centre, Hartech Automation has taken more space, while STN Power Systems has announced jobs and launched new ventures.

Mr Jackson said the partnership with the BIC was about more than filling offices.

He said: "It's also about what happens within them.

"We want to see businesses starting up, scaling up, investing and creating jobs for local people, and we're determined to make sure these centres deliver the greatest possible benefit for Sunderland's economy."

The BIC is drawing up plans to promote the centres’ flexible workspace and link tenants to wider support through its business network.

The organisation has more than 30 years’ experience supporting start-ups, innovation and growth.

In 32 years, the BIC says it has helped more than 7,000 people start businesses and supported more than 1,000 companies to innovate and grow.

BIC chief executive Brett Griffiths said the organisation could build on the foundations laid during its first six months running the centres.

He said: "These are three landmark assets for Sunderland, and our shared ambition with the council is to attract more businesses, help existing tenants grow and make these centres places where businesses really thrive."
