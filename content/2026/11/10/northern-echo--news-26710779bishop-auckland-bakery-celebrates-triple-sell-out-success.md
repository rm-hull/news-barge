A Bishop Auckland bakery that began life in a home kitchen has celebrated a major milestone after selling out at three locations across County Durham in a single day.

Village Cakery, owned by Andy Hope and Bradley Skeen, sold out at its Fore Bondgate shop and pop-ups at the Manor House Hotel and Harewood House.

In a post, the bakery said: “We've sold out at all three locations today.

“The shop, Manor House Hotel and Harewood House.

“What a day. A massive thank you to everyone who came out to support us, and the fabulous organisers at Little Bird Markets and Gather North Farmers & Indie Markets.”

The success marks another chapter in the rapid growth of the business, which has become a familiar name at markets and events across the North East.

Village Cakery was originally run from the owners' Cockfield home, where they built a loyal customer base through celebration cakes, brownies, cookies and other sweet treats.

The business soon became a regular fixture at farmers' markets and artisan events, and in April 2024, Andy and Bradley opened their first permanent premises on Fore Bondgate, describing it as a dream come true.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21687832.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Selection of blondies and traybakes on display at Village Cakery, the Bishop Auckland bakery that recently sold out across three County Durham locations. *(Image: Sarah Caldecott)*

The pair, who are both Bishop Auckland born and bred, said they had always hoped to establish a shop in the town and help bring more independent businesses to the high street.

Since opening, the bakery has combined life as a town centre shop with an increasingly busy programme of pop-up events, markets and fairs across the region.

The business has also extended its opening hours, with the Fore Bondgate shop now open from 11am until 8pm, Wednesday to Saturday, reflecting growing demand from customers.

The latest triple sell-out suggests that demand shows little sign of slowing down as the business heads into the festive season, traditionally one of the busiest periods of the year for independent food and drink traders.
