A LOCAL MP has called on the Government to take tougher action to tackle the illegal use of nitrous oxide after volunteers in Ilkley reported finding large numbers of discarded canisters across the town and surrounding area.

Keighley and Ilkley MP Robbie Moore has urged ministers to make nitrous oxide canisters harder to obtain, despite recreational use and personal possession being outlawed in 2023 when the substance was reclassified as a Class C drug under the Misuse of Drugs Act 1971.

Nitrous oxide, sometimes known as "NOS" or "laughing gas", has a number of legitimate uses in healthcare, dentistry and the catering industry. However, before the ban came into force, it was the third most used recreational drug among 16 to 24-year-olds in England.

Speaking in the House of Commons, Mr Moore highlighted concerns raised by members of volunteer litter-picking group Litter Free Ilkley, who say they continue to find significant numbers of nitrous oxide cylinders despite the ban.

![](https://www.ilkleygazette.co.uk/resources/images/21692939.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Canisters of nitrous oxide collected by volunteer litter pickers in the local area *(Image: submitted)*

The MP raised concerns about both the environmental impact of discarded canisters and what he described as the growing road safety risks associated with their use.

Mr Moore said: "The evidence is clear. In Keighley and Ilkley and right across the country, nitrous oxide canisters are causing dangerous driving on our roads, putting all road users at risk and are a blight on our streets.

"Innocent people across the United Kingdom have already lost their lives in accidents because of a driver who had used nitrous oxide. I am proud I was a member of the government which made its recreational use illegal, but now this government must go further and make it harder for these canisters to fall into the wrong hands.

"Without tough action, more people will be killed or seriously injured due to the use of nitrous oxide and I am not willing to stand by and let it happen."

![](https://www.ilkleygazette.co.uk/resources/images/21692940.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Canisters of nitrous oxide collected by volunteer litter pickers in the local area *(Image: submitted)*

Mr Moore said nitrous oxide poses a particular challenge for police because it can be difficult to trace in the body, making prosecutions harder even when there is evidence suggesting someone has driven under its influence.

He called on Leader of the House Sir Alan Campbell MP to allow time for a parliamentary debate into the dangers posed by nitrous oxide and to examine what further measures could be taken to restrict access to the canisters.

The MP also praised the work of Litter Free Ilkley and thanked volunteer Moo Ping Vivienne Boey for drawing attention to the issue.

"I want to thank Litter Free Ilkley for all of their vital work around Ilkley and the surrounding areas, and member of the group Moo Ping Vivienne Boey for raising their work with me," he said.

"As residents will know, we have an ongoing problem with dangerous driving in Keighley and Ilkley. I regularly raise residents' concerns with the senior police officers and will continue to do so, and I urge anyone who witnesses or knows someone driving intoxicated to report it."

![](https://www.ilkleygazette.co.uk/resources/images/21692985.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Moo Ping Vivienne Boey, of Litter Free Ilkley, who raised concerns about discarded nitrous oxide canisters with Ilkley and Keighley MP Robbie Moore *(Image: submitted)*

Moo Ping Vivienne Boey said Ilkley volunteers had become increasingly concerned by both the scale of nitrous oxide littering and what it may indicate about ongoing misuse in the area.

She said: "Litter Free Ilkley volunteers have found cylinders in areas where cars can remain unattended for long periods, as well as along paths, among bushes and vegetation.

"Common locations include Moor Road and its lay-bys, Cow & Calf car park, the Suspension Bridge along the river and Denton Road.

"Our concerns extend well beyond littering to the evidence of serious nitrous oxide misuse in and around Ilkley, particularly the potential danger posed by individuals driving under its influence.

"We have been finding nitrous oxide cylinders during litter picks for several years, but this summer has been the worst, particularly at the height of summer around the Suspension Bridge.

"We have become increasingly concerned that these discarded nitrous oxide cylinders represent more than just a nuisance for litter pickers. The repercussions of people inhaling the gas pose a genuine road safety risk in our area, particularly if individuals drive under its influence.

"Without meaningful action, the problem will continue to grow, creating unnecessary risks to public safety, environmental quality and community wellbeing."

She said the group was encouraged by the response from Mr Moore and welcomed his commitment to continue pressing the issue in Parliament.

She said: "We are delighted that Robbie Moore MP has taken this issue on board and presented it to Parliament. He has confirmed his intention to pursue it through an adjournment debate, following a recommendation from the Leader of the House of Commons. This will help ensure the issue receives the parliamentary attention it deserves."

Figures gathered by Litter Free Ilkley show volunteers recorded 153 nitrous oxide cylinders across their wider collection area during September, including around 53 found in Ilkley, with 10 of those recovered in Menston.

Feedback from other litter-picking groups suggests the issue extends well beyond Ilkley.

Jeffery Yates, from Baildon Litter Free, said: "I think we must collect 50-60 a month, mainly in lay-bys and places where cars can pull in along the outer roads. The airport viewing area is a particular hotspot.

"We also find them in 'hidden' car parks where offenders can use them without fear of scrutiny. Menston High Royds car park springs to mind."

Meanwhile, Sandra McLean, of Litter Free Leeds, said more than 2,500 nitrous oxide canisters had been collected across Leeds since the beginning of the year.

Mr Moore now hopes to keep pressure on ministers through Parliament, arguing tougher restrictions are needed to reduce misuse, improve road safety and prevent further harm to communities across Keighley, Ilkley and beyond.
