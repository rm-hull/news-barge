David Walliams has been accused of sexually inappropriate behaviour by women, including two who claim to have been “groomed” by the comedian when they were 15.

The former *Britain’s Got Talent* judge allegedly used his celebrity status to prey on young girls.

One woman claims she met Walliams aged 15 and was having sex with with him by the age of 17. She said: “I think I was groomed. 100 per cent.”

Walliams has been revealed as the celebrity who sought legal action to prevent publication of allegations against him.

The 55-year-old had sought an injunction against *The Guardian* and ITV News because of a risk he would take his own life, after two previous suicide attempts.

The organisations have now published allegations, after the appeal deadline passed. Walliams has denied any wrongdoing.

Writing to the investigation’s journalists, Walliams wrote: “Looking back, I realise that at times I behaved thoughtlessly towards other people I had casual sexual relationships with. I realise I did not always take people’s feelings properly into account. That is something I regret.

“And whilst a power imbalance is inevitable when one party is famous, abusing that power is something else entirely and it is something that I never did.”

“I am not a sexual predator. I have not broken the law, but I am devastated that some of the women I met 10 to 20 years ago have now expressed hurt or anger towards me.”

## HarperCollins staff were not allowed go to Walliams’ house alone, former employee claims

## Walliams ‘told woman she was a good friend for not speaking to media about him’

David Walliams told one of the women who made allegations against him that she had been a “good friend” for not speaking to the media, it has been claimed.

Former model Lilly Creightmore accused Walliams of grooming her when she was a young adult and pursuing her “at a vulnerable age and place in my life, for sexual gratification”.

She told ITV: “I do remember him once messaging me, ‘Oh, you’ve been such a good friend, you’ve never gone to the papers about me’.

“So he obviously knew what he was doing was inappropriate.”

Ms Creightmore told *The Guardian*: “Yeah, I was a young adult, but I was a vulnerable young adult. [He was] making me feel special, plying me with gifts and then sort of disregarding me when he wasn’t getting what he wanted.”

Lawyers for Walliams say he does not remember messaging Ms Creightmore and denies grooming her or behaving inappropriately. They say that he did not target her because she was vulnerable or young, nor did he manipulate or coerce her into sleeping with him.

They say the gifts and offers to help her came with no expectations. They say he had understood their friendship, which lasted for about five years, to be special.

## Government is 'concerned' over use of injunctions, minister says

Justice minister Alex Davies-Jones said the Government is “concerned” about the use of injunctions and is “looking at what more” it can do.

Responding to shadow justice secretary Nick Timothy in the Commons, she said: “I want to reassure him that this Government is determined to do all we can to ensure that anyone who is a perpetrator of these kinds is held to the full force of the law.

“That includes looking at what we can do regarding injunctions.

“He will know that applications for privacy injunctions are considered independently (by) the courts, and it would not be appropriate for the Government to comment.

“However, we are concerned about the use of these, and the minister for courts is looking at what more we can do with Slapps (strategic lawsuits against public participation), injunctions.

“He will know we have already taken action on NDAs for sexual abuse cases, and what more we can do to prevent abuse of process throughout the judicial system.”

Mr Timothy had asked about the use of “abuses of process”, including abuse of injunctions and superinjunctions “which stop scrutiny, accountability, and the work of the free press?”
