Sir Julian Smith, MP for Skipton and Ripon, backs a Conservative plan to save £36 billion on welfare, with the two-child cap restored. Updated November 2026.

Sir Julian Smith, the Conservative MP for Skipton and Ripon, has backed his party’s plan to save £36 billion on the welfare bill, saying “work should always pay” and that taxpayers need confidence their money is spent responsibly.

The plan, published by the Conservative Party on Tuesday 10 November, sets out the changes the party says it would make in government: tighter rules on sickness benefits, an end to benefits for foreign nationals, the return of the two-child benefit cap and lower Universal Credit for people out of work for more than six months who have not worked before. It is an opposition proposal and would only take effect if the Conservatives won the next general election.

## What Sir Julian said

In a statement issued the same day, Sir Julian said the proposals would target spending at the people who need it most while tackling fraud and long-term unemployment.

“A strong welfare system should always protect the most vulnerable, but it must also be fair to taxpayers and encourage those who can work to do so,” he said.

“These proposals would help more people into employment, tackle fraud and abuse, and ensure support is focused on those who need it most.

“At a time when welfare spending continues to rise, it is right that we have a serious plan to make the system fairer, more sustainable and better focused on helping people achieve independence through work.

“Work should always pay, and taxpayers should have confidence that public money is being spent responsibly.”

## Where the party says the savings come from

The party says Britain spends £183 billion on working-age welfare. Its plan, set out in speeches by party leader Kemi Badenoch and Shadow Work and Pensions Secretary Helen Whately, puts a figure on each change:

* **Sickness benefits, £13.7 billion:** people would no longer be able to claim for mild mental health conditions or routine back and joint pain.
* **Foreign nationals, £9.5 billion:** no benefits or social housing for foreign nationals with indefinite or limited leave to remain. The party says this would free up 230,000 social homes.
* **Housing benefit reform, £4.5 billion.**
* **Two-child benefit cap restored, £3.1 billion.**
* **A tougher household benefit cap, an end to the VAT exemption on non-adapted Motability cars, and a fraud crackdown, £3 billion between them.**
* **Child Disability Living Allowance, £2.8 billion:** a move to what the party calls a support-based model.
* **Long-term unemployment, £0.5 billion.**

Together those add up to £36.1 billion, the party says.

## Universal Credit after six months out of work

The biggest change for people already claiming is to Universal Credit. Under the plan, people who have worked and paid in would keep getting it as now. Those the party describes as having “chosen not to work” would see their payments fall by a third after six months, and the money would be paid onto a “Back to Work” card that cannot be used for alcohol, cigarettes, gambling or cash.

The party says more than a million people have been unemployed and claiming Universal Credit for over six months.

## Young people and jobs

For under-25s, the party says it would halve what it calls Labour’s “Jobs Tax” and scrap large parts of the Employment Rights Act, to bring back Saturday and summer holiday jobs. It says there are almost a million young people not in education, employment or training.

On disability benefits, the party says the number of people claiming Personal Independence Payment has doubled since 2019 to 4 million, and that people with milder conditions would get treatment and support in place of cash.

The Conservative Party’s full welfare plan is on its website. Sir Julian’s own website carries his statements as MP for Skipton and Ripon, the seat that takes in Ripon and Nidderdale.
