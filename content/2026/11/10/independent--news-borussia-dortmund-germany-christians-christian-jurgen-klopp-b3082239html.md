Germany and Borussia Dortmund midfielder Felix Nmecha has responded to criticism of goal celebrations linked to an evangelical network by saying they’ve been misunderstood and are not meant to promote “intolerance, discrimination, or hate in any form.”

Nmecha, a devout Christian, was criticized by the Dortmund official supporters’ group at the weekend for celebrating goals with a gesture signifying the “The King’s Return” campaign linked to “Ballers in God,” a network of Christian soccer players.

Nmecha previously faced allegations of sharing homophobic and transphobic social media posts.

“My celebration is not intended to promote myself or any organization,” the player wrote on Tuesday on Instagram. “Rather, it is about laying my ‘crown’ before the soon coming King and pointing to Jesus, my hope and strength! My message has never been about promoting intolerance, discrimination, or hate in any form.”

Nmecha added: “As Christians, our message is one of love and respect for everyone, regardless of where they come from, what they believe, or what their worldview may be.”

The Dortmund fans’ group referenced research by German broadcaster ARD that accuses the “Ballers in God” network of labeling homosexuality as a sin and abortion as part a plan by Satan.

“We oppose religious networks that, under the guise of faith, propagate a worldview in which individuals are denigrated or excluded based on their sexual orientation, gender identity, way of life, or personal choices,” the supporters said in a statement.

Dortmund said on Sunday that it understood the fans’ concerns and would speak to the player.

News agency DPA reported that Germany coach Jürgen Klopp has already spoken to Nmecha about the issue at the national team camp in Herzogenaurach, Bavaria, before upcoming Nations League games.
