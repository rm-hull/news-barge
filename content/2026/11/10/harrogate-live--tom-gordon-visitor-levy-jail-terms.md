Harrogate MP Tom Gordon asked the Justice Secretary three questions on 14-year jail terms for tourist tax evasion and got no answer. Updated November 2026.

Harrogate and Knaresborough MP Tom Gordon asked the Justice Secretary three questions in the Commons on Tuesday 10 November about the prison places needed for a new offence of deliberately evading the tourist tax, which carries up to 14 years in jail, and none of them was answered.

The offence is part of the Visitor Levy (England) Bill, which had its first reading in the Commons last week. The Bill would let mayors and strategic authorities charge a levy on overnight stays, and the Labour Mayor of York and North Yorkshire, David Skaith, has welcomed it.

## The question and the answer

Mr Gordon, the Liberal Democrat MP, put his question at Justice Questions as topical question T7. Hansard records it as:

“Last week the Government announced their Visitor Levy (England) Bill, which will create a number of new offences, including a 14-year maximum prison sentence for deliberate evasion of a tourist tax, all while the Government are still releasing prisoners early. Has the Department carried out a justice impact test, and when will the impact assessments be published? What assessment has the Department made of the additional prison spaces that would be needed?”

The reply, from the Lord Chancellor and Justice Secretary, Alex Norris, did not deal with the impact test, the publication date or the number of prison places:

“The hon. Gentleman will not be surprised to hear that when any decision is taken across Government, it is done in the round and we have those conversations with our colleagues in other Departments. Giving local leaders the power to make that decision, if they want it, is important and, of course, if people do not follow it, there ought to be sanctions.”

## “Labour needs to get its priorities in order”

Mr Gordon set the 14-year maximum against the state of the prisons.

“650 more offenders were released early today because our prisons are full,” he said. “There are fewer than 2,100 spare places left, and Refuge has warned that domestic abuse perpetrators are among those walking free.

“I asked the Justice Secretary three simple questions: has his department tested the impact, when will the assessments be published, and how many prison places will this need? He couldn’t answer any of them.

“Harrogate and Knaresborough’s hotels and B&Bs are already under huge pressure. Labour needs to get its priorities in order and publish the impact assessment before this Bill goes any further.”

His figures come from BBC reporting, his office says: 650 prisoners released on 10 November in the second phase of the early release scheme, 86,982 people in prison and room for 2,076 more.

## What the Government says about jail

The Ministry of Housing, Communities and Local Government, which is behind the Bill, has said that prison is a last resort. In a post on its media blog on 5 November, it called reports that accommodation providers face 14 years in jail for not paying “misleading”.

“The fact is, no one will go to prison for an honest mistake or a minor breach like an occasional error when collecting the levy,” the department said. “Just as is the case for other charges, like VAT, prison would only be considered in the most serious cases, including where the levy has been deliberately evaded.” Sentencing, it added, is always a matter for the courts, and only in serious cases could money be recovered over a period as long as 20 years.

Mr Gordon’s office points to the same post as the department’s confirmation of the 14-year maximum for deliberate evasion.

## Two local MPs against the levy

Mr Gordon is the second MP covering this area to challenge the Bill in a week. Sir Julian Smith, the Conservative MP for Skipton and Ripon, called the overnight levy “a mad plan” on 5 November and said it would be an uncapped tax on rural hospitality.

## What it means for you

Nothing changes yet for visitors or for anyone running a hotel, guest house or B&B in Harrogate, Knaresborough or Ripon. The Bill still has to pass through Parliament. After that, the department says, mayors and local leaders will consult businesses and residents and could set out their plans by March 2028 if they want a levy. That local consultation is where accommodation owners will get their say on whether North Yorkshire charges one and at what rate.

The jail term applies only to deliberate evasion, on the Government’s account; an honest mistake in collecting the levy would not lead to prison. The progress of the Bill can be followed on the Parliament Bill page. Residents can contact Mr Gordon through his website or at his constituency office at Spa Lane Studio, 1A Spa Lane, Harrogate HG2 7JF.
