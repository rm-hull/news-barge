The Scottish Government has been urged to deliver a £100 million funding increase for universities in its forthcoming budget, The Herald can exclusively reveal.

Universities Scotland, which represents the sector, has made a submission to the government outlining the case for higher levels of resource and capital funding. It says that it has limited its requests to “essential measures needed to lessen the financial pressure on universities over the coming year.”

The call to increase funding comes a few months after the release of a report highlighting an annual £200m funding gap for Scotland’s universities in 2023/24. Changes since that time, especially the reduction in foreign students, suggest that the gap will have widened.

In 2024, The Herald revealed that publicly-funded tuition fees cover just 58% of university’s course teaching costs on average.

In its recent submission to the government, which The Herald understands was provided to ministers in October, Universities Scotland has now called for a 5% real terms increase in resource funding, which covers day to day operations and the costs involved in teaching.

This would result in a settlement of £876.2m for the university sector in Scotland, a boost of almost £58m compared to the previous year.

Universities Scotland believes that the increase is necessary to address a “multiyear funding gap” that has continued to grow despite widely-publicised moves to cut staff from a number of institutions. Several universities are currently facing industrial action campaigns in response.

The report states: “We cannot continue delivering more for less, nor can we cut our way to sustainability. Years of real terms decline in per student funding undermine our offer to learners, and put Scotland’s international competitiveness at risk.”

The government is also warned that the increase is needed in order to protect “teaching excellence”, and that “publicly-funded university tuition has been under-resourced since at least 2016/17.”

In addition increased resource funds, the government has been asked to provide a total capital budget of just over £420m, which would represent a 9% real terms funding improvement and would help to protect universities’ ability to engage in world-leading research.

Ministers are also urged to establish a new University Capital Loans Scheme in response to “years of underfunding” which have “constrained universities’ ability to invest in their estates and other essential infrastructure.”

The report notes: “Listed buildings, dispersed campuses, unforeseen issues such as RAAC and cladding, and a host of other challenges have stoked an estates maintenance backlog that now exceeds £1 billion. Modern, attractive, properly equipped facilities are critical to sustaining an effective, competitive, and high quality offer to students and staff, but are increasingly difficult to achieve.”

Arguing for the establishment of the new capital fund, Universities Scotland says that its members should be seen as “a trusted partner, offering reliable, fully costed opportunities to realise tangible returns from repayable Government investment.”

Finally, the Universities Scotland report calls on the Scottish Government to create a University Transformation Fund, a commitment to which “would be an important first step in supporting and empowering the sector to act, and start delivering effective change.”

Ultimately, the report notes that the requested funding changes will not completely fill existing financial gaps.

Universities Scotland says that it is presenting “a case for national investment”, and that while a single budget cycle cannot solve every funding problem facing the sector, the government should use the opportunity to avoid exacerbating the issues that are currently harming Scottish higher education.

Writing exclusively for The Herald, Professor James Miller, the Convener of Universities Scotland, highlighted that “costs have risen” and “expectations have increased”, but that “investment has failed to keep pace.” He described recent joint work between the government and universities as a “significant breakthrough”, and argued that with the scale of the financial challenges affecting the sector now clear, the question to be answered is “whether budget decisions will follow evidence.”

Professor Miller also told The Herald:

“The Scottish Government has already accepted that a significant university funding gap exists. The question now is whether budget decisions will follow evidence. The December budget cannot solve every problem. It can however avoid making a known problem worse.

“Allowing the funding gap to grow would not simply create difficulties for universities. It would make the eventual solutions more expensive, more disruptive and more difficult to deliver. This is not a sector pleading for special treatment. It is a case for national investment.”

Commenting on behalf of the Scottish Government, Tertiary Education Minister Alyn Smith MSP said:

“We are committed to supporting our world-leading universities, and in 2026-27 our investment in universities will exceed £1.4 billion including an increase of over £55 million, or 5%, in resource and capital funding.

“As part of the Scottish Government’s 2027-28 budget process, we will continue to engage with Universities Scotland.”

The government subsequently confirmed that the 5% increase will be in cash terms rather than real terms.
