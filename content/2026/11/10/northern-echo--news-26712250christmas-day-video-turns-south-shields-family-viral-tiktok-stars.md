A North East family who turned a Christmas Day Tiktok into one of the North East's biggest social media success stories say they are determined not to let online fame change who they are.

The Williams family, from South Shields, have amassed more than 700,000 followers and 56 million TikTok likes by sharing everyday life, but never planned to become social media stars.

Vicky Williams, 38, said the family was thrust into the spotlight when a viral video of her daughter Miley began attracting millions of views on Christmas Day 2024.

After 13-year-old Miley broke her iPad, her parents, Vicky and Stephen, repeatedly told her they would not replace it.

Miley's parents ultimately decided to surprise her with an iPad for Christmas, and record her reaction when she opened it.

Vicky said the family was “overwhelmed” when the clip first went viral, topping 28 million views and two million likes since being posted.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21690556.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Vicky Williams said the family was thrust into the spotlight when a viral video of her daughter Miley began attracting millions of views on Christmas Day 2024. *(Image: @thewilliamsfamily168)*

Other posts from their account soon began gaining attention at a similar speed, with Miley among the first to realise what was happening.

Mrs Williams said Miley had been interested in social media since she was eight or nine and excitedly showed her parents the rising figures as her dream of internet stardom took shape.

Mrs Williams said: “It just happened overnight.”

“The videos were just flying. It was absolutely mental.”

## When online fame became real life

Since the family's original viral video, the account has grown into the Williams family documenting their everyday life, along with fashion content and trending dances from Miley, who is turning 15 this month.

Mrs Williams said the account’s global reach was “insane”, with fans tuning in to follow the family’s lives.

She added: "We're just an ordinary family from South Shields, and everyone's obsessed with my daughter Miley.”

Fame quickly moved beyond social media and into everyday life, Mrs Williams said.

Trips to the shops, family days out and visits to local events regularly result in Miley being stopped for photographs or conversations with followers.

Even Mrs Williams has become recognisable in her own right, often hearing herself referred to as "the Williams mam" by strangers who have followed the family's journey online.

The growing audience also attracted companies keen to work with Miley, which Mrs Williams said was “confusing” at first.

She said: "People were messaging us saying, 'We've seen your daughter's video. Do you want to work with her?'

"And I was thinking, what are you on about?"

Miley has received gifts and PR packages from brands like Shein, and recently hosted a New Look event at the Metrocentre, which Mrs Williams said she "loved," as it offered a chance for her daughter to translate her social media success into partnership opportunities.

Mrs Williams believes Miley inspires other children by giving them a behind-the-scenes look at life as a teenage influencer.

## Keeping her feet on the ground

As Miley's popularity has grown, her parents have become increasingly conscious of the pressures that can come with social media fame.

Mrs Williams said that while her daughter is mostly used to the nature of the internet, hurtful comments can still affect her self-esteem.

Miley's parents manage both the family's account and Miley's individual profile, keeping an eye on messages and dealing with negative comments on her behalf. Mrs Williams said their priority is ensuring Miley enjoys creating content without being exposed to the more toxic side of life online.

Behind her social media stardom and goals of reaching one million followers, Miley remains a school pupil with a life away from the camera.

Mrs Williams describes Miley as "down-to-earth" and says material things do little to impress her. Instead, she enjoys spending time with friends and family and remains focused on goals away from social media like cheerleading and boxing.

Mrs Williams said Miley wants to become a midwife and sees social media as just one part of her future.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21690521.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Mrs Williams said Miley wants to become a midwife and sees social media as just one part of her future. *(Image: Vicky Williams)*

She aims to study midwifery at university while making videos on the side, if she still wants to.

Mrs Williams said the family would support her whatever she chose, adding: “She knows what she wants in life now.

"I'll support her with anything she wants to do.”

What makes the family proudest, however, is not Miley's brand opportunities or follower count, but her commitment to giving back.

Despite the gifts and opportunities her profile brings, Miley regularly donates clothes, toys and other items to charity instead of keeping them for herself.

Vicky describes Miley as having “the biggest heart” and says her passion for local causes matters far more than followers or viral success.

That desire to remain grounded is linked closely to where the family comes from.

Mrs Williams said staying rooted in South Shields has always mattered to them, even though much of the family’s audience lives elsewhere.

The family hope their videos show the positive side of life in the North East and challenge misconceptions about the area, rather than changing their content to win a different audience.

Mrs Williams said they wanted to "show that Newcastle and the North East isn't a bad place because it's not.”

She added: “We've got lovely people around where we live.”

The family’s rise has also given them a sense of responsibility as representatives of the area.

Mrs Williams said: “It is a massive thing that we are probably the only family in the North East that's getting big.”

While millions of people may now watch snippets of their lives online, the Williams family remain proud of their South Shields roots and determined to use their platform to showcase the North East in a positive light.

As viral South Shields teenager Miley looks towards adulthood, university and life beyond social media, her family are determined that the values which first shaped her will matter far more than viral fame ever could.
