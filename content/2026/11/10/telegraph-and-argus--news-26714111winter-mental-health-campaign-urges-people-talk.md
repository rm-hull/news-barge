A NEW mental health campaign is urging people to start talking this winter.

Launched across Bradford District and Craven, the "Hope begins with a conversation" campaign aims to encourage residents to look after their mental wellbeing and seek support early as the colder months approach.

The campaign highlights the challenges people may face during winter—such as shorter days, colder weather, financial pressures, loneliness, bereavement, caring responsibilities, and physical health concerns—and promotes Healthy Minds, a hub for local mental health services, advice, and self-help resources.

Dr Junaid Azam, a GP in Bradford, said: "Winter can be particularly challenging because many pressures can come together at the same time.

"People may be coping with loneliness, financial worries, grief, caring responsibilities, or concerns about their own health, while the shorter, darker days can also affect how we feel.

"It's important to remember that you don't need to face these challenges alone.

"Reaching out and talking to someone can make a real difference.

"Often, hope begins with a conversation, and help is available if you need it."

The campaign encourages people to recognise when they may need support and to reach out to a friend, family member, work colleague, or health professional as a first step.

It also suggests simple ways to support wellbeing during winter, such as taking time to pause, doing something creative, staying active, or checking in with someone they trust.

The campaign is built around four key themes:

* Take a moment, which encourages people to pause and rest when overwhelmed;
* Make something, which promotes creative activities to support mental wellbeing;
* Move your way, which encourages people to find enjoyable ways to stay active;
* Talk to someone, which highlights the importance of staying connected and reaching out.

Through these themes, Healthy Minds will share practical advice, resources, and wellbeing tips on its website and social media channels throughout the winter.

The campaign will also include information for parents, carers, and families to help children and young people care for their mental wellbeing during the colder months.

Residents are encouraged to check in on friends, family members, and neighbours who may be struggling, as even a brief conversation can make a difference.

Whatever challenges winter may bring, help is available. Hope begins with a conversation, and nobody needs to face difficult times alone.

For more information, visit www.healthyminds.services.
