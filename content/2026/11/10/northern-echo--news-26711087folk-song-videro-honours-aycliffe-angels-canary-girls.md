A North East folk singer has marked Remembrance Day by paying tribute to her grandmother and the women who risked their lives working in munitions factories during the two world wars.

Teesside musician, writer and poet Sara Dennis has revisited her a cappella folk song *Spend Your Money Quick!*, inspired by her grandmother Ada Frances Daniels, creating a new video incorporating historic footage.

Ada worked at the Royal Ordnance Factory in Aycliffe during the Second World War, making munitions.

Sara said: "My grandmother was one of these women.

"She travelled from Middlesbrough to work at Aycliffe, handling faulty detonators and undertaking work she knew could cost her life.

"I grew up knowing about her experiences, and they've stayed with me."

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21689200.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Baby Margaret Daniels (later Mrs Powdrill) here aged under one year, with her mother Ada (née Roberts) taken in 1944 *(Image: Supplied)*

Released in 2016, the song celebrates the women who worked in munitions factories during both world wars.

Sara has created a new video using archive footage of First World War munitions workers at Woolwich Royal Arsenal.

The video ends with a dedication to Ada and all the women who risked their lives in munitions factories: "You all deserve the medals you did not receive."

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21689201.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Ada is taken at Leven and Sara Dennis' mother Lynne Waller (nee Daniels) *(Image: Supplied)*

Ms Dennis said: "I wanted to write something that celebrated these women as people, not just as wartime heroines.

"They worked incredibly hard and faced extraordinary dangers, but they also wanted to enjoy themselves, earn their own money, buy nice things and have a good time.

"I wanted the song to reflect that spirit, with a little humour and irreverence."

Born in Middlesbrough in December 1916, Ada travelled to Aycliffe to identify and remove faulty detonators, one of the factory’s most dangerous jobs.

Ada believed the factory women faced dangers as serious as those confronting men serving overseas.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21689203.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Folk singer Sara Dennis *(Image: Supplied)*

She also narrowly avoided the bombing of Middlesbrough railway station in August 1942 because she had the day off.

The song links two generations of munitions workers: the First World War’s “Canary Girls”, whose skin turned yellow after exposure to explosives, and the Second World War’s “Aycliffe Angels”.

Sara drew inspiration from Madeline Ida Bedford’s First World War poem Munition Wages, about a working-class woman enjoying her independence while aware of the dangers of munitions work.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21689200.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Baby Margaret Daniels (later Mrs Powdrill) here aged under one year, with her mother Ada (née Roberts) taken in 1944 *(Image: Supplied)*

She said: "It's also important to remember that the women who worked in these factories didn't receive the same recognition as those serving in uniform.

"I wanted to give them a voice and acknowledge what they contributed."

Sara hopes the song will revive interest in the stories of wartime munitions workers.

She also wants to work with local historians, filmmakers, archives and heritage organisations to develop the project.

In addition to her music, Sara is a published poet and author.

Her works include The Blonde, An Appointment with Dr Kafka and Stories from the Memory Lane Café, a collection of memories from people with dementia.

The music video for Spend Your Money Quick! is on YouTube.
