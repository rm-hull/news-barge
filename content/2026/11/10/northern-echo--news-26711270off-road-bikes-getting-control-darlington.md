The misuse of off-road bikes and antisocial behaviour in Darlington is “getting out of control”, the council’s opposition leader has claimed.

Councillor Jonathan Dulston, leader of the Conservative opposition on Darlington Borough Council, has organised a public meeting about crime and antisocial behaviour in the town.

Darlington Conservatives said it had surveyed thousands of residents, with crime, antisocial behaviour and the illegal use of off-road bikes among the concerns raised.

The group is now organising a meeting where residents can raise their concerns directly with those responsible for tackling the problems at the Junior Unionist Club on Thursday, November 12.

Cllr Dulston said: “The misuse of off-road bikes and antisocial behaviour in Darlington is getting out of control.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/20306624.jpg?type=mds-article-620) *(Image: POLICE)*

“Residents are understandably frustrated and increasingly feel that those responsible are getting away with disrupting lives and damaging our communities.

“I would encourage residents to attend this meeting and make their voices heard.

“The police, the council and our Member of Parliament have all been invited so that these concerns can be put directly to them.”

It comes after Darlington Borough Council approved a new policy for dealing with antisocial behaviour around its more than 5,300 council homes.

The policy includes action against the misuse of motorbikes, quad bikes, e-scooters and mopeds. This includes dangerous riding and using vehicles to intimidate residents.

The council can take action ranging from warning letters and fines to court orders. In serious cases, council tenants could face action over their tenancy.

Darlington MP Lola McEvoy has written to government ministers about the town's anti-social problem and said: "Masked-up riders on illegal and unlicensed e-scooters and dirt bikes are causing havoc and it must stop.

"I’ve had enough of it."

Cllr Dulston added: “This is about accountability, but it is also about working together to develop a clear, practical plan that the public can have confidence in.

“Residents deserve to know what action will be taken, who will be responsible and how progress will be measured.”

The Chief Constable, Darlington MP Lola McEvoy, Police and Crime Commissioner Joy Allen, council leader Stephen Harker and shadow policing minister Matt Vickers MP have all been invited.
