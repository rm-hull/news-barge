Suella Braverman was paid £100,000 to be handcuffed to Katie Price in a new Channel 4 reality series.

*Celebrity Handcuffed* will force the Reform UK MP and the former glamour model to carry out daily tasks together while competing against other famous pairs.

According to the latest register of MPs’ interests, the former home secretary completed 200 hours of filming for production company Freemantle and agreed to deliver ongoing publicity.

The format expands on a civilian version launched in March, in which people with opposing views were chained together continuously for a £100,000 prize.

Among the other duos competing in the show are Love Island star turned campaigner Georgia Harrison and model Lady Victoria Hervey, while former footballer Neil Ruddock will be *handcuffed* to drag queen Bimini.

Rounding off the *celebrity*pairings are Made In Chelsea’s Mark-Francis Vandelli and former Atomic Kitten singer Kerry Katona, and* celebrity* DJ Fat Tony and Princess Olga Romanoff.

![Braverman will be handcuffed to Katie Price on the show](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/08/07/8/42/Screenshot-2026-08-07-at-09-52-10.png?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Jonathan Ross, who hosted the civilian version said: “I loved being part of the civilian series of *Handcuffed*, and I can’t wait to see how the* celebrity* version pans out.

“I’ve done my fair share of *celebrity* shows, but nothing quite like this.

It’s chaotic, it’s hilarious, and I’m so excited for viewers to see what happens when you handcuff a group of famous faces together and put them under pressure.”

Tim Hancock, commissioning editor at Channel 4, said: “*Celebrity* * Handcuffed* takes a format audiences couldn’t stop talking about and supercharges it with some truly unexpected pairings.

“Just like the civilian series, the *celebrity* version will be laugh-out-loud funny, gloriously awkward and full of surprises, and will capture something bigger about modern Britain: even when we seem to disagree on almost everything, we might have more in common than we think.”

The show is due to be released next year.

Last month, Mrs Braverman caused controversy after her comments on English identity.

A dispute over racism was triggered after Mrs Braverman said on the BBC’s *Question Time* programme that non-white people born in the UK could be British, but not English.

Tory leader Kemi Badenoch accused Mrs Braverman of practising "identity politics, where people keep putting other people into boxes".
