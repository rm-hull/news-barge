Four North East music acts have secured five-figure touring support through a new partnership aimed at helping the region's artists take their music on the road.

The artists, all from the Tees Valley, are among nine recipients of the UK Artist Touring Fund (UKAT) and will each receive up to £10,000 to support their touring ambitions.

The successful acts are former Tees Valley Artists of the Year Finn Forster and Amelia Coburn, folk duo Smith & Liddle, and Hartlepool indie band Marketplace, the current Artist of the Year.

Amelia Coburn, a singer-songwriter from Teesside, said: "Being awarded UKAT funding has been a genuine lifeline at this stage in my career.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21686985.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Marketplace *(Image: Credit Megan Wyrill)*

"Not only has it allowed me to take session musicians and crew on the road with me, including an indispensable tour manager who keeps everything running smoothly, but I can also pay the whole team (myself included) a proper wage.

"It’s also meant we can afford a hotel room rather than crashing on a mutual friend’s couch for the night.

"It’s these seemingly small comforts and additions to the touring party that can make the difference between touring being an exhausting financial strain and something genuinely enjoyable and sustainable."

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21686986.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Smith and Liddle *(Image: Supplied)*

The funding comes through a partnership between the Northern Music Export Office (NMEO)—supported by the Tees Valley Combined Authority—and the Featured Artists Coalition (FAC).

Since the partnership was unveiled in August, it has led to a significant increase in successful funding awards for Northern artists, rising from two in the first phase of UKAT funding to nine in the second.

The Northern Music Export Office, powered by Generator, aims to help northern artists build sustainable careers, reach new audiences, and grow their international potential.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21686987.jpg?type=mds-article-620) *(Image: Supplied)*

It works in collaboration with the Tees Valley, North East, and West Yorkshire Combined Authorities.

The region’s Artists of the Year programme has also contributed nearly £30,000 in financial backing, alongside business support to help emerging talent progress in the music industry.

Tees Valley Mayor Ben Houchen said: "Seeing we have four of the nine grant winners here shows just how much our region punches above its weight in the music industry.

"We want our talent to fly – and this funding and dedicated touring support will help these artists take their music to new audiences and build their live careers while meeting the growing costs of life on the road."

Mick Ross, chief executive at Generator and the Northern Music Export Office, described the awards as a "milestone achievement."

He said: "This is a milestone achievement for the Northern Music Export Office and a significant step towards delivering the ambition we set out to achieve when establishing the office.

"Seeing nine artists from the North East, Tees Valley and West Yorkshire benefit from UKAT funding through this co-investment is hugely positive.

"Through this initiative, we have been able to double the level of investment available through the Northern Music Export Office, alongside a substantial increase in the number of Northern artists successfully securing touring funding.

"This support will help more Northern artists reach national audiences, build their live careers and gain the experience, confidence, and opportunities they need to become export-ready."

Established earlier this year by the FAC, in partnership with the Music Managers Forum and Musicians' Union, UKAT was created to address the growing cost of touring crisis facing artists across the UK.
