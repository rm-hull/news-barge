A new border control facility has opened in the North East as part of continued investment aimed at expanding the region’s international cargo operations.

The government-approved Border Control Post at Newcastle Airport will inspect and clear flowers, plant products, fruit and vegetables on site, cutting delays and costs for businesses.

Worldwide Flight Services opened the facility to speed up clearance closer to goods’ final destinations.

Leon McQuaid, director of aviation development at Newcastle Airport, said: "The new Border Control Post marks another important milestone in the growth of Newcastle Airport's cargo services, strengthening our position as a gateway for international trade.

"We are grateful to Worldwide Flight Services for its investment in the facility, which will help businesses move goods more efficiently and provide a faster route to market while supporting economic growth and investment across North East England and beyond.

"The continued investment in our cargo infrastructure reflects the growing demand for our services and we welcome discussions with cargo and aviation-related businesses looking to expand their operations through the airport."

The Border Control Post is part of the airport’s wider plan to expand its cargo operations.

Earlier this year, executive jet centre and handling specialist Samson Aviation added a main deck loader and other ground equipment.

The equipment allows the airport and Samson’s team to handle more narrow-body and wide-body passenger, charter and cargo aircraft, plus heavy machinery and luxury vehicles.

Newcastle Airport handles 5,000 tonnes of freight a year and is one of the UK’s leading cargo gateways.

The airport operates 24 hours a day, offers expert handling and has direct access to the strategic road network.

More information about the airport’s cargo operations can be found at www.newcastleairport.com/corporate/doing-business-with-us/cargo-opportunities.
