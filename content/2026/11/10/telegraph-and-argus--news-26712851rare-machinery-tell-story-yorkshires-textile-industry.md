A NEW display is set to showcase textile machinery and shed fresh light on the region’s industrial past.

Historic equipment has been relocated from Bradford Industrial Museum to Calderdale Industrial Museum, where it will help tell the story of Yorkshire’s world-famous worsted cloth production as part of a major new display.

The relocation was made possible by an annual special award from the Worshipful Company of Tin Plate Workers alias Wire Workers, which funded the transport and installation of the machines.

Councillor Ian Walker, Bradford Council’s executive member for neighbourhoods and communities, said: "Bradford District Museums care for an outstanding collection that preserves the story of our district's industrial heritage.

"We are delighted that these machines have found a new home on display, where they can be enjoyed and understood by the public rather than remaining in storage.

"This loan demonstrates how museums can work together to share collections and bring Yorkshire's remarkable industrial history to life.

"It’s an excellent opportunity to ensure important objects from the district’s collection continue to educate and inspire while contributing to a broader regional story about the industry that shaped communities across West Yorkshire."

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21691429.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Tim Kirker Collections Director for Calderdale Industrial Museum Association and Elizabeth Bonfield Master of The Worshipful Company of Tin Plate Workers alias Wire Workers *(Image: Emma Dunn Calderdale Industrial Museum Association)*

The machinery, originally part of Bradford District Museums & Galleries’ nationally significant collection, includes four machines that form a drawing set.

These machines work in succession to straighten, blend, and draw out wool or cotton fibres before they are spun into thread.

They follow the Old French System and will complement the Bradford system already displayed in the museum’s Worsted Gallery.

The project also highlights the deep industrial connections between Bradford and Calderdale.

Bradford gained global recognition as the "worsted capital of the world," while Calderdale was home to generations of skilled textile workers and manufacturers who helped power the Industrial Revolution.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21691441.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The relocation was made possible by an annual special award from the Worshipful Company of Tin Plate Workers alias Wire Workers, which funded the transport and installation of the machines *(Image: Emma Dunn Calderdale Industrial Museum Association)*

Tim Kirker, collections director for Calderdale Industrial Museum Association, said: "I'd like to thank James Garside & Sons Ltd for their work on the relocation – using the same purpose-built lifting cradle that they made in 1985, when the museum was being built.

"By working together with our colleagues in Bradford, we're ensuring these important pieces of textile history can help tell a richer and more complete story of the industry that shaped communities across West Yorkshire."

Calderdale Industrial Museum’s collections and exhibitions teams will now assess the machinery, plan any necessary restoration, and develop interpretation materials for visitors.

It is hoped that the enhanced gallery will open in 2027 - a challenging target for CIMA’s volunteer workforce to achieve.
