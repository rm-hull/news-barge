A climbing wall at Barnard Castle's Teesdale Leisure Centre could reopen after campaigners secured fresh talks with Durham County Council over bringing the long-unused facility back into operation.

Hopes have been raised after discussions were reopened with senior council figures about allowing climbers back onto the wall on an initial trial basis.

Posting on Facebook, the campaign organiser, who says they have spent years lobbying the authority to reconsider the closure, revealed there may finally have been progress after "another year of pestering and pleading".

Any reopening would be subject to strict conditions.

The wall would need to be self-funding from the outset and demonstrate it can remain financially viable in the long term.

To meet those requirements, plans are being developed to establish a Community Interest Company (CIC) which would run the facility on a not-for-profit basis.

The organisation would aim to share responsibilities among volunteers and experienced climbers while building a sustainable future for the wall.

If approved, the CIC hopes to deliver coaching sessions for both children and adults, community climbing nights, as well as access for group bookings and parties.

The organiser has also launched a recruitment drive for people with climbing, coaching and climbing wall experience who may be willing to help deliver the project.

The prospect of the wall reopening is likely to be welcomed by the local climbing community, with supporters campaigning for years to see the facility brought back into use.

Durham County Council confirmed discussions are taking place but stressed there is still significant work to be done before any plans can move forward.

Deborah Holmes, Durham County Council's strategic manager of wellbeing, sport and leisure, said: "We are keen to see the reopening of the climbing wall and will be working closely with the campaign organiser to try and make this a reality.
