Dame Judi Dench recalled moments of childhood mischief as she remembered her formative days in York while on stage in the city.

The Oscar-winning actor received a standing ovation at York Theatre Royal on Sunday, where she appeared to talk about her new book, ‘Is It Too Late to Make a Run for it?’, with Brendan O’Hea.

York-born Dame Judi shared memories, acting tips and anecdotes during the evening.

She also praised her time at The Mount School in York.

![Standing ovation for Dame Judi Dench at York Theatre Royal](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/standing-ovation-for-dame-judi-dench-at-york-2.webp)

“I couldn’t be more grateful for my upbringing and belonging at The Mount,” she said.

“Being in York is so special. My entire time at The Mount was wonderful and how I feel about it never changes.”

She also recalled some moments of childhood mischief:

* “Once I threw a slipper down the boarding stairs railings with unlucky timing as it landed on the head’s tea tray as she was walking through the corridor. It was rather a good shot!”
* “Me and my friend from boarding once set off a fire extinguisher which caused a surprising amount of damage.”
* “The Mount wet walks (rainy walks) were not my favourite. With my friend Suzy we took coins for the phone box and mummy would collect us and take us home for tea, then douse us with the watering can so we looked suitably wet going back.”

Two years ago, when Dame Judi appeared at The Grand Opera House, the school gave her a talking teddy bear called George for her collection.

A message from the bear was voiced by Prep School pupil Arabella. On Sunday, Dame Judi told the audience that she with in the audience, and Arabella waved from the Dress Circle.

![Standing ovation for Dame Judi Dench at York Theatre Royal](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/standing-ovation-for-dame-judi-dench-at-york-scaled.webp)

Arabella, said, “I love being the voice of Dame Judi’s bear, George, and reading out her favourite poem, and it was great to hear her talk on stage and wave at me. I feel so lucky.”

Dame Judi also mentioned that the school had sent her a card with a video of Prep School pupils reciting The Owl & The Pussycat, and had planted a cherry blossom tree called Accolade in its grounds.

Anna Wilby, Head of The Mount, said: “We are so proud that Dame Judi attended The Mount, acted on our stage, and continues to inspire our pupils. Her fond words mean so much to us all.”

She said creative arts remain a priority, pointing to a new dance studio and productions of *Matilda*in the prep school and* Oliver* in senior school coming up soon.
