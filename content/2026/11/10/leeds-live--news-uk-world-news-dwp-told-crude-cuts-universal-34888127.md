# DWP told 'crude cuts' to Universal Credit and benefits unavoidable

## The Department for Work and Pensions has been warned "crude cuts" may be the only option under Andy Burnham

The Department for Work and Pensions has been cautioned that "crude cuts" could be the only viable option under Andy Burnham. Prime Minister Burnham and Chancellor John Healey have committed to reducing the welfare bill.

However, they have insisted there would be no "crude cuts" to welfare payments, reports Birmingham Live. Former Conservative Party adviser Adam Smith has warned this stance may prove untenable. He told the Telegraph: "The uncomfortable truth for Labour is that there can be no fundamental realignment of incentives away from welfare and towards work without some reduction in benefits."

Smith went on to say: "So restricting one element of universal credit will not be enough. Ministers also need to pull other levers."

Smith, who previously served as chief of staff to Tory Sir Jeremy Hunt, continued: "The reality is that Burnham and Healey cannot tackle the problem of young people out of work without fundamentally changing incentives.

"And they cannot change incentives while promising that no existing claimant will lose out. If they try to have both, we will be left with another expensive programme that helps a few while the system as a whole drives worklessness, and the benefits bill, ever upwards."

Providing an update on DWP cuts, Mr Burnham said last week: "I come at it a different way. I want to talk about how many people am I going to help into work?".

"If you go at it with a number like you've just said, I think this is exactly what I was saying before about politics done the wrong way. And the changes I'm trying to bring, they've done that before: 'Oh, we need to cut this amount'.

"And then straight away it's into a kind of a cuts agenda without really thinking through how do you help people from where they are to a better position." He went on to say: "If you come at it through that normal Westminster mindset of saying 'we must cut x amount from the system', well, it will fail again. And that's what we've seen in politics in recent times."
