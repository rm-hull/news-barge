# Former Canadian Minister of Foreign Affairs Stéphane Dion to Speak at Freie Universität Berlin

## Professor of political science to hold lecture titled “Canada and Europe: Strategic Partners Beyond the Trump Years” on November 18, 2026

№ 138/2026 from Nov 10, 2026

In what ways can Canada and Europe strengthen their strategic ties to implement a strong and effective long-term partnership, especially given the multiple ongoing global crises and the shadow cast by the Trump presidency? Professor Stéphane Dion will be addressing these questions in his lecture titled “Canada and Europe: Strategic Partners Beyond the Trump Years” at Freie Universität Berlin on Wednesday, November 18, 2026, at 3:00 p.m. The political scientist, former Canadian Minister of Foreign Affairs, and diplomat will draw on both his academic expertise and political experience in the talk, which will be held in English.

President of Freie Universität Berlin, Professor Günter M. Ziegler, will hold the welcome address for Dion. The thirty-minute lecture by Dion will be followed by a discussion. The event is being organized by the Chair of German Politics at Freie Universität Berlin’s Otto Suhr Institute of Political Science in cooperation with the Forum of Federations. This international organization launched by the government of Canada is based in Ottawa and develops and shares comparative expertise on the practice of federal and decentralized governance through a global network.

Dion was a professor of political science at the Université de Montréal and currently holds the position of Diplomat in Residence at the university. Prior to occupying the position of Minister of Foreign Affairs between 2015 and 2017, he held several other roles in the Canadian Cabinet, such as Minister of Intergovernmental Affairs and Minister of the Environment. From 2006 to 2008 Dion was leader of the Liberal Party and leader of the Official Opposition. He served as Canada’s ambassador to Germany from 2017 to 2022, followed by a posting to France and Monaco until 2025, and was also appointed special envoy to Europe from 2017 to 2025.
