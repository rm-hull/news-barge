The new Berlin coalition has compiled and distributed its “Household User Manual” to more than 300 negotiators from the other parties. They aim to enact measures that are “reliably financially viable and actually implementable.” Negotiations will prioritise plans already firmly agreed upon in the red-green-red exploratory paper. These are now to be fleshed out in detail and financially secured.

The following specific points are listed:

* Rent cap for state-owned housing companies
* 4-Point Plan for Economic Development
* Expansion of cycling infrastructure and bus lanes
* Additional funds for climate adaptation and the energy transition
* Reduce the social ticket price to 9 euros and offer a corresponding discounted Germany social ticket.
* Rent control
* Additional funds for addiction prevention and drug consumption rooms, as well as a clean city
* Neighborhood canteen
* Youth Culture Card
* Free Museum Sunday
* Renovation of guardhouses and fire stations for the police and fire brigades

Internally, some negotiators have expressed doubts as to whether the planned 220 million euros will be sufficient for this list. Furthermore, according to the guidelines for the negotiating teams, there are other “financially relevant agreements” reached during the exploratory talks. These include, among other things, the socialisation of the Charité hospital, the expansion of the subway system, and the children’s hospital.
