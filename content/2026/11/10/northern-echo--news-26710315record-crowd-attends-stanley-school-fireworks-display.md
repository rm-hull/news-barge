More than 2,000 families turned out for a school fireworks spectacular in a County Durham town as the annual event attracted its biggest crowd yet.

The Family Fireworks Event, organised by Stanley Learning Partnership (SLP) and sponsored by moov Estate Agents, lit up the sky above Greenland Community Primary School in Stanley with a 20-minute display of 1,200 fireworks shells set to music.

Mark Stewart, CEO of SLP Multi Academy Trust, said: "We’re incredibly grateful to moov for sponsoring this event – it’s a fantastic way to bring our school communities together.

"The fireworks display is always a highlight, but what makes it special is seeing the joy on the kids’ faces.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21687038.jpg?type=mds-article-620)More than 2,000 families enjoy SLP’s biggest fireworks night yet. *(Image: Supplied)*

"Events like this aren’t just about fireworks, they’re about giving our families a chance to unwind together, away from the pressures of daily life.

"The fact that we’ve done this for eight years shows how much it means to our community."

The event, now in its eighth year, was open to families from SLP’s 11 schools across Derwentside and welcomed Benfieldside Primary School for the first time, following its recent addition to the trust.

Tickets were priced at £3 per person to keep the event accessible, with funds raised going towards future educational and family-focused initiatives.

Fifty SLP staff and parent volunteers supported the evening, taking on roles from crowd management to running stalls.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21687039.jpg?type=mds-article-620)A spectacular 1,200-shell display dazzles crowds at Greenland Community Primary School *(Image: Supplied)*

An eight-year-old pupil from Benfieldside Primary said: "It looked like a dragon breathing fire."

Iain Strachan, sales director at moov Estate Agents, said: "We were thrilled to support a truly local event that gives kids a magical night out.

"The turnout, the fireworks, and the buzz in the air exceeded all expectations.

"We’re already brainstorming ways to make next year’s event even bigger – maybe a ‘glow-in-the-dark’ zone for the kids or a charity raffle to support local causes.

"It honestly couldn’t have turned out any better."
