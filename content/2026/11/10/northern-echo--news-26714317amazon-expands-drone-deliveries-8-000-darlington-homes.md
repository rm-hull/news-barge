Amazon drones are now able to drop off parcels to 8,000 Darlington homes in a major expansion of its fleet in the town.

Thousands more households will now be able to have everyday items dropped from the sky after Prime Air secured the “world’s first” safety authorisation. 

The tech giant currently only has 600 customers eligible for drone delivery - but the approval means roughly 8,000 can now get items in under two hours.

The tech giant first started the ground-breaking trials back in January from its fulfilment centre at Symmetry Park and was ready to be green-lit a full commercial service in September.

Since the start of the year, it has been operating a limited service from the 465,000 sq ft warehouse for beyond-visual-line-of-sight flights in less densely populated areas.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21437128.jpg?type=mds-article-620)An Amazon drone at Darlington's fulfilment centre *(Image: ANDY FUTERS)*

Now, aerospace regulator Civil Aviation Authority (CAA) has granted the firm a SAIL 4 (Specific Assurance and Integrity Level 4) Operating Authorisation and Flightworthiness Validation.

It means flights can now be carried out over more of the town, laying the foundation for the service to expand across the UK.

To grant SAIL 4, the CAA assessed Prime Air's operation, aircraft, organisation and approach to risk, and determined the operation met a level of standards.

Prime Air’s drone, the MK30 aircraft, which holds CAA approval to conduct operations, is built with multiple layers of safety and an industry-leading detect-and-avoid system.

The systems continuously monitor the surrounding airspace during each flight, recognising other aircraft, people, animals and obstacles, and deciding whether action is needed.

The drones are taught to steer clear of hazards, such as clotheslines or trampolines, and an independent monitoring computer oversees the main flight-control system.

If it detects any issues, it can instantly be returned to base or a safe landing elsewhere.

A wide range of items can be delivered by drone, from beauty to household essentials, so long as they weigh up to 5 lbs (2.2kg) and can fit in the aircraft.

It is available to customers with a garden or driveway within a 7.5-mile (12km) radius of the Darlington warehouse - and eligible customers will see the drone delivery option at checkout.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21445561.jpg?type=mds-article-620)How Amazon is operating its drone fleet from Darlington *(Image: ANDY FUTERS)*

Tees Valley Mayor Ben Houchen said: “Once again, our region is leading the way with a global giant on our doorstep.

“Not only have Amazon chosen Darlington for a UK-first, but they’re now expanding”

“It’s fantastic to see more people across the town able to use this, and I want to see even more people benefit from faster, more convenient deliveries.

“It’s yet another huge vote of confidence in Teesside, Darlington and Hartlepool – and we’re showing the sky isn’t the limit.”

Theunis Scheepers, Prime Air Europe director, said earning the approval “took years of disciplined engineering and a commitment to safety from day one”.

He said: “I want to thank the CAA, who worked alongside us at every step. For customers in Darlington it means getting what they need quickly, safely, and reliably, and it is the foundation for bringing Prime Air to more of the UK."

Sophie O'Sullivan, director of future safety and innovation at the CAA, said: "Innovation is transforming aviation, and the CAA's role is to ensure that happens safely.

“As technologies such as Amazon's drone delivery services mature and become more commonplace, we're helping to create conditions that allow innovation to thrive, while maintaining the high safety standards that underpin UK aviation.
