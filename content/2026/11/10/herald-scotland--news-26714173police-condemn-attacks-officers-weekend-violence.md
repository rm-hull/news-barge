Police have condemned the "shameful and cowardly" assaults on officers during a weekend that saw a spate of attacks on serving staff in separate incidents.

Three officers were assaulted during a disturbance and violent disorder in the vicinity of Congress Road on Saturday night in Glasgow. Two officers required hospital treatment.

The disorder broke out after a Bare Knuckle Fighting Championship event held at the OVO Hydro on Saturday evening.

Two men, aged 32 and 27, will be reported to the Procurator Fiscal after they were arrested and charged with offences including assaulting police, obstructing police and threatening and abusive behaviour.

Three other men were arrested and issued with recorded police warnings for threatening and abusive behaviour and breach of the peace.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21693554.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Police activity outside the OVO Hydro in Glasgow following a bareknuckle boxing event on Saturday *(Image: Peter O'brien via Facebook)*

Officers continue to carry out extensive enquiries, including examining CCTV footage.

Further arrests are expected and anyone with information about the disturbance is asked to contact Police Scotland on 101.

In a separate incident on Saturday morning, a car was stopped by officers in Midlothian.

A 23-year-old man who was driving the car struck one of the three officers in attendance, and the officer was taken to hospital for treatment.

The driver was arrested and charged and was expected to appear at Edinburgh Sheriff Court on Monday, November 9, 2026.

In a third incident, officers were violently attacked as they dealt with a report of a disturbance on a bus in Edinburgh.

Two officers were treated by paramedic colleagues at the scene.

A 38-year-old man arrested and charged in connection with the incident was also due to appear at Edinburgh Sheriff Court on Monday, November 9, 2026.

Deputy Chief Constable Alan Speirs said: "This weekend, we saw a catalogue of violent incidents across Scotland, with officers being injured, assaulted and requiring hospital treatment as they attended calls during their shifts.

"These acts against our officers are shameful and cowardly. Not one of those officers left their home and family to go to work expecting to become a victim of assault. Being assaulted is simply not part of being a police officer.

"Our latest performance figures show that, on average, 21 officers are assaulted on duty every day. We are seeing a difference due to measures aimed at reducing violence against officers through our Your Safety Matters programme and the introduction of Body Worn Video. However, assaulting our officers is simply unacceptable."

He added: "We place the highest priority on the safety and wellbeing of our officers, and anyone who has been assaulted is encouraged to make use of the support available."
