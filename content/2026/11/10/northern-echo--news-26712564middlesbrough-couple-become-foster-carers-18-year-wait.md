A North East couple who dreamed of having a big family have found the home they always wanted through fostering, almost two decades after they first applied.

Trish, 48, and Martin, 49, from Middlesbrough, began fostering through Fostering People North East five years ago after first applying 18 years earlier.

They were unable to foster at the time due to caring for their son Adam, who had medical needs.

Trish said: "We always wanted a big family.

"We wanted lots of kids, but unfortunately, it just didn’t happen.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21691757.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Foster parents Trish and Martin foster with Fostering People in the North East *(Image: Supplied)*

"We experienced multiple pregnancy losses, and our birth son was born with some medical needs.

"So we thought about adoption or fostering, but we were rejected because we wouldn’t be able to give other children the amount of attention they would need while caring for our son, Adam, too.

"They told us to wait until he was independent, and that’s what we did—we waited until Adam was 18 and then applied again."

The couple were approved to foster in 2021 and welcomed their first foster child just two weeks later.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21691759.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Foster mum Trish with birth son Adam *(Image: Supplied)*

They now care for an eight-year-old boy, as well as a 16-month-old and an eight-week-old.

With their support, the eight-year-old is expected to return to his birth family in the coming years.

The younger children may either go home, move in with relatives, or be adopted.

Trish said: "It’s going to be really difficult to say goodbye to our foster kids, but fostering is, by its nature, temporary.

"We are the stopgap they need, where they’ll get ready to be with their families again or move on to people who will love them forever.

"But we want to make sure that, while they’re here, we surround them with love and give them the compassion and room they need to grow and develop in the best way possible.

"It’s my job to make sure those children leave here knowing that somebody cares and that they are important."

The couple are passionate about challenging the stigma surrounding children in care and helping them build confidence.

Trish said: "It’s not the child’s fault that they are in care.

"They weren’t treated right and have been badly let down.

"So if they behave in a particular way, it’s not a choice the kids are making; it’s a cry for help.

"That’s what people need to understand.

"Our foster son used to always ask, ‘Am I a good boy? Am I a good boy?’ because he always thought he wasn’t worthy, that it was his fault he was in care.

"So every morning and every night, I would tell him, ‘You are kind, you are brave, you are enough.’

"After around six months, that helped change his mindset, and he doesn’t ask anymore.

"When you see a child go from tense and wary to open and relaxed, knowing you’ve helped them achieve that—it’s the best feeling in the world and the most rewarding thing we’ve ever done."

There are 6,040 children in care across the North East, including 493 in Middlesbrough.

Fostering People is encouraging anyone interested in fostering to call 0800 077 8159 or visit fosteringpeople.co.uk/locations/middlesbrough.
