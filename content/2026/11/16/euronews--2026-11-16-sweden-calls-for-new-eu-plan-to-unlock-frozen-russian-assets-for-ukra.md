## Sweden urged the EU to use frozen Russian assets to supprt Ukraine, with Foreign Minister Maria Malmer Stenergard telling Euronews that the financial risks should be shared among member states.

Sweden has called on the European Commission to draw up a new proposal to use frozen Russian assets to help finance Ukraine, with Foreign Minister Maria Malmer Stenergard urging European countries to step up their military and financial support for Kyiv.

Speaking to Euronews, Stenergard said she hoped the Commission was working on a plan to move the assets out of Belgium and share the financial risks among EU member states.

She described the €90 billion loan agreed by the EU as an important step, but warned that bilateral support for Ukraine had since declined.

"We need more bilateral support. We need more loans, but we also need to use the frozen Russian assets," she said.

Stenergard also called for continued European military assistance, arguing that Russia was showing no interest in peace and that European countries should focus on increasing support for Kyiv while stepping up pressure on Moscow.

The foreign minister, who recently visited Abu Dhabi during negotiations aimed at ending the war, said she had seen nothing to make her optimistic about the prospects for a diplomatic breakthrough.

She insisted that Europe must have a seat at the negotiating table, arguing that any settlement would have far-reaching implications for European interests, including Ukraine's potential EU and NATO membership.

"We are not mediators," she said. "We should be at the table, but we should be on Ukraine's side."

Stenergard also warned that Russia was taking increasingly bold risks through hybrid attacks against European countries, particularly in the Baltic region.

She said Sweden had raised spending on civil defence to strengthen public preparedness and resilience, and called for greater European unity in the face of Russian attempts to sow division.

"What they basically want is to have us split up and also decrease support for Ukraine. So we should increase support for Ukraine. Stay united."
