**A hacker has pleaded guilty to a string of online crimes, including trying to extort the makers of Grand Theft Auto 6 after stealing dozens of unfinished clips of the game.**

21-year-old Arion Kurtaj, from Oxford, carried out the hack in 2022, and was originally sentenced to an indefinite stay in a secure hospital due to the severity of his autism.

But, in an unusual move, he was subsequently deemed fit to stand trial and released to a normal prison in January.

On Monday, Kurtaj pleaded guilty at Woolwich Crown Court to the Rockstar Games hack and a handful of others which he carried out when he was 16 and 17 years old with the hacking gang Lapsus$.

Kurtaj breached Rockstar Games in 2022 whilst he was on bail for multiple other hacking offences and under police protection.

He was being housed in a Travelodge in Bicester to protect him from rival hackers who threatened to attack him for his stolen stashes of Bitcoin.

With his computer confiscated, Kurtaj hacked Rockstar using an Amazon Fire Stick plugged into his hotel room television along with a Bluetooth keyboard and mouse.

He stole internal company data including 90 clips showing nearly 60 minutes of gameplay from the unfinished and hugely anticipated Grand Theft Auto 6.

He then broke into the company's internal Slack messaging system to declare: "If Rockstar does not contact me on Telegram within 24 hours I will start releasing the source code."

The company refused to cooperate so he posted the clips and source code on a forum under the username TeaPotUberHacker.

Rockstar Games said it cost the firm $5m (£3.79m) to recover from the hack, plus thousands of hours of staff time.

The company also said the breach revealed for the first time that one of the playable characters in the game was a woman - a secret it was guarding closely for marketing purposes.

At the time, doctors deemed Kurtaj unfit to stand in a traditional trial due to the severity of his autism, so instead he was given what is known as a trial of the facts.

The jury was asked to determine whether or not he committed the alleged acts - not if he did so with criminal intent.

In December 2023 the court ruled that he would remain at a secure hospital for life unless doctors deem him no longer a danger.

A routine assessment late last year deemed that he was now fit to stand a criminal trial which was scheduled to take place at Woolwich Crown Court.

But Kurtaj pleaded guilty to all offences.

He sat in the dock with short hair and an unkempt beard and wore a suit and buttoned-up white shirt but no tie.

The now 21-year-old blinked frequently during the hearing and stood to confirm his name, and pled guilty to all counts with no visible emotion.

The proscution described Kurtaj as a 'leading light' in the teen gang Lapsus$

Those included hacks on BT/EE, Revolut and Uber where he used social engineering techniques to gain access to internal systems.

The court heard that during his hack on Uber he posted a picture of a penis as part of his attempts to force the company to pay a ransom.

His hacks on the companies are estimated to have caused around £10m of damage.

Using the data stolen from the firms, he and other members of the gang targeted cryptocurrency investors.

The prosecution argued that the gang stole at least $300,000 from individual victims.

Kurtaj also pleaded guilty to physical assaults on workers whilst in custody including multiple biting incidents.

Whilst in prison he was caught with a mobile phone after sending a taunting text message to police and posting updates on Snapchat.

Kurtaj will be sentenced at a later date.

Kurtaj's Snapchat posts were the first sign for reporters that he had been released from a secure hospital

In the 2023 original trial, another Lapsus$ member, who was 17, was found guilty at Southwark Crown Court.

The 17-year-old, who could not be named at the time due to his age, was sentenced to an 18-month Youth Rehabilitation Order, including intense supervision and a ban on using VPNs online.

Earlier this year that same hacker, now named as Thalha Jubair, was jailed for five years and six months after continuing his cyber crime offences with another group known as Scattered Spider.

Amongst victims was Transport for London which was subjected to a disruptive cyber attack that caused around £39m of damage and caused chaos inside the firm.
