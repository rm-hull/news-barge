## Albania's PM Rama criticised a band for playing a Serbian nationalist song claiming Kosovo as Serbian land at the European Commission’s enlargement conference. Marta Kos said it does not reflect the Commission’s position, while Pristina called it unacceptable.

Albanian Prime Minister Edi Rama criticised the performance of a Serbian nationalist song that claims Kosovo as part of Serbia at the opening of the EU Enlargement Summit, prompting outrage in the region.

"I don't know if it was deliberate or because of ignorance. But a Serbian nationalistic song was performed here on the eve of this event," Rama said at the summit on Monday. "To add insult to injury, it was performed under the beautiful title Melodies of Unity."

"It's not that these words hurt the feeling of Albania or of the Albanian nation or of the Albanian citizens of the Republic of Kosovo, but it is about the need to let go of anything that brings us back in the past," he continued.

Slovenian band Marko Hatlak & Kapobanda played “Oj Kosovo, Kosovo” on Sunday evening at the concert opening the European Commission’s annual enlargement conference.

Playing such a politically charged song at a Commission-hosted conference outraged Kosovo, which saw it as a provocation.

“This is not cultural exchange. The song advances explicit political claims over the territory of the Republic of Kosova. Its inclusion in an official European Commission event dedicated to enlargement, unity and good-neighbourly relations is unacceptable,” Agron Bajrami, Kosovo’s ambassador to Belgium, said on X.

The band was meant to play a song from each candidate country, including Kosovo, which has no formal candidate status and is not recognised as a state by five EU countries. Yet the song chosen for Kosovo is Serbian in language and in sentiment.

“I regret the choice of the song by the musicians. It touches sensitive feelings. Particularly given the painful history and the different experiences of the communities in Kosovo which are coming from this area,” Enlargement Commissioner Marta Kos told reporters on Monday.

“I want to stress that this song in no way reflects the position of the European Commission or myself with respect to Kosovo and its history. What is clear is that we cannot change our shared past, but we can choose our shared future,” Kos continued.

The song was written after Slobodan Milošević’s landmark 1989 Gazimestan speech, in which the then Serbian president cast Kosovo as the heart of Serbian national identity, on the eve of the Yugoslav wars.

"We all need to engage for transforming our neighbourhoods. This is what all this is about. Because at the end of the day, we are not seeking to come here. We are seeking to bring Europe in there," Rama added.
