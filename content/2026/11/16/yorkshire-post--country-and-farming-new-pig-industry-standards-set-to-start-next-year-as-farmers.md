# ​New pig industry standards set to start next year as farmers lose £25 a pig on production costs

## Pig industry leaders have pledged to press on with improving industry standards despite mounting pressures impacting on the sector.

Red Tractor Pigs Sector Board chair Stewart Houston, whose career has seen him farm pigs near Ripon, said the standards body recognised a consultation over standards was coming at “a really bad time for the sector”, just as many producers are having their contracted volume of pigs cut by suppliers.

He added, if approved, the new standards would likely be introduced towards the end of next year.

The proposals come amid difficult market conditions for the UK pig sector and thousands of pigs backing up on farms, partly due to an imbalance of supply and demand, compounded by low pig prices.

![Red Tractor says proposed changes to its pig sector standards have been triggered by animal activists and rising temperatures.](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOmY1OTY5YzA0LTk4NmMtNDYzZi1hZDU5LTcxMmI4ZDRlZjQzMjpiODNmMzJkYy0zOTUwLTQ2NTktOTNmMy05OTQwNzMwZmUxYTg=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


Farmers are losing almost £25 a pig on average, as mounting production costs continue to outpace prices paid to producers, Agriculture and Horticulture Development Board figures this week showed.

Red Tractor is inviting farmers, growers and wider industry stakeholders to have their say on proposed changes to farm assurance in a six-week consultations, which started this week, also covering combinable crops and sugar beet, fresh produce and poultry.

Paul McLaughlin, chief executive at Red Tractor, said the proposed changes aimed to bring clearer, more practical standards and reduce unnecessary duplication, representing “a significant step forward”.

He said: “We have heard clearly from farmers and growers that assurance needs to work better for them. We need to take out unnecessary complexity not add to it.”

Mr Houston, who has been a leading figure in Yorkshire’s most economically important agricultural sector for many years, said many of the changes had been triggered by the campaigns of animal rights activists or the high summer temperatures.

He said pig activists had been known to drag the bodies of pigs out of the dead bin after illegally entering farms at night “to make it look like it’s a really bad farm”.

Mr Houston added: “So we’ve strengthened the standard that says those pigs have to be marked when they are put in the disposal bin.”

He said the cost of introducing evaporative cooling to reduce heat stress would be mitigated by the losses from stressed sows, particularly those about to farrow.

Mr Houston said: “We’ve strengthtened the moving, handling and ethanasia standards to create a culture of care.

"Standards are all very well, but we don’t want people to do them because they have to do them. There needs to be a culture of wanting to be the best.”

He said many of the existing standards had been “rationalised”, with numerous checks being cut to simplify the process while Red Tractor inspectors would be directed to look for positive aspects alongside issues of concern.

One of the main changes includes a move to introduce a standard for flexible or free farrowing.

Mr Houston said Red Tractor had proposed that producers could not introduce new conventional crates, but where they do, the standard would be 6.5sq metres per pen, which was in line with a forecast EU move.
