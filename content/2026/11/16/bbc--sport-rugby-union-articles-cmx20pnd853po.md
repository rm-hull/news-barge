**England's Will Joseph is in line to retain his place for Saturday's blockbuster Nations Championship meeting with New Zealand afterscoring five tries against Japan.**

In the absence of the injured Immanuel Feyi-Waboso, Joseph is understood to be in pole position to reprise his role on the wing after a performance packed with pace and finishing ability.

Joseph scored a hat-trick inside the first 19 minutes, on the way to becoming only the fifth England men's player to score five tries in one match.

He also headed the team's charts for metres carried (224) and top speed (21.7mph).

The 24-year-old won his second Test cap in Saturday's 78-15 thrashing of the Brave Blossoms, four and half years after making his debut as a teenager.

After a late cameo as a replacement in England's second Test win over Australia in July 2022, Joseph struggled to fulfil his promise. Boyhood club London Irish went into liquidation and a stint at Harlequins was hampered by injury and a lack of game time.

However, Joseph has been a key part of Gloucester's resurgence this season and, watched by elder brother and former England centre Jonathan Joseph, he made a sensational return to international rugby at Allianz Stadium last weekend.

"He is just so fast. He doesn't look too fast in the first two steps and then he is just gone. He gets away from people," said former England wing Chris Ashton on BBC Radio 5 Live of Joseph's ability.

"He floats, doesn't he?" added England team-mate Ben Earl to BBC Sport.

"The way he carries himself, he does really move like his brother. They are so, so similar."

With Feyi-Waboso ruled out of the autumn by an ankle injury, Cadan Murley is the other specialist wing in England's squad vying with Joseph for a spot alongside likely first-choice Tommy Freeman in the back three.

After victories over Australia and Japan this autumn, England are second in the Nations Championship northern hemisphere standings and in line for a play-off meeting with South Africa on finals weekend on 27-29 November.

However, that could change considerably, with New Zealand leading the southern hemisphere table and second-place South Africa taking on Ireland, who are behind England only on points difference.
