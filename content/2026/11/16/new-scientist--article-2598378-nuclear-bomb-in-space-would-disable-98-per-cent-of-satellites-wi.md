A single nuclear explosion in space would wipe out 98 per cent of satellites within a week, sending vital technology around the world into disarray from which it would take years to recover, warn experts.

Zachary Petersen at the University of Wisconsin–Madison and his colleagues modelled the effect of a 1 megaton explosion 500 kilometres above the South Atlantic. They found that an initial spike of X-rays from the blast was damaging, but it wasn’t the most disruptive part: electrons released by radioactive fission would become trapped in Earth’s magnetic field, exposing satellites to a kind of artificial radiation belt, over and over again.
