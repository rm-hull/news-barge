Residents of two rival cities have set themselves an impossible task - trying to push iconic landmarks “seven inches to the left.”

​The viral challenge is tied to a long-running rivalry between St. Louis and Chicago, with the latest development unfolding at Missouri’s Gateway Arch on Sunday.

​Footage showed crowds gathered around the 630-foot-tall monument, each placing their hands on the steel structure and pushing with all their might.

Given the structure’s 43,000 ton weight, the crowd was unsurprisingly unsuccessful in its efforts - though it appears actually moving it is not really the point.

​Meghan McCabe, a St. Louis local, explained to St. Louis Public Radio why she and so many others had gathered at the Gateway Arch.

​“We’re here to unite for the memes,” she said. “Together we can make the Gateway even a little bit more south.”

![Locals in St Louis gathered on Sunday in an effort to move the Gateway Arch 'seven inches to the left'](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/16/10/00/Screenshot-2026-11-16-at-10-27-54.png?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&auto=webp)

​Before the effort began, attendee Yovani Carrillo delivered a nondenominational prayer asking for support in moving the Arch to those gathered at the monument.

​The St. Louis-based event was promoted by an Instagram account named “STLAintBad,” which posted a flyer last week telling people to gather at the structure at 12.07 p.m. on November 15.

​The image of the flyer was captioned: “I just feel like we’re stronger than Chicago.”

Sunday’s event has its roots in a rival effort to push a landmark seven inches that unfolded in Chicago a week earlier.

![The Gateway Arch is over 600 feet tall and weighs over 43,000 tons](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/16/10/1241616730..?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&auto=webp)

​Hundreds of Windy City residents tried moving Cloud Gate, a structure known as “The Bean,” seven inches to the left on November 8. ​

An Instagram account satirically suggested that there was a man trapped beneath the monument, drawing hundreds to the 12.47 p.m. event.

​“New findings suggest that there might be (sic) latch door under the right side of the bean,” a Partiful invitation to the event read.

“If we move the bean about 7 inches to the left, we might be able to access that door and give the man inside the bean resources.”

![The effort to push the Gateway Arch seven inches did not succeed, but one attendee said that those who attended showed a ‘sense of community’](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/16/11/09/Screenshot-2026-11-16-at-11-33-32.png?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&trim=215%2C1%2C0%2C0&auto=webp)

​St. Louis and Chicago have been locked in a rivalry ever since the 19th Century, when they both represented major economic powers in the Midwest.

Today, that rivalry exists mainly in the form of competitions between the Chicago Cubs and St. Louis Cardinals baseball teams.

​Zane Stobie, who brought a tape measure to the event in St. Louis, explained to St. Louis Public Radio that the event represented a form of community.

![Chicagoans tried to move the Cloud Gate sculpture, also known as 'The Bean,' last week (file photo)](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/16/10/2166200644..?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&auto=webp)

​“I think it really shows a sense of just being whimsical, having a good time, a sense of community, and just living life the best way that you can,” he said.

“If you meet some people and just enjoy a Sunday morning, it’s the best way to do it.”
