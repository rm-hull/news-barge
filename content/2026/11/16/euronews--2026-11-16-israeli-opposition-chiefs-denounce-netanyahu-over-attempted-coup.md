## Netanyahu's main rival, former army chief of staff Gadi Eisenkot, wrote on Sunday that the caretaker prime minister "cannot remain at the head of the government for a minute longer".

Two Israeli opposition leaders denounced an "attempted coup" on Monday following press reports that outgoing Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's office tried to frame members of an Arab-Israeli party ahead of last month's election.

"As more and more details come to light, it becomes clear: This wasn't an attempt to disrupt the elections. It was an attempted coup," Yair Lapid, who headed the opposition in the previous legislature and co-chairs the Beyachad list, wrote on X.

Israel's Channel 12 television reported on Sunday that on the eve of the 27 October election, Netanyahu's chief of staff Ido Norden had phoned in a complaint to the police about members of the Arab-Israeli Raam party.

Norden reportedly pointed the police to a purported intelligence source, a former employee of Netanyahu's office, who provided information that led to the arrest of three Raam members.

The three men were later released when the police determined that the information was unfounded.

Netanyahu subsequently accused Arab parties of electoral fraud after they gained a record number of seats. His Likud party issued a statement denying the latest allegations.

The Israeli newspaper Haaretz also reported that David Zini, head of the Shin Bet domestic security service, sought to wiretap leaders of the other Arab slate, the Joint List, but the attorney general turned down his request due to lack of evidence.

Following the report, Joint List leader Yousef Jabareen also denounced an "attempted coup by Netanyahu".

"If the findings are confirmed, this is a criminal mechanism that operated from the heart of government to rig the election results. Everyone found responsible must be held accountable, including Netanyahu," he wrote on X.

Raam's number two, former police chief Yoav Segalovitz, called for an urgent investigation.

"These suspicions require an immediate, independent and thorough criminal investigation that reaches everyone involved, at every level and in every role," he wrote on X.

"Netanyahu cried out that his election was being stolen, while he himself tried to steal the country and is still trying to do so now," he added.

Netanyahu's main rival, former army chief of staff Gadi Eisenkot, wrote on Sunday that the caretaker prime minister "cannot remain at the head of the government for a minute longer".

Neither Netanyahu's bloc nor Eisenkot's opposition secured the 61 seats required to form a government following last month's election.

The two Arab parties, the Joint List and Raam, have indicated support for Eisenkot who was tasked by President Isaac Herzog with forming a government.
