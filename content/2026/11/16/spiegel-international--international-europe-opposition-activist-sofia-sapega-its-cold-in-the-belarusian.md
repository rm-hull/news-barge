It’s 12:46 p.m. on May 23, 2021, when Ryanair flight FR4978 from Athens to Vilnius veers off course. An announcement informs passengers of an unscheduled landing. On board are the Belarusian opposition figures Sofia Sapega, 23, and her boyfriend Roman Protasevich, 26 – returning from a vacation together. Sapega, as she recalls today, looks at her watch – by now they should already be approaching Vilnius in Lithuania. Instead, they are on their way to Minsk. Sapega glances over at her boyfriend.

"We reacted completely differently to the announcement. He got nervous, stood up and started shouting at the flight attendants. He told them that there was no way we could land on Belarusian soil. I stayed strangely calm."

In her mind, she says now, she was already thinking about finishing her degree. But she would miss the graduation ceremony. "Three years later I got myself one of those square caps and a gown and celebrated after the fact." Sapega points to the 18th century Augustinian monastery that houses the European Humanities University, the Belarusian university in exile, located in the Lithuanian capital of Vilnius.
