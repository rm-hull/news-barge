A “controlling and violent” former Royal Marine who shot his wife dead after she told him she was leaving was allowed to keep two licensed shotguns despite police knowing he had a history of domestic abuse.

John Perkins shot Kathryn Perkins at their home in Devon with a double-barrelled shotgun before killing himself with his second weapon.

The couple’s inquests in Exeter were told that Perkins, 66, had repeatedly been violent towards Kathryn Perkins, 67, during their 42-year-old marriage, beginning when he head-butted her during their honeymoon.

The coroner, Alison Longhorn, area coroner for Devon, Plymouth and Torbay, concluded that Kathryn Perkins had been unlawfully killed and John Perkins killed himself.

Perkins’ violence towards his wife and his anger issues were recorded in his medical records and passed to Devon and Cornwall police when Perkins applied for his shotgun licence to be renewed.

Even so, police approved his application and Perkins was allowed to keep his shotguns, which he said he wanted for pheasant and deer shooting.

Longhorn said she was concerned that when someone applied for a shotgun licence, the scrutiny of any history of domestic abuse did not appear as “thorough, considered or as carefully and accurately documented as it should be”. She said she would write to Devon and Cornwall police and the Home Office about the case.

The relationship continued to deteriorate and shortly before he shot his wife in May 2025 Perkins searched the internet to find out if it was possible to get a silencer for a shotgun.

Devon and Cornwall police’s handling of firearms applications came under intense scrutiny for allowing Jake Davison to have the pump action shotgun he used to kill five people in Plymouth in 2021.

In a statement read out at the inquest, the couple’s son, Bradley Perkins, described his father as violent, controlling and unpredictable.

He said that when he was 11, his father punched him in the face, leaving him with a black eye and prompting social services to become involved. He also spoke about a time when Perkins punched his wife in the head as they drove home from a ski trip.

By May 2025, Bradley Perkins said his mother had decided to leave her husband and went to their house in Exmouth, Devon, to tell him.

Bradley Perkins said when he failed to hear from his mother, he went to their home and found his parents’ bodies. “I was screaming and could not believe what I was seeing,” he said.

Kathryn Perkins’ sister, Barbara, described Perkins as a bully and an “egotistical narcissist” who had head-butted his wife on their honeymoon.

She said her sister had contacted domestic abuse support at one point and that in 2010 John Perkins had attended an NHS anger management course but did not complete it because he thought it was “rubbish”.

The inquest was told that Perkins’ medical records recorded that he was referred on to the course because he had “smacked” his wife.

Police were told about this by Perkins’ GP when he asked for his firearms licence to be renewed in 2023.

An experienced officer was sent to the couple’s address and spoke to Kathryn Perkins but she said she had no concerns about her husband having shotguns.

Police also knew that in 1995 Perkins had had an altercation with a neighbour but this was disregarded as irrelevant.

Devon and Cornwall police told the inquest changes were being made to how licensing applications were considered when concerns about domestic violence were raised but they had not yet been completed.
