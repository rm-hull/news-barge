## “Heat pumps are already delivering emissions cuts and protection from fossil fuel prices.”

Heat pumps can cut a quarter of the European Union’s energy emissions, as leaders are urged to “stop fiddling while the world burns and do what works”.

New analysis from the European Heat Pump Association (EHPA) found that fitting heat pumps in every building and “compatible industrial process” – powered by renewables like solar and wind – would avoid around 600 million tonnes (Mt) of CO2.

This is more than the annual emissions of Germany, the bloc’s biggest economy, or the 17 smallest EU countries combined.

## What are heat pumps and how do they lower emissions?

Depending on the type, heat pumps work by capturing energy from air, water or the ground and turning it into heat or cold air – rather than burning fossil fuels on-site.

They work in a similar way to a refrigerator but in reverse, using a liquid chemical that turns into a hot gas through compression.

This hot gas releases its heat to your central heating, warming your home. The gas then cools down, turning back into liquid form, ready to start all over again.

For every one kilowatt-hour (kWh) of electricity they consume, they typically deliver three to four kWh of heat.

According to EHPA, each residential heat pump installed displaces around 1,100 m3 of gas and two tonnes of CO2 per year on average, if the small amount of electricity it uses to run is from renewable sources.

There are already around 30 million residential heat pumps installed in Europe, with around three million added in 2025. This is one million fewer installations than the four million target set out by the European Commission.

Heat pump technology has become enmeshed in a “wave of misinformation” surrounding their ability to work in cold temperatures, their noise, and whether they can be installed in old buildings.

Euronews Earth has already debunked these myths here.

## ‘Climate change does not wait’

“Leaders need to stop fiddling while the world burns and do what works,” says Paul Kenny, director general of EHPA. “Heat pumps are already delivering emissions cuts and protection from fossil fuel prices.”

Kenny urged nations to make it easier to install heat pumps in every building and low temperature industry by ensuring upfront support, affordable, clean electricity and a “smooth” grid connection.

“Climate change does not wait and nor should we,” he adds.
