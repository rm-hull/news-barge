# Rejoining the EU is starting to look like common sense for Andy Burnham: Yorkshire Post Letters

## Peter Packham, Chair, Leeds for Europe, Roundhay, Leeds.

Westminster wisdom held that Labour politicians had to let head rule heart on Europe – stay silent about Brexit’s damage, else lose seats in Yorkshire and the North of England.

Your front-page report on Best for Britain’s YouGov megapoll proves Prime Minister Andy Burnham faces no such ‘head or heart’ dilemma (Poll puts pressure on PM to rejoin EU, November 9).

Clinging to Sir Keir Starmer’s Brexit red lines could see Labour lose 36 of its 43 MPs across Yorkshire. Conversely, committing to rejoining the EU, the single market or the customs union means keeping nearly two-thirds of its seats. Electoral calculations and principled conviction combine.

Like voters, Colne Valley MP Paul Davies recognises Brexit’s “lasting negative effect” on Yorkshire and insists all options, including full EU membership, be on the table; or as Tom Brufatto of Best for Britain says, pledging to restore EU membership is “vital” to “win back the trust of voters”.

Your accompanying Comment piece argues a new referendum would likely be an “all-consuming” “misstep”. Yet arguing that voters must remain trapped by a past mistake because charlatans lied ten years ago – and might lie again – is a depressing surrender of democracy.

Andy Burnham promises “an open and honest conversation”.

Less partisan media outlets such as the BBC and The Yorkshire Post can play their part in keeping us all honest by analysing the validity of claims rather than just platforming opposing statements with no proper scrutiny – as if that’s “balance”.

No single issue can consume “every ounce of political bandwidth and operational capacity”, as you suggest. Unless we let it. Dealing with Brexit issues dovetails with the aims of a government wanting to grow the economy and plough more resources into public services.

If we don’t deal with it now, then when? It will continue to be the constant, year-after-year drain it has been for the past decade – leaving problems this generation created even harder for future ones to resolve.

When both your head and your heart are telling you the same thing, doesn’t rejoining start to look like just common sense?

In the same November 9 edition, Tony Galbraith again argues for EU trade barriers to protect British factories. That would be counter-productive.

Andy Burnham has promised to “re-industrialise”, and better EU relations must be an essential part of that; not isolating ourselves further from continental supply chains and European customers.

J. A. King, Thurgoland, Sheffield

Over the recent months the Leeds for Europe group have bombarded this letters page with all the reasons they think the UK should rejoin the EU. This revolves around free movement of EU nationals to fill our universities, so in that case I can see where they are coming from.

But to reopen the floodgates to the rest of the EU would be a disaster for the working man and our youth in general particularly as unemployment in this country is rapidly rising.

Why should anybody with a modicum of common sense wish to be controlled by unelected out-of-touch commissioners from Brussels?
