# Watch: Snow begins falling in North Yorkshire as Met Office warns up to 15cm could hit tomorrow

## Snow has already started falling in parts of North Yorkshire as the Met Office warns that up to 15cm could accumulate on higher ground tomorrow, with travel disruption expected.

Footage from Goathland in North Yorkshire shows snow beginning to fall as the region prepares for a spell of wintry weather.

The Met Office has issued a yellow snow warning for Tuesday (Nov 17), covering North Yorkshire, South Yorkshire and West Yorkshire.

Forecasters have warned that some roads and railways could be affected, with longer journey times expected for motorists and public transport passengers.

There is also a small chance of power cuts and disruption to mobile phone services.

## Will it snow in Yorkshire tomorrow?

According to the Met Office, a band of rain is expected to move northwards across the UK overnight on Monday (Nov 16) and into Tuesday morning.

As the rain encounters colder air, it is likely to turn to snow for a time, particularly across higher ground.

Areas at elevations of 100 to 200 metres or above are most likely to experience snowfall, while any snow falling at lower levels is expected to be temporary and melt quickly.

Where snow settles, accumulations of between 1cm and 5cm are forecast.

However, some locations above 300 to 400 metres could see between 5cm and 15cm of snow.

Strong easterly winds could also cause localised drifting, potentially making conditions particularly difficult on exposed roads and higher-level routes.

## Which parts of Yorkshire could see snow?

The Met Office yellow weather warning covers North Yorkshire, West Yorkshire and South Yorkshire.

Higher ground across these areas is most likely to experience accumulating snow, although the exact locations affected will depend on temperatures and the movement of the weather system.

Lower-lying towns and cities may see a brief spell of sleet or snow, but any snowfall at these elevations is expected to melt quickly.

The Met Office expects snow to gradually turn back to rain from the south and throughout Tuesday morning.

This could also result in some minor disruption from surface water.

## Could the snow cause travel disruption?

The Met Office has warned that snowfall could cause disruption to roads and railways, particularly on higher routes.

Motorists, bus passengers and train users could face longer journey times, while strong easterly winds may cause snow to drift in some exposed areas.

There is also a possibility of ice building up on infrastructure, including power lines.

Drivers are being urged to plan journeys carefully, allow extra time and check for road closures or public transport delays before setting off.

The Met Office also recommends carrying essential supplies in vehicles, including warm clothing, food, water, a blanket, a torch, an ice scraper and a phone charger.

The yellow warning was updated at 9.24am on Monday (Nov 16), with the Met Office reducing its southern and eastern extent and bringing forward its end time.

Forecasters have warned that weather warnings can change quickly and are encouraging people to check the latest forecast before travelling.
