## It comes after a network failure triggered a rare nationwide grounding of all planes on Friday.

The Trump administration is pushing Congress for $50 billion to overhaul the United States’ ageing air traffic control system after a network failure triggered a rare nationwide grounding of all planes on Friday.

Thousands of flights were halted, and passengers across the US are still struggling to reach their destinations as investigators say they are working to establish exactly what went wrong.

Silent on the grounding for two days, President Donald Trump on Sunday broke his silence on his social media platform Truth Social, saying Congress should approve the money “desperately needed” for the system, which he called “RIDICULOUS” for being 50 years old.

Congress approved $12.5 billion (10.95 billion euros) in 2025 to modernise the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA)'s systems, some of which date to the 1960s. The first phase is due for completion by December 2028.

FAA Administrator Bryan Bedford told lawmakers in September that a second phase would cost at least $10 billion (€8.7 billion) more, and a September bill from Republican Sen. Eric Schmitt sought about $30 billion in new investment.

Democrats say they want answers on how existing funds have been spent and why the request has grown.

## What caused the network failure?

US Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy on Friday said he wants $50 billion for air traffic control and said he would send lawmakers a detailed request on Monday.

According to Duffy, Friday's problem began when a federal contractor replaced a section of old copper wire with fibre cable at a site in El Dorado, Arkansas. When the router was switched on, a fault emerged at a facility in Amarillo, Texas, cascading into the ground stop.

The FAA said “excessive data” flooded the system at a rate of 1,000 connection attempts every four to five seconds, destabilising aircraft communications and radar feeds. The network stabilised after the contractor blocked the two locations.

Duffy said officials are “pretty good” that the outage was not a cyberattack, as intelligence agencies have not detected the “chatter or the footprints” they would expect to see.

Duffy, who appeared on five Sunday talk shows, said the funds are needed to build a “brand new architecture” for the network. He said telecoms firms including AT&T, Verizon and Lumen could deploy it “tomorrow” if funding were in place.

Pushing back, Democrats say they back an upgrade but are questioning the price tag. Those on the House Transportation Committee say most of the money appropriated last year remains unspent.

“Explain to Congress, again, why they need more money?” said Rep. Rick Larsen, the committee’s ranking Democrat, and Rep. André Carson in a joint statement. Rep. Rosa DeLauro, the top Democrat on the House Appropriations Committee, said Congress has already shown it is willing to invest.

The dispute previews the sharper scrutiny the administration is likely to face when Democrats take control of Congress in January.

According to Duffy, operations should be at full capacity by Monday, but travellers heading to or connecting through the US should check with their airline for rebookings, as backlogs may take time to clear.

The US operates the world’s busiest airspace, with the FAA handling roughly 45,000 flights a day, making a nationwide ground stop potentially disrupt connections and long-haul schedules far beyond American airports.
