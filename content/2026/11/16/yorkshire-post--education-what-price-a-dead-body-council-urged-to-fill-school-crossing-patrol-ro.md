# 'What price a dead body?' - Council urged to fill school crossing patrol role

## A grandfather says a local authority should “get their finger out” over a school crossing patrol on a busy road.

David McArthur believes Redcar and Cleveland Council needed to do better and asked: “What price a dead body, especially a child?”

Two school crossing patrol officers previously worked in tandem on both sides of the carriageway where children from St Margaret Clitherow Primary School exit St Margaret’s Grove onto Normanby Road, a few hundred metres away from a busy junction.

But one officer was moved to another site several months ago to cover an absence.

![David McArthur pictured on Normanby Road, South Bank.](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOjk1ODdhOTVjLWNiMWMtNGZkNC1hNWQyLThhNDljMjdjNGRlMjpkNTlhOTA0OC04N2JkLTQ5ZTEtYTM5YS1kOWUyY2MzMzBjMjc=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


Mr McArthur, who is originally from Normanby and occasionally collects his grandchildren from the school, said it was a “vital role” and the current situation was unsafe with near misses involving pedestrians and vehicles.

The council said the other school crossing patrol officer was due to return and pointed out, while it had a duty of care, it was not a statutory service being provided.

A council representative also said the limited hours attached to such roles – a maximum of 10 hours per week – were an issue for many people, but defended its recruitment processes and pay scales.

Mr McArthur said his daughter narrowly avoided being hit by a car at the location in question, which was the “final straw” for him.

He said: “I’m a grandad that crosses with my grandchildren and it’s very scary with one lollipop person.This road is wide, it’s busy and it needs two people.”

Mr McArthur said he had made various pleas to the council “who don’t seem to be listening”.

He said: “Why don’t they just double the pay rate, everyone will be queuing up and they won’t be short of staff.They just need to do better. You can’t do half a job.

“To me it’s logical; okay it is a bit more money, but so what.What price a dead body on a road, especially a child?They need to get their finger out.

“The people at the top who pay the money need to have a little think about how safe our children are on the roads with this situation.”

Mr McArthur said the council’s insistence it was not a statutory service was a “kop out”.

The 69-year-old added: “When there were two [crossing patrol officers], drivers going one way will see one of them and the people going the other way will see the other.

“I’ve seen a bloke going straight through with his car while people were crossing as he did not see the lollipop lady as she wasn’t in front of him, she was to the side.

“It’s nose to nose traffic on that road and they [motorists] may not see a traffic warden on the other side of the road, especially when the weather is bad.”

Bev Brown, from the council, said: “This crossing has always had two patrols, the problem is that we have had to move one to cover an empty crossing on Fabian Road, Eston.

“We have recruited for Fabian Road and that person is awaiting his DBS check back, so as soon as we have total clearance the other school crossing patrol will return back to Normanby Road.”

She said the council was “within the right pay scales” for the roles, but the problem was “folk do not want only a few hours a week”.

“We are not a statutory service and it’s parents and guardians responsibility to take children to and from school,” she added.

“The council do not have to put two officers on this site, we do it as a duty of care, however when we need staff at other crossings the first one to be swapped around are the double crossings.

“This makes a lot of sense, rather than leaving other busy crossings empty.

“We have a good recruitment service and have a lot of interviews, but folk want a lot more hours than we can give.It has nothing to do with salaries and recruitment.”

Earlier this year council leader Alec Brown conceded there were “gaps” within the service after the issue was highlighted by some members, but funding had been maintained and he wanted to see school crossing patrol officers numbers increase.
