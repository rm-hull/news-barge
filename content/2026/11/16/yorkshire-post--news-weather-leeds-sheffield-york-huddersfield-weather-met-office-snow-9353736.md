# Met Office hour-by-hour forecast for Leeds, Sheffield, York, Huddersfield and more as snow warning issued

## The Met Office is forecasting wintry showers for Leeds, Sheffield, Huddersfield and Bradford.

The yellow warning, which has a high (50-75%) likelihood and a moderate impact, will be in place for Sheffield, Huddersfield, Bradford and more from 00:00 to 11:00.

Sheffield is covered by the snow warning, with sleet and a temperature low of 2°C forecast during the warning period.

Leeds is currently just outside of the Met Office’s warning area, however again sleet is predicted with a low temperature of 1°C.

![A Met Office snow warning will be in force for parts of Yorkshire from 00:00 to 11:00 on Tuesday November 17.](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/jpim-static/image/2026/11/16/13/42/YouTube-Thumbnail-00-x-1080-px)-(26).jpg?trim=0,2,15,0&width=640&quality=65)


York and Whitby are also outside of the warning area. At present, the Met Office forecast is not showing wintry showers for these places.

The snow warning reads: Snow may cause travel disruption over higher ground early Tuesday.

“Some roads and railways likely to be affected with longer journey times by road, bus and train services. There is a small chance of power outages or degradation of mobile phone service.

“Snowy, wintry weather can cause delays and make driving conditions dangerous. Keep yourself and others safe by planning your route, giving yourself extra time for your journey. Check for road closures or delays to public transport and amend plans if necessary.

“If driving, make sure you have some essentials in your car in the event of any delays (e.g., warm clothing, food, water, a blanket, a torch, ice scraper/de-icer, a warning triangle, high visibility vest and an in-car phone charger).

“A band of rain is expected to head northwards across the UK during Monday night and Tuesday morning. As it encounters colder air across the highlighted region, it is likely to turn to snow for a time, mainly affecting locations at 100-200 m altitude or higher. Any snow falling below this level will likely be temporary and quickly melt. Where snow does accumulate, depths of 1-5 cm are likely; some locations above 300-400 m could see 5-15 cm.

“This may cause travel disruption on higher level routes, particularly in combination with a strong easterly wind which could also cause localised drifting. Some accretion of ice onto infrastructure such as power lines is also a possibility. Snow will gradually turn back to rain both from the south and through the morning on Tuesday, perhaps resulting in some minor disruption from surface water.”
