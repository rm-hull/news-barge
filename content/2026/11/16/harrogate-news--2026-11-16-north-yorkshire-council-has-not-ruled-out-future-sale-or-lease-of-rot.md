**North Yorkshire Council has confirmed it has not formally considered ruling out the future sale or lease of land at Rotary Wood, as the planning dispute over Harrogate Spring Water’s proposed factory expansion continues.**

The council was asked whether it could rule out selling or leasing the land, potentially preventing a future transaction involving the woodland while the planning appeal remains unresolved.

In response, North Yorkshire Council said it had not formally considered ruling out a sale or lease of the land.

> **North Yorkshire Council said:**
>
> The council has not formally considered ruling out the sale or lease of land at Rotary Wood.
>
> If a sale or lease is proposed in the future, it would be considered through the normal decision-making processes.

## Background to the planning dispute

Harrogate Spring Water, which is owned by multinational food and drink company Danone, has been seeking permission to expand its bottling facility on Harlow Moor Road in Harrogate.

The proposals have been the subject of a lengthy planning process, with outline planning permission granted in 2017. The subsequent reserved matters application dealt with the detailed aspects of the development, including its layout, scale, appearance, access and landscaping.

The expansion proposals would extend the existing facility towards Rotary Wood, an area of community woodland in the Pinewoods which was planted by local schoolchildren in 2005.

The plans have attracted considerable local opposition, with concerns focused particularly on the proposed loss of trees and the impact of the development on the surrounding landscape.

On 17 April 2026, North Yorkshire Council’s Harrogate and Knaresborough Area Planning Committee unanimously refused the reserved matters application, despite planning officers having recommended approval.

Councillors gave two principal reasons for refusal. They considered that the proposed landscaping was inadequate to compensate for the loss of existing planting and habitats, and that the appearance of the proposed extension lacked local distinctiveness and was unsuitable for its sensitive landscape setting.

## Planning appeal now under way

Harrogate Spring Water subsequently confirmed it would appeal against the council’s decision. The company has challenged the refusal through the Planning Inspectorate and has requested that the appeal be considered by way of a public inquiry.

The company has argued that the expansion is important to its future operations and would bring economic benefits to Harrogate, including more than 50 additional permanent jobs.

The appeal means the council’s reasons for refusing the detailed application will be examined through the planning appeal process. North Yorkshire Council has confirmed that it intends to defend its decision.

## Future of the council land

The council’s latest statement leaves open the possibility of a future sale or lease of land at Rotary Wood. However, it does not indicate that Harrogate Spring Water has made an offer, that negotiations are taking place or that the council is actively considering a disposal.

Instead, the authority says that any proposal to sell or lease the land would be considered through its normal decision-making processes.

The council has therefore not committed to ruling out a future transaction or to retaining the land permanently.

The distinction is important: the planning appeal concerns the refusal of the company’s development proposals, while any future decision about the sale or lease of council land would be a separate matter.
