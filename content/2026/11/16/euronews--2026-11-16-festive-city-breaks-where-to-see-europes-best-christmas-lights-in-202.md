## From London’s iconic illuminated streets to the Capitale de Noël Strasbourg, explore the European cities where Christmas lights turn winter evenings into something magical.

As winter settles in across Europe, destinations are preparing to light up the festive season with dazzling streets and illuminated historical landmarks.

Some displays follow centuries-old traditions, while others use bold contemporary design and large-scale installations to bring a new look to the festivities.

Many of the displays are free to enjoy, making them an accessible way to experience a winter city break during the colder months.

## Where to go in Europe to see Christmas lights

### Copenhagen, Denmark

Christmas comes early at the Tivoli Gardens in Copenhagen – well, sort of. Running now until 3 January, the Danish capital’s amusement park takes on a festive makeover for Christmas, with thousands of twinkling lights, illuminated trees, decorations, seasonal food stalls and plenty of rides.

### Ghent, Belgium

Ghent offers an atmospheric Christmas with a medieval centre that looks its best after dark. Illuminated dragons hang above bridges, their reflections shimmering in the canals below. The market winds through the old town with more than 150 wooden chalets, framed by the towers of St Bavo's Cathedral, the Belfry and Sint-Niklaaskerk. After exploring the market, visit the moated Gravensteen castle or join a chocolate tour.

### Ljubljana, Slovenia

In Ljubljana, Christmas comes with a story as stars, crystals and abstract patterns shimmer above the streets and squares of the old town. This year’s theme, “Chaos. In order”, invites visitors to look up and spot the patterns hidden within "apparent disorder". The displays will be on from 27 November to 14 January. For a different view, take a boat ride along the river and watch the city’s famous bridges glow from the water.

### London, England

London comes alive at Christmas and its best displays are scattered across the city, each with a character of its own. This year, Carnaby Street has brought a 1990s theme to Soho, while Regent Street’s famous glowing angels spread their wings above the traffic. Covent Garden shines with gold bells, giant baubles and more than 300,000 lights. Beyond the classic locations, head to Kensington to see "London's most Christmassy pub", the Churchill Arms, which is covered in real conifers and thousands of lights.

### Málaga, Spain

Christmas in Málaga brings mild winter sunshine, and Calle Larios will light up for the festive season on 27 November. Away from the city centre, La Concepción Botanical Gardens will glow with a light trail themed “Christmas in the Garden of Oz”. Hunt for handmade ceramics and nativity figures at the markets, and look out for the city’s various nativity scenes, including the huge display at City Hall.

### Munich, Germany

In Munich, the Christmas lights are at their most atmospheric on Marienplatz, where a towering tree glows against the Gothic façade of the New Town Hall. The tree is lit for the first time on 20 November, as the Christkindlmarkt opens, and you can browse the wooden stalls with a mug of Glühwein in hand.

### Paris, France

Start at the Arc de Triomphe in Paris, where you can look down the Champs-Élysées at two long rows of trees wrapped in Christmas lights. The avenue’s illuminations run from 22 November to 4 January, and its Christmas market returns this year after a decade away. Over on Boulevard Haussmann, stop at Galeries Lafayette to see the Christmas windows designed by French fashion designer Jean-Charles de Castelbajac. Step inside to see the department store’s giant Christmas tree beneath its stained-glass dome, then head along the boulevard to Printemps for its knitting-themed window displays.

### Prague, Czechia

Christmas in Prague, the City of a Hundred Spires, looks like a storybook come to life. In Old Town Square, the stalls gather beneath the astronomical clock, so you can warm up with a chimney cake while horse-drawn carriages roll past and music drifts from the stage. Climb the Old Town Hall tower for views over the Christmas market, then head to Charles Bridge at dusk to see the lamplighter during Advent.

### Riga, Latvia

Picture a fairy tale where the streets are cobbled, the air smells of mulled wine, and lights glow beneath a medieval skyline – that is Riga at Christmas. In the Old Town, strings of lights hang above the wooden stalls in Dome Square, where the city’s Christmas tree stands beside the cathedral.

### Strasbourg, France

Strasbourg's Christmas market has been running since 1570, making it the oldest in France and earning the city its title of the “Capitale de Noël”. Each December, facades, windows and balconies glow with light, and the scent of cinnamon and mulled wine drifts through the streets. A giant Christmas tree lights up Place Kléber, while hundreds of wooden chalets fill streets and squares across the city centre.

### Tallinn, Estonia

In Tallinn, Christmas begins beneath the towering tree in Town Hall Square, where wooden stalls fan out across the cobblestones and the air smells of glögi and fresh gingerbread. The tree lighting takes place on 20 November and the Town Hall Square market runs until 6 January. After dark, follow a lantern tour through the narrow streets and then finish with a spin on the Old Town Ice Rink on Harju Street.

### Vienna, Austria

Christmas feels like a waltz in Vienna: Elegant, unhurried and set against some of Europe's grandest streets. Illuminated chandeliers hang above the Graben, Kohlmarkt glows gold, and Kärntner Straße sparkles. Each Christmas market has its own personality, from the lively crowds at Rathausplatz to the smaller, prettier market at Belvedere.

### Zürich, Switzerland

In Zürich, Christmas arrives on Bahnhofstrasse, where thousands of shimmering light crystals hang above the shopping street. The display, called “Lucy” after The Beatles’ song "Lucy in the Sky with Diamonds", runs from 19 November to 4 January. Stroll towards the lake and you will pass sparkling shop windows. At the Wienachtsdorf, one the city’s largest Christmas markets, stalls offer handmade gifts and local delicacies, and a warm fondue in the chalet is the perfect way to end the evening.
