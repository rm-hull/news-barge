## Five EU nations announce Rwanda deportation hubs by mid-2027. Plus, EU aid chief Hadja Lahbib warns Russia is weaponising winter in Ukraine, Lithuania’s FM Kęstutis Budrys opposes Moscow talks before a ceasefire, and Robert Pattinson is revealed as the next James Bond.

## On today's show:

Five European Union member states have announced plans to set up deportation centres in Rwanda, marking a major shift in Europe’s push to toughen its migration policy. After months of negotiations, Greece, Denmark, Germany, Austria, and the Netherlands have confirmed plans to establish offshore deportation centres for irregular migrants in Rwanda. The first pilot facilities are set to become operational by mid-2027. We bring you the latest reactions and analysis on what the move means for European asylum policy.

EU humanitarian chief Hadja Lahbib told *Europe Tonight* that Russia is "turning winter into a weapon of war" as she unveiled a fresh package of humanitarian aid for Ukraine. She also refused to rule out a new wave of Ukrainian refugees entering Europe this winter.

In the same time, Lithuanian Foreign Minister Kęstutis Budrys told this programme that Europe should not speak to Moscow until a ceasefire is in place in Ukraine, following the EU’s appointment of peace negotiators last week. He also voiced scepticism over a new Austrian proposal on how Europe should approach peace talks.

And finally, After weeks of speculation, British actor Robert Pattinson has been officially revealed as the next 007, taking over from Daniel Craig as cinema’s most famous spy.
