A hiker in a Maine town couldn’t believe it when they found more than $55,000 in cash in the woods. Four years on from the unexpected discovery, Falmouth residents themselves are set to benefit.

The huge sum was discovered back in 2022, when a local happened upon a safe lying on the ground while strolling near the central fire station in Falmouth, around five miles from Portland.

​When police cut the safe open, they found $55,737 inside. Despite subsequent inquiries, no one claimed the money, Falmouth Police Chief John Kilbride explained to *The Portland Press Herald**.*

State law mandates that unclaimed money discovered by authorities must be used for a charitable purpose. Earlier this month, Falmouth Town Council voted to allocate the mammoth sum to the Low Income Home Energy Assistance Program, Council Chair Bryce Hach confirmed to WGME.

![After a passerby discovered $55,000 in the woods, the town of Falmouth in Maine has finally decided on how it wants to spend the money](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/16/16/2026-11-01T005507Z_51312066_RC2HEOARLVAN_RTRMADP_3_USA-ELECTION-MAINE.JPG?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&auto=webp)

​“It was certainly an intriguing way of getting funds,” Hach said. “This was not taxpayer money. This was not something that was the product of any thoughtful policy. This was literally a find in the woods.”

​Upon cutting open the safe and discovering the money inside, police set about checking whether authorities based in both Maine and New Hampshire had received reports of a large sum of money being stolen from a bank or person, Kilbride told the *Press Herald*.

​Falmouth police also posted notices in newspapers, but the cash remained unclaimed. The police chief said that authorities chose to hold on to the money - which he guessed could be related to a drug exchange - in case it had been stolen from the home of an elderly person who might not have noticed its absence.

“Cash of this magnitude is very unusual,” Kilbride said. “I’ve never experienced anything like that in my 30 years.”

Police confirmed to WGME that the money was traced to a New Hampshire bank, but how it found its way to the woods remains a mystery.

![Falmouth Town Council Chair Bryce Hach says that the money was a 'gift' to the community ahead of the winter](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/16/15/49/Screenshot-2026-11-16-at-15-26-44.png?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&trim=74%2C3%2C35%2C0&auto=webp)

The decision to allocate the funding comes just weeks away from the start of the winter. Around half of households in the Pine Tree State rely on heating oil to keep warm in the colder months, according to the American Community Survey.

​However, the cost of heating oil has risen in recent months, with prices hitting a record high at the end of October when they reached $6.05 per gallon, the Maine Department of Energy Resources says. As of November 9, that figure had dropped slightly to $5.84.

Hach told WGME that he hopes the money will be able to support families through the winter.

​​“Knowing how needed these funds are for heating right now, knowing how expensive fuel is for heating and knowing we’re going right into the winter,” he said. “This was a true gift for our community and one that literally fell in our lap.”

​Town manager Nathan Poore suggested to the *Press Herald* that the program could see $500 being granted to each applicant, with the remaining funds being used in subsequent handouts.

“This is the people’s money,” ​Kilbride said.
