## Trump is planning to discuss nuclear disarmament when he meets with Russian President Vladimir Putin and Chinese President Xi Jinping in Shenzhen, China this week.

US President Donald Trump said on Monday that he may meet with North Korean Supreme Leader Kim Jong-un in China this week.

Trump made the comments while speaking to reporters as he departed the White House to begin his trip to Asia, which will see him arrive in the Philippines early on Tuesday.

The president is then set to travel on to China, where he is scheduled to take part in trilateral talks with his Russian and Chinese counterparts Vladimir Putin and Xi Jinping.

"We may meet with Kim Jong-un also," Trump told the media. "So that would be good. We have the chance to accomplish a lot."

Trump is planning to discuss nuclear disarmament when he meets with Putin and Xi in Shenzhen in southeastern China this week, on the sidelines of the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (APEC) summit.

"We're going to talk about denuclearisation. Tough subject because it's very hard for any of us say we'll get rid of our nukes," Trump said on Monday. "Especially since you do have other countries."

He added that the wars in Iran and Ukraine would also come up, as well as "economics and trade."

In August, Trump talked up his relationship with Kim and said the pair would meet at some point in the year, although he gave no further details.

"The fact that I get along with him, that's a good thing not a bad thing. He has 57 very powerful nuclear weapons. Should have never allowed it to happen," the president said at the time, adding: "If I were president, I wouldn't have allowed it. But he's got them."

The director general of the International Atomic Energy Agency (IAEA), Rafael Grossi, said in September that the reported expansion of North Korea's production of weapons-grade nuclear material was a "cause for serious concern."
