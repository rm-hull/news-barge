A technical glitch that delayed departing flights on Monday afternoon has been resolved, United Airlines confirmed, coming days after a system failure at the Federal Aviation Administration forced a nationwide pause on air traffic.

The Chicago-headquartered carrier stated that airborne aircraft and its United Express regional services experienced no disruptions despite delays to certain departures.

In its statement, the company noted, "There is no indication that this was a cybersecurity-related issue."

United issued a public apology via social media for the disruption caused to travelers.

![Data from tracking service FlightAware recorded 418 delayed United flights by late Monday afternoon](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/16/22/2304604948..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Audio shared on the ATC platform, a service integrating live air traffic control radio feeds with real-time aircraft tracking, captured a United pilot in Houston asking permission from the tower to remain at the gate until system functionality returned.

Data from tracking service FlightAware recorded 418 delayed United flights by late Monday afternoon.

The incident is the latest in a series of high-profile technological failures across key industries in recent days.

Earlier in the day, thousands of people were unable to access online accounts at several leading U.S. banks following a technical issue at JPMorgan Chase.

On Friday, the FAA ordered a nationwide ground stop after a newly installed router malfunctioned, overloading the aging air traffic control infrastructure. A day prior, Microsoft suffered a major disruption to its Outlook service, which was fully restored by Saturday.
