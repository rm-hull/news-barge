**Residents across York and North Yorkshire are being asked for their views on next year’s council tax contributions towards policing, fire and rescue services, with the Mayor also considering introducing funding for local bus improvements.**

David Skaith, Mayor of York and North Yorkshire, has launched a public consultation on the additional funding needed to support North Yorkshire Police and North Yorkshire Fire and Rescue Service.

For the first time, the consultation also includes the possibility of adding a transport element to the mayoral precept, which would provide dedicated funding for bus services across the region.

The consultation opened on Monday 16 November. The Mayor says rising costs and increasing demand mean additional funding is required simply to maintain current service levels.

The aim, he says, is to keep any increases as low as possible while maintaining effective public services and delivering value for money. The Combined Authority says more than 80% of respondents to last year’s consultation supported this approach.

> **David Skaith, Mayor of York and North Yorkshire, said:**
>
> We want to keep precepts as low as possible, while making sure that our public services have the funding to deliver for you and to keep our county safe.
>
> For the first time, we’re looking at adding a small amount to invest in our bus services, focusing in particular on making it easier for young people to travel across our county.
>
> Before I make any decision, I want to hear from you. Please share your views on what this means for our police, fire, and transport services.

## Changes to policing and fire governance

The consultation comes after responsibility for policing and fire governance transferred to the Mayor in May 2024, following the creation of the York and North Yorkshire Combined Authority.

The former Police, Fire and Crime Commissioner role was abolished, with its responsibilities transferred into the new Mayoral arrangements.

The change brought oversight of policing and fire and rescue services under the Mayor, although North Yorkshire Police and North Yorkshire Fire and Rescue Service continue to operate as separate organisations.

With residents now being asked to consider further contributions through council tax, questions remain about the financial impact of the restructuring, including the costs of the new arrangements and any administrative savings achieved.

The latest consultation announcement does not provide a breakdown of those costs or identify the savings made since the transfer.

It also does not explain how the financial benefits of bringing these responsibilities together have been assessed or whether any efficiencies have reduced the overall cost to taxpayers.

## Funding for police and fire services

The Mayor says investment in policing has helped North Yorkshire remain one of the safest places in England and Wales, with the lowest police force area crime rate nationally, according to recent Home Office crime data.

The Combined Authority points to investment in neighbourhood policing, tackling crime and anti-social behaviour, and improving support for victims and vulnerable people.

For North Yorkshire Fire and Rescue Service, the authority says investment has helped provide financial stability and protect frontline services, while supporting improvements in prevention, road safety and operational capacity.

## Bus services included for the first time

The proposed transport element of the precept would provide local funding for improvements to bus services across York and North Yorkshire.

The Combined Authority says responses to its Local Transport Plan consultation have shown support for more affordable and accessible transport, better connections to work and education, and services that people can rely upon.

The Mayor says the proposed investment would particularly focus on improving travel opportunities for young people.

The consultation offers several options, ranging from the funding needed to maintain current service levels as costs rise to options that would allow further investment in local priorities.

However, the announcement does not set out the proposed funding amounts or explain what the different options would mean for individual council tax bills.

## What is a mayoral precept?

A precept is an amount added to household council tax bills to fund specific services.

There are two types of mayoral precept in York and North Yorkshire.

The Police and Crime Precept is ring-fenced for use by North Yorkshire Police. The Mayoral General Precept can be used in part or in whole to support North Yorkshire Fire and Rescue Service. The Mayor can also use it for other public purposes.

A mayoral precept funds services across the combined authority area, rather than just the individual council area in which a household pays its council tax.

## Have your say

The consultation is open to people who live, work or study in York and North Yorkshire.

Residents can submit their views through the York and North Yorkshire Combined Authority precept consultation.

The consultation closes at 10am on Monday 4 January 2027.

Following the consultation, the Mayor will consider the responses before presenting his proposals to the Police, Fire and Crime Panel and the Combined Authority at the start of the new year.

The consultation provides an opportunity for residents to comment on the proposed funding levels and whether they support additional investment in policing, fire and rescue services and local transport.
