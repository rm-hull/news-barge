Crowds lined the streets of Lima, Peru, on Sunday, 15 November, as the revered Lord of Miracles icon was carried by truck to Las Palmas Air Base, where Pope Leo XIV was due to celebrate Mass the following day. Worshippers waved papal flags and held portraits of the pontiff as the procession made its way through the capital.

One woman scattered flower petals as the icon passed. One of Peru's most cherished religious symbols, the Lord of Miracles was set to be present at the largest gathering of the Pope's visit to the country, on the final day of his 12-day Latin American tour.

Outside the air base, hundreds of Catholics had already begun queuing, prepared to spend the night waiting for a chance to get close to the Pope. Some sang and prayed, while others shared food they had brought with them. Street vendors sold rosaries, T-shirts, portraits and even plush toys depicting Leo XIV.

Some devotees described the overnight wait as a sacrifice worth making, while others expressed hopes that his visit would bring peace and unity. Meanwhile, volunteers entered the air base to prepare for Monday's Mass.
