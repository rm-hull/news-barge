An Army major who survived a deadly Iranian strike in Kuwait has hit out at the Pentagon over its handling of the war.

Speaking to Norah O’Donnell on CBS News’s revamped *60 Minutes* Sunday, Maj. Stephen Ramsbottom discussed the attack on the base at which he was stationed on March 1, the second day of the conflict.

Six of Ramsbottom’s fellow soldiers were killed in retaliatory Iranian drone strikes on Port Shuaiba, prompting the major to tell the show the facility’s fortifications were sub-par and had not been built to withstand drone bombardments.

Likening the Kuwaiti base to a trailer park, the whistleblower said: “The protection that was there was to stop car bombs and threats like that. There was nothing there designed to protect from an aerial attack.”

![Maj. Stephen Ramsbottom told 60 Minutes he had raised security concerns with his superiors ahead of a deadly attack on Kuwait](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/16/13/11/Stephen-Ramsbottom.png?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&trim=4%2C416%2C0%2C30&auto=webp)

Concerns about troops’ safety had been raised with unit commander Brig. Gen. Clint Barnes, he said, adding that he personally had issued a warning to Maj. Gen. John Hinson, who is responsible for logistics for U.S. Central Command.

“I brought it up in twice in various meetings, basically saying, ‘Hey, I don’t see how we’re protected here,’” the major said, recalling that he was told: “We don’t have to worry about protection.”

“I feel this is a tactical failure by the Army, our leadership, and myself – all the way down to my level,” Ramsbottom said. “We just did not plan this well, and this needs to be a lesson that we learn.”

The major claimed to have seen his base on a map of likely Iranian targets compiled by U.S. intelligence before it was struck, adding: “I saw those reports, there was definitely an X where we were.”

![Six U.S. service members were killed in the Iranian attack on Port Port Shuaiba in Kuwait on March 1. From top left: Sgt. Declan Coady, Master Sgt. Nicole Amor, Maj. Cody Khork, Master Sgt. Noah Tietjens, Maj. Jeffrey O’Brien and Chief Warrant Officer 3 Robert Marzan](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/06/20/Iran_US_Port_Shuaiba_Attack_Victims_79645.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Ramsbottom further claimed he was promised a counter drone system that never arrived: “We don’t know if it was ever even ordered or requested”.

Ramsbottom has filed a whistleblower complaint with the House and Senate Armed Services Committees claiming that negligence among leadership was the true cause of the fatalities suffered in Kuwait, also pointing out the port had “no physician, no fixed aid station, and only one ambulance” with which to care for its wounded.

Connecticut Democratic Sen. Richard Blumenthal, the ranking member on the Senate’s Permanent Subcommittee on Investigations, said Sunday he had reviewed the complaint and written to Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth demanding “full disclosure about the Port Shuaiba attack.”

“The families of the six fallen servicemembers, the wounded survivors, and the American people deserve full transparency and accountability. It is time for a reckoning,” he added.

The Pentagon declined to speak to *60 Minutes*about Ramsbottom’s filing.* The Independent* has reached out to the White House for comment.

![Ramsbottom hit out at “tactical failures” by the Army and its leadership](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/05/15/download.-4..jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Another contributor to the show, Joey Amor, the widowed husband of Master Sgt. Nicole Amor, who was killed in the same attack, told *60 Minutes*: “If I’m being frank, it’s – it’s on them. They killed my wife.

“They killed these soldiers. And it’s not political. There are individuals in that command that made choices.”

Amor’s intervention on behalf of his late wife recalls the campaign fought by family members of crew aboard the USS *Abraham Lincoln*, who were forced to lobby the Navy about the poor conditions on board that vessel during its recent deployment.

Elsewhere in the special, recapping eight months of investigative reporting, O’Donnell revealed that Iran had struck at least 31 U.S. military and diplomatic sites in eight countries across the Middle East since the war began.

“Our analysis of satellite imagery and new video shows the scale of destruction the Pentagon has never fully disclosed,” she said.

Hegseth was forced to defend his record on the Iran war earlier this month in response to efforts to impeach him over his handling of the conflict.

![Pete Hegseth has defended his record on the Iran war amid efforts to impeach him over the conflict](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/10/23/13/GettyImages-2304464390.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The secretary called the war “a courageous and historic effort that will ensure Iran never gets nuclear weapons”, and branded the impeachment attempt a “sham”.

Reacting to the failed attempt, Trump also insisted the war had been a “success” and that it was “very unfair” for his political enemies to pursue the secretary.

He has repeatedly said in his post-midterms analysis that he would fight the war all over again if he had to because it was necessary to prevent Tehran from obtaining a nuclear weapon, a claim Hegseth has also made, warning of the danger posed to the West by “crazy Islamists.”

Meanwhile, Iran’s Revolutionary Guard Corps fired six more drones toward a U.S. air defense radar facility in Bahrain Sunday, calling into question Trump’s recent claim that the conflict – which cost his fellow Republicans so badly at this month’s midterms – is “effectively over.”
