Forecasters have issued a yellow warning for snow across areas in North Yorkshire, with wintry weather bringing the potential for travel disruption.

The warning, which stretches from Staffordshire in the West Midlands to south-west Scotland, will be in force from 12am until 11am on Tuesday.

Some roads and railways are likely to be affected, and there is a "small chance" of power outages and an impact on mobile phone service, the Met Office said.




The affected area covers much of Yorkshire, as well as Derbyshire, Northumberland, Greater Manchester, Cumbria and Lancashire.

Forecasters said a band of rain is expected to head northwards across the UK through Monday night and Tuesday morning which will likely turn to snow after it encounters colder air in the warning area.

Locations at 100-metre altitude or higher will mainly be affected, with any snow falling below this level likely to be temporary and quickly melting, the Met Office said.

In areas where snow does accumulate, depths of 1-5cm are likely with some locations above 300-400 metres potentially seeing 5-15cm, it added.

Met Office chief forecaster Dan Suri said: “The greatest chance of settling snow will be over ground above 200 metres, with 1-5 cm possible in places and potentially 5-15 cm above 300-400 metres.

“Combined with strong easterly winds, this could bring some travel disruption to higher-level routes.

“Through Tuesday morning the snow will increasingly turn back to rain, with some minor surface-water impacts also possible.

“Away from higher ground, and so for many people, rain rather than snow will be the main story, although it will continue to feel cold in the north.

“Temperatures will be closer to average further south, before more settled conditions gradually develop here late this week.”

It comes after the UK saw its coldest night of the season so far on Sunday, with a low of minus 7.7C recorded at Tulloch Bridge in the Scottish Highlands.

The UK Health Security Agency (UKHSA) has issued a yellow cold health alert for the North West, North East and Yorkshire and the Humber, in place until 9am on Thursday.

These alerts mean weather conditions could result in an increased use of healthcare services and a greater risk to life for vulnerable people, the agency said.
