A former North Yorkshire Police inspector has been sentenced after being convicted of exposure and public order offences.

Matthew Millington, a retired police inspector who was employed as a police staff member at the time, committed the offences ​​in August 2025.

On 29 August, Millington approached a woman in Scarborough and made sexual comments towards her.

Two days later, he was seen in the Crossgates area of the town with his trousers lowered and masturbating.

He pleaded guilty to exposure at York Crown Court on 16 September.

Millington received an 18-month community order. He was also required to complete 50 hours of unpaid work, notified that he will or may be barred from working with children and vulnerable adults, and made subject to the notification requirements of the Sexual Offences Act 2003 for five years.

He was also ordered to pay £500 prosecution costs.

But he denied an offence of using threatening, abusive or insulting words or behaviour with intent to cause harassment, alarm or distress.

Millington was found guilty by Teesside Magistrates. Today he was sentenced for the public order offence and given a six-month community order, which will run concurrently with the previous order.

He was also made subject to a six-month prohibited steps order banning him from entering the place where the offence took place, ordered to pay £500 in compensation to the victim, along with £200 costs and a £114 surcharge.

## ‘Breach of trust’

Millington, who was based at Scarborough Police Station, resigned from his police staff role before any court appearances.

Following the sentencing, Detective Superintendent Mark Hosking, Head of Professional Standards at North Yorkshire Police said: “The public rightly expect police officers and staff to uphold the highest standards of honesty, integrity and professionalism, both on and off duty.

“Matthew Millington’s actions represented a serious breach of the trust placed in him and were completely incompatible with the standards expected of those who serve the public.

“I would like to thank the victims and witnesses in this case for coming forward and reporting what they saw.

“Although they felt their information may not have been significant, their reports enabled us to secure key evidence, make a swift arrest and bring the case before the courts.

“Violence against women and girls remains a priority for North Yorkshire Police. No matter how small a concern may seem, we will always listen, take reports seriously and act on the information provided.

“We remain committed to ensuring that any officer or member of staff who commits a criminal offence or falls below the standards expected by the public is robustly investigated and held accountable for their actions.”
