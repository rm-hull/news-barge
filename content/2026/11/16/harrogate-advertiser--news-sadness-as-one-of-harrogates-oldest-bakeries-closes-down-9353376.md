There has been sadness after one of Harrogate’s oldest traditional bakers closed down after being run by the same family for nearly 90 years.

Famous in the town for homemade cakes, pastries, breakfast items and well-filled traditional sandwiches, Higginsons was founded in 1939 and has continued successfully through 17 members of the same family at 21–22 Regent Parade all the way from the Second World War.

A highly-regarded institution of Harrogate’s traditional retail sector, the bakery’s present day owner Fredrick Brooksbanks cited soaring electricity costs, rising wage bills, lack of a successor, and general unsustainability as the reasons for the closure.

One regular customer said: “Higginsons has been an institution for working people for so many years.

“Just think of the millions of delicious bacon, breakfast roll sandwiches they must have made since they first opened their doors.

“The staff were lovely and always remembered their customers by name.

“It’s sad that the true traditional British loaf is disappearing even in cultured Harrogate”.

Higginsons shut down its furnace for the final time on Friday, November 13 while its doors closed at 2pm.

Traditional bakers are beginning to become a thing of the past in the Harrogate area.

Among the few remaining ones still flying the flag are the Avenue bakery in Bilton in Harrogate and Hirsts on the High Street in Knaresborough.
