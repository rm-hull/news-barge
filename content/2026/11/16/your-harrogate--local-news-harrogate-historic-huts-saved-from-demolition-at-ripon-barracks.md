Two wooden huts where British and Allied army engineers trained during the Second World War have been saved from demolition at Deverell Barracks in Ripon.

Following a three-year campaign by Ripon Military Heritage Trust, site owner Homes England has agreed to dismantle the huts and give them to the trust. Demolition work at the barracks is due to begin early in 2027.

The trust says the barracks are the country’s last surviving intact wooden-hutted militia camp dating from 1939.

The buildings chosen for preservation are the lecture hut and one of the camp’s two instruction huts. According to the trust, they are the only known examples of their type.

![](https://mmo.aiircdn.com/409/6afad95b54d6db6265b.png)

The trust plans to store the huts while it looks for a site where it can rebuild them and tell the story of Ripon’s School of Military Engineering and its role in the war.

Guy Wilson, chairman of Ripon Military Heritage Trust, said:

> “We are most grateful to Homes England for giving us this opportunity to preserve these unique buildings.
>
> "Now the work begins to store them safely and work with planners, politicians and the community to find an appropriate site on which to rebuild them to tell Ripon’s fascinating military history.”

The trust has also been able to keep items from the barracks, including beds, cupboards, a cell door and an air raid siren.

Homes England has given the trust the opportunity to dismantle and keep four metal Twynham huts before demolition begins. These Cold War huts replaced the Nissen hut and are closely associated with the Corps of Royal Engineers.

Tom Baker, senior planning and enabling manager at Homes England, said:

> “As part of our planning for the redevelopment of Deverell Barracks, we have worked with the RMHT to make a range of original materials available for reuse, including former military buildings, furniture and documents.
>
> "We recognise the Trust's ambition to create an educational facility that reflects the history of the site and are pleased to have been able to contribute to that effort.”
