This year’s flu is surging earlier than usual in the U.S. - but federal health officials stress that doesn’t mean the season will be more severe.

Some 24,000 positive flu tests were recorded since early October, as well as 82 deaths, according to the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention. Positive test numbers during the same time period in past seasons reached only a few thousand.

At least 2.6 million flu cases, 36,000 hospital admissions and 2,100 deaths have been confirmed this season, which began in August. Whereas there were only 780,000 illnesses, 8,000 hospitalizations and 490 fatalities at this time in 2023, the last time the CDC reported total cases at this point of the year.

Louisiana, Texas and South Carolina are seeing the highest flu levels so far, and cases are elevated in nearly a dozen other states. But high numbers may not define the rest of the season, the agency points out.

“This early-season influenza activity is currently higher than typically observed at this time of year, but an earlier increase in activity does not necessarily lead to a higher seasonal peak or greater overall severity,” the CDC stated.

## What’s ahead?

This season is expected to be similar to last year for all ages, a CDC outlook predicted in September.

Flu activity commonly peaks between December and February and the holidays raise the risk of spread. Hospital admissions and ER visits will likely rise nationally by the end of the month, the CDC said, but it’s too soon to tell what’s ahead long-term.

The season usually runs through May.

“It is too early to know what viruses may predominate or to say whether there might be implications on flu vaccine effectiveness this season,” the CDC noted.

## Vaccination now

One major factor in determining the flu’s impact is vaccination levels, which have fallen in recent years. Less than 44 percent of Americans received a flu shot last year compared to more than half of Americans in 2021, CDC data shows.

![A map shows the current flu impact across the US. Levels are high in three southern states, including Louisiana, Texas and South Carolina](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/16/14/12/FLU-MAP.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

This year, vaccines are expected to offer good protection against circulating variants of the flu. Vaccines prevented an estimated 180,000 hospitalizations during the 2024-2025 season.

The early surge means people need to get their shots now, doctors say.

“The flu shot remains the most powerful tool available to help prevent serious illness caused by the influenza virus,” Dr. Michael Ben-Aderet, associate director of Hospital Epidemiology at Cedars-Sinai Medical Center, said in a September statement.

“I urge everyone eligible to receive the vaccine to get it.”

*The Independent* has asked the CDC for current flu vaccination data.
