## King Philippe had refused De Wever's resignation earlier this month and asked him to continue with budget talks, giving him until 16 November to solve the impasse.

Belgium's King Philippe has given Prime Minister Bart De Wever more time to work out a new cost-cutting budget with his coalition partners after the latest round of talks failed to produce an agreement.

"The king has asked the prime minister to continue the negotiations with a view to reaching an agreement as soon as possible and thus avoiding a damaging political and budgetary crisis for the country," the Royal Palace said on Monday.

De Wever, a Flemish conservative, will report back to the king on 23 November, it added.

The prime minister had informed the monarch in an audience earlier on Monday that he had "not yet managed to reconcile the differing views within his coalition on the budget, but that there is still a willingness to seek solutions together," according to the Palace statement.

Philippe had refused De Wever's resignation earlier this month and asked him to continue with budget talks, giving him until 16 November to solve the impasse.

De Wever is seeking €10 billion by 2029 to plug a deficit gap, but coalition parties are at odds over how to find the funds.

Measures put forward have included spending cuts and a VAT increase.

Weekend talks had seen four of the five governing parties agree on the broad outlines of a deal.

But the leftist Vooruit party stood firm, arguing that high earners should have to contribute more to public finances to help offset some of the cuts.
