Australia’s second-largest retailer has been targeted in a cyber-attack, though it is still too early to tell whether any of its data has been stolen or compromised.

Coles revealed the threat on Monday morning, saying it was investigating with support from the Australian Cyber Security Centre and global security experts.

“Coles Group Limited is currently investigating a cyber security incident,” the company said in a statement to the stock exchange.

“We have not identified any evidence that customer, team or supplier data has been compromised or removed from Coles’ systems.

“Operations continue and customers are able to shop as normal with Coles in stores and online.”

Coles apologised to its customers for any concerns they might have at “a time of increasing cyber incidents in Australia and around the world”.

“We have security measures in place to protect customer and supplier accounts and are continuing to closely monitor our system,” it said.

“We recommend you regularly update your password with a strong, unique password for your account and not reuse the same password across multiple services.”

Coles also warned customers to be cautious if they received unexpected emails or SMS messages purporting to be from the retailer, and not to click any links.

In a separate and unrelated incident, Woolworths said it was aware of reports from customers who purchased compromised e-gift cards. The ABC first reported on Monday some customers had lost hundreds of dollars after buying the company’s Everyday WISH gift cards through its rewards website or app, only to find the sums loaded on to them had been spent by someone else.

Some customers told the national broadcaster they had lost up to $500, with transactions showing up at stores in different states to where they purchased the cards.

“We are aware of reports of an issue affecting a number of customer e-gift cards and are looking into the matter as a priority,” a Woolworths spokesperson said in a statement. “We recognise the concern this has caused affected customers and we apologise to customers impacted. We encourage any customer who notices a transaction they don’t recognise to contact our Gift Card Support team.”

The company says it is working through all reported issues and will look into replacing affected e-gift cards.
