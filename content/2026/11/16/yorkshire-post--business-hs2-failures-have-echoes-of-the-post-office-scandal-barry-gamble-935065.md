# HS2 failures have echoes of the Post Office scandal: Barry Gamble

## Yorkshire should have been celebrating the opening of the HS2 High Speed rail service next month.

December 2026 was earmarked for trains to be running from London Euston to Birmingham with legs through to Manchester and Leeds to follow in the early 2030s offering a London-Leeds journey-time of just 81 minutes.

But there will be no such celebrations. Originally envisaged to cost £30bn for the line through to the North of England, thus far £40bn has been spent on a line now to end near Birmingham. This reduced service is not expected to be in operation before 2036 with connections to the existing rail network probably not before 2043; 17 years hence.

Meanwhile the Treasury has confirmed HS2 spending will continue at the rate of £7bn per annum such that an overall cost well in excess of £100bn is in prospect.

So this one-time flagship project is being halved in scope, allowed to be more than a decade and a half late while costing more than three times its original budget. Any cost/benefit analysis which underpinned its construction is long since shredded.

Meanwhile the HS2 board is undertaking a ‘reset’. They conclude that the project has to be completed since the cost of not continuing exceeds that of completion but a recent article in industry journal Construction News questions this key assertion.

HS2 might expect that we admire the gleaming concrete structures now in place but in taking land and property along the route, from landowners and individual householders, HS2 have created problems aplenty.

The route through North London and beyond has displaced many from their homes while in open countryside sliced and diced lots of farming businesses. Many of these have yet to have fair compensation agreed, are unclear as to the precise boundaries with the HS2 land and continue to suffer nuisance from road diversions, noise and heavy construction traffic.

The nuisance also affects many more living near the huge number of construction sites opened up along the route.

Had land boundaries and fair compensation been agreed and paid in early course and construction completed in line with the original timetable those affected would no doubt have been philosophical about the impact on their lives and livelihoods recognising the wider benefit to the economy from this transport infrastructure.

As it is many landowners and other residents remain in despair as to the poor communication and obfuscation which has characterised their dealings with HS2, over many years now, and with no end in sight. Over the last five years I have seen this at first hand after being approached to advise a number of affected landowners and their agents. They struggle to have meaningful engagement with the behemoth which is High Speed Two (HS2) Limited and the various international construction contractors through whom it operates.

Culturally one might sense disturbing echoes of the Post Office Horizon scandal where an aloof board allowed an all powerful contractor to mark its own homework and was totally disconnected from the impact of its operations on key stakeholders. Whether these might be the taxpayer, the would-be rail traveller or affected landowners it’s difficult to conclude, that as we approach a date which should have been a cause for celebration, the HS2 governance and its underpinning culture is much, if any, different.

Barry Gamble is an experienced board director and advisor and chairman of Boardroom City Debates. He is a member of the 2025 Institute of Directors Commission ‘NEDs reimagined’ and of the 2024 IOD working party to the report ‘The Post Office Scandal, a failure of governance’.
