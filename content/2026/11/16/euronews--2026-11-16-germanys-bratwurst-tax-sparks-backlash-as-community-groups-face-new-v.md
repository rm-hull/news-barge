## Under new rules, public bodies such as municipalities and towns are to be generally liable for VAT when they engage in commercial activities.

Controversy is brewing in Germany over the country's so-called "bratwurst tax."

From 1 January, new VAT rules are set to apply across all German municipalities, potentially meaning that key local community groups such as volunteer fire brigades could have to pay tax on sales of things such as bratwursts at village festivals and other local events.

The changes stem from section 2b of Germany's VAT Act, which has been repeatedly delayed but which is now expected to come into force. Under the rules, public bodies such as municipalities and towns are to be generally liable for VAT when they engage in commercial activities.

That means if a volunteer fire brigade operates as part of the municipality and not as an independent association, VAT would have to be paid on sales at local events.

Since 1 January 2026, a reduced tax rate of 7% has applied to food sold at snack bars or in restaurants. However, those affected have to keep detailed records of their sales and issue receipts.

On the website "Kommunal.de," Christian Erhardt-Maciejewski has taken a satirical swipe at the tax in a piece titled "Hurrah, the tax office is grilling the fire brigade."

He writes: "I can already imagine what will happen now: the most resourceful brigades will set up a support association so that they can carry on selling sausages. The fire brigade festival becomes an association festival, the grill master turns into a treasurer, mustard becomes a question for the statutes. A whole new form of volunteering is born in Germany: finding ways to dodge the tax authorities."

In the Schleswig-Holstein state parliament, the CDU, Greens and FDP are calling for the new rules to be put on hold or for additional exemptions to be introduced.

"If the bratwurst at the Easter bonfire becomes a case for the tax office, something has gone seriously wrong," Christopher Vogt, the chair of the FDP parliamentary group, said, per NDR.

Vogt urged the state government in Kiel to "urgently provide support for local volunteers."

There has already been some movement in North Rhine-Westphalia, where the state's finance minister, Marcus Optendrenk, reportedly issued a decree clarifying that bratwurst sales by volunteer fire brigades are to be exempt from VAT.

"Anyone who volunteers with the local fire brigade deserves a tailwind rather than paperwork," Optendrenk said.
