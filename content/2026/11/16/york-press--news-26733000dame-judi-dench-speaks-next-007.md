An acclaimed Oscar-winning actress from York has added her views to those of hundreds of others waiting for the announcement of the next actor lined up to play one of cinemas most iconic roles.

Dame Judi Dench, who played M in eight James Bond films, said the next 007 should be a fresh face before it was confirmed that Robert Pattinson would be taking on the role.

According to digital entertainment brand Page Six, speaking in an interview with British Vogue, Dame Judi said: "A young actor we’ve not yet heard of," in response to being asked who should take on the title role.




The 91-year-old star of stage and screen has long been associated with the franchise, having portrayed the head of MI6 during Pierce Brosnon and Daniel Craig's occupancy of the role of intelligence officer.

Robert Pattinson and Nicholas Hoult have reportedly screen-tested for the part of the suave British spy, according to Page Six.  
  
Other names mentioned in the reporting include Josh O’Connor, Callum Turner, Jack Lowden, Harry Styles and Theo James.

Page Six reports that reflecting on her time as M, Dame Judi told The Times: "I loved playing M because it was great to be able to tell all those men where to get off; put them in their place.

"You’re a sexist, misogynist dinosaur," she says to Pierce Brosnan's character at their first meeting.

"I was drunk with power.

"I also got to wear some very nice suits and pretend I knew what I was talking about and to muck about with gadgets when, in real life, I can’t even work the ironing board."

Despite her deep connection to the character, according to Page Six Dame Judi told The Times she wasn’t upset when her time with the franchise came to an end.

She said to the national newspaper: "Of course I didn’t cry.

"Why on earth would I cry after 20 glorious years and eight Bond films?

"Which, by the way, is one more than Roger Moore, the longest-running 007 to date."

Dame Judi’s comments come following Daniel Craig’s departure in 2021.

Mr Craig played the role across five films, including the 2012 blockbuster Skyfall.

The next Bond film, directed by Denis Villeneuve, is set for release in 2028.
