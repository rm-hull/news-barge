Forecasters are warning of disruption with up to 15cm of snow set to fall in parts of the UK, as temperatures plummet and weather warnings come into force.

A yellow warning for snow is in place from 12am to 11am on Tuesday and stretches from the West Midlands to southern Scotland, with the Met Office warning of potential travel disruption.

In a cold start for this week, the forecaster predicts wintry showers in the far north east while elsewhere faces persistent rain, and snow for northern hills on Monday night.

Temperatures are expected to get as low as 0C in Aberdeen while the mercury rises slightly overnight, into what Met Office meteorologist Aidan McGivern described as an “unsettled picture” on Tuesday.

Forecasters have said a band of rain is expected to head northwards across the UK through Monday night and Tuesday morning which will likely turn to snow after it encounters colder air in the warning area.

![Some snow is expected in the north of England and Scotland on Tuesday](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/16/11/04/Screenshot-2026-11-16-at-11-15-25.png?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Locations at 100-metre altitude or higher will mainly be affected, with any snow falling below this level likely to be temporary and quickly melting, the Met Office said.

In areas where snow does accumulate, depths of 1-5cm are likely with some locations above 300-400 metres potentially seeing 5-15 cm, it added.

Forecasters have warned of potential travel disruption on higher-altitude routes, with the possibility of ice forming on infrastructure like power lines.

![A yellow weather warning for snow is in place for Tuesday](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/15/22/31/Screenshot-2026-11-15-220303.png?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Wednesday is expected to be breezy with a mix of rain, showers, and bright spells across the UK. Temperatures will be around 11-13C across the south east and west of England, while further north the mercury dips below 10C with lows of 6C in Lerwick.

Meanwhile, the south will see scattered showers in the south on Thursday and Friday before it becomes sunnier and colder in the north. As it gets colder towards the end of the week,

Cold weather health alerts were issued by the UK Health Security Agency (UKHSA) for many places in England - including the North West, West Midlands and Yorkshire and The Humber. They will be in place from 9pm on Sunday until 9am on Thursday.

![The forecast for 4:30am on Tuesday morning. Grey and white areas indicate snowfall, and blue areas indicate rain](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/15/22/11/Screenshot-2026-11-15-220119.png?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

These alerts mean weather conditions could result in an increased use of healthcare services and a greater risk to life for vulnerable people, the agency said.

Ethan Douglas, operational meteorologist at the Met Office, said the cold conditions could lead to an ice risk on the roads, causing “potentially hazardous travelling conditions”.

He told the Press Association: “On Sunday night and Monday morning, there is the potential for some disruptive ice (given the preceding rain and cold night) and snow.

“The snow risk will be constrained to Scottish hills, bringing difficult driving conditions there.

“The ice risk may be present more widely across the north of the UK, causing extended journey times and potentially hazardous travelling conditions.”
