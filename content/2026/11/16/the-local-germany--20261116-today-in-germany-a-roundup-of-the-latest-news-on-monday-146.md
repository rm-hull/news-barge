**Monday's top story:Berlin records highest number of diphtheria cases since records began**

Berlin has reported eight confirmed cases of diphtheria this year, the most since data collection began in 2001, according to the State Office for Health and Social Affairs (Lageso). Two people have died.

Seven of the eight infections are linked to an outbreak that mainly affects homeless people, and the result for one case is still pending. Lageso said the skin form of the disease is often associated with poor hygiene, which can frequently be linked to homelessness, alcohol abuse or drug abuse. Seven patients had skin diphtheria, while one had the respiratory form.

Before 2024, Berlin never recorded more than one case a year. That year, cases rose to five. Across Germany, the Robert Koch Institute (RKI) has counted 46 cases so far this year.

Once known as the "angel of death for children", diphtheria is now rare thanks to vaccination. The RKI says the vaccine protects reliably against symptoms but not against infection. It puts the risk of death at five to ten percent, and the risk is higher for children under five and adults over 40.

The Standing Committee on Vaccination (Stiko) recommends vaccination for everyone. Infants have three doses, and boosters follow at ages five to six and nine to 17. Adults need a booster every ten years.

**German armed forces report sharp rise in soldiers with mental health problems**

The number of Bundeswehr soldiers suffering from mental health problems has risen significantly over the past year, according to the defence ministry's representative for post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD).

Peter Zimmermann told the Redaktionsnetzwerk Deutschland that PTSD cases have climbed by about 50 percent, from 186 to 279. Reports of other mission-related conditions rose by roughly 40 percent, from 112 in 2024 to 159 in 2025.

In total, the number of people affected has grown from 298 to 438. "That's quite a lot," he said.

Zimmermann conceded that simplified statistical tracking may partly explain the increase. He thinks a bigger factor is that images from the wars in Iran and Ukraine are acting as triggers, intensifying symptoms and prompting soldiers to seek treatment. They also revive memories for veterans of Afghanistan, Kosovo and Mali.

Stigma remains a serious obstacle. Soldiers fear that comrades will no longer take them seriously, that their careers will stall or that they will be seen as "wimps". They also worry about their families. Zimmermann said such fears are no longer justified but still linger.

The psychiatrist said four to five years typically pass between a traumatic event and the start of treatment, which is "far too long". "People need to seek help sooner," he said.

**SPD MPs push back against plan for deportation centre in Rwanda**

Several MPs from the centre-left Social Democrats (SPD) are opposing a planned deportation centre in Rwanda, to be set up by Germany and four other EU countries.

Interior Minister Alexander Dobrindt (CSU) is due to meet partner countries in Vienna on Monday. According to diplomats, they have already agreed to open the first "return hub" in Rwanda.

SPD domestic policy expert Sebastian Fiedler told Politico he cannot back the plan unreservedly, as he has "far too many questions" and hears much criticism within his parliamentary group.

He said a model "with a dictator ruling the country" raises unanswered questions, and it is unclear whether it would survive a court challenge.

SPD lawmakers Rasha Nasr and Hakan Demir told the Tagesspiegel: "We must stop Dobrindt's plans." They say the SPD was not consulted, and that deportations should go to countries of origin or places with which people have a connection.

Germany is working with Denmark, Austria, the Netherlands and Greece.

**AfD nominates 1990 World Cup winner Thomas Berthold for federal president**

The far-right Alternative for Germany (AfD) has nominated former World Cup winner Thomas Berthold as its candidate for federal president.

Party leaders Alice Weidel and Tino Chrupalla said on X that the 62-year-old, a "world champion without a party membership card", is "ideally suited to be Federal President for all Germans".

Berthold began his career at Eintracht Frankfurt in the early 1980s, then played in Italy, for Bayern Munich and, until 2000, VfB Stuttgart. He won 64 caps and lifted the 1990 World Cup. More recently he has been known for appearing at protests against Covid-19 measures, which Chrupalla praised.

Stephan Mayer (CSU) told the Rheinische Post that Berthold had made "extremely questionable and crude remarks" during the pandemic and belongs to the "Querdenker" movement. He called him an outstanding footballer but "not a serious candidate for the highest office in the land".

SPD politicians mocked the move. Dirk Wiese said: "Every team has its idiot." Ralf Stegner called it "a crazy idea".

Frank-Walter Steinmeier's term ends early next year. Ilse Aigner (CSU), backed by the CDU/CSU, SPD and Greens, is considered certain to be elected on 30 January.
