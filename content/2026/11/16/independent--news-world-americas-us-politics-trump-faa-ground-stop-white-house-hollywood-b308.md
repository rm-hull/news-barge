As a nationwide ground stop brought American air travel to a halt last Friday, stranding tens of thousands of passengers, President Donald Trump was in the Oval Office meeting the actor Jon Voight and calling a series of Hollywood figures to discuss legislation to revive the entertainment industry, according to a new report.

The president spent the opening hours of the crisis huddled with the Oscar-winning actor, a longtime Republican who has been outspoken about his support of Trump for years, *The Atlantic* reported.

The pair were reportedly working the phones to buttonhole a roster of show-business power players about the bill, even as airports nationwide descended into chaos, leading one of the people they called to reportedly ask Trump whether his focus might be better spent on the air-travel emergency.

Instead of addressing the emergency, Trump posted on Truth Social about the legislation - titled the Motion Picture, Television, and Entertainment Revitalization Act - and complained that the film and television industry was “MADE IN AMERICA, and must STAY IN AMERICA.”

He did not address the disastrous travel meltdown until days later, when he blamed the equipment and his predecessor, former President Joe Biden. The outage was the fault of “very old” and “broken” hardware, some of it more than 50 years old, he told reporters, and of a Biden administration he accused of having wired “copper into glass.”

![The president spent the opening hours of the FAA crisis huddled with Oscar-winning actor Jon Voight (left), a longtime Republican who has been outspoken about his support of Trump for years](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/16/21/1197128590..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

He praised Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy and the FAA’s handling of the shutdown, said no one was hurt, and took no responsibility himself.

“I don’t take any responsibility,” Trump said of the episode. “They did a great job.”

Trump’s apparent preference for Hollywood is nothing new. He has long courted entertainment-industry figures and talked up plans to use federal policy to revive film and television production in the U.S.

He lashed out at journalists on Monday as he departed the White House for a trip to Asia when he was asked about why his administration is pumping tens of millions of dollars into White House renovations and other Washington, D.C. projects while demanding billions from Congress to repair the nation’s air traffic control systems.

![A four-hour nationwide ground stop brought American air travel to a halt last Friday, stranding tens of thousands of passengers including at New York’s LaGuardia (pictured)](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/16/17/35/GettyImages-2304497343.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

He singled out NBC News correspondent Yamiche Alcindor for particular scorn, calling her “pathetic” and suggested she lacked “talent” after she questioned his spending priorities, while defending his “flawless” White House renovations that he said cost “peanuts.”

“You’re so pathetic. I have a responsibility to make sure the symbol of our country is in decent shape. It takes 10 minutes a day,” Trump said. “This doesn’t cost money. This is peanuts. And I pay for it. This doesn’t cost money. This is peanuts. This is called talent. Talent. You don’t have talent. You don’t know what a broken column is. … I spent very little time to do it and losers like you come out and say, ‘Why are you focused on the White House?’”

Writing on Truth Social a day earlier, Trump had echoed Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy’s demand for as much as $50 billion for air traffic upgrades.

“If Congress approves the desperately needed money for Air Traffic Control, we can get it built quickly and efficiently,” he said. “It will be the BEST SYSTEM anywhere in the World. Much of our current air control system is 50 years old — RIDICULOUS!”
