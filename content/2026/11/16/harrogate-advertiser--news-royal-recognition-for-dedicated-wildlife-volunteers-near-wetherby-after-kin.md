A group of volunteers in a village near Wetherby has been awarded the equivalent of an MBE.

East Keswick Wildlife Trust has been awarded The King’s Award for Voluntary Service for 2026.

For the past 36 years, EKWT and its army of volunteers have worked to safeguard local sites of wildlife importance and have established five local nature reserves, all with public access.

David Smith, Chairman of East Keswick Wildlife Trust, said: “The King’s Award for Voluntary Service is much more than an honour for East Keswick Wildlife Trust.

“It will increase public awareness, attract volunteers and funding, strengthen partnerships and support the Trust’s long-term conservation work, safeguarding local wildlife and natural habitats for future generations.”

He explained that the Trust’s founding principles are conservation and the education of the public, particularly young people, in the understanding, enjoyment and conservation of our natural flora and fauna.

East Keswick Wildlife Trust is one of a number of UK charities, social enterprises and voluntary groups to receive the prestigious award this year.

The King’s Award for Voluntary Service aims to recognise outstanding work by volunteer groups to support their communities.

It celebrates groups that are truly outstanding and making a big difference to their local community, groups where volunteers rather than paid staff are in the driving seat and groups that have the highest standards in everything they do.

It was created in 2002 to celebrate Her Late Majesty The Queen’s Golden Jubilee and was continued following the accession of His Majesty The King. 2026 marks the third year of The King’s Award for Voluntary Service.
