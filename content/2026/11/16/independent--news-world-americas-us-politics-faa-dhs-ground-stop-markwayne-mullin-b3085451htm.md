No-one will be fired after flights across the U.S. were grounded on Friday creating travel chaos, the head of the Department of Homeland Security has said.

Thousands of flights were delayed or canceled as a result of the rare nationwide ground stop, which officials have said was due to a technical failure.

Appearing on Fox News’s *The Sunday Briefing*, DHS secretary Markwayne Mullin was asked by anchor Peter Doocy: “First question for you, Secretary. Who’s getting fired over this?”

​“Oh, I don’t think anybody’s going to get fired over this,” the DHS secretary replied. “This was an issue with the equipment, Peter. What we were trying to do - and I’m not trying to get ahead of FAA - I deal with terrorist threats and the whole of government organization, which is what we did Friday night.

“But what it appears to be is that during the process of trying to make an old, 50-year-old system - which is basically an old dial-up system - work with the new technology we have today, it’s caused problems.”

![DHS Secretary Markwayne Mullin says that no-one will be fired over the rare ground stop issued on Friday](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/16/8/49/Screenshot-2026-11-16-at-08-16-13.png?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&auto=webp)

​The Federal Aviation Administration said Sunday the ground stop - which was enacted at around 3.30 p.m., with normal services resuming by 8.15 p.m. - was issued out of an “abundance of caution.”

​The administration stated that its “legacy equipment” became unstable when its FAA Telecommunications Infrastructure was flooded with data, with 1,000 connection attempts being made every four to five seconds.

An initial investigation by the FAA into the incident listed two sites, one in Amarillo, Texas, and another in El Dorado, Arkansas, as possible locations linked to the systems being flooded with data.

​Speaking on Fox News, Doocy asked Mullin whether a cyberattack could have been linked to the rare ground stop.

![Hundreds of thousands of passengers were left waiting on Friday, after the ground stop caused major disruption](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/15/00/2304625671..?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&trim=0%2C0%2C0%2C0&auto=webp)

​“So, you said that you assumed this might be a cyberattack,” the anchor said. “Isn’t it possible that a bad actor, whether it’s a state leader, a state government, or just some hacker somewhere, could have flooded these routers in Arkansas with data to shut things down? That’s still a possibility, right?”

​“Well, here’s where I come from, Peter. I don’t believe in coincidences until I’m proven wrong, and right now I’m being proved wrong,” the DHS secretary responded. “So is it a possibility? Possibly.

​“I would love to point it at a bad actor because then we know who to go back and punch,” he continued. “But the fact is, it isn’t. It really appears to be with 98 percent certainty that this was because of a 50-year-old system that we’re trying to operate in today’s world.”

​The ground stop unfolded just a day after a major Microsoft outage disrupted the tech firm’s services. Microsoft says that Thursday’s outage, which disrupted Outlook, Teams, and Copilot services was caused by a technical issue.

![The ground stop was issued at around 3.30 p.m., with flights resuming at around 8.15 p.m.](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/14/19/2304620043..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

​“So you said, just now, that you don’t believe in coincidences until you are proven wrong,” Doocy began. “Your first comment the last time we saw you on Friday night was the FAA failure and Microsoft outage on back-to-back days were not related.

​“But you get why so many people watching this think that something big is happening here, right?”

​Mullin dismissed theories about a link between the two outages.

​“Yeah, I get that, and there’s conspiracy theories running all over the place,” he answered. “But to say these are connected in any way, it’s an easy way to jump to it, but it’s not.”
