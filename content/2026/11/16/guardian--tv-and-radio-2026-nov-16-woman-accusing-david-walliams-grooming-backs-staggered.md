A woman who has accused David Walliams of grooming her has said she supports a staggered age of consent.

Walliams, 55, has been accused of using his power and celebrity status to prey on young women and teenagers for sex, a joint investigation by the Guardian and ITV News has found.

Alex has alleged she was 15 and studying for her GCSEs when she first met Walliams, one of Britain’s best-known television personalities and top-selling children’s authors. She has alleged they began having sex when she was 17 and Walliams was 34.

Alex said she would have saved herself “a lot of trauma” if a staggered age of consent had been in place when she was a teenager.

“As a parent myself now, I would absolutely love for there to be a change of law. It’s something that is incredibly close to my heart, and I would feel very, very proud and happy if this could get over the line,” she told the Guardian and ITV News.

“It would protect everyone … from people that can abuse the law as it currently stands. I think it’s really important that teenagers are able to have relationships with each other. I think that the staggered element to it, I think works really well.”

Alex was speaking out after a woman who alleges she was raped by Russell Brand when she was 16 waived her right to anonymity to campaign for a staggered age of consent. Mia Latter told the Sunday Times she was in an abusive three-month relationship with Brand when he was 30 and she was 16.

She has called on the UK government to introduce a law that would make it illegal for adults over a certain age to have sex with 16- and 17-year-olds.

Last week, Alex told the Guardian and ITV News that she was “groomed” by Walliams. “I never said no … but I felt unable to say anything or have any control in that situation once I was with him,” she said.

“OK, it’s legal to have sex when you’re 17, 18, 16 even, but the context of who you’re having sex with – if that person is double your age, if that person has known you from a young age and promised you things and is in a much bigger position of power than you, both in age, in work, in finance, in stature and size, like, everything, I really don’t think that’s OK.”

On Monday, Alex backed calls for a change in the law to “make it a lot harder for predators to act”.

She said she would have had a different perspective on Walliams’ behaviour had the law already been in place when she was a teenager.

“I grew up in a culture where it was normalised,” she said. “There were people saying to me at the time that this feels really wrong, but it was a bit more accepted, and it still happened more. And I think if I grew up in a world where [I] knew that that was illegal, and it was more clear that that shouldn’t have been happening, and that was wrong, then I think that I would have been a lot more wary of that and scared by it, and probably seen it more clearly for what it was at a younger age.

“I feel like if we can make the children of today know and understand from a young age that’s illegal, that’s against the law, that that person shouldn’t be talking to you in that way, shouldn’t be engaging with you in that way, and certainly shouldn’t be acting on it, it would make you pause and hope and I think it would definitely stop a lot of incidents happening.”

Walliams’ lawyers have said the suggestion he groomed Alex is false and that she did not raise, and he did not believe, that she had any concerns about the sex at the time. The lawyers add that Walliams was mortified to learn that their brief, consensual relationship had caused her sadness. Walliams has denied all the allegations against him.
