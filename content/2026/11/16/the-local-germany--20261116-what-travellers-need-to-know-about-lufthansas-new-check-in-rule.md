If you check in online for Lufthansa, Swiss, Austrian or other Lufthansa Group flights, a booking code and surname might no longer be enough. You'll also need a free user account called a Travel ID.

Many frequent flyers will probably have an account already, so the change is more likely to affect occasional flyers.

The rule, which is being phased in by departure airport, reached Munich on Monday and will affect travellers from Frankfurt from November 30th.

**What's changing?**

Until now, the routine was simple. You got the "check-in is open" email, clicked the link, typed in your booking code and surname, and your boarding pass appeared.

For many flights, that no longer works – to check in through the airline's website or app, you'll have to log in with a Travel ID.

The account itself isn't new. It has existed since 2022 and lets you log in to several group airlines (as well as Miles & More) with one login. What's new is that it’s becoming compulsory for online check-in.

At the time of writing, the airlines affected are:

* Lufthansa
* Discover Airlines
* Swiss
* Austrian Airlines
* Brussels Airlines
* Air Dolomiti
* Edelweiss

**When does the new rule come into force?**

The rollout started on October 20th and is being introduced in stages:

October 20th: Schengen-area airports, excluding Germany, Austria and Switzerland.

November 2nd: European countries outside Schengen, plus airports in Germany (except Frankfurt and Munich), Austria and Switzerland (except Zurich).

November 16th: Munich, Zurich, and flights from the US, Canada and Mexico.

November 30th: Frankfurt and all other regions.

So if you're flying from Munich this week, the rule applies to you now. Frankfurt travellers have two more weeks.

According to Lufthansa, passengers will be told about the requirement by email before departure, and the check-in invitation and check-in page will carry a notice too.

Registering for a Travel ID requires an email address, your first and last name, your date of birth and a password. Lufthansa says it doesn't ask for anything not already needed to book a flight.

If you want to check in online, it's sensible to set up the account before departure day, not at the last minute.

The account lets you save your details, pre-fill forms and see your travel information in one place. You can also link it to Miles & More if you wish.

**Who doesn't need a Travel ID?**

The new rule covers online check-in through the airline's website or app. There are several situations where it may not be necessary:

* If you check in at a check-in counter or a self-service kiosk, you don't need a Travel ID.
* You're travelling with family or friends on one booking. If one person checks in everyone on the booking, that person's Travel ID should be enough.
* According to Lufthansa, exceptions may also apply to selected tour operator and charter bookings, where the booking details provided by the travel agency or tour operator are used.
* Tickets from other airlines are also exempt, as are flights operated by other airlines but booked on a ticket issued by a Lufthansa Group airline.
