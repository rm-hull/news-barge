# 'Opportunity' - ​Farmers ‘could achieve ambitions’ in EU trade deal

## A decade on from the National Farmers Union’s council voting overwhelmingly to remain in the European Union, its president has told Northern farmers the industry could achieve some of its key ambitions as the Government seeks to re-establish trade lost as a result of Brexit.

The Northern Farming Conference at Hexham Auction Mart heard NFU President Tom Bradshaw describe the government as having become “desperate to talk about how we get closer to the EU in the future”.

Mr Bradshaw was speaking after the UK and EU agreed a draft deal on a sanitary and phytosanitary (SPS) agreement to eliminate post-Brexit checks from food and agriculture exports.

The food deal is being regarded as the most significant part of a reset with the single market, with the ambition of clearing red tape that damaged exports after Brexit, with the government claiming it could add £5.1bn in benefits every year.

In spite of the NFU’s conviction over the potential negative effects that Brexit could bring, polls and studies have indicated between 50 and 53 per cent of UK farmer voted Leave.

Some farmers have since claimed the resulting downturn in agricultural exports was due to the Brexit deal, rather than Brexit itself.

If ratified, the deal would clear the way to resuming exports to the EU of fresh sausages, burgers, shellfish from domestic waters and seed potatoes, end the need for £200 Export Health and £25 Phytosanitary certificates.

For its part, the UK looks set to agree to "dynamic alignment" – adapting to existing EU regulations on plant, animal and food trade standards.

Mr Bradshaw said: “If this fails for farming, that dream won’t happen. I think that’s where we have got a little bit of leverage now. They have to try and make sure this works.

"We’ve been absolutely clear about what we need.

"We need to continue access to the science and technology, the gene-edited crops the plant protection products that we’ve already registered here that they haven’t registered in the EU.

"We’ve got to make sure there’s no cliff edge."

He then warned against the government looking to take EU legislation further, creating an unlevel playing field for British farmers.

Mr Bradshaw said: “For too long this country has just gold-plated EU legislation which has made it impossible to compete with even EU producers, let alone those that are importing food from further afield.

"Reduced friction at the border offers opportunity.

"Since 2019 our food exports to the EU have fallen by over a third and the EU is by far and away our biggest market with 68 per cent of our food exports going to the EU.

"It’s not the SPS deal or trade deals that do trade. It’s people.

"If the government are absolutely wedded to making this work… we’ve got to make sure we have people on the ground that will work to open up those markets.

"There’s no empty spaces on those shelves in the EU with a sign saying waiting for British products.

"We’re going to have to work hard to really open up those markets and get those markets back.”
