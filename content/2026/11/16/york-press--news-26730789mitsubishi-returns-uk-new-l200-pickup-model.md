## Mitsubishi L200 review: a capable, comfortable pickup marks a strong UK return

The all-new Mitsubishi L200 Series 7 pickup

Five years is a long time to leave an empty space in Britain’s new-car showrooms, particularly for a name with Mitsubishi’s following. In 2021, its transition into an aftersales-only business in the UK felt like the loss of a familiar face: a manufacturer whose appeal rested on tough working vehicles, adventurous engineering and some memorable rally cars.

Now, with new vehicles having returned to the UK from summer 2026, I’m genuinely glad to see it back.

Indeed, I’ve been fond of the marque ever since watching English rallying star Richard Burns driving a Lancer Evo through the Welsh forests in the late 1990s.

A major part of the UK comeback is the new L200 Series 7, a pickup with rather different priorities from a rally car, but an equally clear sense of purpose.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21720502.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The all-new Mitsubishi L200 Series 7 pickup

Alongside the returning Outlander PHEV, it gives Mitsubishi something substantial with which to rebuild its British presence.

There is sense in putting a familiar working name at the front of the revival. The L200 has a reputation to build upon, and this latest version arrives with a new diesel engine, a re-engineered chassis and the promise of greater comfort without sacrificing its usefulness.

Its handling makes a strong impression. For something weighing more than two tonnes, the L200 disguises its mass remarkably well through corners. It feels composed, rather than constantly reminding you how much vehicle you are asking to change direction.

![.](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21720507.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The interior of the Mitsubishi

Autumn supplied a revealing examination. On leafy roads, I could feel the rear beginning to struggle for grip, but what stood out was how quickly the electronics sorted out the balance and restored stability. The intervention was noticeable, yet reassuringly effective: an initial movement was promptly brought under control.

The Barbarian’s Super Select 4WD-II system is an important part of its appeal. Unlike the Titan’s simpler setup, it allows four-wheel drive to be used on ordinary tarmac, not just loose or slippery off-road surfaces. Switching from rear-wheel drive into that road-friendly four-wheel-drive setting is possible at up to 62mph.

Active Yaw Control helps cornering stability through brake intervention, while seven driving modes tailor the responses to different conditions. The overall impression is of useful engineering doing its job.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21720504.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The all-new Mitsubishi L200 Series 7 pickup

Power comes from a new 2.4-litre four-cylinder bi-turbo diesel, producing 204PS and 470Nm, paired with a standard six-speed automatic gearbox. Maximum torque is available between 1,500 and 2,750rpm: the emphasis is on useful pulling power rather than sporting pretensions.

Mitsubishi quotes 0–62mph in 12.6 seconds and a 111mph maximum. Official combined economy is 33.3mpg, with CO2 emissions of 222g/km.

More relevant to many buyers are the working numbers. Both versions have a 3,500kg braked towing capacity, with payloads of 1,092kg for Titan and 1,025kg for Barbarian. The 6,250kg gross train limit also matters.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21720500.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The all-new Mitsubishi L200 Series 7 pickup

The load bed is 1,555mm long and 1,545mm wide, sufficient for a standard Euro pallet. Beneath it sits a ladder-frame chassis, which Mitsubishi says has 40 per cent greater torsional rigidity.

Cabin equipment acknowledges that pickups often have duties beyond the building site.

Both versions provide five seats, a nine-inch touchscreen, smartphone connectivity and a 360-degree camera. At 5.32 metres long, this is a vehicle in which parking assistance has an obvious purpose.

![.](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21720508.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The side profile of the vehicle

Titan combines cloth upholstery with practical vinyl flooring. Barbarian brings synthetic leather, heated front seats, electric driver’s seat adjustment, dual-zone climate control and wireless phone charging.

Prices start at £36,295 on the road for Titan and £39,995 for Barbarian.

My enthusiasm for Mitsubishi began with a rally car in a Welsh forest. This pickup provides a very different, thoroughly convincing reason to renew that long-held enthusiasm.
