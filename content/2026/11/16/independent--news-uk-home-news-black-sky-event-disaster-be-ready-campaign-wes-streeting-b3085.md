The past several weeks have seen widespread disruption across multiple industries in Europe, with supermarket refrigerators hacked, a major Microsoft Outlook outage, and a series of bomb hoaxes.

Fears are now growing about what could be next, amid warnings that the UK population is not ready for even more threatening incidents that could wreak havoc on daily life.

Governments around the world are urging their citizens to prepare for a ‘Black Sky’ event - in other words, a total national outage of the absolute necessities.

UK defence secretary Wes Streeting has urged the public to be “alert but not alarmed” over the threat of such events as part of the UK government’s ‘Be Ready’ campaign.

![A Black Sky event could cause widespread blackouts](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/03/16/15/37/01KKV9PS9Q9JGPWBFYSFMBC0NM.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

But a group of experts in the House of Lords have warned the Britain is “seriously underprepared” for emergencies and threats, including cyberattacks and extreme weather, and told the government to stop being “squeamish”.

*The Independent* takes a look at how a Black Sky event could unfold - and how to prepare.

## **What is a Black Sky event?**

The concept of a Black Sky event is not specific, rather it could be caused by a variety of different factors.

A natural disaster or other types of extreme weather, for example flooding or wildfires, could cause widespread power cuts.

Or the cause could be something much more sinister, such as a cyber attack by hostile states or hacktivist groups.

A multi-layered cyber-attack could see electricity networks falter, mobile phone signals cut, water pumping stations halted, shop refrigerators down and food therefore spoiled.

It could see hospitals and public transport disrupted, and homes left without heating or the ability to cook.

![Wes Streeting has warned of the need for more resilience](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/16/18/01M7GHSQ8JDDFAAY9N7DX9MKZG.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

People are being advised to buy wind-up or battery radios to ensure they can still get updates during a power cut. The BBC has developed Black Sky emergency plans, in which 100 staff members would run an emergency radio studio with food, water and fuel supplies to last for three days.

Defence and resilience experts say it is vital that the UK can withstand a Black Sky event for three days. This is reflected in the phrase "nine meals from anarchy" - the claim that a civilised society is only around three days away from chaos and social breakdown in the event that food supply is lost.

## **What has the UK government said?**

The British public has been told to prepare for food shortages and widespread disruption from cyber attacks and emergencies under a new national initiative.

The 'Be Ready' campaign marks the beginning of a three-year drive to boost household emergency preparedness, with Mr Streeting unveiling updated advice for threats such as flooding and wildfires.

UK households are advised to take simple steps in case everyday services are disrupted, like keeping a three-day supply of cash, bottled water and tinned food at home.

A new government website has also been launched with an emergency kit checklist that can be downloaded.

Mr Streeting told BBC Newsnight: "It's encouraging that people are taking practical steps to be ready.

![Sales of essential goods like tinned food and bottled water have risen following the UK's "Be Ready" emergency preparedness campaign that was launched on October 22](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/10/15/2304452242..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“Having a few everyday essentials at home, from tinned food to torches, helps households prepare for disruption, whether that's flooding, power cuts or burst pipes. We can all play our part in making Britain more resilient."

## **What is included in the ‘Be Ready’ checklist?**

The government says people should put together an emergency kit that includes key provisions you and your household would need during a short-term incident lasting three days, such as a power failure or a disruption to your water supply.

The checklist includes:

* Bottled water - enough bottled water for three days. Each person needs about 3 litres for drinking a day in addition to water needed for cooking and washing.
* Non-perishable food that doesn’t need cooking, such as ready-to-eat tinned meat, fruit or vegetables (and a tin opener). As with water, how much you need will vary based on your own circumstances. Don’t forget food for pets.
* Hand sanitiser and wet wipes for hygiene purposes when the water is off.
* Light and warmth – pack a torch and spare batteries as well as blankets, sleeping bags and warm clothing
* A portable power bank for charging your mobile phone.
* A battery or wind-up radio to get updates during a power cut – a car radio can be used, however in severe weather it might be safer to stay inside.
* A first aid kit (or some first aid items) including items such as waterproof plasters, bandages, a thermometer, antiseptic, eyewash solution, sterile dressings and gloves, medical tape for dressings, and tweezers.
* Baby supplies such as nappies and baby formula – ready made or ‘ready-to-feed’ formula is best as you may not be able to boil water or sterilise bottles.
* Money and documents – Keep cash in a mix of notes, coins and cards. Copies of IDs and documents. Also write down any important phone numbers and information such as regular medication and doses.
