Pope Leo XIV bid farewell to his adopted homeland of Peru on Monday with an "hasta luego", concluding an extensive 12-day South American tour that tested his stamina following lung cancer surgery.

Leo emerged from the Vatican’s residence in Lima to begin his final morning, visiting a care home for the elderly that he tended to during the COVID-19 pandemic, ahead of a huge Mass at a capital air base expected to draw up to 1 million people.

Speaking to the crowd outside the residence, Leo thanked Peruvians for accompanying him on his trip.

"Have a good day, thank you very much, and until later!" he said.

![Pope Leo said “Hasta luego’ to his second homeland of Peru](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/16/16/2026-11-16T155139Z_24751433_RC23POAHFQNU_RTRMADP_3_POPE-LATAM-PERU.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

For the Chicago-born Leo, who spent two decades in Peru as a missionary priest and bishop, the journey was an emotional homecoming to a place he credits with teaching him how to be a shepherd. Upon arrival, he declared that Peru was his "patria" or homeland, and sang the national anthem alongside President Keiko Fujimori. Leo holds both American and Peruvian citizenship.

Throughout the trip, it became clear that Leo modelled his ministry in Peru—and now for the universal Catholic Church—on the figure of the first archbishop of Lima, St. Turibius of Mogrovejo.

Mogrovejo, who lived from 1538 to 1606, is revered for denouncing abuses against Indigenous and enslaved people by Spanish colonisers. He is also remembered for traversing his vast diocese on foot and horseback to minister to his flock, learning Quechua and Aymara to better preach to Indigenous members and supervising the translation of church teachings so they could understand them.

Following his example, Leo travelled across Peru to places where the Catholic hierarchy rarely goes, ascending high into the Andes mountains and venturing deep into the Amazon rainforest. On his final full day on Sunday, Leo went from his former diocese of Chiclayo on the northern Pacific coast to the southeastern Andean city of Cusco, onwards to the northeastern Amazonian city of Pucallpa and back to Lima on the coast.

![Pope Leo started his South America tour in Uruguay](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/07/14/download..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The climatic and altitude changes alone were exhausting—moving from hot, humid Chiclayo to chilly, rainy Cusco (at an oxygen-challenging altitude of 3,400 metres or about 11,150 feet) to the steaming heat of the Pucallpa rainforest and back to the grey humidity of Lima.

It was a daylong lesson in what it means to be a shepherd. But it also served to say "So there!" to the nay-sayers, including some in the Vatican who had questioned whether Leo would have to change the course of his pontificate given his lung cancer diagnosis.

On Monday, Leo proceeded with his programme as planned, making clear he was not saying "adios" or goodbye forever to Peru. At the home for the elderly in Callao, where he visited residents, Leo bid farewell again, saying "hasta pronto, hasta luego," another way of saying "see you soon."

Callao Mayor César Pérez told The Associated Press that Father Roberto, as he is known to the Peruvian people, would drive approximately 12 hours from Chiclayo in northern Peru to Callao during the pandemic to ensure the well-being of older residents.

Leo delivered food and supplies to the home but did not enter the facility in order to protect residents from the virus, Pérez said.

The Peru stop capped a visit that began on 6 Nov. in Uruguay and proceeded to Argentina, the homeland of Leo’s predecessor Pope Francis, who never returned during his pontificate.

![Pope Leo met Argentine president Javier Milei during his trip](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/08/22/2026-11-08T224707Z_53766224_RC2YJOA9HLSG_RTRMADP_3_POPE-LATAM-ARGENTINA.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The trip itself was in doubt after Leo underwent lung cancer surgery on 13 Oct. in Rome. But Leo said as the visit began that he had "no doubt" that he would make it.

One of the most meaningful visits for Leo was to the sanctuary of Mogrovejo in the city of Zaña in the diocese of Chiclayo. Leo revealed he had come there the night before he was ordained a bishop to pray, so important to him was the saint who died there after falling ill during a missionary visit.

"This is a kneeling place for Robert Prevost," the Rev. David Farfan, the parish pastor, told AP. Many of the pope’s actions, he said, "helping people in the world, as a peacemaker, he did them while also kneeling in this sacred place because a saint passed away here."
