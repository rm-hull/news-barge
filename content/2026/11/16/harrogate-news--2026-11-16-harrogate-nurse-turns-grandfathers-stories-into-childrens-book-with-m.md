**A Harrogate nurse has turned childhood memories of her grandfather into a children’s book, with a message about kindness, family connections and the importance of sharing stories between generations.**

Sarah Rutter, who works at Harrogate District Hospital, is the self-published author of *The Little Red Balloon*, a story inspired by her grandfather and the tales he shared with her and her brother while they were growing up.

Sarah describes her grandfather as a traditional Yorkshireman, full of character, warmth, humour and wisdom. As she grew older, she came to appreciate how special those memories were and wanted to create something that would preserve that feeling for other families.

That inspiration became *The Little Red Balloon*, a children’s story exploring family, imagination, kindness and the memories made with the people we love.

Although written for children, Sarah hopes its message will also resonate with parents, grandparents and older generations, encouraging families to spend time together and share stories.

Since publishing the book, Sarah says she has been overwhelmed by the support it has received locally. She has also donated copies through Harrogate Hospital and community charity initiatives, helping bring storytelling and kindness into the local hospital community.

Alongside her nursing work, Sarah is now visiting local schools and hopes to reach more children through storytelling sessions. She wants to encourage them to think about kindness, family connections and the importance of making memories with the people around them.

Sarah said her aim is to share a simple message: kindness costs nothing, memories last forever, and connections between generations are incredibly special.

For Sarah, the book has become more than a writing project. It is a way of keeping her grandfather’s stories and Yorkshire spirit alive while encouraging a little more kindness in the next generation.
