At just 16, Ayat was forced to flee the only home she’d ever known.

The teenager, who was then still in school, had reluctantly come out to her mother after years of knowing she was gay.

In return, she was told she would be sent to Egypt to wed her much older cousin in an arranged marriage.

“They took my passport, they took all of my documents, booked me a holiday ticket, and said, ‘You're going tomorrow’. So it was either run away or get married to this guy.”

A desperate Ayat, who grew up as part of a Muslim family in Sheffield, left behind her five siblings, as well as years of abuse and neglect, to move in with her girlfriend.

She was excited by the freedom. It was the first time since she was aged six that she didn’t have to cook for a whole household and could choose what to do and what to wear.

But, after that relationship broke down, she was suddenly without a family or a safe and warm place to live.

The next four years were turbulent and often unsafe for Ayat as she moved between accommodation and, at times, even pitched a tent in the park.

![The Independent hopes to turn this empty property into housing for young people](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/12/15/21/project-nest-house-before-af61a9c9.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

*The Independent* has now set out to help the many young people like Ayat who are struggling to cope without a secure roof over their heads.

This publication has teamed up with charity EveryYouth as we set out to raise £200,000 to turn empty and derelict properties in Sheffield, Fife and Basingstoke into homes for homeless young people aged 16 to 25 as part of Project Nest.

The young people EveryYouth supports often have the odds stacked against them before losing their home, with 90 per cent experiencing traumatic childhoods and around half being physically abused by a parent or guardian.

Andy Burnham has thrown his support behind the *The Independent*’s campaign. He said: “My first instruction as prime minister was to end rough sleeping. We have to stop accepting it as a fact of life. But government can’t do it alone, and ending homelessness for good means making sure young people never reach the streets in the first place.”

![The prime minister said he was ‘proud’ to back The Independent’s campaign](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/12/16/download..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Ayat first sought help from EveryYouth’s delivery partner, Roundabout, in 2018, and the charity secured her accommodation at a hostel run by another organisation.

She went on to enrol in university, but was subjected to a violent and racist attack six months into her course.

“The pressure got a bit too much after that, so I couldn't continue on. I failed one module, and that module was a big module, so I couldn't pass, and I dropped out,” she said.

By the beginning of 2023, she was in council housing and struggling to keep up with her mounting bills and lack of support.

“I needed help with a little bit of guidance because my bills were getting a bit too much, and I think I was just skipping a few things as well because of the pressures. My mental health just dropped,” she said. “Roundabout came, and they kind of saved the day.

“They didn't just see me as a girl that gave up. They did turn around and say, ‘No, look, you've got things that are going for you, don't just give up just yet’.”

Roundabout helped Ayat sort out a plan for her finances and put her into their peer education programme, which sends young people into schools to talk about their experiences of being homeless.

She said the charity reignited her dream of being a teacher, which is something she never thought she would achieve.

![The Independent has joined forces with EveryYouth](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/11/13/27/PrimaryBlack-ProjectNestLogo.png?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“I didn’t think anything of myself,” she said. “I never had a choice of happiness.

“I was losing my tenancy, I'd just been assaulted, I dropped out of university, I had no aspirations, and I was also in a relationship that really wasn’t the best. [Roundabout] took me on like a family.”

Ayat is now living in council housing and has landed a job as a teaching assistant – something she says was only made possible by the experiences she gained through Roundabout.

“Every single aspect of my life now, it does come back to Roundabout. Everything I am now is because of them.”

She believes young homeless people are being overlooked and are refused the chance to turn their lives around.

“We have less experience, less knowledge, and fewer resources as well, and everyone older than us gets all these resources for free, and then talks about how we're getting it handed to us,” she said.

“Nothing's getting handed to us. If anything, we're getting it harder.”

EveryYouth’s chief executive Nick Connolly said: “EveryYouth helps homeless young people build lives they are proud of. Today, homeless young people must choose between supported housing, which is vital to help them navigate the early stages of adult life, and employment, which is the key that unlocks their future.
