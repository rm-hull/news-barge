The California coast was pummeled again on Sunday by huge waves that inundated beaches, flooded local streets and forced the closure of popular piers.

The National Weather Service office for the San Francisco Bay Area warned that surf up to 35ft (11 meters) was possible, along with dangerous rip currents and sneaker waves.

“Stay out of the water, stay off rocks and jetties, and never turn your back on the ocean!” the weather service said on social media.

On Saturday, waves above 50ft were recorded in San Mateo county, just south of San Francisco.

Surfers weren’t deterred, taking advantage of giant waves at the famous Mavericks beach near Half Moon Bay.

Farther south, the Santa Cruz county sheriff’s office issued an evacuation warning near the coastal town of Capitola that took the brunt of the powerful surf.

![Two people react with shock as large waves crash near them by pier](https://i.guim.co.uk/img/media/70830e3afd7212c46044deab0c533417fdca12d6/0_0_6581_4389/master/6581.jpg?width=445&dpr=1&s=none&crop=none)

In southern California, several piers, including ones in Ventura, Manhattan Beach, Huntington Beach and San Diego’s Pacific Beach, were closed during the weekend as they were slammed by waves.

At La Jolla Cove near San Diego, known for its lounging sea lions, water overflowed the beach and crashed up a staircase leading to the sand.

Conditions were calming by Sunday afternoon, but large waves were expected all week, forecasters warned.

The ongoing super El Niño has warmed the ocean, leading to an increased volume of seawater.
