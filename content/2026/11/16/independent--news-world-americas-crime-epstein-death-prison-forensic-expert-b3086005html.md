A leading forensic scientist has said he believes that Jeffrey Epstein was killed in a New York prison and did not take his own life.

Professor Michael Freeman told BBC Newsnight that he believed that the convicted paedophile was strangled after reviewing the New York City medical examiner’s file, contradicting the official explanation for his death.

Epstein’s 2019 death in a Manhattan jail cell was officially ruled as a suicide by Dr Barbara Sampson, the city’s chief medical examiner at the time.

He was awaiting trial on sex trafficking charges.

Prof Freeman, who has worked on high-profile cases for US attorney generals, believes that Epstein was assaulted, hit in the face and then strangled.

He did not see the body but spent a year examining Epstein’s autopsy file, which was provided by his brother Mark, who has always claimed that he was killed.

![Photo inside Jeffrey Epstein's cell in MDC, New York after his death by suicide in 2019](https://static.the-independent.com/2026/01/30/17/41/ewagrfegre.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Photographs of the body show a deep haemorrhage to his left shoulder and injuries suggesting an attack, he claimed.

The forensics expert also told the broadcaster that fingernail marks could also be seen above a ligature mark on Epstein’s neck, which “would be consistent with strangulation, particularly from behind. And the attempt by the victim to try to remove the ligature from the neck”.

He added: “The injuries and the way the body was found don’t match up.

“There’s only one remaining explanation then, and that is that the death was as a result of a homicide.”

“What I'm saying as a certainty is he didn't get all of those injuries from self-inflicted hanging,” he added.

Prof Freeman presented his findings on Monday at the Middle Temple, a legal society in London. He has so far been unsuccessful in publishing his findings in a peer-reviewed journal.

He said he had “no idea” and wasn’t concerned about who might have killed Epstein if his interpretation of the evidence was proven to be true.

"That is not something I have an opinion about. That isn't any part of any investigation that I've done," he said.

![Epstein was found dead in his prison cell on 10 August 2019 morning in the MCC Manhattan while awaiting trial on sex trafficking charges](https://static.the-independent.com/2022/09/13/16/newFile-11.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Asked whether his views veered into conspiracy theory, he told the BBC: “I have the luxury of being able to say, don't take my word for it, let everybody look at it.

“If you think I'm still a conspiracy theorist because I'm looking at something that looks like a strangulation and say, I think it is a strangulation and I don't think it could be a hanging, show me where I got it wrong.”

Epstein pleaded guilty in 2008 in Florida to soliciting prostitution from a minor, a conviction that led to a controversial plea deal and a short jail sentence.

He was arrested again in July 2019 and charged with sex trafficking of minors, accused of recruiting and abusing underage girls in New York and ⁠Florida.

He had been on suicide watch at the jail but was taken off prior to his death.

The ruling of the New York chief medical examiner was later backed up by a review from the Department of Justice (DoJ).

*The Independent* has approached Dr Sampson for comment.

She has previously said: “I stand firmly behind our determination of the cause and manner of death for Mr. Epstein.”
