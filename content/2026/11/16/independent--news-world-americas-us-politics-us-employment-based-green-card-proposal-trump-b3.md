The Trump administration has proposed new limits on a green card program that allows skilled foreign workers to settle permanently in the United States.

The US Labor Department put forward the plan on Monday in the administration's latest move to restrict legal immigration.

Under the program, known as the Permanent Labor Certification program or PERM, large American technology companies and outsourcing firms obtain employment-based green cards for foreign employees.

The cards allow those workers to move from temporary work status to permanent US residency.

Companies using the program must first show there are not enough qualified and available ⁠US workers for a given job opportunity.

They must also show that hiring someone from abroad will not affect the pay and working conditions of ​similarly employed Americans.

![Trump holds a printout of an article about immigration numbers as he speaks to the press before boarding Air Force One at Joint Base Andrews, Maryland, on August 14, 2026](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/16/21/47/GettyImages-2289926445.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The proposed overhaul would push employers to go to greater lengths to prove that a foreign hire would not hurt US workers.

It would extend that requirement to occupations that had formerly been exempted because they were deemed to be in short supply, including professional nurses and scientists of "exceptional ability."

The move is President Donald Trump's latest attempt to restrict the program. It forms part of his administration's broader effort to reduce the number of foreign-born people in the US and narrow their pathway to residence and citizenship.

In October, the US government blocked major technology companies from using PERM.

Those affected included software maker Adobe, major IT outsourcers and Microsoft, which is the largest filer of permanent labor applications through the program, according to Labor Department data​.

Employers would also be blocked from seeking PERM certification for foreign workers within two years of laying off US workers in similar roles.

The Labor Department's proposal would additionally require employers to modernize their recruitment methods to ensure they are reaching the "broadest pool of high-quality US workers," such as through electronic job advertisements instead of printed ones.

The proposal is due to be published in the federal registry on Tuesday. It will then be subject to 60 days of public comment before it can take effect.
