President Donald Trump is facing renewed calls for Congress to invoke the 25th Amendment to force him out of office over his continuous posting of AI slop.

For as long as he’s been in politics, Trump has been known for his social media sprees. But as technology advances, he’s been more frequently posting AI-generated content, such as photos of himself as Jesus and maps redrawn to expand U.S. control.

Now, critics say the president has gone too far after posting apparent AI-generated photos on Saturday of himself cradling Markwayne Mullin’s son, Jim, as the Homeland Security secretary holds back tears in the background.

The photos were attached to a post about how Trump supported the Mullin family when Jim suffered a brain injury as a teenager.

The events in the story are similar to those told by Markwayne Mullin during a Senate hearing earlier this year. But the boy in the photos doesn’t resemble Jim at the time of the injury — which happened six years ago.

“25th amendment now!” the X account Republicans Against Trump wrote on Sunday.

![Mullin did fight back tears when talking about his son, but it wasn’t in the Oval Office like the AI-generated photos suggest. It was at his confirmation hearing in March, after Trump chose him to replace former Homeland Security Secretary Kristi Noem](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/16/20/2305013767..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The amendment states, in part, that if the vice president and a majority of the Cabinet declare the president unable to perform the duties of office, the vice president shall temporarily take over the Oval Office. If the president challenges the move, Congress will determine by a two-thirds vote whether the vice president will remain as acting president.

Bill Madden, a vocal Trump critic, wrote on X, “Good f\*\*\* ing gawd! Trump is posting AI slop of himself comforting Markwayne Mullin's son, Jim.

“If Congress doesn't remove this deranged piece of s\*\*\* from office, what is the point of even having constitutional levers to remove someone so obviously mentally ill that he represents a danger to the entire world?”

Another Trump critic, Raymond Baker, wrote the president’s AI-generated post was “demented. Gross. Disrespectful. Manipulative. And 25th Amendment worthy.”

Others also questioned Trump’s mental fitness given the bizarre post.

A Democratic influencer who goes by Jo Jo From Jerz wrote, “He. Is. Out. Of. His. F\*\*\* ing. Mind.”

“Imagine being Markwayne Mullin as Trump posts AI slop of himself cradling AI slop of your injured son and supposedly nursing him back to health, while a fake version of you cries with gratitude in the background. Totally normal. Nothing creepy here,” Ben Meiselas from the progressive news network MeidasTouch sarcastically posted.

![Mullin said Trump offered to send his personal plane to get Jim and his family to a rehabilitation center in California, but he declined because they couldn’t fly](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/16/20/2274478708..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Author James Surowiecki called the AI-generated photos of Trump holding Mullin’s son “grotesque.”

“All else aside, it shows zero grace to exploit another man's suffering to try to exalt yourself,” he wrote.

*The Independent* has reached out to the White House and the Homeland Security Department for comment.

Mullin did fight back tears when talking about his son, but it wasn’t in the Oval Office like the AI-generated photos suggest. It was at his confirmation hearing in March, after Trump chose him to replace former Homeland Security Secretary Kristi Noem.

“My son was a really world-class athlete and on January 17th of 2020, which, mind you, was an election year, he had a really serious brain injury — woke up 26 hours later and he’s a different kid,” Mullin said at the hearing. “The president found out about it, and he gave me a call immediately.”

Mullin said Trump offered to send his personal plane to get Jim and his family to a rehabilitation center in California, but he declined because they couldn’t fly. When they got to the center, Trump “called almost every day for two weeks checking on Jim.”

“He didn’t do it for publicity. He didn’t do it for any show. He was running in one of the toughest elections he’d been in, and the guy was still that concerned about my son,” Mullin said.
