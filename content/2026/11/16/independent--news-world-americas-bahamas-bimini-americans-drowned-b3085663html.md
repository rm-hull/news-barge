Two Americans have died after strong currents swept away a group of swimmers in the Bahamas.

​The pair, a 38-year-old man and a 40-year-old woman, died off the coast of Bimini - the westernmost district of the Bahamas - on Friday.

​The group was swimming beyond an area marked with a warning sign that cautioned against strong currents, officials with the Royal Bahamas Police Force said in a statement.

​Authorities received reports of the incident shortly before 3 p.m., arriving on the scene at the southern point of North Bimini, near Bimini Harbour. Once there, they were informed that several members of the group had been swept away.

​Some of the swimmers were rescued by a ferry boat. However, the man and woman became unresponsive before they were pulled from the water.

![Authorities in Bimini said a man and woman were unresponsive when they were pulled from the water](https://static.independent.co.uk/2025/02/10/15/25/iStock-506251206.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&auto=webp)

The pair were given CPR, with medics continuing to perform life-saving measures at a nearby medical facility. Despite those efforts, an attending physician pronounced them both dead just before 4 p.m.

​Police say that an investigation into their deaths has been launched but have noted that foul play is not suspected. Authorities have not yet released the man or the woman’s identities.

​*The Independent* has contacted the Royal Bahamas Police Force for further comment.

​The incident is the second apparent drowning off the coast of North Bimini in weeks, with 36-year-old British mom Leanne Gregory dying in October while trying to save her two sons.

​The family was on a snorkeling expedition when the two boys were swept away, with the children later being pulled out of the water in time. Gregory, who was unresponsive, was taken to North Bimini Ferry Dock and ultimately pronounced dead.

![The Royal Bahamas Police Force says that the group were beyond a sign cautioning against strong currents (file photo)](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/16/12/06/Screenshot-2026-11-16-at-12-33-52.png?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&trim=253%2C0%2C1%2C2&auto=webp)

​Nathan Gregory, the 36-year-old’s widower, wrote on a GoFundMe page that the mom had held onto one of her sons until he was rescued, but a “freak wave swept the boat away from her.”

​“We were childhood sweethearts, my one and only. 22 years into our relationship at only 36 years old,” he wrote. “We had our whole lives ahead of us, and it has all been taken away.”

​The American Red Cross recommends that those caught in a rip current should swim parallel to the shore until they have escaped its grip, before heading to back inland.
