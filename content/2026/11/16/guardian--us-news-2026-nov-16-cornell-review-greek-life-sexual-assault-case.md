Cornell University is set to announce a “full review” of fraternities and sororities determining their future on campus after an alleged sexual assault there has brought scrutiny to the system at the Ivy League school.

University president Michael Kotlikoff announced the review on Monday in an update outlining several steps the institution has taken after a woman said she was gang raped at an on-campus fraternity’s house.

Cornell had already banned fraternity parties for the rest of the academic year after the gang-rape was alleged in a civil lawsuit that captured international attention.

“I know many in our community have lost confidence in the Cornell administration’s ability to prevent and respond to sexual violence,” Kotlikoff wrote in a letter published on Monday. “You have raised necessary questions about what the administration is doing to keep our students safe and where we have fallen short.

“You have called for greater transparency and better communication about what we are doing so that nothing like this can ever happen at Cornell again. I am committed to doing the necessary work to rebuild that confidence: regularly communicating and engaging about the work we have been doing and will do to build a culture of care and responsibility.”

The changes include re-evaluating policies towards drugs and alcohol on campus, programming changes in sexual assault and bystander intervention trainings, and hiring a new victim advocate to work in the school’s Sexual Harassment and Assault Response & Education (Share) office.

Kotlikoff’s letter came months after a woman named Jane Doe filed a civil lawsuit against several members of an on-campus fraternity who she said raped her after she was drugged in November of 2024. She filed a complaint with Cornell and the university police, but no criminal charges were filed.

Two members of the Chi Phi fraternity were expelled in connection with the case. The others were made to write essays, attend workshops or serve short suspensions.

All deny wrongdoing, while Jane Doe has expressed feeling unsupported and disbelieved by Kotlikoff.

New York attorney general Letitia James is now conducting an investigation into the case.

Cornell was also scheduled to hold a community meeting on Monday evening, followed by two follow-up “listening sessions” for campus community members and staff in the coming weeks.

“These will be opportunities for open, frank, face-to-face conversations – and for advancing the work we can only do together,” Kotlikoff wrote in a note.

In early October, Cornell tapped Sally Yates, a former high-ranking US justice department official, to review the school’s policies around sexual assault.
