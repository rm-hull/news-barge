Ed Miliband has confirmed he will remain in the Cabinet even if the government gives the green light to the Rosebank oil field, despite having previously attacked the development.

The Foreign Secretary stated that collective responsibility would bind him inside Andy Burnham’s government should approval be granted for the site located north west of Shetland.

Energy Secretary Miatta Fahnbulleh, who took over from Mr Miliband after Mr Burnham became Prime Minister, will decide the outcome, with several Labour MPs opposing the project.

Back in 2023, while in opposition, Mr Miliband denounced the initiative as a "colossal waste of taxpayer money and climate vandalism".

Asked directly if he would step down should the government approve it, Mr Miliband replied: "No, definitely not."

He told BBC Radio 4’s Today programme: “I’m a member of the government and I will abide by collective responsibility on this.”

![Ed Miliband said he would not step down if the government approved Rosebank oil field](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/16/09/bf2bde4d63e1e7e360120b18c1a1020fY29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzk0OTA0ODYz-2.79437681.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Decisions on Rosebank and the Jackdaw gas field were inherited by the Labour administration when it took office after legal challenges about their environmental impact overturned previous approvals.

The government has already approved the Jackdaw scheme, with gas now flowing from the site.

But the larger Rosebank development faces resistance from Labour MPs concerned about fossil fuels.

At least 75 Labour MPs have signed a letter opposing the scheme, the *Sunday Times* reported, signalling the largest revolt of Mr Burnham’s premiership so far.

Monmouthshire MP Catherine Fookes told *Today*: “We need to really look at what we’re doing and ensure we don’t exacerbate our climate challenges and make climate change worse.

“And that’s exactly what drilling in the North Sea would do.”

![The government has already approved the Jackdaw scheme, with gas now flowing from the site](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/16/08/2026-11-15T220014Z_645483176_RC20HOAF50SA_RTRMADP_3_BRITAIN-EU-BURNHAM.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Mr Burnham also faces the prospect of resignations from his government if a licence is granted, according to reports, with a parliamentary private secretary (PPS) weighing up the prospect of stepping down if it goes ahead.

The PPS told *The Sunday Times*: “I think they underestimate how strongly people feel about climate change.

“For a generation of us it’s as central to our politics as equality or tackling poverty.”

Jackdaw’s operator Adura, which is also behind the Rosebank scheme, said gas was already flowing after the approval of that site.

Ms Fahnbulleh said: “Last month, I gave Jackdaw the green light and today its gas will start flowing into Britain, thanks to the hard work of the skilled North Sea workers.

“It means homegrown gas from this project will help to heat millions of British homes and businesses this winter and beyond, in a boost for our energy security.

“It also will help minimise the UK’s use of imported higher emission gas as we continue our transition to renewables and nuclear.”

![Energy Secretary Miatta Fahnbulleh](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/10/09/2304404764..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Claire Mueller, Jackdaw project director at Adura, said the gas field is “helping to power Britain”.

She said: “With Jackdaw now producing, and other major projects including Rosebank in our pipeline, we have an opportunity to build a bright future for the North Sea, supporting jobs, investment and Britain’s energy security for years to come.”

Andrew Bowie, shadow energy secretary, said: “One gas field does not make an energy strategy.

“Labour cannot claim to back the North Sea while continuing to block new licences, undermine investor confidence, and hammer the industry with a punishing windfall tax, and with Burnham’s own backbenchers seemingly poised to rebel over the approval of Rosebank, it is clear that Labour cannot be trusted to deliver the energy security we need.

“Just like his predecessor, Burnham is beholden to militant backbenchers who would rather appease their own side than stand up for British jobs and energy security.

“Jackdaw must be a starting gun, not a sticking plaster. Britain cannot afford another prime minister who talks tough but lacks the backbone to act.”

Nina Navid, senior energy campaigner at Greenpeace, said: “The gas produced by Jackdaw will not fix the problems with our energy system.

“It’ll keep us and our bills hitched to unpredictable fossil fuel prices set by the global market.”

It comes after Mr Burnham addressed the UN Cop31 summit in Turkey last week.

The prime minister warned that the “climate crisis is upon all of us now” after a “not normal” summer of extreme heat, wildfires and drought.

“The climate crisis is not a distant threat; it is upon us all now, devastating lives and livelihoods everywhere,” he said.

“Meanwhile, two wars across two continents have sent fuel prices and gas bills soaring for families and businesses across the world. The common thread running through all of this: our dependence on fossil fuels.
