New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani announced on Monday that he is dismantling a police unit condemned for using excessive force against demonstrators, shifting instead to a "civilian-led approach" for handling major public gatherings.

The decision fulfills a campaign promise Mamdani made last year to eliminate the Strategic Response Group. The decade-old anti-terrorism division has faced multiple legal challenges regarding its aggressive tactics at public demonstrations, including mass detentions and armed officers in riot gear using pepper spray.

Appearing alongside New York City Police Commissioner Jessica Tisch for the joint announcement, Mamdani outlined the creation of a civilian-directed team titled the Community Dialogue and De-escalation Division. Its members will operate without weapons or power of arrest.

To replace the disbanded SRG within the New York Police Department, a new branch called the Patrol Support Division will be established, which the mayor said would approach the policing of demonstrations differently.

![NYPD SRG officers and mounted units move back New York Knicks fans as they celebrate winning Game 4 of the NBA Finals between New York Knicks and San Antonio Spurs, on June 10, 2026](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/16/21/08/GettyImages-2280358143.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

"New Yorkers exercising their constitutional right to protest should not be met with the same police response as a terrorism threat," the mayor said in a statement.

The SRG previously drew official criticism in a 2023 legal settlement resulting from lawsuits filed by New York Attorney General Letitia James, the New York Civil Liberties Union, and other civil rights advocates. The legal actions cited excessive force against protesters, reporters, and legal observers during civil rights demonstrations in the summer of 2020.

Civil rights organizations offered a cautious welcome to the announcement, though the NYCLU voiced concern that the Patrol Support Division might simply rebrand the SRG rather than truly disbanding it.

The Legal Aid Society, the primary public defender office in the city, commended the shuttering of "one of the NYPD's most notorious units" in a statement on Monday. However, the group cautioned that the successor unit "will require vigilant oversight to ensure that the SRG's tactics are not repeated."

The NYCLU affirmed that it will be "monitoring this new unit closely, including for compliance with our 2023 settlement with the City."

Tisch, who manages the department's nearly $7 billion operating budget, stated that roughly 500 SRG officers would be reassigned across the department before next spring. A portion of those officers will be moved to the Counterterrorism Bureau.

"Nothing would change about the way that the NYPD responds to crowd control conditions," Tisch said during a joint interview with the mayor on ABC's Eyewitness News.

A mayoral spokesperson did not answer questions detailing how the Patrol Support Division would differ from the SRG, or whether former SRG officers would staff the new team.

Police unions condemned the restructuring, arguing that the decision puts officers and the public at risk.

"The next time your life is disrupted by demonstrators who are blocking bridges and roadways or causing chaos on our streets, remember: Mayor Mamdani is on their side, not yours," Patrick Hendry, president of the Police Benevolent Association, said in a statement.
