The UK’s international isolation as a result of Brexit is “what our enemies wanted”, prime minister Andy Burnham has said in a major speech.

Mr Burnham vowed his government would take a new and more collaborative approach to foreign policy, stating that Britain is “back in the game in a big way”.

“Looking back, Brexit was the first domino to fall in a global move towards more isolationism and protectionism, vested interests rather than values, driving international affairs,” he said.

“And the saddest thing of all is this: it is exactly what our enemies wanted,” he added. “Russia wanted disunity within NATO and a weaker Europe, less able to counter its plans for Ukraine.”

![Britain's Prime Minister Andy Burnham delivers a speech during the Lord Mayor's Banquet at The Guildhall in The City of London](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/16/21/2304974935..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The speech comes days before Mr Burnham visits Brussels for the UK-EU summit, where he is expected to sign new agreements in an effort to reset relations with the bloc.

Mr Burnham used the speech to take the Brexit debate head on, declaring that the UK’s “standing in the world has diminished” in the decade since Brexit.

He described the governments approach as a “repositioning of British foreign policy” and a “clear break from the mistakes of the last 10 years”. He added: “Britain is back in the game in a big way.”

But Mr Burnham laid out three tests for any revised agreements, including sticking to Labour’s manifesto red lines, which prevent deals on free movement, or rejoining the EU, customs union or single market.

The other tests involve bringing Brexit closer to what people thought they were voting for in 2016, and passing his “Makerfield test” by delivering “tangible improvements in people’s everyday lives”.

![Lord Mayor of the City of London Bronek Masojada and his wife Jane, along with Prime Minister Andy Burnham and his wife Marie-France Van Heel](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/16/21/2026-11-16T213553Z_1151291968_RC26POA01UZ2_RTRMADP_3_BRITAIN-EU-BURNHAM.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

He argued in the speech that Britain cannot afford to turn away from the bloc, and that the world has fundamentally changed since Britain voted to leave in 2016.

Britain is “not in a position” to turn its back on a deal that could add up to 0.4 per cent of GDP, Mr Burnham said, stating “that is what is at stake” at Friday’s Brussels summit.

According to *The Times*, one clause in the food and agriculture deal set to be signed on Friday stipulates that if a future government pulls out of the agreement it will be forced to pay a penalty worth several million pounds. The EU would also have to pay compensation if they withdraw.

Mr Burnham said a stronger relationship with Brussels is vital, comparing the UK’s Brexit deal to “the Emperor’s new clothes, where no one is prepared to say the obvious truth”.

“Well, I do try to say things as I see them, and to be frank, we have lost more than we have gained in the last 10 years. The hard Brexit we were given is not good enough for Britain,” he said, criticising the deal agreed by Boris Johnson.

Mr Burnham has previously accused the Tories of having “burned all of our bridges” in the aftermath of Brexit, remarks that have fuelled questions about the prospect of the UK returning to the EU or adopting its freedom of movement rules.

The prime minister has insisted that voters will be “the boss” of any changes that come about.
