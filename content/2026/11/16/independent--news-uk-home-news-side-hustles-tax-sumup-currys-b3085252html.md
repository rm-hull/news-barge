Younger Britons are investing in new technology to expand their secondary businesses, as those aged under 30 look to bolster their earnings amid cost-of-living pressures, according to new data.

Currys’ business division recorded a sharp increase in sales of portable card readers used by small firms to take payments. The retailer reported a 150 per cent year-to-date rise in purchases of devices from Square and SumUp compared to the same period last year.

Sales of Starlink terminals – compact devices offering satellite internet connection – also climbed 93 per cent year-on-year.

The company said the figures show people elevating their side ventures and properly equipping themselves.

Currys chief services officer Dean Kramer said: “A side hustle used to mean a bit of extra cash at the weekend.

“What we’re seeing now is people setting up properly – taking payments, getting online in places they couldn’t before and generally making a small operation look professional.”

![Currys reported a 150 per cent increase in sales over the year-to-date, compared with the same period a year ago, for machines from brands SumUp and Square](https://static.independent.co.uk/2022/06/23/08/22133012-136cdbde-1cb4-4658-85eb-bac1396eb5d2.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Side hustles typically refer to activities that generate cash in addition to people’s main job and popularity has boomed in recent years as people tap into online marketplaces and social media content.

Currys said this was particularly the case for under-30s who are doing so to bolster their income streams.

Its survey of 2,000 UK adults found that a quarter of Britons have an additional income stream to give them more breathing space.

This rises to 43 per cent of under-30s, and 30 per cent for millennials.

Among the most popular side hustles are online selling and reselling, content creation and social media influencing, and selling crafts and handmade goods, the survey found.

Economic pressures were a key driver of activity with a quarter saying they started their venture specifically to help cover rising living costs, while a third said they wanted to have more disposable income.

Some 38 per cent of respondents said cost-of-living pressures had led them to put more time and effort into their side hustle, averaging seven hours a week.

In last month’s Budget, the chancellor pledged to triple the trade allowance – the amount you can earn from a new venture tax-free – from £1,000 to £3,000, and bring in a new allowance to cover a year of living costs for young entrepreneurs.

The government said the move was set to make it easier to earn money from a side hustle.
