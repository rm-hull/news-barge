President Donald Trump is expected to use the $400 million luxury jet he was gifted by Qatar to fly out to Asia this week despite doubts over whether it has received the necessary security upgrades, according to a report.

The president will visit the Philippines and China for high-profile summits and make use of the Boeing 747 that cost $1 billion to retrofit to Air Force One standards after it was delivered last year but which nevertheless still had to be taken out of commission earlier this summer for further enhancements.

Two U.S. officials cited by NBC News have now said that while the plane has received “some improvements and periodic maintenance,” as well as “cosmetic” adjustments to its interior, it is not clear that the jet has been “maxed out” with the necessary improvements, as Trump promised it would be.

“We don’t comment on specific upgrades or modifications to Air Force One,” a senior administration official told *The Independent* when asked about the plane’s status.

“We maintain a consistent posture that ensures a safe, secure, and reliable capability for the president of the United States and routinely evolve the capability as required to maintain that posture. It is the greatest presidential plane ever built.”

![President Donald Trump is set to jet to the Far East aboard the $400 million Boeing jet gifted to him by Qatar but it is not clear whether the plane has yet received the necessary security upgrades](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/07/01/11/2282294098..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

White House Communications Director Steven Cheung has previously insisted the jet is “state-of-the-art” and has been “fitted with high-level security protocols that ensure the safety of the president and his staff.”

The ongoing interest in the plane – which was intended as a “bridge aircraft” until Boeing delivers two new models in 2028 – follows the president’s trip to Turkey in July for a two-day NATO summit where he was informed by the Secret Service that a threat had been made against his life by Iran, leading the agents to advise against his using the aircraft to return home.

Instead, Trump engaged in an elaborate subterfuge in which he appeared to join his team aboard the old Air Force One, only to be ferried inside a catering truck, with only his closest aides for company, to an Air Force C-32A military plane standing nearby.

The C-32A was then flown alongside Air Force One to RAF Mildenhall in southern England, at which point the president left that plane and rejoined his staff for the final leg of the trip back to Washington, D.C.

Trump had originally said the switch back to the old plane was just “for old time’s sake.”

![Trump unveiled the redecorated jet at Joint Base Andrews in Maryland in June](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/06/19/21/2281811202..?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&auto=webp)

When his extraordinary escape was revealed by *The Washington Post*, he said of its Qatari successor: ​“It has a lot of capability, but as I understand it, in about a month or so, they’re going to send it to have it be maxed out. So they’re going to be sending it, and they’ll have it be maxed out.”​

He estimated it would take about a month to complete the work.

Trump subsequently used the plane to fly to Ireland on September 12 without any known issue and flew in it on multiple occasions domestically in October as part of his exhausting but ultimately fruitless schedule of midterms rally appearances.

Asked about its condition just prior to the Irish trip by a reporter in the Oval Office, Trump said: “Interior-wise, the best. Engines, the best. It’s – well – it has a lot of stuff.

“But to max it out, meaning you can go into any territory, it’ll take about 60 days, and it’s being sent in October to be maximum.”

Whether that has allowed sufficient time for the work to be completed ahead of the president’s departure for Manila is not clear, with the White House understandably reluctant to discuss specifics on security grounds and angered by *The New York Times*’s reporting earlier this summer about the plane’s supposed vulnerabilities.

The administration has said the jet will eventually be transferred to Trump’s presidential library in Florida once he leaves office in January 2029.
