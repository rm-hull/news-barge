Patients unsure whether they need to go to A&E will be able to access private consultations with senior emergency doctors from home through a new £120 video service.

Emergency Doctors, which launches on Tuesday, will offer urgent medical assessments for people with symptoms that are concerning but not immediately life-threatening.

The service has been set up by practising emergency medicine consultants who say they want to provide an alternative for patients caught between seeking advice from their GP and attending an emergency department.

Consultations will be available seven days a week, between 8 am and 10 pm, with plans to expand to a 24-hour service in the future.

Patients will be assessed by consultant emergency doctors, who can offer medical advice, arrange prescriptions and recommend further tests or referrals where necessary.

The launch comes as NHS emergency departments face growing demand. The latest NHS figures, published on Thursday, showed that October was the busiest month ever for A&Es in England, with more than 2.5 million attendances.

![Dr Christopher Nordstrom, one of the founders of Emergency Doctors, said the service was designed for people who were worried about their symptoms.](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/16/16/01M7J3PEP91T0MRYG3JASMH5N1.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Dr Christopher Nordstrom, one of the founders of Emergency Doctors and a practising NHS emergency medicine consultant, said the service was designed for people who were worried about their symptoms but unsure where to turn.

“As a practising NHS emergency medicine consultant, I’ve seen first-hand the enormous pressure that faces emergency departments at the moment. Traditionally, we used to call it the winter pressure, but I mean, really, it’s all year-round now,” he told the Press Association.

He said the founders had also identified what they called an “invisible waiting room”, describing people experiencing worrying symptoms who were struggling to get answers and deciding whether they needed to see a GP or attend A&E.

“So, we created Emergency Doctors because I think there has to be another option – a way for people with urgent but not life-threatening conditions to get rapid, direct, private access to consultants from their own home without the need to have to attend in an A&E department,” he said.

Dr Nordstrom described the service as “urgent medical care, effectively within your own living room”.

## **How does the £120 emergency doctor service work?**

Patients can access the service through the Emergency Doctors website, where they will first complete a screening questionnaire.

This is designed to identify anyone with symptoms that could be serious or immediately life-threatening and who should instead call 999 or go straight to hospital.

![The service aims to provide access to a senior emergency medicine consultant without patients having to travel to a hospital.](https://static.independent.co.uk/2025/10/29/10/10/iStock-1309091675.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Those who pass the initial screening can either join a queue for a consultation or book an appointment for a later time.

The service aims to provide access to a senior emergency medicine consultant without patients having to travel to a hospital or wait for an appointment with another healthcare provider.

Dr Nordstrom said the service would focus on acute and potentially serious conditions rather than simply offering routine video consultations.

He said: “It is staffed only by consultants. All of them are specialist register emergency medicine consultants, all working as well within the NHS at consultant appointments.”

He added that the aim was for most patients to leave the consultation with a clearer understanding of their symptoms and a treatment plan.

## **A&E doctors warn of growing winter pressures**

The launch comes amid warnings from emergency medicine leaders that NHS departments are facing significant challenges ahead of winter.

![NHS emergency departments face growing demand.](https://static.independent.co.uk/2024/02/15/11/HEALTH%20NHS%20%2011451041.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The Royal College of Emergency Medicine said a poll of leading A&E doctors found that two-thirds considered their emergency department to be fairly or very unsafe.

Almost nine in 10 said their department was under greater strain than it had been at the same point last year.

Dr Nordstrom said the private service could help ease some of the pressure on NHS emergency departments by assessing and treating people who did not need hospital care.

“The more patients we can assess, treat, and manage, and keep out of A&E, the better it’s going to be for everyone,” he told the Press Association.
