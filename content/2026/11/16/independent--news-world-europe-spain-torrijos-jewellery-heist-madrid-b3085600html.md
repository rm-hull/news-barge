A gang of thieves carried out a £300,000 jewellery heist in Spain, threatening to kill witnesses before torching their getaway car and fleeing into the countryside.

Six people were arrested over the armed raid at a jewellery store in Torrijos, located 55 miles south of Madrid.

The gang – consisting of three men, two women and a teenager – allegedly struck on 28 October – the same day a high-speed train collision killed at least 69 people in the nearby town of Urda.

![The haul was expected to be worth between €300,000 and €400,000 (£250,000-£334,000)](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/16/10/48/Jam_Press_JMP935196-da20af4f.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Three men are thought to have covered their faces before storming into a jewellery shop where they allegedly pulled out a firearm, threatened and assaulted the owners before helping themselves to jewellery on display and valuables locked inside a safe.

The haul was expected to be worth between €300,000 and €400,000 (£250,000-£334,000).

After the raid, the three men jumped into a getaway car where a fourth gang member was waiting.

The Guardia Civil launched a major manhunt, where officers spotted the stolen car on the A5 motorway before following it as it turned off onto a rural track.

All four occupants abandoned the vehicle and set it on fire before running off across the countryside, but officers caught up with them and arrested all involved.

The four suspects were already known to police.

![Four of the six arrested have since been remanded in custody](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/16/10/26/Jam_Press_JMP935184-cd044076.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

A subsequent investigation led officers to two more suspects – a man and a woman – who were arrested in El Casar de Escalona.

They were allegedly caught carrying a backpack containing some of the stolen jewellery, worth around €60,000 (£50,000).

"The intervention of the Guardia Civil's Air Service was significant in locating and tracking the vehicle used in the getaway, allowing four of those involved to be arrested,” said a Guardia Civil statement.

"The four occupants abandoned the car and set it on fire in an attempt to eliminate possible traces of the crime, continuing their escape on foot across the countryside."

Four of the six arrested have since been remanded in custody.

Two suspects are also accused of involvement in a separate robbery at a bingo hall in Madrid, as part of a criminal organisation dismantled following a parallel investigation.

The case has been referred to the Court of First Instance in Torrijos.
