A New Jersey woman is suing Taco Bell after she allegedly choked on a rubber glove that was inside a burrito she bought at the fast food chain.

According to the lawsuit, Jurvany Damas went to a Taco Bell location on 2704 Route 22 East in Union, New Jersey, on August 23, 2024, and ordered a Beef Grilled Cheese burrito.

After taking the food home, she went to take a bite and “felt something stuck in her throat and began to choke,” according to the lawsuit, obtained by Patch.

When Damas opened up the burrito to see what was inside, she discovered that she “had consumed rubber glove material found in the burrito she had purchased,” according to the filing.

Damas then returned to the Taco Bell to show the manager what she had been given to eat, according to the lawsuit.

![A lawsuit accuses Taco Bell of selling a customer a burrito that contained rubber glove material inside](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/16/19/29/GettyImages-1240928221.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The filing claims the fast food burrito was “not reasonably fit, suitable, or safe for its intended purpose” because of the alleged rubber glove material it contained. The lawsuit notes that the “rubber glove material” is “a foreign object that is not an ingredient or component of a burrito as ordinarily manufactured and sold by Defendants.”

The complaint also alleges that Damas had no knowledge of the material inside the burrito before taking a bite and did not misuse or change the burrito before consumption.

It was not immediately clear how much of the rubber glove material was found inside the burrito.

![It was not immediately clear how much of the rubber glove material was found inside the burrito](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/16/19/42/GettyImages-1187444898.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

In the lawsuit, Damas accuses Taco Bell and its parent company, Yum! Brands, Inc., of failing to ensure food sold to the public was free from hazardous substances and foreign objects. It also claims the fast food chain failed to properly inspect its food before giving it to customers and failed to safely operate the restaurant.

Damas suffered “painful, disabling, severe and permanent bodily injuries” in addition to medical costs, emotional distress and loss of income as a result of the incident, the lawsuit claims.

The lawsuit seeks compensatory damages, interest, attorney fees, costs of suit, and other relief that the court deems fair.

*The Independent* has contacted Taco Bell for comment.
