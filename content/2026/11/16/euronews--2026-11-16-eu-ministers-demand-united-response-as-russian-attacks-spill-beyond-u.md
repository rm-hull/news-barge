## Romania, Estonia and Sweden have called for a united European response to mounting Russian threats, from drone attacks in the Black Sea to suspected sabotage, as the EU weighs its role in future Ukraine peace talks.

The foreign ministers of Romania, Estonia and Sweden have called for a strong, united European response to Russia’s full-scale invasion of Ukraine, now approaching its fifth anniversary, as Moscow’s hostile activities increasingly spill over into European territory.

Romanian Foreign Minister Dan Neculăescu said he would seek closer coordination between NATO and the European Union over the “unacceptable” escalation of hostilities off Romania’s Black Sea coast.

“We have had four alerts in 24 hours, and we have had three ships hit over the weekend. This is unacceptable,” he said on his way into Monday’s meeting, which would formally touch on support for Ukraine and the evolving situation in the Middle East as well as the increasingly dangerous situation in the Black Sea.

“I’m going to ask our colleagues and our fellow ministers to continue contributing with capabilities in Romania,” Neculăescu said.

Drones struck three vessels on Sunday near Romania’s Sulina Canal, a key shipping route linking the Danube to the Black Sea and used by Ukraine to export goods. No injuries were reported, and crews extinguished fires that broke out on board, Romanian Defence Minister Stefan Radu Oprea said on Sunday.

Neculăescu said he would also brief his counterparts on the Black Sea Maritime Security Hub, which is being fast-tracked for development by 2027. The hub is intended to identify maritime threats, including drones and attacks on undersea cables.

The ministerial is also expected to continue conversations occurring over last week regarding the substance of what the European envoy at Russia-Ukraine ceasefire negotiations would argue for, and what their mandate could be.

Representatives from five countries – the UK, France, Germany, Italy and Poland – alongside officials from the European Council and NATO, will speak on Europe’s behalf in any future talks, leaders agreed following an informal meeting in Dublin on Friday.

Estonia’s Foreign Minister Margus Tsahkna wrote on social media platform X that the recent attempted sabotage incident against Threod Systems, attributed by Estonia’s security authorities to Russia’s GRU intelligence services, “is yet another reminder that Russia’s hostile activities extend far beyond Ukraine”.

“Estonia is preparing to close its road border crossings with Russia in response,” he added. “Hostile actions must have consequences.”

Mounting a European response to hybrid warfare should follow the same priciples applied to the European envoy, Tsahkna said.

“Nearly five years on, Putin is still making the demands he made in 2021, seeking to dictate Europe’s security arrangements. We cannot allow him to gain through peace negotiations what he has failed to achieve on the battlefield," he said.

Sweden’s Foreign Minister Maria Malmer Stenergard told Euronews that these increased hybrid warfare incidents reflect the Kremlin’s frustration that they are “not winning in Ukraine”.

“They've been trying for almost five years to invade Ukraine now. They have failed,” she said, adding that what is needed now is for Europe to be “united” and for its citizens to be resilient.

“Sweden has spent a lot more money on civil defence to make sure that people are prepared and aware,” Stenergard added.
