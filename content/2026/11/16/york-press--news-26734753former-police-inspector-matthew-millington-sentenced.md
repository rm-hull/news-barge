A FORMER North Yorkshire Police Inspector who carried out a sex act on himself in public has been sentenced for exposure and a public order offence.

Matthew Millington, 56, was sentenced at Teesside Magistrates’ Court for the incidents that took place last August in Scarborough.

Whilst he was retired as an inspector, Millington committed the offences while employed as a police staff member with North Yorkshire Police.

On August 29, 2025, Millington approached a woman in Scarborough and made sexual comments towards her.

Two days later, on August 31, he was seen in the Crossgates area of Scarborough with his trousers lowered and performing a sex act on himself in public.

He appeared at Teesside Magistrates’ Court on July 24, 2026, in relation to the public order offence where he pleaded not guilty but was found guilty following a trial.

He initially denied the exposure offence but later pleaded guilty at a hearing on September 16, at York Crown Court and was sentenced on October 6.

He was given an 18-month community order for the exposure offence and ordered to complete 50 hours of unpaid work, pay £500 in prosecution costs, and was made subject to the notification requirements of the Sexual Offences Act 2003 for five years.

He was also notified that he may be barred from working with children and vulnerable adults.

On November 16, he was sentenced at Teesside Magistrates’ Court for the public order offence where he received a six-month community order to run concurrently with the previous order.

He was also given a six-month "prohibited steps order" preventing him from returning to the place where he had approached the woman.

Millington was ordered to pay £500 in compensation to the victim, £200 in costs, and a £114 surcharge.

He resigned from his police staff role before any court appearances.

Detective Superintendent Mark Hosking, head of professional standards at North Yorkshire Police, said: "The public rightly expect police officers and staff to uphold the highest standards of honesty, integrity and professionalism, both on and off duty.

"Matthew Millington's actions represented a serious breach of the trust placed in him and were completely incompatible with the standards expected of those who serve the public.

"I would like to thank the victims and witnesses in this case for coming forward and reporting what they saw.

"Although they felt their information may not have been significant, their reports enabled us to secure key evidence, make a swift arrest and bring the case before the courts.

"Violence against women and girls remains a priority for North Yorkshire Police.

"No matter how small a concern may seem, we will always listen, take reports seriously and act on the information provided.

"We remain committed to ensuring that any officer or member of staff who commits a criminal offence or falls below the standards expected by the public is robustly investigated and held accountable for their actions."
