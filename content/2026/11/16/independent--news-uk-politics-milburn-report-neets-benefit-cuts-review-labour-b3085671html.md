The government-backed report into young people and work will be released on Tuesday, officials have confirmed, as several of its key recommendations are revealed.

Led by former health minister Alan Milburn, the report lays out steps to tackle the rising number of young people not in education, employment or training (Neet) in the UK, which currently sits at around one million.

Sweeping changes to the benefit system and improved employment support are central to the proposals, which ministers will be expected to set into action during this parliament.

Publishing his report on Tuesday, Labour’s youth unemployment tsar is expected to say: “A lost generation is not a fact of life. It is a choice. And I believe it is the wrong one.

“Not just because it is a fiscal crisis or an economic crisis. But because it is a moral one.

“It is time for a new settlement that makes work not welfare the future for the next generation.”

On welfare, Mr Milburn has recommended a wholesale replacement of the system for those aged under 25. This would be accompanied by stronger and better-funded employment support.

This will not constitute “crude benefit cuts”, his report claims, although the far-reaching proposals will likely be met with some opposition from campaigners and backbench MPs concerned about moving too quickly on reform.

Here are the key takeaways from the landmark report:

## Current benefit system abolished for young claimants

Both Universal Credit and the Personal Independence Payment (Pip) should be replaced with new benefits for claimants under 25, Mr Milburn has said.

The changes would affect over 970,000 young people currently in receipt of at least one of the two in England and Wales.

A new ‘Youth Allowance’ would replace Universal Credit entirely, including its health-related element. This would be paid at the same level as the basic rate, but end the Work Capability Assessment needed to claim the health top-up.

In place of this, young claimants will be able to access a new, devolved Health and Disability Fund, which can provide immediate payment for health-related costs without a long-term assessment.

For those who do have significant ongoing disability-related extra costs, the new Additional Costs Allowance will replace Pip to provide support towards these.

At the same time, the “small minority” of young people who will never be able to work due to ill-health will be eligible for the Severe and Complex Needs Insurance.

The changes would be markedly less generous than current provision, adding extra hurdles – across four new benefits – to claim support on health grounds.

## Jobs for Neets: the Youth Opportunity Service

Central to Mr Milburn’s recommendations is the creation of a new Youth Opportunity Service that would bring together employment, career and health support for young people.

This would be a devolved initiative, with mayors and strategic authorities responsible for delivering the service in their areas. One should be established in every area by 2029, the report states.

![The number of young people not in education, employment or training (Neet) in the UK currently sits at around one million](https://static.independent.co.uk/2025/10/21/00/6e64df958d3695fc2f47746e4a3e6e00Y29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzYxMDQzNTY1-2.19368553.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

In practice, the service will enable a young person to meet with a named adviser within 14 days of getting in touch. A participation plan would then be drawn up within four weeks, setting out “what the young person and the State will do and by when”.

After four months, a route into education, training or work should be established, and this will be a statutory duty for the strategic authority. If a young person is still Neet after six months, they are eligible for the Youth Jobs Grant, a payment of £3,000 to the employer who hires them.

At 12 months, the government will guarantee the young person a paid six-month job through the Jobs Guarantee, whether they are claiming benefits or not.

Ahead of the report’s publication, it was also reported that Employer National Insurance Contributions (NICs) could be reduced for younger employers by doubling the threshold at which companies begin paying NI for this age group from £5,000 to £10,000.

This would happen only “when fiscal conditions allow”, Mr Milburn is set to say, stopping short of recommending an outright cut to the rate at which employer NICs are paid for younger employees.

## Wider public service changes

Turning to education, the report calls for more support for parents to boost the number of children ready for school by age five to 90 per cent, up from the current 63 per cent.

Mr Milburn also echoes calls from the prime minister to rebalance the the curriculum to boost its focus on getting pupils ready for work. This comes alongside an ambition to double the number of young people achieving an apprenticeship over the decade, shifting priority away from universities.

The health system should also provide instant access to needs-based mental health support that does not require a diagnosis, the report states, with fast-tracked support for young Neets.

Outlining the need for his reforms, Mr Milburn writes that £25 is currently spent on youth benefits for every £1 on youth employment support, arguing that this should be rebalanced to £2 to £1.

Around 40,000 young people will be helped into work by 2036, the report claims, while relative poverty is reduced by 50,000 people.
