*The Independent* today launches its Christmas campaign to help lift young people out of homelessness by transforming some of Britain’s empty and derelict buildings into safe and comfortable homes.

We are joining forces with the young person’s charity EveryYouth to launch Project Nest – a groundbreaking new appeal which will give young homeless people the best opportunity to succeed by giving them a secure place to come home to.

The project will simultaneously put a roof over young people’s heads while breathing life into dilapidated buildings that are blighting Britain’s city centres.

More than 123,000 young people were homeless or at risk of homelessness in the UK in 2024 to 2025, according to Centrepoint. There are a myriad of challenges that can push young people into homelessness, including breakdowns in family relationships, domestic abuse and being evicted from supported housing.

At the same time, more than 700,000 properties across the UK stand empty long-term, tainting our cities and towns and wasting precious housing stock that could provide a young person with the stability they need to launch their independence.

![Prime minister Andy Burnham has backed The Independent's Christmas campaign](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/12/08/download..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

With the help of generous readers and supporters, *The Independent* is aiming to raise £200,000 to fund the renovation of three properties in Sheffield, Fife, and Basingstoke. Every single penny raised during the appeal will go to fund these homes to change young people’s lives.

The campaign has already been backed by prime minister Andy Burnham, who has made tackling homelessness a personal mission of his government and has pledged to give “every single person” sleeping on the streets an offer of help by the end of this year.

He said: “I’m proud to support *The Independent*’s Christmas appeal for Project Nest, and the brilliant work EveryYouth does for young people with nowhere safe to call home.

“My first instruction as prime minister was to end rough sleeping. We have to stop accepting it as a fact of life. But government can’t do it alone, and ending homelessness for good means making sure young people never reach the streets in the first place.

“That’s what Project Nest is about: not just putting a roof over young people’s heads, but giving them the support to get into education, training or work and build the lives they deserve.”

![Ayat said EveryYouth helped her remember her dream of teaching and get into stable housing](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/12/12/42/Ayat-Project-Nest.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Your money will support people like Ayat, now 25, who became homeless when she was 17 after running away from home. She had come out to her mother, who responded by trying to force her into an arranged marriage with her cousin in Egypt.

She spent years in and out of different housing, emergency accommodation and even lived in a tent with her girlfriend before reaching out to EveryYouth’s partner Roundabout.

“I needed help with a bit of guidance because my bills were getting a bit too much, and I was skipping a few things as well,” she said. “Roundabout came, and they kind of saved the day.

“They didn't just see me as a girl that gave up. They did turn around and say, ‘No, look, you've got things that are going for you, don't just give up just yet’.”

The charity helped Ayat with her finances and put her into their peer education programme, which sends young people to schools to talk about their experience being homeless. She now works as a teaching assistant and said “everything” she has now is thanks to the help she received.

EveryYouth currently runs more than 60 bed spaces across 21 homelessness hubs around the UK, most of which are in houses rented from local councils at a peppercorn rate. If successful, Project Nest will allow the charity to purchase its first property.

In Sheffield, the money will be used to buy and renovate a derelict three-bed house while in Fife and Basingstoke it will fund the renovation of empty, dilapidated one-bed properties.

The accommodation will specifically serve young people aged between 16 and 25 who are in work. With no savings or family to fall back on, moving into work can bring its own challenges: young people in supported housing can face changes to their support, while those earning a salary can still struggle to afford private rents. Project Nest breaks that cycle, with ultra-affordable, no-deposit homes where young people can live for up to two years, keep a job, save and get support to move into independent living.

Crucially, young people in these homes can also access support offered by youth homelessness charities to address the childhood trauma that leads to homelessness. The young people EveryYouth supports have been failed long before they lose their home. Virtually all grew up in absolute poverty, and 90 per cent experienced traumatic childhoods. Approximately half were physically abused by a parent or guardian, and 70 per cent say they never felt loved.

![One of Project Nest’s houses before and after renovation](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/12/15/08/project-nest-house1-e7e4be72.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

While living in the houses, young people will pay a very low rent contribution, which will cover all their bills, property maintenance and practical support services, which will help them budget and manage their money.

We are asking for readers to donate money to help support this vital cause.

Among the high-profile backers of the campaign is actor and comedian Ruby Wax who said: “No young person should have to face homelessness, especially after experiencing trauma and hardship.

“Project Nest is a genuinely life-changing campaign. By transforming dilapidated buildings into homes, *The Independent* and EveryYouth will give young people somewhere safe to live, alongside the care and practical support they need to take their next steps into education or employment.

“This Christmas, I hope people will give what they can to help make this happen. Every donation will go towards creating these homes and helping young people build a future they can believe in. Please support Project Nest and help change lives.”

![Project Nest turns dilapidated houses into homes for people struggling with homelessness](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/12/15/30/project-nest-house-2-f79b9c80.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

This year’s Christmas campaign from *The Independent* follows our appeal with Missing People last year which raised £165,000 to create SafeCall – a free, nationwide service helping vulnerable children find safety and support.

In 2024, our Brick By Brick campaign supported Refuge to raise more than £585,000 for women and children fleeing domestic abuse, funding two new safe houses for them.

Geordie Greig, *The Independent*’s Editor-in-Chief, said: “Every young person deserves a safe place to call home, yet too many are being forced to choose between a roof over their heads and a job. That is a choice no one should have to make.

“With the help of our readers and supporters, we can turn derelict properties into safe, affordable homes where young people can work, save and build a future. Nine in ten young people experiencing homelessness had traumatic childhoods – they have been let down once already, and we cannot let them down again. Everyone who helps will not only be creating homes, they’ll be changing lives.”

Nick Connolly, EveryYouth’s Chief Executive Officer, added: “EveryYouth helps homeless young people build lives they are proud of. Today, homeless young people must choose between supported housing, which is vital to help them navigate the early stages of adult life, and employment, which is the key that unlocks their future.
