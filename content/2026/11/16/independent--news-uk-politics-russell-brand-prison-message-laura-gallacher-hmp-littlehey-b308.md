Downing Street has said a message from jailed comedian Russell Brand in which he protested his innocence over a string of sexual offences was “clearly appalling” and is being urgently investigated.

Brand, 51, is serving a 10-and-a-half-year prison term after he was convicted of one count of rape and three sexual assaults at Southwark Crown Court earlier this month.

On a new episode of Brand’s weekly Sunday Service podcast, Brand’s wife Laura Gallacher read out a message from her husband in which he claimed: “I am not guilty of the offences I have been jailed for.”

A Downing Street spokesman said on Monday that prisoners were strictly prohibited from making statements that cause distress to victims and are only allowed to speak to the media in exceptional circumstances.

![Russell Brand’s wife Laura Gallacher has shared a prison message from the disgraced comedian (Jonathan Brady/PA)](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/15/19/6b6f6edaf843e5fbd0d75e0b5c55b635Y29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzk0ODU1MDk3-2.86369212.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Asked about the message, the prime minister’s official spokesman said: “This is clearly appalling and is being urgently investigated. We expect action to be taken.

“Prisoners are strictly banned from making statements that cause distress to victims. And speaking to the media is only permitted in exceptional circumstances. Prisoners cannot get around these rules by having someone else publish their comments.

“We expect action to be taken if the rules have been broken.”

In his message, Brand said he had been moved from the category B Wandsworth prison in south-west London, to the lower category C HMP Littlehey in Cambridgeshire – a prison for men convicted of a sex offence.

Ms Gallacher read the message aloud from a notebook. It said: ““Beloved brothers and sisters in Christ, God bless you for your support.

“As you know, I am not guilty for the offences I have been jailed for, so something strange is happening.”

![Russell Brand at Southwark Crown Court during his trial (PA)](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/16/12/53f8c5fbaf2040f4b135a96b9512ae49Y29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzk0OTE4MDI1-2.86369922.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

His message added: “The good news is that Christ is with us when we choose him, and I do.

“I am incarcerated in HMP Littlehey, little as in small, and hey as in hey, I’m in the biggest sex offenders’ prison in Europe.

“The good news is that I have already seen that the staff here are strong, they are kind, and they are dedicated.

“And as for my fellow prisoners, I now know that I am in no position to judge anyone.”

He added: “I don’t know which other men here are not guilty, and the ones that are, they are people that Christ died to save, like you and me. Let us all love, pray for, and forgive one another as God forgave us.”

Ms Gallacher, who is the daughter of golfer Bernard Gallacher, appeared emotional as she read the final line, which said: “Please support my treasured wife and family. I feel your prayers, your brother in Christ. Russell.”

Cabinet minister Ed Miliband had earlier condemned the message, saying it was “disgusting” and that the comedian showed “no remorse”.

The foreign secretary told *Sky News*: “His crimes are heinous and awful. I think it’s revolting, by the way, that he shows no remorse today in his comments.

“No remorse, no sympathy for the victims. Just disgusting comments.”

He also apologised for appearing on Brand’s YouTube show as Labour leader in 2015, saying he was “really sorry I did the interview”.

It comes after justice secretary Alex Norris called for new rules to be introduced to prevent lawyers from using rape myths to defend their clients, according to The Times.

Brand deployed a series of rape myths during his trial, including arguing that his accusers should not be believed because they had not screamed, kicked out or fled when they were being attacked.

Mr Norris has written to the Bar Standards Board (BSB) – the body that supervises barristers – warning it is “unacceptable” for the profession to “perpetuate myths” about rape victims in court, adding that those who flout the rules could face disciplinary action such as being barred from practice.

Brand is expected to serve two-thirds of his prison sentence in jail and will remain on the sex offenders’ register for life.
