Hundreds of students at the University of Melbourne were forced to abandon their final exams on Monday morning after many were unable to access the digital platform needed to begin them.

Jol Scobie, a first-year student at the university, was set to take his Australian politics exam on Monday after preparing for weeks. He had initially been told earlier in the year it was going to be a hand-written essay before instructors changed the test to a digital platform.

The subject was one of several holding exams at the Royal Exhibition Building in Carlton, with hundreds of students in attendance on Monday. About half were able to log on to the digital system and begin, Scobie said, but many others were left sitting at their desks, staring at a blank screen for about 50 minutes.

Students undertaking the Australian politics exam were meant to complete two 1,000-word essays over two hours.

“The [exam coordinators] said: ‘Listen, we can’t get it to work. It’s time for everyone to leave,’” Scobie said. “We were sort of standing there watching hundreds and hundreds of students pour out of the building.”

A university spokesperson confirmed technical issues caused the cancellation of a “small number” of exams on both 11 and 16 November.

“Early indications are that the issue relates to third-party technical partners involved in the delivery of our digital exams,” the spokesperson said. “Our teams are following established contingency processes to resolve the issue and communicate alternative arrangements for the students affected.

“We understand exam disruptions can be stressful for our students, and have support services available to assist students as needed.”

The university did not immediately reply to questions about how many students were affected, and if they would be able to sit makeup examinations before Friday.

Millie Mountford, another first-year student, said there had been limited communication about when a makeup exam would be held and when students and the university would be able to fit them in during an already busy week. Thousands of other students will be sitting their own tests in the coming days, with Mountford herself having another scheduled for tomorrow.

She does not know if it will go forward without any issues.

“I’m just kind of demotivated to study when I don’t even know if it will actually be worth it,” she said. “It’s just majorly inconvenient.”

Charlotte Wickham said the cancelled exam could snarl her travel plans to see her family in Tasmania over the holiday break due to the high cost and limited ability to change flights at the last minute.

“The stress really comes from not knowing if they are going to reschedule [the exams for] the supplementary period,” she said. “We have these questions that we’ve specifically studied for and now we have such a long period of waiting. We’ve planned our lives, and we have to keep these at the back of our minds. Do we have to restudy because the questions are different?”

The issues come after the National Tertiary Education Union organised a 24-hour strike and four-day teaching ban in October amid concerns from members about academic workloads, AI protections and fair pay. Members voted to approve a proposed new enterprise agreement to address some of those concerns earlier in November.

Last week, the Age reported the University of Melbourne was planning to cut almost 180 jobs across mainly non-academic staff after “disappointing” financial results.
