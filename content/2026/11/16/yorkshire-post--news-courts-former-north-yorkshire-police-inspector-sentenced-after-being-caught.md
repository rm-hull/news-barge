# Former North Yorkshire Police inspector sentenced after being caught masturbating in public

## A former North Yorkshire Police inspector has been sentenced after being caught masturbating in public and making sexual comments towards a woman in Scarborough.

Matthew Millington, 56, a retired police inspector who was working as a police staff member at the time of the offences, was convicted of exposure and a public order offence following two incidents in August 2025.

Two days later, on August 31, he was seen in the Crossgates area of Scarborough with his trousers lowered while masturbating in public.

Millington, who was based at Scarborough Police Station, initially denied both offences.

He appeared at Teesside Magistrates' Court on July 24, 2026, charged with using threatening, abusive or insulting words or behaviour with intent to cause harassment, alarm or distress.

Despite pleading not guilty, he was convicted following a trial.

He also initially denied the exposure offence but later pleaded guilty at a trial hearing at York Crown Court on September 16.

On October 6, Millington was sentenced for exposure and handed an 18-month community order, including 50 hours of unpaid work.

He was also notified that he would or may be barred from working with children and vulnerable adults and was made subject to the notification requirements of the Sexual Offences Act 2003 for five years.

He was ordered to pay £500 in prosecution costs.

On Monday (Nov 16), Millington appeared at Teesside Magistrates' Court to be sentenced for the public order offence.

He was handed a six-month community order, which will run concurrently with his existing order.

The court also imposed a six-month prohibited steps order banning him from entering the location where the offence took place.

Millington was ordered to pay £500 in compensation to the victim, £200 in costs and a £114 surcharge.

North Yorkshire Police confirmed that Millington resigned from his police staff role before any of his court appearances.

Following the sentencing, Detective Superintendent Mark Hosking, Head of Professional Standards at North Yorkshire Police, condemned his actions as a serious breach of public trust.

He said: “The public rightly expect police officers and staff to uphold the highest standards of honesty, integrity and professionalism, both on and off duty. Matthew Millington's actions represented a serious breach of the trust placed in him and were completely incompatible with the standards expected of those who serve the public.

“I would like to thank the victims and witnesses in this case for coming forward and reporting what they saw. Although they felt their information may not have been significant, their reports enabled us to secure key evidence, make a swift arrest and bring the case before the courts.

“Violence against women and girls remains a priority for North Yorkshire Police. No matter how small a concern may seem, we will always listen, take reports seriously and act on the information provided.

“We remain committed to ensuring that any officer or member of staff who commits a criminal offence or falls below the standards expected by the public is robustly investigated and held accountable for their actions.”
