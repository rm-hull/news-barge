**Angry captain Andy Robertson says Scotland do not have time to turn things around after a brutal 4-0 defeat by Switzerland consigned them to a third-place finish in their Nations League group.**

Head coach Sebastien Pocognoli - appointed as Steve Clarke's successor in August - has consistently stressed the need for patience as he tries to implement a more attack-minded style.

However, the Belgian has won just two of his first six matches in charge, both against North Macedonia, and conceded 10 goals in that time.

They collapsed worryingly easily in St Gallen against a much-changed Swiss outfit on Monday after a promising start, Bologna midfielder Lewis Ferguson putting through his own goal and early intensity gave way to indecision.

The Swiss struck twice in the early minutes of the second half to make it 3-0 and Zeki Amdouni made it four late on.

'You do not have time in international football'

"First half, I thought we were in the game, we lost a stupid goal, but I thought we had chances and could have been 2-1 at half time," a frustrated Robertson said, on the night he surpassed Kenny Dalglish as his country's most capped men's player.

"By the time you get to 50 minutes and you're 3-0 down, that can't happen in international football.

"It is a really tough night for us. I don't think you've got time in international football, you have to do it.

"These supporters who travel everywhere, are they going to give us time? No. Are the people inside Hampden going to give us time? No. Are the people outside Hampden who are going to be critical and rightly so? No.

"We don't have time, the players do not have time, we have got to step up.

"We have a long break now to March, which is not a good thing, and it is going to be a tough one to swallow."

## 'Passion and fight' lacking - should Pocognoli follow Clarke blueprint?

This was meant to be a special occasion for Robertson, but it was usurped by the debate over Pocognoli's future.

Just six games into his tenure, there are serious questions being asked over whether the former Union Saint-Gilloise and Monaco coach is capable of delivering on his promise of making Scotland easier on the eye, while delivering positive results.

Tottenham Hotspur left-back Robertson was not the only player to deliver a scathing assessment.

When asked if Scotland were on the right path, John McGinn replied: "Well, obviously the trajectory of the results means it's impossible for me to say yes."

Fellow midfielder Billy Gilmour added: "As a team we know we need to show passion and fight. We've not quite done that tonight."

Supporters are already frustrated at Pocognoli's 3-5-2 formation as they see wingers sidelined or - in the case of Ben Gannon-Doak - shoehorned into an unfamiliar wing-back role.

One man familiar with the difficulties of being the Scotland manager is former boss Craig Levein, who believes the Belgian needs to take a leaf out of his predecessor's book.

"Pocognoli is picking up where Clarke left off," Levein said on BBC Scotland. "In that situation, you need to get off to a good start. That's what the players are looking for - that you're going to make them better.

"I'd be thinking 'we need to stop losing goals'. We haven't got a potent threat, so how do we get results? As Steve recognised, you need to be hard to beat."

## 'Pocognoli would be a good PR man'

'We had good moments'

In contrast to his deflated players, Pocognoli put on a brave face post-match and insists he sees positive signs after his first two camps.

"The score is heavy with the 4-0, but I don't think it's a reflection of the game," he said.

"We played a good first half, we created chances, we were dominant in some moments, and we faced Switzerland with different shapes and adapted well from the first minute. We conceded from maybe one of the first chances they had.

"At the end, it's a bad feeling, the score, but I think that the players try to do their best and create moments that we want to see."

Again Pocognoli called for patience and the time which Robertson says Scotland do not have.

"The more we have games together, the more we will try to fix these things," the head coach said. "We have a lot of good response from this camp and it's good for the future as well."

But whatever Pocognoli's selling, former Scotland winger Pat Nevin isn't buying.

"If I'm ever in trouble and I need a PR man, I'm getting him because he's absolutely good at it," he said on Sportsound.

"He's certainly a glass-half-full person. You can talk away any moment and say it's just a moment, but it's four moments, five moments, six moments.

"A really horrible thing in that situation is that if I am running the SFA, I'd be asking the players what they think and just saying 'tell us the truth... are you backing us?'

"If a couple of the main ones say 'we're not sure of the decision-making' then I think it's got to that stage."
