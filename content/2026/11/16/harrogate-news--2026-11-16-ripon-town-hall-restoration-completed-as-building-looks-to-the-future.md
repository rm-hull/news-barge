**Ripon Town Hall’s historic façade has been restored, with the first phase of conservation work completed ahead of schedule and within budget.**

The Grade II\* listed building, designed by James Wyatt and completed in 1799, has undergone a programme of conservation work to repair its historic fabric and restore its distinctive architectural features.

The work was carried out by York-based specialist contractors Pinnacle Conservation and included traditional repairs and repainting using breathable mineral paints. The project was undertaken under listed building consent.

Scaffolding was removed in time for the city’s Remembrance commemorations, and the restored building provided a backdrop to Ripon’s Christmas Light Switch-On on Saturday 14 November, when thousands of residents and visitors gathered in the Market Place.

The Town Hall’s clock has also had its timekeeping improved following representations from Ripon, with North Yorkshire Council undertaking the work.

## Restoration marks first phase of wider plans

> **The Mayor of Ripon, Councillor Andrew Williams, said:**
>
> Ripon Town Hall has stood at the heart of our city for more than 225 years, and it is wonderful to see it looking so magnificent once again.
>
> There’s something particularly special about seeing the scaffolding come down and the building looking its best in time for Remembrance, and then providing such a wonderful backdrop to our Christmas Light Switch-On. The Town Hall is such an important part of the Market Place, and I think people will take real pride in seeing it restored.
>
> But this project is about much more than a fresh coat of paint. It is about restoring confidence in one of our city’s most important buildings and ensuring that it has a meaningful future at the heart of Ripon.
>
> This first phase demonstrates what can be achieved through local ambition and good stewardship. We want the Town Hall not simply to look towards the Market Place, but to look towards the future too, playing a much greater role in the civic and community life of Ripon for generations to come.

> **Valerio Caputi, Head of Conservation Projects at Pinnacle Conservation Ltd, said the work had uncovered evidence of the building’s decorative history, including earlier colour schemes and historic changes to its principal inscription:**
>
> One of the most rewarding aspects of the project was uncovering evidence of the Town Hall’s decorative history, including earlier colour schemes and multiple historic interventions to the principal inscription, demonstrating the significance this feature has held over time.
>
> The works combined traditional lime repair and gilding techniques with modern breathable mineral paint systems, enabling us to deliver bespoke conservation solutions tailored to the building’s needs.
>
> By working closely with Ripon City Council and the Conservation Officer, we successfully completed the project within a challenging public environment, helping to secure the future of this important landmark while preserving its historic character and significance.

## Future of the Town Hall

The completion of the façade restoration marks the end of Phase One of Ripon City Council’s wider plans for the building.

The council and North Yorkshire Council are continuing to work through the legal arrangements for transferring the Town Hall into long-term local stewardship. This includes resolving a history of land charges, leases and other arrangements accumulated over more than 50 years.

Ripon City Council has also welcomed a £10,000 contribution from North Yorkshire Council towards the conservation works, after more extensive repairs to the building’s underlying fabric were found to be necessary than originally anticipated.

Attention is now turning to the building’s future use. Ripon City Council is working with specialist conservation architects on feasibility studies and is planning further public engagement to help shape potential options.

The council hopes to have a series of options appraisals ready for consideration by June 2027. These will examine how the building could be brought into fuller and more sustainable use while respecting its historic importance.

The two councils have agreed in principle to transfer the Town Hall under a 99-year lease, with the final legal arrangements still being progressed.

The restored façade has a lighter, more matt appearance than residents may remember. The finish was informed by detailed microscopic paint analysis, which identified numerous historic paint layers. The breathable mineral paint gives the building a softer appearance than the modern coatings previously used.

With the first phase complete, Ripon Town Hall is once again a prominent feature of the Market Place as the city considers the next chapter for one of its best-known historic buildings.
