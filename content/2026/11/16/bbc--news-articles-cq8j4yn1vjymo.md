**Workers should be able to take paid time off to prepare for national emergencies, a House of Lords committee has recommended.**

In a report, the peers said a new "civic resilience leave" would enable employees to undertake training and emergency volunteering without losing income.

It is one of a slew of recommendations from a 12-strong committee that has been examining how to make the UK better able to respond to crises including war, power cuts, cyber-attacks and natural disasters.

It comes after the government launched a campaign calling on the British public to stock up on tinned food and bottled water to be prepared for emergencies.

In a report published on Monday, peers on the national resilience committee will say the government's campaign, launched last month, was a "welcome step".

But it will argue that Britain is still underprepared for potential disruption, adding that the public did not have the "full picture" about potential threats from Russia, China and Iran, including cyber attacks.

It will recommend that all local councils should draw up registers of volunteers with "relevant skills and capabilities" to help out during crises, as well as lists of buildings in their area that could be used as emergency shelters.

The government should also launch a national campaign for registered volunteers and civil reservists, it added, modelled on the programme launched by the NHS for "volunteer responders" during the Covid pandemic.

## 'Proportionate' leave

It noted that the previous government floated the idea of a "civilian reservist cadre" to help out in times of crisis in its 2021 defence review, but this was dropped when the report was updated two years later.

Although some employers offer time off to staff who volunteer, there is currently no legal entitlement to paid leave for those in roles such as coastguard rescue, firefighting, or the St John Ambulance.

Army reservists can claim up to £400 a day, external from the government to cover lost pay if they are mobilised, but employers do not have to allow time off for training - although some choose to do so.

In their report, the peers said a new right to paid time off could cover "approved" training and volunteering, without specifying exact roles.

They did not recommend an exact leave entitlement, instead suggesting that Labour ministers should work with employers to design "proportionate arrangements" for workers.

## New preparedness app

Elsewhere in their report, peers recommended the government should send booklets to every household with information about what to do in a crisis, modelled on those sent to homes in Sweden and Denmark.

In its evidence to the inquiry, the government said it planned to provide information through its website - but peers rejected this approach, adding it risked "excluding some groups" and would not work during prolonged periods of disruption.

The peers also recommended the development of a multilingual "public preparedness app" to access official guidance on preparing for emergencies.

It should also allow users to automatically share their location with emergency authorities, similar to the 112 Suomi app in Finland, they added.

The Cabinet Office said it would "carefully review" the report, whilst insisting the government was already taking action to improve crisis resilience.

It pointed to work already under way to update departments' secret defence plans, known as the "war book", and a major exercise scheduled for next year to test the the UK's resilience for war.

The government also recently announced new measures to protect the UK's undersea internet cables, including powers for officials to inspect cable landing stations owned and operated by telecom companies.
