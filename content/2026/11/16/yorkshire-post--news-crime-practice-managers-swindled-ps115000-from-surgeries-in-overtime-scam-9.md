# Practice managers used overtime scam to swindle £115,000 from struggling surgeries

## A scheming doctors’ surgery manager and her assistant stole more than £115,000 from their practice, with one later claiming they “deserved” the money.

Angela Gelder and Samantha Lofthouse spent years siphoning cash from the West Leeds Family Practice in a scam involving overtime pay.

Leeds Crown Court heard their fraud contributed to the practice having to make redundancies and strip out services due to cashflow problems.

Prosecutor Andrea Parnham said 55-year-old Gelder was first employed by the group in 2001 and was eventually promoted to practice manager.

Her role involved overseeing payroll and overtime claims.

Lofthouse, 57, was first employed in 2005 as an assistant, and was later appointed assistant practice manager.

Mainly based at the surgery on New Street in Farsley, they also covered the Calverley Surgery on Upper Carr Lane and Glenlea Surgery on Bradford Road, Stanningley.

In 2023, the practice partners became concerned about cash-flow problems, but accepted they had taken on significant renovation projects at the time.

They asked for Gelder to provide a breakdown of monthly outgoings, and having eventually received the report, found salary costs were more than expected.

Lofthouse then unexpectedly quit the practice, possibly over a cancer diagnosis, the court heard.

![Gelder and Lofthouse both defrauded a group practice, stealing more than £115,000.](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/jpim-static/image/2026/11/13/14/56/Gelder-and-Lofthouse.jpeg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


A new practice manager was then brought in to look at staff costs, after which Gelder was suspended and the police called.

It was found that between January 2016 and March 2023, a period of over seven years, the pair had each made repeated false overtime claims.

Gelder had paid herself £67,230 more than she warranted overall, with Lofthouse taking £48,839 more than she was entitled.

The practice found itself in “significant financial difficulties” with Ms Parnham saying the fraud was a “contributing factor”.

Redundancies were made and services reduced.

The court was told that Lofthouse had attempted to claim she was entitled to the money and said Gelder encouraged her, although these claims were later abandoned.

Both admitted a charge of fraud.

Mitigating for Lofthouse, Katherine Robinson said there was remorse and she had no previous convictions and she had not committed offences since.

She pointed to the “significant delay” in bringing the case to court, despite Lofthouse giving an early indication of a guilty plea. She said she also had grandparenting responsibilities.

Ms Robinson said Lofthouse accepted responsibility, despite telling probation that she had “deserved” the money.

For Gelder, Philip Mahoney said she had a close relative in palliative care.

He also pointed to her lack of previous convictions and said she was “not a risk to anyone”, in terms of her facing an immediate custodial sentence

Judge Neil Clark told the pair: “It was a mean fraud offence. In reality, you were thieves who stole from the business you worked for.

“You were in a position of trust. It was discovered you were taking large amounts of money for overtime payments you were not entitled to. It occured over a long period of time.”

Gelder, of Fairbairn Fold, Bradford, was handed a 24-month sentence, suspended for two years, with a probation requirement and 180 hours of unpaid work to complete.

Lofthouse, of North Bank Road, Cottingley, Bingley, was given a 22-month term, suspended for two years and 210 hours of unpaid work.

Proceeds of Crime Act (POCA) proceedings were timetabled for March next year in which it will be determined how the money they stole can be paid back.
