The controversial Jackdaw gas field has begun producing gas weeks after the government approved the project, as Andy Burnham faces a backbench rebellion over his looming decision on the Rosebank oil field.

Owners and operators of the field, Adura, confirmed that has gas has begun to flow from the Jackdaw production platform in the North Sea.

It said the gas will now be supplied to homes and businesses across Britain, eventually producing enough gas to heat the equivalent of around 1.4 million UK homes as it reaches peak production in the coming months.

It comes weeks after energy secretary Miatta Fahnbulleh approved plans for drilling at the Jackdaw gas field near Aberdeen.

![The UK Government has approved the Jackdaw gas field off the coast of Aberdeen (Jane Barlow/PA)](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/05/11/05105717-8e64b6b3-ddc8-4116-b505-82ff1bbae427.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

She welcomed the update from the North Sea site, and said: “Last month, I gave Jackdaw the green light and today its gas will start flowing into Britain, thanks to the hard work of the skilled North Sea workers.

“It means homegrown gas from this project will help to heat millions of British homes and businesses this winter and beyond, in a boost for our energy security.

“It also will help minimise the UK’s use of imported higher emission gas as we continue our transition to renewables and nuclear.”

Claire Mueller, Jackdaw Project Director at Adura, said: “At a time of considerable uncertainty around the world, Jackdaw will provide secure energy from our own waters to heat British homes and power our economy, while reducing our reliance on imports with higher emissions.

“Jackdaw also demonstrates what can be achieved when Britain makes pragmatic, evidence-led decisions about the energy it needs, while properly accounting for our climate responsibilities. It is a positive step towards restoring confidence in the North Sea and among the skilled people and businesses who have built this industry over decades.”

Whilst the government approved Jackdaw last month, it is yet to make a decision on the Rosebank site, which is Britain’s largest remaining untapped oil field, located about 80 miles north west of the Shetland Islands.

Environmental campaigners have urged the government to reject the “even more damaging” Rosebank oil field, whilst the prime minister reportedly faces a rebellion from his MPs who wish to block the approval.

*The Sunday Times* reports that, according to those who have mobilised against the move, 75 Labour MPs have privately signed a letter opposing it.

![Environmental campaigners have urged the government to reject the “even more damaging” Rosebank oil field, whilst the prime minister reportedly faces a rebellion from his MPs who wish to block the approval](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/15/14/2304557864..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The newspaper reports that the rebel MPs believe that approving Rosebank would destroy the UK’s reputation on climate change and would not even reduce energy bills.

Mr Burnham also reportedly faces the prospect of resignations from his government if a licence is granted, it reports, with a parliamentary private secretary (PPS) weighing up the prospect of stepping down if it goes ahead.

The PPS told *The Sunday Times*: “I think they underestimate how strongly people feel about climate change.

“For a generation of us it’s as central to our politics as equality or tackling poverty.”

The letter, which has not been made public yet, is reportedly being based on the strategy taken by the Labour MPs who forced Sir Keir Starmer to abandon planned cuts to disability benefits during his premiership.
