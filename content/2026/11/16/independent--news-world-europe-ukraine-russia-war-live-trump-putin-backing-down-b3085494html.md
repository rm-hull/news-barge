Russian president Vladimir Putin is “backing down” in the war on Ukraine, Donald Trump has sensationally claimed.

“Putin is backing down a little bit on Ukraine. He could have been much tougher, frankly,” the US president said on Monday.

His remark comes just a month after the UN Human Rights Office verified more than 15,000 civilians had been killed and injured in Ukraine during the first eight months of this year.

It found that an average of more than 400 civilians were killed or injured every week – a 55 percent increase from the same period in 2025.

Trump was speaking to the press before departing for a visit to Asia where he will attend two summits, at the end of which will be historic trilateral nuclear talks with Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian leader Vladimir Putin in China.

Meanwhile, Ukraine struck the bridge linking Russia and Crimea across the Kerch strait, ​limiting the flow of traffic, the ​peninsula's Russian-backed governor Sergei Aksyonov said on Monday.

Europe's longest bridge connects Russia with ​the Crimean peninsula, seized by Russia ​from Ukraine in 2014. It is widely viewed as one of Russian President Vladimir Putin's signature ​infrastructure projects.

## Progress being made to end Ukraine war, says US envoy

Progress is being made towards an agreement to end the war in Ukraine, though important challenges remain, says US envoy Jared Kushner.

Kushner, President Donald Trump's son-in-law and adviser, said in a post on X that he and fellow US negotiator Steve Witkoff spoke with Kremlin aide Yuri Ushakov earlier to discuss next steps. He said they also had a productive call with Ukraine and European partners.

"We are making progress, in close coordination with Russia, Ukraine, and Europe, toward an outcome that can end this war, strengthen European security, and create the foundations for lasting peace," Kushner wrote.

"Important challenges remain, and a final agreement will require difficult decisions."

Russian, Ukrainian and US officials held talks in Abu Dhabi last week for the first time in nine months to end the conflict.

## Crimea’s occupying authorities fear new strikes following the attack on the Crimean Bridge — Atesh

Atesh, a partisan Ukrainian group operating inside Russian-occupied territory, said on social media that authorities in Crimea were “bracing for further strikes” on the Crimea bridge following a Ukrainian drone attack on Monday.

The Russian authorities in Crimea are also complaining about a lack of air defences and have expressed dissatisfaction with Moscow’s actions, according to Atesh.

“There are speculations among officials that the Russian Federation’s top leadership is unaware of the real situation on the ground—in Moscow, they allegedly know nothing about either the fuel shortage or the critical power outages,” it said.

## France secretly sent troops to Ukraine – RFI

French soldiers have been working in Ukraine for several months, according to anonymous sources cited by French broadcaster RFI.

The sources said the mission of a small contingent of French troops excludes any combat role.

The French troops have been in Ukraine since at least this summer, maintaining and repairing weapons supplied to Kyiv, and training Ukrainian commanders.

According to the report, the service members on the mission were ordered to say they were traveling to Poland.

The contingent has a “light and modular” structure, consisting of small groups carrying out short-term tasks.

No foreign governments have officially announced the deployment of regular combat troops to fight alongside Ukrainian forces in the country since Russia’s full-scale invasion in 2022.

## Newly released image shows visible damage on Crimea bridge

A newly released image of the Crimean bridge shows the structure visibly damaged after the biggest Ukrainian drone attack since the war began.

The landmark bridge – also called the Kerch Bridge – linking Russia to occupied Crimea, sustained minor damage and several oil refineries were hit on Monday.

## EU sanctions Kremlin troll factory, hacker group in new push against Russian hybrid activities

The EU announced on Monday that it is adopting new sanctions against two entities and 30 individuals involved in Russia's disinformation and cyberwarfare activities.

It includes the Social Design Agency (SDA), a Moscow-based IT company accused of carrying out “large scale information manipulation and interference projects funded, directed and supervised by the Russian Presidential Administration.”

The European Council said it has orchestrated disinformation campaigns specifically targeting EU countries, such as Germany and France, as well as EU institutions and European public support for Ukraine.

It is also listing propagandists such as Russian television figures Vitaly Eliseev and Petr Marchenko, Ukrainian-born blogger and military commentator, Yurii Podoliaka, and Chay Bowes from RT international.

EU foreign ministers approved the new listings under the sanctions regime targeting Russia’s hybrid threats, imposing asset freezes and transaction bans in the EU.

It comes as Europe heads into high-stakes elections, including in Spain later in November and in France, Italy, Poland, and Slovakia next year.

## Trump says he thinks Putin is "backing down" on Ukraine

US President Donald Trump told reporters on Monday that he thought Russian President Vladimir Putin was “backing down” in the war on Ukraine.

“Putin is backing down a little bit on Ukraine. He could have been much tougher, frankly,” he said.

His remark comes just a month after the UN Human Rights Office verified more than 15,000 civilians had been killed and injured in Ukraine during the first eight months of this year.

It found that an average of more than 400 civilians were killed or injured every week in Ukraine – a 55 percent increase from the same period in 2025.

Trump also said he plans to talk about Russia’s war in Ukraine during the historic nuclear talks with Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian leader Vladimir Putin in China.

“We’ll talk about my favourite subject, Ukraine. Ukraine’s losing Ukraine,” Trump told the press at the White House on Monday morning before departing for his visit to Asia where he will be attending two summits.

“Russia is losing 25,000 soldiers in the month. So we’ll be talking about that.”

## Bulgaria rescues crew from capsized wheat ship in Black Sea, transport ministry says

Bulgaria safely evacuated the crew of a cargo ship that capsized in the Black Sea off the country’s coast, ⁠its transport ministry ​said ⁠on Monday.

Bulgaria received a distress signal early on Monday, indicating the Somali-flagged ship was sinking about 40 nautical miles off Cape Kaliakra, the ministry said in a statement.

All 12 crew members on board – eight Turkish citizens and four Azerbaijani nationals – were saved. The rescue operation involved four ships, two from Bulgaria, one from Romania and one from Turkey.

A helicopter picked up the crew ⁠members and they are in good condition, the ministry said.

The ship was sailing from Romania to Egypt and carrying 3 ⁠metric tons of wheat.

The cause of the incident was not clear.

It follows deadly drone attacks against ‌ships off the coast of ​Bulgaria and Romania ‌in recent weeks ⁠that analysts have linked to ⁠Russia's war with Ukraine.

## Kremlin says 'minor' damage after Ukraine hits bridge linking Russia and Crimea

Ukrainian drones struck ⁠a bridge over the Kerch Strait that links Russia and Crimea overnight, but caused only minor damage, the Kremlin and the peninsula's Russia-backed governor said on Monday.

In a call with reporters, Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov said: "Minor damage is being assessed. Traffic across the bridge continues."

The governor of Crimea, Sergei Aksyonov, had said earlier on Monday that traffic would be restricted ⁠until around midday.

The 19-km (12-mile) Crimea Bridge over the Kerch Strait `is the only direct link between the transport network of Russia and the Crimean peninsula, which Russia seized from Ukraine and annexed in 2014.

It has in ‌the past been a key supply ‌route for Russian forces in Ukraine and is widely viewed as one of Russian President Vladimir Putin's signature infrastructure projects.

It consists of a separate roadway and railway, both supported by concrete stilts, which give way to a wider span held by steel arches at the point where ships pass between the Black Sea and the smaller Azov Sea.

The bridge has come under attack by Ukraine several times since Putin sent Russian forces into Ukraine in February ‌2022, and a truck bomb in October 2022 killed ​four people and limited the bridge's capacity for several ‌months.

## Holding talks with Russia on Europe's security now would be premature, EU ministers say

Several European Union foreign ministers cautioned on Monday against discussing Europe's future security architecture with Moscow before a ceasefire is reached in Ukraine, urging Europe not to fall into a Russian trap.

Any such discussions must await a ceasefire in Russia's war in Ukraine and depend on Moscow making meaningful concessions, they said.

After Russia said on Friday it would be open to European countries taking part in peace talks if it saw they genuinely intended to contribute to peace, EU leaders agreed that Europe's team would comprise non-EU member Britain, France, Germany, Italy and Poland, and EU institutions.
