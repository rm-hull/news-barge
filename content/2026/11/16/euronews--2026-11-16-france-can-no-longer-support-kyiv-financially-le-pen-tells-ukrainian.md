## The National Rally party's stance on Ukraine has come under increasing scrutiny over its pledges to withdraw financial aid and questions over historical ties to Russia.

France's hard-right National Rally party's Marine Le Pen confirmed on Monday that she planned to end financial aid to Ukraine if she is elected as president next year, following a meeting with Kyiv's ambassador in Paris.

Le Pen said she "regretted" that France's public finances would not allow it to provide further financial support but that a number of existing commitments would be maintained.

Le Pen is currently topping the polls at around 32% ahead of France's 2027 presidential elections, according to a survey published on 14 November by pollster Elabe. The hard-left France Unbowed party's Jean-Luc Mélenchon is trailing behind at roughly 17%.

As the fifth anniversary of Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine draws closer, a statement issued by the National Rally said that while France could not continue to provide financial assistance to Ukraine, it would honour its "existing commitments" - citing the European Union's €90 billion support loan to Ukraine as well as "material donations, humanitarian aid, and more active diplomacy."

Le Pen described the talks with Ukraine's ambassador to France, Vadym Omelchenko, as "cordial" and an opportunity for her to reiterate her "unwavering support for Ukraine."

Le Pen has faced criticism over her past ties to Russia, which included a 2017 meeting in the Kremlin with President Vladimir Putin and her party taking a loan from a Russian bank that it paid off in full in 2023.

The statement also said the pair had held a "frank discussion" on the future of the conflict, with Le Pen emphasising that "the pursuit of peace - though a difficult goal to achieve - must remain a priority for all."

Le Pen's opponents have warned that by emphasising the importance of ending the war swiftly, she could force Ukraine to accept unfavourable terms.

"If Russia imposes its will on a European nation through brutality, all of Europe is weakened," Macron wrote on Facebook at the weekend.

Taking aim at Le Pen, he added: "Let us be clear: those who seek to limit the resources available to Ukraine are, in effect, pushing for an unjust peace - one that would leave Ukraine permanently vulnerable, and, by extension, leave Europe vulnerable as well."

During their meeting, Le Pen asked the Ukrainian ambassador to tell President Volodymyr Zelensky that she was ready to meet in "either Paris or Brussels."

However, there was no mention of her making the journey to Kyiv, where Zelensky has invited her.
