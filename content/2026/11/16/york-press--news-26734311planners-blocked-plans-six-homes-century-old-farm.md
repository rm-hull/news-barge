## Six-home countryside development brought to a halt

Proposed barn coverison, Barker Rails Farm. Courtesy of applicant.. *(Image: Applicant)*

THE creation of six family homes from farm buildings near Scarborough has been rejected by planners after they concluded that parts of the proposal were not properly justified.

The scheme at Barker Rails Farm, off Brompton Carr Lane near Brompton-by-Sawdon, would have transformed two agricultural barns into six three-bedroom homes while demolishing a third barn.

The applicant said the seven-acre site had been used for agriculture for more than a century, most recently as an egg farm and egg packaging and distribution business.

The plans proposed four homes in the largest barn and two homes in a second building.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21725053.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Proposed barn coverison, Barker Rails Farm. Courtesy of applicant.. *(Image: Applicant)*

Planning documents said the development would create “high quality family accommodation” with large glazed frontages, cedar timber cladding, natural stone finishes and landscaped communal areas.

However, planners concluded they had not been given enough information to determine whether one of the barns could be converted into housing without significant structural work.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21725041.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Current barns at Barker Rails Farm. Courtesy of applicant.. *(Image: Applicant)*

Officers stated: “There is insufficient information to enable the Local Planning Authority to establish the existing structure of Barn Three.”

The proposed demolition of a third barn became another major obstacle for the scheme.

Planners said the applicant had failed to demonstrate why the building needed to be removed as part of the conversion plans and concluded its demolition went beyond what could be justified under the application route being used.

Officers also identified problems with the submitted site boundary plans.

The council found that part of the land proposed for parking fell outside the red-line application boundary, meaning planners could not be certain all elements of the development were included within the application site.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21725042.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Current barns at Barker Rails Farm. Courtesy of applicant.. *(Image: Applicant)*

Despite the refusal, planners accepted that the site had suitable highway access and that the proposed homes would not create significant issues in relation to traffic, flooding or noise.

However, they concluded that concerns over the proposed demolition works, the lack of structural information and inaccuracies in the application plans meant the scheme could not be approved.
