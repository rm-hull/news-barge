## Britain’s Got Talent star spotted busking in York city centre

York singer Finley Barratt-Carter was performing numbers from Johnny Cash and Elvis Presley in a prime York busking pitch this afternoon (Monday, November 16) *(Image: Ben Hardy)*

A singer who found national acclaim on a network TV show which propels previously unknown performers into the spotlight has been seen busking on the streets of York.

Finley Barrett-Carter gained national attention after impressing Britain's Got Talent judges in March with his performance of Elvis Presley’s Burning Love, earning four yes votes from Simon Cowell, Amanda Holden, Alesha Dixon, and KSI.

Eagle-eyed passersby will have seen him busking this afternoon at a popular spot at the junction of Davygate and Stonegate in York city centre (November 16).




He was heard singing hits from US-legend Johnny Cash and "If I can Dream" by Elvis Presley in front of admirers and a queue of lunchtime visitors to Bettys Café Tea Rooms in St Helen's Square.

Mr Barrett-Carter, who studied performance and production at York College, said his time busking in York gave him the confidence to perform at Blackpool’s Winter Gardens for the audition.

His appearance has since been viewed nearly 500,000 times on YouTube, boosting his social media following and public profile in his hometown.

Finley also performed for invited guests at The York Press Community Awards in September.

As reported by The Press, the spot where the Britain's Got Talent star performed today is thought to have some of the best acoustics of the locations in the city.

Musicians, singers and street performers can be found in places such as Parliament Street, King's Square and St Helen's Square, although no busking pitches are fixed as such and performers are encouraged to spend no more than two hours at one pitch.

![St Helen's Square, York](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21724648.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The location around St Helen's Square is thought to have some of the best acoustics for York's buskers *(Image: Ben Hardy)*

The city council says it welcomes buskers and street entertainment to York streets, recognising that they "add colour and vibrancy to public spaces, providing enjoyment for residents, visitors and businesses".

Mr Barrett-Carter has also used his music to support charitable causes.

In March, he raised more than £1,300 for St Leonard’s Hospice through a concert at The Cottage Inn in Haxby, held in partnership with Carefound Home Care.

The event was held in memory of his grandfather Billy and Mark Payne, both of whom were cared for by the hospice and played a role in encouraging his musical journey.
