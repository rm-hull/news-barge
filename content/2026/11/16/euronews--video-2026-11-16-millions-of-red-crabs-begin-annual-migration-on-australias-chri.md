Tens of millions of red crabs have begun their annual migration on Australia's Christmas Island, turning roads and specially built crossings into streams of red on 14 and 15 November 2026.

Following heavy wet-season rains, the crabs are making their way from the island's forests to the coast to breed. Images show large numbers crossing roads, climbing overpasses and following barriers designed to guide them safely along their route. Road closure signs have also been installed to protect the migrating animals.

According to Parks Australia, which oversees the event, preparations begin months in advance. Workers clear underpasses, build special bridges and install kilometres of roadside barriers to keep the crabs away from traffic.

The migration is closely linked to both rainfall and the lunar cycle, with the animals timing their journey to release their eggs into the ocean before dawn, as the high tide recedes during the moon's last quarter.

This mass movement takes place every year, as millions of crabs travel from the forests to the shoreline.
