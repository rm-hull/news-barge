Donald Trump’s MAGA base can’t decide whether the president or weak Republican candidates are to blame for massive losses in the midterm elections.

In what was dubbed a blue tsunami, Democrats took control of the House and Senate while winning historic governor races. Trump and his party are now left to figure out what went wrong and how to rebuild ahead of the 2028 presidential election.

Trump and other administration officials have acknowledged that controversial moves like starting a war in Iran that jacked up gas prices eight months before the midterms hurt them. But the president has, for the most part, refused to take responsibility for the midterm wipeout, despite making himself the face of GOP campaigns in tight races across the country.

Trump insisted that he would’ve won “in a landslide” if he were on the ballot and blamed lousy Republican candidates, the very politicians he fiercely backed weeks prior, and the media for unfavorable coverage.

But what does the president’s base really think?

![When asked what they blame for Republicans’ crushing midterm defeat, 33 percent of people who identified as MAGA said Trump’s actions, while 30 percent said weak GOP campaigns, according to a new poll](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/16/17/19/GettyImages-2293859385.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

When asked what they blame for Republicans’ crushing midterm defeat, 33 percent of people who identified as MAGA said Trump’s actions, while 30 percent said weak GOP campaigns, according to a new Politico poll conducted by PF Global. Just 20 percent said strong Democratic campaigns led to the losses and 17 percent were unsure.

When the same question was asked to people who voted for Trump in the past but did not identify as MAGA, 55 percent blamed the president while just 17 percent blamed weak GOP campaigns. Another 16 percent said strong Democratic campaigns led to the losses and 12 percent were unsure.

“It is very clear that Election Day 2026 was an unequivocal rebuke of Donald Trump, both him personally and his policies,” a GOP operative who worked on midterm races told Politico.

“MAGA needs a serious rebrand if it is going to have any success in the future.”

![Trump insisted that he would’ve won 'in a landslide' if he were on the ballot and blamed lousy Republican candidates, the very politicians he fiercely backed weeks prior, and the media for unfavorable coverage](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/16/17/27/GettyImages-2302566480.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Most Trump voters who identify as MAGA, 59 percent, think the GOP should move closer toward Trump and his agenda, despite the midterm shellacking, while 28 percent said the party should distance itself from the president. Another 13 percent were unsure.

But non-MAGA Trump voters feel the opposite, with 51 percent saying it’s best to distance the GOP from the president and 30 percent saying the party should move closer to him. Another 20 percent were unsure.

While Trump voters remain divided on how to move forward, the White House insists Trump is “delivering results.”

“President Trump will never stop delivering results for the American people and he will undoubtedly go down as the most successful and consequential president in American history,” White House spokesperson Davis Ingle told Politico.
