The National Weather Service in the US has confirmed that an EF1 tornado touched down south-east of Newark in the Salem Woods neighbourhood, Delaware, on Thursday. The tornado reached 160 metres wide, had a path length of 4.4 miles (7.1km) and reached estimated peak winds of up to 100mph (161km/h). There were no injuries or fatalities but windows and roofs were damaged on several homes, a large hotel sign fell on to a number of cars, and a semi truck overturned on I95. Although Delaware often experiences a few tornadoes every year, this is only the second time the state has had tornado activity in November since 1950, according to the National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration. The only other November tornado recorded was in 1989.

Severe storms have also resulted in tornadoes in Argentina as well as large hailstones. A round of severe thunderstorms moved through the central Córdoba province on Friday, bringing hailstones up to the size of a baseball (about 7.5cm in diameter). Parts of the province recorded up to 73mm of rainfall in just six hours early on Saturday. Such storms are common at this time of year. Water vapour from the Amazon meeting the Andes quickly condenses, resulting in massive cloud formations often associated with heavy downpours, lightning, tornadoes and large hailstones.

India’s meteorological department has released an alert for a depression located in the Bay of Bengal with the potential to develop into a cyclone. A low-pressure system formed during the early hours of Friday before developing into a depression on Sunday. The department’s forecast is for the depression to intensify into a cyclonic storm by Tuesday before gradually drifting north-west towards eastern India, with the expectation it will make landfall on Thursday. If the storm were to develop it would become the third of the season and be named Akvan. There is a “watch” level warning for much of the eastern coast on Wednesday and Thursday, with parts of the Andhra Pradesh and Odisha states on an “alert” level.
