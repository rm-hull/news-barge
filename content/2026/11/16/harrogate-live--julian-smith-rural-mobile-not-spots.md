Sir Julian Smith asked ministers how North Yorkshire's remaining mobile not-spots will be fixed. A minister pointed to satellite phone signal for remote places.

Sir Julian Smith, the Conservative MP for Skipton and Ripon, has asked the Government what it will do about the places in North Yorkshire and the rest of rural Britain that still have poor mobile signal or none at all, now that the Shared Rural Network programme has hit its targets.

The minister who answered agreed that gaps remain, and said satellite technology that connects straight to ordinary phones could reach some of the most remote spots where a mast is not practical.

## What Sir Julian asked

Sir Julian raised it at Digital, Culture, Media and Sport questions in the House of Commons. His office says he praised the Shared Rural Network, the programme that has improved mobile coverage in rural communities, but warned that some areas are still struggling with weak or non-existent signal.

With the programme’s targets now met, he asked ministers what plans they have to make sure the remaining “not-spots” are dealt with.

The Skipton and Ripon seat takes in Ripon and Nidderdale.

## What the minister said

Ian Murray, Minister of State for Creative Industries, Media and Online Safety, replied for the Government. According to Sir Julian’s office, he:

* acknowledged that the Shared Rural Network had worked, and agreed that coverage gaps remain;
* pointed to emerging direct-to-device satellite technology as a possible answer for the most remote locations, where masts and other ground-based equipment may not be practical;
* confirmed that the Government will keep working closely with the mobile industry so that the technologies available are used to get rid of the remaining not-spots.
