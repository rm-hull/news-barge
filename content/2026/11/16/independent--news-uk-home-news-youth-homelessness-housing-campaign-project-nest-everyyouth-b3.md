For most young people, leaving home for the first time means heading off to university or perhaps moving into a flat with friends.

But Mohammed’s first night away from his family was a far more frightening experience.

On a January afternoon last year, the 24-year-old was forced to gather his belongings while frantically contacting friends and family across the country, hoping someone would give him a place to stay, or even a car to sleep in.

But as the day became colder and it grew dark, Mohammed was left with no option. Battling sub-zero temperatures, he bought himself a sleeping bag and found a nearby park to spend the night.

Hours earlier, Mohammed’s father had kicked him out of the family home amid an ongoing dispute over him having a girlfriend, which was forbidden in their Muslim household. He told Mohammed: “You're no longer my son.”

“I had nowhere to go. I turned to my friends, I turned to my family, and everyone just kept shutting doors on me,” Mohammed told *The Independent*.

His situation is far from unique. Mohammed is one of the hundreds of thousands of young people aged 16 to 24 who face homelessness in Britain each year.

Many of them, like Mohammed, are in employment and earning wages, while others are desperate for any opportunity to give them a leg-up.

Some 40,000 of these young people are reached by EveryYouth, a network of 21 youth homelessness hubs operating across the UK that help young people get their lives back on course.

*The Independent* has joined forces with the charity to raise £200k, which will be spent renovating three empty, dilapidated properties in Sheffield, Fife, and Basingstoke into safe and affordable housing for young people.

Our Project Nest campaign has drawn the support of prime minister Andy Burnham as we set out to encourage young people to start employment or training and rebuild their lives, while also renovating derelict properties currently wasted and littering towns and cities across the UK.

Mohammed knows the challenges that young people have to overcome just to get back on their feet. At one time, he was waking up at 5am to shower and wash his clothes at his friend’s house, before clocking in for his supermarket job at 7am.

In the hours that he was not working, he was queuing outside his local council, pleading for accommodation.

The council knew he had been sleeping in a park, but told him they could not offer any help because he was employed.

“They said, ‘At the moment, we have no housing available, but we advise you to try to look for a private rental’.”

But Mohammed, who was working 27 hours a week and earning around £1,200 a month, couldn’t afford any accommodation.

“I'd be the first person waiting... I kept going, waiting and waiting and waiting,” he said. “I was there for hours, and I was still getting the same response.”

He had considered quitting his job in the hope it would help him get a roof over his head, but ultimately decided he wanted to stay in work.

“There were times I was thinking to myself, ‘am I ever going to be on my feet again? Am I going to be the same person again?”

![The Independent has set out to raise £200k and give young people a safe place to live](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/11/13/27/PrimaryBlack-ProjectNestLogo.png?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Eventually, the council told him to get in contact with EveryYouth’s delivery partner Roundabout, which provides affordable emergency accommodation, support and life skills to young people in South Yorkshire.

He got in touch with the charity on a Wednesday. By Friday, Mohammed had been given somewhere to live and sleep.

“As soon as I got that call to come view the house, I was just over the moon. I was so excited, I was so happy I didn't have to sleep in that park again. If only you could understand how bad it was.

“The first night in the house, I slept like a baby. I was so happy that I had a window, a bed, a mattress, a comfortable place, a heater.

“I was in such a happy mood that I didn’t have to text my friend in the morning and say, ‘Can I have a shower at yours?’”

Mohammed stayed there for around nine months before the charity helped him organise social housing, where he now lives.

He returned to the park after finding out he was getting permanent housing, as a reminder of how far he’d come. “I was like, ‘Yes, I was here, and now I made it’.”

*The Independent*’s campaign comes at a critical time for young people, who face a perfect storm of housing, employment, and cost of living crises.

Nick Connolly, EveryYouth’s chief executive, said young people are up against “unbelievably difficult” barriers and competing systems.

“If you are a young person with a future who just has been really unlucky and it's all gone a bit wrong for them, there's nowhere that provides the support that you need.

“We're really focused on the support that young people need to move beyond homelessness.

“It's trying to address mental health problems, it's trying to fill educational gaps, and it's trying to give young people the support they need to go and get jobs and move out on their own.”
