Donald Trump reported trades this year that suggest he has held between $13 million and $62 million in Nvidia stock, disclosures that will likely escalate scrutiny into the president’s relationship to the AI giant and his administration’s oversight of an industry at the center of international scandal.

Financial disclosures, reviewed by *The Washington Post,* revealed that the president’s Nvidia stakes grew sharply from an estimated $615,000 to $1.3 million at the end of 2024 to as much as $62 million within the last year. The latest filings ​do not capture trades from the past two months, meaning that the values could be even higher.

The disclosures follow Jensen Huang’s resignation as Nvidia CEO after the revelation of his extramarital affair with ‌Chinese businesswoman Alice Huang, who is accused of having intelligence ties to Beijing.

White House officials have repeatedly maintained that the president does not personally pick or trade individual stocks and Trump has rejected calls to divest his stock holdings. Trump’s assets are in a trust managed by his children and independent financial managers handle his portfolio, according to the White House.

But enormous growth of the president’s financial stakes in Nvidia are in parallel to the president’s close relationship to Jensen Huang and the administration’s regulatory moves that have opened the door for the company to do business in China. The president has routinely defended his administration against criticism over export controls that appear to have been lucrative for both Nvidia and the president.

![President Donald Trump’s stock trades suggest he has held between $13 million and $62 million in Nvidia stock, according to recent disclosures](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/16/17/2298607192..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Nvidia’s stock is trading at roughly $157.72, down over 3.1 percent on the day, as investors react to Huang’s blockbuster resignation ahead of a third-quarter earnings call scheduled for Tuesday.

The company’s board, however, is not considering bringing him back, according to CNBC.

Huang’s resignation last week followed news of his affair with an executive at Megaspeed, a Singapore-based Nvidia customer. U.S. officials have suspected Alice Huang of spying for China, and the Commerce Department had been investigating whether Megaspeed breached U.S. export restrictions.

*The New York Times*reported that U.S. intelligence determined Alice Huang had been recruited to influence Jensen Huang to push Nvidia toward selling Beijing more of its advanced chips.* The Times* also reported Jensen Huang’s second affair, with a Nvidia subordinate, moved the company’s board to force him out last week.

Trump, however, appeared to cast doubt on his own administration’s assessment.

“I don’t know that she’s a spy at all. Nobody's proven that,” he told reporters outside the White House on Monday.

“He probably shouldn’t have been doing, on a personal basis, what he did. You know, from — who knows? That's a different story. But nobody's proven that she's a spy. She may not be a spy,” he said.

![Disclosure of Trump’s financial stake in the AI giant come under heightened scrutiny into his administration’s oversight of the industry and policy moves that opened the door for the company to do business in China](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/13/15/42/GettyImages-2197461218.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“Usually what happens with that is that any woman coming to the country that’s a beautiful woman that comes from China, they say she’s a spy,” he continued, “I don’t know that she was a spy at all. I mean, I really don’t.”

According to *The New York Times*, State Secretary Marco Rubio drafted a classified memorandum on Alice Huang’s alleged scheme and had even briefed the president about her suspected activities while the pair were traveling to China on Air Force One in May — months before Jensen Huang’s admission of the affair.

But Trump reportedly brushed the warnings aside and maintained his relationship with the tech mogul, who frequently appeared at the White House and donated $25 million to the president's ballroom project.

Asked on Monday whether he trusted the intelligence that Rubio reportedly provided him, Trump appeared to suggest he thought the reporting was false.

“Who told me that?” he told reporters. “I haven’t heard that.”

![Nvidia’s stock is down over 3.1 percent on the day November 16 as investors react to Huang’s blockbuster resignation ahead of a third-quarter earnings call](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/16/17/2304940492..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Congressional watchdogs are eager to dive into the president’s stocks and his administration’s policies surrounding AI and Nvidia, which include easing Biden-era export restrictions on American AI chips to China in May 2025. That decision opened the door for Nvidia to sell its chips to approved Chinese customers later that year, with a portion of revenue to the U.S. government.

In December, Trump allowed Nvidia to sell its advanced H200 chips to approved customers in China, with 25 percent of the revenue going to the U.S.

Democratic Senator Elizabeth Warren asked Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent this summer whether the government should investigate Trump’s purchase of as much as $1 million in Nvidia stock — just one week before the Commerce Department formalized those export restrictions. Bessent responded by suggesting that Congress should look into banning its own members from stock trading before looking to the White House.

Trump has sought to diffuse tension among his Cabinet officials in wake of the scandal, following reports that Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick prevented U.S. oversight on companies suspected of diverting Nvidia’s advanced AI chips to China.

That reportedly escalated to a Situation Room showdown between Lutnick and Bessent, who appeared to put the blame on the Commerce chief during an appearance on CNBC last week.

The departments of Treasury, Defense and State and the Office of the U.S. Trade Representative all “were in favor of putting Megaspeed on the entity list for months,” Bessent told the network.

Notably not on that list: Commerce Department.

Trump, however, appeared to acknowledge that Lutnick likely resisted putting Megaspeed — seen as a pass-through to help Beijing evade export controls — on his department’s “Entity List” of firms not allowed to purchase American products.

“He was doing that because you could have — if you didn’t delay it, If you didn’t do something — you could have a blow up with China,” Trump said Monday. “We’re having a very good relationship with China right now, and I understand both sides of it, and I’ll make a decision. But I understand very well both sides.”

Republican and Democratic members of Congress, meanwhile, are jockeying for Jensen Huang to testify before several committees.
