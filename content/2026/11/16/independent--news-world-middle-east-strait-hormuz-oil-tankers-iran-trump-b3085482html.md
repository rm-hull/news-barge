Iran has claimed a sixth oil tanker has been struck in the Strait of Hormuz since Sunday, as tensions in the region continue to escalate.

The United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations confirmed a vessel was hit at around 6am GMT on Monday. While all crew members are safe and accounted for, the full extent of the structural damage remains unclear.

The Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) said half a dozen tankers have now been struck in the strait since Sunday, either by mines or projectiles. The latest incident comes the day after Tehran launched drone attacks against a US military base in Bahrain, following a similar strike Friday.

Bahrain condemned the resumption of attacks as “heinous aggression,” rejecting Tehran’s “flimsy pretexts and baseless claims”.

![The incident follows reports of multiple vessels in the Strait hitting Iranian-installed sea mines](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/08/14/13/2289908021..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“The kingdom also rejects the transformation of its territory into an arena for settling scores and holds Iran fully responsible for these attacks and their consequences,” the country’s ministry of foreign affairs said in a statement.

While the Pentagon has not formally commented on Sunday's strike, US Central Command has rejected claims that the Iranian attacks were successful.

But tensions continue to escalate against a backdrop of further American military deployment in the Gulf. The US confirmed Monday the aircraft carrier USS Theodore Roosevelt has arrived in the Middle East, joining two other carrier strike groups already operating in the region.

Despite the bolstered naval presence, US president Donald Trump has signalled Washington will hold off on ordering fresh strikes – provided there are no new attacks from Tehran or Yemen’s Houthi rebels.

![President Donald Trump has signalled Washington will hold off on ordering renewed strikes against Iran](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/14/14/2026-11-14T110018Z_932004478_RC2LKOAEPGUW_RTRMADP_3_IRAN-CRISIS-TRUMP.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The White House’s latest position comes in the wake of devastating midterm election results for the Republican Party, with voters expressing their anger over soaring prices sparked by the unpopular conflict.

Diplomatic efforts to end the nearly nine-month conflict – sparked by joint US and Israeli airstrikes on Iran back on February 28 – remain deadlocked.

Trump suggested on Truth Social last week, however, there was no prospect of a renewed agreement with Iran to end the war.

“Those days have long since passed,” the US president wrote. “We’ve got their money, hundreds of billions of dollars, and we've got their Strait!”

![The Strait of Hormuz shipping route continues to be a key sticking point in the war](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/03/02/9/30/strait-of-hormuz-map.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

On Sunday, Iranian parliament speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf warned that Iran would not remain trapped in a state of “neither war nor peace,” stressing that Tehran would not sit back and wait to be struck first.

Foreign minister Abbas Araghchi echoed those warnings, arguing further US and Israeli aggression threatens wider regional stability.

Meanwhile, United Nations secretary-general António Guterres used a speech in Manila on Monday to call for a “serious diplomatic process” to break the impasse – warning that the prolonged crisis is already inflicting “devastating consequences” on the global economy.
