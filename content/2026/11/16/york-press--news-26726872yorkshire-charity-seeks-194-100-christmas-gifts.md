## Yorkshire charity needs £194,100 to give Christmas gifts to 3,235 children

A boy hugging his gift *(Image: Provided)*

A CHARITY is pushing a campaign to bring Christmas cheer to thousands of children living in poverty.

Yorkshire Children's Charity is aiming to raise £194,100 through its Yorkshire Together campaign to fund Christmas presents for 3,235 children across the region.

Each child will receive a gift worth £60, carefully selected based on their personal wish list, to ensure they have something special to open on Christmas morning.

Charlotte Farrington, CEO of Yorkshire Children's Charity, said: "I am incredibly proud to call Yorkshire home.

"We are a county with a strong sense of identity, where people care about their communities and where, when things get difficult, we have a long tradition of looking out for one another."

The campaign comes in response to a 62 per cent rise in applications for support compared to last year, highlighting the growing need across the county.

Charlotte said: "There is an enormous difference between having to cut back on Christmas spending and living in the kind of poverty where your child sleeps in their coat because you cannot afford bedding, or where siblings regularly share a pair of shoes."

As part of the campaign, the charity has received applications for 3,235 children identified as living in destitution who may otherwise have nothing to open on Christmas morning.

Children are referred by schools, social workers and other trusted professionals who understand their circumstances.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21679718.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Volunteers preparing the presents *(Image: Provided)*

Charlotte added: "We ask parents and carers what their children would like, and we purchase those presents, allocating £60 to each child.

"The gifts are then passed back to families, who can wrap them and put them under the tree themselves.

"The children don't need to know where the presents came from or feel different from their friends because their family has needed support from a charity.

"They can simply enjoy Christmas morning, while their parents experience the happiness of watching them open something they have wished for."

Volunteers have already filled all picking and packing slots, but the charity now needs donations to fund the gifts themselves.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21714480.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Charlotte Farrington *(Image: Provided)*

Charlotte said: "I appreciate that not everyone can afford to give £60, and I would never want anyone to feel that a smaller donation isn't worthwhile.

"If you can spare £5, £10 or £20, it will be combined with other contributions to help fulfil a child's wish list."

The charity is also encouraging businesses to get involved through fundraising, and welcomes support with deliveries across Yorkshire.

She added: "What I am asking is that we all do whatever we reasonably can, because I have seen Yorkshire people achieve remarkable things when they come together for a cause they believe in, and I believe we can do that again."
