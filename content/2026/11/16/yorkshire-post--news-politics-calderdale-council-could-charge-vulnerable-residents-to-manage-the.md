# Calderdale Council could charge vulnerable residents to manage their money

## Cash-strapped Calderdale Council could claw back £105,000 a year by charging vulnerable residents to manage their money.

Without a formal deputyship policy, the council previously absorbed the cost managing the finances of residents who lacked mental capacity and had nobody else to act on their behalf.

According to papers submitted to cabinet this week, an internal review found the council was not using an established national framework that allows local authorities to recover deputyship costs.

A new standalone policy, that was voted through by cabinet on Monday November 9 could expand the caseload of deputyship appointments from four to around 100.

![Nikki Kelly, the cabinet member for health and social care at Calderdale Council](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOjBmOTI0MDUzLWQwMGQtNDNiNi1hN2ZiLTdlNDRhNzVkNjA3MDo4MDkzMDg3Mi05MWFmLTRiZTktODg2OC0wZDFhMmRhYTg0OWI=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


This could recover a total of £105,400 a year – £78,000 for management work plus £27,400 for Office of the Public Guardian reporting requirements.

There could also be a £94,400 windfall in relation to initial appointment work if the Court of Protection allows.

Cabinet papers say the council manages the financial affairs of 185 people in house, including 181 for whom it is a corporate appointee and four as a public authority deputy.

There are a further 80 appointeeship cases managed through an outsourced arrangement that are expected to come in house, with 20 potentially requiring property and affairs deputyship.

Cabinet member for health and social care, Coun Nikki Kelly, said: “Adopting the policy will strengthen governance, provide consistency in service delivery, support adults who lack capacity and allow the council to recover legitimate costs in line with the national guidance and practice direction.”

Leader of the council Dan Sutherland said: “This is an area where an older person has no significant others within their life that can help them to manage their property affairs. That’s when the council will step in and provide that support.

“This has been done previously, but what we are bringing in is a policy so we can be clear to all involved what the council will do and when it will do it.”
