At least 17 people were killed in two separate shootings in South Africa over the weekend, police have confirmed.

Nine people, including three women, were found dead with gunshot wounds inside a shack in the informal settlement of Jeffsville, located near the capital of Pretoria. The incident is reported to have occurred between 10 pm and 11 pm local time.

Eight others were found dead inside a vehicle in Sweetwaters outside the city of Pietermaritzburg, located near the coastal city of Durban.

![South Africa police define a mass shooting as when three or more people are shot in a single attack](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/14/10/2296829428..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Police have not yet established a motive for either of the killings.

Gauteng Police Commissioner Tommy Mthombeni said the killings could be related to a previous shooting at a nearby informal settlement in Saulsville last year where 12 people were killed and 13 injured.

He said one of the people who was killed during the shooting was a “person of interest” in the shooting that happened in Saulsville last year.

“We cannot rule out that they may be linked, and may possibly have been a retaliation attack,” Mthombeni said.

In the Saulsville case from last year, Mthombeni said police had made a breakthrough and arrested one suspect who is believed to be one of the mass shooters.

Gauteng Premier Panyaza Lesufi, who visited the crime scene with the police on Monday, said the provincial government was coordinating with other provincial departments to support the police in their investigations of similar crimes happening in the province, which includes the city of Johannesburg.

In the Pietermaritzburg shooting, police spokesperson Robert Netshiunda said some families had come forward to help with identifying the victims but it was yet to be confirmed.

South Africa has one of the highest murder rates in the world, averaging about 60 every day.

While there is no standard global definition of mass shooting, in South Africa police define a mass shooting as when three or more people are shot in a single attack.

The country also has high rates of gun crime, including shootings related to local political rivalries.

Authorities say that many of the mass shootings are linked to criminal gangs fighting turf wars or using violence to establish control over communities and areas. The mass killings also tend to happen in the country’s poorest communities

A report from the Global Initiative Against Transnational Organized Crime found that there had been 43 killings in South Africa this year that were suspected to be politically motivated.

![Many mass shootings in South Africa are linked to criminal gangs](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/16/09/2296829409..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

In September, at least 17 people were killed in a mass shooting at a bar in Gauteng. All of the victims, including four women, were declared dead at the scene by the ambulance service.

Last month, at least two people were killed after a gunman opened fire at a memorial service in Katlehong township, located southeast of Johannesburg.

The two victims were members of the youth league of the African National Congress (ANC) party, the largest party in South Africa’s Parliament which describes itself as a “disciplined force of the left”.

The suspected gunman turned himself in while the South African Police Service said that the shooting was being investigated by a political killings task team.
