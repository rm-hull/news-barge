**Campaign groups are calling on Mayor David Skaith to provide clear spending plans and firm commitments for transport improvements across York and North Yorkshire, arguing that his draft Local Transport Plan needs to explain how its ambitions will be delivered.**

The Low Traffic Future alliance says the draft Local Transport Plan sets out a vision for a better-connected transport network, with journeys that are safer, healthier, more environmentally friendly, accessible and affordable.

However, the campaigners believe it lacks sufficient detail about what will actually be delivered and how improvements will be funded.

The alliance has launched its **Better Journeys for North Yorkshire** campaign, bringing together environmental and sustainable transport groups from across the region. It is encouraging residents to support calls for the Mayor to explain his funding priorities and the environmental, health and other benefits the plan is expected to achieve.

## Rail improvements among campaign demands

The Local Transport Plan is intended to set out a vision for transport improvements up to 2050, alongside more detailed spending plans for the next five years.

Campaigners want the plan to support a tram network for York, as well as the reopening of railway links between Hull and York via Beverley, and between Skipton and Colne.

They are also calling for:

* Stopping trains between York and Newcastle.
* More frequent rail services to Scarborough and Whitby.
* Longer platforms at Sherburn in Elmet to allow trains between Hull and Manchester to stop there.

## Better buses and integrated transport

The groups are seeking clear funding commitments for improvements to local bus services, including new routes, higher frequencies, electric and accessible buses, bus priority schemes and better bus stops and interchanges.

They also want better integration between different forms of public transport, including connected timetables and ticketing, more accessible travel information and real-time updates when services are disrupted.

Improved pedestrian, cycling and disabled access to, from and within railway stations is also part of their proposals.

Other requests include support for car-sharing and ride-sharing schemes, bike-share schemes and community transport services such as dial-a-ride.

## Safer streets and active travel

Campaigners are also calling for investment in walking and cycling networks, including safer crossings of major roads and junctions, better street environments and improved pedestrian and cycle signage.

The proposals include making 20mph the default speed limit in built-up areas, introducing school streets and low-traffic neighbourhoods, traffic calming measures and action to tackle pavement parking.

They also want travel awareness campaigns and cycle training to encourage people of all ages and abilities to consider active travel.

Road and path maintenance is another priority, with the groups calling for greater attention to safety, comfort and convenience for people walking, cycling or using mobility aids.

## Plans not prose

> **Roger Geffen, Low Traffic Future, said:**
>
> The fine words in Mayor David Skaith’s draft plan set out a very appealing vision for better transport in North Yorkshire. But delivering that vision needs more than nice prose – it needs proper spending plans and clear predictions of the expected benefits. Otherwise, nobody can tell whether his plan will really provide the solutions the region needs, particularly for young people and others who do not drive.

The campaign brings together representatives from the North Yorkshire Climate Coalition, Forwards York: Forwards Yorkshire, Railfuture Yorkshire, the Minsters Rail Campaign, the York Bus Forum, Zero Carbon Harrogate, Climate Action Skipton, Thirsk Friends of the Earth, and Whitby and Esk Valley Active Travel.

Several of the organisations also plan to submit their own responses to the consultation.

## Residents urged to respond to consultation

The Mayor’s draft Local Transport Plan is currently open for public consultation, with responses due by Wednesday 18 November 2026.

Residents can find out more about the campaign and submit an editable consultation response through the Better Journeys for North Yorkshire campaign page.

The consultation documents are available from the York and North Yorkshire Combined Authority website.

Further information about the campaign is available from Low Traffic Future.
