A young man has been barred from driving his disabled father’s Motability car after a black box recorded him speeding close to 100mph on a 60mph-limit road “for an extended period”, an ombudsman heard.

It was one “extreme” incident during a four-week period where the unnamed man was logged driving poorly when accelerating, braking and cornering.

The young man was subsequently removed from the car’s insurance cover by Motability, prompting a complaint from his father, who said his son was the primary driver and was relied upon for essential travel.

Motability allows up to three drivers such as friends and carers as part of hire agreements set up for people who claim a qualifying mobility allowance, most commonly through Personal Independent Payment (PIP). More than 900,000 use the scheme, with monthly payments paid through customers’ benefits.

In his complaint to the financial ombudsman, the disabled man said he was concerned Motability Operations, which runs Motability, had acted inappropriately and not considered his personal circumstances.

However, the company said its decision to bar his son from using the car was based on two consecutive “red” weeks relating to poor drive scores or extreme high-risk events recorded by the car’s black box.

It also said it sent a warning letter in February this year, before taking the action a month later.

In his decision, ombudsman Simon Wingfield wrote: “From looking at the data a score between 0 and 40 would be considered red. I can see Mr I’s [the disabled man] son had scores between around 20 and 30 for four consecutive weeks.

## What is Motability?

The Motability scheme allows benefit claimants to use their allowance to pay for a car on a three-year lease. They must be claiming one of just six specific mobility-related allowances. This could be through PIP, the Disability Living Allowance, War Pensioners’ Mobility Supplement and a few others.

“The scores were mainly impacted by acceleration, braking and cornering. But I note there was a week in March where an extreme speeding event took place where the car was recorded as travelling close to 100 miles an hour in a 60 mile an hour limit road and was speeding for an extended period.”

He added: “Given the scheme terms allows it to remove drivers after two consecutive red weeks, I don’t find I have the grounds to say it was unfair to remove the named driver because it was acting in line with the terms that Mr I had agreed to.”

The disabled man also raised concerns over the black box system, claiming that since the incident the company had changed the way it used the devices. He said other people used the car, including his wife, and that there were occasions when different users may have connected their phones to the device.

But Mr Wingfield said Motability Operations was “fairly able” to rely on the data it was provided from the black box.

The man’s complaint was not upheld, and his son remains uncovered by the vehicle’s insurance for until March 2029.
