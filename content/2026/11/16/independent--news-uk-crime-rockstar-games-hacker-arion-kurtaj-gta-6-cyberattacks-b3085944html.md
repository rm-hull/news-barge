A computer hacker responsible for leaking online footage from the upcoming Grand Theft Auto VI video game has confessed to targeting a string of major global businesses.

Arion Kurtaj, a 21-year-old from Oxfordshire, admitted carrying out digital breaches against GTA publisher Rockstar Games alongside companies including Uber, Revolut and BT/EE.

The Crown Prosecution Service (CPS) confirmed he entered his pleas at Woolwich Crown Court on Monday. The defendant was identified as a member of the "infamous cybercrime group" Lapsus$, with the offences occurring between August 2021 and September 2022.

Operating online under the alias TeaPotUberHacker, Kurtaj published 90 clips of unreleased GTA VI gameplay and warned he would publish the underlying source code.

The game itself is scheduled to launch on November 19.

During a separate cyberattack targeting Uber, Kurtaj transmitted an obscene image to staff, extracted confidential records, and erased internal files, inflicting financial damage exceeding 2.8 million dollars (£2.1 million).

He further breached Revolut’s infrastructure, exposing details belonging to thousands of customers, while his intrusion into BT/EE servers triggered a rise in unauthorised Sim swaps.

![The 21-year-old, from Oxfordshire, took 90 gameplay videos from GTA VI and released them online under the handle TeaPotUberHacker](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/16/13/2304571192..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Sim swapping allows criminals to trick providers into switching phone numbers to a new Sim card, letting them take control of victims’ calls and texts and giving them access to bank or social media accounts, the CPS said.

Kurtaj was charged in relation to his BT/EE hack, which he committed between August and November 2021, but kept carrying out similar attacks after being moved to a hotel for his own protection in September 2022 using a mobile phone, the hotel TV’s Wi-Fi and a firestick to continue his campaign.

The defendant pleaded guilty to three counts of an unauthorised act to impair the operation of a computer, one count of fraud, one count of securing unauthorised access to computer material with intent and one count of possession of a mobile phone in prison.

He sent a “mocking message” to 999 from the phone in jail.

He will be sentenced at the same court on November 23.

![The attacks took place between August 2021 and September 2022](https://static.independent.co.uk/2023/08/14/18/647d9868e206ed31f8070d7f5d8ae01dY29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNjkyMTE2MjA2-2.22678865.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Robert Hutchinson, specialist prosecutor for the CPS, said: “Arion Kurtaj used sophisticated hacking techniques to carry out a sustained campaign of cyberattacks, causing serious disruption to major international business and putting sensitive information at risk.

“The defendant was involved in a hacking group responsible for attacks which caused millions of dollars in losses.

“His offending was calculated and, even after being charged with serious offences, he continued to target organisations, demonstrating a persistent disregard for the harm he was causing and the law.”

Kurtaj was previously found unfit to plead, and a jury sitting on a trial of the facts found in July 2023 that he had committed the acts alleged against him.

He was given an indefinite hospital order at a sentencing hearing in December that year.

Following a further assessment in 2025, Kurtaj was found fit to plead and the CPS decided to reinstate proceedings.
