**Radio 4 presenter Emma Barnett is set to leave the Today programme this week for a multiplatform new BBC show in the New Year.**

Former Woman's Hour host Barnett has developed a "bold" new show, she says, which will launch in 2027 across BBC Sounds, BBC iPlayer and YouTube.

Her final day presenting Radio 4's flagship morning news and current affairs show will be on Wednesday 18 November.

"For the last nearly six years - it's been incredible to talk to the mighty Radio 4 listeners," she said in a statement. "Having proudly hosted Woman's Hour and now the Today Programme - I've had the rare pleasure of hosting nearly every hour of the morning on this truly unique and special radio network."

She added: "But it's not goodbye to the BBC audience I value so greatly because I have something else for them that I hope will add something meaningful and entertaining to their lives."

Posting on Instagram, external, she said there would be more information on what her new show would be about soon.

"Watch this space and look ready. I am… More soon."

Barnett joined Today in May 2024, taking over from Martha Kearney to work alongside co-presenters Nick Robinson, Mishal Husain, Amol Rajan and Justin Webb. Husain and Rajan have since left.

Today programme editor Rebecca Keating said Barnett had brought "enormous energy" to Today, with "a keen focus" on listeners.

She noted how Barnett had a proven "track record of telling stories of personal hardship that influence public debate" but how she was "also equally at home interviewing British icons like Dame Judi Dench.

"We will miss her generous tea making and wish her every success in her new venture."

In August, Barnett, 41, revealed she had had a hysterectomy and surgery for endometriosis, days before a documentary she made about the condition was broadcast.

She returned to the Today programme explaining she had been absent in recent weeks because she was in hospital "in a case of life imitating work".

Speaking on her BBC podcast, Ready to Talk with Emma Barnett, last year, she told listeners that finding out she was going through perimenopause at the age of 38 felt like she had been "mugged, robbed".

The broadcaster said it was the "first time in my life I haven't really wanted to be a woman - it's the first time I've thought, I'd really quite like to be a bloke".

Radio 4 controller Mohit Bakaya said he was "delighted she's staying with the BBC" and would "look forward to her new show."
