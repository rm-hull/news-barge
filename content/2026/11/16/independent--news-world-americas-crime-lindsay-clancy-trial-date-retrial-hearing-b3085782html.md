Lindsay Clancy is set to stand trial for a second time in March 2027 over the murders of her three young children.

A Massachusetts judge set a new trial date for March 29 during a court hearing on Monday, days after rejecting her bid to have the case thrown out.

Clancy’s first trial ended in a mistrial on Sept. 4 after a single holdout juror prevented the 12-member jury from reaching a unanimous verdict on murder charges. Clancy’s lawyer had argued at trial that she was in the throes of postpartum psychosis when she killed her children at their home in 2023.

After the mistrial, Judge William Sullivan rejected Clancy's argument that the case should be dismissed because a second trial would violate her constitutional rights.

![Lindsay Clancy in court on Monday, Nov. 16, 2026, in Plymouth, Mass.](https://static.the-independent.com/2026/11/16/15/download..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“Because the court concludes that there was manifest necessity for a mistrial based on the jury's deadlock, a retrial on the murder indictments does not violate Clancy's right not to be placed in double jeopardy,” Sullivan wrote last week.

He also denied prosecutors’ request for a gag order, to avoid influencing a future jury, while the case remains pending. Clancy's attorney Kevin Reddington gave interviews during the trial while prosecutors generally didn't speak to reporters. Clancy’s lawyers argued a gag order would’ve amounted to an unconstitutional restraint on speech.

Prosecutors had sought a May trial date, citing scheduling conflicts, but Sullivan chose March 29 in Plymouth Superior Court.

Clancy, 36, faced first-degree murder charges over the deaths of her daughter Cora, 5; son Dawson, 3; and 8-month-old son Callan. If convicted of those charges, she would be sentenced to life in prison without parole.

![Lindsay Clancy’s ex-husband testified at her first trial and has said he forgives her](https://static.the-independent.com/2026/08/25/14/47/2968733-library-6ae83e71.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

No one at Clancy's first trial disputed that she strangled her children with exercise bands on Jan. 24, 2023, in the basement of their home in the Boston suburb of Duxbury. Immediately after the children’s killings, Clancy cut herself with a knife and jumped from a second-story window in an attempt to end ​her own life, leaving her paralyzed.

Clancy's lawyers have argued that she should be found not guilty by reason of insanity, known as lack of criminal responsibility in Massachusetts.

After the mistrial, some jurors said in TV interviews that they were prepared to find Clancy not guilty by reason of insanity but one juror held out. A not guilty by reason of insanity verdict would have resulted in Clancy being sent to a state psychiatric hospital, subject to regular court review.

The holdout juror in her first murder trial, Michael Desronvil, said in an interview that he was struck by Clancy’s “thought process” during the killings and that he thought she was very “methodical” while strangling her children.

Clancy’s ex-husband, Patrick Clancy, found the children’s bodies. He has said in interviews that he forgives her and revealed in a *60 Minutes* interview in September that he and his new wife, a fertility doctor, are starting a new family and plan on sharing the memories of his lost children with their future brothers or sisters.

Lindsay Clancy is scheduled to return to court at 9 a.m. Dec. 30 to discuss trial logistics, security and media arrangements. A pretrial conference is set for Feb. 18.
