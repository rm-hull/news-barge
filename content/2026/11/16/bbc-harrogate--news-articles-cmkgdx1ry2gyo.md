The crash happened in the early hours of Sunday morning just outside Leeds city centre

**A man has appeared in court charged with causing the deaths of two people by dangerous driving after a crash in Leeds.**

Cameron Bell, 22, and 23-year-old Jade Langton were killed in the collision in the early hours of Sunday.

West Yorkshire Police said officers had been following a blue BMW 123D when it failed to stop and crashed into a white Volkswagen Scirocco at the junction of Gelderd Road and Lowfields Road at 00:44 GMT.

Leigh Ross, 39, from Harrogate, appeared on Monday at Leeds Magistrates' Court, where he was remanded in custody to appear at Leeds Crown Court on 17 December. No pleas were entered during the short hearing.

The defendant, of Prospect Place, has been charged with two counts of causing death by dangerous driving, two counts of causing death by driving a vehicle while uninsured, two counts of causing death by driving a vehicle while disqualified and two counts of causing death having taken a vehicle without consent.

Ross sobbed in the dock and his voice broke as he was asked to confirm his date of birth and address.

As he was leaving the dock, he looked towards the public gallery and said: "I'm sorry."

Ryan Thornton, prosecuting, told Monday's hearing that police attempted to get the BMW to stop, but it did not.

Thornton said the car was then driven away from police.

The court heard the vehicle hit the Scirocco on Gelderd Road, resulting in the deaths of Bell and Langton, who were from York.

Two boys, aged 16 and 17, and a 16-year-old girl, who were in the BMW, were also arrested on suspicion of causing death by dangerous driving and had since been released on bail.

The teenagers were taken to hospital for treatment to injuries that were not considered life-threatening.

A West Yorkshire Police spokesperson said specialist family liaison officers were supporting the families of the two people who died.

Anyone with information about the incident, particularly anyone with relevant dashcam, mobile phone or CCTV footage, has been asked to contact officers.

A spokesperson for the Independent Office for Police Conduct said on Sunday it had launched an independent investigation at about 02:30 GMT and sent investigators to the crash scene to "begin gathering evidence".

The spokesperson said inquiries were still "at a very early stage".
