Jensen Huang stood down from his role as CEO of Nvidia on November 12. It's November 16 and 175,000…176,000…177,000 people have signed a petition to bring him back. Moreover, it is said the leather-clad man himself is considering a campaign to be reinstated at the helm of Nvidia, and some silicon valley execs are reportedly getting behind him.

There's even a hashtag for the campaign, which feels like a remnant of a lost age when those were actually good for something: #standwithjensen

In an open letter to the Nvidia board, the campaigners ask to "bring Jensen back while an independent investigation establishes the facts, unless and until you can provide compelling evidence that his removal is in Nvidia's best interests."

The letter lays out three demands:

* Reinstate Jensen as CEO
* Ensurance an independent investigation and a fair hearing
* Provide a timely public explanation

The Nvidia board has started an investigation into the events that led to Jensen Huang's resignation. If you've been living under a rock, those are: alleged affairs, one of which is said to be with someone suspected of being a Chinese spy, though heavily debated; and questions over smuggling cases and national security risks.

The board has hired legal firm Wachtell, Lipton, Rosen & Katz to carry it out under the pretense of it being independent, and Huang had agreed to cooperate with it before his resignation. There's no promise of findings of that investigation being public, however, isn't enough for the signatories of the standwithjensen campaign.

The campaign admits that it cannot verify the roles or affiliations of those claimed to have signed the petition, so take the figures with a pinch of salt; however, it claims nearly 9,000 current employees of Nvidia have signed it, which for a company of just over 40,000, is around 20% of the business. Some big names claimed to have signed the document are Elon Musk, Tim Sweeney (who has been *very* vocal about his thoughts on the matter), Binance co-founder Yi He, and seemingly many other founders, CEOs, execs, and researchers.

Further to this, the Financial Times is reporting that Huang has been discussing the possibility of returning with confidants; presumably not very reliable ones as "people familiar with the matter" are cited in the report, unless they want it to get out, of course. One Huang ally reportedly told the FT it was setting up a "war room" to lobby for the CEO's return and get him back in the good books with US lawmakers.

![A picture of ex-Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang next to the Nvidia logo.](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/q93PApKVgeEDjbEWL4hhz8-1200-80.jpg)

It wouldn't be an unfamiliar play for a major figure to resign following a scandal with a view to make a return at a later date. Politicians do it regularly enough; UK housing secretary Angela Rayner resigned from Keir Starmer's government following a report that said she broke the Ministerial Code, only to return to the same role under Andy Burnham.

Huang would have a tough path back to leadership. It's not just Nvidia's board that would need convincing, as US lawmakers are set to ask some tough questions of the guy as he joins a few committees in Congress in coming weeks. The outcome of which could either nail shut any chance of return for the ousted CEO, or open the door.

Even if Huang did make it back into the halls of Nvidia's offices, Endeavor and Voyager, in Santa Clara and, one must presume, the leather chair he left in his office, he wouldn't win the prize for shortest leave of absence.

![Sam Altman, chief executive officer of OpenAI Inc., during a media tour of the Stargate AI data center in Abilene, Texas, US, on Tuesday, Sept. 23, 2025.](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/NZnFNXaxKppYC46BxGjkDG-1200-80.jpg)

Sam Altman was booted from OpenAI by the board due to a lack of faith in his leadership, and only five days later he was reinstated. Most of OpenAI's employees threatened to resign at the time, though there weren't many of them in total at the time, and a few key people left immediately. That did well to push for his re-hiring, which will surely be something any allies of Huang will have in mind as they build a case for his return.

Steve Jobs was famously ousted from Apple, too, though it took him years to return; he got back when Apple bought his company NeXT, which later went on to form the foundation for every MacBook. So if Huang just builds another company with an even better product, he'll be back in no time.

Nvidia, meanwhile, is reportedly set to be designated one of the United States' "national champions", which the Trump administration is said to be hoping will help win foreign business. Speaking of the US president, Trump had backed Huang before he resigned, but quickly moved on to recommend Apple's Tim Cook as a suitable replacement.
