Men who wear tight briefs face a greater risk of poor sperm count than those wearing classic boxer shorts, a new large-scale study conducted in China has revealed.

The findings provide an easy everyday change to “protect semen quality and sexual function”, according to the study published in the *Asian Journal of Andrology*.

The ideal temperature for sperm production is known to be about two degrees lower than the normal human core body temperature of 37 degrees Celsius.

Scrotal muscles tend to automatically pull the testicles closer to the body when it is cold or let them hang lower when it is warm.

Previous studies have hinted that tight underwear may increase scrotal temperature and affect male reproductive health.

Earlier research suggests compared with looser underwear, tight briefs may increase the temperature inside the scrotum by about 1C.

But large-scale evidence for this claim has remained limited, say researchers from the First Affiliated Hospital of the University of Science and Technology of China in Hefei.

In the latest study, scientists assessed over 26,800 men undergoing fertility evaluation and examined both their semen quality and sexual function.

The participants were divided into two groups according to their self-reported underwear preferences.

One group consisting of 21,221 men preferred wearing loose underwear, while the other group of 5,615 men said they preferred tight briefs.

Researchers found that wearing tight underwear was associated with unfavourable sperm qualities, including reduced semen volume, poorer sperm shape and DNA integrity.

Erectile dysfunction also seemed more prevalent among men wearing tight underwear.

Tight underwear was associated with 18 per cent higher odds of erectile dysfunction and 40 per cent higher odds of premature ejaculation, the study found.

“Tight underwear was associated with poorer semen quality and a higher prevalence of erectile dysfunction and premature ejaculation,” scientists wrote in the study.

The temperature increase inside the scrotum caused by wearing tight briefs could be sufficient to impair the storage of sperm and the final stage of sperm development in the body, the study noted.

“Tighter garments may increase scrotal temperature, which could alter the metabolic and enzymatic processes involved in sperm motility,” scientists say.

However, researchers cautioned that the study only found a correlation and does not establish that tight underwear directly causes these problems.

There was also a higher prevalence of smoking and alcohol consumption among the participants wearing tighter underwear, hinting that these could be the the overall habits of those with poor health behaviour.

Yet, the findings call for further research into the role played by tight underwears on male semen quality and sexual function, scientists say.

This could help establish causality and to clarify the clinical relevance of these findings, researchers say.

“Choosing light underwear may represent a simple, modifiable approach to help protect semen quality and sexual function,” they concluded.
