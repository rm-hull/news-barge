A woman is delighted to have reopened a traditional pub and believes it was a gift from the late Humphrey Smith.

Nichola Oxley-Nero, 46, originally from Buttershaw, officially reopened the Halfway House on Sand Beds last month.

But it may never have happened had it not been for an unexpected and now-treasured telephone conversation with brewery boss Humphrey Smith just weeks before his death.

The pub, owned by the Samuel Smith's brewery, had been closed since 2022.

Established in in 1758 in Tadcaster, the brewery makes a wide range of beers, solely from authentic natural ingredients and own hundreds of pubs across the country.

![inside old pub](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21698250.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Inside the Halfway House in Queensbury *(Image: T&A)*

Having spent a lot of time living in Hong Kong, Ms Oxley-Nero decided to return to her native West Yorkshire.

It was then that she stumbled across the ad for a couple to take over the Halfway House pub in Queensbury and rang the number.

![stone pub](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21698259.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Outside the Halfway House in Queensbury *(Image: T&A)*

She did not expect her call to go straight to Humphrey Smith, the late owner and chairman of the Tadcaster family-run brewery and pub and hotel operator.

![inside old pub](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21698253.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Inside the Halfway House in Queensbury *(Image: T&A)*

After “45 minutes with Humphrey himself”, she considered the offer as a solo landlady rather than as part of a couple.

She said: “I don’t need anyone else.”

## 'Don’t look a gift horse in the mouth'

She says she did not accept the offer immediately.

Humphrey died three weeks after this phone call, and she believes the pub was a gift from him to her.

The brewery called her only after his death.

Ms Oxley-Nero believes Humphrey personally passed her name and number to the brewery.

She says the brewery could only have found her through Humphrey because she never made a formal application.

![woman inside pub](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21698254.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Landlady Nichola Oxley-Nero inside the Halfway House in Queensbury *(Image: T&A)*

She lives by the motto “Don’t look a gift horse in the mouth” and felt like the keys to the Queensbury pub were a gift from Humphrey to herself.

![woman outside stone pub](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21698261.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Landlady Nichola Oxley-Nero outside the Halfway House in Queensbury *(Image: T&A)*

She describes taking on the pub as happening “very organically” and says the phone call was “very lovely”.

She added: “I treasure it.”

When she was handed the large bunch of keys, the landlady says she opened every door first time, as if she instinctively knew which key to use.

![inside old pub](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21698256.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Inside the Halfway House in Queensbury *(Image: T&A)*

## 'Some things are meant to be'

Ms Oxley-Nero says she has worked “in every pub and every hotel in a ten-mile radius” of the area.

She believes hospitality “is in your blood or it’s not” and wants to “create a place like home”.

She added: “Some things are meant to be.”

![inside old pub](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21698255.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Inside the Halfway House in Queensbury *(Image: T&A)*

## She found Humphrey to be 'wonderfully charming'

She told the T&A that she found Humphrey “wonderfully charming” and “very to the point.”

She said: “He was who he was. He listened very well. I will always champion him.”

![inside old pub](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21698257.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Inside the Halfway House in Queensbury *(Image: T&A)*

## But the big question is – what rules apply?

The company’s pubs are known for strict, old-school rules, including bans on mobile phones, background music, televisions and swearing.

Ms Oxley-Nero said: “It’s a quiet place, a home from home.”

Customers can pay by phone, but they must not use phones inside the pub.

She said: “Your phone when you’re paying me isn’t a phone, it’s a credit card, you’re not there doom scrolling.”

She says she is a “real strong advocate” of Samuel Smith’s brewery.

![inside old pub](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21698258.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Inside the Halfway House in Queensbury *(Image: T&A)*

## 'I put my passion into my work'

She also has big plans for the future.

The brewery does not provide soft furnishings such as cushions, so she has spent her own money improving the pub.

She said: “I put my passion into my work, that’s what the pub reflects.”

She plans to transform a “beautiful” area at the back into “the most sought after beer garden within a ten mile radius”.

## 'Without them I have got nothing'

Her final words are a direct message to the people of Queensbury.

Above all, she wants to thank the local community for rallying round the pub opening, which she describes as “phenomenal”.

She said: “Thank you to the village and local community, without them I have got nothing.”

She says Queensbury village “drank me dry in the first week” and compares opening night to “sardines”.

She also thanked her staff: Jade Oxley, Lucy Robinson and Lexi Kerr.
