A Harrogate councillor wants council tax waived for people with under a year to live. Full Council votes on 18 November, but any scheme would wait until 2028.

North Yorkshire councillors will vote on Wednesday on a call to stop charging
council tax to people who are expected to die within a year. Even if they
back it, no scheme would come in before the **2028/29** budget.

The motion was put forward in July by **Councillor Chris Aldred**, who
represents High Harrogate and Kingsley. It was seconded by **Councillor Andrew
Murday** of Pateley Bridge and Nidderdale. It goes to Full Council at County
Hall, Racecourse Lane, Northallerton, at 10.30am on **Wednesday 18 November**
(agenda).

## What the motion asks for

The motion backs a Marie Curie campaign for councils to exempt people with less than 12 months to live. It says 16 other councils had already adopted similar plans in their 2026/27 budgets.

It asked the council to:

* support the Marie Curie campaign
* set out a plan to support terminally ill people in its **2027/28** budget
* base any entitlement on an **SR1 form**, which a clinician completes to confirm a progressive illness and a life expectancy of 12 months or less

## What scrutiny councillors recommend

The motion was sent to the council’s Corporate and Partnerships Overview and
Scrutiny Committee. On 28 September a proposal not to uphold it was voted down.
The committee then voted **12 to 1, with one abstention**, to back the motion
in principle
(committee’s report to Full Council).

But it did not recommend a scheme for 2027/28. Instead, officers would:

* gather North Yorkshire evidence on demand, existing support and how much council tax terminally ill people still pay
* assess the financial, legal and equality effects of the options
* report back with any costed proposal for the **2028/29** budget

That work would run alongside the council’s planned review of its council tax reduction scheme during 2027/28. Mr Aldred told the committee he was happy with that approach, the report says.

## How many people, and the cost

Officers estimated, from Marie Curie figures, that about **924 North Yorkshire
residents** a year might meet the criteria
(officers’ report, 28 September).

A full exemption at the average Band D rate would cost up to about **£2.2
million a year**. Officers called that a “theoretical maximum”. It assumes
everyone eligible applies, everyone lives in a Band D home, nobody already gets
help with their bill, and every award lasts a full year.

## Why officers say North Yorkshire is different

Many councils that have brought in a terminal illness scheme make working-age people on low incomes pay part of their bill whatever happens. Liverpool asks for 16% and Manchester for 15%, the officers’ report says. Their schemes fill that gap.

North Yorkshire’s council tax reduction scheme can already give **up to 100%**
off for people who qualify. The report says that means many terminally ill
residents “may already receive full Council Tax support”. It also warns that an
automatic exemption could mean fewer people are put in touch with the council’s
wider money advice.

The report adds that some councils with generous schemes have still adopted a clear terminal illness policy. North Yorkshire does not have one.

## What it means for you

If you or someone you care for is terminally ill, you do not have to wait for this vote. The council already has three ways to cut a bill:

* **Council tax reduction** for people on low incomes, worth up to 100% of the bill. The bands and the rules are on our Harrogate council tax page.
* **The Exceptional Hardship Scheme**, for households in severe financial difficulty.
* **A discretionary reduction** under section 13A of the Local Government Finance Act 1992, for exceptional circumstances.

The council’s financial support team also helps terminally ill people claim benefits such as Personal Independence Payment and Attendance Allowance under the fast-track Special Rules, the report says.

Harrogate and Knaresborough MP Tom Gordon raised a linked worry last week: that terminally ill people on council tax relief could miss the £100 energy payment.
