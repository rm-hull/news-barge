A Virginia couple was arrested after throwing a massive party for their teenage child involving hundreds of teens drinking underage and setting off fireworks at their home, police said.

Officers in Fairfax County, Virginia, were called to a home around 9 p.m. on November 6 after receiving multiple 911 calls reporting an out-of-control party, the Fairfax Police Department said in a press release.

When officers arrived, they encountered a massive crowd and fireworks being discharged. Callers told police they believed between 100 and 300 people were involved in the rowdy gathering.

Police said Lisa Vaughan, 45, and Aubrey Vaughan, 55, had thrown the party for their teenage child.

A video posted to the Fairfax Police Department’s Facebook page showed Lisa Vaughan telling officers they were “making sure” there was no alcohol at the gathering — to which the officer replied, “Are you joking?”

![A set of Virginia parents were arrested after throwing a rowdy party for their teenage child, police say](https://static.the-independent.com/2026/11/16/19/06/Virginia-parents-arrested-party.png?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“You don’t smell it in the air right now?” the officer later asks.

Another officer adds, “It reeks like liquor. We got drunk kids walking everywhere.”

The clip, taken from police body cameras, shows the officers entering the party as fireworks go off. Officers can then be seen yelling at rowdy teens to go home.

Following an investigation, both Lisa and Aubrey Vaughan were charged with contributing to the delinquency of a minor and maintaining a common nuisance.

Both turned themselves in at the Fairfax County Adult Detention Center, where they were each released on $1,000 unsecured bond.

“This incident serves as a reminder of the importance of safety and supervision when hosting gatherings involving minors,” the Fairfax Police Department said in a press release.

“While these events are often intended to be fun and enjoyable, circumstances can change quickly. We encourage parents and homeowners to remain mindful of activities taking place on their property, take steps to prevent underage drinking, and contact police if concerns arise or a situation begins to escalate.”
