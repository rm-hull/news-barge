# Yorkshire Post clock tower faces demolition under plans for new offices - as clean-up wait continues

## The former Yorkshire Post and Yorkshire Evening Post clock tower which has fallen into a state of disrepair is set to be demolished as part of a new office development, Leeds City Council has confirmed.

Planning permission is currently being sought to build two new office buildings on the land in Leeds where the clock tower currently stands.

It comes as a pledge made six months ago by developers to clean up the existing structure is still yet to be delivered with no sign of any improvement to the famous structure.

The clock tower was retained when the rest of the former newspaper offices were knocked down in 2014.

![The Yorkshire Post and Yorkshire Evening Post clock tower in April 2026. Picture: Gerard Binks](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOjVmOTRlNDZiLWRmN2UtNDI1My05NTVjLTFhZWYyYzYwYmZiMDo2OTJlMWFjNS00YTFkLTQ3NjQtOWU5Yy00MjVkYzEwMDhiZmE=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


While some of the wider office site has since been redeveloped to include an apartment building called The Headline, other plans have yet to come to fruition.

Developer Urbanite, which is backed by private equity firm Prescient, initially got planning permission to build three multi-storey buildings, one office block and two student flats, in September 2023.

The £400m scheme was approved on the basis that the existing clock tower structure would be demolished but the top section with the newspaper branding would be “relocated and reimagined within the proposed public realm as a new public art feature”. It was suggested it could be added onto a newly-created wind mitigation column on the site.

But the wider scheme never came to fruition so the land has stood empty for years while the condition of the clock tower head has substantially worsened.

![The clock tower has been recognised as an ‘important heritage feature for the city of Leeds’. Picture: Gerard Binks](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOjEwMTE1OGFiLTIzMDItNGQ4Yy05YWFhLTczZWRiZmJhOTBjNTo2NzY1NjhhZC1iOWY3LTQ2ZjktOWUwOC0yNTQyMzdhYzhkYTQ=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


It led in April to a joint campaign by The Yorkshire Post and sister title The Yorkshire Evening Post calling for a clean-up operation. Doug Friend, director of Prescient Capital, said in the same month: “We will look to undertake a cleaning exercise shortly."

But no such work appears to have been carried out yet, while in September a new planning application was submitted by Urbanite for the construction of two office buildings.

When the application was submitted, it was reported that the clock tower would be “re-imagined within the development in an appropriate way” following work with the council.

The council has now confirmed it is intended that the existing structure would be knocked down, while details contained within the hundreds of pages of documents submitted with the planning application suggest a digital clock display could be added to one of the new office buildings to “reinterpret” the existing structure.

![An impression of what the planned digital clock display in the proposed new development.](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOjJmMTcyZjY5LWY2MTQtNDA2Yi1iZGUxLTAxMWE4ZGJhMzYyNjo3YTNhMmQ2ZC05NWU2LTQyZDAtOGQyYi1mMTU5NmY1NWViYTI=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


The application says: “One option under consideration is the use of selected areas of the embossed façade as a digital display, with the selective illumination of individual apertures potentially enabling the façade to present the time and ambient temperature.

“Subject to further design development, technical assessment and detailed consideration, this approach could reinterpret the historic function of the Yorkshire Post Clock and Temperature Gauge within the architecture itself. It would offer a means of embedding a familiar and recognisable aspect of the site’s identity into the new building, while celebrating its collective memory and creating a distinctive connection between the development and its historic legacy.”

A council spokesperson said: “A planning application for the former Yorkshire Post site is currently under consideration. As with the previous approved scheme the application proposes the removal of the existing clock tower, which is understood to be in poor condition, while retaining the clock and temperature gauge as part of the redevelopment.

“We recognise the clock tower's heritage significance and its importance to many people in the city, while also acknowledge its current poor condition. We also understand the applicant is considering options to improve the appearance of the tower in the meantime.”

Prescient did not respond to repeated requests for comment.

A decision on the planning application is scheduled for next month.
