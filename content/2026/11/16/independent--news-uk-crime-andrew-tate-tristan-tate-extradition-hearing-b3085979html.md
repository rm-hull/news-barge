Andrew and Tristan Tate will have their final extradition hearings in the New Year after US proceedings due this week were cancelled.

The brothers were given extra time to file their opposition to removal to the UK, where they are charged with rape and sex trafficking, and the hearings set for Thursday and Friday were pulled as a result.

New dates have now been proposed by both sides, with Andrew Tate's hearing to be held on January 14 and Tristan's on January 15.

The court in Miami had earlier refused a request by the pair to have their cases heard together.

Scratching the original scheduled dates, magistrate judge Lauren Louis said: “The parties were previously advised that any extension of the briefing and hearing schedule adopted by the court would result in the final extradition hearing being continued.”

The Tates are meanwhile seeking to challenge their continued detention during the extradition process. Their petition to the court aimed at securing their release is due to be heard at the end of this month, according to their lawyer.

The former professional kickboxers, who hold dual US and British citizenship, have been held in custody since their arrest in July. They were detained after further charges were brought against them by UK prosecutors.

In already published legal documents setting out the prosecution case against the men, UK investigators allege the pair groomed vulnerable women using a “lover boy” model of exploitation before forcing them into creating online pornography through threats and violence.

Andrew Tate, 39, faces a series of charges in the UK, including 10 counts of rape, three counts of arranging or facilitating trafficking for sexual exploitation and three counts of assault occasioning actual bodily harm.

He is also accused of making and distributing indecent images of a child and possessing extreme pornography.

Tristan Tate, 38, is charged with five counts of rape, six counts of assault occasioning actual bodily harm, three counts of sex trafficking and a sexual assault.

The charges relate to alleged offences between July 2010 and August 2017.

Both men deny any wrongdoing.
