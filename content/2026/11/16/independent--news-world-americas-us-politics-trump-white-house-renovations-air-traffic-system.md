President Donald Trump unloaded on reporters who questioned why his administration is pumping tens of millions of dollars into White House renovations and other Washington, D.C. projects while demanding billions from Congress to repair the nation’s air traffic control systems.

It was a chaotic weekend at U.S. airports after a nationwide ground stop of all planes, which Trump blamed Monday on old technology as well as his Democratic predecessor Joe Biden.

He called NBC News correspondent Yamiche Alcindor “pathetic” and suggested she lacked “talent” after she questioned his spending priorities, and defended his “flawless” White House renovations that he said cost “peanuts.”

“You’re so pathetic. I have a responsibility to make sure the symbol of our country is in decent shape. It takes 10 minutes a day,” Trump said. “This doesn’t cost money. This is peanuts. And I pay for it. This doesn’t cost money. This is peanuts. This is called talent. Talent. You don’t have talent. You don’t know what a broken column is. … I spent very little time to do it and losers like you come out and say, ‘Why are you focused on the White House?’”

Trump has overseen a dramatic transformation of the nation’s capital with a multi-billion dollar package of gilded renovations and new construction catering to his tastes, including the demolition of the East Wing of the White House to make way for a massive ballroom that could cost as much as $600 million.

![Donald Trump called a reporter ‘pathetic’ and said she lacked ‘talent’ over questions about his White House renovation projects while he pushes for billions in taxpayer funding for air traffic control upgrades](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/16/15/2304927053..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

A 90,000-square-foot, heavily fortified and militarized ballroom will include “heavy steel, drone-proof roof, missile-resistant and drone-proof columns, bullet, ballistic, and blast-proof glass, Military-grade venting for air conditioning and heating” as well as “bomb shelters, a state-of-the-art hospital and medical facilities, Top Secret military installations, structures, and equipment, protective partitioning, and other features,” according to Justice Department court filings.

Costs for the project have exploded from an initial estimated $200 million to as much as $600 million, half of which is likely to be paid with public funds.

Combined with his other renovation projects, the president has spent or plans to spend more than $1.65 billion in taxpayer dollars throughout the capital.

The multi-million dollar construction of a new concrete helipad on the White House lawn was paid for by Lockheed Martin subsidiary Sikorsky Aircraft, according to the president.

“If you take a look at the White House, it’s flawless,” Trump said Monday.

![Travelers wait in line at a ticket counter at John F. Kennedy International Airport on November 14 in New York. Flight cancellations and delays continue into the weekend after an FAA nationwide ground stop Friday caused disruptions at airports across the country](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/16/16/2304620129..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“It was an embarrassment,” he added. “I know great construction people … So the White House is in beautiful shape. Take a look at the helipad. I hope you like it. Listen to me. The helipad has gotten great reviews. And the nicest of all will be the ballroom, which now people are saying it fits like a glove.”

Trump has also used nearly $20 million in public funds to renovate the Lincoln Memorial Reflecting Pool using no-bid contracts. He is also in the middle of lawsuits over plans to rename the Kennedy Center after himself, construct a “triumphal” arch that will loom over Arlington National Cemetery, and close the East Potomac Links public golf course to build “world class” facility in its place.

But the Trump administration is now asking Congress to approve billions of dollars in new funding to support the nation’s air traffic control systems.

The disruption began Friday afternoon around 3:30 p.m. when the Federal Aviation Administration ordered a nationwide ground stop, keeping planes on the ground for more than four hours after noticing glitches in communications.

Trump reportedly told Fox News correspondent Peter Doocy later that day that “old broken stuff” was to blame.

![Trump’s ballroom, seen under construction earlier this month, is estimated to cost up to $600 million, half of which could tap into taxpayer funds](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/12/21/2303244187..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

But he remained largely quiet about the ground stop through the weekend, instead issuing statements on social media about unrelated topics as frustrated passengers were left stranded at airports across the country.

Trump, who has blamed the Biden administration for the disruptions despite maintaining Republican control of Congress and the White House for nearly two years, later said that upgrades to the system should not be done in “drips and drabs.”

The Republican-controlled Congress has already approved $12.5 billion for the Federal Aviation Administration in a massive domestic spending and tax-cut package last year but Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy has argued it’s not enough to address a system in disrepair.

“If Congress approves the desperately needed money for Air Traffic Control, we can get it built quickly and efficiently,” Trump wrote on Truth Social Sunday.

“It will be the BEST SYSTEM anywhere in the World. Much of our current air control system is 50 years old — RIDICULOUS!” he said.
