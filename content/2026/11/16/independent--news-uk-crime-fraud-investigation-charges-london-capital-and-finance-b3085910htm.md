Eight people have been charged following a Serious Fraud Office (SFO) probe into the collapsed investment firm London Capital and Finance plc (LCF).

The SFO said more than 11,200 investors lost an estimated £251 million when the firm folded in 2019.

Six of the defendants face a charge of conspiracy to defraud. They are Michael Andrew Thomson, 53, Simon Hume-Kendall, 73, Elten Barker, 53, Paul Careless, 51, Robert Sedgwick, 78, and John Russell-Murphy, 52.

Helen Hume-Kendall, 64, is accused of concealing criminal property.

![London Capital and Finance collapsed in 2019 (LCF/PA)](https://static.independent.co.uk/2024/05/07/08/cc2bf1eb9d3e8de9b987af393cfbe215Y29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzE1MTUyOTQ2-2.64655689.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

An eighth defendant, whom the SFO would not name, has been charged with conspiracy to defraud and acting in contravention of a director disqualification undertaking. This individual’s legal team is expected to make an application for anonymity at the first magistrates’ court hearing.

LCF was a Tunbridge Wells-based investment company that raised money by selling mini-bonds to retail investors, marketed as a way to earn high returns, before it collapsed seven years ago.

In a statement released on Monday, Emma Isaac, Head of Division for the Serious Fraud Office, said: “Thousands of investors believed backing London Capital & Finance would yield high returns, instead they lost over £250 million. Today’s charges mark a significant step forward in our investigation into the collapse of the firm and ensuring those responsible are held to account.”

All eight defendants are due to appear before Westminster Magistrates’ Court on 7 December.
