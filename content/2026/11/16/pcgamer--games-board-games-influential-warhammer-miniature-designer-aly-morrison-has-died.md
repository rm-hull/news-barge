![Aly Morrison with some of the Warhammer miniatures he designed](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/DcA9wzwXEiGn796fkbEwok-1920-80.jpg)

Miniature designer and artist Aly Morrison has died, as confirmed by Trish Carden Miniatures and Design on Facebook. Carden, Morrison's ex-wife and former partner in Marauder Miniatures, posted that, "Mharaid and I are heartbroken to announce the passing of Aly Morrison, a very much loved father, veteran wargames miniatures designer and friend to many around the tabletop and the world. Aly passed unexpectedly but peacefully this Wednesday, and will be sorely missed."

Aly Morrison began sculpting for Citadel in 1983 as a freelancer before joining the studio full-time in 1986. He and Trish were then made stewards of Games Workshop's imprint Marauder Miniatures in 1988, where they had free reign to create quirky and characterful figures like the mohawked dwarf trollslayers and the giant with a whaler's beard and a booze barrel strapped to his waist that became archetypal parts of Warhammer.

Morrison also crafted the first miniatures for Blood Bowl players and drew the tokens to represent them in the board game's first edition. During his time at Games Workshop every faction from dark elves to space marines eventually benefited from his touch.

He's also well-regarded as a painter, and his reliance on a dark wash with a hint of purple in it jokingly known as "Aly's Brown Liquid" was legendary. He continued working on the recipe over the years, with a recent iteration described on his blog in 2020 if you want to whip some up yourself.

Though Marauder was eventually folded back into Citadel, Morrison continued at Games Workshop until his retirement earlier this year. The company remembered him by saying, "Aly’s kindness and generosity as a mentor have left an indelible mark on the Warhammer Studio. Humble, magnanimous and with an incorrigible wit, to sit beside Aly and hear his wisdom and love of Warhammer miniatures design, was to be in the presence of a master. No matter your level of experience, he always had something insightful to add, to help make the creation 'more Warhammer'."
