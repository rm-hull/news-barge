**The "beauty of human art" has been celebrated at Thought Bubble, with the comic art festival celebrating its 20th anniversary.**

Thousands of people headed to Harrogate for the event over the weekend, with many dressed as their favourite characters in vibrant costumes.

Attendees praised it for shining a spotlight on "unique small artists", with more than 600 creators showcasing their work at the town's convention centre.

With the rising use of artificial intelligence (AI), graphic novelists at the event said celebrating human-made art was "imperative".

Alice Oseman, who created the hugely successful Heartstopper graphic novel series, was a guest on a panel at the convention.

"It's amazing and so unique," she said.

"I've never seen anything like this in the UK before, where it's so focused on independent creators who are just making their art, on their own, at home and they all get to come here and it's just an explosion of art."

Alice Oseman, creator of the graphic novel Heartstopper, described the event as "so special"

Oseman's work has seen a global response, particularly since Heartstopper was picked up by Netflix and turned into a TV series.

"It's so special, because I love what I do and to get to meet people who feel that same feeling about these characters that I've created, to get to have conversations with them and just celebrate this thing that we both enjoy, it's fun," she said.

For Oseman, it is "so important" for human-made stories to be celebrated.

"It's a scary time to be in as a creative, and AI is a threat to all creative industries," she said.

"So, to get to be around people who are united in the beauty of human art is amazing."

About 10,000 people visited Harrogate Convention Centre over the weekend

Founded in Leeds in 2007, the event is one of the longest-running conventions and comic art events in the country.

Friends Emily Winpenny, Helen Walmsley and Victoria Marshall arrived at the convention dressed as characters from Critical Role, a Dungeons & Dragons-themed web series.

"We have been coming to this since it was in the Royal Armouries in Leeds, so it must be 10 years," Winpenny said.

"It's really nice that it's so independent, there's so many unique small artists.

"They get people from around the world, which is just unheard of because a lot of the more commercial events have the same 50 people."

Walmsley added it was "partly escapism" that brought her back to the festival each year.

"It's a good way to meet people who are like-minded," she said.

"I'm sure all three of us were quite nerdy kids growing up, so it's a good way to find your tribe."

Writer Mohsen Ashraf says it is "imperative" for art to be made by humans

Writer and mythology enthusiast Mohsen Ashraf, who created the Syphon comic series, had a booth at the event for the first time this year.

Compared to the large comic-con events in the United States, he said the North Yorkshire event had a "cosier feel" and was more about the art itself.

"I think what's nice about this convention is people will just come and chat, tell you about the stories they like and you get a little more personal engagement with people," he said.

"When you write a story, it's not about you anymore, it's what you give to the world.

"To see that really come to life with other people is really special."

Ashraf, who also created the Pantheon graphic novel series, said it was "so imperative" to celebrate human-made art.

"Art is an expression of the human spirit," he said.

"I think AI is a tool that can help with some automation, all the basics, but it can't create something new.

"I think that's only something that comes from people.

To be able to have that stay alive and people continue to celebrate that is powerful."
