A Harrogate school with Downton Abbey links has celebrated its 150th anniversary with one of the biggest aerial photographs ever seen in the town.

The picture from the sky saw pupils at Ashville spelled out the number “150” within the school grounds with its landmark clock tower as a backdrop.

Rhiannon Wilkinson, the eleventh and first female Head of Ashville, said she was proud of the heritage of the school, which has been ranked as one of the top 10 independent schools in the North by *The Sunday Times* Parent Power Guide 2026.

“As we look back on 150 years of rich history and educational excellence, we are reminded of the incredible legacy we carry forward.

“This anniversary year provides a wonderful opportunity to reflect on our historic successes, celebrate our vibrant present, and look ahead with immense confidence as we continue to inspire, stretch imaginations, and shape the leaders of tomorrow.”

Ashville’s roots go all the way back to the Victorian era while its former pupils include famous figures from the world of arts and sport.

The first headmaster, Dr William Richardson, was appointed on July 17, 1877, when Ashville was a boarding school for the sons of Methodist Ministers.

There were 30 boys on the roll when the school first opened, two school masters and Dr Richardson.

Given the itinerant nature of ministry, Ashville gave these boys stability of educational provision.

Among the former pupils who have passed through the school since then are Oscar-winning film and stage director Tony Richardson, acclaimed actor Jim Carter OBE, best known for playing Mr Carson in *Downton Abbey, and* Sally Bolton OBE, the former CEO of Wimbledon Lawn Tennis Association.

Rhiannon Wilkinson said: “As Harrogate’s top-performing school and ranked as one of the top 10 independent schools in the North by *The Sunday Times* Parent Power Guide 2026, Ashville enters its 150th year from a position of strength.

“This is underpinned by progressing the next stages in our multi-million-pound masterplan to modernise and reimagine our 70-acre campus in an unprecedented way.”

To herald the start of the year-long programme of celebrations, pupils were presented with a commemorative pin badge funded through the New Friends of Ashville parents’ group.

A celebratory book bringing to life many of the major moments in Ashville’s history is in production, while Ripon Cathedral is set to host a special 150th Anniversary Service next summer.
