Songwriting great Chris Difford has told the Harrogate Advertiser that he considers Harrogate as almost his second home as Squeeze's high profile tour swings into Yorkshire this week.

As the hit-making band prepare to headline Leeds Arena on Friday, November 20, the double Ivor Novello Award-winning songwriter and musician talked about his fondness for the town despite being a Londoner from birth.

Difford says he enjoyed his time at Bettys’ Imperial Room in Harrogate earlier this year in the company of singer Richard Hawley as part of an exclusive celebration of rock n roll legend Buddy Holly.

Prior to that in 2017 and 2018, Chris performed solo shows regularly in Harrogate at the sadly now gone fantastic Warehouse venue around the time of the publication of his autobiography, Some Fantastic Place.

But the man responsible with Squeeze’s co-founder Glenn Tilbrook for timeless songs such as Tempted, Up the Junction, Cool for Cats and Another Nail in My Heart says he is looking forward to the band’s arena gigs with a little more trepidation than those intimate Harrogate dates.

“I’m not nervous about playing but the idea of Squeeze appearing on big arenas on this tour does feel different,” said Chris.

“We are doing some smaller venture like Newcastle City Hall, which I remember playing with Squeeze in the early days.

“But it’s not like playing in Harrogate which seems to have become my second home in recent years.”

With the sub-title of 53 Years of Classic Songwriting, the Harrogate Advertiser suggests that there is no need for messrs Difford and Tilbrook to worry, not when they’ve got songs of the quality of Black Coffee in Bed, Take Me I’m Yours and Labelled With Love up their sleeve.

Squeeze’s latest tour will travel the UK into December with plenty of greatest hits on show.

But also featured will be tracks from Squeeze’s most recent album Trixies, which hit the top 20 earlier this year and was widely acclaimed as one of their best.

Incredibly, the songs on this impressive album were originally written by Difford and Tilbrook in 1974 aged, respectively 19 and 16, but never released.

Was Chris surprised how good the songs were when Squeeze went back to the Trixies demos after all these years, the Advertiser asks.

“No,” replied Chris, now a youthful-looking, well groomed 72-year-old.

“We knew they were good songs at the time but we also knew the early version of Squeeze wasn’t ready to play them live.

“Paul McCartney has done that sort of thing, too, with When I’m Sixty Four on Sgt Pepper, which he wrote when he was 14.

“I think when you’re young writing songs can be easier because your mind is clearer as a young person.”

The Harrogate Advertiser mentions it had seen the young Squeeze in 1978 at Grangemouth Town Hall supporting Simple Minds at a time of punk and high japes.

Chris had seemed a bit tough looking in his leather jacket.

“I suppose I could be a bit tough back then,” said Chris.

“But we all loved having fun then and we put a lot of that into our songwriting, too.

“I suppose it came partly from watching and loving Morecambe and Wise.

“My dad had a great sense of humour which did rub off on me.”
