Donald Trump has expressed support for the return of Jensen Huang as the chief executive of Nvidia, joining Silicon Valley heavyweights in backing the reinstatement of the AI figurehead.

The US president referred to a tech industry campaign for Huang’s rehabilitation, saying a “big group” of people would like him back.

“I think he learned a big lesson. I guess maybe one lesson is more important than ⁠another, but I would certainly not mind seeing that,” Trump told ​reporters as he left the ‌White House on ‌a trip to Asia. “He could return. There’s a big group of ‌people that would like him to return.”

The comments came as the US government announced a crackdown on companies such as Nvidia selling AI chips and servers to overseas datacentres that in turn are supplying computing power to China and other US adversaries.

Elon Musk, the chief executive of Tesla and SpaceX, and a host of other tech bosses have signed an open letter calling for the reinstatement of Huang. The former Nvidia CEO resigned last Thursday amid reports of extramarital liaisons, including with an alleged Chinese spy, Alice Huang, who is no relation. Days before, Huang had admitted being unfaithful to his wife without specifying who was involved.

Trump said on Monday: “Nobody’s proven that she’s a spy. She may not be a spy.” Trump has faced questions after it was reported that a US intelligence report about Huang’s relationship with the alleged spy was circulated among senior figures in his administration.

The reinstatement letter has more than 165,000 signatories and demands that the company’s board members “bring Jensen back while an independent investigation establishes the facts”.

Huang’s resignation ended a 33-year run at the helm of the AI company he co-founded.

According to the Financial Times, Huang has discussed with people close to him a possible return to the company. One Huang ally told the paper they were putting together ⁠a “war room” of Silicon Valley executives to lobby ​for his return ‌and would try to garner support from ‌the White House. The FT also reported on Monday that Nvidia’s board was not considering reinstating Huang.

One analyst downplayed the prospect of a Huang return, describing the notion of the ex-CEO’s reinstatement as “ridiculous”. Dan Ives, an analyst at Yorkville Ives, said the range of issues facing Huang and Nvidia, from personal conduct to questions over China, made an external candidate or the permanent appointment of the interim CEO – Colette Kress, Nvidia’s chief financial officer – the best option.

“We see no chance Jensen comes back to Nvidia as CEO, and for the company the best path forward is with a new external CEO or Colette,” he said, describing the petition as “noise and distraction”.

The new rule announced by the US government on Monday requires companies in Singapore, Thailand, Malaysia, Indonesia and Brazil to apply for US export licences for the most advanced chips in an effort to cut down on ⁠them being illegally diverted.

“The rule ‌is designed to ​ensure that ​existing restrictions on access to sensitive American technology cannot be circumvented through remote computing arrangements involving datacentres in third countries,” the US Department of Commerce said.

Alice Huang, the alleged Chinese spy, worked as an executive at the Singapore-based Megaspeed, an Nvidia customer. According to reports, Megaspeed is under suspicion of diverting powerful Nvidia chips to China in contravention of US export laws.

Megaspeed said on Sunday it was a “legitimate and lawful business” and that Huang had left the business in late 2025. It added that it complied “with all applicable laws and regulatory regimes, including United States export control requirements and local jurisdiction rules in every market where we operate”.

Nvidia shares opened down 2.7% on Monday, knocking about $100bn (£75.8bn) off Nvidia’s market capitalisation, lowering it to $3.82tn compared with $4.5tn before the scandal broke.
