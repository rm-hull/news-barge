## 'Heartbeat' village could lose access to 999 calls

Goathland in Yorkshire where Heartbeat was filmed could lose access to emergency calls.

RESIDENTS in the village made famous by TV drama Heartbeat could be left unable to contact emergency services during power cuts because a local mobile phone mast has no battery backup, MPs have heard.

Alison Hume, Labour MP for Scarborough and Whitby, raised concerns in the House of Commons about mobile phone resilience in Goathland, the village made famous as Aidensfield in the long-running ITV drama.

The MP said residents could be unable to make 999 calls during outages because the village’s mobile phone mast loses service when the power goes off.

Raising the issue in Parliament, Ms Hume said: “The TV drama ‘Heartbeat’, featuring a policeman based in 1960s ‘Aidensfield’ delighted millions of viewers, so it is slightly ironic that, in 2026, residents in the real village, Goathland, cannot make emergency calls during power cuts, as their mobile phone mast has no battery.”

The MP told ministers that EE had informed her it had no plans to install a backup battery at the mast and asked for government support in securing a meeting with the company.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/20997603.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Alison Hume Portrait. Hoc

She said: “EE has told me that it has no plans to install one.”

Responding on behalf of the Government, Alan Campbell, Leader of the House of Commons, agreed that access to emergency services was critical for rural communities.

He said: “My hon. friend raises a really important matter, because access to emergency services is a vital lifeline for our communities.”

Mr Campbell noted that communications providers have statutory obligations to maintain their networks and said the Department for Digital, Culture, Media and Sport works with industry and Ofcom to assess lessons from major outages.

He added that ministers would have heard the concerns raised by the Scarborough and Whitby MP and pointed to a forthcoming parliamentary debate on rural connectivity.

Julian Smith, the MP for Skipton and Ripon, recently raised similar concerns and urged the Government to tackle remaining mobile phone “not-spots” across North Yorkshire and other rural parts of the UK.

Around 85 per cent of North Yorkshire is classed as rural or super sparse, and contains two national parks, the Yorkshire Dales and the North York Moors.

The comments come amid continuing concerns over mobile phone coverage and network resilience in England’s largest county, where residents can be more reliant on mobile services during severe weather and power disruptions.
