The price of gold has fallen to its lowest level in just over a year, having hit new record highs at the start of 2026.

It touched as low as $3,833 (£2,899) on Monday morning, meaning a drop of almost a third (32 per cent) since its high point of $5,634 (£4,262) set back in January.

A massive bull run saw the price of gold breach the $3,000 milestone for the first time as recently as March 2025, before surpassing the $4,000 mark only seven months later and then $5,000 in January of this year.

By mid-March, however, a sell-off had started, coinciding with the Iran war which has had such a devastating inflationary effect on much of the world across 2026.

When inflation heads higher, central banks tend to raise interest rates to help control it.

In turn, this makes assets which do not pay out a return – like gold – less appealing when compared to cash, which can earn a better return via higher rates at no risk.

While gold has traditionally been the market that investors head to when uncertainty reigns in stocks or other risky equities, the additional factor at play is uncertainty over the eventual landing point following the strong surge over the past two years, says Axel Rudolph, chief technical analyst at IG.

“Gold is trading at a one-year low as investors unwind some of the defensive positioning that had pushed the precious metal to record highs earlier in the year. A stronger US dollar, elevated Treasury yields and reduced expectations for near-term Federal Reserve rate cuts are all weighing on gold,” he added.

“The easing of geopolitical tensions is also taking some of the shine off gold’s traditional safe-haven appeal, while the broader rotation away from defensive assets is adding to the selling pressure. After such a powerful rally, the latest decline also looks increasingly like a broader correction rather than simply a short-term pullback.”

Gold is not the only asset facing a plunge, however.

The price of Bitcoin has fallen by a very similar amount, down 31.7 per cent over the past one year and down 26 per cent since the start of 2026.
