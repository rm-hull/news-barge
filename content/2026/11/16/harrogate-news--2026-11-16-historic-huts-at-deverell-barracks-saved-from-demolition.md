**Two historic wooden huts at Deverell Barracks in Ripon are to be saved from demolition, following a three-year campaign by Ripon Military Heritage Trust.**

Homes England, which owns the site, has agreed to dismantle two of the buildings and give them to the Trust, which plans to relocate, restore and repurpose them as part of a project to tell Ripon’s military history.

The huts are part of what is described as the last surviving intact 1939 wooden-hutted Militia Camp in the country. Demolition work at Deverell Barracks is due to begin early in the New Year.

The two buildings selected for preservation are the Lecture Hut and one of the two Instruction Huts. They are the only known examples of their type and will therefore be unique survivors.

During the Second World War, the huts were used to train British and Allied army engineers at Ripon’s School of Military Engineering.

Homes England has also allowed the Trust to select and retain movable items from the barracks, including beds, cupboards, a cell door and an air raid siren. The items will help with the future interpretation of the site.

## Four Cold War huts also to be retained

The Trust has also been given the opportunity to dismantle and retain four metal buildings known as Twynham huts before demolition begins.

The Cold War-era buildings replaced the Nissen hut and are closely associated with the Corps of Royal Engineers. Alongside the 1939 wooden huts, they will form part of a lasting record of the connection between the Royal Engineers and Ripon.

## Ripon’s military history

Deverell Barracks was built in the summer of 1939 as a Militia Camp for Britain’s first peacetime conscripts.

Research by national expert Paul Francis of the Airfield Research Group indicates that it is the largest and best-preserved militia camp remaining in the country. It contains the greatest number of surviving huts and the widest range of hut types, including accommodation, mess, ancillary and office buildings.

Historic England has described the site as an “exceptional survival”.

In October 1940, as bombs fell across southern England, the Royal Engineers School of Military Engineering was evacuated to Ripon. Engineers practised with the newly developed Bailey bridge in the surrounding area and on the nearby rivers Ure and Laver.

After the United States entered the Second World War in December 1941, the school became an important training base for US Army engineers. An American Section was established to train personnel, including in bomb disposal.

Following training in Ripon, an initial group returned to the United States and established the US Army Engineer Bomb Disposal School at Aberdeen Proving Ground in Maryland, based on the Ripon model.

## Plans for the future

The huts will be carefully dismantled and stored securely while discussions continue to identify a permanent site for their reconstruction.

Ripon Military Heritage Trust is working with specialist conservation architects to develop plans to repurpose the buildings for educational, interpretive and community use.

The Trust was founded in 2023 as a charitable incorporated organisation. It is run by volunteer trustees and works to research, conserve, interpret and provide access to the military heritage of Ripon and the surrounding area.

Deverell Barracks was acquired by Homes England from the Ministry of Defence in 2025. The site has planning permission, alongside Claro Barracks and Laver Banks Training Area, for a development providing 1,300 homes, shops and offices, a school and a country park.
