## York poetry event celebrates disabled talent with the spoken word

London-based poet, artist and musician Carys Hannah *(Image: Supplied)*

A poetry showcase is set to return, putting disabled voices centre stage through the spoken word.

Poetry For All will take place at the National Centre for Early Music in York on Friday, November 27, celebrating the talent of disabled poets and performers through live readings.

The event has been running since 2018.

Rose Drew of Stairwell Books said: "We literally couldn’t make it more accessible.

"We have BSL signers for Deaf attendees and excellent acoustics for people with sight loss, to pick up every nuance.

"We also screen the poems as they are read aloud.

"Access to the auditorium is second to none, and if you can’t reach us in person, you can watch and listen on livestream."

This year's headliners include London-based poet, artist and musician Carys Hannah, and Staffordshire’s Ashley Edge, a non-binary, disabled poet, editor and publisher.

The event will be hosted by Ms Drew and poet, storyteller and Fringe host Fay Roberts.

Additional performers include Laurence O’Reilly, Mal Thompson, Fiona Stratton and Alexander Woodward.

DK Poet, founder of Disability Arts, said: "There is something magical about experiencing poetry live – the energy, the emotion, the connection."

Tickets are available from the NCEM website and cost £8 or £5 for concessions.
