Treasured images showcasing well-known landmarks in North Yorkshire towns through the decades have been chosen to feature in calendars now on sale.

Library staff in Harrogate and Knaresborough have worked with the archives team to choose a selection of images.

A third calendar featuring images of Selby has proved so popular that it has already sold out.

For the past two years, the libraries have produced calendars, with money raised from the sales being reinvested to support the service.

Last year, the calendars quickly sold out at both Harrogate and Knaresborough libraries, raising £750.

The Harrogate calendar features images of well-known buildings including the Town Hall, which is now the Mercer Gallery, and the interior of the former Winter Gardens. There are also images of shopfronts and a roller-skating rink in the Royal Hall Gardens.

The Knaresborough edition includes views spanning town centre streets, including the Market Place and main High Street. There are also shots of the castle, once a stronghold of medieval kings, and the River Nidd.

Executive member for culture and arts, Cllr Simon Myers, whose responsibilities include libraries, said: “There is something special about seeing familiar landmarks from years gone by, giving us a chance to reflect on how our towns have changed over time.

“This is the third year of producing calendars in these libraries, which have proved incredibly popular and are sure to spark conversations about local history.

“Whether purchased as a gift or for yourself, every calendar sold helps support our libraries to provide the essential services we all rely on.”

The calendars are on sale at Knaresborough and Harrogate libraries for £6 and are available while stocks last.

All available copies of the Selby edition have sold out, which includes images of The Crescent, Brook Street and Finkle Street. There is also a photograph of Cochrane’s shipyard in 1929, a shipbuilding and repair facility along the River Ouse.
