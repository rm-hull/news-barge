## Olivio Gomes' family called the verdict historic as figures show ethnic minorities are four times more likely to be stopped by police in France.

A French appeals court has jailed former police officer Gilles Guilbert for eight years for murdering Olivio Gomes, a 28-year-old black man he shot dead at the wheel of his car in 2020. That is two years less than the sentence he was given at his first trial.

The court in the Hauts-de-Seine department, west of Paris, France, ruled late on Wednesday that Guilbert, 35, had not acted in self-defence when he opened fire at close range during a night-time stop on 17 October 2020.

Guilbert was also banned for life from serving as a police officer and may not carry a firearm for 15 years.

Guilbert and two colleagues had followed Gomes's car for about 20 minutes, to outside his home in Poissy, a town in the Paris region. The defence said the officers tailed him because he was speeding, but security camera footage contradicted that account.

When Gomes stopped, Guilbert got out of the police car and aimed his gun at him. As Gomes started the engine to drive away, the officer fired three times, killing the father of three.

A court in Versailles had sentenced Guilbert to 10 years in prison in March. His lawyer appealed straight away.

"It's a relief. All these years of struggle, of fighting, there was a lot of doubt. We can finally breathe, mourn our loss," Leonel Gomes, the victim's younger brother, told AFP.

Damien Vinet, one of the family's lawyers, described the ruling as "historic".

Another high-profile case is due before the courts: a police officer will stand trial over the death of 17-year-old Nahel Merzouk, who was shot at point-blank range in Nanterre, near Paris, in June 2023.

Rights groups and campaigners accuse French police of systemic racism and violence. The government and police unions say abuses are isolated.

A report published last year by France’s Defender of Rights found that young men "perceived as Arab, black or from North Africa" are four times more likely to be stopped by police than the rest of the population.
