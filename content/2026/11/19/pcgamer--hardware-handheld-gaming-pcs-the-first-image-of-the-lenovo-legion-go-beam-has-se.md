With the pricing of memory right now, it's hard to get too excited for gaming handhelds, but Lenovo's reportedly more budget-friendly Legion Go Beam does intrigue me. And, with a recent leak, I'm now looking forward to hearing about the full details in the new year.

This leak comes courtesy of Windows Latest, which managed to get its hands on three images, plus the name, and a suggestion that it will be less expensive than the Legion Go 2. Admittedly, the Legion Go 2 and its OLED screen are climbing up to $2,000 right now, so undercutting that isn't super tough.

The Go Beam has a white shell with lime green accents at the bottom, on the face buttons, around the thumbsticks, and on the start and menu buttons. It's a striking theme, even if I fear grubby fingers will make that white far grimmer in a matter of months.

There is, unfortunately, a lot we don't know about this handheld. We don't know the screen's panel technology; we don't know what size and weight it is; we don't know what processor powers it; we don't have a price point, and we don't yet have a release date.

Windows Latest is guessing it will be cheaper than the Legion Go based on its feature set, which is not a bad call.

![Lenovo Legion Go S](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/iJw46uqGYvL8BTWnxfGDge-1200-80.jpg)

You get most of the controls you expect from a modern handheld, with triggers and bumpers, as well as two paddles on the back. There's a single USB Type-C port on the top of the device, and it has a sequence of eight fan holes in the back left.

It is, however, missing the small touchpad under the right thumbstick that comes with the Lenovo Legion Go S (both Windows and SteamOS models). That means navigating the desktop UI could be a little less easy, though at launch it wasn't usable in SteamOS anyways. It also has bigger bezels than the Go 2 and Go S.

Lenovo has been very fond of AMD with its gaming handhelds thus far, using Ryzen Z2, Z2 Extreme and Ryzen Z2 Go chips, but there is a suggestion the Beam could be kitted with a lower-end Panther Lake chip (perhaps the Intel Arc G3 and its Arc B370 iGPU). I haven't spent much time with the G3, but I have been very impressed with the G3 Extreme, from both a performance and battery life perspective.

With Lenovo attending CES at the start of 2027, Windows Latest reckons that is when we will see more about this handheld. But it does note that Lenovo doesn't mention gaming for the January show, so it could be holding out for later in 2027, perhaps Computex in June.

Either way, if Lenovo is putting out cheaper handhelds, that's certainly not bad news for us PC gamers, even if we're still going to be paying an arm and a leg for the memory and storage inside it.
