Flaming torches, blaring trumpets and wooden casks rolled down cobbles or carted on tractors set **Lyon’s thoroughfares abuzz** as costumed revellers led the city's annual wine parade. Drawing dense crowds, the festivities peaked with the**theatrical uncorking of the season’s first bottles** on stage, sparking music, dancing and cheers as the inaugural glasses were handed out.

Hailing from the hills just north of **Lyon**,** Beaujolais Nouveau**hits the shelves just weeks after the grapes are picked, sustaining a quintessential French autumn ritual. Celebrated globally on the** third Thursday of November**, the event heralds the exact stroke at which the new vintage may legally be sold — a light, fruit-forward wine meant for immediate enjoyment rather than long ageing.

What began as a local harvest marker is now a lively tribute to French winemaking heritage, bringing producers and townspeople shoulder to shoulder. Through its torchlit march and **midnight pouring**,** Lyon** once again turned the launch into an exuberant street festival.
