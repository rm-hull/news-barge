Prince Harry is urging Europe and other allies to ramp up their support for Ukraine and bring an end to the war.

In an interview during his visit to Lviv, Harry said it was time to “double our support” as he subtly criticised Donald Trump’s administration over their stance on the conflict.

The Duke of Sussex condemned Russia’s “appalling” attacks on civilian infrastructure and humanitarian aid centres, and said the rest of the world “could and should” do more to help Ukraine.

He also told *The Telegraph* that he would rejoin the British Army to fight Vladimir Putin’s forces if it was required.

The duke said: “I am just incredibly impressed with how Ukraine has held off and pushed back the Russian forces, and now this surely needs to come to an end.

“When we talk about Ukraine being able to live with war, that is quite extraordinary what they’ve managed to achieve.

“But that shouldn’t mean that the rest of us are so impressed by their resilience that we back off with our support. Now we have to double our support in order to put an end to this. We can’t just wait for this to run its course. There has to be more that we can do to bring this to an end.”

![The Duke of Sussex visited Ukraine to attend trials for the Invictus Games](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/19/09/01M7Q9KWWNA7M2P27TERQQYFXH.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

He said recent attacks on civilian infrastructure were a sign of Russia’s desperation and called for other countries to respond.

“International law must mean something. I think the rest of the world could and should do more in that regard,” he said.

The duke, who has served two operational tours in Afghanistan as an Apache helicopter pilot with the British Army, said Britain could learn a lot from Ukraine’s resistance and resilience.

He added: “If asked to sign up again, if the need is there, then I will 100 per cent be there to serve my country alongside my fellow veterans and service personnel.”

Harry told the publication he believed “things are going to change” in January, when Democrats take control of both chambers of Congress.

![Duke of Sussex meets Ukrainian war veterans at the Superhumans Center, a specialized medical, prosthetics, and rehabilitation facility in Lviv](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/15/14/01M7FRSDK15QACAPRV2CAJCE14.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

He said: “I think there’s been a big separation between what American society feels regarding support for Ukraine, which I think is incredibly high and always has been, compared to a certain group – or in this case an administration – that was perhaps thinking about it differently.”

He has previously criticised Trump’s administration’s stance on the Ukraine war and called on the US to uphold its “international treaty obligations”.

The duke also told The Telegraph that he believes Russia is now in a desperate position and should be working on an “exit strategy”.

He said a number of Russian soldiers being killed on the frontline was “insane”, adding: “There is no version where Russia is making any more advances. It’s quite the opposite.

“What I am told is that more strikes from rockets in Russia and drone strikes into civilian infrastructure is just a sign of desperation and a sign that they are losing.”
