Nationwide Building Society paid out £440m to customers in the first half of the year under the Fairer Share scheme which sees its members receive some of its profits.

The mutual delivered strong growth across its key markets of mortgages and savings, as consumers continued to opt for a change of their current account provider - and Nationwide’s £175 switch bonus proving a big lure. It means Nationwide have been top of the switching tables, regularly published by CASS, for eight consecutive quarters.

Delivering the half-year results, chief executive Dame Debbie Crosbie highlighted that Nationwide had secured almost half of all new student accounts being opened and that the building society remained top of UK customer satisfaction surveys too.

“Once again, we’ve delivered market-leading growth in mortgages and retail deposits,” Dame Crosbie said.

“We’ve opened more Nationwide personal current accounts than ever before, including almost half of all new student accounts, and we’ve been recognised as number one in the UK for customer satisfaction. That shows the strength of our mutual model: giving people choice, good value and service they can rely on.”

Underlying profit increased by 23 per cent to just over £1.2 billion, Nationwide said, with savings deposit almost a third (32 per cent) higher on average than the market in the first half of the year. Profit before tax was £673m after factoring in the Fairer Share payment to members.

In the mortgage market, Nationwide continues to be a major player in the UK.

Net lending hit £7.7bn, up from £4.7bn a year earlier, while the share of market balances increased to 16.4 per cent. Nationwide say they helped more than 43,000 first-time buyers into homes, including through its Helping Hand mortgage.

The mutual’s share of the savings market also increased, to 12.4 per cent, as retail customers landed an additional £8.2bn into current and savings accounts with Nationwide.
