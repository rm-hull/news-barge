Joanne Young says that she is living with “a pain that’s hard to put into words” after being sexually abused by multiple men at the behest of her husband.

Over a period of years, Ms Young’s husband, Philip Young, drugged her and advertised her for sex at their home in Swindon over multiple years.

He previously pleaded guilty to multiple charges of abuse against her.

Norman Macksoni, 48, Richard Wilkins, 62, Connor Sanderson-Doyle, 32, and Mohammed Hassan, 38, were all convicted at Winchester Crown Court on Thursday.

Below is Ms Young’s full statement, delivered outside the court.

![Joanne Young outside Winchester Crown Court](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/19/16/2026-11-19T163508Z_543275261_RC23ROARM28H_RTRMADP_3_BRITAIN-CRIME-RAPE.JPG?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&trim=271%2C0%2C3007%2C0&auto=webp)

“The guilty result today is a relief, and it’s a relief not just for me and my family, but a relief for what this means for other women in the future. It means that those who find themselves facing what I have are more likely to come forward, and more likely to have faith in the criminal justice system.

“More broadly, for women and girls who have been abused by men, it sends the message that you can be believed and understood. Knowing what has happened to me without my knowledge or consent has been incredibly difficult to process, and this abuse has cost me my life as I knew it. The person I trusted most in the world betrayed me in the most horrific way.

“This has resulted in a devastating impact on the lives of myself and my children. What happened to me was not the act of one man alone. Others were invited to take part, and they chose to. That choice makes [them] complicit in what was done to me, and today’s verdicts recognise that.

“This case also raises serious questions about how the law deals with consent. Consent has to come from the person themselves, no one should be able to give it on your behalf, and no one should be able to rely on someone else’s word that you agreed.

“At no point did anyone ask me for my consent. I urge the government to look at this as a priority, so that women are properly protected in the future. I live with a pain that’s hard to put into words, and this is something that will affect our lives forever. It is terrifying that this could happen to anyone.

“What I have been through means I now never feel completely safe, and while it can now be hard for me to leave the house, this horrific abuse showed me that home offered no protection either. Even though at the time I believed I was safe, what happened to me destroys trust, and I’m working hard to rebuild my life.

“I would like to give my thanks to my partner, my family and friends, who have supported me throughout.

“Thank you also to Wiltshire Police, the Crown Prosecution Service and the many others who have supported me over the last two and a half years.

“To anyone who has followed the trial and has recognised their own experience, please know that you are not alone and there are people who will believe you. There is support available if you come forward, and refuges that can offer a place of safety.

“What is happening to you is not your fault. To everyone else, please speak out about violence against women and girls.

“This abuse thrives in secrecy, so it is important for both men and women to recognise it as an issue and take action where possible. Silence is not an option if we are serious about preventing abuse.

“Finally, the court has today shown there will be consequences for those who make the choice to abuse. I am determined that their choices will not define me. I will now use my experience to try to help others. I also owe it to myself to live the best life I can with my children and loved ones as we move forward.”
