## Trump, Putin and Xi sign a joint declaration in China with no breakthrough on Ukraine, European leaders meet US envoys in Berlin, Spain’s election rivals debate, and COP31 talks enter their final stretch in Turkey.

## On today's show:

Donald Trump, Vladimir Putin, and Xi Jinping hail "making history" after signing a joint "Declaration on Peace" on the sidelines of the APEC summit in Shenzhen.

Yet despite Trump referring to Putin as "my friend," the talks produced no breakthrough on Russia’s war in Ukraine, overshadowed by fresh Kremlin threats of retaliation over the Crimea Bridge. Is this a dangerous "Yalta 2.0" sphere-of-influence carve-up, or a superpower flop?

Meanwhile, Europe pushes back from Berlin, where the European-led Coalition of the Willing and Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy convened today alongside US negotiators Jared Kushner and Special Envoy Steve Witkoff to hammer out future security guarantees and a peace mandate. Our Germany correspondent, Lena Roche, joins us live from Berlin with the latest.

With just ten days to go before Spaniards head to the polls on November 29, Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez faces off tonight against his conservative rival, Alberto Núñez Feijóo, in a decisive televised debate.

With polls suggesting Spain could swing to the right, former MP and IE University professor Antonio Roldán joins us from Madrid to break down Sánchez’s high-stakes snap election gamble.

And finally, as the COP31 climate conference enters its final stretch in Turkey ahead of Friday's deadline, negotiators remain deeply divided over climate financing and the phase-out of fossil fuels.

After EU Climate Chief Wopke Hoekstra told Euronews that Europeans are focused on implementation rather than money, we ask whether governments have stopped caring about climate—joined by UN Secretary-General candidate and former UN General Assembly President María Fernanda Espinosa, alongside Club of Rome Honorary President and Earth4All Executive Chair Sandrine Dixson-Declève.
