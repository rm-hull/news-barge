Ukraine has struck the bridge linking Russia and occupied Crimea for the third time in a week, hours before Donald Trump, Vladimir Putin and Xi Jinping held an unprecedented trilateral meeting in China.

Train services have been suspended across the 19-kilometre (12-mile) crossing – a flagship project inaugurated by Mr Putin in 2018 and a potent symbol of Moscow’s illegal annexation of the Ukrainian peninsula four years prior.

Kyiv confirmed it hit the bridge overnight into Thursday using domestically produced Neptune coastal missiles, damaging the railway section and halting rail traffic across the Kerch Strait.

The overnight bombardment dealt a humiliating blow to Mr Putin just hours before he joined the US president and his Chinese counterpart for trilateral talks in Shenzhen, China.

![A satellite image of the Crimean Bridge before its collapse](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/19/19/2026-11-19T152613Z_466163552_RC2VQOAPMIAT_RTRMADP_3_UKRAINE-CRISIS-ATTACK-CRIMEA-BRIDGE.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Mr Trump warmly greeted the Russian leader by asking “Who is he? He’s my friend” and declared the trio were “really making history” as they adopted a four-point “peace declaration” on the sidelines of the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (Apec) summit.

While the leaders pledged to hold talks on preventing nuclear proliferation, reforming the United Nations and upholding state immunity before international courts, the joint statement notably omitted any mention of ending the war in Ukraine.

Trump later dialled into a parallel Coalition of the Willing summit in Berlin – attended by prime minister Andy Burnham and Ukrainian president Volodymyr Zelensky – where he reportedly urged European allies to stop funding Kyiv.

Sidelined European leaders voiced alarm at the warm reception afforded to Putin in Shenzhen, with Danish prime minister Mette Frederiksen warning he would emerge “even more emboldened” and “hit us Europeans even harder”.

![Donald Trump’s reference to ‘friend’ Vladimir Putin has sparked concerns on Washington’s shifting rhetoric](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/19/14/19-4bc0a73923de4d3d9c70bea77fc90de6.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Speaking after the Shenzhen talks, Putin directly linked the Crimean Bridge strike to a breakdown in negotiations, claiming he had asked Russia’s Security Council to consider halting attacks on energy facilities and at sea before Kyiv hit the crossing.

“After the strikes on the Crimean Bridge, there will be no agreements. We will respond to these strikes,” Putin said, warning that Russia’s retaliation would be “far more painful than what the Kyiv regime did to the bridge”.

Ukrainian foreign minister Andrii Sybiha dismissed comparisons between the Shenzhen gathering and the 1945 Yalta Conference – where major powers divided Europe into spheres of influence – pointing to the crippled Kerch crossing.

“Shenzhen is not the Yalta of Putin’s dreams. Meanwhile, Russia has practically lost its bridge to the real Yalta in occupied Ukrainian Crimea,” Mr Sybiha wrote on social media. “Time for him to wake up, get real, and end the war he can never win.”

![Russia’s response will be ‘far more painful than what the Kyiv regime did to the bridge,’ Putin vowed](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/19/19/2305290666..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Crimea’s Russian-installed governor, Sergei Aksyonov, claimed rail travel would resume later on Thursday, insisting in a video message that “Crimea is with Russia forever”, while Russia’s transport ministry said nobody had been hurt and engineers were assessing the damage.

Thursday’s missile strike followed two earlier Ukrainian assaults on Monday and Tuesday that severely damaged the parallel road deck.

The attacks come amid deepening transatlantic friction over Kyiv’s long-range strikes inside Russian-held territory.

On Tuesday, Pentagon chief strategist Elbridge Colby walked out of a Nato meeting of the Ukraine Defence Contact Group in Brussels after rebuking Kyiv over its deep-strike campaign, arguing the attacks lacked military value and failed to blunt Russian advances on the front line.

Mr Colby’s abrupt departure – before Ukrainian defence minister Yevhenii Khmara could deliver a classified battlefield briefing – alarmed European allies, sparking fears in that Washington’s rhetoric is increasingly echoing Moscow’s.

British defence secretary Wes Streeting hailed the Kerch Bridge strike as “another example of Ukrainian ingenuity,” while German defence minister Boris Pistorius dismissed Mr Colby’s criticism of Kyiv’s deep-strike strategy as “stupid”.
