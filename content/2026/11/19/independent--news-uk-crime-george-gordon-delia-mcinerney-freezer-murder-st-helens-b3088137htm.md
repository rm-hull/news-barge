A man who allegedly killed his female friend told police officers “she’s in the freezer” as he was led out of his flat following his arrest, a court has heard.

George Gordon, 65, kept the dismembered body of Delia McInerney in a chest freezer, along with her five guinea pigs, for nearly three years after he allegedly murdered her in April 2023, Liverpool Crown Court has heard.

Body-worn camera footage that was played in court on Thursday showed officers knocking on the door to the flat in Gerards Court, St Helens, at approximately 4.30pm on 8 January.

Gordon, wearing a knitted jumper, could be seen sitting in a chair in the hallway of the flat after opening the door to officers.

In a statement, Pc William Roberts said: “The address was in a general state of disrepair, was full of clutter and I identified bodily fluids on a number of surfaces.”

In the footage, an officer can be heard telling a colleague: “This flat is absolutely disgusting.”

![Delia McInerney's body was found in January](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/04/15/01M6KF08YTFPP01J69DXQM108X.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Mr Roberts stated that Gordon was “extremely obstructive” when asked questions about Ms McInerney’s whereabouts.

In the footage, he could be heard telling officers he had last spoken to her four weeks earlier when she was in Cornwall. He subsequently said she had been in Denmark and claimed he was not sure where she was.

Gordon initially did not answer when asked if Ms McInerney was safe and well, but when questioned again and told to give a yes or no answer, he replied: “No.”

He then told officers “she isn’t alive”, but initially said she was not in the flat, at her address, or at a property he claimed to have in Wales.

Footage showed Gordon being escorted out of the property by Pc Roberts. As they walked through the doors in the block of flats, he could be heard saying: “She is in there.”

He then said: “She’s in the freezer.”

As he was put into a police van, he told an officer she was in the freezer and was in two parts.

Earlier, Gordon had requested to change his clothes before going to the police station due to his incontinence. When he was asked if he had any essential medication, Gordon laughed and said he was on 55 to 60 tablets a day, as well as morphine for pain.

He told officers: “I know I’ll be heading to prison after this.”

Police were contacted earlier that day by social services who raised concerns about Ms McInerney, who was 62 when she died.

When officers attended her property in Anfield, Liverpool, at about midday on 8 January, a neighbour put them in touch with Gordon, who said his friend was in Denmark.

![George Gordon denies murder](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/19/17/01M6PCDD0J9C12WJ5AFN20FGS7.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

But police checks showed her phone was online in the St Helens area and she had more than £22,000 worth of debt on credit cards, the jury was told.

Earlier in the day, the court heard from veterinary pathologist Dr Emanuele Ricci who had carried out post-mortem examinations on two of the five guinea pigs discovered in the freezer.

He said his opinion was that the animals, Timmy and Wogglet, died by drowning, with forceful restraint.

Gordon, who did not attend court on Thursday as the jury was told he was not feeling well, denies murder and causing unnecessary suffering to animals.

The trial was adjourned until Monday.
