A dispute has broken out in a Cotswold village between the ex-wife of a baronet and residents over her plans for a new family home in the heart of the parish.

Lady Teresa Clarke, who was married to Sir Toby Clarke, wants to build the stylish three-bedroom home in Bibury, where Sir Toby, who died in 2019, owned part of the Bibury Estate.

The Clarkes’s ties with the village go back a century after Sir Toby’s grandfather, Sir Orme Clarke and his wife Elfrida, who was a Roosevelt related to the 26th US president Theodore, bought the estate’s mansion.

Almost all of the family are also buried in cemetery of the village’s 11th Century St Mary’s Church.

However, Lady Clarke’s bid to return the family to its roots has been hit by opposition, with more than 40 people opposing the planning application.

![Lady Teresa Clarke was married to Sir Toby Clarke, 6th Baronet. They had three children and divorced before he died in 2019.](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/17/17/10/shutterstock_editorial_7526049cd.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

It appears that the trigger for resistance was a proposed deal to gift a small plot of land for an extension of the cemetery and a new school building, which neighbours strongly objected to.

Villager Leslie Smith, whose home would face the school building, said the land earmarked for development was in line to be listed as “local green space” for the district’s Local Plan, which is yet to be published.

He said there were also concerns the new house would impact the historic character of the 627-population village, which was named the most beautiful village in the world by *Forbes*, and attracts thousands of tourists each year.

![Sir Toby was the grandson of Elfrida Roosevelt, who was first cousin once removed to US president Theodore. She lived in Bibury Court with her husband Sir Orme Clarke. Elfrida is pictured here top left with her father Alfred.](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/18/14/36/dist.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“This is a historic part of Bibury that has not changed for a century which is protected by a conservation area,” he told *The Independent*. “If you stick a house here, however tasteful, it is completely incongruous to anything in the area.”

Other villagers raised fears over the “fragile state” of Bibury in the face of overtourism and the impact on the village’s narrow roads.

The parish council initially supported the planning application, however after villager opposition an extraordinary meeting was held last month where councillors heard opposition and decided to rescind on the council’s earlier position.

![The proposed new home would be carefully conceived to blend in wiht the context of 19th Century Cotswold cottages, the planning application says](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/18/15/15/House-plan.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Lady Clarke, who lives five miles away in Coln Saint Aldwyns, told *The Independent* she did not want a dispute, and had wanted to support the village with the needed extension of the cemetery and land for a school site for toddler groups – but claimed that this had been the problem, and it was now likely she would focus solely on building the house for now.

![The site location for the planning application, with the section north of Cemetery Lane where the home would be built. The land south of Cemetery Lane would be gifted to the parish.](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/18/14/01/Layout.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The three-bedroom house, according to the planning documents, would be architecturally influenced by surrounding 19th Century cottages, with steeply pitched roofs, forward-facing gable dormers and specially-designed chimneys.

“I just would like to keep it as uncontroversial as possible really,” Lady Clarke said. “I feel it’s very sad that we’ve had this recent problem and I just think when everybody calms down a bit, hopefully it’ll all move forward.

![Bibury, named the most beautiful village in the world by Forbes magazine, attracts thousands of visitors every year](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/05/16/25/iStock-690431888-9eb5dc80.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“We don’t think it impacts, certainly the house doesn’t impact on anyone really. Unfortunately I was persuaded to do this thing for the cemetery and the school building. I was asked to do that and we unfortunately didn’t realise that it was going to cause such an issue.”

Lady Clarke said she received a positive response to the pre-application earlier this year, and that opposition started coming in only last month from a small number of people, leading to the parish council’s extraordinary meeting. The council, which has voted to abstain, has recommended that Historic England be involved in the decision.

The planning application states that Lady Clarke would use the home for private use for her and her family, who she said had an “emotional attachment” to the village.

![Bibury Court, once the home of the Clarkes, was sold not long after Elfrida Roosevelt died in 1963. Sir Toby later lived with Lady Clarke in Church House in the village](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/18/14/02/Bibury_Court_Hotel_(geograph_2856845).jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

After marrying Sir Toby, the couple lived in Bibury, raising their three children – Theodora, Augusta and Lawrence – who were all christened at the church. After divorce, Sir Toby sold the house in 2012, and later died in 2019.

Lady Clarke, who is the sister of Jacob Rees-Moggs’ wife Helena, said her children had a home in the village until 2022.

“If we can have it [a family home in Bibury], it’d be a great, a wonderful thing to have,” she said. “It’s a very special place, very beautiful and a huge part of the family.”

A planning committee of councilllors at Cotswold District Council will make a decision on the planning application in the coming months.
