Prince Harry says he would rejoin the British Army to fight against Russia if needed, saying he believes Europe has finally “woken up to the threat” of Kremlin leader Vladimir Putin.

On a visit to Lviv in western Ukraine, the Duke of Sussex said he believed the UK and Russia had effectively been in conflict for the past decade.

“Just because there’s not drones, bullets and rockets flying at us, it doesn’t mean that we’re not at war,” he said.

The Duke said the UK could learn a lot from Ukraine’s resistance against Russian aggression, adding: “If asked to sign up again, if the need is there, then I will 100 per cent be there to serve my country alongside my fellow veterans and service personnel.”

“There is a lot that we can learn from Ukraine. I think there’s a lot that the whole world can learn from Ukraine,” he told *The Telegraph*.

Asked whether he was concerned about today’s geopolitical situation, Harry said: “I think we should all be nervous, absolutely. And I think we should be prepared, and I think that those of us that have served should be ready to serve again.”

The duke served two operational tours in Afghanistan as an Apache helicopter pilot with the British Army - from December 2007 to February 2008 and from September 2012 to January 2013. He ended his military career in 2015.

![Harry served twice in Afghanistan as a helicopter pilot](https://static.independent.co.uk/2023/08/30/12/PA-56552408.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Harry and his wife Meghan returned to the UK in August with their two children, after six years of living in the US, following their decision in 2020 to step down as senior royals.

The King’s second son also issued a thinly veiled criticism of US president Donald Trump’s approach to the Ukraine conflict.

“I think there’s been a big separation between what American society feels regarding support for Ukraine, which I think is incredibly high and always has been, compared to a certain group – or in this case an administration that was perhaps thinking about it differently,” he told the podcast.

Discussing the midterm elections and the Democrats’ forthcoming takeover of the House of Representatives and the Senate, he added: “As of January, things are going to change, so I think we should all be looking forward to that.

“Europe has a united front, and it would be fantastic to have more American support for that as well.”

The duke has established a good professional rapport with Ukrainian president Volodymyr Zelensky.

He said he believed, based on his four visits to the country and regular updates from political and military figures, that Ukraine was winning the war but added: “There needs to be some form of exit strategy for Russia.”

The number of Russian soldiers that Ukraine was killing was “insane”, he said.

And he warned it was important not to become complacent, insisting that now was the time to “double our support” rather than step back.

![Russian drone attacks in places such as Odesa are causing devastation](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/18/18/2305175645..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“There is no version of where Russia is making any more advances.

“It's quite the opposite. What I’m told is the more strikes from rockets from Russia and and drone strikes into civilian infrastructure is just a sign of desperation, a sign that they are losing.”

Losing 30,000 or more Russian and North Korean soldiers every month was unsustainable, he said.

“I think it's appalling that civilian infrastructure, especially hospitals and medical centres, are being targeted. Humanitarian aid centres are being targeted as well.

“International law must mean something. So I think the rest of the world could and should do more in that regard.”

He said the war was a psychological one - “to keep everybody awake all night, to bomb them constantly”.

Talking of his Invictus games for wounded service personnel, he said: “The Invictus spirit lives in every single Ukrainian person, not just veterans, that I’ve met.”
