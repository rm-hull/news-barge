A final regulation released on Thursday by President Donald Trump’s administration will make it simpler for businesses in various industries to categorize workers as independent contractors rather than employees, who carry higher financial costs and receive greater legal protections.

The Department of Labor repealed a standard implemented during the administration of former President Joe Biden, a Democrat, substituting it with a legal framework supported by corporate trade groups.

The updated measure could lower expenses for businesses across a range of sectors, including healthcare, trucking, retail sales, and app-based transportation and delivery services like Uber that rely heavily on contractors.

Such firms routinely face lawsuits accusing them of improperly categorizing workers to save money, which they deny.

Labor Department officials stated that the 2024 regulation was legally flawed and that the revised test creates a clearer standard for both companies and staff.

"It’s more important than ever to ensure our labor regulations keep pace with the changing ways that Americans choose to work, and businesses choose to operate," Labor Secretary Keith Sonderling said in a statement.

The regulation takes effect in January, precisely 60 days after its official publication.

## New rule focuses on control

Under the Biden-era policy, distinct factors were established for deciding whether a worker should be categorized as an employee or contractor under federal wage law, and any single element could tip the scales toward employee status.

The standard finalized on Thursday instead focuses on whether workers are subject to a company’s control or have opportunities for profit or loss driven by their own initiative or investment.

Surveys indicate that traditional employees can cost businesses up to 30% more because they receive minimum wage guarantees, overtime pay, unemployment coverage, expense reimbursements, and other protections contractors do not get.

Worker categorization has been among the most contentious labor issues over the past decade. Trade associations campaigned vigorously to repeal the 2024 policy after a congressional push by Republicans to halt it stalled.

The Biden-era policy had reversed a regulation enacted during Trump's first term stating that workers who run their own businesses or work for rival firms—such as drivers taking jobs for both Uber and Lyft—can be treated as contractors. The newly finalized criteria closely resemble that framework.

Supporters of the Biden-era rule have said misclassification of workers is widespread in some sectors, and that repealing the rule would allow employers to strip workers of basic legal protections.
