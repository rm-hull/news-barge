*(Image: First Bus)*

Two-hundred First York bus drivers are set to walk out next weekend in a pay dispute.

The workers will take initial strike action on Saturday (November 28), and Sunday (November 29).

The industrial action, backed by Unite the union, will severely impact bus travel, including park and ride services.




Sharon Graham, general secretary of Unite, said: "First’s profits continue to rise yet it's hard-working York drivers are expected to put up with second-class wages.

"First can afford to put forward a reasonable offer but is choosing not to out of greed."

First Bus has confirmed that a "limited network" will operate during the strike, including Park and Ride.

Megan Hope-Rushforth, service delivery director at First Bus, said: "We are very disappointed and saddened at the decision to go ahead with strikes.

"Industrial action will be damaging to everyone and cause severe disruption to the daily lives of our customers who rely on the bus to get to work, education and for appointments as well as the thousands of people that want to enjoy the city in the lead up to Christmas."

Drivers are calling for pay parity with their colleagues at Yorkshire Coastliner, who earn £16.90 an hour.

York drivers currently earn £15.58 an hour, Unite said.

A spokesperson for the union said: "Earlier this week, First Group reported a seven per cent rise in its operating profits, taking them to £110 million in the six months to September 2026."

Darren Rushworth, regional officer for Unite, said: "First will be entirely responsible for the disruption caused to York residents, visitors and businesses during the Christmas period.

"There is still time to avoid industrial action but that will require First tabling a deal our members can accept."
