# Bring our failing water companies back into public ownership: Yorkshire Post Letters

## Bryn Glover, Kirkby Malzeard, Ripon.

The latest abysmal ratings awarded to most of our water companies have provoked reactions of surprise and dismay in many people.

But to others these ratings are simply the inevitable consequence of the basic constitutions of the companies in question; organisations such as they can either be primarily directed to the provision of service or to the creation of profit.

The German language has two wonderful words to define that difference, which sadly, we do not have in English.

These are Gesellschaft and Gemeinschaft.

The problem arises when organisations are set up which purport to be combinations of the two motivations.

All is well during times of plenty and prosperity, but when times become more difficult and cash supplies dwindle, then difficult choices need to be made. Under UK law (and that of other countries also) it would be unlawful for company directors to make decisions which do not serve the basic interests of shareholders.

So when financial problems loom, it is inevitable (and lawful) that the profit-making interests take precedence over public service. (In German parlance, Gesellschaft wins over Gemeinschaft.)

There is clearly only one long-term solution for our long-suffering water suppliers and sewage removers, and that is to remove from them the onerous burden of needing to service their shareholders. Which is to say, to bring them all back under public ownership.
