The sister of a young woman who died after undergoing a Brazilian butt lift has told an inquest that she struggled with low self-esteem and was searching for an “elusive perfection” through cosmetic procedures.

Alice Webb, 33, suffered five cardiac arrests after receiving a BBL injection from beauty influencer Jordan Parke, known as the Lip King, in Gloucester in September 2024. She later died in hospital.

Giving evidence at Gloucestershire Coroner’s Court, her sister April Palmer described how Ms Webb, a mother of five, had struggled to recognise her own beauty despite reassurance from those closest to her.

“Behind her bright and caring nature, Alice quietly struggled with low self-worth,” Ms Palmer said.

She told the inquest that negative comments had affected her sister, who first became interested in beauty and aesthetics while training as a hairdresser after leaving school.

Ms Palmer said her sister's first cosmetic procedure had given her a small boost in confidence, but she continued to seek what she described as “elusive perfection”.

“She never needed to. She was truly beautiful inside and out,” she said.

Ms Webb later trained and qualified to carry out aesthetic treatments herself, developing her interest into a career. Her sister described her as a perfectionist who cared about the wellbeing of her clients and would advise against treatments if she believed they were not in their best interests.

In a written statement, Ms Palmer also remembered her sister as a warm and generous person who loved country music and spending time with her family.

“Alice will be remembered forever as a fiercely loving mum, a cherished daughter, sister, aunt, and granddaughter, and a loyal friend whose warmth, wisdom, and laughter touched everyone she met,” she said.

The inquest has heard that Mr Parke was filmed carrying out the procedure on Ms Webb. In the footage, he could reportedly be heard laughing and saying: “I’m exhausted”.

The court heard that he administered seven times the recommended amount of filler before Ms Webb became unwell and suffered cardiac arrest.

Mr Parke, 34, from Manchester, had been under investigation for gross negligence manslaughter in connection with her death before he died earlier this year.

The procedure took place at the studio of another beautician, Jemma Pawlyszyn, who told the inquest she had allowed Mr Parke to use her Studio 23 premises as a favour.

In a written statement, Ms Pawlyszyn said she had not been involved in the procedure and had not received or expected payment for the use of her room.

She said Mr Parke had used the premises to train Ms Webb during the day before carrying out the procedure that evening.

Ms Pawlyszyn also told the court that Ms Webb had her own aesthetics business, Crystal Clear, and had undergone the BBL procedure with Mr Parke at least twice previously.

She said she entered the room after Mr Parke told her Ms Webb was feeling faint, and later helped place her in the recovery position while they followed instructions from an ambulance call handler.

The inquest also heard that Mr Parke had previously been issued with prohibition notices preventing him from carrying out BBL procedures in Dudley and Salford, following concerns raised by environmental officers.

A post-mortem examination found a gel-like substance, identified as hyaluronic acid, in the fatty tissue and muscle of Ms Webb's buttock, as well as below the tissue and along her sciatic nerve.

The substance was also identified in her lungs, brain, heart, thyroid gland and kidney.

Home Office forensic pathologist Dr Stephen Leadbetter gave the cause of death as “embolisation of hyaluronic acid from introduction into buttocks”.

The inquest, which is scheduled to last three days, is continuing.
