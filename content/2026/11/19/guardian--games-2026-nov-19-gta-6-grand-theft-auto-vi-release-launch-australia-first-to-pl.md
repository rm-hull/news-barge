Gamers across Australia woke up Thursday morning feeling like Christmas had come early, some bleary-eyed from late-night sessions on the PlayStation, others with grand plans for a weekend foray in Vice City when their day jobs let them loose.

Grand Theft Auto VI, more than a decade in the making, is here. And the reviews, mere hours into release, reflect universal acclaim. The game has already vaulted to the top spot on Metacritic’s list of the best games of all time, sitting above 1998’s The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time with an unprecedented 99/100 score.

Australians who put in a few hours as soon as the game launched at 12am said they were already beyond impressed.

“I had it loaded up bang on midnight,” Pat, 26, told Guardian Australia. “I just got on straight away, it was like being a kid on Christmas morning.”

He spent about two hours exploring the world of Leonida and its metropolis – based heavily on Miami – and his initial thoughts echoed others who described the GTA VI release as the “moon landing” of gaming.

“This is a fully lived-in, breathable world. It’s so much more tangible than so many video games,” Pat said. “I was at a loss for words trying to express to my girlfriend how significant this is.”

![Maggie Brennan, who streams under the banner retromaggie on Twitch, says she is already blown away by GTA VI.](https://i.guim.co.uk/img/media/53ac3eb2cb6dc9001ada3ba916d998589415975e/0_0_2596_1462/master/2596.jpg?width=445&dpr=1&s=none&crop=none)

Ziggy Grant, a longtime fan of the GTA series, spent Wednesday night farewelling the old games before ushering in the new. Grant bought a Japanese-style mini truck in the online version of GTA V, painted it chrome and drove it in a loop around the map with other fans of the franchise.

“I just thought I should do it, it was just a silly thing,” Grant, who lives in South Australia, said, joining many other players on an online server. Around 11.29pm, just before the release of VI in Australia, everyone started to ping away. “The last ride, I had to do it to pay homage to GTA online. … I just got in my biggest plane, and flew off.”

GTA VI was waiting for him. Grant played until about 4.30am before he ran out of steam, leaving the house early Thursday morning to restock up on supplies and “life support”.

The new game is already impressive in its realism.

“It’s a lot like the Gold Coast,” Grant said of GTA VI, adding he’s never missed the launch of a Rockstar product. “This is just like being in a subtropical place on a hot summer’s day. … Everything’s more deliberate, it’s not just this goofy game where you go in and do a rampage.”

“It’s like the world is so alive, it’s just assaulting your senses the whole time.”

![‘I’m just really keen to get home and play it,’ Angus said. ‘Hopefully the work day goes quick.’](https://i.guim.co.uk/img/media/3191830b6980b78e571432fd41fcd8e435b9521e/0_0_2602_1460/master/2602.jpg?width=445&dpr=1&s=none&crop=none)

Angus, 27, has the game downloaded and ready to play but had to be at work on Thursday, “which is unfortunate”. He and two friends have developed a detailed plan involving pizzas, beer and hour-long shifts on a shared TV on Thursday night into the early hours of Friday morning.

“The last one came out when I was pretty much in grade 8,” he said. “I just turned 27, and the hype hasn’t really gone at all. It’s the only game where I’ve genuinely felt excited, it’s like Christmas morning to be honest.

“I’m just really keen to get home and play it. Hopefully the work day goes quick.”

He took Friday off to get in as much time as he could, with the trio planning to pass the controller around between power naps.

Rockstar Games has yet to release data on how many copies of the game have sold, worldwide or in Australia. But internet data and banking trends show it is exceedingly popular.

NBN Co said internet traffic set an all-time record last Thursday when pre-orders for GTA VI went live. But that record was broken again on Wednesday night. The government enterprise behind the Australian broadband network said it saw the most data “ever moved in a single second”.

“Looks like a few Aussies left their GTA VI download to the last minute 👀,” NBN Co wrote on social media. “That’s enough data to download GTA VI at 212GB around 21 times. Every. Single. Second.”

Maggie Brennan, an online creator who streams under the banner retromaggie on Twitch, said the game had been fantastic so far.

“The graphics are beautiful, from the texture of the pores in the character’s skin to the air blowing through their hair. Rockstar have done an amazing job,” she said. “But that water! That water is beautiful.”

![Gamers have been astounded at GTA VI’s graphics – particularly the water](https://i.guim.co.uk/img/media/fc62c7b7f6e201946bb10bf8780273cef16753d2/0_0_3840_2160/master/3840.jpg?width=445&dpr=1&s=none&crop=none)

She said just hours in there was already an untold level of detail for players to interact with. Gamers can renovate their houses, fix their cars, lift weights and follow the story, all buttressed by Rockstar Games’ “unhinged madness that we all love”.

Up in Sydney, Pat said he plans to fit in GTA VI around his work schedule as much as possible, with the whole weekend already written off for time in Vice City.

“I was supposed to have a work event on tonight and it got cancelled,” he said, temporarily hopeful. “But it’s back on now. The amount of sorrow I felt … ”

“I might have to work from home tomorrow,” he added. “I think the light rail might not be running properly … ”
