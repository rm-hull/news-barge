Cyber security specialists have been responding to an artificial intelligence-generated attack on a key European Union institution for almost a month, an official confirmed on Thursday, leaving a number of the bloc's digital systems offline.

The official offered no further details on the nature of the breach or who might be responsible. It represents the first instance of the 27-member union disclosing a cyberattack carried out using an AI tool.

"AI-assisted tools can be used and especially misused to detect vulnerabilities," said Thomas Regnier, spokesperson for the European Commission, the executive branch of the 27-nation EU.

"Indeed we can confirm that this was done with AI tools," he added. "We have seen many global cyber attacks recently, and of course no one is immune to this."

![The cyber attack was AI-generated, according to an EU official](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/19/14/2026-11-16T235818Z_1663851022_RC2B6OAG3GR7_RTRMADP_3_AI-DATA-CENTER-FUNDS.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Regnier noted that the EU initially detected the security incident on 22 October. Officials immediately opened an investigation while implementing "preventative measures."

The incident was first reported by news website Politico, which stated the breach lasted for at least 10 days and impacted systems used for translation, reimbursement, salaries and timesheets. Regnier declined to confirm that report.

"We temporarily took down several applications, and most of them have since then been restored," Regnier said. So far, he added, "we have not detected any evidence of a data breach affecting commission staff as a whole."

Digital security for the bloc's various institutions, which together employ around 40,000 staff in Brussels, is overseen by the agency CERT-EU.

The body published a blog post on 4 November warning of threats connected to a specific utility known as ARTEX. Though created to use AI to find vulnerabilities in digital networks, it has been linked to recent attacks globally.

"We assess that ARTEX’s public availability will likely accelerate adoption by threat actors, resulting in more frequent, faster, and more scalable automated attacks with a higher success rate," the office warned.

The October cyber attacks targeted the headquarters of the EU’s executive body, though no related incidents were reported by the European Council or the European Parliament.
