*Porcelain Crab Feathered Feast*

This exquisite photo of a porcelain crab catching plankton while half-hidden in an anemone was named the overall winner of The Nature Conservancy’s Oceania Photo Contest. The blue of the shadows gives the image an otherworldly feel.

Despite the name and appearance, porcelain crabs aren’t crabs – the similarity is a result of convergent evolution. The photo of the small crustacean was taken on a coral reef off Gili Air Island in Indonesia by Tammy Nicholas.

A shot of a drifter or pelagic crab clinging to a piece of kelp, shown below, won second prize in the wildlife category of the awards. While it looks large, this crab is, in fact, just a centimetre across. The photo was taken near the Poor Knights Islands of New Zealand by Crispin Middleton.

*Ocean Drifter*

A photograph of water lilies growing in the Cenote Carwash near Tulum, Mexico, shown below, took second place in the prize’s plants and fungi category. Cenotes are sinkholes linked to water-filled caves, and this one got its name because taxi drivers used to clean their cars there. Its Mayan name is Aktun Ha.

![](https://www.newscientist.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/SEI320982781.jpg?w=840)

*Pendants*

Vanessa Torres Macho took this image. The Oceania contest is open only to residents of Australia, New Zealand, Papua New Guinea and the Solomon Islands, but the photographs can be taken anywhere.

A shot of sardines pursued by diving Cape gannets, shown below, took second place in the water category of the awards. This photo, by Justin Tan, was taken off Port St Johns in South Africa during the sardine run, when billions of sardines migrate north along the coast, for reasons that are still not understood.

![](https://www.newscientist.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/SEI320982726.jpg?w=840)

*Frenzy*

Finally, the first prize in the people and nature category went to this image, below, of a researcher filming juvenile sperm whales and a calf off the island of Mauritius in the Indian Ocean. Photographer Vanessa Mignon says this filming was done under permit, but it is often done illegally.
