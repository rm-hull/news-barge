Tech billionaire Elon Musk has dumped President Donald Trump’s MAGA base after Republicans lost massively in the midterms.

“I’m not MAGA,” Musk said during a virtual interview from China, where he’s accompanying the president on his trip to Asia. “I’m not sure what MAGA means, but if that means sort of blind adherence to a set of policies by the Republican Party, then I certainly don’t have that.”

Musk, a frenemy of Trump’s who had a controversial stint at the White House cutting down federal agencies, said his efforts to boost Republican candidates with nearly $500 million in midterm donations “failed.”

He told attendees of *The New York Times’* DealBook Summit that he feels “pretty foolish about having spent so much money and not seeing much for it — or maybe it would have been much worse without that.”

!['I’m not MAGA,' Elon Musk said during a virtual interview from China. 'I’m not sure what MAGA means, but if that means sort of blind adherence to a set of policies by the Republican Party, then I certainly don’t have that'](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/19/0/15/GettyImages-2305777177.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The Democrats won key races in reliably Republican states, giving them control of the House and the Senate when the next Congress convenes in January.

The unpopular Iran war that Trump started eight months before the midterms caused gas prices to skyrocket, putting yet another financial strain on Americans already struggling with the rising cost of living.

While the Iran war was seen as a major factor in the GOP shellacking — even Vice President JD Vance admitted as much — Trump has refused to take the blame.

While Trump conceded during a *60 Minutes* interview earlier this month that voters “didn’t like the war,” he pointed fingers at disliked Republican candidates and the media for unfavorable coverage.

![The unpopular Iran war was seen as a major factor in the GOP shellacking, but Trump has refused to take the blame and has instead pointed fingers at disliked Republican candidates and the media for unfavorable coverage](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/19/0/59/GettyImages-2299253024.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Musk isn’t the only vocal Trump supporter to leave MAGA after the midterms. Conservative podcaster Brett Cooper recently explained her decision to cut ties, saying, “There was a lot of gaslighting that took place.”

Cooper listed several issues that ultimately led to her decision to leave MAGA in a new interview with the *Financial Times*, including Trump’s handling of the government files related to the late convicted sex offender Jeffrey Epstein, the Iran war and the administration’s response to economic concerns.

Podcaster Joe Rogan, who endorsed Trump in the 2024 election and has since criticized several of his controversial moves, admitted after the GOP’s crushing midterm defeat, “They didn’t just lose the podcast bros. They lost most of the country.”
