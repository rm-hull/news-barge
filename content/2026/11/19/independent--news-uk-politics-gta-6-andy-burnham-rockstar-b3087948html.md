Downing Street has confirmed that Prime Minister Andy Burnham will not be playing the new instalment of Grand Theft Auto himself, despite the launch dominating the headlines.

Grand Theft Auto VI, described by No 10 as “British success story”, went live for UK players at midnight on Thursday.

The release brought to an end a 13-year wait for the next installment in the franchise.

Rockstar's blockbuster series was largely developed by a team in Scotland, a point that Downing Street was keen to highlight.

A No 10 spokesperson said: “Rockstar Games is a British success story made in Edinburgh, and many fans are already saying GTA has been worth the wait.”

Asked whether the prime minister would be exploring Vice City, Rockstar's fictional take on Miami, the spokesperson said: “His diary is very full. He won’t be playing it.”

![Andy Burnham’s ‘diary is very full’, No 10 says](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/18/12/2305688942..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The game carries an age rating of 18, and schools have urged pupils not to treat its arrival as a reason to miss lessons.

Some schools are even running prize draws for vouchers among pupils who do attend, in an effort to improve attendance.

The spokesperson said children “should be in classrooms today and tomorrow”, adding: “Every day in school matters.”

Players were quick to post their verdicts on social media after the midnight unlock.

The game has been overwhelmingly praised for its graphics and gameplay.

![Grand Theft Auto VI has gone on sale](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/19/13/2305287653..?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&auto=webp)

Critics have given GTA VI universally positive reviews, with the *Guardian*crediting Rockstar with creating “the most extraordinarily, indulgently detailed virtual world that we have yet seen”.* The Independent* gave the game a five-star review, calling it an “absolute masterpiece”.

Gaming correspondent Jake Brigstock wrote: “This is the best gaming studio at the very top of its game; it has delivered not just *the* game of a generation, but the very best game of all time.”

A Uswitch poll commissioned earlier this month suggests some 2.8 million UK adults will play the game on launch day alone.

Many of those players are expected to call in sick to work, while others booked the day off as annual leave.

Reform UK marked the occasion by releasing a video of leader Nigel Farage playing a fake version of the game.

The spoof featured treasury spokesperson Robert Jenrick rugby-tackling a shoplifter at Brixton market in south London.
