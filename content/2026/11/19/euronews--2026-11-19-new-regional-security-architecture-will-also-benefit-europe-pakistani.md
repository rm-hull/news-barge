## In an exclusive interview for Euronews’ World Conversation show, Pakistan’s deputy prime minister revealed the backstage efforts that led to the US-Israel agreement to end the war in June. He also outlined Pakistan's strategy towards a new regional security architecture in coordination with Europe.

Regional peace across the Middle East should now be established through a special mechanism, Pakistan’s Deputy Prime Minister and Foreign Minister Mohammad Ishaq Dar told Euronews, as “we all have to see the new security architecture (...) and the consequences on the global economy” beyond the Gulf region.

In a wide-ranging exclusive interview in Islamabad for Euronews’ World Conversation programme, Dar outlined the complex diplomatic engagements led by the major regional powers, Pakistan, Turkey, Saudi Arabia, and multiple countries in the region with Europe “towards bringing peace and stability to the region which will be effective and beneficial to the entire globe.”

Dar called the mutual defence Mecca Pact, signed by Pakistan, Turkey and Saudi Arabia in August for their collective and individual security and defence, a possible “beginning of the regional security architecture” because, in his view, it will help not only the region but countries beyond it too.

Pakistan’s foreign minister said the UK, France and Germany are engaging with the new security structure, and that Italy is also interested “to be part of the dialogue”.

In the exclusive Euronews interview, Dar revealed how his government took the initiative in the peace mediations between the US and Iran in the early phases of the war and how he urged his Iranian counterpart not to respond to the US-Israeli attacks.

“In the early morning of the first day of the war, 28 February, I called (Iranian foreign minister Abbas) Araghchi to please exercise strength and don't react, because I was wondering if they might react against the Gulf countries, which unfortunately they did,” Pakistan’s foreign minister said.

“The entire (diplomatic) process began on that day," Dar recalled.

He described how the Pakistani leadership “worked hard to bring them around table,” which resulted in the parties shaking hands and sitting down for negotiations.

“It was 21 hours of a marathon session with no real breaks, but only consultative breaks, and, after the fifth round, I was very sure that by the sixth round we have covered so much of the mileage that we reached a point (of agreement).”

“We didn’t give up,” Dar said, and this effort later became the US-Iran Memorandum of Understanding to end the Iran war, signed on 18 June.

He told Euronews that one of the agreement's core points, “which almost every country on the globe supported, was that there should be no tolls, permits, fees or permissions” in the Strait of Hormuz, adding that the EU and the UK also supported this diplomatic process.

“I think that the sea lanes must be seen as a free movement and Pakistan has not given up” on this principle, Dar said, adding that all parties are still “working hard” on achieving this goal.

He also said that Pakistan “continue to persuade both parties to please respect and honour what you signed on 18 June”.
