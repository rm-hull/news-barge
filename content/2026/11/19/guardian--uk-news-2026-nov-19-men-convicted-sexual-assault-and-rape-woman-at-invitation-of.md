A woman who was raped by strangers after being drugged by her husband has warned of “serious questions about how the law deals with consent”, as four men who abused her were convicted on Thursday.

Philip Young, 50, advertised his then wife, Joanne Young, on dating and swinging websites, presenting it as a “kink” for both of them, Winchester crown court heard. In fact, he had been sedating her, plunging her into an “inanimate state” in which anything could be done to her by the men who answered the adverts, jurors were told.

The former Conservative councillor pleaded guilty earlier in the year to dozens of charges of abuse against his former wife, who has waived her right to anonymity.

On Thursday, Connor Sanderson-Doyle, 32; Richard Wilkins, 62; Norman Macksoni, 48; and Mohammed Hassan, 38, were convicted of various offences against Joanne Young after a three-week trial.

The case has drawn comparisons to that of Gisèle Pelicot, who was drugged by her husband and raped by dozens of strangers in France. It can now be revealed that Wiltshire police received advice from the Pelicot investigators on how to approach their inquiries.

The Crown Prosecution Service said it was the first time a jury had convicted multiple defendants relating to organised drug-facilitated sexual assault in England.

After the verdicts, Wiltshire police said that although rare, the true scale of drug-facilitated sexual offences in the UK could be significantly underreported.

In a statement outside court, Joanne Young – whose bravery was praised by the judge – said the convictions meant “that those who find themselves facing what I have are more likely to come forward, and more likely to have faith in the criminal justice system”.

Saying that she now lives “with a pain that’s hard to put into words”, she added: “Consent has to come from the person themselves. No one should be able to give it on your behalf, and no one should be able to rely on someone else’s word that you agreed.

“At no point did anyone ask me for my consent. I urge the government to look at this as a priority so that women are properly protected in the future.”

Sanderson-Doyle, a Ministry of Defence contractor from Swindon, was convicted of sexual assault and assault by penetration; Wilkins, a taxi driver from Swindon, was found guilty of assault by penetration and rape; Macksoni, a former DJ and waste management worker from Bedfordshire, was convicted of rape; and Hassan, also from Swindon, was found guilty of sexual assault.

A further defendant, Dean Hamilton, 47, from Somerset, had admitted one charge of assault by penetration, two of sexual assault and one of rape.

A spokesperson for Wiltshire police, which investigated Young’s offending, said other victims in similar cases may not know they had been targeted.

He added: “While cases involving organised drug-facilitated sexual assault remain relatively rare compared to other forms of sexual offending, intelligence and other recent cases would suggest this may be a significantly underreported crime type.

“Victims are often unaware they have been drugged, may have fragmented memories of events, or may initially attribute symptoms to alcohol consumption. These factors make detection and reporting particularly challenging.”

Joanne said the abuse had had a devastating impact on her life and the lives of her children but that she hoped the verdict would give other victims of abuse the confidence to come forward.

“The court has today shown that there will be consequences for those who make the choice to abuse,” she said. “I’m determined that their choices will not define me, and I will now use my experience to try to help others. I also owe it to myself to live the best life I can with my children and loved ones as we move forward.”

Mr Justice Saini refused bail applications for the four men convicted on Thursday, telling them they all faced “significant custodial sentences”.

All of the men, including Young and Hamilton, will be sentenced on 21 and 22 December at Winchester crown court.

Young, a father of three, pleaded guilty in January to a total of 56 charges made up of 14 counts of voyeurism, 11 of rape, 11 of administering a substance with intent, seven of assault by penetration, four of sexual assault and one of publishing obscene articles in relation to his wife. He also admitted three charges of making indecent images of children, one of possessing prohibited images of children in July 2024 and four counts of possessing an extreme pornographic image between January 2019 and January 2024.

Over more than a decade, Young – who served as a Swindon borough councillor between 2007 and 2010 – drugged and raped his wife and invited other men on websites and apps such as Craigslist, Grindr and FabSwingers to have sex with her without her knowledge.

Prosecutors told the court the case was about whether it was ever reasonable to have sex with an unknown woman without checking with her first.

Sanderson-Doyle responded to an online advert in 2015 and visited the couple’s home in June 2016. Young filmed him sexually assaulting Joanne as she was drugged, blindfolded and restrained, and heard saying “No”.

Wilkins went to the home in July 2016. Young filmed Wilkins abusing Joanne who again was blindfolded, restrained and seemingly asleep, without obtaining her consent. Police footage of his arrest shows the officers identifying him through distinctive tattoos.

Macksoni visited and raped Joanne in February 2017, while Hassan sexually assaulted her in February 2023. Messages and digital evidence showed Young arranged for Hassan to visit and Hassan had sexual contact with her while she believed he was her husband.

Speaking after the verdicts, James Foster, a specialist prosecutor at the CPS, said: “These men chose to commit unspeakable acts of abuse against Joanne Young without bothering to check if she was consenting.

“By building the case with a suspect-centric approach, we could prove to a jury that Philip Young could not provide consent on the victim’s behalf and each defendant played a part in this horrific sexual abuse.

“During the trial, Macksoni, Wilkins, Sanderson-Doyle and Hassan hid behind the claim that they believed she was freely entering into a sexual relationship with them, despite never seeking reassurances that this was in fact the case.”
