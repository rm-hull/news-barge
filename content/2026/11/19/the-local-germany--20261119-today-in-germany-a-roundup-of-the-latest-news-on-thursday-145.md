**Thursday's top story:Ukraine's allies gather in Berlin as Europe seeks seat at the table**

Germany is hosting Ukraine's allies for a "Coalition of the Willing" summit on Thursday, only hours after Donald Trump, Vladimir Putin and Xi Jinping were due to meet in Shenzhen.

Co-hosted by the leaders of Germany, France and Britain, the Berlin talks will draw more than 30 European leaders, Volodymyr Zelensky and US envoys Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff. The aim is to boost Europe's role in ending the almost five-year war, in a process so far dominated by Washington, Moscow and Kyiv.

There are fears that the three powers could decide Ukraine's fate over the heads of Kyiv and Europe. Chancellor Friedrich Merz has urged Trump: "please, no surprises."

Money is also on the agenda. Ukraine faces a 70-billion-euro funding shortfall next year, and the EU has proposed an extra 45-billion-euro loan. Prime Minister Andy Burnham is expected to announce more than 1,000 long-range strike drones.

Kremlin aide Yuri Ushakov called the Shenzhen meeting "the main thing" and Berlin "secondary". A senior Ukrainian source told AFP the China summit "makes no difference" to the Berlin agenda.

**Berlin faces €2 billion-plus bill for civil servants' back pay**

Berlin must pay its civil servants at least two billion euros in back pay for the years 2008 to 2025, the finance department has confirmed to rbb.

The figure is well above earlier estimates. Finance Senator Stefan Evers (CDU) most recently projected 1.2 billion euros. For the current and coming years, officials expect further costs of 180 million euros annually.

The payments stem from a Federal Constitutional Court ruling last year that Berlin underpaid its civil servants between 2008 and 2020. The court found the state had violated the principle of maintenance, under which an employer must provide a livelihood commensurate with a civil servant's position.

The ruling concerns staff under Pay Scale A, including police officers, teachers and Bundeswehr personnel in higher ranks. However, only those who filed lawsuits are entitled to back pay. Paying everyone would cost around seven billion euros for 2008 to 2020 alone, and the state's reserves are far from sufficient.

Berlin must decide by the end of March 2027 who qualifies. A Salary Restitution Act announced by Evers has not yet been enacted.

**Germany's highest court shuts down BSW party's bid for election recount**

Nearly 21 months after Germany's last federal election, the country's highest court ruled on Wednesday that the votes will not be recounted, and there will be no new election – the official final result stands.

This was the answer to a long awaited legal challenge brought by the BSW party (the German Association of Independent Businesses, which was previously known as the Sahra Wagenknecht Alliance).

Following the election, way back in February of 2025, the BSW had very narrowly missed the five percent threshold to enter the Bundestag. Nationwide they had achieved approximately 4.981 percent of second votes.

During its own investigations, the BSW said it found "anomalies", and estimated that the "potential for miscounted BSW votes" was high.

The Federal Constitutional Court, however, found the arguments unconvincing and ruled the election review complaint "inadmissible".

Some voices, even beyond the BSW, suggested that the process to try and challenge a vote count takes too long in Germany, and that election review should be faster and more accessible regardless of which party challenges a result.

“The process takes too long and is too cumbersome,” said Martin Plum, legal advisor to the CDU/CSU parliamentary group to the Rheinische Post. He added that reform of the review process would speed up the process and “promote trust in our democracy.”

**Germany's Future Prize awarded to microchip researchers from Karlsruhe**

Three researchers from Karlsruhe have won the German Future Prize, awarded by Federal President Frank-Walter Steinmeier. They are Christian Koos of the Karlsruhe Institute of Technology (KIT), Matthias Lauermann of Vanguard Automation and Philipp Dietrich of Keystone Photonics.

The team is developing technology to send data faster and more energy-efficiently by using light instead of electricity. Their methods are meant to help test optical microchips efficiently and connect them precisely to one another.

The Office of the Federal President said the aim is to lay the foundation for more powerful data networks and AI data centres.

The prize is intended to honour scientists and engineers who, through excellent basic and cutting-edge research, develop solutions to pressing societal challenges. These solutions should have the potential to succeed economically and open up new areas of growth.

Awarded for the 30th time, the prize is endowed with 250,000 euros. Last year it went to a project developing a fuel cell propulsion system for zero-emission long-distance transportation.

**GLS and DPD to merge in Germany, creating €4 billion parcel giant**

The parcel services GLS and DPD plan to merge in Germany, creating a company expected to deliver 700 million packages a year with revenue of more than four billion euros.

According to a joint statement, Geopost, DPD's French parent, and the British GLS Group have set up a joint venture into which they will contribute their German parcel assets. GLS's owner will hold 80 percent and DPD's owner 20 percent. Regulatory approval is still pending.

GLS chief executive Karl Pfaff told Handelsblatt the denser network should shrink distances between stops by 20 to 30 percent, saving delivery time. The venture plans to invest more than 500 million euros, mainly in IT and the automation of pickup stations. It also aims to expand to about 25,000 package shops and stations by the end of 2030.

The DPD brand will disappear, with all trucks bearing GLS's blue, white and yellow, Geopost chief Yves Delmas said. GLS is expected to employ around 15,000 people.

Market leader DHL generated 8.2 billion euros from two billion German parcels last year. For consumers, the merger will reduce choice.
