# Countryside Alliance accuses Defra of ‘sidelining’ rural affairs in restructure

## The changes come amid warnings from the Country Land and Business Association that rural Britain is in danger of being reduced to “smiling waxworks”.

The Countryside Alliance has hit out at Defra over a planned restructure which it claims will see rural affairs “sidelined” from the Government department’s brief.

Defra’s full name is the Department for the Environment, Food and Rural Affairs, yet a reorganisation by permanent secretary Paul Kissack appears to see the latter not part of any new directorate.

Mr Kissack revealed that it will be restructured into three main groups: water and marine; farming, land and nature; and food, biosecurity and trade.

He said: “Sometimes I find the way we are organised as a department makes it harder to achieve the outcomes that ministers and the public rightly expect of us.

“So early next year we will take on a new shape. We are going to organise ourselves differently - fully integrating our environmental, economic and resilience ambitions in three large groups.”

![Environment Secretary Dame Angela Eagle speaking at the Labour Party conference 2026](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/jpim-static/image/2026/11/06/17/cs-6bedaced-7f27-4530-b1ef-59063f7e527b.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


His statement, however, made no mention of rural affairs other than saying “we continue to champion rural communities”.

Officials explained that this part of Defra’s responsibilities would be taken on by the “strategic centre of the department”, instead of the three listed groups.

The Countryside Alliance has condemned the move, stating that the restructure "will only serve to reinforce the idea that the government does not care about rural communities".

Chief executive Tim Bonner said: “Rural people are crying out for support and for government policies that help, not harm them.

“We hope that the government has learnt from disastrous policies like the family farm tax, and wants to work for the countryside.

“This planned restructure, however, makes no mention at all of rural affairs or communities: this will only serve to reinforce the idea that the government does not care about rural communities.

![Tim Bonner](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOjdjNzQ3ZWFlLTFmNDUtNGUwNy05MDg1LTcwZjFlMzhiYTMwOTpjNTA2ZGM2ZC1hZTEzLTQ4ODAtYmQ0Ni1jODJjNjY1OTc4MGU=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


“Defra must outline where responsibility for rural affairs and communities lies within their structure.

“They are not just the Department for Environment and Food; they also represent more than 10m rural people, who increasingly feel like they have a government that has forgotten them.”

A Defra spokesperson said that rural affairs would continue to be a ministerial portfolio.

They added: “Rural affairs is a key part of Defra’s mission and that's why we are bringing it into the strategic centre of the department, significantly strengthening our ability to embed support for rural communities in every aspect of our work.”

The changes come amid warnings from the Country Land and Business Association that rural Britain is in danger of being reduced to “smiling waxworks”.

Polling of 2,000 people by More in Common for the CLA has revealed that just 9 per cent see it as a place of business and economic activity.

![Paul Kissack, the group chief executive of the Joseph Rowntree Foundation Group. Credit: JRF](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/jpim-static/image/2025/04/13/9/54/Paul-Kissack-(1).jpg?trim=0,0,902,0&width=640&quality=65)


When asked to describe the countryside, respondents spoke of “interesting walks” “somewhere to enjoy fresh air” and “a haven to escape to.” Few mentioned the businesses or people who live there.

The CLA has warned that this romanticised view is making it harder for rural communities to grow, open businesses, and build new homes.

President Gavin Lane said: “Britain loves its countryside but sometimes forgets people must make a living in it.

“Behind the rolling hills and picture-postcard villages are farmers running businesses, employing local people and putting food on our tables.

“We don't want to be smiling waxworks in a rural museum, preserved for visitors to admire. We are entrepreneurs who want to grow, create jobs and build businesses Britain can be proud of.

“When people think of the countryside as something frozen in time, they tend to forget what it takes to keep it alive.

“We need affordable homes for young families, a planning system that allows rural businesses to grow, and the confidence to invest in our future, without tax bills that stunts ambition.”
