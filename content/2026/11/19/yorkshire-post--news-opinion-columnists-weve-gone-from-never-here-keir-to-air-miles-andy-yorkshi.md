# We've gone from 'Never Here Keir' to 'Air Miles Andy': Yorkshire Post Letters

## Peter Auty, Great Hatfield.

We have now moved on from "Never Here Keir" to "Air Miles Andy".

Was there any need for the PM to make a six minute speech to a half empty auditorium at COP31? There are nearly 100,000 delegates there but obviously not enough thought what our PM had to say was really important.

He was then followed by Ed Miliband the Foreign Secretary while Sadiq Khan seemed to only be there to take selfies with UN staff. What a waste of taxpayers’ time and money.

![Prime Minister Andy Burnham departs 10 Downing Street, London, to attend Prime Minister's Questions at the Houses of Parliament.](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOjVmNDYyOTM2LTcyNzQtNDU2ZS05YmYyLTZkYzViZTc0Y2Y3YTo5MGEwYTRlMi0wODA2LTQxNjEtYTYzMy04NjFhZDczYzc5MGM=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


Our bad mannered PM then snubbed the new Brazilian president, (even though the meeting had been pre-arranged), in his haste to cosy up to the European delegates in Dublin.

It now seems very obvious what is more important to the PM and I wonder what the electorate in Makerfield think now.

As to the new sanitary and phytosanitary, SPS, agreement with the EU; whilst the easing of border checks is welcome, it is the new set of rules that farmers will have to follow that is very worrying.

These are on top of all the rules and regulations that the dysfunctional Defra have introduced, plus no say on future rules and regulations from the EU. I hope all farmers are up to speed with it all, but it seems unlikely.

It appears to this writer that the EU are dictating the negotiations and our weak Labour government is acting like a cat without claws, and is prepared to accept everything offered to take the UK back into the EU through the back door, no matter the cost. Taxpayers take note as it is your money they are spending.

Our PM, in the run up to the Makerfield by-election, stated he agreed with referendum result. It doesn't seem like it now, does it?

"Pulling the wool" comes to mind.

**Thomas W Jefferson, Batty Lane, Howden, Goole.**

Peter Packham of Leeds for Europe thinks that rejoining the EU is “starting to look like common sense” (The Yorkshire Post, November 16).

He previously conceded that we would need to see a “large and resilient” majority in favour of rejoining. It’s nowhere to be seen. Recent polling in Andy Burnham’s Makerfield constituency shows voters believe that essential decision-making powers should reside in our own parliament rather than Brussels, by a margin similar to their 2:1 vote in 2016.

Sir Tony Blair says we shouldn’t rejoin an unreformed EU and Lord Cameron says our present problems are not caused by Brexit. Meanwhile our economy is perking up because of our Brexit freedoms (divergence on AI) while the EU continues to languish. Common sense tells us we are better off trading with them as an independent country, rather than submitting to their failed economic model.

**Martin J Phillips, Tinshill Lane, Leeds.**

While seeking an improvement in dealings with the EU is commendable, the UK need to make more effort to form better relations with countries outside the E.U. starting wi the countries in the British Commonwealth.

The term ‘Commonwealth’ is a bit of a giveaway.

I remember before we joined the Common Market that we were able to import good quality, cheap lamb from Australia and New Zealand.

This was blocked once we joined the Common Market. Agreements for trade relations with our Commonwealth should be prioritised. I note there have been moves to strengthen trade relations with India so we should be ok for curries and other excellent food products.
