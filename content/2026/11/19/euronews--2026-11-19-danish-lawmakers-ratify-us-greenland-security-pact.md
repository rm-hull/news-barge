## The deal lets the United States build two new military bases on the Arctic island and stays in force even if Greenland becomes independent.

Denmark's parliament has unanimously approved a security agreement with the United States and Greenland, easing a row over US President Donald Trump’s ambition to take control of the island.

All 109 lawmakers present in the 179-seat Folketing voted for the text on Thursday after its second reading, with no debate and no votes against.

Greenland's own parliament approved the agreement in early November, by 16 votes with seven abstentions.

The pact was signed in New York, United States, on 22 September and adds to a 1951 agreement governing the US military presence in Greenland, an autonomous territory of Denmark.

It also shuts countries outside the European Union and NATO out of strategic sectors. The aim is to stop China or Russia from building influence in Greenland, especially through investment.

Since returning to the White House in January 2025, Trump has repeatedly said the United States must control Greenland for strategic reasons. His remarks have alarmed Denmark, a NATO ally, and other partners.
