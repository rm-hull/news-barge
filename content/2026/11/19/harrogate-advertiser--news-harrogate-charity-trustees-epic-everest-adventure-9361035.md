A children’s cancer charity with a special history in Harrogate has paid tribute to one of its trustees after his remarkable fundraising efforts at the world’s highest mountain.

Set up originally in 1976 by parents and medical staff at Seacroft Hospital in Leeds, Harrogate Candlelighters has became one of the charity’s longstanding groups, raising more than £1 million to support its work over the yeas with children and families affected by childhood cancer.

As well as helping to run the charity, its trustees often contribute through their own fundraising and awareness challenges.

This year has seen one of the longest-serving medical trustees, Mike Richards, Candlelighters Vice Chair, take on a number of activities in aid of Candlelighters, sometimes going to extraordinary lengths.

In October, he travelled all the way to the Himalayas to trek to Everest Base Camp, accompanied by a teddy bear version of the charity’s elephant mascot, Champ.

At an altitude of 5,364 metres or 17,598 feet, Everest Base Camp sits at the foot of the world’s highest mountain.

Although the journey to Base Camp is a trek rather than a technical climb, it is a demanding high-altitude challenge, with the standard route covering around 130 km and typically involving five to seven hours of walking a day over rugged terrain.

Mike Richards said: “The Everest trek was a moving experience, I realised just how amazing the world is and also appreciated how hard life is for young people in less well-developed countries.

“Unsurprisingly, Champ was a source of conversation amongst many walkers.

“I was so pleased he waited for me when we arrived at Basecamp!”

Paying tribute to his efforts, Emily Wragg, Chief Executive of Candlelighters, said: “Mike’s adventure is a brilliant example of the commitment Candlelighters’ Trustees bring to the charity through their support both within and outside their formal roles.

“Trustees have been at the heart of Candlelighters throughout our 50-year history, and we simply wouldn’t be the charity we are today without people volunteering their time, skills and experience to help guide us.”

More than 150 children are diagnosed with cancer every year in Yorkshire.

Candlelighters support children, young people and their families following a childhood cancer diagnosis.

Current trustee Sue Patterson previously ran the Harrogate Candlelighters fundraising group before becoming a Trustee at Candlelighters.
