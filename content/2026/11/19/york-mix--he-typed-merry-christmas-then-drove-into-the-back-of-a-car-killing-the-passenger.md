A North Yorkshire driver who was texting moments before causing a fatal road crash on Christmas Day has been jailed.

Joshua Stephen Jones was driving north on the A1(M) at 8.30pm on Christmas Day in 2024.

He failed to spot that traffic had come to a standstill due to another accident around junction 57 near Darlington.

Jones was driving home having spent Christmas Day with his family.

Just before setting off, he read a text message from someone who had travelled the route he was about to take. The message warned him to be careful as there’d been a “smash” on the motorway.

As Jones approached the area of the collision, he had his car’s cruise control set to 75mph. He did not brake, or appear to react in any way to the traffic jam ahead, and his car crashed into the rear of the Fiesta.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/man-texting-while-driving-phone-car-dreamstime.webp)

It was occupied by a married couple in their eighties. The woman passenger was killed. Her husband, who was driving, suffered serious injuries which required a hospital stay and a long recovery.

Police examination of Jones’ phone showed that he had been using it while driving.

The device was unlocked five times in the ten minutes before the collision. Apps used included Instagram, WhatsApp and Signal.

In the minute before the collision, multiple key-presses were recorded on the phone’s keyboard. The Signal app contained a partly-typed, unsent “Merry Christmas” message.

North Yorkshire Police’s Forensic Collision Investigation Unit concluded that the collision was most likely due to Jones being distracted by his phone.

## ‘A living nightmare’

Jones, 30, from Catterick Garrison, was charged with causing death by dangerous driving, and causing serious injury by dangerous driving.

He pleaded guilty at court on 24 September 2026.

At Teesside Crown Court yesterday (Wednesday), he was jailed for nine years, and given a driving ban of ten years.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/teesside-crown-court-middlesbrough-wk.webp)

In a personal statement, the daughter of the woman who died told the court: “Our hearts are totally broken. We have been subjected to a living nightmare that we will never wake up from.

“And all because of one person’s selfish and reckless actions.

“We have lost a loving wife, mam, grandma and great grandma, who was the centre of our world, as we were hers.

“Our entire lives and Christmas-times will never be the same.”

Investigating officer DC Stephanie Palmer, of North Yorkshire Police, said: “This tragic case serves as a stark reminder of the potentially devastating consequences of using a phone while driving.

“Even a momentary distraction can have life-changing results, reducing a driver’s ability to react to hazards, and putting everyone on the road at risk.

“No phone call, text, or notification is worth risking a life for. We urge drivers to remain focused on the road at all times, and make safety their top priority.”
