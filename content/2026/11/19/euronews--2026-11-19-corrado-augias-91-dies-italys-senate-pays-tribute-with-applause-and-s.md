## Journalist Corrado Augias has died at the age of 91, after decades as a leading figure in Italian journalism.

Corrado Augias, a historic voice of Italian journalism as well as a writer, populariser, author and television host, died on Thursday at the age of 91. The news was reported by the daily newspaper *Repubblica*, with which Augias had been a contributor since its foundation.

"With the passing of Corrado Augias, Italian journalism loses an essential point of reference. Journalist, essayist, playwright, presenter, Augias **was an intellectual capable of deciphering, in every era, the changes in Italian society**, which he was able to narrate with authority," the Usigrai executive said in a statement.

## The career of Corrado Augias, from newspapers to television

Born in Rome on 26 January 1935 to parents of French origin, Augias was admitted to the order of journalists on 1 April 1969. Over sixty years of intense activity, in addition to his long-standing collaboration with *Repubblica*as correspondent and columnist,**from 2001 to 2021 he was in charge of the letters column, featuring correspondence sent in by readers.**

Not only a print journalist, Augias also became well known as a television presenter, with a style marked by elegant, courteous yet incisive manners and a touch of irony, able to engage with the most diverse guests. His fame on the small screen came with the programme he devised for Rai, *Telefono Giallo*, dedicated to famous unresolved crime cases, which he presented from 1987 to 1993.

\*\* In recent years Augias worked mainly with the broadcaster La7, on the programme*La Torre di Babele.*\*\* In addition to his journalistic work, he successfully turned to writing crime novels and essays.

## Applause and a minute's silence in the Senate to remember Corrado Augias

After hearing the news of his death, announced in the Senate during the debate on the school decree by Italia Viva group leader Raffaella Paita, **senators from both the majority and the opposition joined in prolonged applause.**

"We have just learned of the death of Corrado Augias and I would like this chamber to pay him a tribute with applause," Paita said. The President of the Senate, Ignazio La Russa, also joined in expressing condolences, asking for a few minutes of silence.

The Mayor of Rome, Roberto Gualtieri, also paid tribute to the intellectual before the sitting of the city council. "Corrado Augias was **a major figure in culture and journalism, a committed intellectual and a citizen of Rome** to whom we owe a great deal. I want to express all my personal grief, that of the administration and, I believe I can say, of the entire city. I extend my deepest condolences to his family, friends and relatives," Gualtieri said.

The director of La7, Andrea Salerno, also expressed his condolences on Augias's death and his closeness to the family.
