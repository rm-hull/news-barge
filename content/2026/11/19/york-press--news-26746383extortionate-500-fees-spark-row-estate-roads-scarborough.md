## ‘Extortionate’ £500 fees spark fresh row over estate roads

Cllr Tom Seston. Courtesy TS, Reform.,

RESIDENTS on several Scarborough housing estates remain responsible for roads that should already have been completed and adopted by the local authority, council bosses have acknowledged.

Eastfield councillor Tom Seston has challenged North Yorkshire Council over delays affecting developments in the Middle Deepdale area, where homeowners are paying annual estate management charges on top of council tax.

Cllr Seston said residents on new-build estates were being forced to pay what he described as “extortionate” maintenance fees of between £400 and £500 a year while continuing to wait for roads to be adopted.

He told the full meeting of the authority on Wednesday, November 18: “In Middle Deepdale, and a number of new housing developments around the Scarborough area, residents are forced into paying extortionate maintenance fees of £400-£500 a year, in addition to council tax.

“Many roads in Middle Deepdale aren’t adopted, but have a Section 38 agreement in place that entails a deadline for when the road should be completed. Many of these deadlines have long since passed.

“Will the council be enforcing these agreements and release people from these charges?”

Section 38 agreements are legal arrangements between developers and highway authorities that set out how roads on new housing developments will be brought up to the required standard before being adopted and maintained at public expense.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21741190.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Coun Malcolm Taylor.

Responding, the council’s executive member for highways and transportation, Cllr Malcolm Taylor, said responsibility for roads remained with developers until the conditions of the agreements had been fulfilled.

He said: “Section 38 agreement provides the terms by which a council will adopt a road and any required highway features following third-party development.

“Whilst the site is under agreement, it remains the sole responsibility of the developer until the terms of the agreement are fulfilled.”

The senior councillor acknowledged that the issue had become increasingly common as more housing developments had been occupied before roads were formally adopted.

“In recent years we have seen a significant increase in housing development sites across the county becoming occupied prior to adoption,” he said.

“Experience shows that once a site has been sold and occupied, the incentive for developers to undertake any remedial work or accelerate delivery of the works required for highway adoption is reduced.”

Cllr Taylor said the authority continued to work with developers to achieve adoption and could facilitate remedial works through its highways service where required.

However, he suggested the council was reluctant to use financial bonds lodged by developers except as a last resort.

“The calling in of bonds presents a much higher risk to the public purse when bonds are in place, and hence we only pursue this course of action when there is no possibility of the adoption works being completed by the developer,” he said.

The comments come months after Cllr Seston described the issue as a “scandal” and called for national legislation to tackle the growing use of management charges on unadopted estates.

Speaking earlier this year, he said some Eastfield residents had seen annual fees more than double while waiting years longer than expected for roads to be adopted.

At this week’s meeting, Cllr Taylor offered for council officers to meet with Cllr Seston to discuss the progress of individual developments in his ward.
