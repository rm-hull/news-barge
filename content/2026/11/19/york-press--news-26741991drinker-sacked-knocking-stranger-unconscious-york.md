A drinker who ran up to a stranger from behind and knocked him unconscious in city centre violence has been sacked, York magistrates heard.

Bailey Simpson has now also got a criminal record and must pay compensation to the victim.

Antony Farrell, prosecuting, said the other man told police his midnight encounter with Simpson left him with scars as a lasting reminder.

He is now afraid to go out socialising and faces expensive dental work on three front teeth.

“The defendant violated my sense of safety and caused me and my family a tremendous amount of anxiety,” he said.

Defence solicitor Kevin Blount said Simpson, 30, had been sacked from his job as a maintenance worker by his care home employers when he was charged with the assault.

Mr Blount alleged the victim and Simpson had walked towards each other on January 17 and the victim “has basically hit him (Simpson) to the side of the head as he has gone past".

The two had exchanged words and the victim had walked off.

Simpson had decided the matter was not over, ran after the other man and “jumped onto his back and knocked him to the ground", the court heard.

He very much regretted his actions which were “entirely out of character,” said Mr Blount.

Simpson, of Spalding Avenue, Clifton, pleaded guilty to actual bodily harm, his first conviction.

York magistrates told him: “He (the victim) was unconscious for a while. You must have landed on him with quite some force.”

They gave him a 12-month community order with 80 hours’ unpaid work and a probation requirement to work on his behaviour, and ordered him to pay the victim £500 compensation.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/20815341.jpg?type=mds-article-620)A CCTV camera in operation *(Image: North Yorkshire Council)*

Mr Farrell said council CCTV operators spotted the incident - just before midnight on Saturday January 17 near High Ousegate - on their screens.

Simpson ran off towards Stonegate leaving the victim on the ground.

But the operators contacted police and told them what had happened.

Officers caught up with Simpson and arrested him.

Mr Blount read out a letter of apology written by Simpson to the victim in which he said “I genuinely regretted what happened” and “I am truly sorry for any harm or upset I have caused you".

A probation officer told the court Simpson had told her he had “stopped drinking alcohol completely".

She assessed he had issues with controlling his temper and impulsiveness.
