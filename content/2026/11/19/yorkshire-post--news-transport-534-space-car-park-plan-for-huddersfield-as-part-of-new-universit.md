# 534-space car park plan for Huddersfield as part of new university campus

## A new 534-space multi-storey car park is planned for Huddersfield as part of the National Health Innovation Campus (NHIC).

The NHIC is the University of Huddersfield’s vision to improve health outcomes and lead innovation in healthcare for the North of England, the UK and internationally. The university has planning permission for up to seven buildings on the seven acre NHIC site which sits off Southgate, some of which have already come to fruition.

The latest planning application is for a multi-storey car park which would sit on a plot of land adjacent to Crown House. The car park would be split across eight levels and accommodate around 534 spaces. It would feature standard, accessible, Electric Vehicle and motorcycle spaces, as well as “significant” cycle parking. It is also proposed to come with a bin store and service yard.

The primary access point for vehicles would be off Old Leeds Road, with a “clearly-defined” first-floor pedestrian entrance connecting directly to the campus’s wider public realm also part of the plan. A target date for Kirklees Council to make a decision has been set for January 21, 2027.

The campus’ first building, named after Daphne Steele – the UK’s first black matron – opened in 2024 and is home to the university’s School of Human and Health Sciences and some public-facing facilities. The second building – the Emily Siddon – opened in January. It hosts several University courses, a Community Diagnostic Centre (CDC) operated by the NHS, as well as education and training provision.

The third building will be a “Neighbourhood Health Hub” designed to strengthen neighbourhood‑level, preventative and community‑based care. Out-of-hours GP care and a community pharmacy are among the services that will be on offer, according to the University’s website.
