Britain is set to allow 100,000 young European citizens to come to the UK every year as part of a repurposed deal being finalised with the EU, it’s been reported.

Talks between UK and EU negotiators were “going down to the wire”, No 10 said hours before Friday’s summit, at which Andy Burnham hopes to agree closer ties with Brussels.

The UK has provisionally agreed to potentially double the number of EU youngsters coming in under a youth mobility scheme, according to *The Guardian*.

The cap on numbers will rise from the 50,000-80,000 at present up to 100,000. It was also agreed to raise the age limit from 30 to 31.

The number of young British people allowed to go to the EU will be at least matched but could some countries, including Germany, could take unlimited numbers each year.

![Numbers of Europeans arriving each year could potentially be doubled](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/09/09/11/01M22TXX26RTFJ1D0YFA7ZKG18.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Both sides were on Thursday still thrashing out details of the UK’s role in shaping future European food and farming rules.

The prime minister wants a closer relationship with the European Union but has ruled out a return to the single market, customs union or freedom of movement.

His press secretary said Brexit had done more harm than good.

Mr Burnham is due within hours to sign new agreements on food and energy as well as youth mobility.

Sources said the UK was still resisting pressure from the EU on tuition fees for European students coming to Britain under the youth mobility scheme.

The prime minister’s press secretary said: “The talks with the EU are going down to the wire.

![Andy Burnham met European Commission chief Ursula von der Leyen in Dublin last week (Stefan Rousseau/PA)](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/19/20/f8944539c50e1988d09feb5b4cd3d8c6Y29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzk1MjA2MDM2-2.86468843.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“If we secure a deal, the government will bring forward legislation to Parliament next week.

“There is a consensus in the country that change needs to happen.”

He continued: “Brexit has done more harm than good.

“The cost of living has risen, it’s harder for British people to travel and businesses to trade, and the small boats crisis has reached our shores.

“We ended up with a harder Brexit than was promised at the referendum. The hard Brexit that was negotiated has impacted people’s everyday lives, longer queues at the airport and young people missing out on the chance to live, work or study abroad.”

He said Russia posed a much more direct threat to European security.

It’s feared the agreement will prevent the UK from banning imports of foie gras – cruelly produced geese and duck livers – a move that, before the 2024 general election, Labour had pledged to do.

Abigail Penny, executive director of Animal Equality UK, said: “Force-feeding is a crime in the UK, yet we continue to allow foie gras to reach our shores.”

The charity’s hopes of a ban have been repeatedly dashed by successive governments. Campaigners had said any ban would be a Brexit benefit.

![Conservative Party leader Kemi Badenoch](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/19/06/d21ef883a2e20321d0583ca60b3745deY29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzk1MTU0Mjc5-2.86442971.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Tory leader Kemi Badenoch said Labour’s talks with Brussels would take the UK backwards on control of money, laws and borders.

Mr Burnham has raised hopes that Friday’s summit could mean “a really big win potentially” on a “Channel action plan” to tackle small boat crossings.

Asked whether Britain could still walk away from a deal, Mr Burnham said: “There would always be those circumstances because I’m in this to get the best deal for Britain, always.

“But what’s in it for us? There’s the possibility of lower food prices, and I can’t turn my back on that. Lower costs for business, cutting red tape.

“And a really big win potentially – more collaboration on immigration, a Channel action plan, which is a really big deal if we can land that tomorrow.”

He said recent polling showed 73 per cent of Britons favoured closer ties with Europe.

Asked about foie gras, a government spokesperson told *The Independent*: “We are negotiating a food and drink deal that will slash red tape and costly paperwork, make it easier and cheaper to export seafood, boost the British economy by up to £5 billion a year, and back British businesses and jobs.

“We are working closely with businesses to ensure a smooth transition so they can make the most of the benefits this deal unlocks.”
