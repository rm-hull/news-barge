## A Spanish ministry has stopped accepting new applications to recognise foreign university degrees, after years of increases.

A Spanish ministry has stopped accepting new applications to recognise foreign university degrees, amid a controversy over the surge in foreign doctors sitting the country’s medical residency exam.

For the first time, foreign applicants outnumbered Spanish applicants for the national medical residency placement exam, known as the MIR, as highlighted by the Asociación MIR España (AME). Recognitions of foreign medical degrees had been rising for years, though Spaniards still make up the majority of admitted candidates for the exam.

The health ministry reserves a maximum quota of 10% for foreign candidates, meaning they should compete among themselves for just 968 of the positions offered overall. That makes both their chances of admission and their choice of speciality more difficult.

However, this is not how it works in practice. Many foreign doctors apply through the ordinary route thanks to their residence permits. The rules governing the call allow EU citizens and anyone with permanent or temporary residence to apply via the general pool. Only those in Spain on study permits, or who don’t fall into those categories, are left out.

A day after the figures were released, ABC reported that the Ministry of Science, Innovation and Universities, headed by Diana Morant, had blocked new applications to recognise foreign degrees, which is required to sit the exam. Recognitions have been climbing for the past decade, which is at the heart of the controversy.

The ministry said the shutdown of the online portal used for applications is due to a series of attempted cyberattacks on public websites around the world last month. The portal has since partly reopened, but still isn’t accepting new recognition applications.

## Potential consequences

In 2025, there were 85,564 applications for recognition and equivalence of foreign university degrees processed, according to the ministry. That is more than twice the figure for 2024 (39,975) and eight times that of 2017 (10,224).

Only 49,906 of last year's cases were recognitions, which is the only category under which a foreign doctor can sit the exam.

The president of the Organización Médica Colegial (OMC) told RTVE that the situation also affects private health care. He warned that private companies could be tempted to hire doctors who have recognised degrees but no specialty training , on lower salaries and with more precarious conditions.

The next round of the competitive exam for health care posts is expected to take place on 23 January.
