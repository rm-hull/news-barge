**Parents are calling for mandatory CCTV in nurseries after a worker who looked after their children was later exposed as a paedophile.**

Nathan Bennett was jailed in February for multiple counts of sexual abuse, including two counts of rape, against five boys at Partou King Street nursery in Bristol. His crimes were discovered after a CCTV review.

Bennett previously worked as an apprentice for 18 months at another nursery in the Somerset area - which the BBC is not naming – which does not have CCTV.

A number of parents whose children went there said they lived in fear about what could have happened to them at the nursery. The government said a panel was examining the use of CCTV in nurseries.

Bennett's victims at Partou King Street nursery were two and three years old at the time of his offences. In February 2025, concerns were raised over his behaviour with the children, specifically a group of five who the prosecution said he would "favour".

He was suspended from the nursery after CCTV of him putting his hands down a boy's trousers was reviewed.

There is no evidence he committed any offences while he worked at the Somerset nursery, or that there were safeguarding failures there. Avon and Somerset Police said it had not identified grounds to warrant a criminal investigation at the site.

Det Insp Lucy Forde, who led the investigation into Bennett's crimes, previously told the BBC she feared only a fraction of his crimes could be proven.

"He's worked in childcare and has had access to children for a number of years," she said.

"My worry is there will be other victims that we haven't been able to identify, that are too young to understand what's happened to them," Forde added.

After learning of Bennett's offending, a number of parents whose children went to the Somerset nursery shared their worries about what could have taken place. The BBC has changed their names to protect their children's identities.

Rachel, whose children attended the nursery, said she became suspicious of Bennett after witnessing him being overly tactile with her child in her opinion.

## 'I could hear him crying'

The BBC has seen messages from the time exchanged between Rachel and her partner, in which concerns about Bennett's behaviour were discussed, describing him as "weird".

They discussed raising concerns about him and eventually complained to management about their son being repeatedly left in soiled nappies and being sore.

Rachel said they eventually asked for their child to be moved away from Bennett's care.

"My son was always on Nathan's lap - there would be times where I would see him stroking him on his arm, kissing him on the forehead or cheek," she said.

"There was something inside me that knew straight away that's not right," she added.

Rachel said she saw Bennett taking her child into the nappy changing area alone with the door closed on one occasion.

"I came to pick up my son and I could hear him crying - the changing room door was closed so I waited for a while.

"Nathan came out with him, alone, and said 'I'm not sure what's up with him, he never seems to like his nappy being changed','" she said.

Rachel said healthcare professionals had since referred her child for an assessment by specialists who deal with cases of sexual assault.

James, whose children attended the nursery at the same time, said he witnessed Bennett being "too physical" with children.

"Most times I went to pick up my children he was holding a child on his lap or making physical contact with a child," he said.

"More often than not, the child looked like they were trying to get away, but he was holding them there. I didn't see other nursery staff with children on their lap," James said.

He said he verbally complained to nursery managers about Bennett.

"I told them I had observed Nathan being physical with children – in my opinion too physical.

"Their response was 'isn't it great how cuddly he is?'," he said.

Why parents want mandatory CCTV in nurseries

Bessie Martin, a former colleague of Bennett's at Partou King Street nursery in Bristol, previously told the BBC she had also witnessed him holding children on his lap.

"I would see a child try to wriggle away or stand up and walk off, but he'd say 'sit here and we're going to read a book'," she said.

She became a whistleblower and reported her concerns about Bennett to senior management at Partou.

During his trial it emerged parents had raised concerns to the nursery in the six months before he was caught.

James said news of Bennett's conviction led him to worry about the worker's behaviour when he was an apprentice.

"I was concerned that he didn't become a predatory paedophile when he moved to the nursery in Bristol – I believe he was always a predatory paedophile," he said.

James said CCTV in nurseries should be a "minimum requirement".

"If I was to go through choosing a nursery again, it's something I'd need to have in place," he said.

Another parent, Helen, said she wanted to see mandatory, actively monitored CCTV and stronger safeguards, including higher qualification requirements for staff and strict enforcement of two-person working.

"Mandatory CCTV should not be a problem – especially in places with extremely vulnerable people, where it's most needed," she said.

"Something positive needs to come out of such a horrendous thing," she added.

Helen said the uncertainty of not knowing if anything could have happened had led her to "read into everything".

"Any comments [from my child] about nursery now, you don't know how to interpret them.

"My child will sometimes start a conversation saying, 'mummy, I didn't tell you this thing that happened at nursery' and I start shaking every time she says it," she added.

## 'Not a silver bullet'

Katie and John Meehan, whose daughter Genevieve was killed at a nursery in Stockport in 2022, have also been campaigning for mandatory CCTV in nurseries.

CCTV footage showed nursery worker Kate Roughley tightly swaddling nine-month-old Genevieve, strapping her to a beanbag and leaving her unattended. She died of asphyxiation.

John Meehan said that - while CCTV was not a "silver bullet" - use of it could "identify unsafe practices" or provide evidence for the regulator and police when harm occurred.

"For parents entrusting their precious and vulnerable children to a nursery, the confidence and reassurance these safeguards can provide are vital," he said.

The Department for Education said it had strengthened recruitment and safeguarding requirements at nurseries.

It said this included full criminal record checks for staff working closely with children, checks on references before a hire, new safeguarding training and clear processes for staff to raise concerns.

Allegations or concerns that a staff member has harmed a child or is unsuitable to work with children should be reported to the council's Local Authority Designated Officer (LADO).

The nursery where Bennett was an apprentice said one concern was raised following Bennett's conviction, which the LADO confirmed did not meet the threshold for investigation.

The nursery was rated Outstanding by Ofsted at the time of Bennett's employment. The regulator told the BBC there had been no concerns raised during Bennett's employment at the nursery.

In an email sent to parents following Bennett's conviction, the nursery said Bennett was employed as an apprentice and subject to a high level of supervision.

A man who worked at the nursery at the same time as Bennett said he had not noticed any suspicious behaviour but was concerned staff could sometimes be left to work alone, even if they had no previous childcare experience.

"I had never worked in early years or childcare before," he said.

"The chances of being alone when changing a nappy were quite high.

"The door wasn't always open when [Bennett] was changing nappies," he added.

## 'Horrendously overwhelming'

A member of staff who worked at the nursery after Bennett had left, said she had often felt overwhelmed with the number of children she was given to look after.

While she said whistleblowing policies and safeguarding procedures were clearly explained at the nursery and people were encouraged to report anything that did not sit right, she added that complaints she raised about having too many children were not addressed, and she eventually quit her job.

"It was horrendously overwhelming and eventually I couldn't do it anymore," she said.

Rachel said the impact of knowing her child went to a nursery where Bennett was working would be "lifelong".

"The moment I found out he had been convicted confirmed how I had felt that year about Nathan Bennett," she said.

And although she cannot do anything about the lack of CCTV at the Somerset nursery, she said mandatory video monitoring could deter other potential offenders - or provide proof for other families after a crime had happened.

"It didn't deter Nathan Bennett, but without it he would be free to continue committing these crimes," she said.
