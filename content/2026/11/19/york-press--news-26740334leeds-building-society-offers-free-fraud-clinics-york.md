A building society in York is offering free fraud clinics to help residents protect themselves from scams.

Leeds Building Society’s branch, located in York Road, is hosting the sessions as part of an ongoing community programme, giving people the chance to speak face to face with staff and receive practical advice on spotting and avoiding fraud.

The clinics are open to everyone, not just customers of the building society, and cover common types of scams including phishing, impersonation, and online banking fraud.

Stephen Senior, head of financial crime prevention at Leeds Building Society, said: "Fraud can affect anyone, which is why awareness and education are so important.

"Our colleagues in York are there to help local people understand how scams work, recognise the warning signs and know what steps they can take to protect themselves."

The building society has pledged to keep all 51 of its branches open until at least September 2031, emphasising the value of trusted, face-to-face support.

Graham Binns, head of branch network, said: "Branches are about much more than transactions.

"They give people somewhere local where they can sit down and talk to a real person, particularly when they’re worried or unsure about something."

The importance of these clinics was highlighted by a recent incident in which a customer was prevented from losing £10,000.

During a routine visit to withdraw £100, staff identified an unusual £10,000 payment request from the previous day on an account that had seen little activity for more than a decade.

Staff investigated further, invoked the Banking Protocol, and successfully cancelled the transaction, returning the money to the customer’s account.

The fraud clinics offer practical advice to help people recognise suspicious activity and take action if they believe they are being targeted.

Anyone interested in attending a session can visit the York branch or speak to a member of staff.
