Denmark’s parliament has approved an agreement involving Greenland and the United States to strengthen security in the Arctic and North Atlantic.

The vote follows months of pressure by U.S. President Donald Trump seeking to control Greenland, a semiautonomous territory of Denmark, a NATO ally.

The trilateral deal — between Trump and the Danish and Greenlandic leaders — paves the way for more U.S. bases in Greenland and eases tensions over Trump’s repeated calls for the vast island to become part of the U.S.

The 109-0 vote in the Folketing amounts to a final step: The deal was expected to enter into force once Denmark and Greenland had signed on.

Greenland’s parliament approved the deal earlier this month, also with no opposition.

The aim is to bolster the U.S. military presence on Greenland but keep the self-governing island under Danish control. It would still apply if Greenland eventually chooses independence.
