## GEEKOM IT13: 30-second review

My initial impressions of the Geekom IT13 2026 Edition were mixed, shaped mainly by preconceived ideas about these small machines over the years. Essentially, the IT13 2026 Edition has a smaller-than-average form factor, and its casing is finished in a dull blue; these traits are usually apparent on machines at the cheaper end of the market. However, at just over $1,000 / £1,000 at the time of this review, this mini PC is priced at least at the mid-to-upper end.

Taking the small box out, the metal rather than plastic casing gives a good measure of quality, and a closer look at the box shows a decent selection of ports, including a 2.5GbE LAN port, two USB4 ports, HDMI, and an SD card slot on the side.

While the ports on the back are close together, the layout is well thought through, making it easy to connect accessories. One point on the port layout is that there are two USB Type-A ports on the front. While these can be useful, most PC accessories I have looked at in the last couple of years have been USB-C, so having at least one of these newer ports would have been handy.

Powering up, and reflecting the machine's business focus, the final steps of the Windows 11 Pro setup need to be completed. This takes around five minutes, not including updates.

Initial impressions from the software and benchmarks suggest that, while nothing in the specifications really stands out as particularly interesting, performance throughout is solid. Microsoft Office apps run as you would hope, and Windows 11 Pro handles them with ease.

Switching up the demands on the system and running through the Adobe Creative Suite shows that both Lightroom and Photoshop run with surprising ease, and it’s only when Adobe Bridge starts rendering thumbnails that the limits of the internal RAM cause a slowdown.

Video editing with 4K footage from a Canon EOS R50 V, I was impressed with how well it handled with plenty of speed as I scrubbed through the timeline with few slowdowns. Switching to 4K video shot on the Sony A7 IV in S-Log and starting to apply grading highlighted the limits.

At each point throughout the test, the small machine impressed in every type of use, showing that this machine has been designed for general performance, and for this it ranks among the best mini PCs I've tested. ## GEEKOM IT13: Design

The first thing to note about the IT13 is its size: at 117 × 112 × 49.2mm and 652g, it’s one of the smallest mini PCs I’ve looked at in a while. A couple of years ago, we started to see quite a few of these compact form-factor options, but most had cooling issues and the potential for thermal throttling; more recent models have improved cooling and size. The latest processors also draw considerably less power than their predecessors.

The Geekom IT13 is a welcome return to the compact mini PCs we were seeing a couple of years ago, but now with the far more energy-efficient Intel Core Ultra 7 356H, which is a hybrid CPU that works far more efficiently and thus cooler than many older processors.

While the box is small, Geekom is pitching this as a general mini PC equally suited to the office, studio, or home, with power that meets most people’s needs.

Sure enough, the performance impressed throughout the test, and while there are limitations, the assortment of ports and upgrade options certainly appealed.

Starting on the exterior, the front has two USB 3.2 Gen 2 Type-A ports alongside a 3.5mm headphone jack and the power button. On the side is an SD card reader slot, and around the back are another USB 3.2 Gen 2 port and a USB 2.0 port (both Type-A), plus two USB4 ports, a 2.5GbE LAN port, two HDMI ports, and the DC input.

![GEEKOM IT13](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/RCGT7YrfYKBSXrWieGXzUR-1200-80.jpg)

Unlike many of the new mini PCs I’ve looked at recently, this one offers plenty of upgrade potential. Access to the insides is gained by unscrewing the four bolts in the base, then prying the plastic top cover off and removing a further four screws.

As the case is removed, the two antenna wires need to be carefully unhooked from the casing. As these wires are incredibly delicate, you need to take this step slowly to avoid damage.

Inside the machine, it’s packed with 32GB of dual-channel DDR4 RAM, all neatly laid out with the 1TB M.2 2280 PCIe Gen 4 ×4 SSD, which can be upgraded to 2TB. Checking closely, there’s the smaller slot for the M.2 2242 SATA SSD, and this can be up to 1TB. Then, if you need to boost the internal storage further, there’s space for a 2.5-inch SATA HDD.

## GEEKOM IT13: Features

The power of mini PCs has leapt forward this year, especially with the advent of NPUs and the greater resource demands created by AI features in mainstream apps. The boost in AI use has created resource constraints for many older machines and their CPUs.

Older PCs took a blunt-instrument approach, and as long as they had enough resources, these small mini PCs could achieve a surprising amount; hybrid cores have been around since 2020 in the Lakefield processors, and have been steadily improving performance since.

The Intel Core Ultra 7 356H is based on Intel’s Panther Lake architecture and features 16 cores and 16 threads. These comprise four Performance-cores, eight Efficient-cores and four Low Power Efficient-cores. This hybrid architecture lets the processor distribute workloads based on their performance and requirements.

The processor also includes an Intel NPU, and this is rated at up to 50 TOPS. The NPU is only called on by apps that have been specifically optimised for use, and when used, it frees up resources from the CPU or GPU. The NPU will also support compatible local language models alongside Windows AI features; however, at present cloud-based AI processing is still the most common resource for integrated AI.

Essentially, while the overall specifications of the newer Panther Lake processors on paper may seem less impressive than those of older CPUs, resource allocation is far better.

The integrated GPU is Intel Xe3 Graphics for U7-356H, which offers good all-round performance, though it does draw on RAM alongside the CPU. However, for most creative applications, including those from the Adobe Suite, the power available is more than sufficient for most uses.

While many mini PCs use soldered LPDDR5X RAM, Geekom has opted for standard DDR4, letting you expand the 32GB of RAM that comes with the machine up to 64GB. This is ideal for creative applications and gaming, while 32GB will more than suffice for most office-based work.

Unusually, there are three storage slots, and the machine ships with a 1TB M.2 NVMe Gen 4 SSD. Alongside this, there’s room for a compact M.2 SATA SSD and a 2.5-inch SATA SSD, which are crammed into the small body, boosting the storage from the 1TB that ships with the machine all the way up to a very respectable 5TB, although only one of those will be the ultra-fast M.2 NVMe.

One surprise in the feature list was that it has two HDMI 2.0 ports and two USB4 ports, supporting up to four 4K monitors or one 8K display. This is ideal if you have large amounts of data you need to see on screen, and four 4K monitors are perfect for creative applications, although you’re normally limited to two or three.

Obviously, this is an incredibly small machine, which in the past has created heat distribution issues. However, to manage the heat, the IceBlast 2.0 silent cooling system has been integrated, which uses copper heat pipes to pull heat away from the electronics and push it out through vents dotted around the body.

One of the more surprising features is the reinforced metal internal frame, which Geekom claims can withstand up to 440lb (approximately 200kg) of pressure.

The network connectivity is good for an office environment with a single 2.5GbE Ethernet port and Wi-Fi 6E.

One big point about this machine that I really like is that GEEKOM gives you flexibility. So while you have a very powerful CPU, GPU, and NPU combination that can’t be upgraded, the RAM and storage offer far greater flexibility. With the USB4 ports at the back, you can connect to eGPUs and ultra-fast storage if needed.

As a final point on the feature set, this nice, small machine will already save you plenty of desk space, and it also includes a VESA mount. So if you want to mount it on the back of your monitor, that’s possible; and if you’re mounting it in a vehicle or on a rack or rig, the small size makes it nice and easy.

* **Features:** 4.5 / 5

Alongside standard mini PC uses for productivity, creative work, and browsing the internet, the IT13 features an integrated NPU that can boost local AI workloads with compatible apps, although at present many applications rely heavily on cloud-based AI processing. However, with the NPU present, I was keen to check out the AI performance alongside the more usual tasks.

As ever, I started the benchmarks by testing SSD speed, and it delivered impressive results, with transfer rates up to 6,067MB/s. It’s a reasonably fast PCIe 4.0 SSD, although the fastest drives using this interface can exceed 7,000MB/s. The speed of the SSD was highlighted throughout the test, especially as I started with video and photography applications where data was accessed quickly from the local drive to enable fast renders and exports.

As I ran through the Microsoft Office applications, I noted fast loading and use. I launched a large image-heavy PowerPoint presentation alongside Adobe Photoshop and a few browser windows. I noted that as enhancements were made, the amount of RAM I was using in the task monitor was getting close to the upper limit, and whilst the machine still ran well, I could start to feel a slight slowdown when switching from one application to another. Most notably, this was within Adobe Bridge, where thumbnail images started to render slowly.

The upshot is that for most Microsoft Office applications, the computer with 32GB of standard RAM and a 1TB SSD is more than sufficient for the vast majority of people. Even if you’re into video editing or photography, it has more than enough power, not just for lightweight apps but to fully run Adobe Photoshop and Lightroom. Where it will start to slow is when you just leave all applications open with large video and image projects.

When I opened Premiere Pro, an application known for pushing the limits of many mini PCs, I was impressed by how quickly it handled the data. However, the 1TB SSD is rather limited due to the size of the 4K footage that I’d captured on the Canon EOS R50 V. Plugging an external SSD into the USB4 port, whilst slower than the speed of the internal SSD, was still more than enough to provide data and video footage to Premiere Pro. Again, editing standard 4K footage, even with that limit of 32GB of RAM, was exceptionally good.

As I pushed the machine and quickly loaded 4K S-Log footage shot on the Sony A7 IV, I noticed a slowdown as the colour grading started. A 4K five-minute edit was well within this machine's limits, but as the complexity of the edit and grading increased, the timeline took longer to render, and the editing workflow felt sluggish.

Switching over to Blender, I was surprised by how fluidly it could create complex models quickly. The only real slowdown came when rendering those models, especially after I imported them with full texture mapping, and it was obviously due to the limitations of the internal GPU.

Finally, after getting through a good amount of work, I settled down to a bit of gaming; I loaded up *Cyberpunk 2077*and* Indiana Jones and the Great Circle*. The gameplay was quite different between the two games, with* Indiana Jones* running perfectly well at low settings. Overall, the gameplay was good, and I only saw a little graphical glitching during real action.

Switching from Indy to *Cyberpunk 2077*, I instantly realised that the graphics needed lowering, with resolution and effects dropped, including disabling ray tracing. Again, gameplay was possible, but I noted the frame rate was around 19 or 20 fps, which made it a bit jittery and, for me, not ideal for a relaxing gaming session. However, if needs must, you can still get through.

Finally, I wanted to test out the AI potential by running Lemonade. I ran several lightweight LLMs. I started with Qwen3.8-Distilled-2B-NPU2, a relatively lightweight LLM that I installed directly in Lemonade, though I discovered it wasn’t using the NPU, but was relying on the CPU and NPU.

I then ran a series of tests to explore this model's limitations, since it’s relatively small. The first point was just general conversation. Could it hold a conversation, and how accurate was it? Its actual knowledge was pretty good, though compared with online chat, you can tell its limits.

However, when you tie that chat in with, say, summarising a document, it really comes into its own, and it’s good at giving short summaries for emails and text, although I did have to copy everything into the chat window.

Likewise, giving it some data and asking it to summarise that data as a couple of paragraphs again worked well. It’s very technical and straightforward copy, but sufficient enough for when you do need to write a short extract quickly.

Likewise, I asked it to write a simple Python script to copy content from one folder to another, and again, it worked well. It’s very regimented, and I noticed that when I tried to update the code, it would just add to it, so there’s quite a bit of clean-up needed. However, it’s a starting point, and for a place to experiment, especially with Python, HTML and other basic coding, it’s actually surprisingly proficient, although you do have to copy and paste the code from the chat window into something like Visual Studio.

For anything more complex, like analysing images or even creating them, this is well beyond the limits of this particular model. However, other models can work and give you a basic idea of how these local LLMs can be used.

I then installed OpenVINO and ran a few more tests, this time focusing on a model that utilised the local NPU rather than the CPU and GPU. Here, Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct proved to be the perfect option. This approach did mean that Python needed to be installed, and while the NPU kicked into action, the process isn’t as straightforward as you would hope, requiring some knowledge of LLM use. However, it does show the potential.

By the end of the test, the Geekom IT13 proved to be a powerful mini PC, well beyond what I had imagined, but the price at over £1000 does initially seem expensive. However, a quick comparison of other mini PCs with this level of CPU and memory quickly showed that this cost is actually well balanced.

* **Performance:** 4.5 / 5

## GEEKOM IT13: Final verdict

![GEEKOM IT13](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/Wh88ZEZsxLuBwSTG9KP7NR-1200-80.jpg)

The IT13 is an interesting mini PC. It’s small, compact and offers plenty of advanced connectivity options, including two USB4 ports at the back that enable connections to ultra-fast SSDs as well as eGPUs.

Throughout the test, I couldn’t help feeling that while the price is well balanced given the CPU and the potential for expansion and features, it still feels expensive for what is essentially a very solid workplace machine. But then that is the cost of hardware at present.

There’s no doubt that its Windows performance is exceptional. If you’re looking for a straightforward office machine that won’t slow down, even with the entire Microsoft Office suite open alongside browsers and music, it’s an ideal option. The way the cores work also means that when you need that extra power for handling images, it’s there, and when idle, it consumes far less power.

Using Photoshop, Lightroom, and even Premiere Pro, this small machine handled all of them as long as you’re not pushing it beyond general office-based use. So, for example, in an office environment, you’re more likely to shoot straight 4K without advanced codecs and are also unlikely to go into advanced effects or colour grading; in that case, this machine can handle pretty much any standard 4K edit you want to throw at it.

However, as you get more advanced with video enhancements, you can start to see where the integrated GPU struggles. The struggle is also reflected when gaming, and whilst I was impressed overall by the smoothness of gameplay in Indiana Jones and the Great Circle, with a game like Cyberpunk 2077, you can start to see where it falls.

This new mini PC, then, is a great option for any office looking for a compact machine that can do more than a bog-standard office PC. It can handle all Microsoft Office applications in one go; it can handle Adobe Creative Suite, and what surprised me most was its potential for installing and testing out local LLMs, even if just to give you an idea of how they work.

I do find it a bit overpriced for what it can do, since older machines can run traditional apps just as well. However, you’re paying that added premium for one of the latest CPUs and the AI-potential boost through the integrated NPU.
