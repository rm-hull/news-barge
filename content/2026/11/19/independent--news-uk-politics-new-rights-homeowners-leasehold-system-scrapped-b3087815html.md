The government is hoping to scrap what it has described as a “feudal” leasehold system in the biggest shake-up to the rules in a century.

The leasehold system has been in place for a thousand years with an estimated five million homes across England and Wales affected by it. The government has argued that homeowners currently face rising ground rents, expensive fees for basic requests to freehold owners, costly lease extensions and threats of losing their home over small debts.

Housing minister Matthew Pennycook said that the legislation was historic ahead of the Commonhold and Leasehold Reform Bill’s introduction to parliament on Thursday.

“The leasehold system denies individuals control over the homes they live in, and have paid for, and routinely subjects them to excessive extraction,” he said. “It is blighting lives and it is an anachronism in the 21st century.”

“The publication of our historic Commonhold and Leasehold Reform Bill marks the beginning of the end for the feudal leasehold system.

“We’re laying the foundations for a commonhold future and transforming the experience of homeownership for existing leaseholders by giving them greater rights and protections, and providing them with easy and effective escape routes.”

*Here is how the bill will affect homeowners.*##**Ground rent and admin fees to be capped**

Ground rent will be capped at £250 a year from 2028, as the government aims to cut bills for up to a million leaseholders. It’s expected that this will change to a peppercorn ground rent after 40 years as part of the bill.

Ministers will also have the power to cap excessive permission and administration fees such as asking permission to keep a pet or replace the front door.

## Greater transparency around charges

By April next year, the government will enforce greater transparency for leaseholders around information about what they are paying for as well as how their building is managed, maintained, repaired and insured.

By November next year, private landlords will have to provide standardised bills, annual reports and accounts showing what leaseholders are paying for. Leaseholders will also more transparent information about how administration charges are calculated. In 2028, the government will apply this to social landlords as well.

## **An end to threats to take away people’s homes**

The new bill will take away powers that can put a leaseholder’s home at risk over unpaid bills, such as disputed service charges. Mr Pennycook told the BBC that the government would introduce a “comprehensive new legal framework” and court-led system to tackle the issue.

The bill also looks to protect people living on freehold housing estates from threats to take over their home over unpaid estate maintenance charges.

## **Introducing the commonhold**

As part of the bill, there would be no more leaseholds for new flats and homes. Instead, the government hopes to move from a leasehold system to a commonhold standard where buyers own their home outright forever and run their building jointly from day one.

More routes would be set up to help switch to a commonhold by dropping the threshold for existing blocks to convert from 100 per cent neighbour agreement down to 50 per cent support. This means that non-consenting neighbours could remain on modified leases until they sell, but holdouts could not block conversion for the rest of the building.

## **Making it easier to extend leases and purchase freeholds**

Homeowners will receive help to buy 990-year lease extensions to stop them from having to pay their landlord’s expensive costs in most cases.

The bill will also scrap marriage value, an additional cost faced by leaseholders with 80 years or less remaining on their leases who are trying to extend. This cost represents the increase in a property’s market value that results from the extension.

## **The right to better broadband**

Leaseholders will have better rights to request from their landlords a gigabit broadband connection. While landlords can currently ignore these requests, they will have to respond within set timeframes to calls for fast and reliable broadband.
