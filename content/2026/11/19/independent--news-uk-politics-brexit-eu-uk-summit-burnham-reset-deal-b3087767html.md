Andy Burnham will be told that further discussions about the UK’s long-term relationship with the EU will not proceed until his government has decided whether it wishes to reverse Brexit, it has been claimed.

Diplomats have told *The Guardian* that European nations wish for an end to piecemeal “reset” summits with the UK and want the prime minister to decide his next steps domestically before they go further.

It comes before Friday’s EU summit, where it is anticipated the prime minister will forge closer ties with Europe through new agreements on food, energy and youth mobility.

The prime minister has said the current Brexit deal is not good enough and previously said he hoped the UK would return to the EU in his lifetime.

But member nations said future negotiations will only take place once the UK has a fully fledged strategy set out, according to *The Guardian.*

![Andy Burnham is looking to change the UK’s relationship with Europe](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/18/18/08/burnham-eu-Indycomp.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“Clarity is what we need more than anything from the UK. Don’t come back until you know what you want. Work out what your options really are and then come to negotiate,” one EU diplomat told the newspaper.

“The prime minister needs to bring the public with him. We can’t have another negotiation based on just half the country. We’ll only put the effort in if we know it’s not going to change down the line.”

Mr Burnham promised in his Labour conference speech in September that he would lay out different options to the country around the time of the UK-EU summit.

But, with the Brussels meeting to take place tomorrow, Mr Burnham has appeared to prolong the timeline, saying in a speech on Monday he would start a discussion with the bloc on options after the summit, before presenting options to the public at a later date.

The UK heads into the talks with an approach developed by Keir Starmer, but this format of “mini deals” will quickly “reach its limits”, a source told *The Guardian.*

One said the “piecemeal approach is coming to an end” and another said: “It is like a buffet: you can choose to nibble on one or two dishes to start, but at a certain point you have to go for the whole of the buffet.”

Another EU diplomat said there would be a “pause for reflection” after this week’s summit.

It comes as *The Independent* revealed that a major new consultation has found British businesses overwhelmingly back Britain to rejoin the EU.

In research shared exclusively with *The Independent*, three-quarters of firms said restoring EU membership would have the most positive impact on their business.

Just 10 per cent said they wanted to maintain the current approach of resetting relations with the bloc, which is moving too slowly to meet the scale of the challenges facing UK industry, the report by the UK Trade and Business Commission suggests.

However, the talks with Brussels will take the UK “backwards” on control of Britain’s money, laws and borders, Kemi Badenoch has warned.

The Conservative leader said closer ties with the EU would not provide a significant enough boost to economic growth and would risk Britain’s sovereignty.

Writing in *The Daily Mail*, Ms Badenoch said making trade easier was “a good thing”, but stressed: “There is a world of difference between practical co-operation with our neighbours and giving up the freedoms Britain voted for in 2016.

“The three central purposes of Brexit were control over our money, over our laws and over our borders. On each of those, *Burnham* is moving us backwards.”

Meanwhile, former deputy prime minister David Lammy has said Labour should “campaign” for a return to the EU.

Speaking on ITV’s Peston on Wednesday, he said: “I have no doubt that we should campaign to go back in.

“There are only really two choices: we either do a sort of mini-thing with the EU Customs Union or we go back in.

“The idea of the single market is great but actually we can’t be a rule taker, not a country with a seat on the security council. That’s just not going to happen.

“So on that basis, I think we should be clear with the public that they should vote for it, force Nigel Farage and Kemi Badenoch to say that they would actually prefer effectively a no-deal Brexit because they want to pull out of the European Convention on Human Rights – that means you’ve got to renegotiate the trade deal.

He added: “(We should) have that debate. I’m confident we would win that debate.”
