Royal Mail’s delivery standards have deteriorated further, missing key target metrics even as operations undergo major restructuring.

The postal operator managed to deliver just 82.5 per cent of first-class mail on the following day during its second quarter, while second-class performance fell to 87.2 per cent delivered within three working days.

The group’s parent company, International Distribution Services (IDS), was bought last year by Czech billionaire Daniel Kretinsky.

These figures mark a decline from the first quarter of the year, when 85 per cent of first-class letters and 91.4 per cent of second-class items arrived on time.

Under regulatory requirements set by Ofcom, which were reduced in April during universal service reforms, 90 per cent of first-class post must arrive next day and 95 per cent of second-class mail within three days.

Royal Mail insisted operational progress is being made through wider reforms, such as scrapping Saturday second-class post, but warned its turnaround will take time.

It said: “Given the scale and pace of the universal service change, we do not expect progress on quality of service to be linear.

“The introduction of the new delivery model requires time for delivery offices to adapt to the new processes before customers receive the most reliable service possible throughout the transition.”

Royal Mail last month said it was pausing the full rollout of its second-class post overhaul until after Christmas to allow staff to focus on the peak festive season.

Citizens Advice said Royal Mail was on track to miss first-class delivery targets for the tenth year in a row and second-class for seven years in a row.

Tom MacInnes, director of policy at Citizens Advice, said: “Putting a letter in a postbox shouldn’t be like rolling a dice to see if it arrives on time.

“But that’s the pot luck service consumers get with Royal Mail.”

He added: “To date, Ofcom’s efforts to hold Royal Mail to account have made no impact on service.

“That’s why the regulator needs to escalate its action and prevent Royal Mail from raising stamp prices while continuing to miss annual delivery targets.”

Royal Mail also said on Thursday that boss Alistair Cochrane was retiring at the end of the year, to be replaced by IDS and Royal Mail chief financial officer Paul Ablin.
