Susan Smith, who killed her young sons by rolling her car into a South Carolina lake more than 30 years ago, was unanimously denied parole on Thursday.

Smith, 55, was sentenced to life for the 1994 drownings of 3-year-old Michael and 14-month-old Alex, still strapped in car seats, after jurors rejected the death penalty.

Under South Carolina state laws in effect at the time, Smith became eligible to petition for parole after serving 30 years, with subsequent reviews scheduled every two years if denied.

The state parole board unanimously rejected her first application in 2024.

David Smith, the father of the boys, vowed at her initial hearing to oppose his former wife's release for as long as he possibly can and testified at her hearing on Thursday.

![The state parole board unanimously rejected her first application in 2024](https://static.the-independent.com/2026/11/19/18/download.-4..jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

He said 32 years may seem like a long time, but to him it seems like it was yesterday when he buried his boys. He said he was there to advocate for his sons, who could never stand up for themselves.

“She took away one of the greatest gifts God gives us – life. She did that deliberately. It wasn’t an accident; it wasn’t a mistake. She did it deliberately,” he said.

![David Smith stands outside after a parole board](https://static.the-independent.com/2026/11/19/18/download.-5..jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

He made the early morning journey to Columbia alongside the original trial prosecutors to request that Smith remain in prison.

Smith appeared before the state Department of Probation, Parole and Pardon Services via a video connection from prison.

To secure her release, she needed two-thirds support from the seven-member board.

The statistical likelihood of her release remains slim.

Official agency statistics show the board approves parole in just 4% of cases before it, with the vast majority of those granted involving non-violent offenses.

Smith's case is among the most notorious to reach the panel.

![Susan Smith pleading for her sons’ return alongside her then-husband](https://static.the-independent.com/2026/11/19/05/Susan_Smith_Parole_88_48.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

She drew international headlines in 1994 by insisting for nine days that a Black carjacker had driven off with her sons, while the boys lay at the bottom of John D. Long Lake in Union County.

The false claims made by Smith, who is white, relied on a centuries-old racist trope framing Black men as a danger to white women, tapping into crime concerns that were widespread in 1990s America and remain prevalent today.

State prosecutors have long asserted that Smith killed her sons because she believed they caused the wealthy son of her employer to end their romantic affair.

During her trial three decades ago, Smith and her defense attorneys attributed her actions to her mental health, stating she intended to enter the lake alongside her children before backing out at the last moment.

![Three-year-old Michael and 14-month-old Alex](https://static.the-independent.com/2026/11/19/05/Susan_Smith_Parole_84193.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Her attorney repeated arguments raised during her first parole review, emphasizing that she has no other criminal history and poses a low risk of ever committing a crime again.

At her first parole appearance, Smith wept while describing her actions as horrible, stating that God had forgiven her and asking for similar mercy, adding she would give anything to bring her sons back.

Her tearful public pleas for the safe return of Michael and Alex spanned nine days before she eventually confessed to investigators.

An investigative reconstruction demonstrated that it took six minutes for Smith's Mazda to sink beneath the surface, as internal cameras captured water streaming through the air vents and rising steadily. The children's bodies were discovered hanging upside down in their car seats, with one small hand pressed against a window.
