A 12-year-old New Jersey boy who was critically injured after being hit in the neck by a baseball before a game earlier this year has died just days before what would have been his 13th birthday.

Xaiver Taylor was warming up for a Little League baseball game in Maple Shade, about 13 miles outside of Philadelphia, on May 26, when he was accidentally hit in the neck with a baseball and collapsed, CBS Philadelphia previously reported.

He died on Wednesday after spending the last five and a half months on a ventilator in a coma at the hospital, his family wrote on social media.

“Though only twelve years old, Xavier fulfilled a purpose that many never discover in a lifetime. He was a fisher of men,” his family’s “Xavier Strong” organization and the All Things Foundation said in a statement. “His faith pointed countless people to Jesus, and his legacy will continue to echo through generations.”

The local community rallied behind the 12-year-old before his tragic death. Homes and businesses in the Maple Shade area were lit up in blue — his team’s color — as part of a widespread effort to show support for his recovery.

![Xavier Taylor was hit in the neck by a baseball before a game in May, and died on Wednesday from his injuries](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/19/19/54/Xavier-Taylor.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

In the social media post, his family thanked the community for the outpouring of support.

“To every person who prayed, to every family who left a porch light on, to every child who placed a baseball bat outside their front door, to every church, every friend, every stranger, every believer who stood with the Taylor family, thank you,” the statement read.

The boy’s loved ones remembered him as someone who loved God, and vowed that his “legacy will live on.”

The family added: “This is not the end of Xavier's story. It is the beginning of an eternal one. The same God who carried Xavier will continue carrying this family, this ministry, and every life his testimony has touched.”

![The community rallied behind Xavier, with residents lighting up their homes and businesses in blue — his team’s color — to show their support](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/19/19/56/Xavier-Taylor-c02d08f3.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The devastating news was also shared by the Maple Shade Youth Baseball Association.

“Our baseball family is grieving alongside the Taylor family. While we know there are no words that can take away this pain, please know that our thoughts, prayers, and love are with them. Xavier was one of our own, and this loss will forever leave a hole in our baseball community,” their statement read.

The league thanked the community for its support rallying behind the boy over the past nearly six months.

“To everyone who has done something, big or small, to show the Taylor family love and support — THANK YOU. You reminded us all what community is supposed to look like. You showed that when one of our own is hurting, we show up. We stand together. We love each other. And we never stop fighting for our kids.”
