## Aleksandar Čupić's appointment marks a historic breakthrough for the grassroots movement, which ended 14 years of rule by Aleksandar Vučić's party after campaigning against corruption.

Serbia's student-led opposition has taken a major step towards forming a government and ending the era of Aleksandar Vučić, appointing a university lecturer as parliament speaker after its election victory last month.

Aleksandar Čupić, a lecturer, doctor of science and assistant professor at the University of Belgrade, won 140 votes in the 250-member assembly, while the Serbian Progressive Party candidate won just 97.

The new generation of student-backed lawmakers are mostly teachers, professors and experts with established careers but little or no political experience.

It’s the first time in Serbia’s history that a grassroots protest movement has won the majority needed to form a government.

“I have been given a huge honour, obligation and responsibility,” Čupić said after his election. He promised to restore Parliament's dignity and “dialogue and tolerance” among the lawmakers.

“I pledge to be strict and just in this position, as I always have been in life,” he added.

Last month’s parliamentary election marked a significant shift for Serbia.

The students' election victory came after they led anti-corruption protests for two years following a canopy collapse at a train station in northern Serbia that killed 16 people in November 2024. Many involved in the demonstrations blamed the disaster on rampant corruption in construction projects.

Čupić's election as speaker came after more than two days of heated debate, which was dominated by Vučić's accusations that his opponents were attacking and pressuring him and his party. That’s despite the fact that they still control the government administration and other state institutions.

According to Serbia’s laws, a new government must be voted into office within 90 days after the new Parliament's first session. In his role as speaker, Čupić will also serve as Serbia's acting president. He’s expected to schedule an early presidential election in due course, which must be held by the end of the year.

Vučić has accused the students of “revolutionary terror”, following a surge in post-election arrests which included officials, police officers and a private bank owner who allegedly attempted to siphon off money.
