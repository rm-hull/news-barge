Robert Jenrick has been mocked for his “AI slop” parody video of Grand Theft Auto 6 (GTA 6), in which a character intending to be the Reform MP tackles crime and works to reduce the cost of living.

The party’s spokesperson for the economy shared the video on social media on Thursday evening, as Britons eagerly waited to play the hotly-anticipated new video game, released at midnight, 13 years after the previous instalment in the blockbuster series.

In Reform’s parody of the GTA 6 trailer, called “Grand Theft Jenrick”, a Jenrick character, wearing jeans, a white t-shirt and a plaid overshirt, is presented as the protagonist of the new game.

“I didn’t leave my last party on the best of terms”, he says in the video about his defection from the Conservatives, before adding it was the “best thing I’ve ever done”.

The video shows a virtual Jenrick tasked with cutting the cost of living, controlling migration and tracking down fare dodgers.

It has been roundly mocked online.

On X (formerly Twitter), Canning Town Conservatives said: “From running to be the PM, to creating AI slop of yourself. Thank goodness Kemi won.”

Political commentator Luke Robert Black said: “He’s now doing a Trump and making AI slop of himself.”

Oliver Scott took aim at the video’s production, saying: “So many things wrong with this but why is he recording the voice over while underwater?”

X user Mukhtar replied to Jenrick to say: “This is pathetic and embarrassing”.

![The Robert Jenrick character in his GTA parody](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/19/8/19/Screenshot-2026-11-19-at-08-44-29.png?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

It also made its way onto the Accidental Partridge account, devoted to finding examples of Alan Partridge-esque behaviour online.

In another video Nigel Farage is shown ‘playing’ the Jenrick version of the game.

The Reform leader says: “Here’s Brixton Market, a shoplifter, there goes goes Rob Jenrick rugby tackling him to the ground. Rob, what a great enforcer of the law this man is.”

The video shows the Jenrick character on screen as Farage holds a controller. Jenrick is outside a Greggs store having an altercation with the shoplifter character.

Luke Robert Black also commented on that video, saying: “Cringe slop. Also the controller isn’t even turned on.”

The real GTA 6 is expected to break sales records and purchases of gaming consoles and accessories surged in the run-up to launch day.

John Lewis said it had seen a 62 per cent year-on-year increase in sales of gaming consoles and accessories, while Currys reported a 149 per cent increase in PlayStation 5 consoles alone.

Broadband firms geared up for the significant demand that was expected to come with the release, with pre-loads of the game alone breaking records in the UK.

Before the launch, BT and EE called it an “unprecedented gaming download event” because of the size of the game and the number of people expected to play.

Some 2.8 million UK adults are set to play GTA 6 on launch day alone, according to a Uswitch poll commissioned earlier this month.

![Gamers around the world have been waiting years for the new GTA](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/18/09/2026-11-17T224527Z_230550109_RC2YPOA79M30_RTRMADP_3_GAMING-GTA.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Many of them are expected to call in sick to work, while others have already booked the day off as annual leave.

GTA VI returns to the neon-soaked streets of Vice City, Rockstar’s fictional version of Miami, which has featured in previous instalments of the series.

The game follows two protagonists, Jason Duval and Lucia Caminos, who find themselves caught up in a criminal conspiracy in the fictional state of Leonida, inspired by Florida.

The pair become embroiled in a Bonnie and Clyde-style crime story after “an easy score goes wrong”, leaving them “forced to rely on each other more than ever if they want to make it out alive”.
