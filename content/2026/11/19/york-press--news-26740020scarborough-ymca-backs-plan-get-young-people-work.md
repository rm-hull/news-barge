## Yorkshire Coast YMCA backs new support for young people out of work

YMCA Yorkshire Coast has backed an independent review into young people who are not in education, employment, or training *(Image: Simon Oconnor)*

YMCA Yorkshire Coast has welcomed the final report of Alan Milburn’s independent review into young people who are not in education, employment or training, published today.

The review calls for major changes to help young people who are not in education, employment or training (NEET).

It also said it wants to be part of the local response in Scarborough, Eastfield, Whitby and Filey.

Alan Milburn’s independent review, From a Welfare State to a Working State, found that nearly one million young people aged 16 to 24 across the UK are NEET.

Liam Downey, chief executive of YMCA Yorkshire Coast, said: "A lot of what happens next will be decided locally, and that’s where we want to be involved.

"We’d like to be part of the local NEET taskforce, and we’re looking forward to working with North Yorkshire Council, the Combined Authority, employers, schools and colleges to get this right for young people in Scarborough, Eastfield and Beyond"

The report calls for a legal guarantee of opportunity and support for every NEET young person aged 18 to 24, regardless of whether they claim benefits.

Support would be delivered through a new Youth Opportunity Service, to be established in every part of England by 2029.

This service would be designed to help young people re-engage with education, training or work.

The report also recommends financial incentives for employers who hire young people out of work or education for at least six months.

Much of the delivery would happen at a local level, with the service built around existing organisations already working with young people.

Mayors would be able to commission services from community organisations.

Alongside the report, the government is asking councils to set up local NEET taskforces that bring partners together behind a plan to reduce the number of young people who are not in education, employment or training.

Mr Downey said: "It’s good to see the review say that young people want to work, because that’s what we see too.

"There must be opportunities for them, support young people to get the skills to join the workforce, and employers need support to accept a less experienced workforce."

YMCA Yorkshire Coast has raised concerns about one part of the review.

It shares concerns with YMCA England and Wales about the proposal to remove the Universal Credit health element for under-25s and replace it with a single Youth Allowance.

Mr Downey said: "Some young people are living on their own with no family to fall back on, and they mustn’t end up worse off while the new system is being set up.

"Any changes need to protect them from poverty and homelessness."

YMCA Yorkshire Coast is a registered charity and operates independently of national YMCA funding.

The charity said it believes that "people need foundation building blocks to a healthy life; this is what we strive to provide".

All of the funds raised go towards supporting YMCA projects in Scarborough, Eastfield, Whitby, Bridlington, Filey and communities in between.

The organisation is led by its own board of trustees and management team, who design services based on local needs.
