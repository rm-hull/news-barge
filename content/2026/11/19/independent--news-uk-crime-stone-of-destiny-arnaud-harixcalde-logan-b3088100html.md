A man told onlookers it was “his birth right” to sit on the Stone of Destiny as he used a hammer to smash the glass case containing the historic stone.

Arnaud Harixcalde Logan, 36, carried out the attack while visiting the exhibit at Perth Museum on 12 July last year.

An international arrest warrant was issued after he failed to appear at court hearings in March 2026, having moved to Australia.

He was arrested at Edinburgh Airport on his return to Scotland on 7 September.

He pleaded guilty to a charge of malicious mischief when he faced Perth Sheriff Court on Thursday.

Footage taken after the incident was widely shared on social media, with Logan shown standing in the museum’s main hall dressed in a kilt and bonnet, with hands on hips.

The video also shows other visitors looking on from behind a set of glass doors.

![Arnaud Harixcalde Logan, 36, carried out the attack while visiting the exhibit at Perth Museum on 12 July last year](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/19/15/44bad80dd078d5cd06153228f73fb511Y29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzk1MTMyMDc5-2.75731489.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Access to the Stone of Destiny was suspended for several months following the incident while repairs costing £9,071 were carried out.

The stone itself was not damaged.

Logan, who is of no fixed abode, represented himself during Thursday’s hearing, having earlier declined the offer of legal representation.

Setting out the Crown’s narrative, prosecutor Laurell Johnstone told the court that at about 1.45pm that day, two witnesses – referred to as Thompson and Jarych – heard “banging and screaming” and went to investigate.

Ms Johnstone said: “On entering, witness Jarych saw the accused holding a blue and white hammer in his hand, repeatedly hitting the glass casing which holds the Stone of Destiny.

“Members of the public appeared distressed and panicked. Staff began to evacuate everyone from the building.”

She said the pair approached Logan, and Jarych “took possession of the hammer”.

![Access to the Stone of Destiny was suspended for several months following the incident while repairs costing £9,071 were carried out.](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/19/15/5a712dafe3f4b9e68c5baede1c1ef00fY29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzk1MTg3NDY3-2.75731479.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The prosecutor went on: “(The) accused stated he was called Logan and that the Logan clan had been betrayed and that the Stone of Destiny had been stolen from them.

“He stated that it was his birth right to sit on the stone.

“He also stated that he wanted restoration of the French monarchy and independence for Scotland.”

The court was shown footage of the incident, taken by a CCTV camera inside the Stone of Destiny exhibition space.

In it, Logan can be seen bending down in one corner of the space, before moving to the stone and repeatedly striking the glass case with a hammer.

Another visitor can be seen leaving the area, before two figures enter the space and try to put an end to the attack.

Sheriff Jennifer Bain KC deferred sentencing to January 6 for reports to be drawn up.

She told Logan: “This is a very concerning case.

“You now accept malicious damage of a national treasure and the video clearly shows your determined efforts to get to the Stone of Destiny.”

She refused his application for bail, saying that now he had admitted the offence he no longer has the “protection of the presumption of innocence”.

![The Stone of Destiny is the centrepiece exhibition at Perth Museum](https://static.independent.co.uk/2025/07/14/14/bab0abaed2b9508fe766fff3be5a7918Y29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzUyNTgyODg0-2.75731491.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The day before the incident, Logan uploaded a video in which he said: “I’m going to go to Perth tomorrow, I’m going to break the glass and I’m going to sit on the stone because the stone has chosen me, I haven’t chosen it.”

The video description states: “Explaining the situation and proclaiming Scotland’s independance (sic) and French restauration. For a New Auld-Alliance.”

In a blog post from June 2026 entitled “Why did I attack the Stone of Scone?”, Logan claimed the attack was “filled with symbolism” and that the purpose was “not to destroy, but to restore”.

The artefact, also known as the Stone of Scone, was moved to Perth Museum in 2024 as part of a £27 million redevelopment.

A sandstone block weighing 152kg, it has been associated with the Scottish and UK monarchies for centuries.

The Stone of Destiny was used in Queen Elizabeth II’s coronation in 1953 and also in the coronation of the King in 2023.

It was formally returned to Scotland in 1996 to go on display at Edinburgh Castle.

A spokesperson for Culture Perth & Kinross, the charitable trust which manages Perth Museum said: “We are pleased to hear of the court’s decision to remand the individual responsible for the July 2025 incident in custody.

“We remain committed to ensuring the ongoing protection of this important symbol of the nation’s heritage whilst making it accessible to visitors.”
