# A Woman of Substance: County Hall used to film second series

## Filming for the second series of Channel 4’s hit period drama A Woman of Substance is taking place at Wakefield’s County Hall.

Details of the latest production were revealed at a meeting where senior councillors agreed to introduce a new charging policy for TV and film companies using council-owned assets as filming locations.

Production vans and lighting equipment were in place outside the Grade I-listed County Hall as Wakefield Council’s cabinet members approved the proposals at the neighbouring Town Hall on Tuesday.

Matthew Caton, portfolio holder for children, young people and lifelong learning, said: “I think it’s quite topical that we bring this up because at the very moment we have got the Woman of Substance filming at the beautiful County Hall.

![Filming for Channel 4's Woman of Substance took place at Wakefield's County Hall as councillors voted in favour of introducing a new charging policy for production companies using council-owned assets LDRS image](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOmU4NzY2ZmRiLTgzNDktNDg3Ny04NWRkLTRiN2RkZjVlODc0MDo0ZTUyNzJlNy1kMWQwLTQzMTEtYTIxZS1mOTBlY2E0M2E1NTA=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


“I think we have fantastic assets in Wakefield. We have Gerogian, Victorian and indeed some Medieval buildings.

“It’s a common theme for us as a district, as an administration, to say we have been massively undervalued.”

In July Channel 4 announced that the first series of A Woman of Substance, based on Leeds-born author Barbara Taylor Bradford’s 1979 debut novel, was its most-streamed drama of the past five years.

A report to cabinet members said location managers involved in the second series of “the highest grossing and most watched Channel 4 drama of 2026” had made “a serious enquiry” into using Wakefield for filming.

![Filming for Channel 4's Woman of Substance took place at Wakefield's County Hall as councillors voted in favour of introducing a new charging policy for production companies using council-owned assets LDRS image](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOmZkYTkwMWQ3LWIzZGUtNDAxNy05OTcwLWY5ZGI5ODA4ZjZlZDplYTZkOGVmNC0zNzAxLTQ4ODMtOGVmOC05OTBiMGZmZmY3YmU=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


The document also said the district’s profile as a “potentially international filming destination” had risen since Netflix drama Adolescence was filmed at Production Park studios in South Kirkby.

Senior councillors approved the introduction of a new pricing policy to allow the authority to recover the costs of supporting and co-coordinating filming activity.

Coun Caton added: “I’m happy to see that we have got some organisation, we have got a coherent strategy now with regards to film, so we can get Wakefield promoted and get Wakefield on the big screen.

“I think it’ as a place that not a lot of people in the UK know about.

![The chamber in County Hall is still used to hold monthly full council meetings. LDRS image](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOmMwYzNkMDczLTRjNmQtNDNjNC1iNThiLTVjMThiMzRiOWU0OTpmNmI5N2U2MC1hZjhmLTQ3NDUtOTQyMy01ODhhNWVmZDE2YjQ=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


“We have some beautiful scenery around here that definitely needs to be more widely promoted.”

The report said the district had a “wealth of unique and versatile filming locations” capable of attracting film, television, commercial and photographic productions.

They include County Hall, Pontefract Castle, historic buildings and town centres as well as privately owned locations such as Production Park, stately homes and business parks.

Council leader Karl Johnson said: “We are very strong in the creative sector and logistics.

“We really need to champion our creative industries. It’s a massive move forward, this is just strengthening our position.”

The council has received 50 inquiries from production companies since establishing a film office in January.

The creation of the film office was inspired by success in other local authority areas, including Screen Staffordshire, Liverpool Film Office and Bradford Film Office.

The Liverpool Film Office has recorded record growth since 2019, with more than 1,600 productions investing £150m into the local economy, creating over 5,000 full-time equivalent jobs and supporting local

businesses.

The council-run Bradford Film Office recorded having 151 filming days and 40 productions during 2024, which had an estimated economic impact of £7.3m.

The report also said: “The establishment of a dedicated Wakefield film office is essential if the district is to compete effectively with neighbouring local authorities that have already recognised the economic, cultural and reputational benefits of supporting film and television production.

“Filming is also widely known to generate film tourism footfall as people come from the UK and abroad to visit the scenes of their best-loved productions; an advantage of Wakefield being on screen locally, nationally, and internationally.”
