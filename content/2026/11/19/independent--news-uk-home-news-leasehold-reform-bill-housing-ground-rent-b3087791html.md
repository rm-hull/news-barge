Legislation aimed at dismantling the "feudal" property framework by offering owners greater protections and control will be presented to Parliament on Thursday.

Under the Commonhold and Leasehold Reform Bill, the Government intends to cap annual ground rents at £250, eliminate unfair fees, and make freehold purchases and lease extensions simpler and cheaper.

The bill will also ban leasehold for new homes and flats, alongside simplifying transitions to commonhold ownership.

Housing minister Matthew Pennycook said: "The leasehold system denies individuals control over the homes they live in, and have paid for, and routinely subjects them to excessive extraction.

"It is blighting lives and it is an anachronism in the 21st century.

"The publication of our historic Commonhold and Leasehold Reform Bill marks the beginning of the end for the feudal leasehold system.

"We’re laying the foundations for a commonhold future and transforming the experience of homeownership for existing leaseholders by giving them greater rights and protections, and providing them with easy and effective escape routes."

![The Government is seeking to cap ground rents at £250 a year, stop rip-off admin fees and make lease extensions and freehold purchases cheaper and easier through the Commonhold and Leasehold Reform Bill](https://static.independent.co.uk/2023/11/09/10/be53ea09eae18080aa292e5bb7904aedY29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNjk5NjEwNzIx-2.38488394.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The legislation will apply to homeowners in England and Wales.

Professor Nick Hopkins, chairman of the Leasehold Advisory Service, said: “The introduction of this Bill is a significant step towards giving homeowners stronger protections against some of the most damaging features of leasehold.

“Moving towards commonhold, with an easier route for existing leaseholders to convert, is the most significant change to home ownership for a generation.

“It will give owners of flats ownership and control of the building their flat is in, with a legal framework that is fit for purpose for flat ownership.

“We are developing clear, practical guidance on the new commonhold model, helping people understand how it will work and make informed choices.”

The government said more than three million leaseholders will also be better protected from being forced to pay their landlord’s legal costs when they challenge a service charge, which acts as a “huge barrier to fighting costs”.

![The government proposes ending such leaseholds and giving homeowners more control over their properties](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/19/09/2203047484..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Prime minister Andy Burnham said earlier this week: “It is simply wrong that millions of leaseholders can be hit with unfair charges, then face another bill just for challenging them.

“That isn’t right, and where we can act quickly to change it, we will.

“Today’s changes are only the beginning. We’re giving leaseholders stronger rights now, while we press ahead with the bigger reforms needed to put power back in homeowners’ hands and end the feudal leasehold system for good.”

The Government plans to strengthen the rules further from November 2027, when leaseholders will automatically receive detailed information about charges, whether their building is owned by a private landlord, council or housing association.

This includes information about their contribution to building insurance and protections against “excessive or unreasonable” fees for arranging and managing that cover.
