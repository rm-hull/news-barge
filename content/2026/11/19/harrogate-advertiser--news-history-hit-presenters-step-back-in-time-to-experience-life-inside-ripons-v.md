Two History Hit presenters have experienced the harsh realities of Victorian workhouse life in a new special filmed at Ripon Workhouse Museum.

*Could You Survive a Victorian Workhouse?* sees Luke Tomes and Katie Kennedy, known online as The History Gossip, follow very different paths through the historic institution, revealing the stark choices faced by people who entered the workhouse system.

Luke is processed as a vagrant by the Tramp Major, experiencing the strict regime faced by those seeking temporary shelter.

His own clothes are taken away before he is sent to break rocks as hard labour and served a basic meal of gruel.

After being discovered in the Master and Matron’s quarters, he is punished with time in the cells and eventually sent away after two nights.

Katie’s experience explores the life of a resident pauper.

During the Victorian era, people without a settled home could enter a casual ward for short-term accommodation, often in exchange for work.

Unlike Luke, Katie follows the longer-term routine of a workhouse resident, including labour, exercise, prayer and school.

She is interviewed by the Workhouse Guardian, collects vegetables from the garden and is allowed to remain within the institution.

The episode also explores the realities of daily life behind the workhouse walls, from food and sanitation to discipline, punishment and the treatment of those who lived there.

It also examines what happened to residents who died, whether there was any chance of leaving the system and how workhouses eventually disappeared.

Filmed at Ripon Workhouse Museum & Garden, the programme highlights the museum’s restored buildings and exhibitions, bringing the experiences of Victorian residents to a wider audience.

A spokesperson for Ripon Museums said: “We were delighted to welcome Luke and Katie to Ripon Workhouse.

“Their visit brings the museum’s stories to life in a fresh, informative and fun way, while showing how differently people could experience the Workhouse.

“We hope the episode puts Ripon in the spotlight and introduces the museum to a new audience.”
