**DER SPIEGEL:** Mr. Rudd, Thursday’s meeting between Presidents Donald Trump, Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin at the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation Summit in China has raised fears in Europe that this trio of heavyweights might carve up the world between them like a roast. Do you share these concerns about a return to an era of imperial deal-making?

**Rudd**: The good thing is that the Chinese have a historical memory and remember that, in the 19th century, they themselves were part of the meal. I think, firstly, there is zero prospect of a G2, simply because the strategic differences between China and the United States are too great. From Xi Jinping's perspective, it would be unsustainable because his calculation would be that Trump is too unpredictable and that there is no systemic buy-in for any such thing from the United States. During Barack Obama’s presidency, there were flirtations with the idea of a new type of great power relationship, the idea of a G2. But that horse has bolted, to use an Australian expression. It’s gone, out of the paddock. And if there can be no G2, then a G3 simply doesn't work.
