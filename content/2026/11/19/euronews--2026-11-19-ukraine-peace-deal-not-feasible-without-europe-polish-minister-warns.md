## Poland’s Deputy Foreign Minister Ignacy Niemczycki tells Euronews that Europe is indispensable to any Ukraine peace settlement, hinting that Kyiv’s allies are moving towards a military role in enforcing an agreement.

Poland’s Deputy Foreign Minister Ignacy Niemczycki has warned that a peace deal to end Russia’s war in Ukraine is unworkable without European involvement, while signalling that Kyiv’s allies are edging towards a more robust military role in enforcing any settlement.

Speaking to Europe Today, the Euronews morning show, Niemczycki insisted that Europe’s participation in negotiations was a practical necessity rather than simply a question of diplomatic representation.

“Any kind of peace deal is simply not feasible without Europe on board,” he said. “Nothing about the European security architecture should be decided without us.”

Niemczycki was speaking as the Coalition of the Willing, the group of countries working on post-war security guarantees for Ukraine, gathered in Berlin.

He hinted that the Coalition's talks were moving towards a more concrete military commitment.

Asked about the prospect of military enforcement of a peace agreement, the Polish minister said that “our discussions about that are evolving”, stressing the need to address the nature of security guarantees in concrete terms.

His remarks suggest that Ukraine’s European allies are increasingly grappling with the question of how to deter renewed Russian aggression once a peace agreement is reached, rather than merely monitoring a ceasefire.

For Poland, Niemczycki said, the outcome of the negotiations would have direct consequences for its own national security. Allowing Russia to advance closer to Poland’s borders would increase the cost of defending the country, giving Warsaw a vital stake in the terms of any settlement.
