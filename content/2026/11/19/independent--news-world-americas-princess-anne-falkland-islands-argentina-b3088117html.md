Argentina has formally condemned Princess Anne’s official visit to the Falkland Islands, branding the trip a “provocation” as tensions rise between London and Buenos Aires.

In a statement issued on social media, Argentina’s foreign ministry rejected the Princess Royal’s engagements across the UK-administered archipelago – known in Argentina as Las Malvinas – as well as South Georgia, which it claims as sovereign territory.

Buenos Aires accused Britain of carrying out the activities “in the exercise of the illegal British occupation” of the islands and surrounding waters.

President Milei’s rebuke coincides with escalating friction over the Sea Lion offshore oil project, led by British energy firm Rockhopper Exploration and Israeli partner Navitas Petroleum.

![Britain’s Princess Anne pictured on her visit to the Falkland Islands](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/19/16/2304462137..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Last month, Mr Milei’s government petitioned the International Tribunal for the Law of the Sea in Hamburg to intervene and halt the Sea Lion development, after Rockhopper announced plans to begin extracting oil north of the islands by January 2028.

The tribunal is scheduled to rule on Buenos Aires’ request for urgent provisional measures on 28 November.

Mr Milei has previously condemned the drilling scheme as “illegal plundering” of Argentina’s natural resources and has backed legislation in Congress to toughen penalties against companies operating in the disputed waters.

In September, an Argentine court also issued an injunction seeking to block the project following an environmental challenge by domestic campaign groups.

Buckingham Palace presented the Princess Royal’s trip – which runs until 20 November in her capacity as patron of the South Georgia Heritage Trust – as a heritage and commemorative visit, during which she opened a new sports complex in Stanley and unveiled a memorial to polar explorer Sir Ernest Shackleton.

The diplomatic flare-up comes after Fifa hit the Argentinian Football Association (AFA) with a record £235,000 fine in August over a controversial banner claiming Argentine sovereignty over the Falkland Islands during the 2026 World Cup.

Britain has administered the Falkland Islands continuously since 1833 and does not recognise Argentina’s sovereignty claim, maintaining that the islanders hold the right to self-determination and to develop their own natural resources.

The two nations fought a 74-day war over the territory in 1982 following an invasion by Argentina’s then-military dictatorship, which ended in surrender to British forces.

For decades, the United Nations has acknowledged the sovereignty dispute and urged both governments to refrain from unilateral actions in the region.
