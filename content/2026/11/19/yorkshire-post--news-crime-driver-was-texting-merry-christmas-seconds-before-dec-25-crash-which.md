# Driver was texting ‘Merry Christmas’ seconds before Dec 25 crash which killed grandmother

## A 'selfish' driver was texting 'Merry Christmas' when he smashed into another car at 75mph in a December 25 fatal crash.

Joshua Stephen Jones was typing on his phone seconds before he ploughed into a Ford Fiesta which had stopped for a separate collision on the A1(M) near Darlington on Christmas Day last year.

Jones, who was on the same road in a VW Tiguan having spent Christmas Day with his family, read a text 30 minutes before setting off warning him to be careful as there'd been a 'smash' on the motorway.

As the 30-year-old approached the crash area, he had his cruise control set to 75mph and did not brake, slow or appear to react in anyway to the standing traffic up ahead.

His car smashed into the back of a Ford Fiesta carrying a married couple in their 80s who were driving home after spending Christmas with their family.

The smash killed the female elderly passenger in the car, who had been travelling home from seeing her own family.

Her husband, who was driving, suffered numerous serious injuries which required a hospital stay and a long recovery.

Police examinations of Jones's phone showed 'multiple key-presses' in the minute before the collision, and that it had been unlocked five times in the 10 minutes before the crash, with Jones opening Instagram, WhatsApp and Signal.

The Signal app contained a partly-typed, unsent 'Merry Christmas' message.

North Yorkshire Police’s Forensic Collision Investigation Unit concluded that the collision was most likely due to Jones being distracted by his phone.

Jones, a soldier stationed at Catterick Garrison, was jailed at Teesside Crown Court for nine years after admitting causing death by dangerous driving.

He was also banned from driving for 10 years.

The family of the victims described the devastating impact of the collision. In a personal statement, the daughter of the woman who died said: “Our hearts are totally broken. We have been subjected to a living nightmare that we will never wake up from. And all because of one person’s selfish and reckless actions.

“We have lost a loving Wife, Mam, Grandma and Great Grandma, who was the centre of our world, as we were hers.

“Our entire lives and Christmas-times will never be the same.”

After the sentencing hearing, investigating officer DC Stephanie Palmer, of North Yorkshire Police, said: “This tragic case serves as a stark reminder of the potentially devastating consequences of using a phone while driving.

“Even a momentary distraction can have life-changing results, reducing a driver’s ability to react to hazards, and putting everyone on the road at risk.

“No phone call, text, or notification is worth risking a life for. We urge drivers to remain focused on the road at all times, and make safety their top priority.”
