A stray dog has formed an unlikely bond with a pack of wild coyotes in southern Arizona, but what sounds like a movie plot has neighbors worried about her safety.

The dog, nicknamed Sombra, has been seen traveling with the pack for more than two weeks in west Tucson, where she regularly stops for water near resident Patricia Wilson's home.

Wilson first spotted the unusual visitor on her wildlife cameras, which she posts to YouTube. She initially thought the animal might be a puppy that escaped from a local shelter in the fall, but later realized her markings didn't match any missing shelter dogs.

"I always catch her at night on my cameras, and she's a little bit of a shadow. So I named her Sombra, which is shadow in Spanish," Wilson told Fox 10.

While the coyotes appear to have accepted Sombra into their pack, little is known about the dog's past, how she ended up with the wild animals, or how long they've been together.

![Sombra, a dog, drinks from a water bowl while roaming with wild coyotes in southern Arizona](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/18/21/28/Work-900-px-x-600-px-(5).png?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Wilson and wildlife experts worry Sombra may not survive much longer with the coyotes, as the dog appears underweight and too weak to keep up with the pack.

"You can see every one of her ribs. Her nails are long. If you're an animal lover, you take pause," Wilson said.

"She might be hanging with the coyotes now, but she won't keep up long-term, and this could be a disastrous ending. Not sure if they'll turn on her or leave her behind."

Nikki Reck with the Pima Animal Care Center told Fox 10 that rescuers want to get Sombra to safety but face a challenge capturing her without accidentally trapping one of the coyotes.

Because the dog is traveling with the wild animals, rescuers must find a way to separate her from the pack without putting the coyotes at risk.

Wilson said Red Collar Rescue, a nonprofit that helps capture animals in difficult situations, has been in contact with her about a possible rescue.

She's hopeful the group can safely capture Sombra and get her the care she needs.

If rescued, Sombra will need a foster home where she can receive veterinary care and recover after spending an unknown amount of time living outdoors.

The Pima Animal Care Center is looking for potential foster caregivers willing to give her a safe place to recover. Any Tucson-area residents interested in fostering Sombra can contact the Pima Animal Care Center.
