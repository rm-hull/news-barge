President Donald Trump has posthumously pardoned the late baseball star Pete Rose, who was embroiled in controversy over tax crimes and a gambling scandal.

“I have just given a Complete and Total Pardon to the Late, Great Pete Rose, a true Baseball SUPERSTAR & LEGEND, whose record of 4,256 Hits in a Season will never be broken,” Trump wrote on Truth Social Thursday.

Trump then listed off some more of the former Cincinnati Reds first baseman’s stats and then said he, “just like the Great Roger Clemens, who won 354 games, was treated terribly by Major League Baseball in not allowing him to go into the Baseball Hall of Fame. Politics, I guess!”

Rose was banned from the MLB in 1989 after the league found that he violated its rules by repeatedly betting on the Reds as a player and manager from 1985 to 1987.

He was then sentenced in 1990 to five months in federal prison for filing false income tax returns. “I will serve my sentence, pay my debt to society and get on with my life,” Rose, who had pleaded guilty to the tax charges, said, according to a *New York Times* article published at the time. Rose later died at age 83 in September 2024.

![President Donald Trump has posthumously pardoned the late baseball star Pete Rose, who was embroiled in controversy over tax crimes and a gambling scandal](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/19/21/06/GettyImages-1242367936.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

During an impromptu White House press briefing with his longtime friend Trump earlier this month, UFC chief Dana White was asked about the president potentially pardoning Rose.

“The president has said he might consider a pardon for Pete Rose. What is your advice to the president about that pardon?” a reporter asked White, who was at the podium after Trump semi-jokingly offered him the job of White House press secretary. The role remains vacant, though White never ruled it out.

“I don't advise the president on anything ever, but I think it's fair. I think it's fair to pardon Pete Rose,” White said.

Trump then chimed in: “I'd give Pete Rose a pardon. I think Pete Rose was very mistreated...he was caught gambling. Now it's legal, right? But he was gambling on his own team. He wasn't gambling against.”

“He shouldn't have done it. But he went through 40 years of hell. And they should have given him a pardon. They should have given him into the Baseball Hall of Fame a long time ago,” the president continued.

Since Rose was banned from baseball, he was not allowed into the Hall of Fame, but after he died, his ban was lifted, making him eligible for a posthumous award.

![Trump also said last month that he would give fellow former MLB star Roger Clemens, who had his own fair share of scandals, the Presidential Medal of Freedom](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/19/22/19/GettyImages-2299124162.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

In Thursday’s post, Trump also mentioned Clemens, a two-time World Series Champion with the New York Yankees who had his own fair share of scandals.

Clemens’ former trainer, Brian McNamee, claimed that he injected the baseball star multiple times with steroids and human growth hormones from 1998 through 2001. It was also reported by the *New York Daily News* in 2008 that Clemens had a decade-long affair with the now-late country music star Mindy McCready.

Nonetheless, Trump announced during a midterm rally in Texas last month that he will honor Clemens with the Presidential Medal of Freedom — the highest civilian honor.

“This supersedes anything that obviously I've done in my playing career, and to get it from this man, this president, there wouldn't be another president I'd want to get it from,” Clemens said after Trump welcomed him on stage.

The president has raised eyebrows over who he chooses to pardon, including the more than 1,500 people charged in connection with the riot at the U.S. Capitol on January 6, 2021.
