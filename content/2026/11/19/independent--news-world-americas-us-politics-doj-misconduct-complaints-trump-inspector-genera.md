Professional misconduct complaints against Justice Department officials have climbed more than 50 percent over the past two years, threatening to overwhelm the small internal office tasked with investigating them, the agency’s inspector general said Tuesday.

In an annual assessment of the department’s top six management challenges, Inspector General Don Berthiaume warned that the Office of Professional Responsibility — which investigates allegations of attorney misconduct with a full-time staff of just 18 people — risked falling behind on its oversight duties across a workforce of thousands, according to CBS News.

“The lawful and effective exercise of these authorities is indispensable to public safety and the rule of law,” Berthiaume wrote in the report. “It also creates a corresponding management responsibility to ensure that Department investigations and prosecutions are conducted with integrity, objectivity, consistency, and fairness.”

Data included in the review show complaints filed with OPR nearly doubled from fiscal year 2023 during the Biden administration to fiscal year 2026, which ended on October 1.

The OPR logged 2,081 professional misconduct complaints in fiscal year 2026, up from 1,666 in 2025 and 1,346 in 2024 — a roughly 54 percent jump over two years — and nearly double the 1,085 complaints filed in fiscal year 2023, according to the report.

![Complaints of professional misconduct against Justice Department officials have risen more than 50 percent in two years, potentially stretching the limited staff responsible for investigating them, the agency's inspector general said Tuesday](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/19/1/46/GettyImages-2262149675.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

During the 2026 fiscal year, OPR opened 33 misconduct inquiries and closed 35, CBS News reported. The office also launched 10 full-scale investigations, completing nine of them and finding professional misconduct by attorneys in all nine concluded cases, while one probe remains open.

Over the same 12-month stretch, OPR received 50 new FBI whistleblower retaliation complaints and closed 41 matters.

“These results demonstrate that OPR has continued to resolve substantial workloads despite its limited size,” the inspector general wrote. “However, the Department faces risk in its oversight capacity if OPR’s staffing levels are insufficient to manage the workload generated by the Department’s thousands of employees.”

The increase in complaints comes as the Justice Department has lost thousands of employees during President Donald Trump’s second term. Between January 2025 and May 2026, the agency saw a net drop of 10,345 staffers, according to *The Washington Sun*.

![The rise in complaints comes amid a net loss of 10,345 Justice Department employees since President Donald Trump returned to office in January 2025](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/19/1/28/GettyImages-2205129768.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The departures have made it harder for federal prosecutors to try cases across the country and left gaps in key units, including a 40 percent vacancy rate in the Missing or Murdered Indigenous Persons Regional Outreach Program and ongoing staffing shortages in the Bureau of Prisons.

“Workforce reductions may produce mission degradation that aggregate staffing numbers do not reveal, particularly where departures are concentrated in specialized or difficult-to-replace positions,” the report warned.

Berthiaume noted that maintaining ethical standards went beyond managing outside criticism.

“This challenge encompasses, but is broader than, public perception,” the report states, according to *The Hill*. “Integrity and fairness are operational requirements for a law enforcement institution, not merely matters of public image.”

The complaint numbers come as the Justice Department faces questions over criminal probes and prosecutions targeting political opponents of Trump, some of which Trump has publicly encouraged, CBS News reported. The inspector general’s report does not describe what the misconduct complaints are about or say whether any of them stem from those cases.

![The inspector general’s report does not detail the nature of the complaints or say whether any stem from high-profile prosecutions targeting Trump's political opponents](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/19/1/11/GettyImages-2282411707.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Among those targeted in criminal probes or prosecutions over the last two years are former FBI Director James Comey, New York Attorney General Letitia James, Democratic Sen. Adam Schiff, Democratic Rep. Ilhan Omar and former Federal Reserve Chair Jerome Powell.

While the Office of Inspector General investigates general waste, fraud and abuse, allegations involving Justice Department attorneys typically fall under the jurisdiction of OPR, which has less independence than the inspector general and saw its previous leader removed by senior leadership last year, CBS News reported.

The inspector general recently declined to investigate prosecutors who falsely accused Olympian David Hearn of vandalizing the Reflecting Pool, stating the matter belonged with OPR.

In a response included in the report, the Justice Department said it “emphasizes integrity, fairness, and accountability in its investigative and prosecutorial work, with the Office of Professional Responsibility (OPR) serving as a central guardian of ethical standards.”

“Despite resource constraints, OPR fulfilled its mission through disciplined case management, dedicated staff, and effective use of technology,” the department said.
