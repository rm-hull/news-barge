Ripon’s enormous sinkhole problem is to be part of a new BBC documentary investigation.

The city is by no means the only location in the world to be plagued by giant holes which suddenly appear in the ground.

But, as an hour-long show on BBC 2 on Thursday, November 26 called Why Sinkholes Collapse will reveal, it has been one of the worst places in the UK in recent decades for this damaging phenomenon.

The documentary will explore the rising global threat of sinkholes, examining the natural and human causes behind these sudden and often devastating events.

But it will also focus on Ripon which has been particularly susceptible to their sudden appearance, whether on residential streets, near the banks of the Rover Ure or in rural fields just outside the city.

In other countries such as Japan, the sinkholes have led to deaths.

In Ripon, they have led to evacuated streets, areas cordoned off, back gardens collapsing, emergency crew call-outs and cracked walls in houses.

Sinkholes tended to appear in the city in the 1980s and 1990s without warning every two or three years.

But the one that caught the eye of the public came in 2014, the national media descended on Ripon to take a look at a series of large sinkholes which opened up in the space of a month in the Magdalens Close area.

News vans from ITV, BBC York and many unmarked ones arrived to report on the scene after a hug*e* 25ft-wide sinkhole forced the evacuation of residents from three homes.

BBC TV’s Why Sinkholes Collapse hopes to raise public awareness of the hidden hazards below the surface and the work being done to predict and prevent future collapses.

The hour-long programme will highlight how geological processes, ageing infrastructure, burst water mains, and excessive groundwater extraction can all contribute to ground instability.

In Ripon’s case, it seems that the city is one of the UK's most sinkhole-prone areas because water-soluble gypsum rock lies relatively close to the surface beneath the city.

Geological exerts say groundwater flowing from west to east can dissolve the gypsum and creates underground caves that can collapse and form surface craters.

Other sinkhole incidents in Ripon include:

2018: A sinkhole of approximately 5m x 5m opened up on Dallamires Lane.

2026: A sinkhole measuring about 5m x 5m wide and 2m deep opened near homes off North Road.

A BBC spokesperson said: "The programme travels to Ripon where the ground beneath the city contains gypsum, a rock that dissolves to form underground caves.

"When the roofs of these caves collapse, the damage can work its way to the surface, creating a sinkhole."

Why Sinkholes Collapse, BBC 2, Thursday, November 26, 9pm.
