Mark and Lorraine Norwood said they were angry they had been let down

**When Lorraine and Mark Norwood's wedding caterer pulled out less than 36 hours before their big day, the couple were left in "absolute shock and horror".**

In an email sent just before midnight on 1 October this year, their provider, Baba Ganoush, said they were "heartbroken" to say they had taken the "devastating decision to cease trading with immediate effect" and would be unable to fulfill their commitment.

"It was complete disbelief at first," Mark said, after they opened the message on the Friday morning, "then shock, then anger that we were let down so badly".

With less than 24 hours to go, and having already paid £7,000 for a three-course meal and canapes for their 76 guests, they were left scrambling for a replacement.

Mark said: "They must have known for quite some time that they were in financial difficulty.

"They could have given us a heads-up or warning but obviously chose not to and just took the money and ran instead."

While the couple were able to secure an alternative caterer at the last minute, they had to borrow £3,000 from a relative to cover the extra cost, and said they were not hopeful of getting their money back.

Since their wedding, they said they had learned that in the weeks before Baba Ganoush went bust the North Yorkshire-based firm's directors, Matthew and Ashley Hyde, had set up a new catering company.

The BBC found that company, initially called Hyde Hospitality but now going by the name Marjoram Yorkshire Limited, had been registered on 30 August.

According to Companies House, Baba Ganoush - Dining By Matthew Hyde Limited remains "active", though it has 10 County Court Judgments (CCJs) against it, which, as of October, totalled more than £43,000.

In a statement to the BBC, Ashley Hyde said: "We fully recognise the upset, disruption and financial consequences experienced by the affected couples, particularly those who received very little notice, and we sincerely apologise for the distress caused.

"There were other significant factors involved which we are not currently in a position to discuss publicly while the relevant matters are being examined."

Lorraine and Mark managed to secure an alternative caterer for their wedding

Since Lorraine and Mark came forward, the BBC has spoken to other customers, former employees and suppliers who all say they have encountered difficulties with Baba Ganoush.

Sophie, who did not want the BBC to use her surname, said she had paid Baba Ganoush £4,000 to cater for her wedding in June 2027, before the company went into liquidation.

She said that after the company went bust she was contacted by Marjoram Yorkshire asking if she wanted to transfer her existing contract to the new company and saying if she did "100% of all monies" would be honoured.

However, when she declined, she said she had been told that to get her money back she would have to pursue a claim through the firm's liquidators, as the company "was not in a position to give refunds".

She said she was hoping to be able to make a claim through her credit card company.

Other brides-to-be have accused Baba Ganoush of being "pushy and persistent" by demanding payment up front, offering "deals" if they paid off more of their balance quickly.

Ashley Hyde told the BBC she was assisting with wedding insurance claims and was "willing to discuss" other potential resolutions.

She said the company had made it clear to all affected clients that they were under no obligation to enter into a new agreement with Marjoram Yorkshire.

Georgina Schorah worked as Baba Ganoush's office manager before being made redundant

Former Baba Ganoush office manager Georgina Schorah said she had had to take time off for stress in August 2025 after bailiffs - looking to collect unpaid rent for a previous premises - forced their way into the company's York office while she was working alone.

She said she had contacted Ashley and Matthew Hyde asking them to come to the office, but, despite saying they were on their way, they did not turn up.

She said she was subsequently made redundant and spent six months pursuing the company for £2,000 in outstanding wages.

The 29-year-old said she had been forced to take out a loan to cover her expenses and had only received payment after she contacted the Advisory, Conciliation and Arbitration Service (Acas).

She described the company's finances as "chaotic" and said there had been a number of complaints from couples over the quality of the food and a lack of staff.

Georgina alleged the firm would "always try and get away with not refunding them for as long as possible" and accused them of "taking on bookings when knew they were in severe financial difficulty and did not have the staff".

She claimed the firm "managed to go for so long and carry on for so long because the staff were the ones that were picking up the pieces".

Ashley Hyde said Baba Ganoush "strongly disputes" any suggestion it knowingly continued to accept or retain bookings with the intention of not fulfilling them, but the circumstances surrounding the cancellations "were not purely financial".

She cited "operational circumstances" and other factors that the company was "not in a position to discuss publicly".

Max Haynes took the company to an employment tribunal

Another former staff member, Max Haynes, 21, said he spent almost a year chasing the company for £800 he was owed, and ended up taking Baba Ganoush to an employment tribunal.

He claimed that on one occasion, while awaiting payment, the company had sent him a screenshot which appeared to show that a bank transfer had been made, but he said the money did not arrive in his account.

The BBC has also seen screenshots from Georgina, showing similar images which suggested she had been paid, though she said she had not.

Max, from York, said: "I know she [Ashley] would lie to almost everyone who would ask her, where's my money? She'd give some excuse.

"The judge at the tribunal ruled I had won, but it eventually went to bailiffs who went round to the premises twice before she eventually paid me.

"It was a horrible experience to go through. I'd spent hours working for my money to pay for uni - rent, food, travel expenses - money is already tight as a student."

Responding to Max's claims, Ashley Hyde said: "We strongly dispute suggestions that certain employment matters were ignored or left unresolved for prolonged periods.

"Some employees entered into formal settlement and confidentiality agreements with us as part of earlier negotiated resolutions of employment matters."

Adam Gough says he is owed more than £18,000 by Baba Ganoush

During his time working for the Hydes, Max said he had witnessed kitchen staff attempting to source ingredients or equipment for weddings hours before the event because suppliers had not been paid.

Adam Gough, who runs Wharfeside Hire, based in York, said it was like "torture" waiting for payment, saying he was still owed £18,000 by Baba Ganoush for 22 weddings.

Like Max and Georgina, he said in around July 2024 he was sent copies of banking records showing that he had been paid, but said no money arrived in his account.

When asked about Adam's account, Ashley Hyde said she "categorically and unequivocally denies" sending misleading images of bank transfers and said the non-payments were due to technical issues with her bank, Tide.

The BBC has contacted Tide for comment.

Adam, 45, said he had eventually taken Baba Ganoush to court "but they didn't even turn up" and had been "handed a CCJ in its absence".

He said when he had told them he planned to speak publicly about the issues, the Hydes had said they would sue him for slander.

He said they were "very good at getting rid of bad things online", adding: "They're like dogfighters when they hit the bottom."

He said he was not notified when Baba Ganoush liquidated, despite being owed money

When asked if he thought he would ever see his money, he replied: "I am waiting for their next steps."
