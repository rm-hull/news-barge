## Commerzbank has welcomed UniCredit's commitments to keep the German lender listed and based in Frankfurt. This comes as Berlin and UniCredit have agreed on key terms of what would be one of Europe's biggest cross-border banking deals in years.

Commerzbank has welcomed commitments from UniCredit to keep its headquarters in Frankfurt and retain its stock market listing, after the Italian lender and the German government reached an agreement in principle on the proposed takeover.

"We take note of the agreement in principle between the German federal government and UniCredit. We welcome the commitments announced by UniCredit," Commerzbank told Euronews in a statement.

The German bank said the agreement included a commitment to expand its business with mid-sized companies and preserve its ability to serve clients internationally. That was one of Berlin's priorities in the talks, along with keeping the bank in Frankfurt and protecting employees' interests.

"Key decisions will continue to be made in Germany. Employee rights and existing agreements are also explicitly recognised," Commerzbank told Euronews. The bank said its management would work with UniCredit to develop a strategy that builds on the strengths of both banks.

Reuters first reported the agreement. Euronews contacted UniCredit and the German finance ministry for further details. Neither had replied by the time of publication.

A tie-up between Italy's UniCredit and Germany's Commerzbank would create a bank with more than €1.3 trillion ($1.4tn) in assets, making it one of Europe's biggest cross-border banking deals in years.

UniCredit began building a stake in Commerzbank in 2024 and announced an unsolicited bid in March 2026. Despite initial resistance from Berlin and Commerzbank, the Italian lender secured access to just under 50% of the voting rights once the tendered shares are transferred.

Berlin softened its stance in July, when Chancellor Friedrich Merz said his government would not block the takeover, although it opposed the way UniCredit had gone about it.

The European Central Bank gave its approval in October. The European Commission and the US Federal Reserve followed on Monday. UniCredit said it expected the final outstanding regulatory approval by the end of November.

The European Commission examined how the takeover would affect banking services in Germany, where UniCredit already operates through its subsidiary HypoVereinsbank. It found that the banks' combined market shares were moderate in most areas. It identified trade finance as an exception but concluded that the deal raised no competition concerns.

The German government has yet to say whether it will sell its 13.3% stake in Commerzbank.
