## Prime Minister Ali Faleh al-Zaidi has given the Iran-backed group until Friday to leave Jurf al-Nasr, as Baghdad faces growing pressure from Washington to bring powerful militias under state control.

Joint Iraqi forces will begin implementing a plan to redeploy security units in the Jurf al-Nasr area (formerly Jurf al-Sakhr) in the north of Babil province on Friday, the day of the government deadline given to Kataib Hezbollah to leave its stronghold before the armed forces enter it expires.

That's according to an informed source, which spoke to the Iraqi News Agency (INA) on Thursday.

According to the source, the redeployment will take place under security arrangements which divide responsibilities between army units, the federal police and the Popular Mobilisation Forces, as well as securing oil transport routes and reopening roads.

The deployment plan, set out in a document issued by the director of the office of the Commander-in-Chief of the Armed Forces, Lieutenant General Abdul Amir al-Shammari, defines the tasks of the security units in the area, based on official letters dated 15 and 16 November 2026.

## **Details of the deployment plan**

Under the plan, the 38th Brigade of the Defence Ministry’s 10th Division will secure the area from the Ya Hussein berm to the Fadhiliya junction, extending to the boundary with Anbar Operations Command.

The Federal Police’s 17th Brigade will take responsibility for central Jurf al-Nasr, covering the area between the Euphrates River to the east and the Musayyib-Fadhiliya road to the west.

Two Popular Mobilisation Forces (PMF) units – the 72nd and 79th Brigades – will secure the area from the Musayyib-Fadhiliya road to the boundary with Karbala Operations Command.

The plan also assigns an Energy Police regiment to protect oil transport routes. Babil police will set up a checkpoint at the Fadhiliya junction towards Musayyib, while Anbar police will establish another towards Ameriyat al-Fallujah.

Authorities will also begin reopening the road linking Musayyib, Bahbahani, Fadhiliya junction and Ameriyat al-Fallujah.

A counter-terrorism regiment stationed at al-Hamiya camp will remain on standby as a reserve force, under the direct authority of the commander-in-chief of the armed forces, to respond to any emergencies.

On Wednesday, PMF chief Faleh al-Fayyad sent a proposal to the prime minister's office calling for PMF units to take responsibility for securing the area. The letter, seen by the Iraqi News Agency (INA), named the 72nd and 79th Brigades and the PMF Operations Command's Special Forces Regiment, with the Energy Police responsible for protecting the oil pipeline.

Al-Fayyad's letter referred to an earlier directive from the commander-in-chief's office, numbered 1954 and dated 16 November 2026.

The Iraqi government has since confirmed that a formal order has been issued to establish a command headquarters in Jurf, following instructions from the commander-in-chief. The headquarters will bring together one army brigade, one Federal Police brigade and two PMF brigades.

It will oversee all security units in the designated area, with the aim of bringing the different forces under a single chain of command.

## **Qaani holds talks as officials seek to contain crisis**

Separately, Iraqi armed group Kataib Hezbollah said on Thursday that its secretary-general, Abu Hussein al-Hamidawi, had met Iran's Quds Force commander Esmail Qaani.

The meeting was also attended by Badr Organisation chief Hadi al-Amiri and PMF leader Faleh al-Fayyad. In a statement reported by Agence France-Presse (AFP), the group said the discussions focused on the crisis, describing their outcome as "good". It did not disclose when or where the meeting took place.

Two Iraqi political sources told AFP on Tuesday that Qaani had also met Prime Minister Ali Faleh al-Zaidi in Baghdad on Monday to discuss tensions surrounding Jurf al-Sakhr.

Qaani took over as head of the Quds Force after his predecessor, Qasem Soleimani, was killed in a US strike near Baghdad airport in January 2020. He has made several visits to Iraq since then, although his trips are rarely officially announced.

## **Oil smuggling allegations fuel dispute over withdrawal**

Tensions between Prime Minister al-Zaidi's government and Kataib Hezbollah have escalated after he ordered the armed forces to enter Jurf al-Sakhr (al-Nasr) and gave the armed group until Friday to withdraw.

The deadline follows an investigation by a government committee which Baghdad says uncovered evidence of oil smuggling in the area.

Government spokesperson Haider al-Aboudi said authorities had drawn up a plan to clear the area of anyone exploiting Iraq's oil wealth, which belongs to the country's people under the constitution.

Kataib Hezbollah has rejected the allegations, accusing the prime minister's office of fabricating the case to force the group to surrender its weapons.

On Tuesday, the faction organised a media tour of Jurf al-Sakhr, attended by several outlets, including Agence France-Presse (AFP).

During the visit, senior figure Abu Taleb al-Saidi insisted the group would not leave the area "even over our dead bodies", while also saying it did not want "bloodshed".

## US pressure mounts over armed factions

The standoff comes as Washington steps up pressure on Baghdad to bring armed groups with close ties to Tehran under government control and disarm them.

Al-Zaidi, who has led the government for more than six months with US backing, pledged to tackle the issue when he took office. While some factions have indicated they are willing to cooperate, Kataib Hezbollah has refused to give up its weapons.

The final deadline for armed groups to hand over their weapons is 30 June 2027.

Jurf al-Nasr, formerly known as Jurf al-Sakhr, lies around 100 kilometres south of Baghdad.

Iraqi forces and Popular Mobilisation units retook the area from the Islamic State group in 2014. It has remained under tight security ever since, with Kataib Hezbollah among the most influential armed factions operating there.

The return of many residents displaced during the fighting has also yet to take place.
