A police officer who shot and killed a 38-year-old Black man on a bike in Madison in July will not face criminal charges, a prosecutor announced Thursday.

Kiel Baitinger-Peterson, a white officer with more than a decade of experience in law enforcement, fatally shot Corey Ruiz on July 22 in a killing that sparked racial justice protests and renewed calls for police reform.

Baitinger-Peterson was one of four officers responding to calls about someone checking out parked cars and an alleged burglary attempt, police said at the time. They surrounded Ruiz, who was on a bicycle.

“I conclude it was reasonable for the officer to use deadly force when this person resisted officers, produced a knife, and was not affected by multiple attempts to use Tasers while officers attempted to take him into custody,” Dane County District Attorney Ismael Ozanne said in a statement announcing his decision.

![Protesters take to the streets after Corey Ruiz was shot and killed by a police officer in Madison, Wisconsin, on July 22](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/19/20/24/GettyImages-2287404398.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The Wisconsin Department of Justice said immediately after the shooting that two officers attempted to use a Taser on Ruiz but weren’t successful. Ruiz “brandished a knife and injured” Baitinger-Peterson, the department said.

Civil rights attorney Ben Crump, who represents the Ruiz family, said in a statement that there was nothing in the videos that the district attorney showed them on Thursday to justify the shooting.

“The medical examiner told the family that if the officer had not fired that shot to his head, Corey would have survived,” Crump said. “It was clearly an excessive use of force. This police killing in Madison, Wisconsin, was unjustifiable, unreasonable, unnecessary, and unconstitutional.”

## Prosecutor shows the Ruiz family video of the shooting

Ozanne said he reached his decision after reviewing evidence that included police reports, diagrams, surveillance videos, police squad car videos and citizen videos. Madison police officers do not wear body cameras.

“In the video you see they tackle him, they tase him, they kick him and then the police officer executes him,” Crump said during a news conference. “The video is the objective unbiased witness here.”

Crump said it is one of the worst excessive force cases that he has worked on.

“When you are shooting a man who is on the ground, three (times). It hits him in his chest and the kill shot hits him in his head,” he said.

![People light candles at a memorial at the intersection where Corey Ruiz was shot and killed](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/19/20/26/GettyImages-2287404738.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Crump said the medical examiner told the Ruiz family he would have survived had the third shot not been fired and hit him in the head. Ozanne said his decision was a correct interpretation of the law.

“A person does not have to like and/or agree with the law, however I am sworn to apply the law to the facts at hand,” the prosecutor said. “The law applies equally to members of law enforcement and to those who are not.”

The victim's brother, Adam Ruiz, told reporters that the prosecutor has “mistreated” his family.

“We’re destroyed,” he said. “We won’t accept this.”

## Days of protests followed the shooting

Baitinger-Peterson has not replied to an email seeking comment.

![The killing sparked days of protests and forced the city to reckon with calls for police reform to address racial justice issues](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/19/20/52/GettyImages-2287389078.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Executive Director Jim Palmer of Wisconsin Professional Police Association, the police union, said in a statement the decision that the officer's use of deadly force was legally justified “is supported by the evidence and consistent with the legal standards governing the use of force by law enforcement officers.”

It was the third time since 2012 that Madison police had shot and killed someone in the same neighborhood. The killing sparked days of protests and forced the city to reckon with calls for police reform to address racial justice issues.

Baitinger-Peterson and the other three officers who interacted with Ruiz that day have been on paid administrative leave since the shooting.

While bystanders captured the confrontation on video, there is no body camera footage because Madison police officers don’t wear them. All Madison police officers could be equipped with body cameras by the end of 2027 thanks to a $350,000 donation from a local nonprofit that city officials announced in September.

The donation will help cover operational expenses, while other costs are being funded through grants and other means.
