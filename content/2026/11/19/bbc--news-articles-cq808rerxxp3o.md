Prosecutors believe upskirting forms part of a wider pattern of sexual offending

**Girls as young as five have been targeted for upskirting, new police figures show.**

Over the past three years, Wales' four forces received 219 complaints from females up to the age of 87, with 18 people charged, results of Freedom of Information (FOI) requests, external reveal.

Upskirting involves taking a photo or video, external beneath someone's clothing - without their knowledge - with the intention of viewing their genitals, buttocks or underwear.

It was made a criminal offence under the Voyeurism (Offences) Act 2019, external and carries a maximum sentence of two years in prison.

The Crown Prosecution Service, external urged people to report incidents immediately, saying those that led to charges almost doubled between 2022 and 2026.

"As well as being an extremely damaging offence in its own right, our prosecutors often see upskirting forming part of a wider pattern of sexual offending," said national lead for sexual offence prosecutions Siobhan Blake.

Between January 2023 and June 2026, Wales' four police forces received 219 complaints, with the highest being 159 in the Dyfed-Powys area and the lowest, six in Gwent, according to the FOI figures.

Where ages were recorded, 11 involved under-10s, 67 came from girls aged 11 to 17, 35 from those 18 to 24, and 12 from over-55s.

From these 219 complaints, nine people were charged in the north Wales force area, six in Dyfed-Powys, three in south Wales, while Gwent had no data.

A previous FOI to Greater Manchester Police, external showed it received 146 reports between 2019 and 2025, leading to 41 arrests and 10 people being charged.

West Midlands Police received 75 reports between 2022 and 2026, with 23 charged, according to another FOI, external.

"I think a lot of the time [boys] think it's a joke or banter, but it's not funny," said student Rowan Stanger, 21.

"Whilst it is illegal, I don't think a lot of people take it seriously."

She was in secondary school when it was made a criminal offence in Wales and England in 2019, and as a teenager, heard about lots of cases of it happening to friends.

"I was wearing shorts under skirts in school, I think that's what was kind of expected even before I really knew why," she added.

"That is still ingrained in me. I'll rarely wear one without."

Rowan is part of a student-led Time to Act, which fights against sexual violence in higher education.

Emily Hasling, 23, is also part of the Cardiff-based campaign group, and said she remembers when she was in university and going to nightclubs, she would only wear jeans.

"Young boys don't see it as sexual assault or sexual violence, because it's not a physical kind of touching element, it's a photo," she said.

"So that's how they see it 'just a photo'."

Another group member Freya Price, 22, described feeling wary on public transport.

"Obviously trains are quite crowded so if I can't stand in a corner I'll feel quite anxious, because I can't guarantee that someone's not going to violate my privacy," she said.

After Dominique Pelicot was caught filming up a woman's skirt in a French supermarket, far more serious crimes were uncovered against his wife, Gisele Pelicot

In September 2020, Dominique Pelicot was caught after a supermarket security guard saw him filming under women's skirts.

It prompted a police search of his electronic devices, which uncovered thousands of files revealing a decade-long scheme to drug and sexually abuse his wife, Gisele.

A former North Wales Police detective is also due to be sentenced later this year after admitting taking pictures beneath the clothing of a child without consent.

Lee Harshey-Jones, 45, was a detective constable and lead investigator in the case of two-year-old Ethan Ives-Griffiths, whose grandparents were jailed for murder last year, and has given evidence in other high-profile murder trials in north Wales.

He pleaded guilty to one charge of voyeurism and one of perverting the course of justice by applying a factory reset to his mobile phone to conceal evidence.

Last month a former supply teacher was jailed for six years after taking more than 100 upskirt photographs of schoolgirls, including in his classroom, across Greater Manchester.

Recently the operator of the popular amusement ride at the Munich Oktoberfest took proactive measures and handed out free cycling shorts to women, after thousands of voyeuristic videos emerged on social media.

Cycling shorts were handed out to women attending Oktoberfest in Munich

Olivia (not her real name), a teenager from south Wales, said she loves wearing skirts but from an early age has been made to feel uncomfortable in them.

She described how boys in primary school had an obsession with "flipping up girls' skirts", which led to them either wearing shorts underneath or switching to trousers.

This was her first experience of feeling unsafe in them, while she was then upskirted on a night out while at university.

"There was a little stage area and I'm dancing, having a great time," Olivia said.

"I look over to my right and someone has got a camera with the flash on underneath my skirt.

"We made eye contact and he walked off. I was frozen and I didn't know if I was imagining it. I didn't know if I could tell someone or who to tell."

She has not felt comfortable enough to wear the same dress again until recently, describing her head as being "on a swivel" every time she was in a club, and feeling like she could not get too drunk.

Libby Squire was murdered after a night out in Hull in January 2019

In 2019, Libby Squire, 21, was raped and murdered by Pawel Relowicz, who was found to have previous convictions for voyeurism and public sex acts.

Libby's mother Lisa Squire has spent the past five years trying to increase the reporting of non-contact sexual offences, launching the It Does Matter campaign in 2024.

"I know first hand how harmful behaviours that are too often dismissed as 'minor' can build into something catastrophic, and I am committed to ensuring no other family experiences what we have," she said.

The NSPCC said its Childline service, external has heard from young people who have had indecent images of them taken and shared.

"They tell us how devastating the abuse can be on their mental and emotional wellbeing, leaving them afraid, ashamed and unsure where to turn," a spokesperson said.

One 16-year-old described how the same boy tripped her up on the way to her seat every day in her tutor group, laughing and looking up her skirt.

"It just freaks me out and I don't know what to do about it," she said.

A mum called to say her daughter, also 16, had heard a rumour a boy in her year had taken a photograph up her skirt, and shared it with his friends.

She added: "The thought alone is incredibly upsetting for her."

The Children's Commissioner for England said Children's Commissioner Dame Rachel de Souza said: "The fact a number of teachers have admitted or been found guilty of upskirting offences, some in their own classrooms, is particularly shocking.

"Teachers and other school professionals have a clear responsibility to keep children safe, so staff must be alert to the signs of sexual harassment and abuse and ensure all children know where they can turn for help and support if an incident occurs."
