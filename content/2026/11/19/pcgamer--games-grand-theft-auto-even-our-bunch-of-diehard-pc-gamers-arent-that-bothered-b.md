For a bunch of performance snobs, we are a surprisingly forgiving bunch at PC Gamer. Yes, if you try to insult XCOM, you're going to get a slap, but we've only just started playing GTA 6 (well, some of us) and we're mostly over the whole 'only runs at 30 fps' thing already.

It turns out, while we're happy to part with our money to buy expensive components to hit higher frame rates (and earn the right to brag about it), we're also just as happy to settle for less when a game does a lot of other things exceptionally well.

GTA 6 certainly does that. That's the impression I get from most members of the team that have played it so far. Though if Rockstar is listening, when you do get around to our precious PC version, we'd love nothing more than 4K60—maybe more.

## James Bentley, Hardware Writer

![PC Gamer headshots (James Bentley)](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/nXJvUmhon9WghwJuvDVoHR-886-80.png)

I will no doubt appreciate frames above 100 fps when GTA 6 comes to PC. Heck, I'd even just appreciate going up to 60 fps. But in my handful of hours with the game thus far, I just have simply not been that bothered by it. Be it smart ways of masking low frames, or even just the slower, methodical control approach of the game, I'm less frustrated with my PS5 running the game at around 30 fps than I was recently playing Phantom Blade Zero at well above 40 fps on a handheld PC.

Maybe that's down to the world having a little bit of magic to it, maybe it's the engrossing tone and atmosphere, maybe it's just because it looks so darn pretty. Maybe it's because I haven't got to the game's most hectic moments yet and haven't *really* needed to hit a headshot with my console unable to keep up with me. I'll tell you how I'm getting on in another fifty hours. Cool, see you in three days, then?

![Screenshots of Jason in GTA 6 on the Florida waterside.](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/GgdsawGpbSzorU9FDRha5E-1200-80.jpg)

## Fraser Brown, Online Editor

![PC Gamer headshot - Fraser Brown](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/o5oYVAFnHjesaVgoJsQD9E-886-80.png)

I was dreading playing GTA 6 on my OG PS5—a dusty ornament that's been chilling out in my living room this last year doing absolutely nothing. I've got a nice gaming PC in my office (RTX 4090, Intel i9 13900K, 32 GB RAM), so it's been a long time since I've had to deal with games dipping below 80 fps. I have been spoiled.

The second I got to control Lucia, my fears seemed justified. Everything just felt sluggish. But that feeling didn't last. I played GTA 6 for around 5 hours last night (I am very tired) and I haven't thought about the frame rate once since that initial section. Whether I'm getting into shootouts with coke smugglers, speeding through the Keys at night, or bouncing around at sea, GTA 6 looks great and feels great.

> I can still tell this isn't 60 fps, but it is smooth.
>
> Fraser Brown

I can still tell this isn't 60 fps, but it is smooth. I'm sure I've encountered drops, but crucially I haven't noticed them. Sure, it's inevitably going to look better and run better on PC, but the lower performance on my PS5 quickly ceased to be an issue, and I'm not regretting playing it on console.

## Phil Savage, Global Editor-in-Chief

![PC Gamer headshot - Phil Savage](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/4sHLcbB96zPH9vjoBAiMYC-886-80.png)

GTA 6 is the kind of game where an uncapped framerate would be nice, but it doesn't feel essential. Like Red Dead Redemption 2 before it—a game I also played at 30 fps before it launched on PC—its pacing is measured. It feels designed around the limitations of the consoles it's launching on, prioritising detail and depth over twitch-based combat and precise interaction. It's exactly what I'd expect from a modern Rockstar game. Also like Red Dead Redemption 2 before it, I still feel the sense of longing for what this game will look like on PC. Make no mistake: the version currently available is a preview for what GTA 6 will be at its best. It's great, but those who can wait will be treated to a superior experience.

It's also possible that the 30 fps cap will feel more restrictive once the online mode arrives—whenever that will be, and in whatever form it takes. It's one thing to handle a lower framerate within Rockstar's meticulously created campaign. It's quite another in a lobby of other players. If GTA 6's multiplayer is even half as chaotic as the current GTA Online, every frame is going to count.

## Jacob Ridley, Managing Editor, Hardware

![PC Gamer&#039;s Managing Editor, Hardware, Jacob Ridley on a colourful background and with effect applied.](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/2YNigoLXbckPdRPDe3stwA-886-80.png)

As I was booting up GTA 6, I was expecting to be annoyed by the game's paltry frame rate. And I was—at first. Much like Fraser, during the opening scene with Lucia, I saw my fears come to life on the big screen in front of me. Objects juddering and blurring as I swung my camera around in the cramped cell. Did I just waste £80 on GTA 6 on PS5 when I should've held my nerve for the PC version?

Truth is, the frame rate has had nowhere near as much of a negative impact on my experience while I've been traversing the Floridian wilds as Jason. In part, I think that's because there's a lot more going on, as cars, shops, people, and poultry fly past me as I break most state, federal and ethical laws. That reduces the chance of my eyes fixating on any one object and noticing the adverse effects of the game's lower fps, or the ways Rockstar has gone about covering for it.

> Low fps is one thing, but motion blur is the devil.
>
> Jacob Ridley

Low fps is one thing, but motion blur is the devil. That's not just my opinion, that's been scientifically verified (sorta) to be the opinion of PC Gamer readers. Admittedly, some motion blur may be required when the frame rate is this low, so I have played around with a few settings in GTA 6 to find the perfect balance. I'd heard through the grapevine that higher-than-default motion blur (default is 3, goes up to 10) may be preferential to reduce the more obvious juddering that is present at low fps, but whoever says that is no friend of mine. I've landed on setting motion blur to 1 on the standard PS5, as it feels the cleanest overall. I wouldn't blame anyone for turning it off entirely, especially on a more powerful console where the frame rate may not dip as much.

Of course, I do agree with the rest of this lot: in an ideal world, playing the PC version, I'd be pushing a much higher frame rate with motion blur banished to the abyss where it belongs, and a full selection of graphics settings to tinker with to my liking. Yet, to my surprise, I'm not as much of a console version curmudgeon as I thought I'd be.

Is the low frame rate noticeable? Yep. Would it be better at a higher frame rate? Also yep. Is the low frame rate a detriment to my overall enjoyment of GTA 6? Only a smidge.

## Mollie Taylor, Features Producer

![PC Gamer headshot - Mollie Taylor](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/W9VNF2qWSreZXDkwcVR2tF-886-80.png)

I don't consider myself much of a framerate snob, but I'll admit I was side-eyeing GTA 6's 30fps *just a little bit.* Well, that was before the game came out. Now that I have my hands on it, with roughly six hours spent lollygagging around Leonida? I'm hardly noticing it, though I'll most definitely be appreciating a smoother experience once the PC version rolls around.

Like Jacob said, motion blur definitely seems to be masking it somewhat—and I am traditionally a motion blur hater, so it's taken a little getting used to—but I'm genuinely pleasantly surprised at the balance Rockstar has managed to strike between a smooth performance and a dense, rich world.

## Christopher Livingston, Senior Editor

![PC Gamer headshot - Christopher Livingston](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/wC7aUjGb8pW4neHoNS25gF-886-80.png)

GTA 6 taught me that I don't much care about frame rates as much as I expected. I got to start playing it a few weeks ago and didn't have anyone to compare notes with, and my first thought was: *Huh, they got it to run at 60 fps after all!* It all felt very smooth, even when I realized it was 30 fps after all. It was only a bit later I started noticing very occasional drops, like while driving during a gradual lighting chance from day to night.

> I think it being consistent is key.
>
> Christopher Livingston

But that's really infrequent. I think it being consistent is key. I'm on a PS5 Slim, by the way, not a Pro. That's not to say I still can't wait to see it at higher frame rates when it finally gets to PC. But 30 fps feels mostly fine for the time being.

## Dave James, EIC Hardware

![Dave James - Human](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/Ti9gYoetCsh9crRvpUzKD9-886-80.png)

It was actually spending a lot of time playing with handheld gaming PCs and laptops with integrated graphics that reminded me I shouldn't give as much of a shit about frame rate as I do. Largely that obsession is born of having high-end hardware sat in my desktop PC, but when crashing around with a range of handhelds I'm reminded of the old days when 30 fps was a target for PC games, too.

It's also reminded me that it really depends on the game engine, as well. Not every 30 fps game looks the same as other 30 fps games. And Rockstar has obviously worked incredibly hard to make sure that 30 fps looks as good as it possible can. Otherwise why would that be a solid target for all four of the console platforms it's released on?

> For the most part Rockstar has done a great job of keeping things smooth.
>
> Dave James

I've been playing GTA 6 on the Xbox Series X, which does suffer from the odd dip in frame rate, too—though oddly I've only really noticed it during canned animations the characters do starting certain actions, such as opening the trunk of a car or pulling something out of it. But for the most part Rockstar has done a great job of keeping things smooth. And not just by using motion blur. For the record, I'm running that at 0 and have not looked back.

I'm less happy about the vignetting that I'm getting around the darkened edges of the screen, but I'm playing it blown up on a 4K projector so I've got plenty of screen real estate to enjoy. And I have been, thoroughly enjoying it, I mean. The frame rate, for me, hasn't got in the way once.
