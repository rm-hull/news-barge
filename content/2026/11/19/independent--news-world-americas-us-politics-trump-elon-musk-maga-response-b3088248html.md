Donald Trump responded after Elon Musk declared that he’s “not MAGA” and the tech mogul expressed confusion about the ideals of the Trump-led movement.

“He’s a friend of mine,” Trump told NBC News Thursday, while returning from his trip to Asia. “What can I tell you? I mean, you have to ask — there are lots of definitions out there.”

“MAGA, to me, means something very specific, and when people veer from it, they don’t do very well,” the Republican president added. “But Elon is — Elon’s got very good intentions. I mean, he was with me the whole — he traveled with me.”

Musk, the billionaire CEO of SpaceX and Tesla, made his remarks during a remote interview with *The New York Times*’ Andrew Ross Sorkin Wednesday.

The sit-down came three weeks after the midterm elections, when Democrats recaptured control of both the House and the Senate, paving the way for two years of divided government. Musk donated millions to back MAGA candidates and once proclaimed himself “dark, gothic MAGA” as he backed Trump and his presidential machine.

![President Donald Trump responded Thursday after Elon Musk distanced himself from the MAGA movement](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/19/20/57/GettyImages-2305779156.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“I’m not MAGA,” said the GOP mega-donor and former head of the Department of Government Efficiency, Trump’s cost-cutting initiative.

“Well at least I’m not like, I’m not sure what MAGA means,” Musk continued. “If that means sort of blind adherence to a set of policies by the Republican Party, then I certainly don’t have that. I think there are positives and negatives in both parties.”

The South African-born billionaire also expressed disappointment with his decision to bankroll Republican candidates in the midterms to the tune of nearly $500 million.

He said he felt “pretty foolish about having spent so much money and not seeing much for it — or maybe it would have been much worse without that.”

On Election Day, Democrats romped to victory in a string of red states, amid voter frustration over the Iran war, cost of living, gas prices and government corruption. As of Thursday, the party had won 56 Senate seats to Republicans’ 44 and 257 House seats to the GOP’s 172.

![Last year, Musk became one of the most prominent and powerful figures in Trump’s second administration, when the president tapped him to spearhead DOGE](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/19/20/22/GettyImages-2217853025.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

By distancing himself from Trump’s decade-old movement, Musk has dramatically reversed course from his prior statements and actions. At a rally in 2024, he declared, “I'm not just MAGA, I'm dark MAGA.” In the past, he’s also been spotted wearing hats that read: “Make America Great Again.”

Last year, the Tesla CEO became one of the most prominent and powerful figures in Trump’s second administration, when the president tapped him to spearhead DOGE, which slashed federal jobs and budgets.

Trump and Musk had a public falling out this past summer, but the pair appear to have patched things up since then. The businessman has met with the president at the White House several times in recent months and traveled with him to China in May.

Democrats greeted Musk’s latest remarks with skepticism, saying his effort to distance himself from MAGA would not derail their plans to investigate him and other Trump allies when they retake control of Congress in January.

Arizona Senator Ruben Gallego responded bluntly in an X post, writing to Musk: “You are still getting a subpoena.”
