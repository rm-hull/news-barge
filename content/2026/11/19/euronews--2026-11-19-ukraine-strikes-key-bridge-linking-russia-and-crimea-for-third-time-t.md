## The Russian transport ministry said on Thursday that no one was wounded in the attack and that train services across it had been temporarily halted.

Ukrainian forces struck the bridge connecting Russia to the occupied Crimean peninsula again on Thursday, less than two days after a previous strike collapsed an entire section of the crossing.

It’s the third time in a week the key transport artery had been targeted.

“Ukrainian Neptune missiles struck the railway section of the Crimean Bridge, with hits recorded near the arch and on the tracks. The extent of the damage is still being assessed, but train traffic has stopped,” Ukraine’s Ministry of Defence wrote in a post on X.

“For Russia, the bridge is a conveyor belt for its war, carrying fuel tank cars and ammunition wagons to its forces in the south. That conveyor has broken down.”

On Tuesday, Ukraine had struck the bridge with missiles and drones, severing the road section in two.

The 19-kilometre road-and-rail link is a key symbolic target for Ukraine and has been so throughout Russia’s full-scale invasion.

Sergei Aksyonov, the Moscow-appointed head of Crimea, said railway traffic would be suspended until the afternoon following the latest attack.

"Our railroad workers are making heroic efforts to deal with the aftermath of the enemy attack," Aksyonov said.

The Russian transport ministry said on Thursday that no one was wounded in the attack and that train services across it had been temporarily halted.

Road traffic has been impossible since the Tuesday attack completely destroyed a section of the highway.

Thursday's strike came just hours before a historic meeting between Russian President Vladimir Putin and his Chinese and American counterparts, Xi Jinping and Donald Trump, in China.

The bridge is seen as a symbol of Putin's rule and Russia's illegal annexation of Crimea, and functions as a key supply route for the region.

It has been struck several times, including in an October 2022 blast in which an explosive-packed truck was detonated on the bridge, causing months of disruption.

Russia annexed Crimea in 2014, one of the opening moves in what became a deadly war between Kyiv and Russian-backed separatists in the east of the country, ahead of Moscow launching its full-scale offensive in 2022.

## **Refineries hit**

Meanwhile, Ukraine also struck two oil refineries in southern Russia, Ukrainian officials said, as President Volodymyr Zelenskyy praised the effectiveness of domestically-produced weapons used to damage the Kerch Bridge.

Ukraine’s General Staff said the strikes hit storage tanks at the Ilsky refinery and a processing unit at the Afipsky refinery, both in Russia’s Krasnodar region.

The two refineries, also targeted on Sunday night, can together process around 95 million barrels of oil a year and supply Russian forces on the front lines in southern and eastern Ukraine, it said.

Russian officials reported attacks on two industrial facilities in the region but gave no details.

Ukraine has repeatedly targeted refineries as it seeks to hurt Russia’s oil sector, a mainstay of its economy, and Moscow presses on with the invasion of its neighbour.

“Our technologies and companies are developing very well,” Zelenskyy said in a social media post.

And Russian forces have focused on knocking out power in Ukraine before the winter, aiming to erode the morale of civilians.

Attacks last month killed at least 372 Ukrainian civilians and wounded 1,981 others, a number 95% higher than in October 2025, the UN Human Rights Monitoring Mission in Ukraine said on social media.

This year, the war has brought “significantly higher” civilian casualties than in previous years, the agency said.
