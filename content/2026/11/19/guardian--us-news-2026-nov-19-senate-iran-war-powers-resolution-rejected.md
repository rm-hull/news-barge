The Senate on Thursday rejected a war powers resolution aimed at forcing Donald Trump to halt fighting with Iran, with Republicans once again defending a conflict that played a major role in their sweeping midterm election losses.

The House of Representatives had approved the resolution in September, and it was the first to be voted on by either chamber since the 3 November vote in which the GOP lost control of both chambers of Congress beginning next year.

The measure failed in a 48-51 vote that broke largely along party lines, with John Fetterman of Pennsylvania the sole Democrat to oppose it, and Susan Collins of Maine, Rand Paul of Kentucky and Lisa Murkowski of Alaska the only Republicans to vote in favor.

Democrats have introduced a steady stream of war powers resolutions since the war began in February with a joint US-Israel attack on Iran. While Trump’s Republican allies have succeeded in voting many of them down, one passed both chambers in June.

However Trump ignored that resolution, prompting Democrats to continue their efforts to make use of the 1973 law to force an end to a conflict that polls indicate is unpopular with the public and has caused prices for gasoline and diesel to increase across the US.

All House Democrats along with seven Republicans – several of whom lost re-election in the midterms – had supported the measure that came up for consideration before the Senate on Thursday. But Republicans largely stood behind the conflict, including Thom Tillis, a North Carolina senator, who is retiring at the end of the year after supporting previous resolutions, and Bill Cassidy, an outgoing senator of Louisiana, whose support was crucial for the June measure’s passage.

Chris Murphy, a Democratic senator, missed the vote, after recently suffering a stroke.
