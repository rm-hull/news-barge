Kemi Badenoch has stood by her suggestion that because a double amputee can run marathons, more disabled people should be in the workforce.

The Conservative Party leader faced criticism from Prime Minister Andy Burnham for the comments, who described them as “crass and insensitive” and called on her to withdraw them.

Meanwhile, the Paralympian in question, Richard Whitehead, claimed she was “weaponising his sporting success” to justify her proposed £36 billion of welfare cuts.

Appearing on BBC Radio 4’s Today programme, Ms Badenoch was asked whether she accepted the comments were insensitive.

She replied: “No, no, no. I do not accept it. I will speak the truth, and I will say what I believe is common sense.

“What I know is that if people can work, then we should encourage them to work. What I’m saying is actually a message of hope that if people even with significant problems can work, then those who have ADHD and mild anxiety should go to work.”

![Kemi Badenoch said she stands by the comments she made during a speech in the House of Commons](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/19/09/78fadf462dca2049cdfb8bd27bcefb75Y29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzk1MTY2ODM1-2.86530367.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The Conservative leader acknowledged she had used “extreme examples to illustrate things”, and added: “But if someone can do that, then yes, your condition of ADHD or anxiety is mild. We didn’t have a situation where people with such low-level lying conditions were going on disability.”

Ms Badenoch suggested BBC presenter Nick Robinson was engaging in “faux outrage”.

She said she was not prepared to take part in a debate about “language and tone policing”.

She added: “I will say things as they are. People may disagree with things that I say, and that’s fine; this is a free country, people are free to disagree, but they know where I’m coming from.

“I start from first principles: If you can work, you should work, and the Conservative Party is going to help you get a job. We’re not going to destroy them. We’re going to get Britain working again.”

The row followed a speech earlier this week in which the Opposition leader set out the reasoning behind her proposed cuts to disability benefits.

She told her audience: “The reality is that millions of people can and do contribute to our economy, to their families, to their communities, even when they face serious challenges.

“Double amputees run marathons. People who are housebound do wonderful creative work from home. People with autism run some of the biggest tech companies in the world.”

![Prime Minister Andy Burnham called the comments ‘crass and insensitive’](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/19/13/8063b13249ca3fb5d20d37adad5a59d4Y29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzk1MTgxNDg2-2.86528571.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Mr Whitehead later said he was “gobsmacked that somebody’s sporting success would be weaponised in this way”, *The Guardian* reported.

He added: “To say everybody should work because double-leg amputees run marathons, I was like, wow. It’s very divisive language.

“I understand the welfare system needs a shake-up, but you shouldn’t be blanket-taking money away from individuals who need it; it’s their lifeline. There are families out there relying on the support.”

The dispute reached the Commons on Wednesday, when Labour MP Charlotte Nichols raised it at Prime Minister’s Questions.

She asked Andy Burnham whether he agreed with Mr Whitehead that “the achievements of our sportsmen and women with disabilities should never be weaponised against others”.

Mr Burnham said: “I thought the comments by Richard Whitehead do deserve a response from the Right Honourable Lady (Ms Badenoch).

“They were crass and insensitive, and I think she should withdraw them.”

![Athlete Richard Whitehead celebrates after finishing the New York City Marathon](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/19/14/2026-11-01T184706Z_827468298_UP1EMB11G6GH0_RTRMADP_3_ATHLETICS-NYC-MARATHON.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Mr Whitehead has run more than 100 marathons, set world records and won four Paralympic medals.

After Ms Badenoch’s speech, he said on social media: “Disability should never be judged through isolated examples of individual achievement, but through an understanding of the barriers people and families face every day.

“The fact that some double-leg amputees can run marathons does not mean that disability support is unnecessary.

“Equally, the fact that some people with ADHD thrive despite limited support does not mean that others do not need meaningful assistance…

“Support should be based on people’s actual needs, not on comparisons with someone else’s achievements.”

A Labour Party spokesperson said: “Kemi Badenoch’s Conservatives neglected a whole generation of young people – leaving one million not in education, employment or training. That’s a scandal that Labour won’t allow to continue.

“The Tories’ own shadow work and pensions secretary has admitted Badenoch’s plan for crude cuts would rip away support from half a million disabled children, pushing vulnerable families into hardship. At the same time, they want to cut taxes for the wealthiest in society. That’s truly shameful.

“Andy Burnham’s Labour Government will deliver a new settlement for young people. We’ll support them into work by investing in their success, so everyone has a fair chance to get on.”
