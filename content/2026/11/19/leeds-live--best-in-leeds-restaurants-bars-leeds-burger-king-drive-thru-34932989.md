# Leeds Burger King drive-thru gets green light to serve until 5am

## Leeds City Council granted a late-night refreshment licence and planning variation for its drive-thru service

A Burger King branch has been granted permission by the council to operate its drive-thru facility until 5am.

The fast food operator applied to extend the hours during which motorists can place orders at Kirkstall Retail Park. The company already held authorisation to run its indoor restaurant and drive-thru from 7am until midnight.

Leeds City Council approved a late night refreshment licence for food orders between 11pm and 5am. Burger King also required a variation to its planning permission to operate the Savins Mill Way drive-thru during those hours.

A council report noted there were worries about noise pollution and additional traffic if extended opening was permitted.

However, it stated: "The restaurant occupies a commercial town-centre location adjoining Savins Mill Way and the A65. It is surrounded by retail units and a petrol filling station, with no residential properties in close proximity."

The opening hours of the interior premises, near retailers Matalan, Boots and B&M and Morrisons, would remain unchanged, Burger King confirmed.

There were also apprehensions regarding possible anti-social behaviour and vehicles queuing overnight.

The report added: "From 11pm, however, the internal restaurant would close and sales would be restricted to the drive-thru."

The current premises licence mandated the monitoring of external areas and customers being requested to depart quietly.

Burger King received approval to construct the drive-thru near busy Kirkstall Road in 2024. A council planning panel initially voted to turn down the proposal following concerns raised over road safety.

It was ultimately approved that October after planning officers concluded that the impact on traffic was insufficient grounds for refusal.

The application to amend the planning permission stated that measures were already in place to tackle anti-social behaviour, including CCTV surveillance and an incident log.

Burger King's planning report stated: "Notices will also be displayed within the restaurant and premises to request that patrons respect the needs of local residents and leave the area quietly, which will be overseen by restaurant staff."
