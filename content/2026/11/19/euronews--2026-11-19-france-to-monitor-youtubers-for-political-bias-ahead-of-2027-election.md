## France will hold its presidential election on 18 April and 2 May next year, with far-right leader Marine Le Pen currently leading in opinion polls. Parliamentary elections are expected to follow in June.

France's media regulator, Arcom, announced on Thursday that it will monitor online influencers and content creators for political bias ahead of next year's presidential election, marking a major expansion of its powers.

The change reflects the growing influence of YouTubers and other content creators, whose audiences, particularly among young people, can sometimes be larger than those of traditional media outlets.

"There's a new phenomenon, it’s here and it's massive," Arcom president Martin Ajdari announced at a press conference in Paris.

Under Arcom's rules, presidential candidates must receive airtime proportionate to their projected electoral support during the initial phase of the campaign. However, from 5 to 30 April, all candidates must be given equal airtime.

Ajdari stressed that Arcom would only monitor major French video content creators, while audio-only podcasters would not be affected.

"This issue arises in very concrete terms for creators whose audiences exceed those of traditional television channels," Ajdari added.

France has been at the forefront of efforts to regulate online content, passing legislation in 2023 that introduced potential prison sentences and fines for influencers who fail to disclose paid promotions or advertise fraudulent products.
