## Kremlin critic Bill Browder tells Euronews that a weakened Vladimir Putin is using peace talks to buy time, hoping a far-right victory in France will fracture European support for Ukraine.

Russian President Vladimir Putin is playing for time in Ukraine in the hope that France’s far-right leader Marine Le Pen wins next year’s presidential election and undermines European support for Kyiv, Kremlin critic Bill Browder has told Euronews.

Speaking to Europe Today as the Coalition of the Willing gathers in Berlin to discuss security guarantees for Ukraine, Browder said Putin was counting on political shifts in Europe to achieve what he had failed to secure on the battlefield.

A victory for Le Pen could jeopardise French military and financial assistance to Ukraine and trigger a wider fragmentation of European support, Browder suggested.

"If she were to win and follow through on her promise to end French aid for Ukraine, she might also intervene to try to end European aid for Ukraine. If that were to happen, then he buys himself the biggest victory he could ever have," he said.

Browder, who has spent more than two decades campaigning against the Kremlin, argued that Putin was in a considerably weaker position than when he first began exposing corruption within the Russian regime.

“He’s in a very weak position today,” Browder said, pointing to the military losses and economic damage Russia has suffered since launching its full-scale invasion of Ukraine.

However, he argued that Putin needed the conflict to distract ordinary Russians, giving him little incentive to end the fighting. "He has no interest in peace for one simple reason: he needs the war in order to distract the people of Russia from his own failings," he said.

Browder was also sceptical about the diplomatic manoeuvring surrounding the peace process, suggesting that Putin’s priority was to prolong negotiations until the political circumstances became more favourable to Moscow, rather than make the concessions necessary to end the war.
