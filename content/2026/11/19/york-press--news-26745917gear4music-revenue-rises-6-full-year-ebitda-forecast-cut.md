York-based Gear4music has reported growth in revenue and gross profit.

The online retailer of musical instruments and equipment announced a 6 per cent rise in revenue to £85.4 million for the six months ending September 30, up from £80.7 million during the same period last year.

UK revenue grew by 3 per cent to £51 million, while European and rest-of-world sales saw an 11 per cent increase to £34.4 million.

Andrew Wass, executive chair of Gear4music, said: "Building on the significant progress achieved in FY26, we delivered further revenue and gross profit growth in the first half of FY27 despite the challenges facing discretionary consumer spending.




"Trading in FY27 Q3 to date has been affected by lower paid-search traffic, which we believe reflects changes in the presentation of search results and evolving customer search habits as AI becomes more prominent.

"The expansion of advertising within AI-powered search services is creating additional opportunities to reach customers, and we are making good progress in increasing the visibility of our websites across these platforms, positioning us to benefit as search behaviours continue to evolve.

"We remain confident in the group’s long-term prospects and will continue to invest in marketing, technology and logistics to support profitable growth."

Gross profit increased by 4 per cent to £23.6 million, with a gross margin of 27.7 per cent.

EBITDA fell from £6.9 million to £5.7 million, reflecting setup costs for the company's new automated UK distribution centre.

Operating profit dropped to £2.1 million from £3.4 million, while profit before tax declined to £1 million from £2.7 million.

The company reported net bank debt of £24.3 million, up from £16 million, attributed to investment in the new distribution centre and seasonal inventory build-up.

The company noted that Q3 revenue to date is down 13 per cent year-on-year, which it attributes to reduced paid-search traffic and changes in online search behaviour, including the rise of AI-generated results.

Despite the dip, revenue remains 19 per cent ahead of the same period in FY25.

Other-brand sales have also been impacted by the strengthening of sterling against the euro, leading to short-term pricing pressures from European competitors.

The company now expects full-year EBITDA to be around £11.5 million, below current market expectations.

Gear4music said the first phase of its new automated UK warehouse is now operational ahead of the peak trading period.

The company plans to issue a trading update on January 19, 2027, following the Christmas trading period.

Consensus market expectations before this update were for full-year revenues of £200.2 million, EBITDA of £16 million, and profit before tax of £6 million.
