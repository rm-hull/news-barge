Fiona, the world-famous hippo who won hearts across the globe after surviving a premature birth, has died at Cincinnati Zoo 11 days after swallowing a visitor’s knit hat.

The zoo’s animal care team had been caring for Fiona around the clock, attempting to help her digest and pass the hat. Staff kept Fiona hydrated, offered her special treats, and closely monitored her movements.

In an attempt to assist her digestive system, caregivers even borrowed a vibration platform from a nearby horse farm and encouraged her to stand on it several times each day in the hope that the motion would help move the object through her body.

“It is with extreme sadness that we share with you the devastating news that Fiona did not make it after multiple attempts from our amazing vets and keeper team to help her through what ended up being a medical emergency,” the zoo wrote on Thursday afternoon.

![Fiona the hippo has died 11 days after swallowing a zoo visitor’s hat](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/19/21/20/Fiona-(2).jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“The vet staff tried everything they could to help her after she ingested a hat that ended up in her habitat by accident last week. Despite multiple attempts to move the hat out of her system, it was ultimately too much to overcome.

“The team performed necessary supportive care today in an attempt to save her, but it was not successful and she did not recover. We know everyone in the community will be crushed by this news, as we all are.

“Please keep her keepers and everyone who tried to help her in your thoughts as they try to navigate and come to terms with this shocking news. They are also, understandably, asking for space at this time.”

Cincinnati Zoo director Dave Jenike added: “We are in shock at the moment. We did not expect this outcome. She fought until she couldn’t anymore.”

## Hat flung into Fiona’s enclosure

Fiona swallowed the hat 11 days ago after a visitor tossed it in the air, which caused it to get caught in a ceiling fan. The fan flung the hat into a narrow gap above the exhibit’s viewing windows, into her enclosure.

![The zoo’s animal care team had been caring for Fiona around the clock, attempting to help her digest and pass the hat](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/19/11/57/Fiona-health-update.png?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Zoo spokesperson Michelle Curley said it was entirely accidental.

Veterinarians sedated Fiona twice to monitor her condition, according to Mike Wenninger, the zoo’s director of animal health. An ultrasound on Tuesday showed intestinal bunching, signaling that her body was attempting to push the obstruction through, while an examination using a nine-foot endoscope failed to locate the item.

"Her care team even offered her a sniff of coffee, a scent she has always liked," Curley said Wednesday in an update. "And they were happy to see her respond."

The zoo had said that surgery wasn’t an option in adult hippopotamuses. Jack Kottwitz, assistant professor at Michigan State University’s College of Veterinary Medicine, explained that their complex digestive systems make such procedures extremely complicated.

![Fiona rose to fame in January 2017 when she was born six weeks premature](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/19/21/36/17103539_10154894796575479_8707537426280339718_n.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

"They have a multichambered stomach for digesting plants — sort of like a cow — but they don’t regurgitate and chew cud like a cow does,” Kottwitz said. “Those multiple chambers, tissue folds, fluid, and digesting food easily ‘hide’ the hat, making removal with an endoscope from the stomach chambers very difficult.”

## Fiona’s rise to fame

Fiona would have been 10 years old this January. She inspired millions with her story of survival and raised awareness and love for her species during her short life.

She rose to fame in January 2017 when she was born six weeks premature. At birth, she weighed just 29 pounds (13.2 kilograms), 25 pounds (11.3 kilograms) below the lowest birth weight ever recorded for her species.

Her progress was closely followed by fans and her popularity grew to the point that books were written about her. She also inspired a beer line, an ice cream flavor and all kinds of clothing. One couple even got engaged in front of her exhibit, with Fiona appearing in the photo from underwater behind them.

“Fiona was such a gift,” said Cincinnati Zoo Executive Chair of the Board Thane Maynard. “I have never known an animal so beloved in all my life. From the day she was born, she was like lightning in a bottle. And, she will be remembered and beloved many years from now.”
