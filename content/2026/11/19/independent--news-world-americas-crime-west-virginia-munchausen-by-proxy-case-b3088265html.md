A West Virginia mother has been charged with felony child neglect after investigators said she fabricated her toddler’s inability to eat, prompting nearly 175 medical visits and an unnecessary abdominal surgery.

The mother, Emilee Guzman, was charged with child neglect resulting in serious bodily injury following an investigation by the Monongalia County Sheriff’s Office into conditions at her home in Morgantown, West Virginia. Two other adults living in the house — her husband, Milton Alexander Guzman Cisneros, and Caitlin Jean Meredith — were each charged with two counts of child neglect creating a risk of bodily injury.

A child abuse pediatrician at WVU Medicine Children’s diagnosed the 2-year-old’s case as medical child abuse, a form of abuse more widely known as Munchausen syndrome by proxy, the sheriff’s office said.

The investigation began in early October after authorities received reports of inconsistencies in the toddler’s medical history and concerns about the welfare of two children living at the residence on Jones Avenue.

According to criminal complaints and police statements, detectives reviewed extensive medical records and found that the 2-year-old had been taken to roughly 175 doctor appointments in just over two years. During those visits, Guzman repeatedly told physicians that the child refused or was unable to eat and suffered from avoidant/restrictive food intake disorder, known as ARFID.

![Emilee Guzman faces a felony count of child neglect resulting in serious bodily injury after investigators accused her of faking her toddler’s illnesses](https://static.the-independent.com/2026/11/19/21/33/Emilee-Guzman.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Investigators also said Guzman told friends and relatives that the child had rare medical conditions, including microgastria, a congenital abnormality in which the stomach is abnormally small.

Relying on those accounts, a pediatric surgeon performed a procedure on October 1 to place a gastrostomy tube, or G-tube, directly into the toddler’s stomach.

When detectives interviewed the surgeon on October 27, he told them he would have pursued less invasive treatments had he been given accurate information about the child’s diet, according to official reports. The surgeon noted that both the insertion and removal of a feeding tube could be painful and could expose a child to serious medical complications.

![Milton Alexander Guzman Cisneros faces two counts of child neglect creating a risk of bodily injury following an investigation into living conditions and child welfare at a Morgantown residence](https://static.the-independent.com/2026/11/19/21/11/Milton-Alexander-Guzman-Cisneros.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

In an October 13 interview with investigators, Guzman acknowledged that she had knowingly withheld information from doctors regarding how much the toddler was actually eating, court documents state. She told authorities she had misrepresented the child’s eating habits because she feared medical providers would suspect she was not properly caring for the toddler and would alert Child Protective Services.

After the child was removed from Guzman’s custody, foster caregivers observed the toddler eating a wide variety of foods without difficulty and without using the feeding tube, officials said. Caregivers also reported that the child appeared uncomfortable with the device and resisted attempts to clean around it.

![Caitlin Jean Meredith faces two counts of child neglect creating a risk of bodily injury after deputies executed a search warrant at a Morgantown home](https://static.the-independent.com/2026/11/19/21/30/Caitlin-Meredith.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

During the course of the investigation, deputies executed a search warrant at the Morgantown residence and documented severe sanitation issues. Official reports described accumulated trash, scattered food, cockroaches, a heavy infestation of flies and gnats, and dried feces believed to be from cats and possibly humans. Investigators expressed particular alarm that a toddler recovering from abdominal surgery was living in an environment that posed a high risk of infection to the surgical site.

Detectives also recovered digital messages between Guzman and Meredith that detailed mistreatment of a 9-year-old living in the home, including mockery over incontinence, demands that the child clean the house, and threats and descriptions of physical punishment, according to court records.

The sheriff’s office declined to release further details about the two children to protect their privacy.
