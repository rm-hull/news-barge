## MEPs are calling on the European Commission to “ease” the regulatory burden on EU companies and deploy “horizontal” trade defence tools to tackle Chinese competition.

The EU needs a “systematic” approach to China that goes beyond its current sector-by-sector trade strategy, according to a cross-party coalition of MEPs.

In a letter to European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen and EU Trade Commissioner Maroš Šefčovič, the MEPs warn against pursuing more deals like the one the Commission struck with Beijing in October to curb imports of Chinese hybrid cars.

“Carving out single-sector export limits cannot substitute for both, faster regulatory simplification and comprehensive defence of Europe’s industrial baseline against systematic market disruption,” reads the letter, initiated by Slovak MEP Miriam Lexmann (EPP).

The EU faces a trade deficit with China of €1 billion a day, while a surge in low-cost Chinese imports is widely seen as a threat to thousands of jobs across European industries.

EU countries gave the Commission a mandate in June to negotiate with Beijing to rebalance trade relations while developing new trade defence tools. In October, France and Germany called for a new instrument that would restrict market access for foreign competitors causing serious market disruption.

In the letter sent to the Commission president, MEPs from the centre-right European People’s Party (EPP), the largest political group in Parliament, the liberal Renew group, the Socialists and Democrats (S&D), the Greens and the Eurosceptic European Conservatives and Reformists (ECR) call for “horizontal” quotas and tariffs to address Chinese overcapacities.

The MEPs also highlight “weak” customs enforcement, which enables tax evasion and breaches of safety rules, and warn that lengthy EU investigations risk leaving European companies facing closure before protective measures are introduced.

“The Commission must now implement a systematic approach,” the letter adds, including regulatory simplification to “ease regulatory burden” on EU companies and the deployment of “horizon-wide” trade defence instruments. The MEPs also call for “strict local supply chain content rules” and the “harmonisation” of EU customs controls.

The letter comes as Šefčovič prepares for talks with his Chinese counterpart, Commerce Minister Wang Wentao, in January and March next year.

The Commission is also expected to unveil the new trade defence instrument sought by Paris and Berlin before the end of the year.
