**A mysterious "sand body" has been uncovered during renewed excavations at Sutton Hoo.**

The site, near Woodbridge in Suffolk, is renowned for its royal burial ground and ship burials that have transformed historians' understanding of medieval England.

Now, yet more mysteries have been uncovered after the television archaeology series Time Team returned for a special dig in 2025, which has now been chronicled in a four-part documentary season.

These include an Anglo-Saxon grave and evidence of a second burial site beneath it, as well as beads, tweezers, a knife and a brooch.

![Angus Wainwright and Helen Geake at Sutton Hoo](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/24fd/live/12261c90-228f-11ef-8e49-81ac224e5d7e.jpg)

Angus Wainwright, from the National Trust, and Helen Geake, who is part of Time Team

The excavations, which took place over the course of a month, were carried out in Garden Field, a separate cemetery from the site's famous royal and 7th Century ship burial grounds.

Angus Wainwright, an archaeologist with the National Trust, said the latest dig put the focus on normal people and their ancestors and descendants.

"Until now, archaeologists have focused on the royal burial mounds," he said.

"Yet these mounds stood in a landscape of significance to the Saxon kings, as well as one familiar to the everyday East Anglians who lived in the villages and farms."

![Sir Tony Robinson on site at Sutton Hoo during Time Team dig in 2025. He is in the middle of talking and wearing a navy jumper over a polo shirt.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/f046/live/4696b410-e34b-11f1-99aa-0f40b013ad11.jpg)

The findings are being revealed during a new four-part Time Team documentary series hosted by Sir Tony Robinson, which airs on YouTube

Excavated from the late 1930s onwards, the site, cared for by the National Trust since 1998, has unearthed countless treasures, including the Sutton Hoo helmet.

During the recent excavation, the team uncovered one of the site's sand bodies, made when a long-decomposed body leaves behind a ghost-like shape almost like a fossil.

"It was certainly a high point of this latest dig, to find another of these bodies in the Garden Field cemetery," added Wainwright.

![Archaeologists at work in one of the trenches at Sutton Hoo](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/5bb9/live/6effdb70-e34b-11f1-99aa-0f40b013ad11.jpg)

The National Trust said the discoveries raised further questions about the size, chronology, and character of the cemetery

Another major focus of the investigation has been the Bromeswell Bucket, a rare Byzantine artefact discovered at Sutton Hoo.

Following the recovery of missing fragments during Time Team's first season in 2024, the team returned to investigate its contents and piece together its story.

Dr Helen Geake, Time Team's Anglo-Saxon specialist, said she was "thrilled to finally locate and excavate the burial" but there were "still many questions to answer".

"What date is the bucket? What lay behind the choice of cremation and inhumation on this part of the site?" she said.

"And what is the significance of the inhumation grave with its tantalising hint of another below?"

![Aerial view of the main trench that was opened as part of the 2025 dig.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/5b6e/live/c6616500-e34b-11f1-99aa-0f40b013ad11.jpg)

This was the main trench that was opened up by the team during the 2025 dig

The findings are being revealed in the four-part seeries which are being aired on YouTube throughout November.

They raise further questions about the size, chronology, and character of the Garden Field cemetery, and how it fits into the wider story of Sutton Hoo.

Tim Taylor MBE, Time Team creator and series producer, said returning for a new series in partnership with the trust made for some "fascinating discoveries".

"As ever with archaeology, the more we uncover, the more questions we find ourselves asking," she said.

"That's exactly what makes it so exciting, and I can't wait for everyone to see what the team has been up to."
