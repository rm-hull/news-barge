The union behind strike action on York buses is predicting “travel chaos” over the weekend when it happens.

Unite says 200 First Bus drivers are set to walkout on on **Saturday 28 and Sunday 29 November**.

It’s in a dispute over pay.

The workers, who are on £15.58, are demanding pay parity with the city’s Yorkshire Coastliner drivers, who are paid £16.90 an hour.

On Tuesday, First Group reported a seven per cent rise in its operating profits, taking them to £110 million in the six months to September 2026.

Unite general secretary Sharon Graham said: “First’s profits continue to rise yet its hard-working York drivers are expected to put up with second class wages.

“First can afford to put forward a reasonable offer but is choosing not to out of greed.”

The union say industrial action will “escalate severely during the Christmas period” if the dispute is not resolved.

Unite regional officer Darren Rushworth said: “First will be entirely responsible for the disruption caused to York residents, visitors and businesses during the Christmas period.

“There is still time to avoid industrial action but that will require First tabling a deal our members can accept.”

As we reported yesterday, First Bus is preparing to operate a limited network, including Park and Ride, on strike days and will provide further information nearer the time.

It said it was “very disappointed” with the decision to go ahead with the walkout, saying it had submitted two pay offers to Unite members, which were “were well above current inflation and backdated to April this year”.
