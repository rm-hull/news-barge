President Donald Trump's defamation lawsuit against the *New York Times* and book publisher Penguin Random House may proceed, a federal judge has ruled.

It is another win for Trump in his long-running legal campaign against media companies that he accuses of treating him unfairly.

Trump has filed several lawsuits against news outlets since 2024, alleging that they defamed him or fraudulently misled the public with false coverage.

Just last week, a British judge ruled that his $10 billion lawsuit against the BBC could also proceed, in what the president hailed as a “huge victory.”

Trump’s critics have denounced the lawsuits as attacks on the free press.

![The New York Times building in NYC](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/19/18/2257008194..?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&auto=webp)

The lawsuit against the *New York Times*and Penguin Random House cited a book about Trump by two* New York Times* reporters and what it characterized as "false, malicious, defamatory, and disparaging" articles that he says were aimed at sabotaging his chances in the 2024 presidential election.

The *Times* and Penguin have denied defaming Trump. They said in a court filing that his case is legally flawed and aimed at scoring political points.

Representatives for the *Times*, Penguin Random House and Trump's personal lawyers did not immediately respond to requests for comment.

U.S. District Judge Steven Merryday, an appointee of George H.W. Bush, handed down the ruling in federal court in Tampa, Florida.

![Trump has accused the NYT of ‘malicious’ articles that he says were aimed at sabotaging his chances in the 2024 presidential election](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/20/14/2299457003..?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&auto=webp)

Merryday threw out two of Trump's claims but said that the rest of his complaint plausibly alleged that statements accusing Trump of "planning and perpetrating complex crimes" were “based on incomplete, minimal information."

The lawsuit against the *Times* and Penguin concerned two articles and a book that cast doubts on his origin story as a savvy businessman who ascended in the real estate and entertainment industries.

Trump cited 22 statements from the book by *Times*journalist Peter Baker, titled* For Trump, a Lifetime of Scandals Heads Toward a Moment of Judgment*, and 12 from the articles by Susanne Craig and Russ Buettner.

They include claims about Trump’s role in creating his hit reality TV show *The Apprentice*, his inheritance from his father, his business ventures, and his upbringing in New York City.

The *Times* and Penguin said in a motion to dismiss that Trump's lawsuit failed to explain how the statements were defamatory and included subjective opinions that cannot be proven true or false.
