**Dogyu Lee, Euronews Doha bureau**

## The US, Chinese and Russian leaders pledged to hold talks on nuclear risk reduction, but made no mention of North Korea in their joint peace declaration.

US President Donald Trump left China on Thursday without meeting North Korean leader Kim Jong-un, a get-together he had sought during his visit to Shenzhen for a rare three-way meeting with Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin on the sidelines of the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (APEC) summit.

Trump had repeatedly raised the possibility of meeting Kim during his Asia trip, although a White House official had said no such meeting was planned.

“We may meet with Kim Jong-un also, so that'll be good,” Trump told reporters at the White House on Monday.

Meanwhile, Kim inspected sites in North Korea’s South Hamgyong province on Tuesday and Wednesday, according to South Korean news agency Newsis.

The two leaders have not met face-to-face since June 2019.

Although the three leaders agreed to pursue dialogue on nuclear risk reduction and non-proliferation, the joint “peace declaration” made no explicit reference to the Korean Peninsula or Taiwan.

However, Chinese state news agency Xinhua later reported that the three leaders had exchanged views on the Korean Peninsula, Ukraine, nuclear non-proliferation and the situation in the Middle East.

The summit has fuelled concerns in South Korea, Japan and Taiwan that major powers could make decisions affecting regional security without consulting those directly affected.

## **South Korea fears being sidelined**

Before the three-way meeting, South Korean media compared it to the Yalta Conference in 1945, where leaders of the US, the UK and the Soviet Union discussed the post-war international order.

Seoul has been keen to avoid major powers making decisions on the Korean peninsula without its involvement.

Although the omission of the Korean peninsula from the declaration has somewhat alleviated concerns over a second Yalta, it has raised concerns that denuclearisation is slipping down the major powers’ diplomatic agenda, according to South Korean news outlet Money Today.

Park Won-gon, a professor of North Korean studies at Ewha Womans University, told the outlet that China and Russia may have sided with Pyongyang.

Kim Jae-chun, a professor at Sogang University, said the three powers view North Korea’s nuclear programme very differently.

However, Newsis suggested that the absence of a Trump-Kim meeting for the second consecutive year may ease concerns over potential US recognition of North Korea as a nuclear-armed state.

South Korean President Lee Jae-myung reiterated, during Thursday’s APEC summit, the importance of achieving peace and stability on the Korean peninsula to bring prosperity to the region.

## **Japan seeks reassurances from Washington**

The three-way meeting has raised concerns in Japan, where relations with Beijing are tense.

Japanese Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi exchanged greetings with Xi during APEC, but the two leaders did not meet formally.

A Japanese ruling-party lawmaker said Japan’s exclusion from the three-way meeting could be seen as a diplomatic setback, Fuji TV reported.

However, Takaichi said at a press briefing in Shenzhen that she did not believe Japan’s interests would be undermined by the summit, Japanese broadcaster TBS reported.

Takaichi is also arranging a phone call with Trump to discuss the outcome of his talks with Xi and Putin and reaffirm bilateral cooperation, TV Asahi reported, citing Japanese government sources.

Japanese media also highlighted the three powers’ position on the International Criminal Court (ICC), which is currently headed by Japanese judge Tomoko Akane.

The joint declaration called for respect for the immunity of state officials and said that the ICC and similar institutions must not abuse their jurisdiction.

In March 2023, the court issued an arrest warrant for Putin over the alleged unlawful deportation and transfer of Ukrainian children.

## **Taiwan wary of great-power diplomacy**

As with South Korea, mentions of "Yalta 2.0" emerged in Taiwanese media in the lead-up to the meeting. Taiwanese broadcaster TVBS highlighted concerns that Taiwan could be used as a bargaining chip in the negotiations.

The question of Taiwan was raised in the bilateral meeting between Xi and Trump earlier on Thursday.

Tsai Jung-hsiang, a professor of political science at National Chung Cheng University in Taiwan, told the island’s Central News Agency that the US and China have very different views on strategic stability.

While Trump has aimed to avoid conflict over Taiwan during his time in office, Xi wanted the US to recognise Beijing’s position on sovereignty and territorial integrity, Tsai said.

However, he said he did not expect any changes to US policy regarding Taiwan as a result of the meeting.

State-run media in China said the three-way meeting allowed the three major powers to work together to promote international security and stability.

Xi said the meeting was “positive, constructive and fruitful”, as the three nations were committed to “world peace”, Xinhua reported.

Earlier, Xi said the three nations should hold regular high-level meetings and establish mechanisms to prevent and manage crises.

Neither proposal was explicitly included in the four-point peace declaration.
