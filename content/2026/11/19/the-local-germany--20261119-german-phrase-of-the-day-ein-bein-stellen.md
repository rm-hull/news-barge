The phrase "*jemandem ein Bein stellen*" is just as likely to turn up in a heated office meeting as on a football pitch. It’s useful, vivid and surprisingly good grammar practice.**What does*Ein Bein stellen* mean?**

Literally, *jemandem ein Bein stellen* means "to put a leg in someone's way".

It describes tripping someone up or, figuratively, undermining or sabotaging a person – usually sneakily and on purpose.

Grammatically, the person being tripped is in the dative (*mir*, for example) and with this expression it’s always* ein Bein*(a leg), regardless of whose leg is being described.**READ ALSO: German grammar tips - The strange case of the 'dativ**e'

So in the past (*Perfekt*) tense, you could say* Er hat mir ein Bein gestellt*(He tripped me up). If you want to use the expression with a second verb, use* zu*and the infinitive:* Sie versucht, mir ein Bein zu stellen* (She’s trying to trip me up).

The expression is thought to come from wrestling and hand-to-hand combat, where hooking an opponent's leg was a standard way to topple them. These days, you’ll hear it in the playground, on the football pitch, in the office and in political commentary.

**Why do I need to know*Ein Bein stellen*?**

This phrase gives and gives. For a start, it gives you two expressions for the price of one because it works both literally (as in a clumsy foul) and metaphorically (as in a colleague who "forgets" to pass on your message).

You can also turn the phrase on yourself – and sooner or later we all trip ourselves up somehow. In German *Ich habe mir selbst ein Bein gestellt* means “I tripped myself up” or “I sabotaged myself”.

Finally, it also gives you a (relatively) painless workout in using German’s dative and accusative cases.

Grammatically, the phrase takes two objects. The person being tripped is in the dative (*jemandem*,* mir*,* dir*,* ihm*,* ihr*,* uns*,* euch*,* ihnen*) and the leg is in the accusative (* ein Bein*, because* das Bein*is neuter).**READ ALSO: German grammar tips - The curious case of the 'accusative'Use it like this:**

*Der Verteidiger hat dem Stürmer kurz vor dem Strafraum ein Bein gestellt.*The defender tripped the striker just outside the penalty area.* Er hat mir absichtlich ein Bein gestellt!*He tripped me on purpose!* Meine Kollegin versucht ständig, mir bei diesem Projekt ein Bein zu stellen.*My colleague is constantly trying to undermine me on this project.* Mit dieser unüberlegten Aussage hat er sich selbst ein Bein gestellt.*

With that thoughtless remark, he tripped himself up.
