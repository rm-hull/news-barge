# ‘I thought she’d been killed’: Leeds dad’s horror after daughter hit outside school

## A Leeds father has told how he feared his daughter had been killed after they were knocked down on a zebra crossing outside a primary school.

Edward Hardcastle and his daughter Lorna-Lily, aged 10, were hit by a Skoda Yeti outside Lower Wortley Primary School in Leeds on September 17.

Lorna-Lily suffered a serious head injury and was in hospital for a week, while Edward sustained a fractured knee and a cut to his scalp.

![Edward Hardcastle with his daughter Lorna-Lily.](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/jpim-static/image/2026/11/19/15/23/FB_IMG_1790628773061.jpeg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


His daughter, who had just been to a dance class, has been left with cognitive issues, headaches, memory problems and anxiety around cars.

Edward, 43, and Lorna-Lily’s mother Samantha, 45, now want to help raise awareness of drivers’ responsibilities, in conjunction with their lawyers Irwin Mitchell on Road Safety Week

Edward said: “What happened that day still haunts me, particularly seeing Lorna-Lily laying on the road.

“At that point, I didn’t even know if she was still alive. It was terrifying but I couldn’t even think about my own injuries, all I wanted to do was get to my little girl.

![Lorna-Lily was off school for three weeks after the crash.](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/jpim-static/image/2026/11/19/15/50/FB_IMG_1790628883974.jpeg?trim=506,4,6,0&crop=&width=640&quality=65)


“When we were told she had a head injury, we were devastated. She’ll always be our bright and funny Lorna-Lily but her life was changed in a split second through the actions of a careless driver and that is something I’ll never get over.”The Skoda driver admitted two charges of causing serious injury by by careless driving and was sentenced to 12 months community service and was also disqualified from driving for 12 months.

Edward, an HGV driver, was off work for around 11 weeks before going back on a phased return.

Meanwhile, Lorna-Lily was absent from school for around three weeks.

Since the incident, Edward and Lorna-Lily’s mum Samantha, 45, have reported an increase in their daughter’s headaches and some concern around her memory, temper and patience. They also said she has difficulty expressing herself when excited and suffers from anxiety and nervousness, particularly around vehicles.

Edwards added: “No sentence will ever make up for what we’ve been through, but by speaking out I hope I can make others realise the risks on the roads if you’re not careful.”

Peter Lorence, the specialist road traffic collision lawyer at Irwin Mitchell representing Edward and Lorna-Lily, said after the hearing: “We hope this case reminds all drivers of the responsibility they have to protect pedestrians, particularly children and others who are vulnerable on the road.”
