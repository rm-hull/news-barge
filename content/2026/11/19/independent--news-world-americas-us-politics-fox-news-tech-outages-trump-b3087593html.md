The Trump administration has insisted that the recent slew of outages affecting the country’s technology infrastructure is not connected — but even Fox News isn’t buying it.

Last Thursday, Microsoft users couldn’t access their emails or attend virtual meetings due to an issue that arose during a network upgrade. Then, the Federal Aviation Administration grounded all flights for more than four hours on Friday thanks to an outage that stemmed from a contractor replacing old copper wire.

The outages continued into this week, with companies JPMorganChase reporting a software bug in its systems and Apple acknowledging issues caused by an internal update. In the most stark problem yet, an internal network that the National Security Agency uses to communicate classified information went down Tuesday during routine maintenance.

Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton tried to quell worries at a House Homeland Security Committee hearing Wednesday, saying, “I understand the timing of these incidents can raise questions and it should. The American people should ask us these questions.

“But based on what we know today, after a great deal of diligence, these are separate service outages associated with maintenance and technical changes. They are not a coordinated or state-sponsored attack.”

![Fox & Friends co-host Lawrence Jones has questioned the Trump administration’s stance that the recent slew of tech outages is not connected](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/18/23/24/Screenshot-2026-11-18-at-6-35-28-PM.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

But Fox News, a conservative network known to defend Trump’s agenda, has surprisingly questioned what the government and other officials have told the public.

“I’m ok with the government, the companies, the experts saying, ‘Don’t panic,’” *Fox & Friends* co-host Lawrence Jones said early Wednesday morning before the congressional hearing. “But at the same time, don’t tell us that it’s not connected.”

“I don’t need a coding degree to understand when there’s multiple outages in different sectors across the country — from banking, to technology, to Microsoft, to Workday, to the government, to the NSA — all happening at once every single day, that it’s connected,” he continued.

Jones asked the Trump administration for honest answers.

“And so I just wish someone that is in the government would explain honestly what’s going on and say, ‘Look, we may not have the solution right now,’ or, ‘We’re working through the solution right now, but we understand your concern.’ And they’ve yet to do that right now.”

![Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton tried to quell worries at a House Homeland Security Committee hearing Wednesday, saying the outages ‘are not a coordinated or state-sponsored attack’](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/18/23/42/GettyImages-2305725511.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Later Wednesday at the congressional hearing, Representative Nick LaLota, a New York Republican, addressed Clayton: “You said before, Mr. Director, that there’s no indication of a coordinated cyberattack, but have our intelligence agencies affirmatively ruled out a connection or have you simply not yet found one?”

Clayton explained that intelligence officials have “looked inside the government, inside the private sector” for TTPs, which stands for techniques, tactics and procedures “that have been used in the past for that type of activity.

“Do we see any of them here? We do not see them.”

While experts have cautioned against jumping to conclusions that the outages are from malicious actors rather than just mundane errors, they have also warned against a society that relies too heavily on hyper-connected technology.

”The real cause for concern isn't a single master threat—it is the underlying reality that our digital economy has become so hyper-connected that isolated software bugs, database corruptions, or routing errors now trigger immediate, high-visibility disruption across millions of lives,” Fran Villalba Segarra, founder and CEO of INTERNXT, a cybersecurity company based in Spain, previously told *The Independent*.
