Scotland is moving closer to introducing higher council tax charges on homes worth more than £1m, with the Scottish Government publishing legislation to create two new bands for high-value properties.

The Council Tax (High Value Dwellings) Bill proposes additional council tax bands for properties valued at more than £1m and £2m, which are currently grouped together in the highest existing band.

Deputy First Minister and Finance Secretary Jenny Gilruth described the publication of the bill as an “important milestone”, saying the proposals would make the tax system fairer and help fund local services.

Under the plans, properties worth between £1m and £2m would be placed in a new council tax band I, while homes valued at £2m or more would fall into band J.

Homes in band I would be charged 2.886 times the current council tax rate for band D properties in their local authority area. Those in band J would pay 4.628 times the band D charge.

The properties are currently included in band H, the highest existing council tax band, which covers homes valued at £212,000 or more based on property prices in 1991.

A targeted valuation exercise is now being planned to identify properties worth £1m or more.

Ms Gilruth said the legislation would add two bands to the top of the council tax system, asking those with the “broadest shoulders” to contribute more towards local services.

“If passed, the Bill will introduce new council tax bands for the small percentage of homes worth more than £1 million, asking those with the broadest shoulders to contribute more towards local services, whilst responding to the housing emergency we are facing,” she said.

## How much council tax would £1m homes pay in Scotland?

Under the proposals, council tax charges for properties valued between £1m and £2m would be calculated at 2.886 times the standard band D charge in their local authority.

For properties worth £2m or more, the multiplier would rise to 4.628 times the band D rate.

The actual amount homeowners would pay would therefore depend on the council tax rate set by their local authority.

The bill also proposes changes to support for people with serious cognitive impairment, including those living with dementia or who have suffered a serious brain injury.

Under the legislation, eligibility for council tax discounts or exemptions could be backdated to the point of diagnosis, potentially increasing the financial support available to those affected.

Local authorities would also be given powers to introduce council tax discounts in response to local circumstances, including fires, flooding and other natural disasters.

Ms Gilruth said the proposals formed part of the Scottish Government's wider approach to taxation and investment.

She pointed to measures including free tuition, free prescriptions and plans to extend a £2 bus fare cap across Scotland, saying these were cost-of-living measures not available elsewhere in the UK.

The Council Tax (High Value Dwellings) Bill must still pass through the legislative process before the proposed changes can come into force.
