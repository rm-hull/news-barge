Parents need clear, uniform safety guidance to keep children in rear-facing car seats until at least four years old, a charity has urged.

A report published on Thursday by the Royal Society for the Prevention of Accidents (Rospa) calls on the NHS and the Government to expand advice to "make clear rear-facing recommendations consistently available".

Rospa explains rear-facing seats are safer for young children due to how their bodies develop.

"Their relatively large heads and developing necks need particular protection: in a frontal collision, a rear-facing seat supports the head and torso together," the report notes.

The document compares advice provided to families in the UK and Sweden.

Figures from the Swedish Transport Administration in the report show small children in forward-facing seats are five times more likely to die or be seriously injured in collisions than those facing backwards.

Swedish officials recommend rear-facing seats until at least four to five years old, supported by Sweden's automotive, insurance, and road safety sectors.

![Drivers face fine up to £500 and three penalty points for using incorrect restraints](https://static.independent.co.uk/s3fs-public/thumbnails/image/2019/06/25/14/car-seat-stock-image.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“Sweden has among the lowest fatality rates for small children in cars, largely because most parents use rear-facing restraints for older children up to four or five years of age, or longer,” Dr Maria Klingegard, traffic safety researcher at Swedish company Folksam Insurance Group, says in the document.

The NHS England website advises parents to use a rear-facing car seat for “as long as your child fits into it” as this offers “better protection” in the event of an accident.

Under UK law, children must usually use an appropriate car seat until they are 12 years old or 135 cm tall, whichever comes first.

Car seats can be chosen based on a child’s height or weight and height-based seats must be rear-facing until children are 15 months old.

After a child reaches this age, their height-based seat can be forward-facing.

Front airbags must also be deactivated before a rear-facing baby seat is fitted in a front seat, the Government website states.

Jenny Everson, from Sheffield, and her mother Anne were seriously injured and needed surgery after another driver lost control and crashed into their car in December 2023.

Ms Everson’s two-year-old son Marlo, who had been travelling in a rear-facing car seat, received only cuts and bruises.

![Car seats can be chosen based on a child’s height or weight and height-based seats must be rear-facing until children are 15 months old](https://static.independent.co.uk/s3fs-public/thumbnails/image/2015/12/11/12/child-seat-rex.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Of her son, she said: “An emergency responder told me his rear-facing seat had saved his life.

“Marlo is five now and he still travels rear-facing.

“I feel it’s my duty to tell other parents what happened so they can learn from it.

“I’ve spoken to so many people who had no idea that rear-facing seats were so much safer.”

Rebecca Guy, senior policy manager at Rospa, said: “Parents want to make the best decision for their child’s safety, but many aren’t aware of the benefits of keeping children rear-facing beyond infancy.

“This report highlights the importance of making sure families have access to clear, evidence-based information when choosing a car seat.

“By giving parents the right information at the right time, we can help them make decisions with confidence.”

The report recommends that families receive clear and consistent advice, starting in pregnancy and reinforced as the child grows, about keeping them rear-facing until they are at least four years old.

It also calls for improved data from crashes which would help researchers understand which seats children were using, they way they faced and how they were fitted.

A Department for Transport spokesperson said: “The death of any child on our roads is a tragedy.

“We already follow strict international safety regulations on child *car* * seats*, and have no plans to mandate the use of rear-facing* seats* for children up the age of four.

“This is consistent with the international expert group which decided against mandating rear-facing *car* * seats* for children up to four years old as they are more prone to being installed incorrectly, posing a greater risk of injury, particularly in smaller vehicles which are more common in the UK.”
