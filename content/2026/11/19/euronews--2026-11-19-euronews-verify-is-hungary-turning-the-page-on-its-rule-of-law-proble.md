## Hungary is under new leadership, and reforms are underway. But can Péter Magyar restore the rule of law and win back the EU's trust?

EU affairs ministers met on Thursday, 19 November, for the EU's annual rule of law dialogue, which examines judicial independence, corruption, media freedom, and institutional checks and balances.

This is a regular review, not a special hearing against Hungary or a vote on sanctions.

However, the timing is significant, as Brussels assesses whether the changes under Hungarian Prime Minister Péter Magyar are delivering lasting results.

"I think we have seen clear efforts to break with the rule of law situation that has existed under Viktor Orbán and that the new government under Magyar has really put an effort into implementing reforms to restore judicial independence," Thu Nguyen, the Acting Co-Director at the Jacques Delors Centre, told Euronews Verify.

## Why is the EU still keeping Hungary under scrutiny?

Since taking office in 2026, Magyar's government has moved quickly to reverse some of the policies that put Hungary on a collision course with the EU.

The emergency rule by decree has ended.

The controversial Sovereignty Protection Office, which investigated civil society organisations and media outlets, has been abolished.

Hungary has also joined the European Public Prosecutor's Office and introduced new anti-corruption and judicial reforms.

A European Parliament delegation visiting Hungary in October praised the pace of these changes, describing them as substantial progress.

Despite all that, Hungary is also still subject to a separate EU procedure known as Article 7.

Launched in 2018, it addresses concerns about a serious risk to the EU's founding values, including democracy, the rule of law and fundamental rights.

## Could the UK rejoin the EU?

Today's episode also focuses on the long-awaited EU-UK summit that will take place this Friday, 20 November.

After a decade of Brexit, London now wants closer ties with Brussels, not a divorce.

And there is public support too: 56% of Britons would now vote to rejoin.

The reset began under Keir Starmer and picked up pace under Andy Burnham, who stirred the Brexit debate but stopped short of committing to rejoining.

Five European leaders have called for closer collaboration in areas, including on energy.

But will the UK be able to re-join under the same conditions as before, including its opt-outs?

"If the UK were to rejoin the EU, it would not be rejoining the EU that it left," said Sébastien Maillard, associate fellow at Chatham House.

"It's much more a geopolitical EU that would be joining, and it has to be ready [...] to accept the idea of European sovereignty. I think if the UK is ready to go fully on the idea that there is a need to side with the EU as a member state, to build sovereign capacities independently from the US or from China, then it can negotiate all kinds of opt-outs."

## How long would it take for the UK to rejoin the bloc?

The UK's Prime Minister, Andy Burnham, has held off on the possibility that he could ask to rejoin at some stage, while ruling out major changes before the next election.

"This government has been clear that at least until the next election they are still committed to those red lines, so we wouldn't expect to see the UK's relationship change in this parliament with the European Union," said Oliver Nicholls, senior policy manager of Best for Britain

Yet, the first step to rejoin the bloc is getting the conversation started in the UK.
