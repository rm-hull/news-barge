An entire Virginia school district has shut its doors just ahead of the Thanksgiving holiday after discovering hundreds of recently hired staff were not checked for complaints of child abuse or neglect.

Albemarle County Public Schools said its 29 schools and facilities would be closed for more than a week starting Thursday after discovering that more than 500 people hired by the district over the past three years had not been properly screened.

The schools will be closed from November 19 through the end of Thanksgiving break, and will reopen November 30 as to “not risk the safety of our students,” acting Superintendent Chandra Hayes said in a letter to parents Wednesday.

The district, which is headquartered in Charlottesville, Virginia, serves more than 14,000 students from preschool through 12th grade, and has more than 2,800 employees.

School officials discovered the oversight during a review of the onboarding process, where they found that new hires were subject to criminal background checks — but were not checked against the Child Protective Services Central Registry.

![The Albemarle County Public School District in Virginia has closed until after Thanksgiving after officials discovered they hadn’t been screening new hires properly to check for child abuse claims](https://static.independent.co.uk/2024/09/09/08/iStock-1659858986.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The registry is maintained by the Virginia Department of Social Services and is used to confirm there are no complaints of child abuse or neglect.

“Specifically, over the past three years, there were more than 500 people hired by the division who passed their criminal background checks, but their information was never submitted for the Social Services registry review. We investigated the matter and took immediate action,” Hayes wrote in the letter.

Hayes noted that “the personnel responsible for submissions to Child Protective Services did not consistently follow established processes.”

The employees affected work in schools and departments across the entire district, prompting Hayes to shutter all schools.

“I made the decision that we must close all schools until we have this issue resolved,” Hayes said.

She said the district shared the findings with the Virginia Department of Education, which agreed that staff who work directly with children must have their Social Services screening complete before being able to work with students again.

The Department of Social Services is working to finish the screenings, with staff who work with children being prioritized so the district can reopen schools quickly.

If an employee is found the subject of a founded complaint of child abuse or neglect, the district said it will “act immediately to protect students.”

“Trust is built through honesty, especially when the news is hard. I’ll keep you informed as the checks are completed,” Hayes said. “I will receive a daily report from the Virginia Department of Social Services on the progress of the application review.”

“With so little notice and the holiday season so close, I know this will be hard on many families, and I am sorry for that disruption. However, the safety of your children must always come first, and I believe this is a necessary action.”
