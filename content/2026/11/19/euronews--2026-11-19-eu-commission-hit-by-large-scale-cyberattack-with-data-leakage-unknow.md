## The European Commission's internal HR systems have been targeted by a malicious cyberattack, with EU cyber experts still assessing whether sensitive personal information has been compromised.

The European Commission has suffered a major cyberattack, but it is not yet clear who is behind it or whether sensitive staff data was stolen.

For several days, Commission officials could not access Sysper, the internal HR platform where they check salary information, request holidays and sick leave, submit timesheets and sign up for training.

On Thursday, Sysper was working again, whereas the health insurance Joint Sickness Insurance Scheme (JSIS) remained unavailable, an official told Euronews under the condition of anonymity.

Beyond these relatively minor disruptions, most civil servants barely noticed the systems had been compromised. CERT-EU, the cybersecurity service for the EU institutions, is now trying to assess the damage.

The key question is whether the attacker, who has yet to be identified, gained access to the data held in Sysper, JSIS and other HR systems. That would not normally open the door to the Commission’s internal documents, which are hosted on a separate system.

Still, the platforms hold sensitive personal details, including officials’ names, pay grades, home addresses and contact details. Such a database could fuel far more sophisticated campaigns targeting individual staff.

EU officials are yet to receive official internal communications about the incident, as the extent of the damage is still being assessed.

The Commission is not new to cyberattacks. In March, its cloud infrastructure hosting its Europa.eu websites was compromised, resulting in the theft of around 91.7 GB of data, including personal information and email content.

In January, a cyberattack targeted the Commission's central mobile-device infrastructure, potentially exposing staff names and phone numbers.

The Commission did not immediately reply to Euronews’ request for comment.
