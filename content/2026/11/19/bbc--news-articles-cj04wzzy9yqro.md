**The most senior advisor to the Prince of Wales has said this week's visit with the Princess of Wales to India was "particularly special" and described the royal couple "as very much a team".**

Prince William and Catherine are now back in the UK after spending three days in Mumbai and Delhi - the first time they have travelled together on overseas royal duties since the princess's cancer treatment.

The visit focused on the prince's Earthshot Prize which were hosted in Mumbai. His wife last attended the Earthshot awards when they were held in Boston in 2022.

"The prince has really enjoyed having the princess alongside him for Earthshot, and for her to see first-hand how much it has grown in scale since she last attended in Boston," Ian Patrick, the prince's private secretary told reporters.

"She's enormously proud of what he's achieved, and it has been particularly special for them to experience this moment together. They are very much a team."

The prince and princess also met the Indian Prime Minister Narendra Modi at his residence in Delhi where they discussed UK-India partnerships.

In a social media post after the meeting, the prime minister said it had been "a pleasure to meet their Royal Highnesses" and praised the Earthshot Prize for "showcasing Indian ingenuity in addressing global environmental challenges".

The meeting was a late addition to the prince and princess's plans. Prime Minister Modi altered his schedule to return to India from a summit in the Philippines to ensure he could meet the royal couple.

"The prince and princess have had a thoroughly enjoyable time in India. They've been incredibly touched by the warmth of the welcome they've received," said Patrick.

William and Catherine's India visit was their first overseas trip together since Catherine's cancer treatment

The couple were last in India for an official tour in 2016.

"It's been wonderful for them to return together after ten years," the private secretary said. "They were incredibly inspired by the people they met. They leave with very happy memories and a real enthusiasm for building on the connections they've made with the next generation."

There was a focus on young people and their potential during the three day visit. This year, for the first time, there was a Children's Earthshot Awards where 15,000 schools across India took part creating solutions for environmental problems in their own communities.

"We cannot look into the eyes of the next generation and tell them that we knew what was happening, we knew what was at stake, and yet we did not do everything in our power to change it," Prince William said in his speech at the Earthshot Prize Awards Ceremony. "They will not judge us by the promises we made, but by the world we leave behind."

"The prince and princess don't want young people to feel unheard or that the challenges facing the planet and society are beyond their control," said Patrick. "They want them to feel optimistic, empowered and part of the solutions."

The Earthshot Prize was designed as a ten-year project - it is now in its sixth year and Prince William remains ambitious for it.

"Companies including Amazon, IKEA, Nvidia, Standard Chartered and Uber are now stepping forward to help their impact spread even further," said Jason Knauf, the chief executive officer of the Earthshot Prize.

"This is enormously encouraging but the prince wants to see more of the world's biggest businesses and investors get involved... every business and investor who joins us helps these solutions grow faster, building a healthier, happier and more prosperous future for us all."

Now that the prince and princess are travelling together on international duty, the visit was an insight into way their royal trips will operate in future.

What did we learn from Kate and William’s India trip?

The days of royal tours lasting two weeks will not be the approach of the royal couple who think they make a big impact over shorter trips with the additional help of social media to amplify their engagements. They also want to limit the amount of time they spend away from their three children.

"The prince and princess want their overseas travel to have purpose, meaning and impact. It's about building stronger relationships, creating opportunities for collaboration and supporting projects that deliver real results," explained Patrick.

Despite plans to keep future royal tours short, it's believed a visit to Australia could be in the plans for the couple in 2027.

At the Earthshot Prize Awards ceremony on Tuesday, Prince William was heard by reporters saying "Big year next year, Australia!"

Kensington Palace has not yet confirmed any further travel plans for the Waleses.

They last visited Australia in 2014 with nine-month-old Prince George. It is possible that their three children could join them on a future Australia trip.
