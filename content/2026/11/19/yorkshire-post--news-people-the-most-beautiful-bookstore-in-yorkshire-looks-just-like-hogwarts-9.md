# The ‘most beautiful bookstore in Yorkshire’ looks just like Hogwarts

## Winter is on the horizon, and as the weather gets drizzly, I’m on the lookout for atmospheric bookstores.

According to social media, one store is a particular showstopper. I stumbled across Bradford Waterstones on Instagram and had to double-take; it looked like stepping into Hogwarts.

Famed for its Venetian Gothic architecture, the bookstore is set inside the historic Wool Exchange. On social media, people are raving about its elaborate design – in fact, some have nicknamed it the ‘most beautiful bookstore in Yorkshire.’

![](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/jpim-static/image/2026/11/18/10/47/20261116_153120.jpeg?trim=0,4,338,0&crop=&width=640&quality=65)


On a rainy Monday, I couldn’t resist putting this fantastical bookstore to the test. Would it be as stunning in person?

Boarding a train to Bradford Forster Square, I was surprised at how conveniently it was located. The store is set on Hustlergate, a cobbled street near The Broadway shopping centre. Leaving Bradford Forster Square, walking to Waterstones took less than five minutes.

If first impressions count, the building packs a punch. I was immediately struck by the grandeur of the Wool Exchange: crafted from Bradford sandstone and crowned with a clock tower and 150ft spire.

I entered through large glass doors, and my attention was instantly drawn to the ceiling.

![](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/jpim-static/image/2026/11/18/10/17/20261116_153223.jpeg?trim=283,4,55,0&crop=&width=640&quality=65)


It was an overcast afternoon, but light still flooded through intricately patterned windows. The beams were entirely exposed, revealing a complex web of curved braces and struts. There were maintenance works underway – metal framework at one side of the store – but otherwise, it was like stepping into a time capsule. Everything had been meticulously preserved.

The selection of books was excellent. I wandered between travel, cooking, fiction, and music sections. There was even a stand dedicated to ‘Trending Titles,’ which featured novels recommended by book influencers. “Don’t miss out on the gossip,” its sign read.

Perhaps the most fitting was an entire section dedicated to Harry Potter. I browsed shelves of kitschy memorabilia and a beautiful collection of paperbacks, from *Harry Potter and the Philosopher’s Stone*to* The Tales of Beedle the Bard*.

![](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/jpim-static/image/2026/11/18/10/18/20261116_153116.jpeg?trim=1039,12,985,0&crop=&width=640&quality=65)


Dotted along the shelves were handwritten notes and recommendations – pausing to read these, it felt like joining a little book club.

It wasn’t just handwritten notes, either. The Wool Exchange was constructed in 1867 and, for a century, it was an important local trading hub.

Across the ground floor, polished granite pillars are marked with numbers, once allocated to individual vendors. In one corner stands a statue of the 19th-century politician, Richard Cobden, nicknamed the 'Apostle of Free Trade.' The building might be embracing a new life through literature, but it’s clearly keeping one foot in the past.

Giving Richard Cobden’s statue a wave, I climbed the staircase to a wrought-iron balcony, where Cafe W is located on a mezzanine floor.

The counter displayed a mixture of sandwiches, cakes, and bakes – many priced at less than £5. “I’m desperate to try the hazelnut praline,” another customer whispered as she passed by. After some deliberation, I settled on a £3.30 slice of ‘sticky toffee biscoff’ cake and a £3.20 cappuccino.

There were two seating choices: tables alongside a floor-to-ceiling window overlooking Hustlergate or ‘library view’ tables near the mezzanine railing. I chose the latter and tucked into my order with an aerial view of the bookshelves below.

As the sky darkened outside, the Wool Exchange grew even cosier. Hidden behind polished columns, visitors made the most of reading corners, tucked into nooks and crannies with their books of choice. It might be a bookstore, but with its cafe and cosy atmosphere, Waterstones felt similar to a community library.

As a child, I was fascinated by tales of witchcraft and wizardry. With its elaborate architecture, this Waterstones definitely reminded me of Hogwarts – I’ll be returning for a cosy coffee and nostalgia fix.
