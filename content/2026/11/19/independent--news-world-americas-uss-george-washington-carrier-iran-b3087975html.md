The USS George Washington has left the Middle East, returning the U.S. military to two aircraft carriers supporting the war in Iran.

Its departure came in the same week that the USS Theodore Roosevelt arrived, briefly putting an unusually high three carriers in the waters off Iran.

Cmdr. Katie Koenig, a spokesperson for the U.S. Pacific Fleet, said Wednesday that the Japan-based carrier was now under its control and “conducting routine operations” in the Asia-Pacific region.

![The USS George Washington aircraft carrier is photographed off the coast of Argentina during joint exercises in 2024](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/19/08/Iran_US_Aircraft_Carrier_64627.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The George Washington had arrived in the region in August. Before that, it had been pulled from a patrol in the South China Sea shortly after reports of mental health and supply issues emerged aboard another long-deployed aircraft carrier in the Middle East, the USS Abraham Lincoln.

The Lincoln set a record for uninterrupted time at sea, at more than 240 days. This shuffle is the latest movement of aircraft carriers in the Middle East during the war with Iran. The carriers have served as the linchpin of a U.S. flotilla that has blockaded all merchant shipping to and from the Islamic Republic.

The Trump administration is looking to keep squeezing Tehran's economy by preventing its oil from reaching global markets. The USS George H.W. Bush, which arrived in the region in April, and the Roosevelt are now the two carriers there. They are joined by more than a dozen destroyers, cruisers and a littoral combat ship.

![The naval presence is helping maintain the blockade on Iran. The U.S. has also carried out some limited strikes on Iranian targets in the Strait of Hormuz](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/18/09/Iran_Strait_of_Hormuz_92767.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The naval presence is helping maintain the blockade on Iran. The U.S. has also carried out some limited strikes on Iranian targets in the Strait of Hormuz. The strait is the critical waterway where nearly a third of the world's oil passed through before the war began Feb. 28.

The disruptions to shipping through the strait have raised global energy prices.

The unpopularity of the Iran war and the resulting economic effects contributed to major losses for President Donald Trump's Republican Party in this month's midterm elections.

Trump has offered mixed signals on whether he plans to return to major combat with Iran. Any negotiated deal to end the fighting appears to be a long way off. Trump has recently warned Iran that the U.S. military is “locked and loaded” and that the U.S. was keeping an eye on Pickaxe Mountain, a facility linked to Iran's nuclear program, for signs of activity.
