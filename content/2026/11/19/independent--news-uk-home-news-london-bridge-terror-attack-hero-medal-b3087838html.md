An off-duty police officer who was seriously injured during the 2017 London Bridge terror attack has had his bravery medal returned to him after selling it at auction for more than £20,000 to support his young family.

Sgt Charles 'Charlie' Guenigault, who ran towards three knife-wielding attackers, put his George Medal up for sale to help secure the financial future of his partner and their three children.

But after the medal sparked a bidding war at London auctioneers Morton & Eden on Wednesday, the winning buyer decided to give it back to him.

The buyer, who has not been named, said they were so moved by Sgt Guenigault's actions that they did not want him to have to part with the award.

The medal had been expected to sell for between £6,000 and £8,000, but eventually fetched £20,400.

![Sgt Charles 'Charlie' Guenigault with his George Medal](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/19/11/12/BNPS_LondonterrorHeroMedal_03.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Sgt Guenigault said: "This is a completely unexpected and extremely generous gesture.

"I’m so thankful to be able to keep the medal with me for the years to come.

"This will give the medal a new and even deeper meaning for me."

![George Medal of bravery](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/19/11/04/BNPS_LondonterrorHeroMedal_01.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

He said selling the award had been a "deeply personal decision", made with his mental health and his family's future in mind.

"The proceeds from the auction will help me create new, fun and happy memories with my three amazing children and my incredible partner Hannah," he added.

David Kirk, from Morton & Eden, said the buyer had been moved to act after reading about the sale.

"He was so in awe of Guenigault’s actions that he didn’t want him to have to part with the medal," he said.

"We couldn’t be happier with the outcome of this sale in all respects."

![Sgt Guenigault said he had never taken the honour for granted, particularly knowing that some recipients had died and received their awards posthumously.](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/19/11/1051885824..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Sgt Guenigault was an emergency response officer based in Southwark and was living in Bexley, south-east London, at the time of the attack on 3 June 2017.

He had finished his shift and was heading home after watching the Champions League final with friends when the violence unfolded.

After responding to a member of the public who had been stabbed, he went to help two British Transport Police officers who were being attacked.

Although off duty, unarmed and without protective equipment, he ran towards the attackers and tried to distract them and protect those nearby.

He pushed one attacker away from the two officers, suffering a stab wound to the back of his neck. He then moved further into the group, where he was stabbed repeatedly in the face, back and hands. Despite his injuries, he managed to lie still after collapsing to the ground.

Members of the public, including Ellen Gauntlett and Justin Jones, stayed with him and provided first aid until he could be taken to hospital.

He spent a prolonged period in critical care at King's College Hospital and required further operations for injuries to his lungs and stomach.

![Sgt Guenigault receiving his George Medal from the Queen](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/19/11/43/BNPS_LondonterrorHeroMedal_04.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The three attackers had driven a van into pedestrians on London Bridge before continuing their assault with knives in Borough Market. Eight people were killed and 48 injured before the terrorists were shot dead by police.

Sgt Guenigault received the George Medal for "confronting armed terrorists to protect others".

The award was presented by the late Queen at Buckingham Palace in October 2018.

He also received a Police Bravery Award, which he shared with the late PC Keith Palmer, and a Pride of Britain Award.

Reflecting on the significance of the George Medal, Sgt Guenigault said he had never taken the honour for granted, particularly knowing that some recipients had died and received their awards posthumously.

He said the auction proceeds would help him look towards a more positive future with his family – but the buyer's decision means he will also be able to keep the medal that recognises his actions that night.
