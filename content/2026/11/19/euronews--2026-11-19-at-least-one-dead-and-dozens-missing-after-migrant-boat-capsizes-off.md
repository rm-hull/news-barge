## The latest sinking comes amid a sharp rise in departures towards the Canary Islands, with more than 11,000 people reaching the Spanish archipelago in the first two weeks of November.

At least one person has died, and dozens more are missing after a boat carrying around 100 migrants capsized off the coast of Senegal while attempting to reach Spain, local officials have said.

The latest incident, which is thought to have happened in the early hours of Wednesday morning, comes amid a surge in departures towards the Canary Islands, with more than 11,000 people reaching the archipelago in the first half of November alone.

The boat, described by some sources as “rickety”, sank off the coast of Yenne, a town south of the Senegalese capital, Dakar.

“The accident happened around 3 a.m.," said Ousmane Sène, secretary general of the Yenne mayor's office.

“When we went to the scene, we saw the boat on the shore.”

Sène added that firefighters recovered one body while police arrested one survivor.

According to other survivors, there were around 100 people on board at the time it sank.

Sène confirmed that two other bodies were also recovered in nearby villages, but it’s not clear if they were people who drowned in this particular incident.

Recently, Senegalese coastal areas have become major departure points for migrants attempting the treacherous sea journey to Europe. Many of them are hoping to reach Spain’s Canary Islands, an archipelago off West Africa which lies more than 1,000 kilometres south of the European mainland.

According to Spanish government figures, more than 11,000 migrants reached the Canaries in the first half of November – that number was more than half the total for all of 2025.

This week’s sinking comes shortly after a disaster which saw at least 39 people die after a migrant boat sank near the town of Kébémer in northern Senegal.

Senegalese authorities are making efforts to crack down on people attempting the dangerous migration route and have made dozens of arrests.

On Wednesday, the country’s navy said it had “rescued” over 1,400 migrants at sea in just 48 hours.

Also on Wednesday, Senegalese President Bassirou Diomaye Faye spoke out against the resurgence of migrant departures toward Europe and called for strengthened controls along Senegal's coasts, too.

“The acceleration and complexity of irregular migration demonstrate the urgency for the government to intensify... the diligent and efficient implementation of public policies concerning youth, education, vocational training, youth employment and entrepreneurship, as well as the economic empowerment of women,” said Faye.
