Eight people were injured on Wednesday evening when a driver struck Palestinian residents at four separate locations in Kafr Qasim, a city in central Israel.

Footage shared on social media appears to show the car accelerating towards people, all Palestinians, at several locations in the city, striking them before driving away. Three of those injured were in moderate condition, and several of the victims were women leaving a nearby wedding, the Times of Israel reported.

Haaretz has reported that investigators are examining whether the driver struck people deliberately. Avi Levy, a police district commander, said the incident appeared to be a hit-and-run and that the driver had tested positive for drugs. He said the Shin Bet security service was taking part in the investigation and said: “As of this moment, nothing is coming up. The suspect has no criminal record.”

The incident prompted Arab lawmakers and activists to accuse Israeli politicians of fuelling hostility towards Arab citizens. Benjamin Netanyahu has called Arab parties “terror supporters” and said partnering with them would put Israel’s security at risk. The parties won a record 19 seats in the Knesset in the country’s recent election.

Yousef Jabareen, the chair of the Joint List, said the incident “raises difficult questions about the claim that the driver lost control”. He said: “I’ve been warning for weeks that the incitement against Arab citizens will turn into assaults and even assassinations.”

Said Issa, a community activist in Kafr Qasim, described the incident as an “act of terror”. “Someone who loses control of his vehicle might hit one or two vehicles, but not multiple people in several different places,” Issa said. “The attacker struck at four locations, one after the other.”

In a separate incident in the occupied West Bank, the Palestinian foreign ministry called on Monday for an Israeli settler to be held accountable after eight Palestinians were run over and injured in Tuqu, south-east of Bethlehem. Local sources told the Anadolu news agency that the driver had first struck three young men, then returned and ran over five others who had gathered to help them.
