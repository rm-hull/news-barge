The woman who steered the Trump administration’s anti-DEI crusade and attempts to block transgender athletes from women’s sports is now calling out “bigotry” in the Republican Party.

Harmeet Dhillon, who announced her resignation as chief of the storied Civil Rights Division at the Department of Justice two days after the midterms, told NewsNation that she is now “free” to speak her mind and “call out what’s right and what’s wrong.”

Dhillon has returned to her private law firm, where she previously mounted high-profile legal battles involving gender-affirming healthcare, school policies on gender identity, and Donald Trump’s attempts to reverse his 2020 election loss.

Inside the Civil Rights Division, Dhillon scrapped investigations into police departments accused of racial discrimination, tossed out more than 50 school desegregation cases and dropped lawsuits intended to protect voting rights.

Instead, she pivoted to aggressive intervention in elections and directed the DOJ to root out what she called “woke ideology,” including transgender athletes in girls’ and women’s sports, “anti-Christian bias,” and institutions with diversity, equity and inclusion initiatives.

![Harmeet Dhillon, who ran the Justice Department’s Civil Rights Division, before resigning after the midterms , says the Republican Party must address ‘bigotry’ if it wants to win elections again](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/06/16/22/2278801248..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Her leadership faced widespread criticism from civil rights groups who accused her of wildly departing from the division’s core mission to combat discrimination and protect the rights of historically marginalized groups.

Dhillon, who was born in India and is a member of the Sikh faith, has also continued to face racial and ethnic discrimination over her religious background in the years after she delivered a Sikh prayer during the 2024 Republican National Convention.

“It’s unfortunate because a political party is not a religion, and you can only win elections through addition, not subtraction,” she told NewsNation Wednesday.

“My religion doesn’t proselytize,” she said. “The reason I did this prayer is the same reason why Jews and people of other faiths do prayers at a national convention, which is to show the breadth of acceptance of other people in the party.”

Dhillon suggested that she was restricted from speaking out against bigotry within the ranks of the Republican Party while working for the Trump administration.

“The higher up you are in the administration, the more the White House and whatever agency you work for controls your media interviews and what you say. I’ve honored those restrictions, and the day I resigned I was free from those restrictions,” she told NewsNation.

“And I think it is very important for the future of this country that we have a vibrant Republican Party, and that means that this type of drift into bigotry and antisemitism needs to be called out and stopped,” she added. “We have to not be afraid to call out what’s right and what’s wrong. I’ve gotten to that point in my career where I really don’t care about what people think.”

![Dhillon, who faced racial and religious discrimination after delivering a Sikh prayer at the 2024 RNC, steered the DOJ’s anti-transgender and anti-DEI push as chief of the Civil Rights Division under Trump](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/19/17/2161596798..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

She said some Republicans want to “squander” the party’s potential by excluding minority groups.

“We’ll never win an election as a Republican Party if we’re practicing subtraction and not addition,” she added. “I do have a big voice, and I kind of got tired of not being able to defend myself.”

In her final months in office, Dhillon spearheaded the Justice Department’s failed attempts to extract voter data from more than two dozen states before the midterms over allegations of widespread noncitizen voter fraud, part of the Trump administration’s wider anti-immigration campaign.

She also mounted the largest-ever deployment of election monitors in Justice Department history, with more than 900 election observers sent to polling locations in all 50 states.

Under her leadership, Dhillon lost more than three quarters of the unit’s workforce, a move she called the “biggest single personnel self-purge in DOJ history.” In an interview with right-wing personality Glenn Beck last year, she said she welcomed the departure of career prosecutors, saying “the job here is to enforce the federal civil rights laws — not woke ideology.”

![Dhillon says she is now ‘free’ to speak her mind after leaving the administration and is warning that the GOP’s ‘drift into bigotry and antisemitism’ will lead to more election losses](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/19/17/1246596607..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“She has defended the civil liberties of every American and enforced the law without fear or favor,” Attorney General Todd Blanche said November 5. “The Department is grateful for her dedicated public service, and we wish her every success in her future endeavors.”

Stacey Young, a former Civil Rights Division attorney who now leads a network of Justice Department alumni, called Dhillon “the most destructive, lawless and hateful” division chief in its history.

“Americans’ civil rights don’t enforce themselves,” Young said in a statement after Dhillon’s resignation. “For almost 70 years, the Civil Rights Division brought to fruition the civil rights laws that Americans demanded. Dhillon decimated it.”

Dhillon used the Justice Department to “target the LGBTQ+ community,” according to Sharon McGowan, vice president of policy and litigation at the Human Rights Campaign, the nation’s largest LGBTQ+ civil rights group. Dhillon prioritized “white grievance and partisan allegiance to Trump’s anti-civil rights agenda,” added Janai Nelson, president of the NAACP Legal Defense Fund.

With her return to Dhillon Law Group, she will spearhead what the firm calls an “Anti-Woke Corporate Accountability Initiative” to drive out DEI in the workplace and a First Amendment campaign to defend “businesses, entrepreneurs and individuals” who say they have been accused for their “political beliefs, religious convictions or lawful business activities.”

“I am returning to my private practice to do what I have done my entire career, take on powerful institutions when they violate the law,” Dhillon said in a statement announcing her return to the firm.

“We will take the fight against illegal DEI into corporate boardrooms and courtrooms, stand up for American workers and businesses facing ideological discrimination,” she added.

“The First Amendment has been my passion for decades, and we are going to defend it aggressively.”
