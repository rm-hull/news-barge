Lindsay Clancy's lawyers filed an appeal to Massachusett’s supreme judicial court over the mistrial that halted her first trial.

At its heart is her lawyer's view that the judge in the first murder trial should have questioned the lone holdout juror before declaring a mistrial.

Clancy, 36, is a mother of three who faced first-degree murder charges over the deaths of her daughter Cora, 5; son Dawson, 3; and 8-month-old son Callan.

“Respectfully, contrary to the trial court's stated belief at the time, it did have viable choices other than declaring a mistrial... Any successive trial, currently scheduled to begin on March 29, 2027, would violate Ms. Clancy's Double Jeopardy rights,” the appeal reads.

In Massachusetts, appeals in first-degree murder cases go directly to the state's supreme judicial court.

![Lindsay Clancy in court on Monday, Nov. 16, 2026, in Plymouth, Mass.](https://static.the-independent.com/2026/11/16/15/download..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The appeal does not explicitly state that questioning the juror would have changed the outcome, but that the judge's failure to question the juror was an error.

“Without inquiry, there can only be guesswork, which does not satisfy the high standard of manifest necessity,” the appeal continues.

Clancy will face a second murder trial after her first ended without a verdict, a Massachusetts judge has ruled. Judge William Sullivan set the new trial for March 29 at a hearing on Monday in Plymouth Superior Court. Prosecutors had pushed for a May start because of scheduling conflicts, but Sullivan settled on March 29.

![Defendant Lindsay Clancy, the Massachusetts woman whose trial on murder charges for killing her three children ended in a mistrial in September, sits beside attorney Kevin Reddington in court during a hearing that set a date for her re-trial, in Plymouth Superior Court](https://static.the-independent.com/2026/11/19/10/2026-11-16T154931Z_833172471_RC23POA1KO2W_RTRMADP_3_MASSACHUSETTS-CRIME-CLANCY.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

A conviction on those charges would carry life in prison without parole.

Her first trial collapsed on Sept. 4, when a single holdout juror prevented the 12-member jury from reaching a unanimous verdict on the murder charges.

Some jurors said in TV interviews after the mistrial that they had been prepared to find Clancy not guilty by reason of insanity, but one juror held out.

That holdout juror, Michael Desronvil, said in an interview that he was struck by Clancy's “thought process” during the killings and that he thought she was very “methodical” while strangling her children.

Sullivan had already rejected, after the mistrial, Clancy's argument that the case should be dismissed because a second trial would violate her constitutional rights.

“Because the court concludes that there was manifest necessity for a mistrial based on the jury's deadlock, a retrial on the murder indictments does not violate Clancy's right not to be placed in double jeopardy,” Sullivan wrote last week.

He also denied prosecutors' request for a gag order, to avoid influencing a future jury, while the case remains pending.

Clancy's lawyers argued a gag order would've amounted to an unconstitutional restraint on speech.

Her attorney Kevin Reddington gave interviews during the trial, while prosecutors generally didn't speak to reporters.

![Lindsay Clancy’s ex-husband testified at her first trial and has said he forgives her](https://static.the-independent.com/2026/08/25/14/47/2968733-library-6ae83e71.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Her lawyers have argued she should be found not guilty by reason of insanity, a defence known in Massachusetts as lack of criminal responsibility.

A verdict of not guilty by reason of insanity would have meant Clancy being sent to a state psychiatric hospital, subject to regular court review.

No one at the first trial disputed that Clancy strangled her children with exercise bands on Jan. 24, 2023, in the basement of the family's home in the Boston suburb of Duxbury.

Her lawyer argued at trial that she was in the throes of postpartum psychosis when she killed her children at their home in 2023.

Immediately after the children's killings, Clancy cut herself with a knife and jumped from a second-story window in an attempt to end her own life, leaving her paralyzed.

Her ex-husband, Patrick Clancy, found the children's bodies.

He has said in interviews that he forgives her.

He revealed in a 60 Minutes interview in September that he and his new wife, a fertility doctor, are starting a new family and plan on sharing the memories of his lost children with their future brothers or sisters.

Clancy is due back in court at 9 a.m. on Dec. 30 to discuss trial logistics, security and media arrangements.

A pretrial conference is set for Feb. 18.
