An autopsy has revealed new details about the death of a school teacher who plunged from a Six Flags rollercoaster.

​Hassan Jihad Ibn Kariem died on October 12 after he was ejected from the Goliath roller coaster at Six Flags over Georgia in Austell, a city around 20 miles from Atlanta.

​The 42-year-old’s death came weeks after Six Flags Magic Mountain was hit with a series of lawsuits alleging that one of its own rides caused traumatic brain injuries.

​Kariem was determined to have died from blunt force injuries after falling onto the roof of a park building, according to an autopsy report reviewed by 11 Alive. He was also found to have extensive injuries across his body, including his legs, arms, torso, neck, and head.

​At the time of his death, the Cobb County Police Department announced that it had launched an investigation into the school teacher’s death after he was flung from the ride and struck several other park guests before falling.

![​Hassan Jihad Ibn Kariem died on October 12 after he was ejected from the Goliath roller coaster at Six Flags over Georgia](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/15/22/Roller_Coaster_Death_594_6.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

​“He didn’t fall directly onto us; he fell onto a girl two rows in front of us,” a witness recounted to 11 Alive. “He fell and hit the cart three different times; they got it right where the pictures for the Goliath is tooken (sic). He was in our pictures.”​

In a statement, Six Flags CEO John Reilly said that seat belts would be added to the Goliath ride.

​“I know trust is earned through actions and not words,” he said. “As CEO of Six Flags, I take that responsibility personally.”

Initially, the organization had closed nine roller coasters across its parks - including Goliath - following the death.

​Kariem was sitting beside his niece when he fell from the ride, a family member told ABC News. They had traveled to the park to celebrate her birthday.

“She said he fainted or passed - her words were ‘he passed out,’” Joshua Nelson, the girl’s father and Kariem’s brother-in-law, told WSB-TV. “And she said it went up again and his latch, I guess, snapped or came loose and he fell.”

![Goliath (pictured) was closed by the park, which says that seat belts will be installed on the roller coaster](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/15/09/2299688226..?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&trim=0%2C0%2C0%2C0&auto=webp)

​The autopsy does not list any findings about the ride’s safety restraints but does note that the man was 5’9” and weighed 301 pounds, 11 Alive reports.

​Kariem’s LinkedIn page shows that he had worked as a technology teacher at Wesley International Academy from 2014 to 2023, before becoming the Atlanta-based school’s IT manager.

​In a statement obtained by *PEOPLE*, a representative for Atlanta Public Schools shared the organization’s condolences with Kariem’s family.

​“He was a cherished member of the Wesley International Academy and APS families whose presence and impact will be deeply missed by our students, staff, and all those whose lives he touched,” the spokesperson said. “Our thoughts are with everyone mourning his loss during this difficult time.”

![Kariem (shown here in an undated photo) worked at a school in Atlanta. He died at the age of 42](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/16/14/Roller_Coaster_Death_35233.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Family members told WSB-TV that Kariem had previously survived cancer twice. He and his daughter also survived a car fire just three weeks before his death.

​Kariem’s death came just weeks after the permanent closure of the X2 roller coaster at Six Flags Magic Mountain, which followed the filing of a series of lawsuits alleging that the ride caused traumatic brain injuries.

​The ride was billed by the amusement park as a “rite of passage for the ultimate daredevil” and reached speeds of 76 mph.
