Australian prime minister Anthony Albanese faced sharp criticism by opposition and Greens politicians after he joined a group photograph with Russian president Vladimir Putin at a summit of Asia-Pacific leaders in China despite calls to sit it out over the war in Ukraine.

Mr Albanese posed for the family photo at a gala dinner for leaders at the Apec summit in the Chinese city of Shenzhen on Wednesday. He stood in the back row, metres from Mr Putin, who took the centre stage alongside Chinese leader Xi Jinping and US president Donald Trump. This was Mr Putin’s first attendance at Apec in nearly a decade.

Mr Albanese said he did not want to disrespect Mr Xi as the host. He told journalists on the sidelines of the summit: "I will bear in mind the wishes of our host, but we'll also make our position very clear when it comes to Ukraine."

David Shoebridge, the Greens leader who has opposed sending military aid and weapons to Ukraine, said Mr Albanese should have taken a tougher stance.

“We are seeing the prime minister travel the world, getting mugshots with these powerful hard-right figures. One day it's Modi from India, the next day is Trump from the United States, and now it's Putin from Russia,” he told reporters.

"It's about time that he started expressing Australians' values which seem to be a very long way away from who he chooses to do his photo ops with."

The Australian Federation of Ukrainian Organisations had urged Mr Albanese not to appear in the photo, saying it would ignore Russia's gross violations of international norms. Vasyl Myroshnychenko, Ukraine's ambassador to Australia, told SBS News he hoped Mr Albanese would "continue to be guided by Australia's very prominent pro-Ukraine stance". He said: "Substance matters more to Ukraine than style."

Deputy prime minister Richard Marles defended the leader. Asked on *Today* whether it made him uncomfortable to see Mr Albanese "all smiles" near a man accused of war crimes, Mr Marles said Apec was a very important gathering for Australia because the vast bulk of its trade happened within the region.

"When the host puts together the family photo, you respect what the host does, but let's be really clear: Australia's position in relation to the war in Ukraine is utterly steadfast. We utterly oppose Russia's illegal and immoral invasion of Ukraine, and we have stood by Ukraine. And a family photo doesn't change any of that."

Ahead of his address to the summit on Wednesday, Mr Albanese called for "more dialogue, less military conflict" in Ukraine and the Middle East.

"We do say that any solution and peace which we want to see in Ukraine needs to be on Ukraine's terms."

![US president Donald Trump, left, speaks during a meeting with Chinese president Xi Jinping on the sidelines of the Apec summit in Shenzhen](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/19/06/download.-1..jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Mr Putin used the summit to condemn European sanctions on Moscow, which were put in place after Russia invaded Ukraine in February 2022. He said at the summit: "Artificial barriers at one level of the value chain lead to costs across all other links." He claimed Europe was experiencing a "humiliating decline".

Ukrainian president Volodymyr Zelensky, who is absent from the summit, said in a post on social media that any issues concerning his country should be decided with his participation. He said: "This is the only approach there can ever be."

Asked whether he would follow former prime minister Tony Abbott, who said in 2014 that he would "shirtfront" Mr Putin, a term for a physical confrontation, at the G20 summit in Brisbane over the shooting down of Malaysia Airlines flight MH17, Mr Albanese said he would not take lessons in diplomacy from his predecessor. Thirty-eight Australians were killed when the plane was shot down over eastern Ukraine.

The photo preceded talks on Thursday between Mr Trump, Mr Xi and Mr Putin, the first meeting of its kind between the three leaders. The Ukraine conflict and the US-Israeli war on Iran are believed to be up for discussion.
