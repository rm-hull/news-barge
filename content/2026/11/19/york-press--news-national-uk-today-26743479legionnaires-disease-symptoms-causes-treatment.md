## Legionnaires’ disease cases reported in the UK

A Legionnaires’ disease outbreak was reported in Rotherham in South Yorkshire in September, and two people have died after contracting it.

However, the UKHSA said it was “not currently confirmed if the disease contributed to, or caused the deaths”, reports the BBC.

The health agency has reassured the public that the risk of more outbreaks “remains low”.

A “small number of cases” were reported in the Kimberworth Park area but no more have been confirmed since early October.

## What is Legionnaires’ disease?

Ian Budd, lead prescribing pharmacist at Chemist4U, said: “Following reports of an outbreak of Legionnaires’ disease in South Yorkshire, there may be concern among the wider public.

“Legionnaires’ disease is a lung infection caused by the bacteria ‘Legionella’, and while it is not particularly common in the UK, it can be very serious.”

## How is Legionnaires’ disease passed on?

The pharmacist explains that this disease is often transmitted when someone breathes in tiny droplets of water that contain Legionella bacteria.

You can find contaminated droplets from various common water sources such as showers and taps.

Mr Budd also explains that they can come from cooling systems, humidifiers and hot tubs, especially when the water systems aren’t maintained properly.

He adds: “Importantly, Legionnaires’ disease is not normally passed from person to person, and simply drinking water containing the bacteria does not usually cause infection.

“Although Legionella bacteria can occur naturally in sources such as rivers and lakes, infections are more commonly associated with contaminated man-made water systems that allow the bacteria to multiply and produce airborne droplets.”

## What are the symptoms of Legionnaires’ disease?

If you have Legionnaires’ disease, you’ll initially have flu-like symptoms.

Mr Budd explains: “The symptoms of Legionnaires’ disease initially resemble the flu, meaning that especially at this time of the year, when respiratory infections can spread quickly, it is important to monitor your symptoms closely.”

## Ambulance response categories explained

You can expect early symptoms to include:

* Cough
* Fatigue
* Headache
* High temperature

Some people may also experience:

* Shortness of breath
* Chest pain or discomfort
* Coughing up blood
* Diarrhoea
* Confusion

If you have these symptoms, you need to make sure you tell a healthcare professional if you have recently been anywhere the bacteria could have entered the water supply, including hotels, hospitals or spas.

## How is Legionnaires’ disease treated?

Antibiotics are the most common way to treat the disease, but more serious cases could need hospital treatment.

This can involve oxygen therapy or, in very severe cases, assistance with breathing.

Mr Budd warns: “These symptoms should never be ignored.

“Legionnaires’ disease can cause severe pneumonia and other health complications; it is important that you seek medical advice by contacting your GP or NHS 111.”
