A neo-Nazi-obsessed teenager who encouraged strangers to self-harm online has avoided a custodial sentence.

Judge Mark Dennis KC instead imposed a three-year youth rehabilitation, which carries intensive supervision, at the Old Bailey on Thursday.

The judge also barred the 17-year-old from unauthorised access to the internet and handed down a criminal behaviour order.

The youth, who is from Cambridgeshire and cannot be identified for legal reasons, had admitted a string of offences connected to his “right-wing activities” online and in the real world between January 2025 and January 2026, when he was aged between 15 and 16.

His guilty pleas covered charges of collecting information for terrorist purposes, encouraging or assisting serious self-harm, two charges of making indecent photos of a child, possession of cannabis and two counts of damaging property.

It was in the “best interests” of the defendant and public not to lock him up, said the judge.

Judge Dennis noted the defendant had shown “disturbing behaviour” from the age of 11 but said he was motivated to achieve “shock value” and “notoriety” rather than by “genuine ideology”.

The court heard the defendant had developed an interest in the proscribed Maniac Murder Cult (MMC), a neo-Nazi group on the Com network linked to several violent attacks globally.

![Police first raided the youth’s address in April 2025](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/18/10/2305163430..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Among material recovered by officers was a video of the youth dressed in black with his hood up, forming the letters MMC with his hands before using a knife to cut the neck of a soft toy animal.

A further montage showed him spray painting graffiti on a local building and stabbing a car tyre with a knife, the Old Bailey heard.

Hand-drawn swastikas were found by the court to have been put by the defendant on posters and clothing.

Judge Dennis said: “This offending would appear to be directly connected to an increasing addiction to an area of the dark web, namely Com network.”

When police first raided his address on 2 April 2025, officers uncovered a “manifesto” in which the youth claimed to be a “racist and Nazi”, the court heard.

In it, he stated: “I live a sh\*\*\*\* life and no one likes me. I’m a loser which gives me more of a reason so do what I have to do?”

He described a plan to take two guns and kill “at least 3-5 or wound at least 6 and kill myself”.

Officers also found a “call to arms” manifesto of a man who killed two people in a shooting at a gay bar in Bratislava in 2022.

It contained practical terrorist information, including tips on mental preparation for an attack and how to assess targets. A stash of indecent pictures and videos of young children was also held by the youth, the court heard. Other content depicted dismemberment of animals and videos of self-harm.

A further review of his computer showed that he had encouraged Discord users to self-harm, the court was told.

On 28 January 2026, the defendant created a network on Telegram entitled Inferno in which he encouraged self-harm and rape. Prosecutor Lisa Goddard said the defendant knew some of the members of the Inferno network, not just online.

When one user offered to rape his girlfriend and revealed she was aged 21, the youth responded: “Why not 12. Or younger?”

In chat about targeting a female online, he told a user to invite her to join the Inferno group so she could “cut on cam”, adding that he wanted her to “set herself on fire for us”.

He also directed others to seek out self-harm posts on Twitter and “bully” them.

Ms Goddard said that it was “relevant” that by this time the defendant had been charged with other offences and was awaited an appearance in youth court. On 30 January, police found 16 grams of cannabis in a black bag belonging to the defendant.

In an earlier police interview in July 2025, the youth, who has been diagnosed with attention deficit hyperactivity disorder (ADHD), said he had done things “for a joke”.

In August 2025, the defendant, then on police bail, damaged a local village hall and sports pavilion with a group of friends, causing some £6,000 worth of damage.

Judge Dennis said: “This was a targeted act on both occasions with the intention of causing significant damage and motivation would appear to be to obtain imagery content for their Com network group.”

![The youth told police he had done things ‘for a joke’](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/18/10/2305163258..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

In mitigation, Kieran Galvin said the defendant wanted to say he was “sorry”.

Mr Galvin said: “The person behind the keyboard on the internet is not the person this young boy is.”

Amid an “arms race” of online “braggadocio”, he was in reality a “shy and timid person” who did not hold any deep-seated racist views, Mr Galvin said. During the sentencing, Judge Dennis KC criticised internet networks for failing to protect young people.

He said: “This case is not about right-wing extremism.

“This is all about internet abuse in which the young in particular are vulnerable in respect of which there seems to be inadequate protection or control from the networks – or whoever – to stop it happening in the first place or stop it continuing.”
