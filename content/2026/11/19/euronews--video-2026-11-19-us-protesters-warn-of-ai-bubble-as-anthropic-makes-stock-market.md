**Priced at $83 a share, the artificial intelligence company behind the Claude chatbot raised over $80 billion and achieved a $1.5 trillion valuation** on its Wall Street debut. Inside Nasdaq, co-founders**Dario and Daniela Amodei** rang the opening bell to celebrate the start of trading; outside, protesters chanted against the flotation and the concentration of tech-sector wealth in a demonstration that saw**three people detained by police**.

The listing **overtook SpaceX’s blockbuster June offering**, highlighting relentless investor enthusiasm for AI despite growing talk of overheated technology valuations. Robust demand carried the share price clear of its earlier**$70 to $78 guide range**. Yet demonstrators warned that ordinary investors could pay the price if the AI surge ends in a financial bust. Jose Gonzalez, among those protesting outside, questioned who would bear the brunt of a market reversal, arguing that wealthy investors enjoy protections unavailable to the public.

The meteoric surge of artificial intelligence is sparking hope and alarm in equal measure. While **global spending on AI approaches $850 billion in 2026** and enthusiasts hail a sweeping economic and technological revolution, economists and watchdogs warn of**a looming financial bubble, swelling debt and growing inequality,** alongside fears that automation will wipe out jobs and funnel vast wealth into the coffers of a few tech titans.
