The wreck of the first Australian warship built in the country was found 128 years to the day after it sank off the north coast of Queensland state, officials said on Thursday.

HMCS Spitfire was launched in Sydney in 1855 to fight a potential Russian invasion. But the 19-metre schooner had been operating as a fishing boat four decades later when it sprung a leak and foundered on 21 August 1898, off Piper Island.

A Royal Australian Navy mine-hunter vessel found and exposed 16m of the wreck three months ago, also on 21 August, after a search using a dive team and remotely operated vehicles, a joint statement from multiple agencies said.

“It is remarkable luck that Spitfire was discovered on the 128th anniversary of its sinking,” Queensland heritage officer Celeste Jordan said in the statement.

The discovery was not announced sooner because of reporting processes between government agencies required under laws designed to protect shipwrecks, the Defence Department said on Thursday.

## Built during Crimean War over fear of Russian invasion

Bearing the designation of HMCS, meaning Her Majesty’s Colonial Schooner, it was commissioned in 1855 during the Crimean War when the British colonial government of New South Wales in Sydney feared the Russian Pacific Fleet would invade, Australian National Maritime Museum archaeologist James Hunter said.

Britain was at war with Russia in a conflict centred on the Crimean Peninsula. Gold had recently been found in New South Wales and the neighbouring colony of Victoria, making them lucrative targets for conquest.

The Australian colonies relied on Britain’s navy for defence but British warships were rare in the most distant reaches of the empire.

The Crimean War ended in 1856 without the Australian two-mast sailing vessel ever being used in any battle. Spitfire became a pilot vessel in 1857 at Moreton Bay in what would soon become the Colony of Queensland, but was then still part of New South Wales.

The Queensland government sold Spitfire in 1885 into private ownership for use as a fishing boat.

## Sydney shipbuilder quickly delivered warship

Shipbuilder John Cuthbert delivered Spitfire only two months after winning the contract. The speed indicated the warship’s construction had already been underway for a different purpose, Mr Hunter said.

It was built from native timbers, including an ironbark keel, blackbutt frames and kauri planking. The ship had copper sheathing below the waterline.

Russia’s Pacific Fleet then was mostly iron-hulled, steam-powered and “armed to the teeth warships”, Mr Hunter told the ABC.

Spitfire was armed with a single cannon that fired 14.5kg cannonballs. “Would that have bounced off the side of one of those larger, Russian iron-hulled warships? Probably,” Mr Hunter said.

There are no plans to salvage the wreck, which is largely buried in seabed sand. Authorities are yet to reveal Spitfire's exact location because it lies at a depth that can be reached by scuba divers.

The ship is protected by federal and state law.

Vice admiral Matthew Buckley, chief of the Royal Australian Navy, said that Spitfire represented the beginning of the service's continuing story.

“Spitfire occupies a unique place in Australia’s naval history as the first warship built in Australia for Australian service,” he said.
