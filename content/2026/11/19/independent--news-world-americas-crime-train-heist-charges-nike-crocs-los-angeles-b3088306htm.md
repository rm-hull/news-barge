More than a dozen people have been charged over a massive train heist operation targeting $22 million in cargo from major retailers.

A federal indictment unsealed Thursday describes how an organized group targeted more than three dozen trains carrying Nike sneakers, Crocs footwear, power tools and other consumer goods that were imported into the United States through Los Angeles and Long Beach, which are home to the busiest ports in the country.

Trains that transport products to stores and customers around the country have become a big target for large-scale theft in recent years, especially at large ports in the U.S. that receive hundreds of millions of dollars of shipments every day. The relatively low-tech robberies have affected the world's largest retailers, including the likes of Amazon.

The men would disable the trains at a planned location by busting the brakes, the indictment alleged, at which point the goods would be offloaded onto trucks. The goods were then peddled to an unnamed company that would resell the stolen merchandise for profit.

“The scale of the theft is staggering,” said First Assistant United States Attorney Bill Essayli, promising to prosecute any future efforts to steal the merchandise.

The men shared numerous photos and videos in group chats of themselves busting into the hulking train cars and posing with wads of cash and stolen merchandise.

They also shared AI photos of themselves captioned “Los Trenes,” which means the trains in Spanish, beneath glamorized photos of men throwing boxes of shoes off trains into nearby cars as police helicopters descended overhead.

Thirteen men were indicted for conspiracy and theft of interstate railroad cargo in connection with the heists and two were also charged with violence against railroad carriers.

If convicted, the men could face anywhere from five to 20 years in federal prison.
