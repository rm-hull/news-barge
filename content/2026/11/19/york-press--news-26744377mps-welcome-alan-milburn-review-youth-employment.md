## York and North Yorkshire MPs welcome Alan Milburn report on jobless youth

L-R: Kevin Hollinrake, Luke Charters, and Rachael Maskell *(Image: NQ)*

A landmark youth employment review has been welcomed in general by York and North Yorkshire MPs.

The review, led by former Labour Cabinet Minister Alan Milburn, examines the reasons why nearly one million young people across the UK are not in education, employment, or training, and calls for major reforms to help them into work.

Rachael Maskell, MP for York Central, said the review offers an opportunity to transform support for young people, but she warned that success will require coordinated action at every level.

Ms Maskell said: "For those not in work, this package provides an enormous opportunity.




![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/17073030.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Rachael Maskell, Labour MP for York Central *(Image: Pic supplied)*

"We cannot have 1 million young people written off.

"However, this is going to need a whole city effort to really ensure that the investments we make in our young people will bring significant benefit to them and our future."

She outlined three key areas of focus following the review.

First, she called for a rapid expansion of further education, highlighting long-standing issues with low pay, staff recruitment, and restrictive entry requirements in the sector.

She said she will work with the Government, York College, and Askham Bryan College to help shape the future of further education.

Second, Ms Maskell pointed to the need for more tailored employment opportunities in York Central, where 400 people are registered as unemployed.

She said employers should work with her and the Youth Hub to offer roles to every young person seeking work, including those with complex health needs.

Third, she raised concerns about changes to disability support, particularly the move from Personal Independence Payment (PIP) to new forms of assistance.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21738642.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Luke Charters, Labour MP for York Outer *(Image: Pic supplied)*

Ms Maskell said it is essential that no young person is pushed into poverty by the reforms.

Luke Charters, MP for York Outer, also welcomed the review and said too many young people have been failed by past policies.

Mr Charters said: "Every young person deserves the best possible start in life, but for too long successive governments have failed to deliver that.

"Nearly a million young people are now out of education, employment or training.

"That's a crisis, and we can't just accept it."

He said the issue is personal to him as the youngest parent in Parliament, and that he regularly hears concerns from families in York.

Mr Charters praised York’s educational institutions but acknowledged that many young people still face barriers to employment and training.

He said: "That's why I welcome Alan Milburn's review.

"We've got to give young people a fair chance, whatever their background.

"There's some really important work already underway, but we need to go further.

"We simply cannot afford to let another generation down."

The review coincides with the Government’s £2.5 billion investment in the Youth Guarantee and Growth and Skills Levy.

The funding aims to support nearly one million young people over three years through expanded apprenticeships, work placements, personalised employment support, and financial incentives for employers.

Mr Charters said it is vital that young people in York benefit from the full range of new opportunities.

Conservative MP for Thirsk and Malton, Kevin Hollinrake, said the review is welcome, but he warned that current government policy is making it harder for businesses to hire young people.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21738613.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Kevin Hollinrake, Conservative MP for Thirsk and Malton *(Image: Pic supplied)*

This was something he is told when meeting businesses, yet as an apprenticeship fair he hosted in Thirsk showed, there is much potential for them delivering opportunities.

Mr Hollinrake said: "The Government has made it more expensive and more difficult for businesses to employ people, particularly young people looking for their first job.

"Higher employers' National Insurance contributions, rising wage bills and additional employment regulations are putting enormous pressure on businesses already struggling with costs."

He argued that the Conservative approach—cutting the “jobs tax” for under-25s and reducing business rates for high street employers—would do more to create real, sustainable jobs.

Mr Hollinrake said: "Young people deserve more than another government scheme.

"They deserve a real job, the independence that comes with earning a living and the chance to build a successful future."

"Because ultimately, governments don't create sustainable jobs. Businesses do."
