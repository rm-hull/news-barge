I'm not quite sure what to make of this so I'm going to just put it out there: A new Fallout game might soon be headed our way, and it's a hidden object game.

A Wasteland Full of Cats, as it's known, recently appeared on the Australian Classification Board website (via the Fallout subreddit), and the title pretty much says it all. From the rating:

*This game is a hidden object game and the objects you need to find are cats. You play as one of the cats in the Vault. One day there is an accident in the Vault and all of the cats who live there escape out into the Wasteland. It is up to you with the help of some NPCs you meet along the way to find them and return them to the Vault. This game has 14 locations, 33 rooms + a vault of recognizable levels from the Fallout franchise with light puzzle mechanics that uncover some hidden levels that aren’t required to finish the game. There is a final boss fight where the player has to shoot some Rad roaches in order to save the last remaining cats and humans.*

The rating says A Wasteland Full of Cats is being made by Devcats, the Brazilian studio behind the Full of Cats game series, and—this is the really interesting part—it's being made "in partnership with Bethesda and the Fallout franchise."

Is this legit? Is the next Fallout release a hidden object game? I don't know, but I do know that the Australian Classification Board generally isn't party to pranks and hoaxes. I also know that a lot of Fallout fans will be rolling their eyes and moaning at the idea of hidden object Fallout, but hear me out: Hidden object games are good, actually.

Sure, you're not going to be prowling through horrific underground labs or gunning down super mutants in a life-or-death struggle for survival. But hidden object games are chill and relaxing, a great way to wind down after a busy day robbing liquor stores and shooting cops with your significant other. They're a simpler kind of fun, which makes them kind of kin to the really early days of videogames, when we were all ready to accept that a blocky blob of pink on the screen was a dragon.

All of which is to say that even if you have no interest in playing a Fallout hidden object game (although I think you should), it's far from unthinkable that Bethesda would make one—or have someone make one for it, I suppose. (I would also point out that you could say basically the same thing about Fallout Shelter, and it's been chugging happily along for well over a decade now.)
