President Donald Trump’s nominee for director of the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency has refused to call the riot at the U.S. Capitol nearly six years ago an attack.

Michael LoSapio, who is currently the chief information security officer at software firm Palantir, was grilled by Senator Richard Blumenthal, a Connecticut Democrat, during his confirmation hearing before the Senate Homeland Security Committee on Thursday.

“Was there an attack on the Capitol on Jan. 6, 2021?” Blumenthal asked, to which LoSapio replied, “I don’t know.”

A shocked Blumenthal asked LoSapio, “You don’t know?”

On that day, a mob of Trump supporters stormed through barricades at the U.S. Capitol, smashing windows and destroying furniture. Many police officers were beaten and pepper-sprayed during the riot. Five police officers died in the aftermath, four by suicide, and one rioter was fatally shot by a police officer during the chaos.

![Michael LoSapio, chief information security officer at software firm Palantir, was grilled by Democratic Senator Richard Blumenthal at his confirmation hearing for director of the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/19/20/03/GettyImages-2305904562.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

LoSapio admitted at the hearing, “No, I don’t believe it was an attack, no,” adding that he “wasn’t involved in politics” at the time.

“You weren’t involved in politics,” Blumenthal repeated in awe. “I know what I saw on the news,” LoSapio said in response.

“Were you alive on the planet?” Blumenthal asked, to which LoSapio replied, “I was.”

Palantir, co-founded by Trump ally and billionaire tech investor Peter Thiel, is a major contractor for the Army. It signed a $10 billion 10-year contract with the Army in July 2025, “ensuring Soldiers have rapid access to cutting-edge data integration, analytics, and AI tools,” the Army said in a press release at the time.

Moments before being questioned about the January 6 riot, Blumenthal asked LoSapio, “Who won the popular vote in 2020 in the presidential election?”

“I'm not going to answer the question about an election six years ago,” LoSapio said.

“You're in charge of election security, correct?” Blumenthal probed. But LoSapio continued to dodge the question, telling the Democrat, “I'm here to talk about the future.”

![‘No, I don’t believe it was an attack, no,’ LoSapio said Thursday before the Senate Homeland Security Committee, adding that he ‘wasn’t involved in politics’ at the time](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/19/20/23/GettyImages-2305905088.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

LoSapio’s confirmation hearing came after a rash of outages to the country’s technological infrastructure.

It started last Thursday when Microsoft users couldn’t access their emails or other services. Then on Friday, the Federal Aviation Administration grounded all flights due to a technical issue from a contractor replacing old copper wire.

The outages continued this week as thousands struggled to access their online bank accounts on Monday because of a software bug in JPMorganChase’s systems. Then on Tuesday, in what was probably the most concerning outage yet, an internal network that the National Security Agency uses to communicate classified informationwent down during routine maintenance.

The Trump administration has maintained that these outages are not linked to each other or caused by any nefarious activity, with Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton saying at a House committee hearing Wednesday, “They are not a coordinated or state-sponsored attack.”

![LoSapio’s confirmation hearing came after a rash of outages to the country’s technological infrastructure that has spooked Americans. The Trump administration has maintained that these outages are not linked to each other or caused by any nefarious activity](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/19/20/26/GettyImages-2305765189.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Blumenthal also asked LaSapio whether the country is safe from cyberattacks on Thursday, to which he replied, “I feel like there’s more we can do.”

The Democrat then asked LaSapio to confirm that if he headed the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency, he’d have to replace workers ousted during the Trump administration’s efforts to cut what they saw as government waste.

More than 130 workers at the agency were let go early on in Trump’s second term, according to the Associated Press.

“I’m going to investigate and dig in and understand where our gaps are and, if needed, come back to this committee and request more resources,” LoSapio said.
