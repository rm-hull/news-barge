Nearly half of Leave voters now want stronger cooperation with the European Union on immigration, while a third want closer trade and defence links, according to a new poll.

They are even more likely to want the UK and the EU to work together on migration than the average voter, according to the research released just hours before Andy Burnham travels to Brussels for a summit designed to undo some of the damage of Brexit.

Speaking at Prime Minister’s Questions (PMQs) on Wednesday, Mr Burnham slammed the current arrangements as not “good enough for Britain”.

He also hit out at Brexiteer Tory MPs he dubbed “zombies” who had burnt bridges with Europe and urged MPs from all parties to support a new agreement he claimed would bring down food prices and cut red tape.

![Andy Burnham is set to travel to Brussels for a summit designed to undo some of the damage of Brexit](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/15/19/01M7CH79KZGQKX4YNH4DGFTRHZ.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The new poll, by Savanta, found the public as a whole chose cutting trade barriers as a main priority for the summit in Brussels (44 per cent), ahead of migration cooperation (37 per cent) and closer defence ties (35 per cent).

Leave voters chose migration (48 per cent), ahead of trade and defence (36 per cent and 37 per cent).

Reform UK voters also chose migration (50 per cent), ahead of trade and defence (24 per cent and 34 per cent.

The survey, of 2,195 Brits, found that 44 per cent of Tory voters chose trade and defence, with 41 per cent picking immigration.

Mr Burnham is due to sign new agreements on food, energy and youth mobility, having said the current Brexit deal is not good enough.

![Lib Dem Europe spokesperson Dr Al Pinkerton, whose party commisisoned the poll, said ‘even Conservative and Leave voters want Britain to tear down trade barriers with Europe’](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/18/18/2295405159..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The Liberal Democrats, who commissioned the poll, accused Tory leader Kemi Badenoch of abandoning her base ahead of the summit.

Writing in the Daily Mail, the Tory leader warned that Labour’s talks with Brussels will take the UK “backwards” on control of Britain’s money, laws and borders.

The Lib Dem Europe spokesperson Al Pinkerton said: “Kemi Badenoch has abandoned her own voters. Even Conservative and Leave voters want Britain to tear down trade barriers with Europe, but their party leadership is so blinded by Brexit dogma that it refuses to listen. Badenoch is more interested in defending Brexit than defending British businesses.

“Millions of voters are ready to move on from the Brexit arguments of the past, but Westminster is still fighting them. Labour must stop making the same mistake.

“Friday’s UK-EU summit is a chance for the prime minister to put economic growth ahead of political cowardice. That means finally putting a customs union and Single Market membership on the negotiating table.”

It comes amid reports the UK and Europe are working to finalise a youth mobility deal which would grant those under 30 easier travel, work and study between the UK and EU as early as 2028.

According to the i newspaper, the language is still under negotiation as both sides wrangle over the cap on how many people can access the scheme, with the figure expected to be between 80,000 and 100,000 visas.

The deal could also include reduced UK university fees for EU nationals.

The Conservatives have been approached for comment.
