## French President Emmanuel Macron said that Ukraine's NATO accession was brushed aside in early 2022 in a "completely different" context.

French President Emmanuel Macron backed "reopening the question" of Ukraine's NATO membership on Thursday, while urging Kyiv's allies to do the "maximum" to provide it with the necessary air defence systems.

"Faced with the maximalist nature of the Russian position, I believe we need to reopen the question of Ukraine's accession to NATO," said Macron, following a meeting of the Coalition of the Willing in Berlin, co-chaired by France and the UK.

Macron stated that Ukraine’s NATO accession had been set aside in early 2022, albeit in a "completely different" context.

Following Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022, Western allies ruled out Kyiv's NATO's membership for the short-term, fearing that admitting a country at war with Russia could trigger a direct NATO–Russia confrontation under Article 5.

Following two day US-mediated peace talks in Abu Dhabi on 10 and 11 November, Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy declared that US negotiators had floated the idea of Ukraine withdrawing from the eastern Donbas region in exchange for NATO membership.

He said that Kyiv was not prepared to concede territory, but said that "specific security guarantees" were being discussed with NATO and the EU.

"The question arises, including for us Europeans, of what happens the day after, because Ukraine will be a country with a very well-equipped and sizeable army. For our long-term security, Ukraine’s membership of NATO makes sense", stated Macron.

Macron's remarks came hours after Russian President Vladimir Putin, US President Donald Trump and Chinese President Xi Jinping held a historic trilateral in China.

Zelenskyy added that if Western countries laid out assurances, he would consult Ukrainian society on the proposition, saying he had “no right to make this decision alone”.
