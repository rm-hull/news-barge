## European champions Italy and four European debutants are among the 48 teams taking part, while last year’s finalists Portugal and Austria are absent. Qatar is hosting the second of five consecutive editions from 2025 to 2029. The final is on 13 December.

The FIFA Under-17 World Cup got under way in Qatar on Thursday, with 48 teams taking part, including 11 from Europe.

Eight matches were on the opening-day programme at Doha’s Aspire Zone, including France against Haiti, Greece against Egypt and hosts Qatar against Panama.

The 104-match tournament ends with the final at Ahmad bin Ali Stadium on 13 December.

Greece, Montenegro, Romania and Serbia are making their first appearances at the U-17 World Cup. For Montenegro, it also marks the country’s first appearance at any FIFA World Cup.

Italy enter as European champions after beating Belgium on penalties in June’s final, while France are seeking their first U-17 world title since 2001.

Belgium, Croatia, Denmark, Ireland and Spain complete Europe’s line-up.

Defending champions Portugal and last year’s runners-up Austria are both absent after failing to qualify.

The Dominican Republic replaced the US, which withdrew over security concerns. FIFA said the US decision was linked to an existing travel advisory for Qatar and that its own security assessment remained unchanged.

Qatar is hosting the second of five consecutive editions, from 2025 to 2029. The tournament expanded from 24 to 48 teams last year.

All matches except the final will be played across eight pitches at Aspire Zone.

The teams are divided into 12 groups of four. The top two in each group and the eight best third-placed sides will advance to the round of 32. The group stage ends on 27 November, with the knockout rounds beginning on 30 November.

Past participants include Neymar, Phil Foden and Victor Osimhen.
