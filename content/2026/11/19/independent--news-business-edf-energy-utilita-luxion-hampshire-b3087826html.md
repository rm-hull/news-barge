EDF Energy is to add around 1.3 million UK customers to its books after agreeing to buy the pay-as-you-go supplier Utilita.

The acquisition will lift EDF's share of the UK household energy market to more than six million customers.

The deal was announced on Thursday morning and is expected to be completed in early 2027.

Until then, the two companies will operate independently.

Utilita Energy was founded in Hampshire and serves around 1.3 million residential and commercial customers in the UK.

EDF has said Utilita's “specialist prepayment expertise” and model centred on “affordability and support” will help the companies grow in the UK market.

![EDF has agreed a deal to purchase pay-as-you-go supplier Utilita (GH tech/Alamy/PA)](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/19/09/19094316-77d3a4c6-e72e-4511-bb89-95fad4661463.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Simone Rossi, chief executive of EDF in the UK, said: “This is a significant step in our ambition to grow EDF's UK customer business.

“We've invested in improving the experience and products we offer customers, and Utilita brings further expertise that will make us stronger.

“Together, we will have the scale and skills to keep developing services and support for the households who rely on us.”

The agreement was struck between EDF and Utilita's owners, Luxion Group, for an undisclosed fee.

Bill Bullen, the chief executive of Utilita who started the company in 2003, said he was “delighted” to have reached an agreement with EDF.

He said the integration will be “carefully planned” to minimise disruption for customers and employees.

He said: “The agreement builds on an established relationship between the companies, following the successful transfer of 47,000 of Utilita's residential customers to EDF in November 2025.

“Post-completion in 2027 our plan is that Utilita will continue to operate from Hutwood Court near Southampton, the home of its operation and to around 600 of its 1,500 employees.

“Integration of the businesses will be carefully planned and phased, with a clear focus on minimising disruption for customers and employees.”

Both companies were clear that nothing will change immediately for customers.

They added that customers would hear directly from their supplier about any future changes.
