President Donald Trump’s anger with JD Vance is growing after the vice president blamed the Iran war for massive Republican losses in the midterm elections, according to a new report.

Vance issued a stunning admission a day after Democrats took control of the House and Senate, writing on X, “The reason we lost is the war.”

Trump has publicly brushed off the comments, conceding that his No. 2 was, for the most part, correct. But privately, the president has complained to his confidants that Vance should have added that it was still the right decision to fight Iran, people with knowledge of the matter told *The New York Times*.

“If you'd like to argue it was worth it, that losing the Senate for the next 6 years was worth it, then do so,” Vance said of the unpopular war that caused gas prices to skyrocket. “Argue it was a worthwhile cost to pay. Agree or disagree, that's at least honest. But everyone knows why we lost. And any honest conversation about where we go from here must start there.”

Days after the stunning post, Trump told reporters Vance “should have made one other statement,” which the president said was, “If you had it to do again, would you do it again?’ The answer is yes.”

![Trump has publicly brushed off Vance's comments, but privately, the president has complained to his confidants that his No. 2 should have added that it was still the right decision to fight Iran, according to the report](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/18/19/45/GettyImages-2304623706.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Trump got close to taking some responsibility for the GOP midterm shellacking during an interview with *60 Minutes* earlier this month.

“They didn't like the war. They didn't like the fact that gasoline went up because I had it down at a low. And there's a great dislike, incredibly, for Israel,” the president told correspondent Ross Douthat.

But then Trump shifted the blame to the media for unfavorable coverage while also pointing the finger at Republican candidates, telling Douthat, “I think if I was on the ballot, it would have been a much different situation…You had to transfer those voters, however, to candidates that [voters] didn't like.”

Vance later showed a united front among the administration on the Iran war, telling Fox News’ Laura Ingraham in an interview that aired Monday, “There’s no daylight.”

“The president of the United States, he makes decisions. And, obviously, within the administration we have debates and discussions about exactly what to do. When the president makes a decision, he is owed a Cabinet that helps implement that decision,” Vance said.

*The Independent* has reached out to the White House for comment.

![Vance later showed a united front among the administration on the Iran war, saying, 'There’s no daylight'](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/18/19/12/GettyImages-2301178745.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

While fuming over Vance’s post-election analysis, Trump has privately praised Secretary of State Marco Rubio, suggesting he could be a strong presidential candidate in the 2028 election, according to the Times’ sources.

In the wake of the November 3 election, Rubio has fiercely defended the Iran war, saying Trump “identified a very real threat…and he addressed it.”

“And he addressed it even though, oftentimes, the right thing to do for the country — confronting difficult challenges for the security of our country — doing it isn’t necessarily popular but necessary,” he told reporters.

Vance and Rubio have both been floated as potential nominees for the Republican Party in the next presidential election.

A new Reuters/Ipsos poll found that 62 percent of Republicans and Republican-leaning respondents believe Vance would be an excellent or good candidate for the GOP presidential nomination in the 2028 election, while 58 percent said the same for Rubio.
