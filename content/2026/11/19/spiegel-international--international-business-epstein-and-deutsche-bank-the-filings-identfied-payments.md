Deutsche Bank learned from the newspaper that a PR disaster was looming: In 2018, the *Miami Herald* reported at length on women who accused Jeffrey Epstein of sexual abuse, some of whom were still minors at the time of the alleged acts.

Particularly alarming to the bank: The articles named people who were thought to have helped sustain Epstein's system of abuse. Deutsche Bank employees were familiar with them: Some were apparently beneficiaries of The Butterfly Trust, founded by Jeffrey Epstein, which held an account at Deutsche Bank – and which transferred significant quantities of money to young women in Eastern Europe.

"What do they do for Jeffrey Epstein?" a bank employee asked in an internal email in December 2018, referring to the possible Epstein helpers. Clarity was imperative, she wrote.

The question came rather late. By then, Epstein had been a Deutsche Bank client for more than five years. The convicted sex offender, his companies and his closest circle held more than 40 accounts at the financial institution. The bank knew of his conviction and of additional allegations of sexual violence against minors.
