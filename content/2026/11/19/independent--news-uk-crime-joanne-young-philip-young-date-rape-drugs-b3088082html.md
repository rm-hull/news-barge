Philip Young was first investigated by the police almost a decade before he was arrested, when he was questioned over “date rape” drugs that had been illegally imported from India.

Young pleaded guilty to drugging and raping his wife Joanne Young over a 13-year period after police launched an investigation in July 2024.

However, detectives found the potential red flag dating back to 2015 had been investigated by police before being dismissed.

The UK Border Agency intercepted 120 tablets of a class C controlled drug when they were impounded by Parcelforce in Coventry, having been mailed to Young at his Swindon address, the trial at Winchester Crown Court heard.

When officers from Wiltshire Police visited Young on 23 July 2015, he insisted that he had no knowledge of the order and showed he had made no payments for the drugs.

Young, who has served as a Tory councillor and a school governor, also suggested his details had been obtained from his consultancy business by someone else in order to get them into the country.

![Philip Young drugged and raped his wife over a 13-year period](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/19/16/01M7T7341QSR38H35WJHHCRVEK.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The trial heard that despite not getting the medication on that occasion, Young had still been able to access the drugs from 2018 when he was prescribed them for a sleeping problem.

A Wiltshire Police spokesman defended the action taken by officers, saying: “With the benefit of hindsight and the evidence ultimately uncovered during this investigation, it is understandable that questions may be asked about whether there was an earlier opportunity to identify Mr Young’s offending.

“However, based on the information available at the time, the attending officer’s actions were both appropriate and proportionate. The circumstances were carefully assessed, and the officer’s decision was made on the evidence then available.

“At that point, Mr Young was a person of previously good character, with no criminal convictions, and there was no intelligence, information or evidence to suggest he posed a risk of committing the serious offences that have since come to light.

“The interaction was dealt with reasonably and in line with the information known at the time.

“As this case demonstrates, individuals responsible for serious offending do not always present as the people we might expect, and it was only through the subsequent investigation and the evidence obtained that the full extent of Mr Young’s criminality became apparent.”

Another delay in arresting Young occurred after the National Crime Agency (NCA) was alerted on 10 July 2023 that he had uploaded three extreme pornographic images containing bestiality on to his Kik messaging account.

But because of a “backlog”, the case was not passed on to Wiltshire Police until April 2024 which did not send officers to question him until 4 July 2024 when the extent of Young’s offending against his wife, Joanne, began to emerge.

The Wiltshire Police spokesman said that a risk assessment had been undertaken on the type of images and whether to prioritise the case.

He said: “As part of that assessment, the researcher and supervising officer would have established that the images did not involve children, Mr Young had no previous convictions, cautions, or intelligence relating to sexual offending, and he was not residing with any young children.”

He added: “Taking these factors into account, the case was assessed as a lower priority when compared with investigations involving registered sex offenders, individuals suspected of sharing child sexual abuse material, or suspects who had access to or were living with children.

“As a result, available resources were prioritised towards those cases presenting the highest risk of harm.”

The force spokesman explained that monitoring the websites and apps that Young used to advertise his wife for sex, such as Craigslist, Grindr and Fab Swingers, could identify offending but the situation was constantly changing.

“Online platforms can present opportunities for offenders to communicate, share information and identify potential victims, and it is important that technology companies continue to work closely with law enforcement to identify and disrupt criminal activity wherever it occurs,” he said.

“However, it is also important to recognise that the functionality of Craigslist today is significantly different from that which existed historically.

“A number of sections that previously attracted concerns from law enforcement agencies have been removed or changed over time, and offenders seeking to commit crimes often migrate between different websites, forums and encrypted platforms. This means that tackling offending of this nature is not simply about monitoring a single website.

“Effective prevention requires a combined approach involving technology providers, law enforcement, regulators and the public. Intelligence sharing, reporting mechanisms, safeguarding measures and robust investigations all play a vital role.

“Where evidence is identified that online platforms are being used to facilitate criminal activity, police will work with domestic and international partners to identify those responsible and take appropriate action.”
