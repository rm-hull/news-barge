A major Harrogate business which is proud to have been supplying local homes and businesses across the North with LPG since 1932 is to invest in new multi-million-pound headquarters.

After being based at Hampsthwaite for more than 90 years, the independent and family-owned Northern Energy is expanding into a new head office building in Boroughbridge.

Approximately 60 members of Northern Energy’s 120-strong team will be based in the new offices, which are due to open in April 2027.

The move comes less than 12 months after the company opened a new state-of-the-art storage and distribution depot in Boroughbridge after outgrowing its existing site.

James Illingworth from Northern Energy said: “This development marks a new chapter for us, giving us a large LPG storage and distribution facility next to the A1.

“It represents a big step towards a more sustainable future by reducing our carbon footprint and operational emissions, whilst maintaining the high level of service our customers enjoy.

“In addition to boosting our storage capacity, it will futureproof our operations with the infrastructure needed to blend renewable liquid gases into our fuel mix as customer demand continues to grow.

“Our new offices will give us a modern HQ designed to support our expanding team.

“It will be a place where people aspire to work, where everyone can be productive and fulfil their potential, as well as reinforcing our position at the forefront of rural energy supply in the UK.”

Construction is now under way on at the 10,000 sq ft site with several leading North Yorkshire businesses appointed to do the work on the new headquarters.

The Boroughbridge depot complements Northern Energy’s eight other fuel depots across the country, which predominantly supply off-grid areas.

The new site has three bulk LPG storage tanks, with a combined capacity of 360,000 litres.

One-third of this capacity has been designed to accommodate renewable liquid gases in the future.

The interior fit-out for the new offices has been designed by Harrogate-based Studio 93.

Jake Harris, senior interior designer at Studio 93, said: “From the outset, it was clear Northern Energy wanted design to play a central role in its new HQ, giving us the opportunity to develop a distinctive identity for every space in the building.

“Features include a welcoming entrance that sets the tone for the rest of the building.

“This leads to a suite of meeting rooms and a choice of quiet working environments, including dedicated spaces and pods that support focus, privacy and any neurodiverse needs.

“There is also a dedicated wellbeing room, changing rooms and stunning breakout spaces that make the most of views across the surrounding countryside.

“A key challenge was making the ground-floor café work harder than a conventional staff canteen, allowing it to transition from an everyday social hub into a flexible venue for evening events.”
