When families look at schools, they naturally ask about teaching, results, subjects and opportunities. Less often do they ask what the setting itself makes possible.

At Queen Mary’s School, near Thirsk, that matters more than you might expect. This boutique girls’ day and boarding school is based at Baldersby Park, a historic listed building in the North Yorkshire countryside, with parkland, woodland, a river and horses all within the grounds.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21705128.jpg?type=mds-article-620) *(Image: Queen Mary’s School)*

The setting is not simply part of the view — it shapes what girls can do day to day. With river access, a climbing wall, horses and extensive grounds all on site, outdoor education can be woven naturally into school life rather than reserved for occasional trips or activity days. Girls can ride, take part in sport and outdoor adventure, and make use of the landscape around them as part of their everyday experience.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21705129.jpg?type=mds-article-620) *(Image: Queen Mary’s School)*

There is a strong sense that this is a country school in the truest sense. Horses are part of the landscape, dogs are welcome on site and teachers will regularly bring their own into class. Girls are learning and growing up in a house with a rich history, but one that is very much lived in rather than preserved at a distance. For boarders especially, Baldersby Park is part of school life well beyond the final lesson of the day.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21705124.jpg?type=mds-article-620) *(Image: Queen Mary’s School)*

That relationship with the outdoors feels particularly relevant when so much of childhood is structured around classrooms, cars, organised activities and screens, with time outside often something that has to be deliberately planned. At Queen Mary’s, it is simply part of the day.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21705123.jpg?type=mds-article-620) *(Image: Queen Mary’s School)*

“There is a great deal children learn from the environment around them,” says Head, Fe Beadnell. “Academic progress is hugely important, but education is also about giving children the chance to test themselves, become more independent and experience the world beyond the classroom. We are very fortunate that our setting gives girls those opportunities every day.”

The scale of the school matters too. Queen Mary’s is deliberately small, which means pupils know the place well and are known by the people within it. Younger and older girls mix naturally, boarders and day girls share the same spaces, and the school has the feel of a close community where every child is truly known.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21705130.jpg?type=mds-article-620) *(Image: Queen Mary’s School)*

Taken together, the historic house, river, sports facilities, animals and countryside create a very particular environment in which to grow up. They give girls opportunities to be outside, take on challenges, build independence and experience school as something broader than lessons and timetables.
