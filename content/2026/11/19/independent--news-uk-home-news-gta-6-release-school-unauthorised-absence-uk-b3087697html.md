The highly-anticipated release of Grand Theft Auto VI (GTA 6) has left schools across the UK worried about a potential rise in absences.

The latest iteration of the action-adventure game, developed by Rockstar Games, launched today.

In preparation for the so-called “GTA flu” (where children pretend to be ill so they can skip classes and play the game), several schools and trusts sent letters to parents.

“Whilst we understand that the release of a highly-anticipated game can generate considerable excitement among young people, we would like to remind families that students are expected to attend school as normal,” one letter read.

“Any absence for the purpose of gaming or leisure activities will be recorded as an unauthorised absence.”

Another letter is reported to have urged parents to maintain normal bedtimes to ensure children arrive at school “rested” and ready to learn.

## What are the laws behind letting your child play truant for the day?

According to the UK government, local councils and schools can use “various legal powers” if your child is missing school “without a good reason”.

Authorised absences tend to cover situations such as physical or mental illnesses, exceptional circumstances or medical appointments that cannot be arranged outside of school hours.

This includes a Parenting Order, which requires parents to go to parenting classes.

“You’ll also have to do what the court says to improve your child’s school attendance,” the government’s website states.

If the council thinks you need support getting your child to go to school but you’re not co-operating, they can apply to a court for an Education Supervision order.

This is where a supervisor is appointed to help get your child into education.

![You can be fined for letting your child skip school if you don’t have a ‘good reason’](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/04/02/19/39/iStock-1448272566.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

If the local authority is concerned your child is not getting an education, they can get a School Attendance Order.

You’ll have 15 days to provide evidence that you’ve registered your child with the school listed in the order or that you’re giving them home education. If you do not, you could be prosecuted or given a fine.

Local councils can also issue each parent a fine of £80, rising to £160 if it is not paid within 21 days.

If parents get to three fines in a three-year period, the penalty increases to £160.

If your child is off school 3 or more times within the 3 years you will not be fined but may be taken to court.

If you’re taken to court, you could get a fine of up to £2,500, a community order or a jail sentence of up to three months.
