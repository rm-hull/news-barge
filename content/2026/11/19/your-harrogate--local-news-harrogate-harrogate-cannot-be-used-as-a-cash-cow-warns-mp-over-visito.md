Harrogate and Knaresborough MP Tom Gordon has called for any money raised locally through a visitor levy to stay in the area.

Speaking during a Commons debate on the Government’s Visitor Levy Bill, the Liberal Democrat MP said local businesses supported a charge on overnight stays in principle but were concerned about where the money would go.

The Bill would give the Mayor of York and North Yorkshire, David Skaith, the power to introduce a charge based on a percentage of the cost of an overnight stay.

Mr Gordon said:

> “Local businesses tell me they support a visitor levy in principle.
>
> "What they can't accept is a far-off mayor in York funnelling the money away.
>
> "Harrogate cannot be used as a cash cow to pump money into other areas and a mayor's pet projects.”

His comments came as Mr Skaith launched a separate consultation on a proposed council tax charge to help fund bus services.

The charge could raise up to £2 million a year.

Mr Gordon said:

> “If ministers want this levy to work, every penny raised in Harrogate and Knaresborough must be ring-fenced for Harrogate and Knaresborough.
>
> "That means dualling the Knaresborough to York line, fixing our pothole-riddled roads, and backing the town and parish councils that run festivals and events and look after our toilets and flower beds.”

He also questioned how much say local leaders would have over whether to introduce the levy.

He said:

> “Local leaders can object, but the Mayor has the final word.
>
> "That isn't devolution. Quite frankly, it's a con.”
