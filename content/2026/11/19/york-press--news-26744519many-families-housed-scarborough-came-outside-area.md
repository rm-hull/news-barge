## Are people really being moved into Scarborough from elsewhere?

Scarborough South Bay. Courtesy NYC

ONLY a fraction of households currently living in temporary accommodation in Scarborough came from elsewhere in North Yorkshire, councillors have been told.

A senior councillor has rejected suggestions that large numbers of people from former Selby and Ryedale districts are being housed in the town, revealing that around four per cent of those in temporary accommodation came from outside Scarborough.

Tom Seston, Reform councillor for Eastfield, raised the issue at a full meeting of North Yorkshire Council, questioning how many people from the former Selby and Ryedale districts had been placed in Scarborough over the past year.

Cllr Seston said: “Over recent months, there’s been a large number of people being housed in Scarborough from the former Ryedale and Selby district areas.”

He also claimed some people placed in the town had “extremely challenging behavioural and addiction issues” and asked how former district councils were meeting their housing duties without relying on accommodation in tourist destinations such as Scarborough.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21030658.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Cllr Tom Seston. Courtesy TS, Reform.,

Responding, Cllr Simon Myers, the council’s executive member for arts, culture and housing, said the issue was often viewed through what he described as an “othering” narrative.

He told the meeting: “There’s a narrative in housing that all the problems are caused by other people. It’s the othering culture that lives in British politics.”

The Conservative councillor said there were currently 72 households in temporary accommodation in Scarborough, but only three families had come from outside the area.

He said: “There are 72 households in temporary accommodation in Scarborough, and at the moment, there are three families who didn’t come from Scarborough. Two came from Selby and one from Hambleton.”

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21637862.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Cllr Simon Myers speaks at the North Yorkshire Council executive committee meeting.

The three households represent 4.2 per cent of all households currently living in temporary accommodation in Scarborough.

Cllr Myers added that housing placements worked in both directions across North Yorkshire.

He said: “There are two families from Scarborough in temporary accommodation in Ryedale.”

It comes as Alison Hume, Labour MP for Scarborough and Whitby, said some people living close to temporary accommodation sites had been left feeling “anxiety, frustration and fear”.

Speaking in Parliament last week, she told ministers that problems were increasingly concentrated around a small number of town-centre properties used for emergency temporary accommodation.

At the full meeting of the authority on Wednesday, November 18, Cllr Myers argued politicians across the county too often blamed housing problems on people arriving from elsewhere.

Referring to Kevin Hollinrake, Conservative MP for Thirsk and Malton, he said: “If you speak to Kevin Hollinrake, he’ll tell you that all the antisocial behaviour there in Ryedale is caused by people in social housing who have moved there from Scarborough.

“So you all do it, you all blame someone else.”

According to Cllr Myers, most households in temporary accommodation in Scarborough already have local connections to the area.

He said: “The fact of the matter is that most of the people in temporary accommodation in Scarborough are from Scarborough; there are three who aren’t, and at its peak there were ten.”
