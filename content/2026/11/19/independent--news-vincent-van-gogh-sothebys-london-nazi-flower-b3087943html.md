A vibrant landscape painted by Vincent van Gogh weeks before his death has sold for $190.4 million at auction, a record for a work by the artist.

“Châtaigniers en fleurs” (Chestnut Trees in Flower) exceeded its $180 million estimate at a Sotheby’s auction in New York on Wednesday. The sale went ahead after an agreement between its current owners and the heirs of a Jewish family that sold it in Nazi-era Germany.

A day before the auction, Sotheby’s announced an agreement between the Blaquier-Arrieta family, which has owned the painting since 1975, and the descendants of Hedwig and Samuel Fischer, who sold it in 1936.

A lawyer for the Fischer heirs had asked for the sale to be postponed until Sotheby’s showed the painting had not been sold under duress.

The auction house said that “in recognition of the painting’s complex Nazi-era history and the suffering endured by the Fischer family during the Holocaust, the two families have reached a mutually satisfactory resolution.” It did not disclose details of the agreement.

The Fischer heirs said they were “grateful for the trusting and fair negotiation and collaboration with Sotheby’s and the Blaquier-Arrieta family, which make it possible to acknowledge the suffering of the Fischer family during the Nazi era.”

Van Gogh painted “Châtaigniers en fleurs” in a burst of creativity after his release from a psychiatric asylum in May 1890. He took his own life two months later.

The previous record for a van Gogh was $117.2 million paid for “Verger avec cyprès” (Orchard with Cypresses) at a Christie’s auction in 2022.

Paul Cézanne’s portrait “Arlequin” sold for $111 million in Wednesday’s sale of a dozen Impressionist and post-Impressionist works amassed by the late Argentine art collector Nelly Arrieta de Blaquier and her sugar magnate husband, Carlos Pedro Blaquier.

The 12 works sold for a total of $412 million.
