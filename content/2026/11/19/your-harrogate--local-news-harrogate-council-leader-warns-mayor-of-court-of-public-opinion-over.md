The leader of North Yorkshire Council has warned that Mayor David Skaith will face the "court of public opinion" if he continues to "shortchange" the county over funding.

Conservative councillor Carl Les made the comments at today’s full council meeting as he explained the authority’s decision not to pursue legal action against York and North Yorkshire Combined Authority over a disputed £20.4m in highways funding.

The council had previously threatened legal proceedings after accusing the Labour mayor of allocating North Yorkshire less money for road maintenance than it would have received under the Department for Transport’s funding formula – and diverting some of the authority’s money to City of York Council.

However, Cllr Les said legal advice had indicated that a High Court challenge would be unsuccessful, even though he remained deeply unhappy with the mayor’s decision.

He told councillors:

> “In effect, the mayor is entitled to make such a decision, however poorly evidenced it might be and however poorly considered.
>
> “The High Court would support that, so we would lose. But the High Court is not the only court. There’s also the court of public opinion.
>
> “If the mayor continues to shortchange the people of North Yorkshire, as it appears he will, we will call that out, and that court will also deliver its verdict in the future.”

Cllr Les was responding to a question asking for an update on the council’s previous proposal to take legal action against the combined authority.

He said he had strongly expressed the council’s disappointment to the mayor over the decision taken in March, which he claimed would leave North Yorkshire £20.4m worse off over the current funding period compared with the Department for Transport’s formula.

However, he revealed that discussions between officers from the council and the combined authority were continuing following the council’s formal letter before action.

He said those discussions could lead to a more favourable outcome:

> “Because of our letter before action, there are ongoing discussions between our officers and those from the combined authority, and that may see some positive movement on this matter."

The mayor’s office rejected the suggestion that North Yorkshire was being shortchanged and insisted that the county was receiving record investment in its roads.

A spokesperson for the mayor said:

> “The mayor’s transport fund has given both councils more money than ever before to fix our roads and make them safer for everyone who uses them.
>
> “As a result of North Yorkshire Council’s lack of fully developed transport schemes, and to ensure this money is spent rather than returned to the Government, the mayor intends to increase funding for pothole repairs in North Yorkshire even further over the next three years.
>
> “The public rightly expects this significant increase in funding to be seen across the county.
>
> “The mayor will be holding the councils’ feet to the fire to make sure they deliver the improvements people expect and deserve.”

A decision on the latest funding proposals is expected at a combined authority meeting on Friday, December 18.
