London Ambulance Service (LAS) has declared a critical incident after being hit with more than 7,000 emergency calls a day for three days in a row – surpassing demand typically seen on New Year’s Eve.

Call handlers logged 7,326 calls on Monday, 7,366 on Tuesday and 7,369 on Wednesday, marking a rise of roughly 500 calls a day compared with last week and far exceeding the 5,500 calls recorded on a standard busy day.

The surge has been driven in part by cold weather and an early spike in viral and respiratory illnesses, with life-threatening emergencies involving breathing difficulties rising by nearly 20 per cent so far this November compared with the same period last year.

Overall Category 1 incidents – the most severe, life-or-death emergencies such as cardiac arrests – are running 10 per cent higher than at the same point in 2025.

![More than 7,000 emergency calls have been logged in the last three days, according to the LSA](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/01/05/13/41/Winter-weather-Jan-5th-2026-1x19gyak.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Declaring a critical incident enables the trust to suspend non-essential activities and redeploy clinically trained managers into frontline, patient-facing roles to help reach the sickest patients faster.

Chief operating officer Craig Harman said: “The number of calls has remained very high for several days, so we have declared a critical incident to take extra steps to reach patients who need us most urgently.

“Colder weather can have a serious impact on people’s health, particularly older people, young children and those with existing health conditions. We are already seeing very high demand this winter, including an early rise in respiratory and viral infections, with no sign of easing in November.”

To ease the strain, Mr Harman said the service is putting extra ambulances on the road, adding clinicians to assess patients over the phone and working with London hospitals to speed up A&E handovers so crews can get back out on duty more quickly.

He urged Londoners to look out for vulnerable neighbours and relatives during the cold snap and to call 999 only for genuine life-threatening emergencies.

Members of the public with less urgent medical needs are being advised to contact NHS 111 online or by phone, speak to their GP, or visit a local pharmacy, while eligible residents are urged to take up their winter flu jab as soon as possible.
