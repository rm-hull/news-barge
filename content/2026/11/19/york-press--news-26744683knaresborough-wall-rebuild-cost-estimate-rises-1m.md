The cost of rebuilding a collapsed riverside wall in a North Yorkshire town is now estimated at more than £1m, with a local councillor questioning why problems with the structure were not addressed sooner.

North Yorkshire Council has revealed the latest estimate for repairing Viaduct Terrace, beneath Knaresborough's historic railway viaduct, in a report to councillors.

The figure is almost three times the £350,000 previously budgeted for the work, according to Knaresborough councillor Matt Walker, who said he had warned from the outset that repairs would cost at least £1m.

The council has also confirmed that construction work is not expected to begin until July 2027, about 17 months after the wall collapsed in February this year.

The report states that investigations into the collapse identified a combination of factors, including historic structural problems, erosion around the adjacent viaduct foundations, difficult ground conditions and weaknesses associated with the retaining wall constructed in 2010.

Cllr Walker said he was surprised by the reference to problems associated with the earlier construction.

He said: “The thing that surprises me is that they're now saying there have been issues with the wall dating back to 2010, but nobody has ever mentioned that before.

“If they knew there was a problem, why weren't they looking to resolve it? Why was it allowed to collapse?

“If there was a known issue, they should have done something about it, shouldn't they?”

The councillor said he was due to receive a private briefing from the engineering team responsible for the repairs next Wednesday.

He added: “The question I'll be asking is, if they knew it was a known issue, why didn't they do something sooner?”

Cllr Walker said the revised cost estimate was less of a surprise.

He said: “They originally put a budget of £350,000 on it, but I said right from the start that it was going to cost more than that.

“I said it would cost at least £1m, and the report now seems to confirm that.”

The latest update, which will be considered by North Yorkshire Council's Harrogate and Knaresborough area committee on Thursday, November 26, states that the preferred solution is to reconstruct the entire retaining wall.

Officers say the Knaresborough scheme is unusually complex because of its location beside the River Nidd, beneath an operational railway viaduct and within a sensitive heritage setting.

The area also contains listed structures, homes and businesses, while the riverside is a popular destination for visitors.

According to the report, extensive design, environmental and engineering work has been necessary to develop a permanent solution addressing the underlying causes of the collapse rather than providing a temporary repair.

Ground investigations are scheduled to begin during the week commencing December 7.

The council is working with Network Rail, the Environment Agency and Yorkshire Water on the proposed scheme.

Subject to the findings of the investigations and the necessary approvals, construction is currently programmed to start in July next year.

The update follows a petition presented to councillors in September calling for urgent repairs before further winter weather caused additional deterioration.

Residents warned that delays could increase risks to public safety, nearby properties and the riverside environment.

Councillors subsequently requested regular progress reports and better communication with residents until the repairs are completed.
