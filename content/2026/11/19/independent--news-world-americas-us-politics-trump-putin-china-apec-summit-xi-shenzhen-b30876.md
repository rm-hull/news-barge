Donald Trump gave Vladimir Putin a warm reception when the leaders met for closed-door talks on the fate of war-torn Ukraine.

The U.S. president pointed, smiled and called out “Who is that? He’s my friend!” as he walked into a room with his Russian counterpart in Shenzhen, China, ahead of an unprecedented trilateral with Chinese leader Xi Jinping.

Trump said of Putin: “We’ve had a great relationship for many years. We know each other for a long time, and I think a lot of wonderful things can come out of this, or at least start. But I think they’ll come out of this.”

The Kremlin said after the meeting that the pair had discussed the ongoing war in Ukraine, and that Putin left in an “excellent” mood.

Putin aide Yuri Ushakov said the war was the main topic of conversation during their 35-40 minute tête-à-tête, and that they had also discussed the relationship between their two countries.

![Donald Trump met Vladimir Putin and Xi separately ahead of the trilateral talks, calling the Russian leader a "friend”](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/19/07/download.-2..jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

President Trump has sought to mediate an end to the nearly five-year conflict between Russian and Ukraine and this month restarted peace talks with diplomats from both sides.

But so far, talks have failed to produce a breakthrough, and allies fear Trump has been pressuring Kyiv to make concessions to its invader while lifting sanctions and cutting deals with Moscow.

Trump on Thursday also appealed to president Xi to help end the war and expressed hope that their three-way meeting with Putin would yield results.

“We're looking to both help with respect to the war with Russia and Ukraine,” the American president said. “I'd like to see it ended.”

![The two world leaders gave each other a warm reception as they met for talks](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/19/8/51/bfeff330c7434a519bd3cf7322ba4555-ap.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Trump said both presidents felt strongly about oil having to flow through the Strait of Hormuz, which has been disrupted by the Iran war the US launched in February. Xi did not mention Ukraine or Iran in his remarks, and has long resisted directly weighing in on the European conflict.

The summit in Shenzhen has fuelled concerns in Europe that decisions affecting the war and the future of the continent could be taken without European input.

But Trump’s envoy Jared Kushner said on Thursday that he and Steve Witkoff would head to Berlin to meet with Ukrainian president Volodymyr Zelensky and European leaders to discuss the conflict and talks.

Ukraine’s main allies in European are meeting for a ‘Coalition of the Willing’ summit to show support for Kyiv as Europe pushes for involvement in the US-brokered talks with Kyiv and Moscow.

![A grinning Vladimir Putin waves after his meeting with Trump](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/19/08/2026-11-19T062612Z_1917592886_RC2UQOAMF5N6_RTRMADP_3_APEC-SUMMIT-USA-RUSSIA.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

German officials said the Berlin meeting would focus on reaffirming a commitment to robust and legally binding security guarantees, which would come into force following a ceasefire.

Its timing is intended to allow Ukraine's European allies to give a quick, joint response to any announcements from Shenzhen, they said.

The leaders of Germany, France, Britain, Poland and Italy and also NATO head Mark Rutte are expected to attend.

The United States and Europe, close allies for decades after the end of World War Two, have increasingly moved apart as Trump's administration has scaled back support for Ukraine and insisted its NATO partners pay more for their own defence.

![China's President Xi Jinping,Trump and Putin arrive for their trilateral meeting at Kylin Villa](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/19/08/2305276449..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

On Tuesday, the differences were exposed when Germany and Britain rejected U.S. criticism of Ukraine's campaign of deep strikes on Russian oil infrastructure and commercial warehouses.

British Prime Minister Andy Burnham said the Berlin meeting would signal that Ukraine's European allies maintain their firm support and that “we are united as ever in securing a just and lasting peace that sees an end to this senseless war”.

Britain will announce new details on a support package for Ukraine, including long-range drones capable of striking deep into Russia, as well as surveillance and logistical drones.
