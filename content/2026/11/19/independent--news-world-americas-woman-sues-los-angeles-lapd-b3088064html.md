A woman who lived in a Los Angeles city shelter is suing the city over the allegations made against her. She alleges she was falsely accused of throwing her fetus out of the window of the shelter. The accusations included a claim that she left the newborn to die in 2024.

She was then called a “baby killer” by fellow residents when she returned to the shelter from a hospital. Identified only as Jane Doe, the plaintiff alleges in her Los Angeles Superior Court lawsuit that she was wrongfully shackled to her hospital bed, interrogated, threatened with criminal prosecution for homicide, and verbally abused for hours while she was suffering the aftermath of a stillbirth.

The investigation continued for a year, which the lawsuit contends was a clear violation of the legislature's intent in amending the Reproductive Privacy Act in 2022 to prohibit the state from taking punitive action against people based on their actions or omissions regarding pregnancies, miscarriages, and stillbirths.

![The plaintiff alleges in her Los Angeles Superior Court lawsuit that she was wrongfully shackled to her hospital bed, interrogated, threatened with criminal prosecution for homicide, and verbally abused for hours while she was suffering the aftermath of a stillbirth](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/11/15/2267127320..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“Defendants blatantly violated Ms. Doe's rights, causing her extreme and ongoing harm and mental duress,” the suit alleges.

A representative for the City Attorney's Office did not immediately reply to a request for comment on the suit filed Wednesday.

Doe has both epilepsy and a history of trauma, the suit states. She went to Good Samaritan Hospital in November 2024 for severe stomach pains and was surprised to be told she was pregnant and miscarrying a deceased first-trimester fetus, according to the suit, which further states that she was sent home to pass the deceased fetus alone.

“In truth, Ms. Doe was approximately 29 weeks pregnant with a deceased fetus and had just been sent home to deliver the deceased fetus without any medical assistance or even an accurate diagnosis,” the suit alleges.

Doe's stomach pains became increasingly debilitating when she returned to the city shelter where she lived, and late that night she passed large clots of blood and tissue, leaving her terrified and seeking emergency help,the suit states.

![A woman walks past a Skid Row sign pointing out a population of "Too Many" in Los Angeles](https://static.independent.co.uk/2021/04/27/17/GettyImages-1232545964.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

But paramedics who arrived initially refused to transport her because her hospital records from earlier that day indicated she was merely experiencing a first-trimester miscarriage, the suit further states. At her insistence that she needed medical help, the paramedics took her toLos Angeles General Medical Center and learned she had lost a significant amount of blood, the suit states.

"Ms. Doe's trauma should have ended there, but it did not," the suit states. Two LAPD officers arrived, handcuffed her to her gurney and told hershe was a suspect in the discovery of a fetus found in an alley outside ashelter, the suit states.

Doe explained that she wasn't even aware that she had passed the fetus at her shelter that night and that another hospital had told her that the fetus was already deceased, according to the complaint.

"Ms. Doe showed the detectives her discharge papers from Good Samaritan Hospital corroborating her diagnosis, but the detectives nonetheless continued their interrogation for hours," the suit states. Doe was not arrested, but residents and strangers at the shelter called her a "baby killer" when she returned, based on news reports, the suit further contends.

But about three months later, two LAPD detectives contactedDoe and told her they were investigating her for a variety of possible charges,including first-degree murder, based on their "unfounded and obviously wrong insistence that Ms. Doe had given birth and then thrown a living newborn baby from a window," the suit states.

![The LAPD continued its investigation for a year despite Doe's presentation of medical records confirming that her fetus died in utero, the suit states](https://static.independent.co.uk/2025/05/24/21/31/iStock-1137574199.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The two detectives are co-defendants in the suit. The LAPD continued its investigation for a year despite Doe's presentation of medical records confirming that her fetus died in utero, the suit states.

"During this time, defendants made multiple inflammatory and harmful public statements regarding Ms. Doe in response to heightened public interest around the incident," the suit says, adding that the City Attorney's Office ultimately told Doe in writing that the investigation was closed but could be reopened.

"This left Ms. Doe in continued limbo and wracked with fear and anxiety," according to the suit, which seeks unspecified compensatory and punitive damages. Doe continues to suffer from periodic episodes of extreme emotional stress in which she "relives the events of November 13, 2024, and everything that followed," the suit states.
