# Christmas preparations in Ripon offered chance to visit much-loved pie shop: Carl Les

## Christmas is coming. The first decorated and lit Christmas tree has been spotted in the front window of a private house. Although mid-November seems somewhat early, it is better not to criticise in case it is connected with serious illness or a family member being posted far away before Christmas week.

Stores are putting up their decorations before Decemner, but apart from wishing Christmas spending to come earlier, leaving it until later in the year can cause logistical problems trying to erect them when busy.

Town lights are already erected, but not illuminated. Where we live now we are equidistant between Bedale, Masham, Thirsk and Ripon, and share our shopping and services between them.

Ripon appears to be the first out of the stalls, and although unable to attend the actual switch-on ceremony by Santa and the Mayor, I went to see the preparations being undertaken by the city council led by their energetic mayor, my council colleague Andrew Williams, in preparation for the event which fills the Market Square with over 3,000 people.

![Carl Les is leader of North Yorkshire Council.](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOjJjYzZhYzMzLTM1N2YtNDRmMS05ODJjLTc2MDZhNWZhMTRlNjplNTM5M2I1Ni03YzZkLTQ1NDYtOTQ5My1mNTY2M2ViOGQ4ZDg=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


The square was full of busy with road closures, diversion signs, crowd barriers, a funfair setting up, but what impressed me most was the redecorated Town Hall, a North Yorkshire Council owned building which we have recently handed over on a long lease to the City Council as part of double devolution as we call it.

With so many properties that came to the new unitary council at Local Government Review time, it would be unlikely we could find the finances for redecoration on top of the backlog of essential maintenance. Ripon City Council have stepped up to this challenge using their own resources and finding other funding streams to do what matters most to their community.

The redecoration of the Town Hall really does brighten that end of the Market Square, and may encourage other landlords to follow suit. Passing through after dark, the lights are impressive too. Well done Mr Mayor, the city council too, and Santa of course!

I took the opportunity to pop into Appleton’s pie shop, one of the oldest businesses in Ripon, in one of the most attractive shop fronts around. Years ago when our family business was starting out on the old Great North Road in Leeming Village, and later on the bypass at Leeming Bar, the only hot food that we sold apart from in the restaurant was in the “snack bar”, the power house of the business, and this was hot sausage rolls baked in our own onsite bakery, and Appleton’s pork pies, the best for miles around.

We sold thousands of them over the years, delivered freshly-baked every morning apart from Sundays, and I remember Charlie the van driver carrying in the flat trays of 36s, precariously balanced on his flat-capped head. I was told on some days we sold more than the pie shop in Ripon.

Even though there was a good pie producer in Leeming Bar at the VoM (Vale of Mowbray Bacon Company) and we bought our bacon from there, we stayed loyal to Appleton’s.

When VoM was bought by a London-headquartered concern, whenever the chairman came to visit, whilst inspecting the factory he would send his chauffeur to Ripon to collect an Appleton’s family pie to take home for himself. The business was owned then by the Gaunt family, now passed to the Sternes who have upgraded the business even further, with smart shops in local market towns. The pies remain as good as ever.

We suffered from the Microsoft outage along with many global businesses and the country’s infrastructure. The Chair of the Commons Science and Technology Committee may say this shows the “strategic and economic vulnerability” of depending on a small number of technology providers, mainly US based. However until homegrown competitors come to market it is difficult to see how alternatives can be procured.

Certainly although we can be resilient with power supplies by having back-up generators to provide power, it would be almost impossible to have a back-up tech platform. In that we have to rely on the supplier to have the resilience measures to cope.

Where we can help is with others who work with us, as resilience is becoming annually more important. We are a key part of the Local Resilience Forum, actually chairing it, bringing together all key players like the council, police, fire and health. The government has issued a national warning to households to have three days supply of essentials “just in case”, and we encourage parish councils to prepare their own emergency plan.

We don’t do it for them as some would want, as the local council has the knowledge of what local resources are available, with contact numbers, what facilities are in the community, like a village hall, with toilets, kitchen and heating, and importantly who has the key! If evacuations are necessary because of floods or fire or worse, with that level of local knowledge we can support with our Major Incident Resource Team. We will provide advice and guidance from our Emergency Planning Team.

Carl Les is the leader of North Yorkshire Council
