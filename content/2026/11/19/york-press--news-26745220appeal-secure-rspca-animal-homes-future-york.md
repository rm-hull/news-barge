## Appeal launched to ensure York animal home can keep rehoming animals in need

Seven-month-old Malinois cross Beverley was one of 30 dogs on the site *(Image: Kevin Glenton)*

Meet Beverley – a three-legged resident dog who is backing an appeal to help secure the future of an animal home in York.

RSPCA York, Harrogate and District Branch, an independent charity working alongside the national RSPCA, is around seven years into a £5million development plan to overhaul York Animal Home in Landing Lane, a site it has occupied for more than 40 years, and which it said is necessary to meet modern licensing requirements and keep rehoming animals in need.

The seven-month-old Malinois cross was one of 30 dogs on the site in the position of having arrived at the doors who need a safe place to recover, or to find trust, or a loving home.

![RSPCA York, Harrogate and District Branch staff with a seven month old Malinois](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21739581.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Beverley's journey was shared by Ruth McCabe, the branch's animal centre manager (seated) and Marie Sandall (left) *(Image: Kevin Glenton)*




Officials said the buildings that served animals in that time were no longer fit for the future and new ‘state of the art’ structures are taking shape on the site.

A puppy unit, cattery and multi-purpose unit are all part of the first phase of the planned work.

Branch manager Peter Gorbert outlined the vision which is several years from completion in a talk and tour during an appeal breakfast for local groups and businesses today (Thursday, November 19).

![RSPCA York, Harrogate and District Branch manager Peter Gorbert, capital campaign lead Lucy Lowthian ad chair of trustees Jon Latham](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21739565.jpg?type=mds-article-620)RSPCA York, Harrogate and District Branch manager Peter Gorbert, capital campaign lead Lucy Lowthian add chair of trustees Jon Latham spoke at the appeal breakfast this morning (Thursday, November 19) *(Image: Kevin Glenton)*

The project has already received funding from Pets Foundation and they are waiting to hear the outcome of a grant application to the national RSPCA.

Funding is still to be secured for the full cost of the first phase of the project but construction is now well underway.

Peter said while it’s an exciting time for the team of dedicated staff and volunteers, the plan can only work with donations and support from people and businesses in the city of York.

Peter said: “Our new puppy unit is due to be completed this year and hopefully will start taking in puppies in January, with our brand-new cattery scheduled for completion in July - weather and funding allowing."

“The puppy unit will increase our capacity from two litters up to six litters – at an average of six or seven puppies per litter."

“The unit will also allow us to work more flexibly, taking in individual puppies and larger litters or larger breeds."

“Each of the units will have dedicated indoor and outdoor areas for the puppies and will create a more comfortable environment for the mothers."

![Works taking shape for a new development at RSPCA York, Harrogate and District Branch](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21739578.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Funding is still to be secured for the full cost of the first phase of the project in Landing Lane and construction is underway *(Image: Kevin Glenton)*

The 24-space cattery is most needed, according to Peter.

He said they currently are not taking in any cats, and prior to the demolition their maximum capacity has been reduced down to 20.

The aim of the new unit is to provide larger, climate-controlled units that provide indoor and outdoor areas for the cats featuring multiple levels for the cats to explore.

Additionally, the cat pods will be in smaller blocks, providing a calmer environment for cats and reducing the potential for diseases to spread.

The unit will also have its own dedicated kitchens, storage and laundries.

The new facilities will also ensure animal safety and minimise the risk of outbreaks of diseases, Peter Gorbert added.

Landscaping is planned across the whole site and the final phase of the rebuild work includes updating the staff and human-facing facilities, but this is still several years off.

Peter said: “The need and demand for catteries like ours has never been greater across England and Wales."

“The RSPCA has a waiting list of 650 cats, animals that would go into centres like York Animal Home and the knock-on effect is that the national RSPCA is currently only taking priority cases when ideally, everyone wants to save every animal."

“Our cattery will make a big difference in helping to reduce this national waiting list and relieving some of the pressure on the system."
