Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin has found himself in “primetime timeout” after his appearance on Fox News frustrated Trump administration officials, according to a new report.

Mullin, a former Oklahoma senator, joined Fox News Monday evening to discuss the recent spate of high-profile tech disruptions, including at Microsoft, JPMorgan Chase and air traffic control systems.

The secretary told host Sean Hannity that “SI” or super intelligence — President Donald Trump’s preferred term for artificial intelligence — could be to blame for the outages, citing “one guy who is very smart with tech.”

White House Chief of Staff Stephen Miller — who was traveling in Asia with Trump at the time — determined that this remark was too off-script, sources familiar with the matter told *Politico Playbook*. The outlet had earlier reported that “Trump administration group chats lit up with ‘WTFs’” as staffers watched Mullin’s TV appearance.

By Tuesday, Miller had sidelined Mullin, instead putting forward Secretary of State Marco Rubio and Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton to handle the public response.

![White House Chief of Staff Stephen Miller placed Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin in ‘primetime timeout’ after Trump administration officials grew frustrated by his Fox News appearance, according to a new report](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/19/15/58/GettyImages-2301354597.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“The Administration took the reins—even overseas—and put together a coordinated response to inform the American people,” a White House official told *The Independent*. This is a perfect example of why Stephen was put in the role as Chief of Staff — he knows all the different components, every member of the cabinet trusts him, and he can quickly get them coordinated to deliver on a unified goal.”

White House spokesperson Taylor Rogers added that there is no evidence the string of tech outages were caused by “malicious foreign cyberattacks or SI-related attacks.”

A Department of Homeland Security official echoed much of that sentiment, telling *The Independent*: “Secretary Mullin has made clear: there is no indication of a coordinated cyber attack or malign threat behind the recent tech outages. There are no active threats to the homeland at this time, and the American people are safe.”

Microsoft blamed a massive Thursday outage that disrupted workplaces around the world on a software bug. JPMorgan Chase cited a similar problem after its banking services were thrown into disarray Monday.

Meanwhile, Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy attributed Friday’s air traffic control outage, which triggered a rare nationwide ground stop, to a router problem.

These assurances haven’t entirely quelled speculation, including from some officials, that the outages may be linked to AI — or to one another.

![During a Monday Fox News interview, Mullin told host Sean Hannity that ‘SI’ or super intelligence could be to blame for the recent outages, citing ‘one guy who is very smart with tech‘](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/17/13/43/Screenshot-2026-11-17-at-8-21-37-AM.png?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Mullin’s sidelining was not solely caused by his Fox News interview, *Politico* reported.

Frustration had been building since November 9, when he signaled he was open to granting permanent legal status to illegal immigrants who came to the U.S. as children.

“If we want to have an immigration reform where we can look at DACA [Deferred Action for Childhood Arrivals] and figure out a way that these individuals can become maybe legal permanent citizens or something like this, that’ll be up to Congress to do that,” the DHS secretary told reporters . “I’ll be open to talk to anything.”

Miller, the architect of Trump’s hardline immigration agenda, handed Mullin his “primetime timeout” just weeks into his tenure as White House chief of staff.

Trump announced that Miller would replace his boss, Susie Wiles, on November 6, three days after Democrats reclaimed the House and Senate in the midterm elections.
