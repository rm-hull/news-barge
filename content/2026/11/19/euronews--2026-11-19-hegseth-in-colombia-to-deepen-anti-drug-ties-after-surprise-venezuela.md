## Colombian Defense Minister Jorge Eduardo Mora had told his country's parliament that the Pentagon chief would hand over a C-130 Hercules military aircraft during the visit.

US Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth arrived at a military airbase in Bogotá on Wednesday for talks on security cooperation, following an unannounced stopover in Venezuela.

Colombian Defense Minister Jorge Eduardo Mora received Hegseth at the airbase in the country's capital. Mora had told Congress the previous day that the Pentagon chief would hand over a C-130 Hercules military aircraft during the visit.

Hegseth standing alongside Colombian ministers, "In each country, according to each country's laws, but in working together, we are going to find, we are going to hunt, we are going to kill and defeat narcoterrorists." "

He added that "each country wants to deny safe haven to those cartels while respecting the sovereignty, the laws and the constitution and the congress of each individual country, but bonding and working together to take a fight to a common enemy in way that has never happened before."

Following a closed-door meeting with Hegseth, Colombian Foreign Minister Omar Bula said the government will ask Congress to approve joint military operations with the US. Washington has already supported counter-narcotics strikes inside Colombia.

Bula said President Abelardo De la Espriella “has been very clear about his country’s sovereign position, and it is also very clear to the secretary of war after the conversation we had.”

## **Washington and Bogota, allies in the drug war**

Hegseth's visit follows an impeachment attempt in Congress that left him fighting to keep his job less than two years into his tenure.

Thomas Massie, a Republican lawmaker critical of Trump, accused him of mismanaging the war with Iran, overstepping his authority in personnel decisions, and carrying out extrajudicial killings through strikes on boats accused of smuggling drugs in the Caribbean and Pacific. Hegseth has survived the push for now.

Colombia has traditionally been one of the US' closest allies in the region and Washington and Bogotá have long worked together against drug trafficking, linking the world’s largest cocaine market with one of its biggest producers.

Colombia’s new right-wing president, Abelardo de la Espriella, who took office in August vowing to confront the country’s many trafficking groups, has since stepped up military cooperation with the US and intensified the crackdown.

Hegseth’s trip comes after Vice President JD Vance cancelled a planned visit to Colombia this month. Secretary of State Marco Rubio visited in September and pledged to “restore” ties with Bogotá after four years of leftist government.

And most recently, a top US military officer, General Dan Caine, visited Colombia last week to coordinate joint efforts against drug trafficking.

The two countries are carrying out a major offensive against criminal gangs that Washington classifies as terrorist organisations.

More than 300 US soldiers recently trained with 400 Colombian troops in anti-trafficking exercises, which US Southern Command (SOUTHCOM) said highlight “the deep and enduring security partnership between the United States and Colombia.”

US troops have also carried out joint operations against trafficking and violence in other Latin American countries, including Mexico and Ecuador.
