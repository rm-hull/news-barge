A federal judge stated on Wednesday that the United States Immigration and Customs Enforcement agency "made a mockery of court orders" in Colorado, finding government officials in contempt for instantly re-arresting a man whom the court had ordered released.

U.S. District Judge Charlotte Sweeney declined to pursue additional measures, stating that officials had "cured the contempt by ceasing the practice at issue" based on sworn testimony delivered in court.

"ICE's new 'policy' of immediately re-detaining petitioners following release made a mockery of court orders mandating petitioners' immediate release," the judge detailed in court records.

Over the previous six weeks, at least 18 people in Colorado were held in custody beyond their required duration or re-arrested minutes after their court-ordered release, CPR News, the news division of Colorado Public Radio, reported.

Legal experts estimated the true figure could be higher since some immigrants acted without attorneys.

![](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/19/17/2298640681..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Operating within the Department of Homeland Security, ICE has spearheaded Republican President Donald Trump's immigration crackdown and deportation campaign.

Rights organizations contend that the effort violates due process and free speech rights, while creating hazardous conditions and sparking racial profiling concerns, particularly for ethnic minority communities.

The Trump administration maintains its actions align with legal standards and target improved domestic security. ICE and DHS did not immediately respond to requests for comment regarding the judge's statements on Wednesday.

Democrats, who won majorities in both chambers of Congress in the midterm elections earlier this month, say they intend to hold the Trump administration accountable for its immigration practices.
