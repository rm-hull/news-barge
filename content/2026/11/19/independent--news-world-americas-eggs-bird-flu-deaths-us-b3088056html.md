Cal-Maine Foods, the nation’s largest egg producer, is planning to kill nearly 1.8 million birds after bird flu was detected at a facility in Florida.

The tally includes 1.4 million egg-laying hens and 360,000 juvenile “pullet” chickens, or 2.7 percent of its flock.

“Production at the facility has temporarily ceased as the company follows the protocols prescribed by the U.S. Department of Agriculture,” Cal-Maine Foods said in a Wednesday release.

The egg giant, which was part of a massive Justice Department settlement over egg prices in July, has dozens of facilities throughout the southern and eastern U.S., including the ability to house up to 57 million egg-laying hens.

It’s that network that provides “operational flexibility and resilience” right now, Cal-Maine Foods said, and the company is working to minimize potential impacts to customers.

![The nation’s largest egg producer is going to kill nearly 2 million birds at a facility in Florida due to bird flu](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/14/09/2198241291..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“The company remains dedicated to robust biosecurity programs across its locations; however, no farm is immune to HPAI,” the release noted. “HPAI continues to be present in the wild bird population, and the extent of possible future outbreaks, particularly during migration seasons when the risk is heightened, cannot be predicted.”

The announcement comes as wholesale egg prices rose over the past week, according to a report from the Department of Agriculture, with wholesale eggs $0.27 higher at $0.73 per dozen. But they’re a far cry from the historic egg prices seen in 2024-2025 as bird flu surged, when carton prices reached over $8.

Egg prices can increase as flocks are killed to slow the spread of bird flu because it has the potential to impact the nation’s supply.

Bird flu is spreading throughout the wild animal population and throughout other farms - including a mink farm in Utah - leading to the killing of nearly 1 million chickens in Illinois in October. More than 2 million birds have been impacted in the last 30 days, an Agriculture Department tracker shows.

It’s also resulted in the death of a dairy worker in Washington state, which saw the first-ever cases in cattle last month. It was the first such death in the U.S. this year.

![More than two million birds have been affected by bird flu in the last 30 days, federal data shows](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/19/17/52/GettyImages-2200486983.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Close to 1,400 people have been monitored for bird flu since October 4, according to federal data.

But health officials maintain that the risk to human health and the general public is considered low.

Humans are infected when the virus enters the eyes, nose, mouth or is inhaled. So far, there has not been recorded transmission from one human to another. But, experts fear that day could happen, as the virus mutates.

“Here we have something that is an obvious danger and we’re not doing anything about it,” Gigi Gronvall, a biosecurity expert at the Johns Hopkins Bloomberg School of Public Health, told *The New York Times* earlier this month.
