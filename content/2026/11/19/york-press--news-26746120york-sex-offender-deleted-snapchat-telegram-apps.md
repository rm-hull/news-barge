An online sex offender desperate for a social life told police he couldn’t cope with an order enabling police to track his internet behaviour and monitor him.

Aiden Bradley, 31, was handed the five-year sexual harm prevention order in 2023 when he was convicted of attempting to communicate sexually with a girl and to meet her after sexual grooming, York Crown Court heard.

In January 2025, police carrying out a routine monitoring visit at his York home found that he had been regularly breaching the order by repeatedly installing and deleting social media apps including SnapChat and Telegram on his phone without telling them, said Thomas Harris, prosecuting.

“He said he couldn’t go for five years on this order and it isn’t fair,” said Mr Harris.

John Nixon, defending, said: “He was suffering from extreme isolation at that time living in quite miserable circumstances. It shows how deep his need was for social interaction that he achieved it by social interaction online.”

He couldn't get work in York because employers didn't want to take on someone on a sexual harm prevention order.

Since then, Bradley had moved out of York and back to Saltburn-by-the-Sea where he was now in work.

Bradley, formerly of Jacobi Close, off Water Lane, Clifton, pleaded guilty to seven breaches of the sexual harm prevention order.

Recorder Simon Jackson KC said Bradley’s breaches were partly so he could contact women.

That could lead him back into offending.

But he had kept to the order for 20 months.

After reading psychological and probation reports on Bradley and his learning difficulties, Recorder Simon Jackson KC, said he had concerns that Bradley would have problems coping with prison.

He suspended a 16-month prison sentence for 24 months on condition Bradley works with the probation service on rehabilitating himself and does 150 hours’ unpaid work.

He must pay £150 prosecution costs.

Mr Harris said among 12 prohibitions the order banned Bradley from deleting apps on his phone and using apps that deleted their history such as SnapChat which can automatically delete messages after a set period of time.

When police visited Bradley in 2023, they found that between December 28, 2024, and January 24, 2025, he had installed SnapChat 41 times and deleted it 42 times, deleted Telegram 16 times, and on eight occasions had deleted an app called Pinalove which links men with Filipino women.

He had also deleted Fiorry, a transgender social media app, RobinHood, an app for investing stocks and shares, and wash.me an app for finding laundries.
