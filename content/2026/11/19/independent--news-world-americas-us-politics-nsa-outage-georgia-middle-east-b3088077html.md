A National Security Agency facility in Georgia that reportedly monitors cybersecurity in the Middle East - where the U.S. is in the middle of a conflict with Iran - has been hit by the ongoing intelligence communications outage, stretching into a third day.

While the NSA’s most sensitive and important intelligence networks are still functioning, employees at the Georgia facility were using workarounds and backup systems to share information, according to CNN, which first reported the outage Tuesday.

The outage in Georgia is the latest in a recent string of technology failures that have caused major disruption impacting flights, email systems and online banking.

There was concern about how the disruption in Georgia’s facility could impact internal communications related to the war in Iran, the outlet reports.

A cyberattack has been ruled out by U.S. officials, who told CNN the outage was linked to software updates after researchers recently discovered vulnerabilities last month.

![A National Security Agency facility in Georgia that reportedly monitors cybersecurity in the Middle East - where the US is in the middle of a conflict with Iran - has been hit by the recent communications outage](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/19/15/57/GettyImages-918218262.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The NSA outages started on Tuesday and are ongoing. AI was used to identify vulnerabilities, but organizations cannot keep up with AI's speed. As a result, the problems are not patched quickly enough.

At the NSA’s Maryland headquarters, the situation was described as a “code orange” on a voicemail inbox recording for employees, obtained by CNN, which meant the campus was closed for most staffers for the time being.

*The Independent* has contacted the NSA for comment.

In a statement Tuesday, the agency characterized the disruption as “a temporary degradation during scheduled routine maintenance.”

“While some internal systems were affected, mission-critical capabilities remain fully operational and our ability to defend national security continues,” the NSA said. “NSA's infrastructure is designed with multiple layers of redundancy to maintain operations during planned maintenance or unexpected events.”

Natasha Bertrand, CNN’s national security correspondent, said that many employees at the Georgia facility are having to work from home amid the outage.

“That in and of itself is very disruptive because they can’t access this sensitive information and classified intelligence of course, when they are simply on their home networks,” Bertrand told her colleague Erin Burnett on Wednesday night. “And so while it remains unclear when this is actually going to be fully restored, what we’re hearing right now is that this could go even into Friday before this is fully operational again.”

![At the NSA’s Maryland headquarters (pictured) the situation was described as a ‘code orange’ in a voicemail inbox recording for employees, obtained by CNN, which meant the campus was closed for most staffers for the time being](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/17/16/1227141714..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The NSA collects and analyzes foreign electronic communications and protects sensitive government communications. Its exact workforce is classified, but it is estimated to employ tens of thousands of workers.

Last Thursday, Microsoft Outlook, Teams and AI Copilot were hit by a massive outage that threw workplaces around the world into disarray and stretched into Friday. Microsoft attributed the disruption to a software bug compounded by heavier traffic and reduced server capacity.

Then, the Federal Aviation Administration ordered a rare nationwide ground stop Friday — only the second occurrence since the September 11, 2001 attacks — that led to thousands of flight cancellations and left passengers stranded across the country. Trump administration officials blamed the disruption on a routine router upgrade in Arkansas, and have called on Congress to authorize “emergency” FAA funding.

President Donald Trump said he wasn’t worried and told Americans to “relax” amid the widespread anxiety.

​“Tell them to relax. Everything’s good,” he said Wednesday on Fox News. “These are individual events.”
