## York train passenger head-butted policeman and assaulted security officer

A police officer and security officer were injured in Saturday night violence by a passenger. (Stock image of police at York Station) *(Image: Staff)*

A rowdy train passenger who injured two officers in Saturday night violence at York Railway Station has to pay nearly £1,300 compensation.

Company boss Paul Jones, 47, spent seven hours drinking in the city centre before going to catch his train home, York magistrates heard.

They said in the station he head-butted a long-serving police officer to the nose, leaving him with breathing difficulties and knocked over a security officer.

Defence solicitor James Dixon said Jones believed the police were heavy-handed in the way they dealt with him and he had responded impulsively.

Jones had suffered minor injuries.

A probation officer told the court Jones was minimising what happened in the station toilets before the violence and minimising his role in the violence.

He had told her he had come to York with friends to thank them for building a kitchen in his house and had been drinking steadily from 12pm to 7pm, initially with food.

By the time he arrived at the station, he reckoned he was drunk, but not completely so and he was remorseful for his actions.

Jones, of Merrybent Drive, Merrybent, Darlington, pleaded guilty to causing actual bodily harm to the police officer and assaulting the security officer.

The prosecution dropped a charge relating to matters in the toilet that led to the authorities dealing with Jones.

Magistrates told Jones the two victims were “just doing their job".

His head-butt had left the police officer with pain and difficulty in breathing.

“Here we have a police officer for 35 years, who is now anxious about going to work because of your behaviour towards him,” they said and ordered him to pay the policeman £1,000 compensation.

He must also pay the security officer £100 compensation, £85 prosecution costs and a £114 statutory surcharge.

Magistrates gave him a 12-month community order with 100 hours’ unpaid work.

Antony Farrell, prosecuting, said the violence happened when the British Transport Police officer was detaining Jones on Saturday, April 18.

Mr Dixon said Jones ran a business employing seven people, was a family man and had behaved out of character.

The violence had been “impulsive action in the heat of the moment as part of a relatively short incident.”

The probation officer said drinking was a “very significant factor” in Jones’ offending but that binge drinking such on April 18 was rare for him.

Normally he had two glasses of wine a week.
