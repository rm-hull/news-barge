An elderly California man’s wife and daughter were arrested Tuesday in connection with his murder, four years after his severed head was discovered in a trash bag in the woods.

Mary Fitzmaurice, 45, was arrested on suspicion of murder, while her mother, Georgia “Gwenn” Fitzmaurice, 75, faces accessory charges, in connection to the 2022 death of Paul Fitzmaurice, 73, according to the Placer County Sheriff’s Office. Detectives took the women into custody Tuesday after executing a search warrant at their Citrus Heights home.

Investigators believe Fitzmaurice’s daughter killed him before conspiring with her mother to dispose of his remains. The condition of his decomposed remains initially prevented detectives from identifying him or linking the case to any missing-person or homicide reports.

The arrests followed a lengthy investigation that eventually identified the victim as Paul Fitzmaurice, who had never been reported missing despite having been dead for years, the sheriff’s department said.

“We discovered that Gwenn and Mary had kept up the façade to family and friends that Paul was still alive, and in fact, they were still receiving Paul’s Social Security benefits and his CalPERS [California Public Employees’ Retirement System] pension,” Placer County Sheriff Wayne Woo said per KXTV.

The investigation began September 28, 2022, when a woman walking her dog discovered a severed human head inside a trash bag in a wooded area of Foresthill, in Placer County’s Todd Valley community. The discovery came as the Mosquito Fire was burning in the region.

Investigators initially struggled to identify the victim because of the condition of the partially decomposed remains. An autopsy was performed, and forensic specialists at California State University-Chico developed a preliminary age and race profile. They also created an artist’s rendering of the man’s face using his skull.

The victim’s DNA was entered into a national law enforcement database, but the search failed to produce a match.

In January 2024, investigators sent a DNA sample to Texas-based Othram Labs for forensic genetic genealogy testing. The laboratory developed a genealogical profile that helped detectives identify possible distant relatives.

With help from the FBI, investigators traced the family tree, interviewed relatives and collected additional DNA samples for comparison. A match from a relative in another state ultimately confirmed the victim’s identity as Paul Fitzmaurice.

The identification led detectives to his family in Citrus Heights, California, where they learned he had lived with his wife and daughter before his death. No one had filed a missing person report, according to authorities.

Investigators also learned that the women offered relatives explanations for his absence, including claims that he struggled with substance abuse and regularly left home for days at a time.

Woo said the women told relatives Fitzmaurice was a recluse who regularly left home but returned about once a week.

Neighbors also noticed his absence, but said no one ever told them he had died.

“I wondered what happened to Paul a few years ago,” a neighbor who didn’t want to be identified told KXTV. “I hadn’t seen him around. I asked how he was doing, and nobody ever even said he passed or he died or he was sick or anything. They just stopped talking.”

Detectives searched the family’s home Tuesday with assistance from the FBI’s Evidence Response Team, leading to the arrests.

Placer County District Attorney Morgan Gire said prosecutors were reviewing the evidence and considering additional charges against both women.

A motive for the killing has not been made public. Investigators also have yet to locate the rest of Fitzmaurice’s body, as they did not find additional remains during the search of the family home.
