The estimated cost of security at the upcoming 2028 Summer Olympics in Los Angeles could hit $4 billion, according to a report.

Protecting more than 11,000 athletes, spectators, and other high-profile attendees will likely cost between $3.5 billion and $4 billion, Los Angeles Police Chief Jim McDonnell told the *Los Angeles Times.*

The figure is much greater than previous estimates — and goes beyond the $1 billion that Congress approved last year to go toward policing the major sporting event.

“The billion dollars is certainly helpful, but that’s not going to cover the cost of just policing Los Angeles for the Olympic Games during that period of time,” McDonnell said.

“I think that is about $1.3 billion that we’re going to need,” for Los Angeles alone, he noted. “And then to police all of the other venues, I think it’s probably going to be closer to [a total of] $3.5 to $4 billion.”

![The cost of protecting over 11,000 athletes, spectators and officials at the 2028 Los Angeles Olympics may cost as much as $4 billion](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/06/29/11/1270512351..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Various athletic venues are not just spread across Los Angeles, but outside of Southern California and even as far as Oklahoma City, which will host softball and canoe slalom.

Olympic organizers do not have funds set aside for security outside of event venues, and it was not immediately clear who will pay if security costs go above the $1 billion that came from President Donald Trump’s One Big Beautiful Bill.

It was also unclear if McDonnell would remain police chief. Los Angeles Mayor-elect Nithya Raman previously suggested she would replace him before the Olympic Games open on July 14, 2028.

“My hope is that we’ll be able to work very closely together and ensure that our people in the field get what they need to be able to do the job, and that we have a great relationship moving forward,” McDonnell said.

Raman’s staff told the *Times* that she will do everything within her power to keep the city safe during the event “regardless of leadership.”

However, California officials have been warning for months that they think they will need more funds to keep the event secure. As many as 16,000 police officers are expected to be deployed to the Olympics, drawn from a pool of as many as 30,000 officers that will come into Southern California from elsewhere.

McDonnell said it would take 9,400 officers a day just to protect Olympic competition venues.

“It’s going to cost a lot of money to be able to do what needs to be done to police the Games apparently,” he said, later noting that the new Democratic Congress that will be seated in January may help secure the extra funds needed.

“It’s going to probably be good for LA because there is such a strong Democratic Party representation here,” McDonnell told the *Times*. “I think we will be able to make our pitch and hopefully be able to be heard and to be able to get the resources necessary to do the best job we can.”
