![Screenshots of Jason in GTA 6 on the Florida waterside.](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/wwqgVZnrFhreMzXmgrXGqD-1920-80.jpg)

I've been playing GTA 6 through the morning to try to see what the fuss is about, and now I totally get it. After mere hours in the game, I'm already coming to terms with the scale of it; not just the size of the map, but the attention to detail on every object and interaction. But I have to remind myself, this is sure to be the worst version of GTA 6 we'll ever play.

That sounds like an insult, but I mean the exact opposite. If this is where the game is at launch, at its most barebones, running on years-old consoles on their way out, then think of what it will be like at its peak.

## Show me the graphics menu

I work for PC Gamer, so forgive my snobbery when I say the PC version should be far superior to what we're currently playing on consoles.

A part of that is the possible frame rate bump from running on modern PC hardware. The standard PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series S/X are powered by pretty outdated architectures by today's standards: RDNA 2 and Zen 2. You're looking at the Radeon RX 6000 series and Ryzen 3000 series for PC equivalents.

These chips have recently received support for more modern ML-powered FSR upscaling techniques from AMD, though Rockstar has only seen fit to enable FSR 4 on the Xbox Series S to help the underpowered console along. Sony also has AMD-powered upscaling tech for the PS5 Pro, but the standard PS5 doesn't get a whiff of that.

![Screenshots of Jason in GTA 6 on the Florida waterside.](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/GgdsawGpbSzorU9FDRha5E-1200-80.jpg)

It's only really the PS5 Pro with a more modern GPU—loosely equivalent to an RX 7800 XT—that might temper expectations for how fast the game may run on a modern gaming PC. Rockstar has said the PlayStation 5 Pro wouldn't be capable of a steady 60 fps even with the more powerful hardware compared to the base model; however, hints at the sluggish CPU as perhaps the weak link. The PS5 Pro runs the same old eight-core CPU as the standard PS5, and if Rockstar's engine can make use of faster, more multithreaded CPUs with lots of cache, we should see a performance bump on PC.

That's not to exclude older gaming PCs. The Xbox Series S can cope with GTA 6, so should many gaming PCs of all levels. Notably so if Rockstar offers a range of upscaling options.

It's ubiquitous upscaling that should make an even bigger difference to an eventual PC release and performance on all manner of PC. We know the game supports FSR 4.1, as both Sony and Microsoft are already using this as the foundation for their upscaling technologies on console, and Red Dead Redemption 2 offers both FSR and DLSS on PC. Since that game's release, we've seen the advent of frame generation technologies from both AMD and Nvidia, which could further increase how smooth GTA 6 feels on PC.

As a serial settings tweaker, I've been sorely disappointed by the sliders, or lack thereof, available to me in GTA 6 on the base PS5. I want levels to this stuff; an advanced menu for advanced men like me. I want to manage the foliage detail, draw distance, and ray-traced effects. Speaking of which, I'm sure we'll have the option to enable ray-traced shadows, currently exclusive to the PS5 Pro, and crank up the quality of most effects in the game on PC.

The non-RT shadows are one of the few noticeable drawbacks about the standard PS5 version of GTA 6 I'm currently playing. They can pop in and out from reflections, and there is a jagginess to them in places.

## Modding makes everything better (mostly)

![Franklin flosses as chaos proceeds around him.](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/XRzUhVDLNMo4nvydh5Lb7L.gif)

Is any game going to be modded more than GTA 6? Our Joshua has been busy modding GTA 5 to try to turn it alchemy-like into GTA 6 for months now, and he never ran out of mods to install. While he may have strayed from his original mission almost immediately, the message is clear: GTA 6 is going to have a seriously busy mod scene.

The game will be all the better for it, too. Not every mod is going to enable a roving band of militant furries (Joshua's words, and no need to mod those into GTA 6) or trap the player in a tiny car; I'm sure GTA 6 will actually be a better game for the efforts of modders. It just takes a PC version and some intrepid people to make it happen.

## We're all in this together

This one is easy; one of the reasons for GTA 5's long-lasting legacy is the combination of GTA Online and custom jobs via the creator. Neither of which are actually here for GTA 6 yet. So on a very basic level, and you don't have to be a PC gamer to agree on this point, GTA 6 is going to get a lot better for the eventual online mode. And there will be an online mode, right? Even if Rockstar hasn't technically announced one is on the way yet.

On the flipside, if you're constantly getting griefed by a guy that only plays GTA 6 all day and won't let you leave your house in GTA Online, perhaps you could argue the game was better when it was single-player only.

## Crackin' console, lad

![Xbox Series X SoC exposed in rendered shot](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/qvPKjQQQFR7yLwTH4uU9Ab-1200-80.jpg)

It pains me to admit it, but GTA 6 is not only going to *eventually* run better on PC. It might be the definitive version in my eyes, but console gamers do have a high-fidelity future ahead of them, too. Both Sony and Microsoft are planning new consoles that seem pretty likely to come out in roughly a year's time—providing the memory crisis doesn't ruin everything—and AMD is set to be providing more powerful silicon for these systems.

We're expecting to see the RDNA 5 architecture make its way into the next-gen consoles, paired with Zen 6 CPU cores of some size and description (these may be heterogeneous). That would put the consoles on a level playing field with PC components likely to launch around the same time (Zen 6 should be available on desktop by then, while perhaps a more limited launch for RDNA 5 by early 2028).

So that whole spiel about how current-gen consoles are years behind our fancy PCs won't get me so far once these new systems come out. I'll have to find a new stick to bash consoles with… er, not enough RGB LEDs? Anyways, the definitive GTA 6 experience on console is still yet to come—that'll be the version that's ready to make the most of these more capable units sometime next year or in 2028.

## Patches, patches, and more patches

![Screenshots of Jason in GTA 6 on the Florida waterside.](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/aDmnUCm5tHFK5Fuvbk72xD-1200-80.jpg)

There are going to be a helluva lot of patches, upgrades, and DLCs to come for GTA 6. Remember, GTA V got a new first-person mode with the then-next-gen PS4 and Xbox One, so there's plenty of opportunity for genuine upgrades down the line.

Though, saying that, by recent PC standards (poor ports, game-breaking bugs, and more), this GTA 6 launch has been relatively issue-free. I mean, I've seen a couple of visual glitches and my colleagues have had some strange men falling out of the sky, but besides those open-world oddities you may expect of a game of this size and stature, it's been pretty smooth sailing so far.

I just hope the PC version can claim a similarly clean sweep for its eventual launch. So far, no word on when that might be.
