## Avoid the dead zones

![A hand holding a DualSense controller in front of GTA 6 artwork](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/bfpSx9VTb5SQ5iSkG9Wg8c-1200-80.jpg)

One of the comments on the last tip says there's a much more pressing option to change if you want the game to feel more responsive: change the dead zones in the controller settings.

Games tend to ignore the first little bit of motion you apply to the thumbsticks, because if they didn't, you'd get loads of annoying twitching when your thumbs move a little, and small mistakes while you correct your movement. This is the dead zone, and the larger the dead zone is, the more motion is ignored.

Some people are saying the dead zone is too big by default in GTA 6, so you have to move the sticks a lot before the game responds, so it *feels* sluggish. You can change it, though, so maybe it's worth giving it try!

This isn't the same as the framerate dropping, but could be mistaken for it: one Reddit user says they stopped some choppiness by changing a few settings on their PS5. (Though I should note that if you turn off HDCP, you won't be able to use your PS5 for streaming or Blu-rays.)

## To blur, or not to blur

![Jason and Lucia riding motorcycles in GTA 6](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/bCj2Kp3H4VQyTP9wz7AyEA-1200-80.jpg)

The first big tip: disable motion blur! Or… maybe not? This seems like it's going to be a debate early on. Quite a few people are recommending disabling motion blur to really be able to see the beauty of the game — but others are saying that keeping it on is essential on OLED TVs (which have pixels that refresh in a different way to LCD screens).
