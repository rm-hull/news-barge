Argentine authorities have arrested the wife of an Italian fugitive convicted of terrorism-related crimes on suspicion of helping him escape house arrest by rappelling from a fifth-floor apartment in Buenos Aires last week.

Betina Erika Köpcke, 77, remained in custody Thursday after being arrested in the same neighborhood where her husband, Leonardo Bertulazzi, 74, had been confined, Security Minister Alejandra Monteoliva said.

Bertulazzi, a former member of Italy’s left-wing Red Brigades militant group, was awaiting extradition to serve a 27-year prison sentence for aggravated kidnapping and possession of explosives, among other crimes. The group was responsible for kidnappings and killings of judges, police officers, business executives and politicians in Italy during the 1970s and 1980s.

On Nov. 10, Bertulazzi used ropes and a harness to descend from his fifth-floor apartment to a neighboring house before reaching a vacant lot. Security camera footage showed him climbing over a gate and jumping onto the street.

Monteoliva said evidence recovered from a cellphone Bertulazzi left behind suggested his wife helped plan the escape. The phone contained photographs taken in October 2025 showing Köpcke near the gate through which he fled, the Security Ministry said.

Argentina’s Security Ministry offered a reward of 20 million pesos (about $12,000) for information leading to Bertulazzi’s capture.

Authorities did not immediately detect the escape because Bertulazzi had tampered with his electronic ankle monitor.

A person familiar with the investigation told The Associated Press that Bertulazzi placed the device inside a container lined with a thermal blanket and filled with animal fat to simulate human body temperature and pressure. The person spoke on condition of anonymity because they were not authorized to discuss the investigation publicly.

After escaping, Bertulazzi traveled by bus through several towns north of Buenos Aires, where surveillance cameras recorded him entering a store to buy a hat, the person said.

Bertulazzi was granted refugee status in Argentina in 2004, but authorities revoked it in August 2024, clearing the way for his arrest. Following a series of appeals, Argentine courts authorized his extradition to Italy in 2026.

Monteoliva vowed to pursue anyone who helped him flee.

“We will investigate every last detail,” she said. “It doesn’t matter who they are or what role they played.”
