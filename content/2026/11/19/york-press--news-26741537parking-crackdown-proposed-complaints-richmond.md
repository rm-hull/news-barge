Plans to introduce new parking restrictions on one of Richmond town centre's last stretches of unrestricted roadside parking have divided residents.

North Yorkshire Council is considering introducing two-hour disc parking on a short section of Cravengate.

The proposals affect a stretch of road with space for around four cars, which is currently the only section of Cravengate without waiting restrictions.

Council officers say the changes have been proposed following complaints about motorists leaving vehicles for extended periods, making it difficult for local residents to find somewhere to park.

The consultation attracted four responses in support and three objections.

One supporter claimed the spaces were being permanently occupied by people staying in holiday lets and others who left their vehicles parked for weeks at a time.

The resident also alleged that some motorists were moving one car only to immediately replace it with another, while several spaces were being occupied throughout the working day by people employed in the town.

They argued that introducing restrictions would give local residents, businesses and their customers more opportunities to park.

However, council officers acknowledged that no formal parking survey had been carried out to establish the extent of these problems.

Another supporter backed the proposals on the condition that residents' parking permits could be used in other parts of Richmond where permit exemptions apply.

But opponents argued that removing the unrestricted spaces would cause difficulties for visitors and residents of nearby Victoria Road.

One objector said it was useful to have unrestricted parking in Cravengate for visitors and guests who needed to stay for longer than two hours.

They also raised concerns that alternative long-stay parking could be difficult to access for people with limited mobility.

They pointed out that previous parking restrictions on Cravengate had already reduced the number of unrestricted spaces from ten to four, and the latest proposals would remove the remaining provision entirely.

Under the proposed restrictions, motorists would be allowed to park for a maximum of two hours between 8am and 6pm, Monday to Saturday, with no return within one hour.

Vehicles would need to display a parking disc showing their arrival time, while those with an appropriate residents' permit would be exempt from the time limit.

Richmond councillor Stuart Parsons has indicated his support for the changes.

Council officers have recommended approval despite the objections, saying the restrictions would encourage a fairer turnover of parking spaces.

The cost of installing signs and road markings is expected to be no more than £1,500.

A decision is due to be considered by the council's highways executive member on Wednesday, November 25.
