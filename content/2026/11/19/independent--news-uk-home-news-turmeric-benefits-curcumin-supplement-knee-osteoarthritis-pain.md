People with knee arthritis may get little or no pain relief from taking turmeric supplements, according to researchers behind a new review.

Turmeric contains anti-inflammatory compounds known as curcuminoids, which have long been thought to help the most common form of arthritis, osteoarthritis.

The Cochrane review was conducted by an international group of researchers and looked at nine existing studies covering 1,245 people with knee osteoarthritis.

Researchers compared turmeric supplements against a placebo, non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs such as ibuprofen and diclofenac, and paracetamol.

The studies examined different turmeric extracts and curcuminoid-based products, which came in different strengths and doses.

Participants were aged 52 to 62 on average, while around two-thirds were women. The studies were conducted in Australia, Belgium, India and Thailand.

Cochrane said the trials were also relatively short, lasting between four weeks and three months.

Taken together, the results showed that – when compared to placebo and anti-inflammatory drugs – turmeric was not more effective.

However, the team said some studies were limited and the available evidence does remain uncertain.

Dr Ivan Shirinsky, lead author of the review and a practising rheumatologist, said: “As a clinician and researcher, I am not surprised by these findings.

“Osteoarthritis is a complex and still incompletely understood disease. Despite intensive research, effective pharmacological treatment options remain limited, and many promising approaches have yielded limited or inconclusive evidence of clinical benefit.

“A better understanding of the mechanisms underlying osteoarthritis may help us develop more effective treatments in the future.

“I would not routinely recommend turmeric supplements to my patients with knee osteoarthritis.

“However, if a patient asked me about them, I would explain what we know and what remains uncertain about their benefits and potential harms, and help them make an informed decision.”

Previous reviews have suggested turmeric may improve pain and physical function in people with osteoarthritis, but Cochrane raised concerns over the trustworthiness of 10 of these clinical trials and excluded them from the final analysis.

Dr Shirinsky said: “This review highlights the importance of assessing the trustworthiness of clinical trials.

“Such assessments help identify studies that require further clarification before their findings can be used to inform conclusions about treatment effects.”
