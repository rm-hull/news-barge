# Covid-19: is the United Kingdom prepared for the next pandemic?

![Covid-19: do you feel like the country is prepared for the next pandemic? (Photo: Getty)](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOmFiMjkxZjdjLWRhOGItNDA3Zi1iY2ZlLTdiMTBmZmM1NWQ1Nzo2OTE1YWQ4ZS1iM2IwLTQ4ODAtYWYzNC0yNzU5OTI4ZTVjZDc=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=990&quality=70&enable=upscale)


## It will come as no surprise to readers of this newspaper that campaigners have declared the United Kingdom’s test-and-trace system, developed with a view to saving lives during the Coronavirus pandemic, an ‘abject failure.’

No surprise because the lives lost in the chaos that ensued remains branded into the psyche of the nation, not least by those who now look across at that empty armchair where a loved one lost to Covid-19 once sat.

Again, understandably, the Covid-19 Bereaved Families for Justice Group is calling for, ahead of the publication of yet another report into what went wrong, this time by Baroness Heather Hallett, chairwoman of the UK Covid-19 Public Inquiry, findings to be truthful, transparent and trustworthy.

It is no secret that the frailties and shortcomings of those in power at the time, not least the married, philandering Health Secretary Matt Hancock, who found time during this county’s hour of need to have an affair in the workplace, on camera, whilst charged with protecting life and limb from an indiscriminate killer virus. However, that latter point is the pertinent one: Covid-19 was a fast-paced, deadly virus about which we knew little-to-nothing. All and any reviews, with the greatest of respect to Baroness Hallett, will tell us little more about what went wrong and who was to blame.

One can only hope that a robust, well-informed, emergency action plan has been formulated by more capable individuals than presided over the previous catastrophe, one that will be forthcoming to the general public should such a virus rip through the population once again.
