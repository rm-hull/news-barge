## Featured in this article

If you have an iPhone, the Apple Watch is the best wearable. It's the watch I'd wear if I weren't testing other fitness trackers. But what's the best Apple Watch to buy? With three models, each with unique attributes and features, this question has gotten harder to answer.

For the past few years, Apple has contended with a series of troublesome lawsuits and bans that led to the removal of its blood oxygen sensor (which is back). Last year, the company introduced a ton of new features, like satellite messaging and hypertension notifications. If you need help telling them apart, this guide breaks down what features are available on which watch, and whether older models are still worth the price. The latest operating system is watchOS 27, and I also discuss the Apple Watch's newest fitness-tracking features.

Be sure to check out our other related buying guides, including the Best Smartwatches, Best Fitness Trackers, and Best Sleep Trackers.

## Honorable Mention

**Apple Watch Series 10:** The Series 10 features the same S10 chip found in all the latest Apple Watches. This was the first watch to feature a slim design and a wide-angle screen that fits nicely under your shirt sleeves. It has fast charging and is also compatible with watchOS 27 (with an Apple Intelligence-enabled iPhone). It doesn't have a 24-hour battery life, which makes sleep tracking tricky unless you prioritize wearing it overnight and go without it for much of the day. That said, it often goes on sale for significantly below the MSRP, so if you see it, it's worth snagging.

## Avoid These Watches

It's been years since we’ve seen retailers carry the Series 1, Series 2, or Series 3. You may see them on resale sites, but they're not worth the price. WatchOS 27 only works with the Series 9 and newer, so we no longer recommend buying the Series 4 or 5, or the second-gen SE. The Series 1 isn't waterproof, neither the Series 1 nor the Series 2 has any cellular capability, and none of these watches are compatible with the latest watchOS version.

## Frequently Asked Questions

What Are the Fitness Features on the Apple Watch?

There are many reasons to wear an Apple Watch besides the health features. Maybe you just want quick access to your text, calendars, or Siri. Maybe you want to keep track of your kid or make sure your elderly mom doesn't fall down.

Still, I have been following the Apple Watch's development for years, from a fairly standard wearable accessory to a fully featured fitness tracker that now compares favorably against the high-end Garmins and Suuntos of the world. Since its inception, Apple has gone all in on the watch as a personal health device, with CEO Tim Cook even going as far as to say that the watch will save your life. Hypertension notifications and sleep tracking are a significant step forward, although the long-touted noninvasive continuous glucose monitor has yet to make an appearance.

In addition to health-related features, Apple has also unveiled additions to its workout programs over the past few months. The AI-enabled Workout Buddy in Fitness is perhaps the most prominent. Apple has also started offering Fitness+ in more countries and launching new workout programs, like three-week strength training programs that are designed to jump-start your workout routines.

Apple Watch data is compatible with many more fitness and workout apps besides Apple's proprietary Fitness+. For more information on which app is the right pick for your Apple Watch, check out our guide to the Best Fitness and Workout Apps. Fitness+ is also available on the iPhone, iPad, and Apple TV.
