## Featured in this article

Choosing the best action camera used to be simple: Go with GoPro. That's still good advice, as the GoPro Mission 1 Pro is our top pick, but there are also some competitors worth considering, especially if your needs don't require the more expensive Mission 1 Pro.

The decision is not easy, especially since so many of them look alike, have similar features, and do the same thing—at least, on paper. So, to figure out what exactly makes the best action camera, we dove with them, climbed with them, surfed with them, and handed them to reckless 14-year-olds on bikes. We found several great options that will survive your adventures on the ski slope, at the beach, or at the skate park.

Be sure to also read our guide to the Best 360 Cameras, as there's considerable overlap these days with action cameras. In some cases, a 360 camera might be the better option; most can shoot in a single-lens “action camera mode." We've also got guides to the Best Travel Cameras, Best Mirrorless Cameras, and Best Instax Cameras.

## The B-Roll

**About all those cheap cameras on Amazon:** Readers often ask, why spend $400 on a GoPro when they can get a camera with 4K video for $100? It's a fair question, and the answer comes down to software, primarily image stabilization. Action cameras are designed to be strapped to helmets or clipped to your chest while you skydive, rock climb, or race through the city on a scooter. Without stabilization, the results are something even your closest friends won't sit through. So yes, you'll get 4K footage with the cheaper cams, but it'll be footage no one wants to see. In our view, you're better off spending another $100 for an older GoPro or DJI Osmo Action on sale.

## Best Accessories to Trick Out Your Camera

Once you have an action camera, you're good to go for most use cases. GoPro, Insta360, and DJI all provide helmet mounts and other ways to stick your camera where you want it. But there are some nice extras that can make getting that shot you're dreaming of even easier. Here are a few:

**A good microSD card**: The action camera you buy may include an SD card. Insta360's SD cards are pretty good, actually, but I tend to use SanDisk's Extreme cards because they're fast and waterproof. You can pick up a 512-GB card for around $110. DJI has some specific recommendations for microSD cards to use with the Action 6. Of their list, the one I recommend is the Lexar Professional Silver Plus 512-GB card ($165). Sadly, like all flash-memory products, a shortage has led to skyrocketing microSD card prices.

**GoPro Media Mod ($100)**: The Hero 13 Black's sound capture is OK out of the box, but if you want higher-quality audio, this is where you start. Not because the Media Mod mic captures great sound—it's better than the camera, but still not great. What it offers is a microphone jack. Plug in a high-quality microphone, and you'll finally have awesome sound. GoPro has listened to users and wisely included this feature out of the box for the Mission 1 Pro, which will have a media mod as well, but not necessary just to connect external audio.

**Handlebar/seatpost/pole mount ($40)**: This is my favorite mount for mountain biking, but it'll also work on ski poles and any other round object you want to clamp it to.

**Motorcycle accessory bundle ($60):** If you ride, this mount for Insta360 cameras (Ace Pro, X5, etc.) is a must-have. It's one of the most secure clamps I've ever used, almost as good as a bench vise.

**Yallsame tripod mount ($12)**: Action cameras offer many ways to mount them, but one that's curiously missing is the traditional quarter-inch tripod screw mount. The GoPros have them, but this three-pack of adapters solves the problem for the others.

**Polarizer and ND filters**: If you shoot around water, through glass, or in any other high-glare situation, a polarizing filter will help cut that glare. Neutral density (ND) filters hold back light to let you shoot at a wider aperture in bright light, helping to increase the amount of intentional motion blur. Both are great additions to your action camera kit. Here are the filters by camera:

- **GoPro Mission 1 ND filters ($100)** : These are hot off the press, and I have not tested them yet, but they work the same way the Hero 13 ND filters below do, automatically adjusting the camera settings when they are attached. This is a godsend for most of us since figuring out the best settings to use with an ND filter on is complicated. GoPro has done all the hard work for you.
- **GoPro Hero 13 Black ND filter Four-Pack ($90)** : These work like the Mission 1 ND filters above when it comes to automatic adjustment, but I have used these quite a bit. They're great for increasing the motion blur in fast shots on bright, sunny days—especially if there's snow on the ground.
- **DJI ND filter set for the Action 6 ($79)** : The Action 6 does not automatically detect and adjust settings, but depending on what you're shooting, there are plenty of guides online to getting good results with these filters.
- **Insta360 Ace Pro 2 ND Filters ($122)** : These strangely expensive filters work well with the Ace Pro 2.
- **Third-party ND filters** : These are very hit-or-miss. Beware of slip-on filters as they often cause internal reflections. Others I've tried have had some pretty bad vignetting. For older GoPros, I like Freewell's polarizing and ND filters ($20). I suggest starting with an 8-stop ND or combo ND and polarizing filter.

**Floaty ($35):** Another one for the water shooters, but well worth the money since it'll keep your GoPro from ending up at the bottom of the sea. I haven't tried it, but there's one for the Insta360 Ace Pro 2 ($26) as well. DJI doesn't make an official case for the Action 6, but there is a floating grip ($59).

**Selfie stick ($25):** Surprisingly for someone who hates being on camera, I've tested a lot of selfie sticks, and they're almost all fine, but I keep grabbing this Insta360 version when I head out the door. It's lightweight, small enough to fit at the bottom of my bag, and it's affordable. If you want to go big, this Insta360 Extended Edition Selfie Stick ($100) can imitate a low-flying drone, perfect for use in national parks and other places where drones are forbidden.
